# V.01 — Water Decoction Methods
## *Tang (汤) · Kashaya (क्वाथ) · Tisane · Cold and Long-Simmering Methods*

---

### Overview

Water decoction is the oldest and most universal preparation method across all eight source traditions. Its advantages: water is accessible; the process is simple; the resulting preparation is immediately bioavailable (aqueous suspension); and the heat of decoction itself transforms certain compounds into more active forms.

Water, however, is a selective solvent — it extracts water-soluble compounds efficiently (polysaccharides, alkaloids as salts, glycosides, tannins, some terpenes) but does not effectively extract fat-soluble compounds (essential oils, fat-soluble vitamins, resinous terpenoids). Understanding this selectivity is the practitioner's first preparation principle.

---

### TCM Standard Decoction (Tang 汤)

**Vessel:** Ceramic or glass (never aluminum or copper for most herbs — reactive metals alter compound profiles)
**Water:** Filtered or spring water preferred; mineral water adds a subtle mineral profile
**Standard ratio:** 1 part herb to 10 parts water by weight (10g herb : 100ml water), simmered to 50ml reduction

**Method:**
1. Soak dried herbs in cold water for 20–30 minutes (rehydrates cell walls, improves extraction)
2. Bring to boil, reduce to gentle simmer
3. Standard simmering time: 30–45 minutes for roots and barks; 15–20 minutes for leaves and flowers
4. First decoction: pour off liquid, retain solids
5. Second decoction: add 50% original water volume, simmer half the time
6. Combine first and second decoctions — drink warm in two doses (morning + evening)

**Pre-decoction (先煎):** Certain minerals (Calcium fluorite, Dragon Bone 龙骨, Oyster Shell 牡蛎) must be simmered 30–60 minutes before adding other herbs — their mineral matrix requires extended hydrolysis.

**Late addition (后下):** Aromatic herbs (mint, peppermint, lavender) added in the final 5 minutes only — their volatile essential oils evaporate with extended heat.

---

### Ayurvedic Kashaya (क्वाथ)

The Ayurvedic standard is similar but typically uses a more concentrated reduction ratio:
- **Standard ratio:** 1 part herb to 16 parts water, reduced to 4 parts (4:1 concentration)
- Herbs are typically soaked overnight in cold water, then brought to boil and simmered
- Kashaya is consumed warm, often with honey or jaggery, before meals

**Shodita (purified) herbs:** Many Ayurvedic herbs require preliminary purification (Shodhana) before decoction — typically a brief pre-boil with specific substances (milk, cow urine, ghee, triphala water) that neutralize toxic compounds. He Shou Wu (with black bean water) and Aconite (with water + salt) are examples across traditions.

---

### Cold Decoction (for Yin herbs)

Yin-nourishing herbs that contain heat-sensitive compounds (certain glycosides, mucilages, volatile aromatics) require cold water extraction:

**Cold decoction method:**
1. Place herb (3–6g fine powder or sliced) in 300ml cold filtered water
2. Allow to macerate 8–12 hours (overnight)
3. Strain and drink cold or at room temperature
4. Best for: Shatavari, Licorice, Marshmallow root, Slippery Elm, Solomon's Seal, Pearl Powder

---

### Long-Simmering Method (for Deep Jing Herbs)

Jing-restorative herbs (Deer Antler, Cistanche, Rehmannia, He Shou Wu, Shan Yao) and animal substances (bone marrow, E-Jiao) require extended decoction to fully extract their structural Jing compounds:

**Long-simmering method:**
- Add Jing herbs to stone pot with 1 liter water
- Simmer on lowest possible heat for 2–4 hours (or use a slow cooker on "warm" setting 6–8 hours)
- Keep covered — water vapor should barely escape
- Add water as needed to maintain volume
- Final concentration should be strong and slightly viscous

**The "one-pot meal" protocol (Jing Restoration Congee):**
Combine: Deer Antler slices + Cistanche + Goji + Black Rice + Bone Marrow bones + Ginger + Salt
Slow cook 4–6 hours. The food-medicine integration is the traditional preferred method for the deepest Jing tonics — the nourishing matrix of food amplifies their action.

### Signs of Optimal Decoction

| Indicator | Optimal | Over-decocted | Under-decocted |
|-----------|---------|----------------|----------------|
| Color | Deep amber to brown, clear | Dark brown-black, turbid | Light/pale, thin |
| Taste | Concentrated, characteristic | Bitter, acrid, unpleasant | Mild, flat |
| Viscosity | Slightly thick | Very thick, syrupy | Watery |
| Aroma | Present and characteristic | Diminished or burnt | Strong and unintegrated |

### Vessel Materials — TCM/Ayurvedic Notes

- **Clay/ceramic (preferred):** Maintains electrical neutrality; infuses subtle mineral quality from the earth vessel; the traditional choice across all traditions
- **Glass:** Neutral; excellent modern choice; allows visual monitoring
- **Stainless steel:** Acceptable; neutral; easy to clean
- **Iron (traditional TCM iron pot):** Specifically recommended for Blood-building formulas (He Shou Wu, Dong Quai, E-Jiao preparations) — the pot itself adds trace dietary iron to the preparation
- **Copper:** Rarely used; specific traditions use it for specific herbs; generally avoid unless specified
- **Aluminum:** Never use — reactive metal; leaches into acidic herbal preparations
