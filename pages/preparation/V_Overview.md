## Scope and Purpose

Volume V addresses the *application science* of this Codex — the gap between knowing which substances to use and knowing *how* to use them effectively. The most sophisticated understanding of a substance's pharmacology is rendered useless by improper preparation, incorrect dosing, poor stacking choices, or mistimed delivery.

All eight source traditions devote substantial attention to preparation methodology — often as much as to the substance itself. The Chinese pharmacopoeia specifies decoction times, vessel materials, water types, and cooking sequences. Ayurveda defines the *Shodhana* (purification) and preparation methods as inseparable from therapeutic effect. Avicenna discusses menstrua (extraction solvents), compound formulation, and timing extensively. This is not superstition — these are empirically derived optimizations of bioavailability, compound transformation, and therapeutic delivery.
