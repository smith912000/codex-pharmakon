# V.02 — Tincture and Extraction Methods
## *Alcohol Tinctures · Glycerites · Oil Macerations · Spagyric Method*

---

### Menstruum Selection Guide

The menstruum (solvent) determines which compounds are extracted. Matching the menstruum to the herb's active compound profile is the primary preparation decision:

| Solvent | Extracts Well | Does Not Extract | Best For |
|---------|--------------|-----------------|---------|
| High-proof alcohol (60–95%) | Alkaloids, resins, essential oils, most phytochemicals | Polysaccharides, mucilages | Resins (myrrh, boswellia), alkaloids (berberine), essential oil-rich herbs |
| Low-proof alcohol (20–45%) | Glycosides, tannins, some alkaloids | Resins, essential oils | General tonic herbs (ashwagandha, ginseng, eleuthero) |
| Vegetable glycerin | Tannins, glycosides (moderate) | Resins, essential oils, alkaloids | Children's preparations; alcohol-avoiding practitioners |
| Oil (olive, sesame) | Fat-soluble compounds (vitamins A, D, E, K; carotenoids; fat-soluble terpenes) | Water-soluble glycosides, most alkaloids | Topical preparations; fat-soluble nutrients |
| Apple cider vinegar | Alkaloids (as acetate salts), minerals, tannins | Resins, most glycosides | Mineral-rich herbs (nettle); digestive bitters |

### Standard Alcohol Tincture Method (1:5 — Traditional Ratio)

**Materials needed:** Dried herb, amber glass jar, chosen alcohol percentage, strainer, amber dropper bottles

**Method:**
1. Weigh herb: for 1:5 ratio, use 100g herb per 500ml menstruum
2. Grind or chop coarsely to increase surface area
3. Place in clean glass jar; pour menstruum over to fully cover + additional 10%
4. Seal tightly; shake daily or twice daily
5. Macerate in a cool, dark location for **4–6 weeks** minimum (Ayurvedic Asava/Arista method extends to 1 month minimum)
6. Press and strain through cheesecloth; wring to extract maximum liquid
7. Bottle in amber glass; label with herb, ratio, menstruum, and date

**Dosing (1:5 tincture):** 2–5ml, 2–3 times daily in water

### Fresh Plant Tincture (1:2)

For herbs with high water content or heat-sensitive active compounds (valerian, passionflower, fresh ginseng):
- Immediately tincture fresh plant (do not dry first)
- Use 95% alcohol to account for the plant's high water content (blends to ~60-70% final)
- Ratio: 500g fresh plant per 1 liter alcohol

---

### Glycerite Method (Alcohol-Free)

- Use vegetable glycerine diluted to 60% with water (60% glycerine : 40% water)
- Macerate 6–8 weeks (glycerine is a slower solvent than alcohol)
- Effective for: children's preparations, liver disease contraindications, simple non-resinous herbs
- Shelf life: 1–2 years (shorter than alcohol tinctures)

### Oil Maceration (Solar and Thermal Methods)

**Cold/Solar Method (Solar Infused Oil):**
1. Fill a glass jar with dried herb (fresh herbs introduce water → mold)
2. Cover completely with carrier oil (olive for skin, sesame for traditional Ayurvedic, coconut for tropical herbs)
3. Place in warm sunny location or a warm water bath (40°C) for 4–6 weeks
4. Strain through cheesecloth; press thoroughly

**Hot Method (Faster):**
1. Heat oil and herb in double boiler at 60°C for 4–8 hours
2. Strain while hot
3. Best for: calendula, arnica, St. John's Wort, frankincense, myrrh (oil infusions)

### Spagyric Method (The Alchemical Tincture)

The Spagyric method (from the Greek *spao* — to draw apart + *ageiro* — to reunite) is the Paracelsian contribution to herbal extraction. It is based on the principle that the complete medicinal plant contains three essential principles: **Sulphur** (the essential oil/active principle), **Mercury** (the water/fluid principle), and **Salt** (the mineral residue/body).

Conventional tinctures only extract the Sulphur and Mercury principles. The Spagyric method reunites all three by:

**Step 1 — Mercury extraction:** Standard alcohol tincture as above

**Step 2 — Calcination (Salt extraction):**
- Take the spent herb from the tincture pressing
- Dry completely in oven at 100°C
- Place in ceramic crucible; calcinate in fire or kiln at 400–600°C until grey-white ash remains
- This ash contains the plant's mineral salt matrix (water-soluble salts not extracted by alcohol)

**Step 3 — Reunion:**
- Dissolve the plant ash in a small amount of distilled water
- Filter through paper filter to remove silica and charcoal
- Evaporate the filtered mineral water until a white mineral salt precipitates
- Add this mineral salt back to the original tincture and dissolve completely

**The resulting Spagyric tincture** contains the complete pharmacological profile of the plant — the alcohol-extracted phytochemicals (Mercury + Sulphur) plus the mineral matrix (Salt) that would otherwise remain locked in the spent material.

Traditional use: highest-potency, full-spectrum extraction; believed to carry the "vital intelligence" of the complete plant. Preferred by Paracelsian and Spagyric practitioners for deep constitutional work.

**Dosing (Spagyric):** 7–21 drops, 2–3 times daily — far lower dose than standard tinctures due to increased potency and mineral inclusion.

### Quality Markers for Tinctures

- **Color:** Characteristic of the herb (ginseng = amber; reishi = dark red-brown; myrrh = golden amber)
- **Taste:** Characteristic and concentrated — should have the herb's recognizable profile
- **Clarity:** Slight turbidity is normal (especially glycerites); cloudy with floating particles suggests contamination
- **Shelf life:** Alcohol tinctures (60%+ final) = 5–10 years; glycerites = 1–2 years; oil infusions = 6–12 months
