# Chaste tree fruit — 蔓荆实 *manjing shi* (Shennong bencao jing)

## *The fruit of the creeping beach shrub Vitex rotundifolia (synonym Vitex trifolia), our identification (high confidence) from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification; Sun Xingyan's own note equates the plant with the "mu jing" of other texts, which is a point of doubt* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Shennong bencao jing (SBJ).** *Manjing shi* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The identification as *Vitex rotundifolia* is ours. The source is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`); the entry is in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 蔓荆实 *manjing shi*, same file, line 1251).

**The Shennong text proper** (the flavour paragraph): "Flavour bitter, slightly cold. It governs cold and heat in the sinews and bones, numbing-obstruction and cramping; brightens the eyes, firms the teeth, frees the nine orifices, and removes white worms. Taken for long, it lightens the body and makes one resist ageing. The small *jing* fruit is the same. It grows in mountain valleys." (味苦，微寒。主筋骨间寒热痹、拘挛，明目坚齿，利九窍，去白虫。久服，轻身、耐老，小荆实亦等。生山谷。) The sentence on the "small *jing* fruit" (小荆实) says a second, smaller fruit of the *jing* shrubs is "equal" in the same respects; the text does not describe it.

**The Mingyi bielu layer** (the later additions, as Sun prints them, line 1253): "Grows at Hejian, Nanyang and Yuanju, or on the high banks of the Pingshou capital village, and in the fields. Gather the fruit in the eighth and ninth months; dry in the shade." (生河间、南阳、冤句，或平寿都乡高岸上，及田野中。八月、九月采实，阴干。) This is the habitat and harvest, and it is a later layer, not the Shennong text.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 1255).** Sun quotes the *Guangya*: "*mu jing* is *man jing*" (牡荆，蔓荆也), and the *Guangzhi*: "the *Chu* jing" (楚荆也). He then argues that, since *mu* and *man* are close in sound, the *Ben jing* gives no place of origin under *man jing* because it already did so under *mu jing*, and that "now some make them two entries, which is wrong" (今或别为二条，非). This is Sun's opinion, not part of the classic. It matters for identification: if *mu jing* and *man jing* are one plant, the entry covers what later writers treat as two.

**Preface.** The upper class "mainly nourishes life, to correspond to heaven; non-toxic; taken much or long it does not harm people" (same file, line 43).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, the fruit of *Vitex* species is a small aromatic drupe whose volatile oil is rich in terpenoids, and the genus is known for flavonoids. No analytical source for this species was retrieved here, so no compound is named. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic assigns the fruit to cold and heat "in the sinews and bones", to numbness and cramping, to the eyes and teeth, to the nine orifices and to "white worms" (the text does not say which worm). Several of these do not map onto a modern diagnosis. Nothing is recommended, and no present-day dose or protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only the Mingyi layer gives any handling: gather the fruit in the eighth and ninth months and dry it in the shade (Mingyi layer, line 1253). The Shennong text gives no preparation form and no dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

*Vitex rotundifolia* is a shrub of sandy coasts, and the dried fruit is a recognised drug in Chinese pharmacy today; that is a general statement and not a finding. Whether it affects pain, vision, teeth or worms has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong).** One flavour and one nature are printed: bitter (苦), slightly cold (微寒). The text gives no further quality. Upper class by placement.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the upper classic, and the paragraph carries a long-taking clause: "Taken for long, it lightens the body and makes one resist ageing" (久服，轻身、耐老; line 1251). *Qing shen* is "lighten the body" and *nai lao* "resist ageing"; both are the classic's own terms and are not translated into any other tradition's vocabulary here. The preface (line 43) gives the upper class its meaning: nourishing life, non-toxic, fit for long taking. The additional line that the "small *jing* fruit" is equal in this respect is the classic's, and the text does not say what that fruit is. The claim is the classic's, in the layer of Shennong text proper, and no Mingyi or Sun statement supports it further.

**Practitioner's note.** What a cultivator does with this is limited by the source: the classic says it may be taken for long, but it gives no dose, no preparation and no season of taking. Nothing in the text allows a regimen to be built. Because the identity of the fruit (see Sun's note and the Contraindications) is not secure, the honest practice is to treat this as a record of how the tradition graded the fruit, and not as an instruction.

### Contraindications

- **Identification.** Sun treats *man jing* and *mu jing* as one plant. The common modern *Vitex* species differ, and the spine lists separate entries for *Vitex agnus-castus* (I_1219, Dioscorides) and *Vitex negundo* (I_1386, Sushruta, Avicenna); do not transfer any statement between them.
- **Long use.** The classic's long-taking clause is not a safety finding. No modern safety data was retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, children, medicines.** The text says nothing; no use is supported. [USER: verify]
