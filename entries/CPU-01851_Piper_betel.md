# Piper betel — Naga-valli; Tambula (Tamvula); betel-leaf (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Piper betel, the third of three Latin forms that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints on one line (OCR "Betelo,  Coevica  Betel,  Piper  Betel,", line 32424) against the Sanskrit name Naga-valli (OCR "Netga.'Valli", line 32364). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters do not print Naga-valli. They print Tamvula once and the translator's English "betel-leaf" in three passages, and the appendix prints a second Sanskrit name, Ta'mbula, on the line before the Latin "Peper  Beltle."* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Naga-valli in its spellings (Nagavalli, Nagavalli, Nagaballi, Nagavallika), by fuzzy matching of every word against Nagavalli, Tambula and Tambuli, and for the English words "betel", "betel-leaf", "piper", "areca" and "pan". Naga-valli is not found. The betel leaf is found in three chapters, in four passages.

*Sutra ch.46 (food and drink), potherbs.* `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1763 to 1771: "The leaves of the Tamvula are sharp, heat-making (in their potency) and bitter, astringent and pungent in taste. They tend to aggravate the Pittam, are aromatic, prove beneficial to the voice, and remove viscidness in the organism. They pacify the Kapham and Vayu in the system, are appetising, pungent in digestion and deodorant, and tend to remove the fetid smell in the mouth, cleanse it of all impurities and alleviate all itching sensations experienced inside its cavity. This ends the description of potherbs." The OCR prints "Tamvula" and "(in their potency;"; spellings are normalised here only for punctuation.

*Sutra ch.46, after the meal.* Lines 3222 to 3232 (page header "562", line 3224): the eater should subdue the *Kapham* after a meal "by partaking fruit of an astringent, pungent, or bitter taste, or by chewing a betel leaf prepared with broken areca nut, camphor, nutmeg, clove, etc., or by smoking, or by means of anything that instantaneously removes the viscidity in the cavity of the mouth".

*Chikitsa ch.24 (hygiene and prophylactic measures).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch24.txt` lines 142 to 158 (page header "483", line 132): "A betel-leaf prepared with cloves, camphor, nutmeg (Jati), lime, araca-nut, Kakkola and Katukadhva (Lata-kasturi), etc., should be taken (chewed after meals), as it tends to cleanse the mouth, impart a sweet aroma to it, enhance its beauty and cleanse and strengthen the voice, the tongue, the teeth, the jaws and the sense-organs. It checks excessive salivation, soothes the body (Hridya), and acts as a general safeguard against throat disease. A betel-leaf (prepared as before) proves wholesome after a bath, after meals, after anointing as well as after rising from sleep. A person suffering from Rakta-Pitta, Kshata-Kshina, thirst, or parchedness of the mouth should refrain from taking betel-leaf, the use of which is equally forbidden in such diseases as anaemia, internal dryness of the organism and epilepsy."

*Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter, Kshina-Valiyam Vaji-Karana).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` lines 29 to 47, under the head "Means of Vaji-Karana": "Various kinds of (nutritious and palatable) food and (sweet, luscious and refreshing) liquid cordials, speech that gladdens the ears, and touch which seems delicious to the skin, clear nights mellowed by the beams of the full moon and damsels young, beautiful and gay, dulcet songs that charm the soul and captivate the mind, use of betel-leaves, wine and wreaths of (sweet-scented) flowers, and a merry careless heart, these are the best aphrodisiacs in life." The Sanskrit word behind "betel-leaves" is not printed in the file, and the chapter does not give a Latin name. The tie of this passage to Naga-valli rests on the translator's English "betel-leaf", on Tamvula in Sutra 46, and on the appendix pairings above. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Piper* is a genus of the pepper family (Piperaceae), and the betel vine is grown for its leaf, which is aromatic. The areca nut, lime and spices with which the text says the leaf is prepared are other substances, and the composition of the quid is not stated here. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the names the chapters print, the betel leaf is a potherb with stated qualities (Sutra 46), a leaf chewed after a meal to subdue the *Kapham* and to cleanse the mouth (Sutra 46 and Chikitsa 24), and one of the "means of Vaji-Karana" (Chikitsa 26). The Codex gives no protocol and no dose. Chewing betel with areca nut and lime is associated with serious harm to the mouth, which is a general fact stated in the Contraindications and not a finding of the text.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparation the chapters give is the leaf "prepared with" cloves, camphor, nutmeg, lime, areca nut, Kakkola and Lata-kasturi, "etc.", chewed after meals (Chikitsa 24, lines 142 to 149; the shorter list in Sutra 46, lines 3222 to 3232). No quantity, frequency or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed (the writer proposed (a); lowered at review because the chapter prints only the translator's English "betel-leaves", one pleasure among several, not a Sanskrit name or a drug with a stated action).* The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26, lines 29 to 47) names "betel-leaves" among the "Means of Vaji-Karana", with food, cordials, music, wine and flowers, and calls these together "the best aphrodisiacs in life". That is a place for the betel leaf in the discipline the Sushruta text calls *vajikarana*, the generative-vitality division. Three limits apply. The word printed there is the translator's English and not a Sanskrit name; the tie to the name Naga-valli rests on the appendix and is positional; and the leaf is listed as one pleasure among others, not as a drug with a stated action on *ojas*, which the text does not mention for it. The rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it. Elsewhere the leaf is a therapeutic and hygienic item (Sutra 46 and Chikitsa 24). [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Little. The text places the betel leaf among the pleasures that accompany a vajikarana regimen and as a post-meal mouth-cleanser, with a list of conditions in which it is to be avoided. It gives no dose and no course. The Codex does not recommend chewing betel, and the areca nut and lime with which the text prepares it are a hazard of their own.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints a list of persons who "should refrain from taking betel-leaf": those with Rakta-Pitta, Kshata-Kshina, thirst or parchedness of the mouth, and in anaemia, "internal dryness of the organism" and epilepsy (Chikitsa 24, lines 155 to 158). As a general fact, regular chewing of betel quid with areca nut and tobacco is linked to oral disease, including cancer; the text does not say so. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
