# Azurite — Fu Qing 肤青 (Shennong bencao jing); aliases Tui Qing 推青 and Tui Shi 推石 (Mingyi bielu)

## *A blue-green stone of the Shennong classic's middle class, which Yang 1998 renders "Azuritum" (azurite, a copper carbonate mineral). Yang's own footnote says it "may also be Malachitum" and that its identity is "not certain", and the commentator Tao Hongjing, cited by Sun, says that by his time nobody recognised it. Identification: azurite or malachite (copper carbonate minerals), by Yang's label only; confidence low. This is not the same drug as Kong Qing, Ceng Qing or Bian Qing, the other azurite-type stones of the classic's upper class, which have their own entries (II_118, II_148, II_149)* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** A copper mineral. Copper salts are poisonous when swallowed, and the classic's middle class "may or may not be toxic". This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take this stone.

**Single-text entry.** Fu Qing is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 English edition (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) and a plain e-text (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_plain_daizhige.txt`) are read as second and third witnesses. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "azurite (fu qing)", from `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` (row "Fu Qing", "Jades and Stones: Middle Class, PDF p.31 (line 1207)", Latin label "Azuritum"); line 1207 is the line of Yang's text where the drug's paragraph begins.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), Sun Xingyan edition.** The entry is 肤青 in the middle classic (卷二 中经), `Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 1813 to 1819. The paragraph reads 味辛，平 ("acrid, balanced"); 主蛊毒及蛇、菜、肉诸毒，恶创 ("governs *gu* poison and the poisons of snakes, vegetables and meats, and malign sores"); 生川谷 ("grows in rivers and valleys"). The Mingyi bielu layer (line 1817) adds 一名推青，一名推石，生益州 ("one name Tui qing, one name Tui shi; grows in Yizhou"). Sun's note (案, line 1819) quotes Tao Hongjing: 俗方及《仙经》，并无用此者，亦相与不复识 ("neither the folk recipes nor the *Xian jing* use this, and people no longer recognise it"). The drug is the last of the stones in Sun's middle-class list, and the list's closing note (line 1821) counts the middle stones as fourteen kinds where the old text had sixteen. It appears again in the drug-relations material, where Sun's entry for 斑蝥 (blister beetle) says 恶肤青 ("averse to Fu qing", line 4621). The plain e-text has the paragraph at line 79 as 肤青味辛。主治蛊毒，毒蛇，菜肉諸毒，恶创。一名推青。生山谷, with no 平 in the flavour clause.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 1207 onward, his page 31): "Fu Qing (Azuritum) is acrid. It mainly treats gu toxins, snake toxins, and all toxins of vegetables and meats as well as malign wounds. Its other name is Tui Qing (Pushing Green-blue). It is produced in mountains and valleys." (the OCR of the page is damaged at several words). Yang's footnote 60 (line 1217) reads: "This medicinal may also be Malachitum. Its identity is not certain." Footnote 61 is a note on the word *chuang* 创 ("malign wound... may also refer to malign sores"). Yang's gloss "Pushing Green-blue" is his rendering of the alias.

**The other azurite-type drugs.** The classic lists four other blue-green copper stones in the upper class, Kong Qing 空青, Ceng Qing 曾青, Bai Qing 白青 and Bian Qing 扁青 (`names.csv` rows 5 to 8, each labelled "Azuritum" by Yang), and the Codex files them at II_118 (Kong Qing), II_148 (Ceng Qing) and II_149 (Bian Qing). Fu Qing differs from them in class (middle, not upper) and in the paragraph (poisons and malign sores, not the eyes and the life-nourishing clauses). The shared Latin label in Yang is a modern editor's identification and not evidence that the classic meant one stone. A search of the other texts on disk for the Chinese and pinyin names found nothing further for this drug.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, azurite is a basic copper carbonate and malachite is also a basic copper carbonate, differing in proportion and in colour; both release copper salts in acid. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Toxic mineral class.**

- **What the text says it is for.** *Gu* poison, the poisons of snakes, vegetables and meats, and malign sores.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no manner of taking it. Tao Hongjing says that it was no longer used or recognised in his time.

The Codex does not translate *gu* poison or "malign sores" into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend swallowing or applying any copper mineral. Treat poisoning, snakebite and wounds as medical emergencies.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic gives no preparation and no dose for Fu Qing. The classic's preface contains a general rule of method for drugs that may be toxic (Sun line 4125; Yang's preface) that begins with a small amount and stops when the illness goes; it is a rule for such drugs as a class and not a dose for this stone. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Azurite and malachite are copper carbonate minerals; copper compounds taken by mouth can irritate the gut and, in amounts that are not large, can poison the liver and kidney. The classic's phrase for the stone's use against other poisons has no modern equivalent and is not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Sun 1799).** Flavour and nature 味辛，平, "acrid, balanced" (line 1815).
- **SBJ (Yang 1998).** "Acrid" only; Yang's line (1207) gives no nature. The plain e-text agrees with Yang in giving 味辛 alone (line 79).
- Sun's text differs from Yang's and the plain e-text only in the added 平 ("balanced"). No organ or channel assignment is given, and none is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* Fu Qing stands in the middle classic (中经), whose preface (Sun line 1677) reads 中药一百二十种为臣，主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经: the middle drugs are "ministers, mainly nurturing nature (养性) to correspond with humanity; non-toxic or toxic, weigh what is fitting; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the emaciated, base [the effort] on the middle classic". The drug's own paragraph names poisons and sores and contains no 久服 clause and no promise of lightening the body, not ageing or prolonging years. The register is therefore (b): framed by the middle class, whose own preface is a caution about toxicity, with no cultivation status attested for the drug. Yang's modern note on the middle class (lines 358 to 360) says that these drugs "are able to cultivate personality or modify temperament" and gives different examples; the note is commentary, and nothing in it concerns this stone.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade copper stone that the tradition's own commentator said nobody used or recognised. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use. A cultivator should not use it.

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC (copper mineral).** Do not swallow it and do not apply it to wounds or broken skin.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Given the nature of the stone, avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Poisoning and snakebite.** These are emergencies; use proper emergency care.
