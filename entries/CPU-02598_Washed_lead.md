# Washed lead — Molubdos Peplumenos (μόλυβδος πεπλυμένος, Dioscorides)

## *A fine lead powder made by beating lead under water and washing off the dark sediment; the identification is the chapter's own description, so confidence in "lead washed into a powder" is high, and the translator's gloss "Lead Molybdomenite — Selenite of Lead" is a modern mineral guess that we do not adopt; the Dioscorides heading marks it TOXIC* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** Dioscorides marks this chapter TOXIC, and lead is a poison. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should make, handle or apply this material.

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 8 November 2026 as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it. It is the preparation of lead of 5-95. The general lead row is CPU-00892, and the burnt lead of the next chapter (5-96, molubdos kekaumenos) is a separate preparation.

**Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter is 5-95, "MOLUBDOS PEPLUMENOS" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, lines 10296 to 10301). The translator prints "SUGGESTED: Washed Lead — Molybdos [Greek] — Lead Molybdomenite — Selenite of Lead" and the marker "TOXIC". The preparation reads: "Washed lead is prepared as follows. Place water into a lead mortar and beat it with a lead pestle until the water becomes black and muddy. Then strain it through a linen cloth, pouring on more water, so that all that which is dissolved may be strained. Repeat the procedure until it seems that you have enough, then letting that which is washed settle, pour out that water and pour in other water, as in the case of cadmia [5-84], and wash it until no blackness remains on it. Then make tablets from it and put it in jars. Some sprinkle water onto clean lead, beat it in a stone mortar with a stone pestle or by hand, pouring on water little by little underhand. When that which has turned black has been allowed to settle they pour it out and form it into tablets immediately. For that which is rubbed longer becomes like cerussa [white lead ore]. Some put a little plumbago [5-100] into the lead dust saying that lead washed this way is better."

The properties follow: "It is able to cool, and is an astringent to close the pores, fill up sores and wounds, and deter fluids that gather in the eyes, and fleshy abnormal growths of ulcers. It is also a blood stauncher, and is good used with rosaceum [1-53] for ulcers on the perineum, venereal warts, and haemorrhoids. It is also used for sores that will not heal, and in general it treats the same things as spodium [calcinated powder] without raising a scab. Unmixed lead is rubbed on to help the strike of a sea scorpion [2-14] or sea dragon [2-15]."

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Toxic.**

- **What the text says it is for.** An applied remedy: to cool and to close the pores, to fill up sores and wounds, to check eye fluxes and the fleshy growths of ulcers, to staunch blood, with rose oil for ulcers on the perineum, venereal warts and haemorrhoids, and for sores that will not heal; unmixed, it is rubbed on a sea-creature sting.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose and no duration. Dioscorides also does not say it is taken by mouth; all the uses read are applications.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend using lead in any form.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives the manufacture in detail (lines 10296 to 10301): beating lead in a lead mortar with a lead pestle under water; straining through linen; repeated washing until no blackness remains, as for cadmia; forming tablets; storage in jars. A variant uses a stone mortar and pestle; over-rubbing makes it "like cerussa"; some add a little plumbago. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Lead is a toxic heavy metal with no safe exposure level; fine lead dust is easily inhaled or swallowed and is absorbed through damaged skin only slightly but the dust is a hazard in itself. Historical lead plasters were astringent because lead salts precipitate proteins. Lead poisoning is a medical emergency. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter assigns no cultivation status. What it shows is a discipline of attention carried to the point of worship of procedure: the vessel is specified, the water is changed until no blackness remains, and the over-working of the material is warned against. It is not a model to follow, since the material is a poison.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing here for a cultivator. The record is a mineral-pharmacy method for external use, and the safe practice is to leave lead alone.

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC.** Do not make, handle, apply or swallow. Lead accumulates in the body.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** Lead is especially harmful to the developing nervous system. [USER: verify against current guidance]
- **Open wounds and the eyes.** Do not apply.
- **Workshop hazard.** Beating lead produces dust and fume; this is a recognised occupational hazard.
- **Identity.** The translator's "selenite of lead" gloss is not adopted.
