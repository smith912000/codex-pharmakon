# Sheep flesh — Avi (sheep), "the flesh of sheep (mutton)" in Bhishagratna's English
## *The meat of the domestic sheep; Bhishagratna prints "sheep (mutton)", and the project's source list gives the Sanskrit as "Avi (sheep)"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Sheep flesh is described in the *Sutrasthana*, chapter 46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's translation (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`). The chapter opens with a statement that food "determines the origin of beings and forms the chief source of their bodily strength and complexion, as well as of the albuminous (Ojas) principle in their organisms" (lines 8-12), and then works through the classes of food one by one. The flesh of animals is sorted into groups, and the sheep falls in the "Domestic Animal Group": "Animals such as horses, mules, cows, bullocks, asses, camels, goats, sheep, and Medapuchhas (fat tailed or Turkish sheep) etc., belong to the group of domestic animals (Gr^myas)" (lines 614-619). The project's source list (`_sources/sushruta.csv`, line 236) records this as "Avi (sheep)", citing *Sutrasthana* ch.46 and Bhishagratna vol. 1; the Sanskrit word does not itself appear in the English lines read, so the list row is the only source for it. [USER: verify]

The group is described first as a whole: "The flesh of domestic animals is possessed of constructive, tonic and appetising properties, is sweet in taste and digestion. It destroys the deranged Vayu and produces the Kapham and Pittam" (lines 621-625, under Bhishagratna's heading "Metrical Texts"). The single animals follow. After the goat ("moderately cooling in its potency ... heavy and demulcent, subdues the Pittam and the Kapham, and is beneficial in nasal catarrh") comes the sheep: "The flesh of sheep (mutton) is constructive, tonic and heavy, and generates the Pittam and Kapham. The flesh of the Medapuchcha is aphrodisiac and has properties similar to those of mutton" (lines 634-641). The Medapuchcha is Bhishagratna's "fat tailed or Turkish sheep"; "aphrodisiac" is said of its flesh and is not carried over to the plain sheep. Beef, the flesh of one-hoofed animals (stated to be like mutton, "slightly saline") and the Jangala group follow in the same passage (lines 643-651).

Sheep flesh also appears outside chapter 46, always as a diet item to be taken or avoided. In the ulcer-patient's chapter (*Sutrasthana* ch.19, line 80) "goat's flesh, mutton" is among the articles of a "Prohibited diet" ("should be regarded as unwholesome", with acid, saline or pungent things, cakes, dried meat, curd, milk and whey). In ch.20 (the chapter on the salutary and non-salutary effects of regimen) goat's flesh and mutton eaten "simultaneously with milk" are an example of articles that "may act as deadly poisons through incompatible combinations" (line 88). In ch.21 "the flesh of a Godha or a goat or mutton" may lead to the aggravation of Pittam "if taken injudiciously" (line 300). In the *Uttaratantra*, ch.40, on diarrhoea, the diet "should consist of venison, mutton or goat's flesh cooked with the tender sprouts of a Vata tree or of blood of a fatty goat duly cooked with curd (Dadhi), oil and clarified butter" (`SS_Uttara_ch40.txt`, lines 767-770). The sheep's blood is separately named as a drink after a venesection with heavy bleeding (ch.14, line 399) and as an ingredient of the *Pichchhila-Vasti* (*Chikitsasthana* ch.38, line 599); those are blood, not flesh, and belong to other entries.

### Active Components

Mutton is mostly water, protein and fat. It is a source of complete protein, iron in the readily absorbed haem form, zinc and B vitamins (notably B12), and sheep fat is rich in saturated fatty acids. Nothing in the lines read names an active principle; the qualities the text gives are those of a food, as it classes them.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Sushruta's sheep flesh is a food whose properties are catalogued, and the text assigns it to certain diets and bars it from others. The Codex does not turn that into a protocol: it records the properties the text prints (constructive, tonic, heavy, a generator of *Pittam* and *Kapham*) and the contexts of use. Mutton is an ordinary food in many places, so there is no legal or hygiene barrier to its being cooked and eaten; the objection to presenting it as a Kenetica practice is that the text gives no cultivation use for it, no preparation and no dose (see the Cultivation Register).

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation named is the one in the diarrhoea diet: mutton, venison or goat's flesh "cooked with the tender sprouts of a Vata tree" (Uttara ch.40, lines 767-769). No quantity, number of days or method of cooking is given there. In chapter 46 the flesh is described, not prepared. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Cooked lamb and mutton are a dense source of protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12, and are high in saturated fat; diet advice commonly limits red meat in people with raised cholesterol or heart disease. Mutton should be cooked through, because undercooked meat can carry bacteria and parasites. The Ayurvedic terms "heavy", "constructive" and "generates *Pittam* and *Kapham*" are the tradition's categories and do not correspond to a measurable property. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Ch.46 prints, for the domestic group as a whole, that its flesh is "sweet in taste and digestion" (*rasa* and *vipaka* sweet), that it is constructive, tonic and appetising, and that it "destroys the deranged Vayu and produces the Kapham and Pittam" (lines 621-625). For sheep it adds "constructive, tonic and heavy, and generates the Pittam and Kapham" (lines 637-639). No *virya* (potency) is printed for sheep flesh; the "moderately cooling" potency in the same passage is the goat's (lines 634-636). No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status found.* The frame is the food chapter: Sushruta opens ch.46 by tying food to bodily strength, complexion and the *ojas* principle (lines 8-12), and he calls mutton "constructive" and "tonic". Those are properties listed for a food, in a group of foods, and they are not a placement in a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) chapter. A search of the chapter files for the English words "mutton", "sheep", "ram" and "lamb" and for *Avika*, *Medapuchcha* and *Aurabhra* found no sheep flesh in any *Chikitsasthana* chapter file, the *rasayana* and *vajikarana* chapters included (searched by word, not read in full), so no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status is claimed; the only sheep products there are the fresh blood in an enema (ch.38, line 599) and the bladder used as an instrument (ch.35, line 164; ch.37, line 671). Against the "tonic" wording the text lists mutton among the prohibited articles for an ulcer patient, a cause of *Pittam* aggravation, and a poison when taken with milk, so the tradition does not treat it as a food to be taken without regard for the person. Whether *ojas* is raised by mutton is not said in any line read, and the claim is not made here. *Sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas* are Samkhya categories, not Sushrutan ones, and no such sorting is offered. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing special with this. In Sushruta, mutton is a heavy, building food that suits some conditions and is forbidden in others, and the same chapters that praise its constructive quality list it among things to avoid when ulcerated, when *Pittam* is aggravated, and with milk. If it is eaten, it is eaten as food, cooked and with attention to the person's state, and not as a substance for cultivation.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene:** mutton must be cooked through; undercooked or poorly stored sheep meat can carry bacteria and parasites, and raw sheep offal is a particular risk.
- **Conditions where the text says to avoid it:** for an ulcer patient (ch.19), with milk (ch.20), and where *Pittam* is aggravated (ch.21). These are the text's own cautions and are not medical advice.
- **Modern dietary caution:** it is high in saturated fat; people with high cholesterol, heart disease or gout are commonly advised to limit red meat. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** the flesh requires slaughter, and many readers will abstain for ethical or religious reasons; the text treats meat as ordinary diet and gives no ruling either way in the lines read.
- **Conservation status:** domestic sheep are livestock and are not a conservation concern.
