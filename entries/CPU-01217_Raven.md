### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The attestation rests on one list row and nothing else. `_sources/ebers.csv` line 147 reads "Raven, , animal" with the source "Ebers Papyrus; Ghalioungui 1987 trans., Glossary I (Medicaments)" and the uncertainty flag "yes". Ghalioungui's translation is not on disk, so the row could not be checked against its glossary, and it gives no body part, complaint, preparation or Egyptian word. The Ebers text that is on disk is Bryan's 1930 English version (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`; "Bryan" below), and it does not support the row. A search of the whole file for "raven" and "ravens" returns no hit. In Bryan's chapter VII, "Organic Remedies" (heading at line 1357), the list of animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (line 1388) runs from lion, hippopotamus and crocodile through "Vulture", "Bird-of-Passage", "Swallow", "Pigeon", "Goose" and "Duck" (lines 1390-1400) and does not contain a raven. Bryan states the limit of his own list in the sentence before it: "the identity of a considerable number of the animals mentioned is still beyond us" (line 1388).

Bryan does print bird ingredients under Egyptian names he leaves untranslated: excrement of the *adu*-bird (line 1362), heart of the *mesa*-bird (line 1963), the *per-baibait*-bird with honey for splinters (line 2335), brain of the *uuat*-bird (line 2935), blood of the *nehur*-bird (line 3841) and neck of the *gabgu*-bird (lines 4033, 4040). Whether Ghalioungui's glossary reads any of these as a raven cannot be told from the sources on disk, and this entry does not guess. The one bird Bryan names by a Latin binomial in the passages read is *Coracias garrula* (line 4224), the roller, whose fat is smeared on to prevent wasp stings. It is a different bird.

**Other texts on disk, not tagged and not attestations.** The raven is named in two other sources, and neither uses it as a drug. The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 Tibetan text lists the raven among the birds "that survive by their hunting skills" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 2914), says that poisoned food "if seen by a raven, it caws" (line 3094), and prints the raven in its animal glossary as *Corvus corax tibetanus* (OCR "Corrus corax tibetanus Hudgon", lines 6910-6912). Sushruta, in Bhishagratna's English, uses "raven" only as a hair colour (Sharira ch.4 line 517). No retrieved text assigns the raven a medicinal use, so no tag is proposed.

### Active Components

No part of the raven is named in the sources, so no composition can be given. Bird tissue is, generally, protein and fat. Ravens are omnivores and eat carrion, which matters for hygiene and not for pharmacology (see Contraindications). This entry makes no claim about a constituent.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Egyptian (EP).** The Ebers material is organised by complaint and records no qualities or classification of substances. Nothing is printed for the raven. The Tibetan source above does classify the raven, as a bird of prey that feeds on raw meat (MTK line 2914), and states that the meat of such birds "has rough, light and sharp qualities" (line 2931). That is a statement about the meat of a group in the Tibetan dietary chapter, not about the raven as a drug, and the raven is not tagged FMT. It is therefore not applied here.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*), and the person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*), but no spell, no recitation and no such term is attached to a raven in anything retrieved. Substance identity in the Ebers corpus is a modern hypothesis, and for this row the identification is flagged uncertain by the list itself. No deity is attributed. Bryan, who warns about identification (line 1388), does not list the raven at all.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing to do with this entry. The attestation is a single list row that the retrieved Ebers text does not confirm, and no use is recorded. A cultivator should treat it as an open question for the Ghalioungui glossary, not as a substance.

### Contraindications

- **Not usable:** no part, preparation or purpose is sourced; nothing here can be applied.
- **Hygiene and infection:** wild birds, and scavengers such as ravens above all, can carry bacterial and viral pathogens; handling or eating one is a real infection risk.
- **Legal and conservation status:** wild birds, including ravens, are protected by law in many countries, and taking or possessing one can be an offence. [USER: verify]
