### Origin & Tradition

Scammony is the dried **resinous juice of the root** of a Levantine bindweed, a perennial twining *Convolvulus* of the Eastern Mediterranean and Near East. The milky juice, collected by cutting the crown of the thick root and gathering the exudate that dries to a brittle, grey-black resin, was one of the most important and widely-traded purgatives of the ancient and medieval pharmacy — "Aleppo scammony" being the prized commercial grade. Dioscorides treats it among the strong purgatives in Book IV, and from his time onward scammony was a frontline drastic cathartic, valued for acting **swiftly and copiously** to evacuate "phlegm and bile."

The defining chemistry of scammony is its **resin glycosides** (the "jalapin/convolvulin" class shared with its relatives jalap and the morning glories): complex glycosidic resins that, once they reach the intestine and are hydrolysed/activated, powerfully irritate the mucosa and provoke a large outpouring of fluid and violent peristalsis — a hydragogue catharsis that empties the bowel with force. Because the action is so harsh and griping, scammony — like colocynth — was almost always **compounded and corrected**: combined with milder aperients, gums and aromatics (and sometimes "killed" by trituration with almonds or quinces) to temper its violence and reduce the intense colic. The medieval *Confectio Hamech* and many compound purgatives leaned on scammony as their driving resin.

In Unani and Arabic medicine scammony (**saqmunia / mahmudah**) holds exactly this place — a strong, hot purgative for stubborn constipation and for evacuating gross humours, always carefully corrected. Its commercial value also made it one of the most frequently **adulterated** drugs of the old trade, cut with cheaper resins, chalk or starch, so that judging genuine scammony was itself a recognised pharmacognostic skill.

### Active Components

- **Resin glycosides (convolvulin and jalapin-type glycoresins; the "scammonin" fraction)** — the principal drastic-purgative principles; complex glycosides of hydroxy-fatty acids that irritate the intestinal mucosa and stimulate a hydragogue (water-drawing) catharsis. - **Scammonin** — the traditional name for the active resin fraction.
- **Gums, starch and inert matter** — the non-resin remainder; genuine scammony is judged largely by its **resin content**, and adulteration was historically detected by resin assay.
- **Note:** unlike the morning-glory seeds (*Ipomoea*/*Turbina*), scammony's purgative resins are **not** the indole/ergoline psychoactives; its action is purely cathartic.

- **Unani/Arabic mapping:** *saqmunia / mahmudah* — hot-dry; a powerful mushil (purgative) for phlegmatic/bilious states; always corrected (islah) to reduce griping.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy — absolute.** Drastic purgatives are uterine-stimulant/abortifacient-risk; contraindicated.
- **Inflammatory or obstructed bowel, haemorrhoids, fissure.** A violent irritant cathartic is contraindicated in any fragile, inflamed or obstructed gut.
- **Children, elderly, debilitated, dehydrated.** Fluid/electrolyte loss is dangerous; contraindicated.
- **Renal/cardiac compromise.** Catharsis-driven fluid shifts hazardous.
- **No raw or casual use.** Specialist/historical framing; always corrected and compounded when used at all.
