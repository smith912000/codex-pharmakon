# Malva alcea — Alkea (Dioscorides 3-164, "Alcea"); "Hollyhock [Mabberley]" on the translators' line

## *Malva alcea [Linnaeus], the last of three Renaissance and Linnaean names on the translators' SUGGESTED line for chapter 3-164 (Alcea [Fuchs, Brunfels], Alcea vulgaris major [Bauhin]), glossed "Hollyhock [Mabberley]"; a second line, "[other usage]", adds Alcea cannabina, Althea cannabina, "Egyptian Hemp"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk names this plant under this name. The entry rests on one chapter of one text. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed under the chapter heading, and not Dioscorides' own identification. The SUGGESTED block is in two parts: the first line gives three names, of which the last is the Linnaean *Malva alcea*, glossed "Hollyhock [Mabberley]"; the second line, marked "[other usage]", gives a different plant, *Alcea cannabina* (also *Althea cannabina*), "Egyptian Hemp". The entry takes the Linnaean binomial of the first line. The English gloss "Hollyhock" names a plant that other entries in the Codex treat under another binomial (see Overlap). The Codex does not state the identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The heading *3-164. ALKEA* is at line 7918, the SUGGESTED lines at lines 7919 and 7920 and the chapter text at line 7921. The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, row 570. The chapter stands in Book Three ("Roots of Akanthoda or Prickly Plants" in the running head), after 3-163 *ALTHAIA* (line 7910, the marsh mallow) and before 3-165 *KANNABIS EMEROS* (line 7922, cannabis).

The whole chapter reads: "Alcea is a kind of wild mallow having cut-in leaves similar to those of the holy herb. It has three or four stalks, a bark similar to cannabis [3-165], a little flower similar to a rose, and five or six broad white roots almost a foot long. A decoction of these (taken as a drink with wine or water) cures dysentery and hernias."

So the chapter places the plant (a kind of wild mallow), describes it by comparison and by count (leaves "cut-in" like those of the "holy herb"; "three or four stalks"; a bark like cannabis, the cross-reference being to 3-165; "a little flower similar to a rose"; "five or six broad white roots almost a foot long"), and gives one use: a decoction of the roots, drunk with wine or water, for dysentery and hernias. It gives no habitat, no season, no other names and no quantity. The "holy herb" is not explained in the chapter. The phrase appears once in the English text (line 7921), and the Codex does not identify it. [USER: verify] The translators' index lists *Alcea*, *Alcea vulgaris major* and *Alcea cannabina* at page 534, the page printed after the chapter (lines 14532 to 14548), and *Alcea rosea* at page 267 (line 14556), which is a different chapter, 2-144.

**The chapter before.** 3-163 *ALTHAIA* (line 7910; SUGGESTED *Althaea officinalis*, "Marsh Mallow, White Mallow") is also "a kind of wild mallow", with a root that is "clammy" and white within. Its root decoction drunk with wine "helps dysentery, pains in the hips, trembling, and those troubled with hernia" (lines 7911 to 7917). The two chapters therefore share the dysentery and hernia claim, in the same words in part. The Codex does not read the 3-163 uses as belonging to the plant of 3-164, and it does not say that the plant of 3-164 is the same as that of 3-163. The author of the text keeps them as separate chapters.

**Other places in the same text.** The chapter is used as a comparison by its neighbour: in 3-166 *KANNABIS AGRIA* (line 7931), the stems of the wild cannabis are "similar to those of althea [3-163]", and that chapter does not cite 3-164. The name *alcea* is also printed as an alternative name in another chapter, 3-169 *ALISMA* (line 7945, glossed *Plantago-aquatica*, water plantain): "It is also called alcea, damassonium, acyron, or lyron." The translators' lower-case index points *alcea* to page 536 (line 10796), the page of that chapter, and not to 3-164. A plate captioned "Alcea from FUCHS — 1545" is printed at line 7651 on page 510, among the plates of the 3-123 to 3-124 run, and the index entry "Alcea 510" (line 11612) presumably points to it. These are other plants or plates, and nothing from them is attributed to this chapter. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** The chapter 3-164 is not the subject of any existing entry (chapter-level search for 3-164, *Alkea*, *Malva alcea* and the Dioscorides name *Alcea*). Three existing entries are close in subject, none by chapter. I_482 (Hollyhock, *Alcea rosea*; tags AT, CM, DMM, SBJ, SS) is the Codex's hollyhock entry, and the translators' own gloss of 3-164 is "Hollyhock [Mabberley]". Its DMM paragraph treats hollyhock at the level of the mallow group in general, and does not name 3-164 as a locus. Whether 3-164 belongs under I_482 is a question for the owner of the spine, because the translators attach two different binomials (*Alcea rosea* at 2-144, line 5753, and *Malva alcea* at 3-164) to the same English word. I_481 (Marshmallow, *Althaea officinalis*; tags AT, CM, DMM, EP, SS) and I_460 (Common Mallow, *Malva sylvestris*) cover the mallows of 3-163 and 2-144 respectively at the group level. This entry does not merge with any of them, and nothing in them is moved to or from it. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The text prints no constituent, and no analysis of the plant Dioscorides describes was read. As a general fact, the mallow family (Malvaceae) is well known for mucilage in its roots and flowers, which is a soothing, gel-forming carbohydrate. That is a statement about the family and not a finding about *Malva alcea*. Nothing is stated here about its composition. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records a root decoction in wine or water for dysentery and hernias. These are Dioscorides' claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol. Dysentery needs medical assessment, since dehydration can be serious, and a hernia is a mechanical condition that a drink does not correct. The identity of the plant is only the translators' suggestion.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives one preparation: "A decoction of these (taken as a drink with wine or water)", where "these" are the roots. The text does not say how much root, how much liquid or how often. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] It is a general, well-established fact that a hernia is a protrusion of tissue through a weakness in the body wall and is not treated by a drink. The identity is only a suggestion between at least two plants, so a modern study of one mallow would not bear directly on the Dioscorides drug.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** chapter 3-164 prints no warming, cooling, drying or moistening quality and no taste. The qualities printed are of form and colour: cut-in leaves, "a little flower similar to a rose", and "broad white roots".

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter assigns the plant no cultivation status. What it gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a description a gatherer could check, with counts (three or four stalks; five or six roots), a size (almost a foot) and a colour (white), and a comparison with two other plants. It gives no season of gathering. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a short description with one remedy for the bowel and the groin, so the honest answer is that there is nothing here to take up.

### Contraindications

Chapter 3-164 prints no warning. Dysentery needs medical care and must not be left to a home decoction, and a hernia that cannot be pushed back or is painful is an emergency. The identification is only the translators' suggestion, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. The chapter gives no dose, and no safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
