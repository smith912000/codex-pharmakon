### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is Sutrasthana chapter 38, "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam), in Bhishagratna's translation: `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15462, and `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 121.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS).** The chapter opens by saying that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)". Kalamala is printed in the Surasa'di group: Surasa, white Surasa, Fainjjhaka, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika, which "form the group known as the Surasadi Gana" (Vol. I lines 15461-15465). The Metrical Texts read: "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough" (ch.38 file lines 126-130). That statement is made of the group as a whole, and the text does not say which member does what. The chapter closes with a rule of use (ch.38 file line 558): the physician should prepare plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, ghritas or potions "according to the exigencies of each individual case", and "only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group ... or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety" are to be used as the physician decides. That is the only guidance on how a member of a group is taken.

**Second place: Sutrasthana ch.46.** Kalamala is also named in the chapter on food and drink, in the list of "spices and herbs" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 1421; Vol. I line 21081): "The spices and herbs include the Pippali, Maricha, Shringavera, Ardraka, Hingu, Jiraka, Kustumvuru, Jamviraka, Sumukha, Surasa, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Kasamaraddka [sic, for Kashamarda], Kalamala, Kutheraka, Kshavaka ... Chitraka, Mulaka-Potika, Lashuna, Palandu and Kalaya, etc." The Metrical Texts after the list say: "Potherbs have a pungent taste, are relishing, and heat-making in their potency, and subdue the Vayu and Kapham. They are variously used for seasoning food" (lines 1430-1434 of the chapter file). That statement is about potherbs in general; the text does not single out Kalamala. The same names (Sumukha, Surasa, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Kshavaka) recur between the two lists, so the ch.46 company is largely the ch.38 company.
