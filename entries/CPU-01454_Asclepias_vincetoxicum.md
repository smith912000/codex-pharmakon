# Swallow-wort (translator's gloss) — Asklepias (Dioscorides 3-106)

## *Asclepias vincetoxicum, that is Vincetoxicum officinale (the Dioscorides translator's suggestion for chapter 3-106; his line also lists Asclepias syriaca as an "other usage")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000). The binomials below are the translator's SUGGESTED line, not Dioscorides' own identification. **Named only by Dioscorides in this row.**

*3-106 ASKLEPIAS* (heading at line 7493). The translator's line: "Asclepias, Hirundinaria, Vincetoxicum [Fuchs], Asclepias albo flore [Bauhin], Asclepias vincetoxicum [Linnaeus], Vincetoxicum officinale [in Sprague] — Milkweed [other usage] Asclepias syriaca — Milkweed, Silkweed Thapsia asclepium [Loudon] — Deadly Carrot", marked POISONOUS. The text (lines 7498): "Asclepias sends out many long little branches (similar to cissus), and slender roots with a sweet scent. The flower smells strongly, and the seed is rather like that of securidaca (that which gives peace). It grows on hills. A decoction of the roots (taken as a drink in wine) helps those with griping and those bitten by poisonous beasts. The leaves are applied for malignant sores in the breasts and womb. It is also called cission, or cissophullon."

The chapter gives the habit, root, flower, seed and habitat, and two uses. The earlier chapter 3-56 (I_1017), the Aesculapian panaces, cross-refers to this one through the translator's note "see 3-106". The Latin names *Hirundinaria* and *Vincetoxicum* appear in the gloss with Fuchs's attribution. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The binomial is from the early herbals (Fuchs, Bauhin, Linnaeus). The text's own description (long little branches like cissus, slender sweet-scented roots, a strong-smelling flower) is thin, and the translator hedges by adding two further plants. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Plants of *Vincetoxicum* and *Asclepias* are generally known to be milky-juiced plants of the milkweed family, many with toxic glycosides. What the plant of Dioscorides' chapter contained was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The text records a root decoction in wine for griping and for bites of poisonous beasts, and the leaves applied to malignant sores of the breasts and womb. The Codex does not recommend the plant. It is marked poisonous and no protocol is given.

### Preparation & Dosing

Root: "A decoction of the roots (taken as a drink in wine)". Leaves: applied (lines 7498). No quantity is printed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Many members of the milkweed family contain toxic glycosides, a general statement. No study of *Vincetoxicum officinale* was opened. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** 3-106 prints "sweet scent" for the roots and "smells strongly" for the flower. No warming or cooling quality is printed. The translator marks the chapter POISONOUS.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is manufactured here. What the chapter offers is the discipline of attention the cultivation spec describes: how to know the plant (many long little branches like cissus, slender sweet-scented roots, a strong-smelling flower, a seed like securidaca), where it grows (hills), and its names (cission, cissophullon). The name carries Asklepios, as the translator notes at 3-56, but the text assigns the plant no status. It records no use for the person's vital constitution. The identification is the translator's. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice. The record is a poisonous plant with a root decoction for gripes and bites, and the abstentive answer is correct.

### Contraindications

The translator marks the chapter POISONOUS. Do not use. The traditional uses recorded are not advice, and snakebite needs medical care. Do not use in pregnancy. No dose is given.
