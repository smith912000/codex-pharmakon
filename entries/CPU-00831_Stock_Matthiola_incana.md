# Stock — Gillyflower, Hoary Stock, Ten-Week Stock, "Leucoion" (Dioscoridean white violet)

## *Matthiola incana* (L.) R.Br. | Family: Brassicaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, CM, DMM
**Energetic Classification:** Warm & Dry — a fragrant, gently warming aromatic (Galenic/Unani, by analogy to the *leucoion* group)
**Training Phase:** Settling Phase — a minor aromatic cordial used to lift and steady mood rather than to treat disease.

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Assyrian Tablets (AT):** Fragrant garden flowers were gathered by the Mesopotamian herbalists for aromatic waters, unguents and fumigations intended to gladden the heart and sweeten the air of the sickroom. Stock and its wallflower kin belong to this class of *scent-remedies* — valued for perfume and mood more than for any specific internal cure.

**Dioscorides — De Materia Medica (DMM):** Dioscorides treats the *leucoion* (the white-violet / stock-gillyflower group of fragrant crucifers) as a mild external and aromatic remedy: the flowers steeped in oil for warming applications, and infusions used as gentle emmenagogues and to soothe. The classical material does not sharply distinguish *Matthiola* from allied fragrant crucifers, so the attribution is at the level of the *leucoion* group rather than the modern species.

**Broader Materia Medica (BM):** In the Greco-Arabic and later European herbal stream, gillyflowers (stocks and wallflowers, *cheiri* / *khīrī*) were classed as warm, aromatic cordials — the flowers made into conserves, oils and aromatic waters said to comfort the heart and brain and to ease cold, windy conditions. Avicenna's tradition lists the fragrant *khīrī* oil among gentle warming aromatics. By the early-modern period *Matthiola incana* had become overwhelmingly an ornamental, and its medicinal use faded to the folk-cordial margin.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Stock is a *doctrine-of-fragrance* plant: its therapeutic identity is built almost entirely on scent and beauty. The symbolism — sweetness, comfort, the "gladdening" of the spirit — corresponds pharmacologically to a rich anthocyanin/volatile chemistry that produces the vivid colour and perfume, rather than to any potent systemic active. The tradition treats the *experience* of the flower as the medicine.

### Active Components

- **Acylated anthocyanins** — a series of ten acylated pelargonidin 3-sambubioside-5-glucosides (with malonic, sinapic, *p*-coumaric, caffeic and ferulic acid acyl groups) dominate the red-purple, salmon-pink and apricot cultivars; pelargonidin 3-glucoside dominates copper-coloured flowers. These pigments are responsible for the colour and contribute antioxidant phenolic character.
- **Flavonoids and phenolic acids** — sinapic, ferulic, caffeic and *p*-coumaric acids appear as acyl moieties and free phenolics.
- **Glucosinolates / volatile isothiocyanates** — as a Brassicaceae, *Matthiola* carries the family's mustard-oil glucosinolate chemistry, source of pungency and part of the scent profile.
- **Cardiac-glycoside-like compounds** — some *Matthiola* species (notably *M. incana* seed studies in the older literature) have been reported to contain cardenolide/cardiac-glycoside-like constituents. This is genus-level and cultivar-variable; it is flagged cautiously, not as a quantified clinical entity, and is the reason for the cardiac caution below.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Filed as a **Settling-Phase aromatic cordial** — the gentlest possible entry in this volume. Its Kenetica use case is atmospheric and psychological: aromatic waters, flower conserves and scented oils used to lift low mood, sweeten convalescence and comfort the spirit. It is *not* positioned as an internal therapeutic for any defined condition. Any internal use is minimal, traditional and cordial in scale.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, *Matthiola incana* has been studied almost entirely as a **pigment/phytochemistry** subject, not as a clinical remedy:

- Saito et al. (1996), *Phytochemistry*, isolated and structurally characterised ten acylated pelargonidin 3-sambubioside-5-glucosides across red-purple, salmon-pink, apricot and copper cultivars — the definitive account of the flower's anthocyanin chemistry. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0031-9422(95)00824-1)
- Bloor & Abrahams (2002), *Phytochemistry*, describe the major *Arabidopsis thaliana* anthocyanin as a glucosylated version of a *Matthiola incana* flower anthocyanin, confirming the shared acylated-anthocyanin scaffold in this crucifer lineage. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0031-9422(01)00460-5)

**Evidence grade: Traditional (data-sparse).** This is one of the most evidence-thin entries in the volume: the peer-reviewed literature documents pigment chemistry but essentially no controlled pharmacological or clinical data on *Matthiola incana* as a medicine. The historical cordial/aromatic reputation is genuine but rests on tradition alone. The "cardiac-glycoside-like" note is a genus-level caution from older phytochemical reports, not a quantified, clinically validated activity — it is raised to prevent casual internal self-dosing, not to assert efficacy.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic / Unani:** Warm and dry; a fragrant, gladdening cordial of the *khīrī*/gillyflower group. (Attested at group level — Dioscorides, Avicenna's aromatic-oils tradition.)
- **TCM:** No classical Chinese placement (not tagged CM).
- **Ayurvedic:** No classical Ayurvedic placement (not tagged SS). Functionally a fragrant, *sattvic*-leaning aromatic by analogy only — interpretive, not canonical.

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian (AT).** The Mesopotamian record gathers fragrant garden flowers for aromatic waters, unguents and fumigations meant to gladden the heart and sweeten the sickroom, and illness is classified there by causal agency — a substance is read partly by what agency it opposes. But under the standing blocking warning that governs every Assyrian substance identification and tablet reference, and given that *Matthiola incana* is a Mediterranean crucifer whose presence in Assyrian gardens is not securely established, no cultivation status is claimed here. The attribution is at the level of a general class of scent-remedies, not a sourced identification of this species. **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides treats the *leucoion* — the white-violet / stock-gillyflower group of fragrant crucifers — as a mild aromatic and external remedy, the flowers steeped in oil for warming applications and used as a gentle emmenagogue. The account is at group level; he does not distinguish the modern *Matthiola incana* from allied fragrant crucifers. Dioscorides carries no subtle-body system to which a cultivation status could be assigned, so what the tradition offers a cultivator is instead his discipline of attention — provenance, the season of gathering, the sensory assay of a fragrant oil, the guard against adulteration. That is a real practice of judgement, not a vital-body doctrine. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** In the Perso-Arabic and later Greco-Arabic stream the gillyflower (*khīrī*) is classed warm and dry, its fragrant oil counted among the gentle warming aromatics and its conserves among the cordials said to comfort the heart. This places it near the tradition's *mufarrih* (gladdening) drugs that are held to strengthen the *ruḥ* — but that class belongs to the *Kitāb al-Adwiya al-Qalbiyya*, not the *Canon*, and no cardiac-*ruḥ* constituent status is published here for it. The one true cultivation idea of the corpus — conservation of innate heat and radical moisture — is a longevity frame of the whole regimen, not a status assigned to a minor fragrant cordial. The species is attested in use as a warm aromatic; no cultivation grade is recorded. **(b) framed.**

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does almost nothing internal with this plant. Its entire working identity is fragrance and beauty — an aromatic water, a scented oil, a flower conserve used to lift low mood and sweeten convalescence, the *experience* of the flower being the medicine. Across all three traditions it is a Settling-phase cordial of scent, not a substance taken to build or conserve vitality, and the genus-level cardiac-glycoside caution is reason enough to keep any internal use to the culinary-cordial margin. The honest cultivation answer for stock is: enjoy the scent, and do not dose it.

### Contraindications

- **Cardiac caution:** Because some *Matthiola* species have been reported to contain cardiac-glycoside-like (cardenolide) compounds, internal infusions or extracts should be avoided by anyone with heart disease, arrhythmia, or taking digoxin/other cardiac glycosides, and should not be self-dosed by anyone. External and aromatic (scent) use carries no such concern.
- **Pregnancy / lactation:** As a Brassicaceae with traditional emmenagogue associations in the *leucoion* group and uncharacterised internal actives, avoid internal therapeutic use in pregnancy and lactation. Ornamental and aromatic use is unaffected.
- **Allergy:** Standard glucosinolate/mustard-oil sensitivity may apply to susceptible individuals.
- **General:** Absence of clinical data means no internal therapeutic dose can be recommended; keep to culinary/cordial and aromatic use.
