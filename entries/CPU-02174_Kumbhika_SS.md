# Kumbhika — Kumbhika (Sushruta Samhita)

## *Unresolved: a drug named Kumbhika in the Priyangvadi gana of the Sushruta Samhita (and probably in one Uttara-tantra recipe); no Latin name is printed, and the same word names a disease elsewhere; confidence in any identification is low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** Single-text entry. The name is printed *Kumbhika* in the Priyangvadi group of Sutra ch. 38 (Vol. I line 15664), and as *Kumbhikd* in an Uttara-tantra recipe. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it. No Latin name is printed.

**A name with other meanings.** The same spelling is also a disease in the Sushruta Samhita: *Kumbhika* is an eyelid disease (Uttara-tantra ch. 1, `SS_Uttara_ch01.txt` line 273, and ch. 3, line 38), a condition for which excision is prescribed (Sutra ch. 25, `SS_Sutra_ch25.txt` line 25), a term for a type of man in the Sharira (ch. 2, `SS_Sharira_ch02.txt` line 388, "called a Kumbhika (or Guda-yoni ...") and a disease in a classification list in the Nidana (ch. 14, `SS_Nidana_ch14.txt` line 15). Those are not this drug and are not treated here. The Codex cannot say from the translation alone that the drug and the disease share a root.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The gana (Sutra ch. 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 320 to 324; Bhishagratna Vol. I line 15664).* "The Priyangvardi Group:— The group of medicinal drugs known as the Priyangvadi Gana consists of Priyangu, Samanga, Dhataki, Naga-pushpa, Chandana, Kuchandana, Mocharasa, Rasanjana, Kumbhika, Srotohnjana, Padma-keshara, Jojanvalli, and Dirghamula." The metrical text that follows the next group (lines 337 to 342) reads: "The two medicinal recipes or groups prove beneficial in a case of deranged Pitta, favour the healing of ulcers, bring about the adhesion of fractured bones and prove curative in cases of dysentery where the stools are found to consist of lumps of thick and matured mucous (Pakvatisara.)" The text applies this to the Priyangvadi and Amvashthadi groups together.

*A recipe (Uttara-tantra ch. 40, `SS_Uttara_ch40.txt` lines 818 to 821).* "Yavagu duly prepared with the admixture of Vilva and the expressed juice of Gafds'ana (Sallaki), Kumbhika and Dadima and with curd, oil and clarified butter would prove highly beneficial." The lines read do not say for which disease the recipe stands beyond the passage on dysentery (Pravahika) that it continues. The fused spelling *Kumbhikd* and the neighbours Vilva, Sallaki and Dadima (all plants) suggest that a plant is meant here. [USER: verify]

**What the sources do not give.** No Latin name, no part used, no description.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As part of the Priyangvadi group: deranged Pitta, ulcer healing, the knitting of fractures and dysentery with lumps of thick mucus (group action, shared with the Amvashthadi group in the text). As an ingredient of a gruel (yavagu) in a dysentery passage.
- **What the text does not say.** What the plant is.
- The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

One preparation names it: a gruel (yavagu) made with Vilva and the juices of Sallaki, Kumbhika and Dadima, with curd, oil and ghee (Uttara-tantra ch. 40, lines 818 to 821). No amounts are given in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Kumbhika is named in the Sushruta Samhita as a member of a therapeutic group (gana) and in the other places listed under Origin & Tradition. The gana is a grouping by therapeutic action. It is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status, and the text read assigns Kumbhika neither. A search of the whole chapter set on disk (sutra, chikitsa, kalpa, uttara, nidana, sharira) for the name found no occurrence in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters. A disease of the same name is not this drug. Nothing links it to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The status is unattested, and only a therapeutic one is recorded.

**Practitioner's note.** A name in a group list and a recipe, and a homonym of several diseases. The entry exists to keep the name from being confused with either, and gives a cultivator nothing to use.
