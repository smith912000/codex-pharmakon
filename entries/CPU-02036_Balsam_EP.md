# Balsam [EP] — "Balsam" and "Real Balsam" (Bryan 1930, English rendering of the Papyrus Ebers)

## *An aromatic plant resin or oil that Bryan's English calls "Balsam" and "Real Balsam"; the Egyptian word is not given in the text on disk and no botanical identification is printed. The identification is unresolved, at low confidence: Commiphora opobalsamum (balsam of Mecca) is a candidate, and the working list `_sources/ebers.csv` names Balanites aegyptiaca ("Egyptian balsam / desert date") on secondary authority; we adopt neither. Kept as a row of its own because the first-pass merge into CPU-00617 (balsam, Commiphora opobalsamum) assumed an identity the Ebers text does not state.* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Ebers papyrus among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry, and the text is a modern English rendering, not the hieratic: Bryan's 1930 translation, `Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` (the book's own title, "The Papyrus Ebers", appears in the running heads). The name index (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`) holds the row "Balsam, Bryan 1930, Ch. VI Plant Remedies (line 1248)", with the note "Bryan's English identification; forms listed: Balsam | Real Balsam".

**Egyptian (EP).** Bryan's Chapter VI, "Plant Remedies", opens by saying that the Mineral list "by no means" exhausts the pharmacopoeia, that many substances are hidden "under the guise of meaningless words", and that this "applies equally to those substances which were drawn from the Plant world"; the list that follows is of "recognizable Plants, and of their preparations" (text at lines 1231-1233). In that alphabetical list the entry reads "Balsam / Real Balsam" (line 1248), immediately before "Barley-plant". Bryan therefore offers two forms of the name, and he prints no Egyptian word, no source plant and no grade for either.

The one remedy found in a search of the whole text in which the word occurs outside the list is at line 4045, in Bryan's passage on grey hair (lines 4040-4047). It is a remedy "to prevent grey hairs": "Blood-from-the-Neck-of-the-gabgu-bird / Put in Real Balsam and rub therewith" (lines 4044-4045). Bryan introduces it with a remark that this blood is "not only recommended, but backed up by a word-picture of the fortunate user", and after it prints the lines "He stretches up his hand to the back of a living falcon, placing him on his head against a living swallow!" and comments that "the Scribe merely wishes to convey that the medicament overcomes the disease like the Falcon the Swallow." The balsam is here the vehicle in which the blood is rubbed on. The recitation line is in the source and belongs with the drug (the register spec's point about *heka*); we record it and make no claim about its sense beyond Bryan's comment.

No other occurrence of "balsam" was found in the text on disk. No quantity is printed with the remedy in Bryan's rendering.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As far as the lines read go, it is the vehicle of one remedy to prevent grey hairs, rubbed on with the blood of a bird (line 4045).
- **What the text does not say.** What "Real Balsam" is made from, what it does by itself, how much was used.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this substance. The remedy involves the blood of an animal that Bryan himself calls unknown.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the sources read is the grey-hair remedy: the blood from the neck of the *gabgu*-bird is "put in" Real Balsam, and the skin is rubbed with it (line 4045).
