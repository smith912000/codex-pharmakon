# Berberis asiatica — "Berberis Asialica" (Bhishagratna, Appendix 3); "Extract of Berberis Asiatica"

## *The Asian barberry, a spiny shrub whose wood and root bark are the source of the drug extract; our identification: Berberis asiatica Roxb. ex DC., from the Latin name as printed (the e-text twice prints it, once as "Asialica"), with the plain glossary entry as the only basis; confidence medium, because the appendix gives no description and "Berberis Lycium" stands in the next line* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta translation only, in its Appendix 3 name list.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; it is now sourced to two lines of the same appendix.

**Sushruta (SS).** Two lines of Appendix No. 3 of Bhishagratna's translation, headed "Names of Drugs (with Latin synonyms)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, heading at lines 31119-31121). The translator's note at the end of the appendices (lines 33351-33354) says he drew on *Vaidyaka-Sabdasindhu*, Wilson's *Sanskrit and English Dictionary*, *Indian Plants and Drugs* and *Kavirsiji S'iksha*. The Latin names are therefore the translator's glosses from those reference works. They are not words of the Sushruta Samhita itself. ### Active Components

The appendix names no constituent. In general terms, barberries (*Berberis*) are known to contain isoquinoline alkaloids, berberine among them, which give the wood and root their yellow colour and bitter taste. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Berberis* shrubs are grown in gardens and hedges, and berberine is a yellow, bitter alkaloid long known as a dye and as a constituent of the genus. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]
