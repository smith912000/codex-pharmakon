# Astragalus yunnaneansis — srad.dkar.wa, "a type of Gum Tragacanth" (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Astragalus yunnaneansis, as Clark 1995 prints it (the spelling is Clark's; the name index prints it the same way), the first of two binomials for srad.dkar.wa, after the English "type of Gum Tragacanth" and before Oxytropis oxyphylla* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** **Named only by the Tibetan texts in this row.** No other text read for this entry names the plant under this name. The locus is in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance", and the row's binomial is the first Latin name on Clark's line, after an English description. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain, and does not correct the spelling "yunnaneansis" (the name index prints it so too, line 1560). The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1098).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The entry reads: "srad.dkar.wa: type of Gum Tragacanth: Astragalus yunnaneansis: Oxytropis oxyphylla: [has ten names and is of three types, white, black and byehu.srad.ma. The superior or white type grows in sandy areas, has a white, furry leaf and stalk and a bright white flower which cures phlegm disorders. The common type has a bluish hue and] purges first and third stages of oedema. [byehu.srad.ma] has a leaf and flower similar to the above types, a pouch-like seed-pod, small, hard seeds and dries lymph [accumulations]."

Clark's preface (line 82) says "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". So the ten names, the three types, the habitat, the description of the white type, its action on phlegm disorders and the description of the common type are the commentary layer. Outside the brackets are Clark's own identifications (the English "type of Gum Tragacanth" and the two Latin names, editorial and not root text) and the clause "purges first and third stages of oedema", which is the root-text layer. The sentence on *byehu.srad.ma* is marked irregularly: only the name and the word "accumulations" are in brackets, so by the printed marks the description "has a leaf and flower similar to the above types, a pouch-like seed-pod, small, hard seeds and dries lymph" falls outside them, though it reads as description. The Codex reports it as printed and does not decide which layer it belongs to. The text thus gives one clear root-layer action, and no taste or power anywhere in the entry.

Clark's name index prints *srad.dkar.wa* (line 1768), *byehu.srad.ma* (line 1722) and, in the Latin run, *Astragalus yunnaneansis* (line 1560). No other Clark line names the drug. Clark prints one other *Astragalus*, *Astragalus tongolensis* Ulbr., in the sub-entry on *dar.ya.kan* (line 1011), which is a different Tibetan drug and not read for this entry.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (Explanatory Tantra).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. The text prints no *Astragalus* anywhere (a search for the genus found nothing). The glossary prints "Sae kar" among its phonetic names (line 5690) and "srad dkar" among its transliterations (line 5693), in the same third place of their two columns, and chapter 20 (herbal medicines, the section begins at line 3428) reads at line 3464: "Saekar is a diuretic which drains generalized edema and ascites." That *Saekar* is the Men-Tsee-Khang form of *srad.dkar.wa* is a reading from the sound of the name and the order of the glossary columns, and the agreement with Clark on oedema is only suggestive. The text does not state it. The scientific name that falls third in the glossary's "S" block is "Oxytropis kansuensis" (line 5732), which is not the Clark binomial and not an *Astragalus*; the block holds fifteen phonetic names and sixteen scientific-name entries (counting one dash), so the Codex does not rely on that pairing. ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Astragalus* and *Oxytropis* are genera of the legume family (Fabaceae), that gum tragacanth is a plant gum from certain *Astragalus* species, and that some plants of these two genera are generally known as livestock poisons. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the Tibetan drug, which may not be either genus. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a drug that purges first and third stages of oedema (Clark line 1098, outside the brackets), that cures phlegm disorders (the white type, inside the brackets), and that, in its third type, dries lymph; the Men-Tsee-Khang text, under a name read by sound, records a diuretic that drains generalized oedema and ascites (line 3464). These are the texts' own claims and not Codex protocols. Oedema and ascites are signs of conditions that need medical assessment, and the Codex gives no protocol.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica classes the drug by what it does: it purges first and third stages of oedema, in the commentary layer it cures phlegm disorders, and its third type dries lymph. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this entry places *srad.dkar.wa* in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice. In the lines read, the essence-extraction mentions near this entry are for other plants (for example at lines 853, 870 and 971 and, in the entry *mkhan.pa*, line 1078), and *srad.dkar.wa* is not named there. The rejuvenation chapters (Clark chapter 23, Men-Tsee-Khang chapter 23) were read for names and do not name it. Its framing is therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is an oedema-purging and lymph-drying drug in the Tibetan text, and its species is not settled. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. The drug is described as one that "purges", and purgatives can cause dehydration, a general fact and not a finding about this plant. Plants of the genera *Astragalus* and *Oxytropis* include known livestock poisons, and the botanical identity is uncertain, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Oedema needs medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
