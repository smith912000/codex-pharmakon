# White mineral salt — tsabs.ru.tsha (Clark 1995, "White Mineral Salt: Crag halite"); Tsabru tsha (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011), rGyud-bzhi Explanatory Tantra ch.20

## *A salt "of natural and artificial types", the good natural kind white and said to resemble mdung.rtse; Clark glosses it "Crag halite" (rock-salt-like, sodium chloride in general chemistry), but the text does not say so and the spine's single salt entry (CPU-00298) is not assumed to be this one; identification unresolved (low to medium confidence)* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The Codex's list row is `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 212, "Tsabru tsha, , mineral, rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Efficacy of Medicinal Substances); Four Tantras of Tibetan Medicine (English trans.)", flagged as an uncertain identification. Two texts were read.

*Clark 1995* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). Chapter 20 lists the salts in part (3), the earth medicines (line 857). The entry (line 931) reads:

"tsabs.ru.tsha: White Mineral Salt: Crag halite: [has five names and is of natural and artificial types. Good quality white mineral salt is white and resembles mdung.rtse, whilst the artificial type is a white salt]. Both black and white mineral salt increase [digestive] heat, treat distention [of the abdomen], belching, flatulence and phlegm-wind combinations."

The black salt it is paired with is the entry just above (line 930, *kha.ru.tsha*, "Black 'Sanchal' Salt"), which has no action of its own: the action is a joint one, "both black and white mineral salt". By the bracket convention of Clark's introduction (line 82; square brackets carry the commentaries), the tantra's own words are the unbracketed ones, the joint action; the five names, the two types and the likeness to *mdung.rtse* are commentary. The convention was not checked against the Tibetan. [USER: verify] The salty-taste group (line 767) lists "white mineral salt" among eleven salts, separately from "rock salt" (*rgyam.tsha*, line 928).

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`). "Tsabru tsha" is in the salty-taste list (line 3211). In the salt-medicine summary (line 3400, page 203): "Kharu tsha and Tsabru tsha generate heat; reduce flatulence, eructation, abdominal distension and the combined disorders of baekan and loong." It also appears in the group of medicines "that cure cold disorders associated with baekan" (lines 3621-3626). The index lists "Tsabru tsha 187, 203, 221" (line 7529). The Men-Tsee-Khang action matches Clark's joint action for the black and white mineral salts in sense (heat; flatulence, belching, distension; phlegm-wind combinations). The match of the two spellings rests on this agreement and on the sound of the names. [USER: verify]

**What is not given.** In the passages read, neither text gives a dose, a preparation, a formula containing it, or a potency or post-digestive taste beyond the action "generate heat".

**Identity.** Clark's "Crag halite" would, in mineralogy, be sodium chloride; but the text itself separates this salt from "rock salt" (*rgyam.tsha*, "clear, crystalline salt obtained from stone", line 928) and from "white rock salt" (*mdze.tsha*, line 933), so the Tibetan text distinguishes it from kinds that a modern reader might also call halite. It has "natural and artificial types", and the artificial type is simply "a white salt". It is not safe to equate it with common salt. The spine row CPU-00298 (Salt, sodium chloride) lists rGyud-bzhi among its traditions through merged rows; this entry is kept separate because the text distinguishes the kinds, and the audit of 30 October agreed. ### Active Components

None is stated in the texts. As a general fact, the translator's gloss "halite" is sodium chloride; the artificial type is described only as "a white salt", and no composition can be stated for it. No composition is asserted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** Both texts record the salt, with black salt, as increasing digestive heat and as a remedy for abdominal distension, belching and flatulence and for combined phlegm-wind disorders. Persistent bloating and indigestion can signal medical problems. Salt in quantity raises blood pressure and strains the kidneys, and an unidentified "mineral salt" should not be taken as medicine. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation for white mineral salt. The commentary says only that there is a natural and an artificial type. No form, route, measure or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT).** The salt is placed in the salty-taste group (Clark line 767; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3211). Its action is stated as increasing digestive heat (Clark: "increase [digestive] heat"; Men-Tsee-Khang: "generate heat"). Men-Tsee-Khang names the disorders as "baekan and loong" combined, and Clark as "phlegm-wind combinations" (*bad kan* and *rlung*). The Explanatory Tantra states that hot, sour and salty tastes cure baekan disorders and that salty taste pacifies wind (Men-Tsee-Khang line 3224); that is said of the tastes in general. No classification is imported from other systems.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The tantra places the salt among the earth medicines and gives it digestive and flatulence-related actions only. The one substance of the section called "unsurpassed as an essence-extraction substance" is *brag.zhun* (Clark line 870); nothing like this is said of this salt, and no *bcud len* use was found. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation. It is a salt the tantra lists, with black salt, for digestive heat and bloating; its identity relative to common salt is not settled. It is not a recommended practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **High sodium load:** harmful in excess, especially with high blood pressure, heart, kidney or liver disease, and for infants.
- **Persistent bloating, belching, abdominal distension:** may signal medical problems; see a doctor.
- **Unsettled identity:** do not assume it is table salt or any specific mineral; nothing should be bought or used on the strength of the name.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
