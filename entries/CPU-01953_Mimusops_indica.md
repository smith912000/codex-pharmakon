# Mimusops indica — Rajadana (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3); the Sanskrit name is a positional pairing

## *Mimusops indica, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Mimusops Indica.", line 32680) in the column of Latin names; the Sanskrit name it stands against is, by counting down the OCR columns, Rajadana (OCR "Rs^aidana", line 32628), a tree whose fruit the Sushruta chapters describe. The appendix prints a different Latin line, "Mimusops Elengi.", against Bakula and Vakula, so the text itself keeps the two names apart; whether the two Latin names are one species is not settled here; confidence medium for the genus, low for the species* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was opened on 13 October from the extraction (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, line 750), where the Latin line is kept as "not paired to a Sanskrit name (OCR column split)". A first pass on 30 October called it a duplicate of CPU-01807 (`I_1371_Mimusops_elengi.md`, Bakula/Vakula); an audit kept it, because the synonymy of *Mimusops indica* with *M. elengi* is not certain. This entry follows the audit. Whether the two Latin names are one species is a question for a plant list, which was not consulted. [USER: verify]

**The pairing, by counting.** On the page that carries this Latin line, the run of Sanskrit names goes "**Rajadana**" (OCR "Rs^aidana", line 32628), "Raja-druma", "Raja-kosataki", "Raja-masha", "Rajani", "Raja-Sarshapa", "Raja-Vriksha", "Rajika" and "Rakta-Chandana" (line 32643), and on to "Rodhra", "Sahachara", "Sahadeva" and "Sailaja". The Latin run starts after the previous page's Latin lines ("Guilandina Bonducella; Caesalpinia Bonducella", "Basella Rubra", "Putranjiva Roxburghii") with "**Mimusops Indica.**" (line 32680), then "See Aragvadha", "Luffa Cylindrica", "Vigna Sinensis", "See Haridra", "Brassica Juncea", "See Aragvadha", "See Sarshapa", "Pterocarpus Santalinus" and so on. The first Sanskrit name of the run, Rajadana, therefore stands against the first Latin entry, and the neighbours agree: Raja-druma with "See Aragvadha" (a tree whose Latin is given under that name), Raja-kosataki with *Luffa cylindrica*, Raja-masha with *Vigna sinensis*, Rajani with "See Haridra", Raja-Sarshapa with *Brassica juncea*, Raja-Vriksha with "See Aragvadha", and Rakta-Chandana with *Pterocarpus santalinus*. The pairing is sound by count and by neighbours, but it is positional. [USER: verify against the page image]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The name in the chapters.* "Rajadana" occurs in four places in the chapters read, all in the Sutra, none described with a Latin name.

*Group lists (Sutra 38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 310–312).* "The Parushaka'di Group:— The group known as the Parushakadi Gana consists of Parushaka, Draksha, Kat-phala, Dadima, Rajadana, Kataka-phala, Shaka-phala and Triphala."

*The sweet (Madhura) group (Sutra 42, `SS_Sutra_ch42.txt`, lines 288–299).* Among the drugs "generally included within the Madhura group": "... Madhuka (Moula), Draksha, Kharjura, Rajadana, Tala, Narikela, modifications of the expressed juice of Ikshu (Sugarcane), Vala, Ativala, Atmagupta, Vidari ..." and others.

*Fruits (Sutra 46, `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 1120–1146).* "The fruit of such trees as the Ashvatha, the Plaksha, the Audumvura, etc., which pass by the general name of Kshira-Vrikshas as well as those which are known as the Jamva, Rajadana, Todana, Tinduka, Vakula, Phanvana, Ashmantaka, Ashvakarna, Phalgu, Panishaka, Gangeruka, Pushkara, Varti, Villa and Vimvi, etc. are cooling, and astringent in their effect. They subdue the Pittam and Kapham, tend to produce a condition of parchedness in the system, and have a sweet and astringent taste." Further down the passage (line 1143): "the Rajadana is demulcent, sweet, astringent and heavy." In the same list Vakula is named separately from Rajadana, and the Vakula fruit is described on its own lines. So in the Sushruta text the two are different fruits.

*In recipes.* The entry for Kumuda (`I_913_Kumuda.md`) quotes a medicated ghee, the Gauryadi Ghrita (Chikitsa 17), whose Kalka includes "Rajadana" among many drugs; that line was not re-read here, and a search of the Chikitsa files for the OCR spelling "Rajadana" found no hit. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** The extraction also has a separate unverified row for the bare Sanskrit name, CPU-02263 "Rajadana [SS]". This entry and that row are very probably the same drug, one by its Latin and one by its Sanskrit name; neither has been merged. ### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Mimusops* is a genus of tropical trees of the sapote family (Sapotaceae), a family whose members commonly carry latex, saponins and tannins and whose fruits are fleshy. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the Sushruta drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Rajadana as a fruit, in the sweet group, in the Parushakadi group, and among the astringent, cooling fruits that subdue Pittam and Kapham; it is described as demulcent, sweet, astringent and heavy. These are the text's statements about a Sanskrit name, and the Latin name is the translator's. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters read give no preparation for Rajadana and no part-dose. It appears as a fruit and as a member of group lists. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Fleshy tropical fruits are generally edible foods rich in sugars. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Sushruta terms (Pittam, Kapham, demulcent, heavy) are not modern diagnoses or measured outcomes, and the identity rests on a positional pairing, so a study of any *Mimusops* species would not bear directly on the Sushruta drug.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Sutra 46, lines 1120–1146):** as one of the "cooling, and astringent" fruits it "subdues the Pittam and Kapham", with "a sweet and astringent taste"; of itself, "demulcent, sweet, astringent and heavy" (line 1143). Sutra 42 lists it in the sweet (Madhura) group and Sutra 38 in the Parushakadi group. No *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Rajadana alone in the lines read.
- No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text frames Rajadana as a sweet, cooling, astringent fruit and as a member of the Parushakadi and Madhura groups. A search of the chapter files for "Rajadana" found it in the Sutra passages above (and none in Chikitsa under this spelling), and not in the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) or the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30), so the text assigns it no rejuvenative or generative status in the lines retrieved, and the Codex gives it none. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for this drug.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice with this fruit. In the Sushruta scheme it is one of the sweet, cooling fruits, listed in groups, and nothing should be gathered or eaten on the strength of a Latin name that the text does not itself give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read print no warning for the drug itself. The identity rests on a translator's appendix and a positional pairing, and the species is not settled, so no tree or fruit should be gathered or eaten on the strength of this entry.
