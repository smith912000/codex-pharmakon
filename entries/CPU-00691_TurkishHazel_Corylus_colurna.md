# Turkish Hazel / Constantinople Nut

## *Corylus colurna* L. | Family: Betulaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Kernel — sweet, oily, neutral to slightly warm; nourishing, moistening, building. Bark and leaf — bitter and astringent, cool and dry; binding and consolidating.

**Training Phase:** Foundation (kernel, as food). Peripheral / Reference (bark and leaf, as astringent-vulnerary). A **two-organ substance**: the same tree gives a nutritive and a binder, and they are not the same medicine.

---

### Origin & Tradition

Every other hazel is a shrub. *Corylus colurna* is a **tree** — a single straight trunk to twenty-five metres, corky and pale, pyramidal-crowned, native from the Balkans through Anatolia to the Caucasus and northern Iran. That fact organises everything about it: it is the hazel that behaves like timber, the hazel that can be grafted onto, the hazel that stands where the others coppice. Its nut, the **Constantinople nut**, is small, thick-shelled, and wrapped in a ragged, deeply cut husk.

**Attested Traditions**

**Ancient Mediterranean / Graeco-Roman (DMM).** The hazel enters the classical record on two tracks that never merge. As **food**, the nut is a staple of the dried-fruit trade and a fat source — the "Pontic nut" and "nut of Heraclea" names point to precisely this tree's native range, the southern Black Sea coast, though they cannot be pinned to *C. colurna* against *C. avellana* with confidence. As **medicine**, the hazel is classed among the **astringents**: bark and leaf for fluxes, for weeping and ulcerated skin, and — in the strand that persisted longest — for the **veins**: varices, phlebitis, haemorrhoids. Species-level attribution in the classical sources is not recoverable and this codex does not pretend otherwise. **Doctrine of Materia Medica / Herbal Lineage (DMM).** The European lineage carries the astringent-vulnerary doctrine forward with unusual continuity, and the pharmacognostic literature states it plainly: the three *Corylus* species have long been used in traditional medicine for **phlebitis, varicose veins, haemorrhoidal symptoms and eczema** ([Riethmüller et al. 2016, *Nat Prod Commun*, PMID 27319139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27319139/)). It names *C. colurna* explicitly — rare in this codex, where species attribution is usually the weak joint. Two **vascular-wall** indications, one **weeping-skin** indication: the astringent's classic territory. *Tighten the vessel, dry the surface.*

**Chinese Medicine — as contrast .:** CM knows hazel as **Zhen Zi (榛子)**, the kernel: sweet, neutral, entering Spleen and Stomach, **tonifying qi**, nourishing the thin and weak — a **food-grade tonic**, not a treatment agent. The CM reading takes the *kernel* and calls it nutritive, ignoring the bark; the western reading takes the *bark and leaf* and calls them astringent, treating the kernel as grocery. **Neither is wrong.** Different organs of one tree, different chemistry, different medicines — the clearest case in Volume I of tradition-divergence being a **botanical fact rather than a cultural disagreement.**

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

The tree is a diagram of its own two doctrines: an oil-rich seed wrapped in a tannin-rich case, on a tannin-rich trunk. Nourishment inside, binding outside.

*The nutritive doctrine (Zhen Zi, "builds qi") → lipid and tocopherol chemistry.* The kernel is a **monounsaturated fat depot**: MUFA on the order of 80% of the fatty-acid profile, alongside α-tocopherol ([Portarena et al. 2024, *Foods*](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods13203307)). A dense, shelf-stable, oleic-acid-and-vitamin-E food is a real caloric asset in a pre-modern diet, and "builds the thin person up" is the correct empirical description of eating one. Not mystical — nutrition, observed accurately, named in the available vocabulary. **The strongest doctrine-to-chemistry mapping here maps to food, not pharmacology.**

*The astringent-vulnerary doctrine (phlebitis, varices, eczema) → tannin and diarylheptanoid chemistry.* Bark and leaf carry condensed tannins — the class that precipitates surface protein, contracts tissue, and seals a weeping lesion. Astringency is not a metaphor; it is protein cross-linking you can taste. The vein indication runs the same logic inward: if a binder tightens a slack membrane, a slack vessel invites the same reasoning. **That step from skin to vein is where the tradition outruns its evidence**, and the Scientific Correlation refuses to follow it.

Underneath sits a surprise: beyond the expected tannins, the Hungarian *Corylus* bark and leaf work identified **37 phenolics including twenty diarylheptanoids**, the **first** such detection in the genus ([Riethmüller et al. 2015, PMID 26137784](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26137784/)). Diarylheptanoids are the curcuminoid structural class, carrying the anti-inflammatory reputation of *Curcuma* and *Alnus*. Their presence in hazel bark is a **plausible chemical basis** for a vulnerary doctrine tannins alone do not fully explain — a hypothesis with a molecule attached, not a result.

*The taxane digression.* The genus drew attention as a possible paclitaxel source via **endophytic fungi**. That is a **genus-level mycological curiosity and nothing more** — a property of fungi living in the plant, not of the plant, and emphatically **not of the kernel**. Any marketing implying otherwise is fabrication.

### Active Components

**Kernel:**
- **Monounsaturated fatty acids** — oleic-dominant; the caloric and stability core (order of 80% of the kernel FA profile).
- **α-Tocopherol (vitamin E)** — co-located with the oil; the reason hazel oil keeps.
- **Protein and non-structural carbohydrates** — starch accumulation varies with growing conditions.
- **Allergenic seed-storage and defence proteins** — the tree-nut allergen set. **The clinically dominant kernel component.**

**Bark & leaf:**
- **Condensed tannins** — the astringent principle.
- **Diarylheptanoids (≈20 detected)** — curcuminoid-class; first reported in *Corylus* in the cited work.
- **Flavonol-3-*O*-glycosides** — myricetin-, quercetin-, kaempferol-3-*O*-rhamnosides dominant.
- **Caffeic and quinic acid derivatives; flavanones.**

**Husk / shell:** phenolic-rich byproduct; in the retrieved literature an extract for nanoparticle synthesis, not a medicine.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Kernel — Foundation phase, as food.** Admitted on the footing of any dense whole-food fat: a shelf-stable MUFA-and-tocopherol source, useful where caloric density and fat quality are wanted and no tree-nut allergy is in play. Its Kenetica reading is the CM reading — *Spleen-qi-building nourishment* — with the mechanism named as lipid nutrition rather than left as doctrine. It is **food, held to food's standard of evidence and food's standard of risk**, and the risk is anaphylaxis.

**Bark & leaf — Reference only. No protocol issued.** The astringent-vulnerary tradition is well attested and names this species explicitly — more than most Volume I entries can claim. What it cannot claim is a human trial. A characterised phenolic profile, a diarylheptanoid finding, and antioxidant screening are not clinical evidence for phlebitis, varices, haemorrhoids, or eczema. This codex does not promote a bark to a treatment on the strength of a DPPH assay.

**The taxonomic point the entry exists to make.** *C. colurna* is the **rootstock** onto which commercial *C. avellana* cultivars are grafted — so **material sold as "hazel" is overwhelmingly *C. avellana* scion on *C. colurna* root**, and bark from an orchard tree may be either species depending on where the knife went. The phytochemistry distinguishes the species; the folk doctrine does not.

### Scientific Correlation

**Kernel as nutritive (the Zhen Zi / "builds qi" doctrine) — GRADED: WELL SUPPORTED, as nutrition.** MUFA on the order of 80% of the fatty-acid profile, with α-tocopherol, is documented in grafted hazelnut kernel, both varying with orchard density ([Portarena et al. 2024, *Foods*](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods13203307)). Rootstock choice measurably shifts kernel oil content, oil stability, fatty-acid profile and pellicle colour ([Rovira et al. 2022, *Front Plant Sci*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2021.813902)). **The composition is solid. It supports "this is a good fat", not "this is a medicine"** — and note honestly that most of this work measures *C. avellana* kernel on *C. colurna* rootstock: the kernel is usually the scion's.

**Bark/leaf astringency and phenolic basis — GRADED: CHEMISTRY CHARACTERISED, CLINICAL EVIDENCE ABSENT.** According to PubMed, *C. avellana*, ***C. colurna*** and *C. maxima* bark and leaf extracts profiled by HPLC-DAD-ESI-TOF-MS yielded 37 phenolics including 20 diarylheptanoids — the first such report in the genus ([Riethmüller et al. 2015, PMID 26137784](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26137784/)). An HPLC-DPPH method assessed which constituents carry *Corylus* radical-scavenging activity, citing the traditional phlebitis/varices/haemorrhoid/eczema indications as rationale ([Riethmüller et al. 2016, PMID 27319139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27319139/)). **A DPPH assay is a reaction in a cuvette. It is not evidence that a bark decoction treats a varicose vein.** No trial for any of the four indications was retrieved. The vascular doctrines are **UNSUPPORTED**.

**Genomic / agronomic corroboration of the tree's distinct identity — GRADED: SOLID, non-clinical.** De novo transcriptome assembly gave 88,343 unigenes for *C. colurna* against 70,265 for *C. avellana* — the first such dataset for *C. colurna*, the genus's most important breeding resource ([Ulu et al. 2023, *Folia Biol*](https://doi.org/10.14712/fb2023069030099)). Grafted plants on *C. colurna* root take water more deeply and store more root carbohydrate ([Portarena et al. 2022, *Tree Physiol*](https://doi.org/10.1093/treephys/tpab164)). **Substantiates the rootstock story, not any health claim.**

**Taxane / paclitaxel-producing endophytes — GRADED: CURIOSITY, NOT A PROPERTY OF THE PLANT.** **No taxane claim attaches to the kernel, oil, bark, or any preparation above.** [USER: verify before publishing any specific endophyte citation — no primary source was retrieved this run.]

**Tree-nut allergy — GRADED: STRONG, and the clinically dominant fact.** According to PubMed, in 598 children with food allergy **67% carried nut-specific antibodies and 56% were sensitised to hazelnut**; only 27% were mono-sensitised, 73% co-sensitised to two or three tested nuts; nearly one-third had experienced anaphylaxis, and **"even the first exposure to nuts may be sufficient to trigger the anaphylaxis"** ([Łyżwa et al. 2023, *Postepy Dermatol Alergol*](https://doi.org/10.5114/ada.2023.128991)). Among 236 LTP-allergic patients, 54.66% had suffered anaphylaxis and **hazelnut produced symptoms in 44%**, ranking among the foods most frequently provoking anaphylaxis alongside walnut, peach and peanut; cofactors were present in 48.84% of the anaphylaxis group ([Miralles-Lopez et al. 2024, *Allergol Immunopathol*](https://doi.org/10.15586/aei.v52i4.1074)).

**Net grade: a genuinely good food with a genuinely lethal allergy, and a well-attested bark doctrine with no clinical evidence behind it.** This entry's best-supported claim is nutritional; its most important claim is a warning. (Non-medicinal, filed so it is never mistaken for medicine: the tree captures airborne particulates in leaf waxes, [Popek et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/15226514.2012.694498); its extract coats a CuO-nanoparticle biosensor, [Karakuş et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodchem.2023.135918).)

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian (AT).** No Assyrian attestation for Turkish hazel is present in this entry, and none was retrieved. The cuneiform materia medica of the Mesopotamian *asû* and *āšipu* classifies a substance partly by the causal agency (divine hand, ghost, oath) it is set against, but *Corylus colurna* — a Balkan-to-Caucasus tree outside the core Mesopotamian flora — is not identified in the tablet record available here. Under the standing blocking warning that governs every Assyrian substance identification and tablet reference, no cultivation status is claimed. **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The hazel enters the classical record on two tracks: the edible "Pontic nut" / "nut of Heraclea" (a fat source of the dried-fruit trade) and the astringent bark and leaf worked for fluxes, weeping skin and — most persistently — the veins (varices, phlebitis, haemorrhoids). Species-level attribution to *C. colurna* against *C. avellana* is not recoverable from the classical sources, as the entry states. Dioscorides carries no subtle-body system into which a cultivation status could be assigned; what the tradition offers a cultivator is his discipline of attention — provenance (the southern Black Sea range behind the "Pontic" and "Heraclea" names), the astringent assay of bark and leaf, the guard against confusing one *Corylus* for another. That is a real practice of judgement, not a vital-body doctrine. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** No Unani attestation for this species is present in the entry, and the material filed here under the CM code is not Avicennan at all: it is Chinese — *Zhen Zi* 榛子, the kernel read as a Spleen-and-Stomach qi-tonic food. That is a code misapplication (see the attestation flag below), not an *Ibn Sīnā* / Unani source. The strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus — conservation of the finite innate heat and radical moisture, longevity as non-depletion — is nowhere assigned to Turkish hazel in any Unani text retrieved, and no *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed. On the Unani reading proper, therefore: **(c) absent.**

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a cultivation substance. The kernel is a dense monounsaturated-fat-and-tocopherol food, admitted at food's standard of evidence and carrying food's gravest risk — tree-nut anaphylaxis; the bark and leaf are an astringent-vulnerary the codex refuses to promote to a protocol on DPPH data alone. A cultivator eats the nut as a shelf-stable caloric asset where no nut allergy is in play, and does nothing ceremonial with the bark. The one genuine cultivation-adjacent discipline attaching to this plant is provenance and species discernment — knowing that orchard "hazel" is usually *C. avellana* scion grafted onto *C. colurna* root, so a given bark may be either species. That is Dioscorides' attention-practice, not a vital-body operation.

### Contraindications

**⚠️⚠️ TREE-NUT ALLERGY — ANAPHYLAXIS RISK. This is the single most important line in the entry.**

- **Absolute contraindication in tree-nut allergy.** Co-sensitisation is the norm — a walnut, peanut or almond allergy is a reason to suspect hazel, not to clear it. **LTP-syndrome patients** are a distinct high-risk group and hazelnut sits inside it.
- **A first exposure can be the anaphylactic one.** No prior tolerance history makes a first dose safe. Do not "test" a nut in an atopic or nut-allergic person outside a supervised clinical setting.
- **Cofactors matter.** Exercise, alcohol, NSAIDs and infection track with the severe end of the LTP spectrum. A kernel tolerated at rest is not thereby tolerated post-exertion.
- **Kernel oil is not automatically safe.** Refining status determines residual protein; treat cold-pressed oil as allergenic. **[USER: verify]** refining status of any specific product.
- **Emergency provision.** Anyone with a known nut allergy where nuts are present must have adrenaline auto-injector access and someone who knows how to use it — a condition of the kernel's Foundation-phase admission.

**Other:**
- **Bark and leaf: no internal protocol.** Long-term tannin-heavy decoctions impair mineral absorption and irritate gastric mucosa, and the vascular indications are unsupported. Do not self-treat phlebitis, varices, or haemorrhoids with a bark decoction — a delayed real diagnosis (DVT above all) costs limbs and lives.
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** kernel as food, per normal dietary guidance. Bark and leaf — **avoid**; unstudied.
- **Species and organ identity.** *C. colurna* ≠ *C. avellana* ≠ *C. maxima*. Because *C. colurna* is the standard **rootstock** under grafted *C. avellana*, bark from an orchard tree may be either species depending on height. Verify provenance and graft line before accepting bark material.
- **Do not read the endophyte taxane literature as a property of this plant.** No hazel preparation contains a meaningful taxane and none should be represented as doing so.
