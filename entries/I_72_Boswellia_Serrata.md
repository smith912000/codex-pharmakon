## Section 1 — Nomenclature

### Primary Scientific Name
*Boswellia serrata* Roxb. ex Colebr. — Indian Frankincense (Burseraceae)

**Codex Note**: See I_19 (*Boswellia sacra/carteri* — Omani/East African Frankincense) for the primary Frankincense entry covering the ancient religious/trade tradition. This entry specifically addresses *B. serrata* (Indian Frankincense) and its distinct pharmacological profile — particularly AKBA (3-O-acetyl-11-keto-β-boswellic acid) and the clinical evidence in arthritis and IBD.

### Botanical Identity
Medium deciduous tree 3–8m; papery peeling bark; compound leaves; small white-cream flowers; sticky milky resin tapped from bark incisions → air-dried into yellow-orange tears. Native to dry hilly Indian forests (Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat). Distinguished from Arabian *B. sacra* by: higher AKBA content (3.5–5% vs 1–2%); different boswellic acid profile; Indian resin lacks the incensole acetate of Arabian frankincense. The Indian species is the standard for clinical osteoarthritis and IBD trials.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 5-LOX Inhibition (Primary — Specific to Boswellic Acids)

**AKBA**: Directly inhibits 5-lipoxygenase (5-LOX) enzyme → prevents leukotriene (LTB4, LTC4, LTE4) synthesis. Leukotrienes are primary mediators of inflammatory conditions distinct from prostaglandins (COX pathway):
- RA joint inflammation: LTB4-dominated → AKBA particularly effective vs prostaglandin inhibitors (NSAIDs)
- Asthma: LTC4/LTD4-mediated bronchoconstriction → AKBA effective for inflammatory asthma
- IBD (Crohn's, UC): LTB4 colonic inflammation → AKBA demonstrated efficacy

AKBA's 5-LOX inhibition is notably selective — it does not inhibit COX enzymes → no NSAID-type GI ulceration. This selectivity profile makes it potentially safer than NSAIDs for long-term anti-inflammatory use.

### 5.2 Leucocyte Elastase Inhibition

**Boswellic acids**: Inhibit human leucocyte elastase (HLE) → reduce proteolytic cartilage destruction in arthritis. Additional mechanism of joint protection beyond leukotriene suppression.

### 5.3 NF-κB Modulation

**AKBA**: Inhibits IκB kinase (IKK) → blocks NF-κB activation → reduced transcription of pro-inflammatory genes (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, MMP-3).

### 5.4 Anti-Angiogenic / Anti-Tumour

**AKBA**: Inhibits VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) receptor signalling → reduces tumour angiogenesis. Multiple tumour cell lines demonstrate apoptosis induction via AKBA. Ongoing clinical investigation for glioblastoma (brain tumour — AKBA crosses blood-brain barrier).

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Osteoarthritis**: Kimmatkar (2003): AKBA-enriched extract (H15/Sallaki) significantly reduced pain, swelling, and joint flexion vs placebo in 30 OA knee patients. Sengupta (2010, *Phytotherapy Research*): Proprietary AKBA extract (AprèsFlex/Aflapin) significantly superior to ibuprofen for OA knee pain at 90 days.

**Non-inferior to valdecoxib (COX-2 inhibitor) — see I_19**: The cross-reference to the Frankincense entry: *B. serrata* extracts showed non-inferiority to COX-2 inhibitors in the most rigorous RCTs — the landmark Chopra/Etzel data.

**Crohn's disease**: Gerhardt (2001, *Zeitschrift für Gastroenterologie*): H15 (Boswellia extract) vs mesalazine — equivalent reduction in Crohn's Disease Activity Index (CDAI). *B. serrata* extract non-inferior to mesalazine for Crohn's maintenance.

**Ulcerative colitis**: Gupta (1997): H15 significantly superior to sulfasalazine for UC remission in a comparative RCT.

**Asthma**: Gupta (1998, *European Journal of Medical Research*): H15 (300mg 3× daily × 6 weeks) significantly reduced asthma frequency and improved PEF vs placebo.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**5-LOX vs COX**: The 5-lipoxygenase pathway was identified decades after the COX pathway — NSAIDs (aspirin, ibuprofen) inhibit COX; this was the dominant anti-inflammatory discovery of the 20th century. But 5-LOX-derived leukotrienes mediate the inflammatory load in RA, asthma, and IBD independently of COX. Traditional medicine — specifically Ayurveda with *śallakī* — had identified the clinically correct target (joint inflammation, asthma, gut inflammation) before the COX/LOX distinction existed. When modern pharmacology characterised 5-LOX, Boswellia's traditional applications mapped precisely onto the newly discovered pathway. This convergence of 3,000-year-old empirical pharmacology with 20th-century enzyme pharmacology is the paradigm case for how traditional medicine preserves accurate target identification before mechanism understanding.

**Aflapin vs standard extract**: The industry evolution of Boswellia formulation illustrates pharmaceutical optimisation of a traditional preparation. Standard *B. serrata* extract (20% boswellic acids) was replaced by AKBA-enriched extracts (H15, 20% AKBA) and then by Aflapin/AprèsFlex (non-esterified boswellic acid matrix with 20% AKBA but superior bioavailability through oil matrix delivery). Each commercial generation demonstrated superiority to the previous in clinical trials — a rare example of iterative pharmacological improvement of a traditional formulation.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Standardised extract (20% AKBA) — Aflapin/AprèsFlex | 100mg/day | OA; RA; IBD — highest clinical evidence |
| H15 extract (65% boswellic acids, 5% AKBA) | 300–400mg 3× daily | OA; RA; asthma; IBD |
| Crude resin/churna | 1–3g 2× daily | Traditional Ayurvedic; śallakī guggul |
| Topical (20% in base) | Applied 2–3× daily | Local joint inflammation; wound healing |

**Bioavailability note**: Boswellic acids are poorly absorbed without fat. Always take with a fatty meal or milk. Aflapin's non-esterified matrix addresses this — 52% better bioavailability than standard extract.

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Acrid, bitter; warm. Channels: Heart (心), Liver (肝), Spleen (脾). Activates Blood, disperses stasis, relieves pain.

**Ayurvedic**: Bitter, astringent; slightly warming. Reduces Kapha ↓↓, reduces Vata ↓. *Śothahara* (anti-inflammatory), *Sandhi-Śūlahara* (anti-arthritic), *Rasāyana* (minor).

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Pregnancy**: Emmenagogue in resin form — avoid therapeutic doses
- **Anticoagulants**: Anti-platelet activity — monitor with warfarin
- **GI acid conditions**: Raw resin irritant at high doses — standardised extract preferred
- **Drug transport (OATP)**: Boswellic acids inhibit OATP1B3 transporter — potential interaction with statins, certain cancer drugs; monitor
- Generally very well tolerated; superior GI safety profile to NSAIDs
