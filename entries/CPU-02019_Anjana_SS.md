# Anjana — Anjana (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation, 1907)

## *Anjana, a drug that heads a group in Sutrasthana ch. 38. Identification: unresolved. In Sushruta the word is also the general name of a collyrium (eye-salve), and the translator elsewhere glosses it as "black antimony"; no plant identification is printed in the sources read, so the suggestion Hardwickia binata is not supported here; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** One reading in the translation is a mineral eye-salve ("black antimony"). This entry is a historical record only. Nobody should apply or take any of these substances.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk. This is a single-text entry.** Spellings in quotations are normalised silently from the OCR where the damage is plain (for example Nandi for the printed Xandi); the line numbers cited give the text as printed. The word "Anjana" is extremely frequent in the translation, because it is the general term for the collyrium treatment of the eyes (Anjana, as in "Nasya, Anjana, fumigation ..."). Only the occurrences that name it as a drug in a list or give a gloss are used here.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The group locus is Sutrasthana ch. 38, the Anjanadi group (`Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15641 to 15644; chapter file `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`): "The group known as the Anjanadi Gana consists of Anjana, Rasanjana, Nagapushpa, Priyangu, Nilotpala, Nalada, Nalina, Keshara and Madhuka." The metrical text reads: "An attack of haemoptysis readily yields to the curative virtue of the group under discussion. It is anti-toxic in its character and allays the internal burning sensation of the body."

Two further passages name the drug beside others. In Sutra ch. 25 a sutured ulcer "should be covered over with cotton and dusted over with a pulverised compound consisting of the powders of Priyangu, Anjanam, Yasthyahva and Rodhra" (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch25.txt` line 162; Vol. I line 12057). The pairing of Priyangu and Anjana is the same as in the group list. In Uttara ch. 11 an eye compound is "Hingu (Asafoetida), Triphala, Yashti-madhu, Saindhava, Prapaundaika, Anjana (black Antimony), Tuttha (Sulphate of copper), and copper pasted together" (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch11.txt` line 41); the parentheses are the translator's. A translator's footnote in Uttara ch. 10 renders a couplet as "Anjana (black-antimony) rubbed over a stone-slab with clarified butter and breast-milk" (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch10.txt` line 5), and a footnote in Uttara ch. 11 says a word "generally means Srotanjana (black antimony)" (line 139).

**Identification.** The row's note gives none, and the botanical Appendix 3 of Vol. III has no line for Anjana. The translation therefore offers two readings: a plant or other drug in the Anjanadi group, and the mineral black antimony in the eye chapters. The group also contains Rasanjana, a separate name. No plant binomial was found. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent for this drug. If the reading "black antimony" holds for the eye compounds, the material is an antimony compound, and antimony compounds are toxic. This is a general fact and not a finding about the group drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Anjanadi group: haemoptysis, poison, and internal burning (Sutra ch. 38). As a dusting powder with Priyangu, Yasthyahva and Rodhra on a sutured ulcer (Sutra ch. 25).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no preparation for the group drug.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this drug.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparation given is the powder for dusting a sutured ulcer (Sutra ch. 25 line 162). Chapter 38 closes with a rule for all the groups: "Only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group, or a similar number of drugs chosen from the different groups, or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety, or in combination with another, should be used according to the indications of any particular case, as the physician, in his discretion, would determine" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, near the end; Vol. I, ch. XXXVIII). It also says that the groups "will be more elaborately dealt with later on in the chapter on Therapeutics". This is a rule of method for the groups, not a dose for this drug. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Antimony compounds are toxic and must not be put in the eyes or swallowed. The identity of the group drug is unresolved, so no modern correlation is attempted. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Anjana. The qualities given are those of the whole group (Sutra ch. 38, as quoted above): curative in haemoptysis, anti-toxic, and allaying internal burning.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is attested in the Sushruta Samhita, in the therapeutic groups of Sutrasthana ch. 38 and the other passages cited above, and its role there is therapeutic: groups are said to subdue Kapha, Vata or Pitta, to act as vermifuge or aseptic agents, and so on. Chikitsa ch. 26 (the vajikarana chapter, "increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons") and chs. 27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) were searched under the spellings printed here (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` to `ch30.txt`, and ch. 31) and the name was not found in them. The drug therefore carries no rasayana or vajikarana status in the sources retrieved, and nothing is said of ojas for it. The register is (b): framed by the therapeutic gana, with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** An unresolved drug whose name also means the eye-salve. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use. A cultivator should not use it.

### Contraindications

- **Eyes.** Do not apply any collyrium material from this entry to the eyes.
- **Possible mineral toxicity.** If the reading is black antimony, the material is toxic. Avoid.
- **Haemoptysis.** Coughing blood is a medical emergency; seek care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
