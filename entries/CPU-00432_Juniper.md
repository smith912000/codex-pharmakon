**Egyptian — Ebers Papyrus (EP).** Juniper is named at two places in Bryan 1930 (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). A search for "Juniper" returned only these two lines.

- **Line 1299, Bryan's alphabetical list of recognisable plants (Ch. VI).** The list prints "Herbs-of-the-Field Juniper-berries". The extraction row is `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, row 68 ("Juniper-berries", Bryan 1930 Ch. VI line 1299, "Bryan's English identification"). This is a list of English names and does not say which prescriptions use the berry.
- **Lines 1455 to 1475, the remedy attributed to Isis.** The heading reads "ANOTHER REMEDY WHICH THE GODDESS ISIS PREPARED FOR THE GOD RA TO DRIVE OUT THE PAINS THAT ARE IN HIS HEAD". The ingredients are printed one to a line: "Berry-of-the-Coriander", "Berry-of-the-Poppy-plant", "Wormwood", "Berry-of-the-sames-plant", "Berry-of-the-Juniper-plant" (line 1469) and "Honey". The instruction is "Make into one, mix with Honey, and smear therewith in order to make him well forthwith", and the closing words are "When this remedy is used by him against all illnesses in the head and all sufferings and evils of any sort, he will instantly become well." The deities named in this passage (Isis, Ra) are named by the papyrus text as Bryan translates it. The remedy is not a juniper remedy as such: the berry is one of five plant ingredients and the berry is not singled out. Bryan's own comment (lines 1484 to 1486) credits the cure to the poppy berry as a source of opium, and that is Bryan's 1930 reading and not a text of the papyrus.

A sentence of Bryan's, at about line 1489, bears on the frame of this remedy: "Nowhere, it is well to point out, is the Magic Formula advocated as a cure per se, but it is an essential part of the treatment and without it the remedies might well be useless". That is Bryan's characterisation of the papyrus and not a locus for juniper. No spell is printed beside the Isis remedy in the lines read.

### Active Components

Juniper berries (the fleshy cones) are generally known to contain a volatile oil rich in terpenes, along with sugars and resin. The species on this row is not fixed, and the composition of the berry in the papyrus was not read. [USER: verify]

Historical record only. The two lines read give "Juniper-berries" in a list of plants and the berry as one of five ingredients in a headache remedy attributed in the text to Isis for Ra. The papyrus does not single the berry out. The Codex gives no protocol for it.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian — Ebers Papyrus (EP).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The berry stands in one remedy that the papyrus ties to Isis and Ra, and the frame it sits inside is the Ebers one of a drug used together with divine authority and a spoken formula, the *heka* of the spec, where spell and drug form one intervention. That frame is Bryan's general comment on the papyrus, and no spell is printed with this remedy in the lines read. The text assigns the berry no status in the sense of a cultivation grade, and it does not say what the berry does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. The Ebers material is indication-organised (this remedy is against pains in the head), and the identity of the plant is a modern hypothesis resting on a list row. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with juniper. In the papyrus it is a berry in a mixed head remedy, and the honest answer is to do nothing with it on the strength of this entry.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Juniper berries are generally regarded as unsuitable in pregnancy and for people with kidney disease, and species of juniper differ in what they contain, so a berry should not be gathered or eaten from an unidentified shrub. This is general caution and is not found in the sources read. The remedy above is historical and is not advice. Seek ordinary medical care for severe or persistent head pain.
