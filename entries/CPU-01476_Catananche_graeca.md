### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** This plant is named only by Dioscorides (DMM). It was added 2026-10-09 from the extraction list (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) at the locus 4-134, line 9213.

**Identity note.** The binomial is the translators' (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) suggestion and not Dioscorides' identification. The SUGGESTED lines (9214 and 9215) read: "Catananche graeca, Hymenoema graecum, Hymenoema tournefortii — Candy Lionsfoot", then "Catananche caerulea — Cupid's Dart, Blue Cupidone, Blue Succory". Dioscorides' chapter describes two kinds ("one sort" and "the other") and the translators give a single gloss for both. Whether both kinds belong to one species is not settled by the chapter. **Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is in Book Four ("Other Herbs & Roots"), directly before 4-135 TRIPOLION.

*4-134 KATANANKE* (heading at line 9213; text at line 9216). The whole chapter reads: "Catanance has one sort with long leaves like those of coronopis, a thin root, and six or seven rush-like heads in which is seed similar to ervum [2-129, 2-131]. Withered, it bends down to the ground and is similar to the claws of a dead kite [bird]. The other is the size of a little apple; the root is small, about the size of an olive, and the leaves similar in shape and colour to the olive, but soft, scattered on the earth, and jagged. The small seed (like cicer [2-126]) is found on the little stems, bored through in many parts, a Phoenician colour [red]. Some report that both kinds are good for liquid love medicines, and they say that the Thessalian women use them. It is also called damnamene, dionysias, thyrsion, demos, cemos, or crotion; the Magi call it archaras, some, arcopus, the Romans, herba filicula, some, datisca, or Iovis madius, and the Dacians, caropithla."

The chapter gives two descriptions and one use. The first kind has long leaves like *coronopis*, a thin root and six or seven rush-like heads with seed like *ervum*, and when withered it bends to the ground "similar to the claws of a dead kite". The second is "the size of a little apple", with an olive-sized root, olive-like but soft, jagged leaves scattered on the earth, and small red seed like *cicer* on little stems "bored through in many parts". The use is the report that "both kinds are good for liquid love medicines", used by "the Thessalian women". There is no habitat and no medicinal use for illness. The chapter then lists six other Greek names, a Magi name (*archaras*), and Roman, Dacian and other names.

The name "Cupid's Dart" is in the translators' gloss only. The text itself says nothing about Cupid.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides records no remedy. He records a report ("some report") that the plant is used for "liquid love medicines" by Thessalian women. This is a love-charm use reported at second hand, and the Codex records it and does not endorse it. It gives no protocol, and the chapter does not say what a "liquid love medicine" contains or how it is taken. Dosing a person with a love medicine without their knowledge would be harmful.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system. The chapter is entirely a discipline of attention in the spec's sense: it describes two kinds by leaf, root, head and seed, and by how the plant looks when withered. It records the love-medicine use as hearsay, which fits the spec's note that he sometimes records amuletic uses while distancing himself, and recording is not endorsing. It assigns no status as a long-term substance. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice. The only use is a reported love-charm, and no illness is treated. The abstentive answer is the correct one.

### Contraindications

None is printed in the chapter. The plant is not securely identified, and the only recorded use is as an ingredient in a "love medicine", which should not be given to anyone without their knowledge and consent. Do not use in pregnancy.
