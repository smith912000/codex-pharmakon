### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda (Suśruta):** Kadara belongs to the Ayurvedic **Khadira / kaṇṭaka-vṛkṣa** complex — the thorny, astringent-barked leguminous trees (Khadira *Acacia catechu*, Ari-mēda, Kadara and allies) that the classical texts group as **kaṣāya, kaphaghna and kuṣṭhaghna** agents for skin disease, oral/dental disorders, wounds, itching and vitiated-blood conditions. In this reading Kadara is a bark-and-gum astringent used in tooth-and-gum preparations (the twig as a datun), in wound-washes and in skin-disease decoctions, sharing the drying, blood-cleansing, ulcer-healing signature of the Khadira group. Botanical fixation of the classical name is imperfect — "Kadara/Ari-mēda" is applied across several thorny *Acacia*/*Vachellia* species — so this entry anchors the name to *A. farnesiana* while noting the group identity.

**Unani, perfumery & folk medicine:** *A. farnesiana* is famous as the **cassie** of perfumery — its intensely fragrant flowers yield "cassie absolute," cultivated since the 17th century (the epithet *farnesiana* commemorates the Farnese gardens in Rome). In Mexican, Indian and wider tropical folk medicine the bark, pods and flowers are used against **dysentery and diarrhoea, skin infections, wounds, sore throat and (traditionally) tuberculosis** — astringent, antimicrobial applications that echo the Ayurvedic Khadira-group reading. The pods and bark are tannin- and gallate-rich.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Thorn and the Perfume:** Kadara embodies a striking polarity: it is a **thorn tree** (kaṇṭaka — defence, boundary, the astringent that closes and protects) that produces one of the most prized **perfumes** (the opening, ascending, refined signal of cassie). The astringent principle is centripetal — it draws tissue together, arrests flux (grāhī), seals the wound and the gut. The fragrance is centrifugal — it radiates. Doctrinally this makes Kadara a *closing-yet-refining* agent: it belongs to the astringent `[RESET]` class that arrests leakage and heals surface, but carries an aromatic grace that lifts it above the merely drying barks. In Kenetica it reads as a surface-sealing, flux-arresting recovery agent with an aromatic uplift.

### Active Components

Bark, pods and heartwood are rich in **condensed tannins and gallotannins** — **gallic acid** and **methyl gallate** are confirmed markers — with flavanones (**prunin**, i.e. naringenin-7-*O*-glucoside), sterols (stigmasterol glucoside), pinitol, long-chain fatty-acid esters and sucrose. The flowers yield the fragrant **cassie** volatiles (methyl salicylate, benzyl alcohol, farnesol-type terpenes). The tannins and gallates carry the astringent/antimicrobial pharmacology.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Kadara sits in `[RESET]` as an **astringent surface-sealing and anti-infective recovery** agent: its traditional use in diarrhoea/dysentery, wounds, skin and oral infection maps onto arresting flux (grāhī), closing tissue and clearing surface microbial load during recovery. In the Kenetica frame it is chiefly a topical/oral astringent (wound-wash, gum/mouth care, gut-astringent decoction) rather than a systemic tonic, with the cassie-flower aromatic as a secondary, refining note.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the modern evidence is **preclinical (in-vitro / phytochemical)** and supports the astringent-antimicrobial tradition.

- Hernández-García et al. (2018), *Journal of Ethnopharmacology*, characterised *A. farnesiana* fruit extracts and isolated **methyl gallate, gallic acid and the flavanone prunin**, showing antitubercular activity against *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (extracts MIC 100–200 µg/mL; methyl gallate 25–50 µg/mL; prunin active against a multidrug-resistant strain at 50 µg/mL) and activity against dysentery bacteria — explicitly justifying the ethnomedical use for dysentery and tuberculosis. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2018.10.031)
- Castillo et al. (2011/2012), *Journal of Food Science*, found *A. farnesiana* among the most effective of 28 plants against *Campylobacter jejuni* and *C. coli* (minimal bactericidal concentration 0.3 mg/mL), significantly reducing bacterial adherence and cytotoxicity in Vero cells — supporting an anti-diarrhoeal/anti-infective role. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1750-3841.2011.02229.x)

**Evidence grade:** preclinical / traditional. The antimicrobial and anti-tubercular signals are congruent with the astringent, kuṣṭhaghna, grāhī classical use, but there is no human clinical trial evidence at traditional dosing.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṣāya (astringent), Tikta (bitter)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** Grāhī-vraṇaropana astringent of the Khadira/kaṇṭaka group; aromatic (cassie) flower
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha–Pitta śāmaka (pacifies Kapha and Pitta)
- **Karma (actions):** Grāhī/stambhana (astringent, anti-diarrhoeal), vraṇaropana-śodhana (wound cleansing/healing), kuṣṭhaghna (anti-dermatosis), dantya (dental/gum care), krimighna/antibacterial (traditional), raktaprasādana (blood-cleansing, group action)

### Contraindications

High tannin content: constipation and gastric irritation on prolonged/high internal use; avoid in constipation-predominant Vāta states. Pregnancy/lactation: internal use best avoided without qualified guidance. Cassie absolute/essential material is for external aromatic use only, not ingestion; patch-test for skin sensitivity. Botanical-identity caution: "Kadara/Ari-mēda" covers several thorny *Acacia*/*Vachellia* species — confirm the species before sourcing.
