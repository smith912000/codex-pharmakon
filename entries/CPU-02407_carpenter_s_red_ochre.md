# Carpenter's red ochre — Tektonike (Dioscorides 5-112), printed in the translation as "Tectonic"

## *A red ochre that Dioscorides ranks below the Sinopic ochre of the preceding chapter, best when Egyptian or Carthaginian, brittle and free of stones, also found in Spain; the text adds that ochre, when burnt, turns into red ochre. The translator's gloss is "Red Ochre". Identification: red ochre, an iron-oxide earth (the translator's gloss, with the general sense of red ochre as hematite-bearing earth); confidence medium. The English "carpenter's" in the Codex name is not in the text and rests on a reading of the Greek word that the sources on disk do not give [USER: verify]* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Tektonike is named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk. The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-112, *Tektonike* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading line 10409, text line 10411, book page 800 of this translation; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`; list row `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 838, "Tektonike", mineral). The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "carpenter's red ochre (tektonike)", and this entry replaces it.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The whole chapter, in the translator's English, reads: "Tectonic [red ochre] is inferior in every respect to the Sinopic. The Egyptian and Carthaginian are the best — without stones and brittle. It is also found in Spain towards the West. Ochre is burnt and turns into red ochre." The translator's heading gloss is "SUGGESTED: Red Ochre". The chapter gives a ranking (inferior in every respect to the Sinopic), the places of the best (Egyptian and Carthaginian), the marks of the best (without stones, and brittle), a further source (Spain, "towards the West"), and one statement about making it (burnt ochre turns into red ochre). It gives no use at all. There is no indication, no action and no dose in 5-112.

**The chapters around it.** The Sinopic of the comparison is chapter 5-111, *Miltos Sinopike* ("Red Ochre from Sinope", line 10406 onward): "The best rubrica Sinopica is thick and heavy, with a bright colour, without stones, and all one colour, spreading out much when dissolved. It is gathered in Cappadocia from certain hollows. It is purified, carried to the city Sinope (from which it has its name) and sold. It is drying and adhesive; as a result it is mixed with wound plasters and drying astringent tablets. Taken in an egg or as a suppository it stops the intestines. It is also given for liver disorders." The text of 5-112 refers back to this, and the uses in 5-111 are that chapter's and are not transferred to Tektonike. The statement that "ochre is burnt and turns into red ochre" refers to chapter 5-108, *Ochra* (yellow ochre: "Ochre must be chosen which is light and yellow, with a rich colour, unstony, and brittle. That from Athens is like this. We must burn and wash it like cadmia [5-84]. It is an astringent for putrefying sores, dissolves inflammation and small swellings, represses abnormal growths of flesh, fills up sores and wounds (used with wax ointments), and diminishes calluses."), which the Codex files at II_37. Dioscorides also has Lemnian earth in the next chapter (5-113). The Codex's red ochre entry at II_137 is filed from the Ebers material and is not a Dioscorides attestation.

**The name.** The Greek heading *Tektonike* is printed unglossed in the sources on disk, and the English text prints "Tectonic". The Codex name "carpenter's" is a rendering of the Greek stem and no text on disk says why the red ochre was so called. [USER: verify]

A search of the other texts on disk for *tektonike*, "tectonic" and "carpenter" found nothing further.

### Active Components

The chapter names no constituent. In general terms, red ochre is an earth coloured by iron oxide (haematite), and ochres in general are clays or earths with iron oxides or hydroxides; yellow ochre turns red when heated, which fits the chapter's remark that burnt ochre becomes red ochre. The composition of Dioscorides' *tektonike* cannot be fixed from the text. [USER: verify with a mineralogical source before adding any formula.]

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the chapter is that "ochre is burnt and turns into red ochre". 5-108 says ochre is burnt and washed "like cadmia [5-84]" for its own uses, and that instruction belongs to the ochre chapter. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Iron oxides give ochres their red and yellow colours, and heating yellow (hydrated) iron oxide drives off water and leaves red iron oxide. Natural ochres can contain other minerals and traces of heavy metals. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and this chapter has none. What it gives is a discipline of attention in its barest form: a ranking against a better ochre, the places of the best (Egypt and Carthage), and the marks of a good piece (no stones, brittle). No cultivation status is attested for this ochre, and none is given any use.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing for a cultivator to take. The chapter is a trade note on how to judge a red earth, and no practice follows from it.

### Contraindications

- **Do not eat or apply it.** The chapter gives no use, and natural earths can carry heavy metals and grit.
