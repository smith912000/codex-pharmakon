### Origin & Tradition

*Dalbergia odorifera* — Hainan fragrant rosewood, source of the medicinal wood **Jiàng Xiāng** ("descending fragrance") and of the coveted *huanghuali* cabinet timber — is a leguminous tree of Hainan and southern China whose fragrant, resin-rich **heartwood** is the medicine. Like agarwood (I_307), its value lies in the dark, aromatic, slowly-formed heartwood rather than the pale sapwood, and like agarwood its name encodes a *descending* action; but where agarwood governs Qi, Jiàng Xiāng governs **Blood**.

**Chinese Tradition (Jiàng Xiāng):**
Jiàng Xiāng is a warm, aromatic, pungent Blood-and-Qi mover. Its classical actions form a tight, distinctive cluster: it **invigorates the Blood and dispels stasis**, it **moves Qi and relieves pain**, and — the signature paradox — it **stops bleeding** precisely *by* resolving the stasis that causes it (for traumatic bleeding and stasis-type haemorrhage, internally and applied as a powder to wounds). It is a herb of trauma and of the chest: used for stasis-and-Qi-stagnation chest and epigastric pain (a component of modern Chinese cardiovascular "chest-bi"/angina formulas), for traumatic injury with swelling and pain, and for vomiting and abdominal pain from internal cold.

**Tibetan / Himalayan use (FMT):**
Fragrant *Dalbergia* heartwoods are used among the aromatic Blood-moving and pain-relieving woods of the broader Himalayan-Chinese materia medica.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the descending fragrance that moves the Blood:**
Jiàng Xiāng's doctrine is the union of *aroma* and *Blood* — a fragrant wood whose penetrating, descending scent is read as the vehicle that moves the congealed Blood and disperses the stasis-pain. Its most elegant teaching is the bleeding-stasis paradox: in the traditional physiology, much pathological bleeding arises because Blood, blocked by stasis, is forced out of the vessels; resolve the stasis and the Blood returns to its proper course and the bleeding stops. Jiàng Xiāng thus *stops bleeding by moving Blood* — the opposite of a simple astringent styptic like catechu (I_310), and a striking example of the tradition's dynamic, flow-based understanding of haemostasis. The Codex files it to RESET as the aromatic Blood-mover of trauma and the stagnant chest.

### Active Components

- **Flavonoids (isoflavones, flavanones, neoflavonoids, isoflavonoids) and the arylbenzofurans** — the principal bioactive class; *D. odorifera* is chemically rich (~175 identified metabolites), and its flavonoids are considered essential to its pharmacology, with documented anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, anti-platelet/anti-thrombotic, vasorelaxant and anti-angina activities (Zhao et al., 2019).
- **Volatile/essential oil (sesquiterpenes: nerolidol, trans-β-farnesene, and related)** — the fragrant fraction carrying the aromatic Qi-moving and analgesic activity.
- **Phenols and quinones (including dalbergiones)** — supporting antioxidant/anti-inflammatory constituents; note the dalbergiones are also recognised contact allergens (see cautions).

### Scientific Correlation

*Attribution: findings below are from a review retrieved from PubMed; DOI linked.*

A comprehensive review (Zhao et al., 2019, *Journal of Ethnopharmacology*) documents that *D. odorifera* — used in Chinese medicine to "promote blood circulation, relieve pain, and eliminate blood stasis," and applied to cardio-cerebrovascular disease — contains ~175 identified metabolites (flavonoids, phenols, arylbenzofurans, quinones, plus a well-studied volatile oil), with its flavonoids and volatile oil considered essential to activity; modern pharmacology confirms **anti-inflammatory, anti-angina, antioxidant** and related actions and indicates therapeutic potential in cardiovascular and coronary heart disease, while the authors note that mechanisms are not yet fully explained and quality-control data are limited ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2019.112328)).

*Evidence-grade note: the pharmacology (anti-platelet, vasorelaxant, anti-inflammatory) is preclinical/mechanistic and consistent with the traditional Blood-moving use; robust human RCTs of *D. odorifera* as a single agent are limited (it is used in compound cardiovascular formulas). Cardiovascular claims are presented as supervised-adjunct potential, not established monotherapy.*

**Primary Tier:** ◉ Blood — pungent, warm, aromatic; invigorates Blood, dispels stasis, and stops (stasis-type) bleeding.
**Secondary Tier:** ◉ Qi — moves Qi, relieves pain, descends.
**Organ-Channel Map (TCM):** Liver (Blood — invigorates/dispels stasis, stops bleeding), Spleen (Qi, cold-abdominal pain), Heart (chest-bi).
**TCM actions:** Huó Xuè Sàn Yū (invigorate Blood, dispel stasis); Zhǐ Xuè Dìng Tòng (stop bleeding, relieve pain); Xíng Qì (move Qi).
**Dantian / subtle-body affiliation:** Middle Dantian / chest and the Blood pathways — a descending, aromatic Blood-mover of the trunk.

### Contraindications

- **Bleeding without stasis; Yin-deficiency bleeding:** the "stop bleeding by moving Blood" action applies to *stasis-type* bleeding — it is inappropriate (and potentially worsening) for heat- or deficiency-type haemorrhage, where it may increase bleeding.
- **Pregnancy:** contraindicated — a strong Blood-invigorating/stasis-dispelling herb.
- **Anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy:** the documented anti-platelet/anti-thrombotic activity is additive with warfarin, DOACs, and antiplatelet drugs — bleeding-risk caution; discontinue before surgery.
- **Contact allergy:** *Dalbergia* heartwoods (dalbergiones) are well-recognised woodworking contact allergens/sensitisers; dust and topical exposure can sensitise.
- **Conservation:** *D. odorifera* is a heavily-exploited, protected rosewood; sourcing raises genuine sustainability/legality concerns.
- **Evidence honesty:** cardiovascular efficacy is preclinical/adjunctive, not established single-agent therapy.
