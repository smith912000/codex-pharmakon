# Fluorite (Zi Shi Ying) — Zi Shi (紫石), printed "Zi Shi (Flouritum)" in Yang 1998
## *Fluorite in the Divine Farmer's Classic; Yang 1998 prints "Flouritum" (an apparent misspelling of Fluoritum, fluorite, calcium fluoride) for the entry Zi Shi; the spine's name "Zi Shi Ying" is not the name printed in Yang* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Zi Shi is printed in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) on PDF page 25, under the heading "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (heading at line 985, the top of that page; the same heading is printed again at line 1060 on the page after next). The text (lines 998-1003): "Zi Shi (Flouritum) is sweet and balanced. It mainly treats the heart and abdomen, cough and counterflow, and evil qi. It supplements insufficiency and [hence treats] women with 10 year old infertility due to cold wind in the child's palace [i.e., uterus]. Protracted taking may make the center warm, the body light, and prolong life. It is produced in the valleys of Mount Tai." Yang's footnote 44 (lines 1017-1018, the OCR is damaged: "This refers to thl' Iuollntains in what is now Shandong Province") places Mount Tai in present-day Shandong. The words "[hence treats]" and "[i.e., uterus]" are the translator's brackets.

The name needs a plain statement. The monograph prints the name as "Zi Shi" only. The list `_sources/shennong.csv` carries the row as 紫石 (line 13) and `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` carries "Zi Shi" with Yang's identification "Flouritum" (line 13 of that file, locus "line 998"). Neither source prints "Zi Shi Ying" for this entry. The spine name "Zi Shi Ying" is therefore the spine's own and not Yang's. Whether "Zi Shi Ying" is a later or fuller name of the same stone is not stated in the sources retrieved. The spine's note for this row is "high". The identification "Flouritum" is the translator's and no footnote doubts it.

### Active Components

Fluorite is calcium fluoride, CaF2, a mineral that occurs in cubic crystals and in many colours, among them purple, green and colourless, with the colour commonly due to trace impurities and crystal defects. It is only sparingly soluble in water. It is the main mineral source of fluorine in industry. The sources read give no composition of the stone Yang translates.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records the stone for complaints of the heart and abdomen, cough, "evil qi", and a woman's long infertility attributed to cold wind in the uterus, and says that long taking may warm the centre, lighten the body and prolong life. The Codex does not endorse these claims and offers no protocol. A ground fluoride mineral is not a safe thing to swallow: fluoride compounds are toxic in excess, and nothing in the sources read supports taking this stone.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is printed in the monograph and no dose is given in the sources retrieved. The text says only "Protracted taking" (line 1001). [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Fluoride is a well-known ion in dental health at very low intake, and excess fluoride over long periods causes fluorosis of teeth and bone; those are facts about fluoride ions in general and not about swallowing the mineral. Fluorite itself is used industrially as a flux in metal smelting and as a source of hydrofluoric acid and other fluorine chemicals. No medical benefit of swallowing fluorite for the conditions the Classic names is established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- SBJ: "sweet and balanced" (Yang line 998). Class: Superior (heading lines 985 and 1060). "Balanced" is Yang's rendering for neither cold nor hot, neither warm nor cool (his footnote 32, lines 849-855, to the first entry of the Jades and Stones section). No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: an upper-grade drug (上品).* Zi Shi stands under "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (Yang lines 985, 1060). Yang's preface says the superior class medicinals "mainly nourish life and correspond to heaven. They are nontoxic and taking them in large amounts and for a long time will not harm people. If one intends to make one's body light, boost the qi, prevent aging, and prolong life, one should base [one's efforts] on the superior class" (lines 352-358). The monograph itself prints a cultivation line: "Protracted taking may make the center warm, the body light, and prolong life" (lines 1001-1002). The printed grade is a statement of the Classic as translated by Yang in 1998 and is not carried forward from later materia medica works. The page that carries the entry (page 25) prints the Superior heading at its top. The Classic's own "nontoxic" claim for the whole class is a claim of the text and not a fact the Codex accepts. Yang's foreword (lines 312-328) warns that some who took such "external elixirs compounded out of potentially toxic materials" made themselves ill.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: the Classic held it safe for long use. Fluorite is a fluoride mineral and the Classic's claims have no established support, so nothing here supports taking it. For a cultivator the entry is a record of how the Han-period elixir tradition ranked a purple stone among the "nourish life" drugs. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Fluoride compounds are toxic in excess, and a powdered stone sold as a remedy may be a different mineral or may be contaminated. Mineral medicines are unstandardised and their sale is regulated in many places. Children are especially exposed to the effects of excess fluoride on developing teeth.
