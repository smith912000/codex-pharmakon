# Behen root — bahman (بهمن), white and red

## *Behen, the dried root known in the Arabic drug trade as* bahman *in a white and a red kind; the botanical identification is the modern one and is of low confidence, because Avicenna's entry describes only the dried pieces, and the trade names for white and red behen have covered more than one plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

Behen is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry, and the text is four lines. The English name "behen" is the Latinised form of the Arabic *bahman*. The botanical identity is not established here: in the later drug trade the white and red behens are attached to several plants, and I did not open a source that fixes the *Canon*'s drug to one. [USER: verify]

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The *Canon* (Book II, entry *bahman*, Arabic text line 10904) describes the drug as "pieces of wood, which are dried roots, shrivelled and wrinkled, and it is of two kinds, white and red" (قطع خشبية هي أصول مجففة متشجنة متغضنة وهو نوعان أبيض وأحمر). The temperament is "hot and dry in the second." Under the headings that follow it says: for cosmetics, "it fattens" (*musammin*); for the chest, "it strengthens the heart greatly and benefits palpitation"; and for the excretory organs, "it increases the semen, a clear increase" (يزيد في المني زيادة بينة, lines 10906-10908). That is all.

### Active Components

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The *Canon* gives three actions: it is fattening; it strengthens the heart and helps palpitation; and it increases semen. These are the drug's whole record in the text. It is not a recommended modern practice, since the drug cannot be identified from the entry and no safety information is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(b) Framed.* The *Canon* ascribes to this drug a strengthening of the heart and an increase of the semen, both in the Canon's own organ-by-organ terms. These are claims about the faculties, but the text does not call the drug a tonic of the innate heat or radical moisture, and it gives no cultivation status. The entry does not use the word *mufarriḥ* (gladdening); that belongs to a different book and is not applied here.

**Practitioner's note.** The record is short and offers a cultivator no practice. If anything, it records a drug that the Unani trade valued for the heart and for generative strength, and says nothing of how it was taken. The honest course is to leave it as a name and four lines of Arabic until more of the tradition's sources are read.

### Contraindications

- **Identification.** The drug cannot be fixed from the entry, so no safety statement about a specific plant can be made. Do not take unidentified roots.
- **Heat.** The drug is hot and dry in the second degree in the text's terms; the text gives no caution, and none is invented here.
- **Generative claims.** Historical record only.
