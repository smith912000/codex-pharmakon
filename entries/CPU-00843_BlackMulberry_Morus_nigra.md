# Black Mulberry — Shahtoot / Toot-e-Siah / Sang Shen (relative) / Tuti / Morea

## *Morus nigra* L. | Family: Moraceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, DMM, CM, SBJ, SS
**Tags (previous line, retained for reference):** AT, DMM, SBJ, SS, +Avicenna (Unani/Canon)
**Energetic Classification:** Cooling and moist when ripe; astringent-sour when unripe (Galenic/Unani)
**Training Phase:** Cooling & Soothing (mucosal-astringent, blood-cooling)

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **Identity note — read first.** This entry is the **fruit** of *Morus nigra*, the deep purple-black mulberry, and must be kept distinct from **White Mulberry, *Morus alba*** (already in the codex). *M. alba*'s medicinal identity is its **leaf**, carrying the iminosugar **1-deoxynojirimycin (DNJ)** and an antidiabetic, α-glucosidase-inhibiting reputation. *M. nigra*'s classical identity is entirely different: it is the **anthocyanin-rich ripe fruit** used as a **cooling throat and mouth remedy**. Antidiabetic data belongs chiefly to *M. alba* leaf; do not import it here.

**Avicenna / Unani (Toot / Shahtoot).** The Unani tradition is the fruit's doctrinal centre, and it draws a careful *ripeness axis*. The **ripe black fruit** is graded **cold and moist**, a blood-cooler and gentle laxative, prized above all as a **soothing agent for the hot, inflamed throat and mouth** — the basis of the thickened ripe-fruit syrup. The **unripe fruit** is **cold and dry, sharply astringent**, used to *tighten* and treat lax, ulcerated or bleeding gums and relaxed tonsils. The single plant thus supplies both a soothing demulcent and an astringent, selected by ripeness.

**Dioscorides (*De Materia Medica*, morea/sykaminos).** The Greek herbal describes the mulberry and specifically the boiled-down juice of the fruit — the preparation later Latinised as **diamoron** — as a remedy for sore and ulcerated mouths and throats, a gargle for inflamed tonsils and aphthous ulcers. This *diamoron/Shahtoot* gargle is a genuine cross-tradition through-line from Greek to Unani practice.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (Tuta).** Mulberry fruit enters the Indian tradition as sweet-sour, cooling, refrigerant and mildly laxative, used for thirst, burning, and throat/mouth soreness — again the cooling-soothing profile.

**Shennong / Chinese materia medica (Sang Shen, 桑椹).** Chinese medicine uses **sang shen**, mulberry fruit-spike — classically that of *M. alba*, its close relative — as **sweet, cold**, entering Heart, Liver and Kidney, to "nourish yin and blood," moisten dryness, and treat thirst and constipation. *M. nigra* fruit is the doctrinal analogue: a cooling, yin-nourishing, blood-enriching dark fruit. (The species attribution differs; the energetic reading converges.)

**Assyrian.** Mulberry is plausibly present in the Mesopotamian record, but an exact Akkadian binomial for *M. nigra* is **not reliably attested** [USER: verify against a primary cuneiform source before asserting a native name].

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** The black mulberry's doctrine is written in its **stain**. The ripe fruit's near-black juice — the pigment that dyes fingers and cloth — is the visible sign the traditions read as "cooling, blood-nourishing, throat-soothing." Modern chemistry identifies that stain as **cyanidin-based anthocyanins** (principally cyanidin-3-*O*-glucoside), and the "cooling/soothing the hot throat" doctrine maps directly onto their measured **antioxidant and anti-inflammatory** activity: the pigment that *looks* like cooled blood is an actual radical-scavenger and cytokine-suppressor applied to inflamed mucosa.

The **ripeness axis** of the doctrine is its most impressive feature, because it is a *dose-and-preparation* distinction the traditions drew centuries before phytochemistry could explain it. The unripe fruit's "cold-dry astringency" is its higher unripe-stage acid and **tannin** load — genuinely tightening, genuinely suited to the lax, bleeding gum the Unani texts direct it toward. The ripe fruit's "cold-moist soothing" is the sugar-rich, anthocyanin-loaded **demulcent** syrup — a coating, cooling, anti-inflammatory application. One species, two opposite clinical actions, correctly sorted by ripeness: this is the doctrine functioning as an empirical dosing system, not mere symbolism.

The *diamoron/Shahtoot* gargle is thus a topical anthocyanin-and-tannin application to inflamed oropharyngeal tissue — a doctrine that reads, in modern terms, as reasonable local anti-inflammatory and astringent care, even if the systemic claims remain modest. It is worth marking the *symbolic over-reach* too: the "nourishes the blood" language (shared with the Chinese *sang shen* reading) is signature-thinking on the fruit's blood-red juice, and there is no evidence black mulberry meaningfully treats anaemia. The pigment resembles blood; it does not replenish it. The codex keeps the local throat/mouth indication and flags the systemic blood-tonic claim as unproven association.

### Active Components

- **Anthocyanins** — cyanidin-3-*O*-glucoside (dominant) and cyanidin-3-*O*-rutinoside; the pigment and principal bioactive fraction of the ripe fruit.
- **Phenolic acids** — chlorogenic, cryptochlorogenic and protocatechuic acids.
- **Flavonoids** — total flavonoid fraction with measured radical-scavenging activity.
- **Fruit acids & sugars** — the sour-sweet, demulcent syrup base.
- **Tannins** — higher in unripe fruit; basis of the astringent action.
- *Note:* **DNJ / strong α-glucosidase inhibition is a property of *M. alba* leaf, not *M. nigra* fruit** — see contra-indication on overclaiming.

A word on the **cross-tradition convergence**, which is unusually tight for this fruit. The Greek *diamoron* (boiled-down mulberry juice for ulcerated mouths), the Unani *Shahtoot* throat syrup, the Ayurvedic refrigerant for burning and thirst, and the Chinese cooling *sang shen* all independently arrive at the same three notes: **cooling, soothing, and directed at the mouth/throat or heat-conditions**. When four lineages that borrowed unevenly from one another still land on the same organ (the inflamed oropharynx) and the same quality (cooling demulcence), the convergence is itself a form of evidence — repeated empirical observation of the ripe fruit's local soothing action, later underwritten by the anthocyanin anti-inflammatory data. It is precisely the systemic, harder-to-observe claims (blood-nourishing, metabolic) that diverge and weaken across traditions, which is the pattern one expects when a real local effect is genuine and the extrapolated systemic ones are not.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Deployed as a **cooling mucosal-soothing agent**: the ripe-fruit syrup ("Shahtoot"/diamoron) as a throat and mouth **gargle/demulcent** for sore throat, tonsillar inflammation and aphthous ulcers, and as a mild blood-cooling, gently laxative food. The unripe-fruit preparation is reserved as an **astringent** for lax, bleeding gums. Its logic is topical and dietetic — local anti-inflammatory/astringent action plus general antioxidant intake — not a systemic metabolic therapy.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- **The pigment and its anti-inflammatory action.** Chen et al. quantified the flavonoids of *Morus nigra* fruit and identified two anthocyanins — **cyanidin-3-*O*-glucoside (8.3 mg/g)** and **cyanidin-3-*O*-rutinoside (2.9 mg/g)**. The total-flavonoid fraction showed dose-dependent radical-scavenging (DPPH, ABTS, OH•, O₂•⁻) and, in mice, **reduced xylene-induced ear oedema and carrageenan paw oedema**, exerted **antinociceptive** effects in the formalin test, and **lowered pro-inflammatory IL-1β, TNF-α, IFN-γ and NO** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0153080)). This directly supports the "cooling/soothing the inflamed throat" doctrine at the mechanistic level.
- **Composition and bioactivity overview.** Ferraz et al. review *M. nigra* and confirm **cyanidin-3-glucoside as the main fruit anthocyanin**, alongside chlorogenic, cryptochlorogenic and protocatechuic acids, and document **antinociceptive, antimicrobial and antioxidant** activities across plant parts ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/2024/3784092)).

The *M. alba* / *M. nigra* distinction deserves a closing emphasis, because it is the entry's whole reason for existing separately. The antidiabetic literature that dominates "mulberry" search results is built overwhelmingly on the **leaf of *M. alba*** and its iminosugar 1-deoxynojirimycin, a genuine α-glucosidase inhibitor that blunts post-prandial glucose. *M. nigra*'s attested classical role is the **fruit** as a cooling throat/mouth remedy, and its retrieved modern evidence is anthocyanin antioxidant/anti-inflammatory pharmacology — not glycaemic control. Conflating the two (a very common error in secondary sources) would import an effect that does not belong to this species-and-part. The codex therefore treats *M. nigra* fruit and *M. alba* leaf as distinct materia, cross-referenced but never merged.

**Evidence grade.** Anthocyanin identity/quantification: **strong**. Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory/antinociceptive activity: **moderate but preclinical** (cell and rodent models; no throat-specific human RCT retrieved this run — the gargle's clinical benefit remains traditionally grounded and biologically plausible rather than trial-proven). **Antidiabetic claims:** deflate — the robust α-glucosidase-inhibition/DNJ evidence attaches to ***M. alba* leaf**, not *M. nigra* fruit, and should not be transferred to this entry.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic/Unani (Avicenna):** ripe fruit cold & moist (blood-cooling, soothing, laxative); unripe fruit cold & dry, astringent (for lax/bleeding gums).
- **Ayurveda (Sushruta lineage):** rasa — madhura-amla (sweet-sour); virya — cooling (sheeta); refrigerant, mild laxative, for thirst/burning.
- **TCM (Shennong lineage):** *sang shen* (classically *M. alba* fruit-spike) — sweet, cold; Heart/Liver/Kidney; nourishes yin and blood, moistens dryness. *M. nigra* as cooling analogue.
- **Dioscorides (Graeco-Roman):** morea/diamoron — boiled fruit juice for ulcerated mouth and throat.
- **Assyrian:** genus plausibly attested; exact *M. nigra* binomial **not reliably attested** [USER: verify].

### Contraindications

- **Do not overclaim antidiabetic action:** *M. nigra* fruit is not a substitute for the DNJ-bearing *M. alba* leaf; patients on hypoglycaemics should not treat black-mulberry fruit as blood-sugar therapy.
- **Ripeness matters:** unripe fruit is sharply astringent and can cause GI upset in quantity; the strongly astringent/sour unripe preparation is not for demulcent throat use.
- **Sugar load:** the concentrated Shahtoot syrup is high in sugar — relevant for diabetic and calorie-restricted contexts.
- **Staining/allergy:** intense anthocyanin staining; Moraceae latex/pollen sensitivity possible in atopic individuals.
- Preclinical evidence base — the throat/anti-inflammatory benefit is biologically plausible but not established by human trials.
