# Pinus mugo — "Pinus mugo" (translator's suggestion for Dioscorides 5-44 *Strobilites* and 5-48 *Oinos pissites*; also 5-43)

## *Pinus mugo Turra, the mountain pine, a low, shrubby pine of the European mountains; the identification is the translator's, printed as a suggestion under three wine chapters of De materia medica (Pinus mugo is one of several pines the translator offers elsewhere in the same book), so it names a kind of tree for the pine cones and pitch Dioscorides uses, not a plant he describes; confidence low to medium* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Dioscorides translation only, as a suggested identification.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Pinus mugo [DMM]". The name is the translator's (Osbaldeston, 2000; `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`), printed in the "SUGGESTED" line under each chapter heading.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Two chapters are given as the loci of the row.

*5-44, Strobilites* (heading line 9995, text line 9998; suggestion "Pinus mugo — Pinecone Wine"). "Strobilites is made from new bruised pinecones steeped in must [grape pulp]. It has the same effects as resinatum [5-43]. Pinecones steeped in must and boiled are good taken copiously as a drink for pulmonary tuberculosis."

*5-48, Oinos pissites* (heading line 10025, text line 10027; suggestion "Pissites — Pinus mugo — Pine — Pitch Wine"). "Vinum picatum is made of moist pitch and must [grape pulp]. First wash the pitch in seawater or brine until it turns white and the seawater runs clean, and then wash it in sweet water. To eight gallons of must add one or two ounces of pitch and let it stand. After it has boiled and is settled put it in jars. It is warming, digesting, cleansing, and purging — suitable to be used for disorders of the chest, bowels, liver, and spleen, as well as disorders of the womb if there is no fever. It is used for deep ulcers, and long-lasting discharges. It is good for coughs, slow digestion, gaseousness, asthmas, and dislocations (especially) applied with unwashed wool." (line 10027)

The name also stands in the suggestion line of 5-43, *Oinos retinites* (line 9993), where it is paired with *Picea abies* (see I_1601). Other chapters of the book use "Pinus mughus" among several pines in the suggestions for the pine resin and pine-cone chapters (for example lines 4202 and 4205); those chapters belong to other spine rows and are not folded in here. No other tradition's text on disk was found to name this pine.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Pine-cone wine: "good taken copiously as a drink for pulmonary tuberculosis" and with the same effects as rosin wine (5-44). Pitch wine: chest, bowels, liver, spleen and womb disorders (if no fever), deep ulcers, long-lasting discharges, coughs, slow digestion, gaseousness, asthmas, and dislocations as an application (5-48).
- **What the text does not say.** It names no tree.

The Codex gives no protocol. Tuberculosis is a serious infection that needs medical treatment, and a pitch-flavoured wine is not recommended.

### Preparation & Dosing

5-44: cones "new bruised", steeped in must (line 9998); no quantity. 5-48: the pitch is washed in seawater or brine until white, then in sweet water; "to eight gallons of must add one or two ounces of pitch", boiled, settled, and jarred (line 10027). These are quantities for making a batch, not doses. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] **Do not use any quantity from this entry.**

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Pine pitch and resin are irritants, and the wines are alcoholic. Tuberculosis is treated with specific antibiotics. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** Chapter 5-48 prints "warming, digesting, cleansing, and purging" (line 10027). Chapter 5-44 prints no quality of its own beyond "the same effects as resinatum". No other classification is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is manufactured here. The chapters offer a discipline of attention to making: new cones, pitch washed until the water runs clean, a stated ratio to the must. The text says "if there is no fever" for the womb use, a condition on use. No cultivation status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** Medicinal wines flavoured with cones and pitch. A cultivator takes from them only the habit of preparing a material carefully and noting the condition on its use.

### Contraindications

- **Alcohol.** Not suitable in pregnancy, for children, for people with liver disease, or before driving.
- **Pitch.** Unwashed or crude pitch is an irritant; do not swallow it.
- **Tuberculosis, asthma, ulcers.** These need medical care; do not rely on a wine.
- **Identity.** The tree is the translator's suggestion. [USER: verify]
