# White rock salt — mdze.tsha (Clark 1995, "White Rock Salt"); Zae tsha (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011), rGyud-bzhi Explanatory Tantra ch.20

## *A salt that in Clark's commentary "resembles clear, dried bubbles of mtshur (alunite) on rocks", which dissolves blood clots and drains lymph from wounds; the Men-Tsee-Khang note on the probable counterpart (Zae tsha) describes a white, soft, natural salt found in damp caves and under mirabilite; it is a separate entry from "rock salt" (rgyam.tsha), so we do not identify it with plain sodium chloride; identification unresolved (low to medium confidence)* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The Codex's names row (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`) gives the locus "Clark 1995 (line 933)"; the batch's check calls it "Rock salt (mdze.tsha, NaCl)", which this entry corrects (see Identity). Two texts were read.

*Clark 1995* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). Chapter 20 lists the salts in part (3), the earth medicines (line 857). The entry (line 933) reads:

"mdze.tsha: White Rock Salt: [resembles clear, dried bubbles of mtshur (alunite) on rocks]. It dissolves blood clots and drains [accumulations of] lymph from wounds."

By the bracket convention of Clark's introduction (line 82; square brackets carry the commentaries), the tantra's own words are the action, dissolving blood clots and draining lymph from wounds; the likeness to dried bubbles of alunite is commentary. The convention was not checked against the Tibetan. [USER: verify] The salty-taste group (line 767) lists "white rock salt" apart from "rock salt".

The entry that Clark prints as "rock salt" is a different one (line 928): "rgyam.tsha: Rock Salt: [has nine names and is a clear, crystalline salt obtained from stone. It resembles male calcite (pho.chong) and has slightly sweet taste.] Rock salt cures phlegm-wind combinations, indigestion and cold disorders." The two salts therefore have different descriptions and different actions.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`). The salty-taste group (line 3211) lists "Zae tsha", with a note (line 3280, page 193): "Zae tsha is a white, soft, natural salt which when burnt, boils with a hissing sound and thereafter becomes a white powder. It is of two types: superior and inferior. The former forms on the cracked wet caves and the latter under mirabititum." (The "mirabititum" is as printed in the e-text; the mineral meant is probably mirabilite, which Clark lists as *ze.tsha*. [USER: verify]) The actions are given twice. In the soil-medicine summary (line 3373): "Zae tsha helps digests calculi and dissolves small calculi." In the salt summary (line 3400): "Zae tsha dilutes blood and drains out chhuser from wounds." The second statement matches Clark's *mdze.tsha* (dissolving blood clots, draining lymph from wounds) closely; the first, on calculi, appears in Clark under the mirabilite entry (*ze.tsha*, line 860: "dissolves stones"). The identification of Clark's *mdze.tsha* with Men-Tsee-Khang's *Zae tsha* rests on the action and the sound of the names. [USER: verify]

**What is not given.** In the passages read, neither text gives a potency or post-digestive taste, a dose, a preparation or a formula containing it. The Men-Tsee-Khang note says that it is "burnt", which describes a test of the material ("boils with a hissing sound") and not a medicinal preparation.

**Identity.** The batch's first guess was "Rock salt (NaCl)". The text does not support that: Clark's own "Rock Salt" is *rgyam.tsha*, a "clear, crystalline salt obtained from stone", and this entry is *mdze.tsha*, "White Rock Salt", with a different look and a different action. The Men-Tsee-Khang description (white, soft, natural, found in damp caves or under mirabilite, hissing when burnt) does not describe halite in any way the text confirms. The entry is therefore filed as "white rock salt, unresolved". [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None is stated in the texts. As a general fact, salts forming in caves and under mirabilite (sodium sulphate) deposits may be sodium or other alkali sulphates, chlorides or nitrates; the texts name none. No composition is asserted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The texts record the salt as dissolving blood clots and draining lymph or *chhuser* from wounds (Clark; Men-Tsee-Khang) and, in Men-Tsee-Khang, as helping to digest or dissolve small calculi. Wounds with clots and drainage, and kidney or gall stones, need medical care. An unidentified cave salt should not be swallowed or put on wounds. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation for white rock salt. The two types (superior from damp cave cracks, inferior from under mirabilite) are a grading by source, and the "burning" is a test. No form, route, measure or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] As general facts: soluble salts do not dissolve calculi in the body; concentrated salts are irritants on wounds; "diluting blood" has no medical sense in the modern use of the phrase.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT).** The salt is placed in the salty-taste group (Clark line 767; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3211). Its actions are given in the tradition's own terms: it dissolves blood clots and drains lymph (*chhuser*) from wounds. The Explanatory Tantra states of the salty taste in general that it pacifies wind (*rlung*) and, with hot and sour, cures phlegm (*bad kan*) disorders (Men-Tsee-Khang line 3224); that is not an action printed for this salt. No potency is printed. No classification is imported from other systems.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The tantra lists it among the earth medicines and salty-taste medicines and gives it wound- and stone-related actions. The one substance of the section called "unsurpassed as an essence-extraction substance" is *brag.zhun* (Clark line 870); nothing like this is said of this salt, and no *bcud len* use was found. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation. It is a cave salt the tantra lists for wounds and small stones, with no preparation given, and it should not be mistaken for ordinary rock salt. It is not a recommended practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Wounds with clots or drainage; urinary or biliary stones:** medical conditions; see a doctor.
- **Unidentified cave salt:** may be a sulphate or nitrate salt; do not swallow in quantity or rub into wounds or the eyes.
- **Unsettled identity:** the text separates it from "rock salt"; nothing should be bought or used on the strength of the name.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
