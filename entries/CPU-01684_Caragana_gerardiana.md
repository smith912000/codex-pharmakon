# Caragana gerardiana — Drama, bra ma (Men-Tsee-Khang glossary, by column position); also Clark's second binomial for mdzo.mo (rGyud-bzhi); "Pea shrub"

## *Caragana gerardiana, as the binomial printed in the scientific-name run of the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary, and as the second of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for mdzo.mo; the Tibetan name "Drama" and the English "Pea shrub" are paired with it in the glossary by column position only* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** **Named only by the Tibetan texts, and under this binomial by the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary and, in second place, by Clark.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. Both Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The extraction note says: "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, row 268). The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (Explanatory Tantra, glossary).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. The Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients prints its columns in separate blocks. In the "D" block (the letter at line 5179) the phonetic names are at lines 5181 to 5196 (twenty-two names, from "Dali" to "Drita sazin"; "Drama" is the seventeenth, line 5191), and the Tibetan transliterations are at lines 5198 to 5213 (twenty-two again; "bra ma" is the seventeenth, line 5209). The scientific names are in a separate run (lines 5215 to 5259), where several lines hold more than one name. Counting them in order, the seventeenth is "Caragana gerardiana" (line 5246, after "Lepisorus clathratus" on the same line), and the English names include "Pea shrub" (line 5250) in the seventeenth place. The glossary does not print them on one row, and the Codex does not state that it pairs them. The pairing is by column position only. Other places in the block agree with Clark: the second binomial, "Hippuris vulgaris", falls against "Dhambu kara" and Clark's *hdam.bu.ka.ra* line, the ninth, "Cassia fistula", against "Dhong ga" and Clark's *dong.ga*, and the last, "Fragaria nubicola", against "Drita sazin" and Clark's *hbri.ta.sa.hdzin*. The count in the middle rests on order and not on rows. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text (line 3457).* In chapter 20, in the section "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (it opens at line 3428), the text says: "The root of the Drama cures hot disorder of the muscle tissues and the channels." The text gives no Latin name in the passage. That the glossary's "Drama" is the name in this sentence is a reading from the identical spelling. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, the same binomial under another Tibetan name (line 1096).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The entry reads:

"mdzo.mo: Caragana jubata Poir: Caragana gerardiana: [is a thorny tree, larger than gra.ma and covered with white ‘fur’. It has a yellow flower and heartwood similar to red sandalwood.] Caragana dissolves blood clots and cures blood fever."

Clark's preface (line 82) says: "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". Inside the brackets (the commentary layer) are the description (thorny tree, fur, flower, heartwood). Outside the brackets (the root-text layer, as far as Clark's brackets mark it) are the Tibetan name, the two Latin names, which are Clark's own identifications and not the root text's, and the clause "dissolves blood clots and cures blood fever", in which the word "Caragana" is Clark's own for the drug. The commentary layer calls this tree "larger than gra.ma", the drug that Clark's line 1095 and the Men-Tsee-Khang "Drama" sentence name. So Clark places *Caragana gerardiana* with *mdzo.mo*, and the glossary's column position places it with "Drama", which is Clark's neighbouring *gra.ma* (that line prints *Caragana franchetia*, *C. brevifolia* and *C. pygmaea*). The two Tibetan texts disagree, and the Codex does not choose. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Caragana*, "Pea shrub", *Drama*, *mdzo.mo* and *Zhomo* found no entry on this plant. ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Caragana* is a genus of thorny shrubs of the pea family (Fabaceae). Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained, which may be another species of the genus. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the glossary's pairing the Tibetan drug "Drama" is a root that "cures hot disorder of the muscle tissues and the channels" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3457). Under Clark's pairing the drug *mdzo.mo* "dissolves blood clots and cures blood fever" (line 1096). These are the texts' claims, attached to this binomial by a column position or by a second place in a list, and they are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Tibetan texts class the drugs behind this binomial by what they cure: the root of "Drama" for hot disorder of the muscle tissues and the channels, and *mdzo.mo* for blood clots and blood fever. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for these names places them in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice. The link from this binomial to either drug rests on a column position or on a second place in a list. Its framing is therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The binomial stands against two different Tibetan drug names in the two texts, and both are therapeutic drugs for heat and blood disorders. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. The identity rests on a column position and on a second binomial in a list, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Fever and bleeding disorders need medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
