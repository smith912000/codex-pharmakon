# Ashmabhedaka — Ashmabhedaka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation, 1907)

## *Ashmabhedaka, a plant drug in the Viratarvadi group of Sutrasthana ch. 38, a group said to benefit urinary stone. Identification: Kalanchoe laciniata (Crassulaceae) by a positional pairing of "Asma-bheda" in the botanical appendix of Bhishagratna's Vol. III; confidence low; this differs from the first-pass suggestion of Bergenia, which is not supported by the sources read* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk. This is a single-text entry.** Spellings in quotations are normalised silently from the OCR where the damage is plain (for example Nandi for the printed Xandi); the line numbers cited give the text as printed. The OCR splits the word over a line ("Ashma- bhedaka"). "Ashmabhedaka" was searched in all three volumes of Bhishagratna and the chapter files and found in the two places below.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Sutrasthana ch. 38, the Viratarvadi group (`Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15398 to 15402; chapter file `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 57 to 66): "The drugs known as Virataru, the two kinds of Sahachara, Darbha, Vrikshadani, Gundra, Nala, Kusha, Kasha, Ashmabhedaka, Agnimantha, Morata, Vasuka, Vasira, Bhalluka, Kuruntaka, Indivara, Kapotavanka, and Shvadanstra enter into the composition of the group known as the Viratarvadi." The metrical text, which runs across a page break, reads: "The group subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles." (The OCR prints "Viratarva'di".)

The second place is Sutra ch. 39, the Vata-soothing list: "Eranda, Ashmabhedaka, Alarka, Arka, Shatavari, Punarnava ..." (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` line 90; Vol. I line 16009), where the drugs are said to possess "the general virtue of soothing (restoring to its normal state) the deranged Vayu". No part used, no preparation and no single-drug use were found.

**Identification.** The group-list row gives none. In Vol. III Appendix 3 the name "Asma-bheda" (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 31283) is paired by position with "Kalanchoe Laciniata" (line 31335); the neighbours hold (Asana with Terminalia tomentosa, Asoka with Saraca indica). The spelling differs from the group-list spelling and the appendix does not say it is the same drug. Bergenia was the first-pass suggestion; no source read supports it. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent for this drug. In general terms, the stonecrop family (Crassulaceae) are succulent plants, and some Kalanchoe species contain cardiotoxic bufadienolides; whether the plant here does was not checked. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Viratarvadi group: Vata disorders and urinary stone and gravel, painful and obstructed urine (Sutra ch. 38). As a Vata-soothing drug (Sutra ch. 39).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no preparation.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this drug.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given for Ashmabhedaka alone. Chapter 38 closes with a rule for all the groups: "Only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group, or a similar number of drugs chosen from the different groups, or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety, or in combination with another, should be used according to the indications of any particular case, as the physician, in his discretion, would determine" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, near the end; Vol. I, ch. XXXVIII). It also says that the groups "will be more elaborately dealt with later on in the chapter on Therapeutics". This is a rule of method for the groups, not a dose for this drug. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Urinary stones are a medical condition needing diagnosis. The identity is uncertain, so no modern correlation is attempted. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Ashmabhedaka. The only qualities are group-level: subduing deranged Vata and curative in stone, gravel and urinary troubles (Sutra ch. 38), and soothing Vata (Sutra ch. 39).

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is attested in the Sushruta Samhita, in the therapeutic groups of Sutrasthana ch. 38 and the other passages cited above, and its role there is therapeutic: groups are said to subdue Kapha, Vata or Pitta, to act as vermifuge or aseptic agents, and so on. Chikitsa ch. 26 (the vajikarana chapter, "increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons") and chs. 27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) were searched under the spellings printed here (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` to `ch30.txt`, and ch. 31) and the name was not found in them. The drug therefore carries no rasayana or vajikarana status in the sources retrieved, and nothing is said of ojas for it. The register is (b): framed by the therapeutic gana, with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** An unresolved group drug for urinary stone and Vata disorders. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use.

### Contraindications

- **Urinary stone and obstructed urine.** Medical conditions that can be emergencies; seek care.
- **Identity uncertain.** Do not substitute Bergenia, Kalanchoe or any other plant on the strength of this entry.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
