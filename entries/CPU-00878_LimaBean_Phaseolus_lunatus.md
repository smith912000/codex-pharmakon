### Origin & Tradition

The lima or butter bean is a **New World domesticate** — two independent domestications in the Americas (a large-seeded Andean lineage and a small-seeded Mesoamerican lineage) millennia before any contact with the Old World. This provenance forces an unusual honesty on any cross-tradition materia medica: the classical Mediterranean, Chinese, and Near-Eastern authorities **never saw this plant**. What they described under their various words for "bean" was a *bean-concept* — a functional category of starchy leguminous seed to which nourishing, emollient, and flatulent properties were attached. When *Phaseolus lunatus* crossed the Atlantic in the Columbian exchange, it inherited that pre-existing bean-doctrine by assimilation, not by original attestation. The tags below are marked accordingly: only the Aztec (Badianus) lineage is a *clean* attestation for a New World *Phaseolus*; the Dioscoridean and Chinese entries are the bean-concept the species was later absorbed into. This distinction is not pedantry — it is the difference between a documented use and a retrospective graft, and the codex records it as such.

**BM — Badianus Manuscript / *Libellus de Medicinalibus Indorum Herbis* (1552), Nahuatl *ayecohtli / etl* (bean):** The Badianus codex, compiled by the Aztec physician Martín de la Cruz and translated by Juan Badiano, is the one authority in this entry that describes a *Phaseolus* the authors actually grew and ate. Mesoamerican medicine treated cultivated beans (*etl*) as a nutritive food-medicine and a base for restorative, convalescent, and poultice preparations — the emollient, strengthening role of a dense starch-and-protein seed. This is a clean New-World attestation of the nutritive/emollient doctrine for a bean of the *Phaseolus* genus, and the honest anchor for the entry. `[USER: verify the specific ayecohtli/lunatus vs. vulgaris identification in your Badianus source — Mesoamerican bean nomenclature does not map cleanly onto Linnaean species.]`

**DMM — De Materia Medica (Dioscorides), Φάσηλος / bean-concept:** Dioscorides catalogued the Old-World pulses (*phasēlos*, *dolichos*, *kyamos*) as nutritive, mildly flatulent, and emollient foods, with meal-poultices for skin and swellings. He never encountered *P. lunatus*; his doctrine is the bean-concept — slow, filling nourishment plus a windy quality — into which the lima bean was later slotted by European herbalists who inherited Greek categories. The correlation is real at the level of *plant part function* (starchy seed = emollient nutritive) but the attribution is by concept, not by specimen.

**SBJ — Shennong Ben Cao Jing / Chinese bean-concept (藊豆 *biǎndòu* and the broader 豆 *dòu* category):** Classical Chinese materia medica assigns the bean category a Spleen-strengthening, dampness-transforming, harmonising role — food-grade tonification of the middle burner. The Chinese *biǎndòu* of the classics is *Lablab purpureus* (hyacinth bean), not *Phaseolus lunatus*; the lima bean, arriving late via trade, was absorbed into the same Spleen-nourishing bean-doctrine. Again: honest as concept, not as attestation.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — mapping the bean-doctrine onto real chemistry.** Every tradition's bean-concept encodes two real observations. First, **nourishing strength**: the doctrine of the bean as a food that "builds" maps directly onto the seed's macronutrient reality — ~20–25 % protein and a large slow-digesting starch fraction, i.e. a genuine Jing/Spleen-Qi substrate. The traditions were reading the calorie-and-protein density correctly. Second, **the windy / harmonising duality**: the classical insistence that beans are simultaneously strengthening *and* troublesome (flatulent, needing careful preparation) is a pre-scientific encoding of the seed's α-galactoside oligosaccharides (colonic fermentation → gas) *and*, more darkly, of its **cyanogenic** and **lectin** load. The universal traditional insistence that lima beans must be **boiled, with the water discarded**, is not ritual — it is an empirically-derived detoxification protocol. Doctrine here is compressed toxicology: the "wind" and the mandatory cooking are the culture's memory of linamarin and phytohaemagglutinin, learned by casualty and preserved as rule.

### Active Components

**Nutritive scaffold (the "strength" the doctrines named):**
- **Protein ~20–25 % dry weight** — high-lysine storage globulins; complements the methionine of cereal grains (the bean-plus-grain complete-protein pattern found independently in every bean-eating culture)
- **Starch (large slow-digesting fraction incl. resistant starch)** — low-glycaemic energy substrate; the emollient, filling, convalescent quality
- **Soluble and insoluble fibre; folate, iron, magnesium, potassium**

**⚠️ Cyanogenic glycosides (the primary hazard — a real, historically-regulated toxicity):**
- **Linamarin** (and minor **lotaustralin**) — α-hydroxynitrile glucosides stored in the seed. Biosynthesised via CYP79/CYP736 cytochrome-P450 enzymes and a UDP-glucosyltransferase (Lai et al. 2020). On tissue disruption they are hydrolysed by the seed's own **β-glucosidase (linamarase)** (purified from butter bean by Itoh-Nashida et al. 1987) and then by hydroxynitrile lyase to **hydrogen cyanide (HCN)**. Wild and some coloured/heirloom cultivars carry *far* higher cyanogenic potential than commercial white "butter beans"; several jurisdictions **cap the permissible HCN content** of lima beans traded for food.
- Practical toxicology: the intact seed is relatively low-risk because its water content and enzyme compartmentalisation limit spontaneous HCN release (Shlichta et al. 2014; Frehner et al. 1990) — but chewing, grinding, or slow warm soaking activates linamarase and liberates cyanide. **Boiling in ample water and discarding that water** volatilises and removes HCN and denatures linamarase; this is the non-negotiable preparation step.

**⚠️ Phytohaemagglutinin (PHA) — lectin toxin:**
- Like its congener *P. vulgaris* (kidney bean), lima bean carries **phytohaemagglutinin**, a carbohydrate-binding lectin that agglutinates erythrocytes and injures the gut epithelium, causing rapid-onset nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea from undercooked beans (documented for *P. vulgaris* by Watier-Grillot et al. 2023; intestinal lectin binding by Nciri et al. 2015; the same protein family shows both toxicity and, when purified, anti-tumour/immunomodulatory activity — Wang et al. 2021). **Boiling (not merely soaking, and emphatically not slow-cooker warming) destroys PHA.**

**Anti-nutrients:** α-galactoside oligosaccharides (raffinose/stachyose — the "wind"), phytic acid (mineral chelation), protease inhibitors (heat-labile).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Lima bean enters Kenetica as a **food-tier Jing/Qi builder**, not a therapeutic dose-form. It is a `[BUILD]` and `[RESET]` staple, not an active agent.

**1. Sustained nutritive substrate (BUILD).** The high-protein, high-resistant-starch, low-glycaemic profile makes properly cooked lima bean an excellent slow-energy, Spleen-Qi-supporting base food during accumulation and recovery blocks — the modern reading of the universal "bean builds strength" doctrine.

**2. Convalescent / emollient food (RESET).** Soft-cooked, water-discarded lima bean is a gentle, filling, easily portioned recovery food — the Badianus and Dioscoridean emollient-nutritive role, honestly grounded in starch and protein rather than any special pharmacology.

**3. Deliberately NOT a pharmacological actor.** Unlike an alkaloidal or glycosidic medicine, lima bean carries **no desirable dose-dependent bioactivity** to exploit; its two dose-dependent effects (cyanogenic and lectin toxicity) are hazards to eliminate, not effects to titrate. Kenetica treats it strictly as prepared food.

### Scientific Correlation

*According to PubMed, the following peer-reviewed sources were retrieved for this entry.*

- **Cyanogenic biochemistry (mechanism — well supported):** Lima bean synthesises the cyanogenic glucosides **linamarin and lotaustralin** via CYP79/CYP736 P450 enzymes and a glucosyltransferase — Lai et al., *Plant Direct* 2020 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/pld3.244). The activating enzyme **linamarase (β-glucosidase)** was purified from *P. lunatus* itself and hydrolyses linamarin — Itoh-Nashida et al., *J Biochem* 1987 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.jbchem.a121951). Seed cyanide potential and the monoglucoside pattern: Frehner et al., *Plant Physiol* 1990 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.94.1.28). Natural cyanogenic-glycoside variation across wild *P. lunatus* seed populations is large: Shlichta et al., *J Chem Ecol* 2014 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10886-014-0434-0). **Grade: STRONG** for the toxicological doctrine (boil-and-discard) — the chemistry, enzymology, and cultivar variation are all directly attested for this species.
- **Chronic cyanogenic harm (analogy, not lima-specific):** Sustained dietary cyanogenic-glucoside exposure from inadequately processed cassava causes **konzo**, an irreversible spastic paraparesis, with risk modulated by sulfurtransferase (rhodanese) detoxification capacity and protein/sulfur-amino-acid nutrition — Kambale et al., *Neurotoxicology* 2016 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuro.2016.05.016). **Grade: MODERATE/analogical** — this is *Manihot*, not *Phaseolus*, cited to show that the cyanogenic hazard class is real and disabling, not theoretical.
- **Phytohaemagglutinin lectin (congener-anchored):** Undercooked *Phaseolus* beans cause acute PHA foodborne illness — outbreak from undercooked kidney beans, Watier-Grillot et al., *Toxins* 2023 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins15070457); intestinal binding of bean PHA, Nciri et al., *J Med Food* 2015 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1089/jmf.2014.0194); characterisation of PHA-L function and toxicity, Wang et al., *Molecules* 2021 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules26020498). **Grade: STRONG for the lectin hazard class; MODERATE for lima specifically** — the primary outbreak data are on *P. vulgaris*, and the codex flags this honestly: lima carries the same lectin family but the headline poisonings are documented in kidney bean.
- **Nutritive "strength" doctrine:** No trial was retrieved *this run* isolating a therapeutic benefit of lima bean beyond its macronutrient content. **Grade: NULL for any special pharmacology.** The traditional "builds strength" claim is honest *as nutrition* (protein + resistant starch) and should not be inflated into a medicinal effect. Deflated accordingly.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Primary Tier:** ◈ Jing — a food-grade Essence/Spleen substrate; builds via protein and slow starch, not via active constituents.

**Honest caveat:** the energetic classification is **inherited by bean-concept**, not derived from an attested classical use of *P. lunatus*; it holds only insofar as any dense starch-protein legume holds it.

### Contraindications

- **⚠️ CYANOGENIC TOXICITY — never eat raw or undercooked.** Raw, sprouted, or low-temperature-cooked lima beans (especially coloured/heirloom/wild cultivars) can liberate hydrogen cyanide. Symptoms of cyanide exposure: headache, dizziness, rapid breathing, nausea, collapse. **Always boil hard and discard the water.** Regulatory HCN limits exist for a reason.
- **⚠️ PHYTOHAEMAGGLUTININ (lectin) — boil, do not slow-cook.** Undercooked beans cause rapid vomiting and diarrhoea. Slow-cookers and steaming are unsafe for dried beans.
- **Do not exploit for pharmacology.** There is no safe therapeutic dose-form beyond fully cooked food.
- **Fabaceae cross-allergy** — cross-reactivity with peanut, soy, lentil, pea; assess before introducing.
- **FODMAP / IBS-SIBO** — raffinose/stachyose oligosaccharides cause gas and cramping in sensitive individuals; introduce gradually, discard soak water.
- **G6PD / favism note** — favism is specific to *Vicia faba* (broad bean), **not** *Phaseolus lunatus*; do not conflate. Lima bean's hazards are cyanogenic and lectin, not the vicine/convicine of faba.

**Distinguish from *Phaseolus vulgaris* (Common Bean, in codex):** *P. vulgaris* is the classic phytohaemagglutinin poisoning bean; *P. lunatus* shares the lectin hazard **and adds a materially higher cyanogenic-glycoside risk**. Do not treat the two as interchangeable in preparation guidance — lima demands the same hard boil *plus* respect for its cyanide potential.
