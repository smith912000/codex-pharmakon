# Beetle — "Beetle" / "Shell-of-a-Beetle" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus animal list)
## *An insect named only as "Beetle" in Bryan's list of the animals that supplied the Egyptian materia medica; no Egyptian word, no species and no binomial is given in the sources retrieved* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter VII "Organic Remedies (animals)" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1404; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 128, class animal, "Bryan's English identification"). Bryan introduces his list of animals at lines 1385-1388: a complete list of the animal materia medica "would not be possible since (as with many of the plant and mineral substances) the identity of a considerable number of the animals mentioned is still beyond us", and the list that follows is of those "whose identity is not in doubt". "Beetle" stands in that list between "Tarantula" and "Bat" (line 1404): "Ass Hog Dog Cat Frog Tadpole Scorpion Eel Electric Eel Crab Swordfish Ostrich Locust Tarantula Beetle Bat Wasp Fly Tapeworm". The list gives no Egyptian name, no species and no use. The parts-of-animals list that follows it (lines 1405-1413) includes "Shell", but does not tie any part to any animal.

**The one prescription in Bryan's text.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "beetle". One passage prescribes the beetle itself, at line 2541, in the chapter whose running head is "Diseases of Women" (lines 2502 and 2510): "ANOTHER Tail-of-a-Tortoise Shell-of-a-Beetle sefet-oil sert-juice Oil. Crush into one and poultice therewith." Bryan's printed summary heads it only "ANOTHER", so the complaint it treats is not given in the text read. It follows a remedy in which a ball of fennel, incense, garlic, *sert*-juice, fresh salt and wasp's dung is put in the vagina, and immediately precedes the remedy for a diseased breast (lines 2536-2544). *sefet*-oil and *sert*-juice are printed untranslated.

**A similar name, not the same item.** "Beetle-wax" appears twice in Bryan's account of eye diseases: "Words to be spoken over Verdigris mixed with Beetle-wax. Add Cyperus thereto and carefully apply to the Eye" (line 2839), and as an "inoffensive poultice" for pterygium after a poultice of honeycomb (line 2842). Bryan does not say that this is wax from the beetle of the animal list, or what it is. It is a separately named drug and is not described here. [USER: verify]

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say which beetle, whether the whole insect or a shell (wing-case) was meant, where it was gathered, or how it was prepared beyond crushing. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis; Bryan offers none beyond the English word.

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it, because the species is unknown. In general, a beetle's hard outer shell (the elytra and body wall) is made of chitin, a nitrogen-containing polysaccharide, with protein and a small mineral fraction. Some beetles contain defensive or toxic compounds (blister beetles contain cantharidin); whether this applies here cannot be said.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation is the one at line 2541: the shell crushed with tortoise tail, *sefet*-oil, *sert*-juice and oil into one mass and used as a poultice. No quantities are printed in Bryan's summary. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution, and for this item the complaint is not even named in the passage read. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); the one recitation near a beetle-named drug, the "words to be spoken over Verdigris mixed with Beetle-wax" (line 2839), attaches to a different, separately named item and is not transferred to the beetle. No recitation is printed with the shell poultice, though Bryan summarises, so that is no proof that the papyrus gives none. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The record is one crushed-shell poultice for an unnamed complaint, with no species and no quantity. A cultivator has nothing to do with it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Unidentified insect:** the species is unknown; some beetles carry blistering or poisonous compounds, and an unidentified insect should not be ingested or applied to skin.
- **Hygiene and infection:** dead insects and animal matter can carry bacteria and fungi; a poultice on broken skin or a breast infection carries risk and needs medical assessment.
- **Allergy:** insect parts can cause allergic reactions.
- **Conservation:** many beetle species are protected; the status of any particular species cannot be given from the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
