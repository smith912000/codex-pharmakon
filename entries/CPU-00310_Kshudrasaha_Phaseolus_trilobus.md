### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda (Suśruta & the wild-pulse group).** Kṣudrasahā — from *kṣudra* ("small/lesser") + a root conveying "enduring / bearing" — is a small wild trailing legume grouped in the Ayurvedic tradition with the **Mudgaparṇī** complex, the "green-gram-leaved" wild pulses that stand as the lighter, cooling, convalescent cousins of the cultivated grams. *Phaseolus trilobus* (now *Vigna trilobata*) is a prostrate, three-lobed-leaflet wild vigna of Indian wasteland, field-margin and grazing land, long gathered as a famine-food, fodder, and a mild medicinal pulse. Within the classical framing it belongs to the **śimbī-dhānya** (leguminous grains) that are treated as *laghu* (light) and *śīta* (cooling) — foods and gentle medicines rather than fierce drugs.

The tradition assigns the Mudgaparṇī-type wild pulses a cooling, light, sweet-astringent character, useful in *pitta*-predominant and *jvara* (fever) states, in *dāha* (burning), and as convalescent nourishment where the digestive fire is delicate and heavy foods are contraindicated. As with several small wild vignas, there is genuine botanical and nomenclatural fluidity between *Vigna trilobata*, *Vigna aconitifolia* (moth bean) and the classical *Mudgaparṇī* (often identified with *Vigna trilobata* or *Phaseolus trilobus* in commentarial identifications) — the identifications are not perfectly settled, and the codex records this honestly rather than forcing a single equivalence.

**Folk and agricultural use.** Across the subcontinent the plant is used as green fodder and a pulse of last resort, and folk-medically the trailing herb and seeds have been used as a cooling, mildly nourishing food-medicine. Its agronomic value as a nitrogen-fixing legume is well documented (see below), which places it firmly among the "renewing-the-soil" pulses.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Lesser Pulse that Endures:** Kṣudrasahā's doctrine in the codex is *the smallness that sustains*. Its name pairs "the lesser" with "the enduring/bearing": it is the humble, prostrate, three-lobed wild gram that grows where nothing prized will grow, that feeds cattle and, in scarcity, people. Where the fierce nightshades open channels and the renewer drains floods, Kṣudrasahā represents the opposite therapeutic register entirely — *the gentle, cooling, nutritive substance that carries the depleted body through recovery*. It is food before it is drug. Its lesson is that not every remedy is an intervention; some are simply the light, cool, sustaining nourishment that lets an overheated or convalescent system settle and rebuild. The legume that renews the soil by fixing nitrogen is, symbolically, the plant that renews the ground of the body quietly, from beneath.

### Active Components

*Vigna trilobata* is a food legume and its constituents are, correspondingly, those of a pulse: **plant proteins, starches, dietary fibre, and legume flavonoids/phenolics**, with the sterol and saponin profile typical of the genus. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Kṣudrasahā is the codex's **gentle cooling-nutritive** entry — a food-medicine for the *convalescent, pitta-warm, delicately-digesting* body. It is not an active pharmacological agent in the way the nightshades or Punarnava are; its role is dietetic and supportive: light, cooling, mildly nourishing sustenance during fever recovery and heat-driven depletion, where heavier pulses would overburden a weak agni. In the training arc it sits in the cooling-convalescent-nourishment phase, functioning as ballast and support rather than intervention.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, there is **very little pharmacological literature specific to *Vigna trilobata* / *Phaseolus trilobus***, and none that validates a clinical medicinal claim. The retrieved indexed work is botanical and agronomic rather than therapeutic:

- She et al. (*Comparative Cytogenetics*, 2020) performed molecular cytogenetic characterisation of five wild *Vigna* species including *V. trilobata*, establishing chromosome number (2n = 2x = 22) and rDNA/fluorochrome karyotype patterns — a taxonomic/genomic study, not a pharmacological one. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3897/CompCytogen.v14i2.51154)
- Sridevi & Mallaiah (*Indian Journal of Microbiology*, 2009) documented phosphate-solubilising *Rhizobium* isolates from the root nodules of *Vigna trilobata* among other legumes — confirming its nitrogen-fixing, soil-renewing agronomic role. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12088-009-0005-1)

**Evidence grade: ABSENT for clinical/pharmacological efficacy; the plant's status here rests on classical Ayurvedic dietetic use, not on modern trial data.** No PubMed-indexed study demonstrates a medicinal effect for this species; the available literature is cytogenetic and microbiological. This entry deliberately does **not** assert pharmacological actions the evidence does not support. Botanical and agronomic claims are attributed to the PubMed sources above; medicinal character is attributed to the Ayurvedic nighaṇṭu tradition and marked as traditional, not validated.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** *Madhura* (sweet), *Kaṣāya* (astringent).
- **Guṇa (qualities):** *Laghu* (light), *Rūkṣa* (dry) — as with the wild grams; some readings give *Snigdha* when well-cooked.
- **Vīrya (potency):** *Śīta* (cooling).
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** *Madhura* (sweet).
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** *tarpaṇa/śītala* — gentle cooling nourishment of a depleted, heated system (dietetic rather than a sharp pharmacological prabhāva).
- **Doṣa action:** *Pitta-Kapha-śāmaka*; may mildly increase Vāta if very dry, hence usually well-cooked with unctuous adjuncts.
- **Karma (actions):** *Tarpaṇa* (nourishing), *Śītala* (cooling), *Jvara-anukūla* (fever-appropriate diet), *Dāhapraśamana* (relieves burning), *Balya* in a mild dietetic sense.
- **Srotas (channels):** primarily *annavaha* (digestive) and *rasavaha* (nutrient-fluid); secondarily *raktavaha*.
- **Gaṇa membership:** grouped with the *śimbī-dhānya* (pulses) and the *Mudgaparṇī*-type wild-gram dravyas; not a member of the Daśamūla or the sharp therapeutic gaṇas.

### Contraindications

- Being cooling, light and Vāta-tending-dry, it is less suited to cold, low-agni, heavily Vāta-aggravated states unless well-cooked with warming, unctuous adjuncts.
- As a legume it carries the usual pulse cautions: flatulence and heaviness if poorly cooked or over-consumed; caution in those with significant Vāta digestive weakness.
- No human medicinal safety/efficacy data exist for this species — treat it as a traditional dietetic pulse, not a validated therapeutic agent.
- Nomenclatural/taxonomic confusion with other wild vignas and with the classical Mudgaparṇī is real — source-verify the material.
- Not a treatment for any disease; the codex records it as a supportive, cooling food-medicine only.
