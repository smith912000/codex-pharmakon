### Origin & Tradition

Few plants have carried humanity's cloth and its contradictions as far as cotton. *Gossypium herbaceum*, the Old World "Levant cotton," is the species most consistently named in the classical and Asian medical corpora — distinct from the New World tetraploids (*G. hirsutum*, *G. barbadense*) that later dominated the textile economy. What unites the traditions below is not the boll's fibre but the pharmacologically active seed and, above all, the root-bark: a warm, acrid, Blood-moving agent repeatedly turned to the reproductive axis. This is a plant whose medicine and whose danger are the same gesture.

**Dioscoridean / Graeco-Roman (DMM):** Cotton entered the Mediterranean pharmacopoeia as *xylon* / *gossypion*, the "wool-bearing plant." Classical and late-antique writers noted the seed's oily, warming quality and the down's use as an absorbent and haemostatic dressing — a wound-packing material valued precisely for its capacity to soak and to staunch. The Galenic reading placed the seed in the warm-and-moist register, a nutritive and mildly demulcent seed-oil, while the fibrous down served the drying, absorbing office of a surgical material rather than an internal drug.

**Ayurveda & Siddha (AT / SS):** In the Indian systems cotton is *Karpasa* (कर्पास), and here the plant is genuinely a medicine, not merely a textile crop. Classical Ayurvedic and Siddha practice used *Karpasa* root-bark and seed as an emmenagogue and uterine agent — to provoke delayed menses, to assist expulsion in obstructed labour, and, in the darker registers of folk practice, as an abortifacient. The Siddha tradition of the Tamil south, with its deep pharmacy of reproductive and *sukra* (generative-essence) agents, likewise held cotton root-bark as a *garbha*-acting drug to be handled by those who understood its force. Seed preparations appear as galactagogues and demulcents in some regional texts — a striking duality, the same plant credited with both promoting milk and moving the womb.

**Chinese Medicine (CM):** Cotton (*mian-hua* 棉花; seed *mian-hua-zi* 棉花子; root *mian-hua-gen* 棉花根) is documented in the later Chinese materia medica as a warm, acrid agent that warms the interior, tonifies Yang and moves Blood. Root and seed preparations were used for menstrual and reproductive complaints, for chronic cough and for Yang-deficiency patterns — and cotton seed became the substrate for one of the twentieth century's most consequential ethnopharmacological observations (see *Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer* and *Scientific Correlation*).

**Sub-Saharan Ethnobotany (SBJ):** Across the cotton-growing belts of West and East Africa, root and seed decoctions appear in folk practice as emmenagogues, as agents to hasten delivery, and as fertility-regulating drugs within traditions where cotton was simultaneously a cash crop, a spinning-and-weaving craft, and a household pharmacy. The plant's reproductive reputation is remarkably stable across these independent lineages — a convergence too consistent to be coincidence.

**Folk Midwifery (FMT) & European Phytotherapy (EP):** Cotton root-bark has a distinct and well-attested history in nineteenth-century North American and European eclectic and folk-midwifery practice, where it was employed as a parturient and emmenagogue — a labour-inducing and menses-provoking agent, and reputedly, within enslaved communities of the American South, as a covert fertility-controlling drug. The Eclectic physicians of that era adopted cotton root-bark into their materia medica on exactly these grounds. European phytotherapy inherited the seed chiefly as a source of fixed oil and the root-bark as a restricted emmenagogue.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Cloth That Unmakes:**
Cotton sits at a symbolic crossroads that no other entry in this volume quite occupies. It is the plant of covering — of clothing, swaddling, the shroud, the bandage — and yet its inner medicine is one of *un*covering, of opening and expelling. The boll that clothes the newborn is the same organism whose root moves the womb. This polarity runs through every tradition that used it: the outer down is drying, protective, staunching (it stops blood at a wound); the inner root-bark is moving, opening, provoking (it brings blood at the womb). In Kenetica this makes cotton a teaching-plant for the doctrine of *the same force in two directions* — the acrid Blood-mover that heals a surface by closing it and acts on the depths by opening them.

There is a further doctrinal layer that must be named plainly and without romance. Gossypol — the polyphenol concentrated in seed, root and gland — became, in the twentieth century, the first widely studied plant-derived male contraceptive, after populations consuming crude cottonseed oil were observed to become infertile. This is the rare case where a folk reputation ("cotton unmakes generation") was vindicated by isolate biochemistry, and where the isolate's toxicity is inseparable from its action. Cotton therefore stands in the Codex as the emblem of the *pharmakon* in its fullest Greek sense: remedy and poison, the gift that clothes and the agent that unmakes, held in one plant. It is filed as an **Advanced-phase, restricted-handling** substance for exactly this reason.

### Active Components

- **Gossypol** — a dimeric sesquiterpenoid aldehyde (a binaphthyl polyphenol) concentrated in seed, root-bark and the plant's pigment glands. Exists as (+)-(S) and (−)-(R) atropisomers with restricted rotation about the binaphthyl bond; the (−)-(R) form is markedly more cytotoxic and more antifertility-active. Responsible for the male-antifertility effect and much of the plant's toxicity.
- **Gossypol-6-methyl ether** and related terpenoid aldehydes — companion phytoalexins of the gossypol family.
- **Fixed seed oil** — rich in linoleic and oleic acids; the demulcent, nutritive fraction (gossypol-depleted after refining).
- **Flavonoids, sterols and organic acids** in root-bark contributing to the emmenagogue reputation.
- **Cellulose down** — the pharmacologically inert fibre used historically as absorbent/haemostatic dressing.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Cotton is taught, never dispensed casually. Its Kenetica role is threefold and strictly phase-gated:

1. **Materia-study anchor (Foundational):** the seed-oil and the down illustrate the demulcent/absorbent axis and the difference between an inert material (fibre) and an active drug (root-bark, seed).
2. **Emmenagogue doctrine (Advanced):** root-bark exemplifies the acrid, warm, Blood-moving class — the same functional family as other womb-acting agents — and is the canonical case for teaching the pregnancy contraindication.
3. **Isolate case-study (Advanced/BM):** gossypol is the teaching-molecule for atropisomerism, for "folk reputation confirmed by biochemistry," and for the ethics of a remedy whose efficacy and toxicity cannot be separated.

Cotton is **not** offered as a self-administered remedy in any Kenetica protocol. Its value is doctrinal and pharmacognostic.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the reproductive pharmacology of cotton is genuinely established rather than folkloric:

- Randel, Chase & Wyse (1992), *Journal of Animal Science*, reviewed gossypol and cottonseed products across mammalian reproduction, documenting that gossypol disrupts oestrous cycles, pregnancy and early embryo development in non-ruminant females, and in males causes infertility through sperm immotility and depressed sperm counts via mitochondrial damage to sperm tails and germinal-epithelium injury — the mechanistic basis of the historic antifertility reputation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2527/1992.7051628x)
- Stipanovic, Puckhaber, Liu & Bell (2009), *Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry*, characterised the (+)-(S)/(−)-(R) gossypol atropisomer ratios across *Gossypium barbadense* accessions, confirming that (−)-(R)-gossypol is the more toxic and more anticancer-active form and that its male-antifertility activity is a real toxicological concern — the biochemical grounding of the "same force, two directions" doctrine. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/jf802756e)

**Evidence grade:** *Moderate for the isolate (gossypol), low-to-moderate for whole-plant traditional preparations.* Gossypol's antifertility and cytotoxic pharmacology is well documented in animal and mechanistic studies; robust modern clinical trials of whole cotton root-bark as an emmenagogue are sparse. The traditional reputation is unusually well-corroborated at the molecular level, but this does not translate into a validated, safe dosing regimen for lay use.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Root and seed — warm, acrid; warms interior, tonifies Yang, moves Blood; channels associated with reproductive and Kidney-Yang patterns.
- **Ayurveda / Siddha:** *Karpasa* — *ushna* (heating) *virya*; seed *snigdha* (unctuous, demulcent, mildly *kapha*-building); root-bark *garbha*-acting (uterine), *pitta*-mobilising in excess.
- **Galenic / Unani:** Seed warm & moist (grade I–II), nutritive; root-bark drying and emmenagogue.

**The short answer, before the detail.** Cotton is a fibre and seed-oil plant with a potent reproductive-axis drug in its root-bark. **The traditions record material and therapeutic uses, not a cultivation status.** No tradition tagged here assigns *Gossypium herbaceum* a standing in its own scheme of nourishing, conserving or refining the person. That is the honest finding and the section is not padded past it.

**Graeco-Roman / Dioscoridean (DMM).** Cotton enters as *xylon* / *gossypion*, the "wool-bearing plant" — the seed read as oily and warming, the down valued as an absorbent and haemostatic dressing. Dioscorides has **no subtle-body system** and none is constructed for him. What the tradition offers a cultivator here is its discipline of attention, and cotton is a clean case for it: the distinction between an **inert material** (the down, a surgical dressing) and an **active drug** (the root-bark and crude seed) is exactly the kind of provenance-and-part discrimination the Greek assay method exists to enforce. Cultivation status: **absent**.

**Ayurveda / Traditional Indian (tagged AT) and Siddha (SS).** *Karpasa* (कर्पास) is genuinely attested as a medicine in the Indian systems — root-bark and seed as an emmenagogue and uterine (*garbha*-acting) drug, seed preparations as demulcents and galactagogues in some regional material. These are **therapeutic assignments to an organ and a function**. No *rasāyana* or *vājīkaraṇa* status is attested for *Karpasa* in the sources retrieved; and although the Siddha pharmacy of the Tamil south is notably rich in *sukra* (generative-essence) agents, **this entry attests cotton only as a *garbha*-acting drug and not as an agent assigned to *sukra***, so no such assignment is made. Cultivation status: **unattested — a therapeutic standing only**. *(Under the canonical legend AT is Assyrian; no Assyrian attestation for this species appears in this entry, and the blocking warning on Assyrian identifications would apply — **absent** on that reading.)*

**Chinese Medicine (tagged CM).** *Mián-huā* 棉花, with seed *mián-huā-zǐ* and root *mián-huā-gēn*, appears in the **later** Chinese materia medica as a warm, acrid agent for menstrual, reproductive and Yang-deficiency patterns. The lateness matters: cotton is not a substance of the early *bencao* stratum, and **no *san pin* 三品 grade is recorded for it in the sources retrieved** — so it carries no position in the upper/middle/lower hierarchy that constitutes the Chinese cultivation scheme, and therefore no relation to *yang sheng* 養生 either way. Its recorded actions (warming the interior, moving Blood) are curative in form, not conservational. Cultivation status: **unattested**. *(Under the canonical legend CM is Unani / Avicennan; cotton is not attested there in this entry, and no relation to innate heat or radical moisture is claimed — **absent** on that reading.)*

**Sub-Saharan ethnobotany (tagged SBJ).** Across the West and East African cotton belts, root and seed decoctions are recorded ethnographically as emmenagogues, birth-hastening agents and fertility-regulating drugs. Attestation here is **ethnographic rather than textual**, and this entry generalises across a belt rather than naming a people — which is itself a limitation worth recording. No cultivation framing is attested. Cultivation status: **absent**. *(Under the canonical legend SBJ is the Chinese *Shennong* register; see the CM paragraph — the same "no grade recorded" finding applies and is not double-counted.)*

**Folk midwifery (tagged FMT) and European phytotherapy (tagged EP).** Nineteenth-century North American and European eclectic and folk-midwifery practice used cotton root-bark as a parturient and emmenagogue, including — as the entry records — as a covert fertility-controlling drug within enslaved communities of the American South. This is a history of **use under duress and of therapeutic and reproductive control**, and it is not a cultivation tradition; treating it as one would be both false and grotesque. European phytotherapy inherited the seed as a fixed-oil source and the root-bark as a restricted emmenagogue. Cultivation status: **absent**. *(Under the canonical legend FMT is Tibetan; no Tibetan attestation for this species appears here, no *bcud len* status is claimed, and no formal category of subtle-body substances exists in that tradition to claim one from — **absent** on that reading too.)*

**Biomedical / isolate (tagged BM).** Not a tradition in the cultivation sense and makes no claim to be one; gossypol's pharmacology is reported under *Scientific Correlation*. It is recorded here only because the tag exists. *(Under the canonical legend BM is Nahua / Badianus. *G. herbaceum* is the Old World species and the New World cotton traditions belong to *G. hirsutum* / *G. barbadense*; no Badianus attestation is claimed, and the *tonalli* / *teyolia* / *ihiyotl* model is in any case a later ethnohistorical reconstruction not present in that manuscript. **Absent**, and the tag is doubtful at species level.)*

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with cotton. There is no regimen here, no long-course substance, no vital endowment being conserved — and the one place where cotton touches a tradition's vital vocabulary at all, it does so **destructively**: chronic gossypol exposure suppresses spermatogenesis, which is depletion, not cultivation, and no tradition tagged here claims otherwise. What survives for practice is discrimination of parts: the down is inert material, the refined oil is food, the root-bark and crude seed are a restricted reproductive-axis drug with an absolute pregnancy contraindication. Cotton is studied, not taken. The register's emptiness is the accurate reading of the plant, not a gap in the research.

### Contraindications

- **ABSOLUTE contraindication in pregnancy** — cotton root-bark and seed (crude) are emmenagogue/abortifacient and embryotoxic. Never use during pregnancy or when pregnancy is possible.
- **Male fertility:** chronic gossypol exposure suppresses spermatogenesis — relevant to men, and a reason crude cottonseed products are not food-safe.
- **General toxicity:** crude gossypol causes hypokalaemia, cardiac and hepatic injury, oedema and dyspnoea in overdose; non-ruminants (including humans) are particularly sensitive.
- **Only refined, gossypol-depleted seed oil** is appropriate for dietary use.
- Not for use alongside other reproductive-axis or Blood-moving agents without qualified supervision.
