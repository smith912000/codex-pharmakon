# Oxytropis kansuensis — Saekar, srad dkar (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Oxytropis kansuensis, the one binomial the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints in third place of its "S" scientific-name run, which the Codex reads by position against the phonetic name "Sae kar" and the transliteration "srad dkar"; Clark 1995 prints Astragalus yunnaneansis and Oxytropis oxyphylla for srad.dkar.wa, "type of Gum Tragacanth"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi, read in two modern editions, and on a species name that only one of them prints, set against the Tibetan name by column position and not on one printed line.** The editions are Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, both in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. They are two translations of one tradition's text and are not two independent traditions. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian, Badianus, Avicenna and Sushruta material on disk for *Oxytropis* found nothing. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (line 348) the key *Oxytropis kansuensis* is taken from the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary line 5732 with the note "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)". It is the **only** binomial the glossary prints at that place, so it is neither the first nor a later one of several. Clark does not print *Oxytropis kansuensis*. His two *Oxytropis* names are *Oxytropis oxyphylla* (second of two binomials for *srad.dkar.wa*, line 1098, `names.csv` line 188) and *Oxytropis chiliophylla* (third of three for *stag.sha*, line 1017, `names.csv` line 136; this belongs to a different drug and is treated with entry I_1325). The pairing of *O. kansuensis* with the name "Sae kar" is the Codex's reading and is set out below.

**Identity doubt.** The glossary's pairing is by position, and the binomial is not Clark's; Clark's line for the Tibetan name gives a "type of Gum Tragacanth" under *Astragalus* and *Oxytropis*, both legume genera. The glossary prints no English name for the slot (a dash), so the two editions cannot be compared by English name. They agree in kind on the action (oedema, below) and differ on the species. The Codex does not choose. The Men-Tsee-Khang editors' note (line 686) says that the botanical terms in their text "are based on the information procured from the Materia Medica Department of this institute"; it names no flora or earlier work. **Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (the "S" block, lines 5669 to 5768).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients (the block ends at the page number 313 printed at line 5770). The glossary prints its columns in separate runs, and the pairing has to be counted.

*The phonetic names* of the first part of the "S" block are fifteen: "Sa dre" (line 5685), "Saegoe" (5689), "Sae kar" (5690), "Sazika" (5695), "Sedu" (5699), "Sengdeng" (5703), "Sergyi metok" (5707), "Sergyi phuebu" (5709), "Ser kue" (5711), "Sewai metok" (5715), "Seyab" (5717), then "Shelta Shhimthikle Shhu khen" (5718) and "Shingkun" (5719). *The transliterations* are fifteen: "sra 'bras" (5687), "se rgod" (5692), "srad dkar" (5693), "sa rdzi ka" (5697), "se 'bra" (5701), "seng ldeng" (5705), "gser gyi me tog" (5707), "gser gye phud bu" (5709), "gser skud" (5713), "se ba'i me tog" (5715), "bse yab" (5721), "shel ta" (5722), "zhim thig le" (5723), "zhu mkhan" (5724) and "shing kun" (5725). *The scientific names* are, in order: "Syzygium cummi" and "Rosa macrophylla" (both on line 5727), "Oxytropis kansuensis" (5732), "Ustilago nuda" (5736), "Punica granatum L." (5736), "Acacia catechu" (5737), "Herpetospermum pedunculosum" (5743), "Luffa aegyptiaca" (5745), "Lethariella semanderi" (5749), "Rosa branonii" (5753), "Chaenomeles speciosa" (5757), "Pinus sp." and "Rabdosia ragosa" (both on 5758), "Symplocus paniculata" (5764) and "Ferula asafetida" (5765). That is fifteen. *The English names* are fifteen slots, two of them dashes: "Black plum" (5729), "Rose" (5730), "-" (5734), "Com ergot" (5739), "Pomegranite" (5740), "Catechu tree" (5741), "Golden flower" (5743), "Sponge gourd" (5747), "Golden dodder" (5751), "Musk rose" (5755), "Tibetan quince" (5760), "-" (5761), "Rabdosia" (5762), "Sapphire berry" (5767), "Asafoetida" (5768).

Counted in order, "Sae kar" is third in the phonetic names and "srad dkar" third in the transliterations, and *Oxytropis kansuensis* is third in the scientific names, with a dash in third place of the English names. The count can be checked at the places where Clark's lines allow it. The first slot, "Sa dre" (*sra 'bras*), stands against *Syzygium* and "Black plum", and Clark's *sra.hbras* (line 952) lists *Eugenia jambolana* among its binomials. The second, "Saegoe" (*se rgod*), stands against *Rosa macrophylla* and "Rose", and Clark's *se.rgod* (line 1042) is "Wild Rose (female type): Rosa macrophylla". The fourth, "Sazika" (*sa rdzi ka*), stands against *Ustilago nuda* and "Com ergot", and Clark's *Sardzika* (line 1117) is "Corn Smut: Ustilago sp.". The fifth, "Sedu" (*se 'bra*), stands against *Punica granatum* and "Pomegranite", and Clark's *se.hbru* (line 909) is "Pomegranate: Punicagranatum". From the sixth slot on, "Sengdeng" stands against *Acacia catechu* (Clark's *seng.ldeng*, line 985), "Sergyi metok" against *Herpetospermum* (Clark's *gser.gyi.me.tog*, line 899, *Herpetospermum caudigerum*), "Ser kue" against the golden dodder (Clark's *gser.skud*, line 1029, "gold thread"), "Seyab" against *Chaenomeles* (Clark's *bse.yab*, line 908), "Shhu khen" against *Symplocus paniculata* (Clark's *zhu.mkhan*, line 1086) and "Shingkun" against asafoetida (Clark's *shing.kun*, line 918). Every slot on both sides of the third is therefore fixed by Clark, and the third scientific name is the only one left for "Sae kar". The pairing remains positional and was not printed in one row. *A difference from I_1240.* I_1240 (*Astragalus yunnaneansis*) counts the same block as fifteen phonetic names against sixteen scientific-name entries "counting one dash", and so does not rely on the pairing. This entry reads the dash at line 5734 as a placeholder in the English column (as the dash at line 5761 and the dashes at lines 5679 and 5680 in the "R" block are), not as a scientific name; on that reading the four runs are each fifteen long and the third place is secure. Both entries name the same line (5732) as third. The point is flagged for the editor. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text (line 3464).* Chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225), in the herbal run that opens at line 3428: "Saekar is a diuretic which drains generalized edema and ascites." The printed index does not list "Saekar" (the neighbouring entries are "Saegoe 206, 208, 220" and "Sazika 211", lines 7481 and 7482). The name is not in the taste groups of chapter 19 and not in the rejuvenation chapter. The sentence gives no Latin name, no part used and no preparation.

*Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1098).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 ("The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines", line 792), within "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). It reads: "srad.dkar.wa: type of Gum Tragacanth: Astragalus yunnaneansis: Oxytropis oxyphylla: [has ten names and is of three types, white, black and byehu.srad.ma. The superior or white type grows in sandy areas, has a white, furry leaf and stalk and a bright white flower which cures phlegm disorders. The common type has a bluish hue and] purges first and third stages of oedema. [byehu.srad.ma] has a leaf and flower similar to the above types, a pouch-like seed-pod, small, hard seeds and dries lymph [accumulations]." Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries. The ten names, the three types, the description, and the action on phlegm disorders are therefore the commentary layer; "purges first and third stages of oedema" is outside the brackets and so is the root-text layer as the brackets mark it. The marking of the last sentence is irregular (only the name and the word "accumulations" are in brackets). The Codex reports it as printed. Clark's name index lists *srad.dkar.wa* (line 1768) and his Latin index *Oxytropis oxyphylla* (line 1621) and *Astragalus yunnaneansis* (line 1560). The spelling "yunnaneansis" is Clark's.

**Overlap.** I_1240 (*Astragalus yunnaneansis*, tag FMT) treats the same Clark line (1098) and the same Men-Tsee-Khang sentence (3464) under Clark's first binomial, and quotes this glossary line (5732) as the third scientific name of the block. This entry and I_1240 are one Tibetan drug under two binomials and are not merged. I_1023 (*Astragalus tragacantha*, Dioscorides 3-23) is a Greek gum tragacanth entry from another tradition and does not cite the Tibetan texts. No other entry on *Oxytropis kansuensis* was found. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Oxytropis* and *Astragalus* are genera of the legume family (Fabaceae), that gum tragacanth is a plant gum from certain *Astragalus* species, and that some plants of both genera are generally known as livestock poisons ("locoweeds"). Nothing in the lines read says that this species is one of them, or what the Tibetan drug contained. No statement about its composition is made. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Men-Tsee-Khang records, under a name read by position, a diuretic that "drains generalized edema and ascites" (line 3464), and Clark records, for the same name under other binomials, a drug that "purges first and third stages of oedema" (line 1098). These are the texts' own claims and not Codex protocols. Oedema and ascites are signs of conditions that need medical assessment, and the Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, name no part used (Clark describes leaf, stalk, flower, pod and seeds and says nothing of a part taken) and give no quantity, frequency or duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Tibetan terms ("generalized edema", "ascites", "first and third stages of oedema") are not measured outcomes, and the species is uncertain between three, so no modern finding can be set beside them. It is a general, well-established fact that oedema and ascites can be signs of heart, kidney or liver disease.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica classes this drug by what it does: it drains oedema and ascites (Men-Tsee-Khang, under the name read by position) or purges stages of oedema (Clark). The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. A search of both Tibetan texts for the drug's names found it only in the lines cited above. It is not in the rejuvenation chapter (chapter 23 in both editions), and Clark's materia medica names other drugs as essence-extraction ingredients (for example lines 853, 971 and 1144), none of them this one. Its framing is therapeutic only, and it attaches to the Tibetan name and not securely to this binomial.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a diuretic or purgative for oedema in the Tibetan texts, with no preparation and no dose, and the species is the glossary's by a positional reading. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. Oedema and ascites need medical assessment. The identity is uncertain, no preparation or dose is given, and because some plants of this genus are generally known as livestock poisons, no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
