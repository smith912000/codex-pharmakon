**Name list.** `_sources/badianus.csv` (the Gates 1939 name list) has no row spelled *xachiocotzotl*. Line 70 reads "Copal-xocotl, Cyrtocarpa procera, plant, Badianus (Gates 1939), yes". That is a near-match to the second name in the spine's title, *copalxocotl*, but it gives a different species in a different genus (*Cyrtocarpa procera*, not *Liquidambar styraciflua*) and is itself flagged id-uncertain. Whether line 70 and this row are one plant, and which tree is meant, was not established by anything read. The spine's identification of *copalxocotl* with sweet gum may be an error. [USER: verify]

**Badianus (BM).** The row says that the plant is attested in the Badianus manuscript of 1552 (folio 48v), and gives the Nahuatl name *xachiocotzotl*, with *copalxocotl* as a second name, the English gloss "sweet gum" and the species *Liquidambar styraciflua*. It names no preparation, no use and no complaint. [USER: verify]

**Project files not used.** `BM_Original_Latin_Nahuatl.md` and `BM_Overview.md` contain no entry for *xachiocotzotl* or *copalxocotl*, and nothing from them is used.

**Other traditions.** The Dioscorides text and the Dioscorides, Ebers, Sushruta, rGyud-bzhi and Shennong name lists were searched for *Liquidambar* and sweet gum. No locus was found. The Dioscorides *storax* chapter (*Styrax*) concerns a tree of a different genus and is not a locus for this row.

**Overlap note.** Existing Volume I entries that touch the same resin: I_227 (Storax, *Liquidambar orientalis*; tags CM, DMM), which mentions *L. styraciflua* only as the American storax used as a substitute, and I_691 (Storax, *Styrax officinalis*).
