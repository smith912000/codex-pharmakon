# Anagyris foetida — Anaguris [Onaguris] (Dioscorides 3-167)

## *Anagyris foetida (the translators' suggestion; "Bean Clover, Stinking Wood")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. **Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), line 7932 (chapter heading) and the following lines.

*3-167. ANAGURIS [ONAGURIS]*, SUGGESTED: "Anagyris foetida — Bean Clover, Stinking Wood". The description reads: "Anagyris is a shrub similar to a tree, its leaves and stems very similar to agnus castus [1-135], with an extremely strong scent. The flowers are similar to brassica, the seed in long little horns shaped like kidneys, variously coloured, solid. They harden when the grapes ripen." Uses: "The tender leaves of this (pounded into small pieces and applied) repress oedema. A teaspoonful is given to drink in passum [raisin wine] for asthma, as well as for expulsion of the afterbirth and menstrual flow, and as an abortifacient. It is given with wine for headaches. It is hung as an amulet on those who have hard labour [in giving birth], yet one must at once (after the woman's delivery) take off the amulet and put it away. The juice of the root dissolves and ripens. The seed (eaten) encourages vomiting excessively. It is also called anagyros, acopon, or agnacopum." The chapter is not marked POISONOUS by the translators. The name index prints ANAGURIS at p.535.

### Active Components

Anagyris is a legume (Fabaceae) and, as a genus, is generally known to contain quinolizidine alkaloids, among them anagyrine and cytisine. The constituents of the plant Dioscorides describes were not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records the plant as a poultice for swelling, a drink for asthma, headache, retained afterbirth and delayed menses, an abortifacient, an emetic seed, and a birth amulet that must be removed after delivery. These are the text's claims. The Codex gives no protocol: the plant is toxic, and the abortifacient and emetic uses are dangerous.

### Preparation & Dosing

3-167 gives "a teaspoonful" of the pounded leaf "to drink in passum [raisin wine]" for asthma, expulsion of the afterbirth and menstrual flow, and as an abortifacient; with wine for headaches; the leaf pounded and applied for oedema; the seed eaten as a strong emetic. The amulet is to be removed at once after delivery. Beyond the teaspoonful for the leaf, no dose is given (none for root juice or seed). [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Quinolizidine alkaloids such as cytisine act on nicotinic receptors and are toxic in quantity. This is a general statement about the genus and no study of *Anagyris foetida* was opened. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter offers a cultivator is the discipline of attention that the cultivation spec describes for this tradition: how to know the plant by its parts, where it grows, what it is called, and what is claimed for it. 3-167 supplies the shrub's look (like agnus castus, a very strong scent, kidney-shaped variously coloured seed in long horns that harden when the grapes ripen), its uses and its names. It assigns no status to the plant. Dioscorides records the amulet and tells the reader to take it off at once after delivery; recording is not endorsing. The identification with *A. foetida* is the translators' suggestion. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a toxic shrub in the general knowledge of its genus, and the honest practice is to recognise it from Dioscorides' description and leave it alone.

### Contraindications

Plants of this genus contain toxic alkaloids, and the seeds in particular have caused poisoning. Dioscorides' own uses (abortifacient, strong emetic) are hazardous. Do not use in pregnancy. None of the traditional uses above is advice, and no home preparation is safe. [USER: verify]
