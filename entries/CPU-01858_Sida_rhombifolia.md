# Sida rhombifolia — Ativala; Ati-vala, Ati-bala (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Sida rhombifolia, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Sida  rhomblfolia.", line 31348) against the Sanskrit name Ativala (line 31305). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print Ativala, Ati-vala and Ati-bala as a drug beside Vala, and give no Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium"; the printed form is "rhomblfolia") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 407 gives the name as "Ativala", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31305; id line 31348)" and the Latin as "Sida  rhomblfolia.". The key is the Sanskrit name Ativala, which is the only Sanskrit name the appendix sets against this Latin. The appendix prints no second name for *Sida rhombifolia*: a search of the Latin column for *Sida* finds "Sida Cordifolia." twice (lines 31347 and 31444), "Sida  rhomblfolia." once (31348), "Sida  Rhomboidea." once (32284) and "Sida  spierosa.     Graveolens." once (32422).

**The pairing, checked by counting.** The page that opens with the number "66" (line 31238) prints thirty-five Sanskrit names (lines 31244 to 31309, with Ananta and Anantamula bracketed as one) and thirty-five Latin entries (lines 31312 to 31350). Ativala is the thirty-third name and "Sida rhomblfolia." the thirty-third Latin entry. The neighbours agree: Atasi stands against "Linum Usitatissimum." (line 31346), Atipatra against "Sida  Cordifolia." (31347), Ativala against "Sida  rhomblfolia." (31348), Ativisha against "Aconitum  heterophyllum." (31349) and Atma-gupta against "Macuna  pruriens." (31350). The column is firm at both ends. Atipatra, not Ativala, stands against *Sida cordifolia* on this page, and the second *Sida cordifolia* line (31444) belongs to Bala-bhadra, so the three names Ativala, Atipatra and Bala-bhadra are three different pairings in the appendix. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Ativala, Ati-vala, Atibala, Ati-bala and OCR variants (the translator's diacritics come through as "Ati-vald", and the final vowel of Ativala is read as "i" in one place), and the hits were read. The name is always listed beside Vala and never alone, and in the lines read the translator never glosses it.

*The rasayana chapter (Chikitsa ch.27).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch27.txt` lines 4 to 9 open the chapter on "elixirs and rejuvinators of the human organism which will make it invulnerable to the inroads of any disease or of decay (Sarvopaghata-shamaniya-Rasayanam)", and its first metrical text (lines 11 to 22) says a physician should prescribe a tonic (*Rasayana*) in youth and middle age only after the system has been cleansed by a Sneha and by emetics and purgatives. The *Vala-Kalpa* follows (lines 153 to 169): "A Pala or half a Pala weight of the (powdered) roots of the Vala should be well-stirred in cow's milk and taken (every day) and the patient or the user should not be allowed to stir out of his room as prescribed before", with a meal of boiled rice, milk and clarified butter after the medicine is digested, and the claim that "Premature old age and senile decay would be arrested for a period of twelve years by taking this elixir continuously for twelve days ... whereas an extension of its course to a hundred days would add a hundred summers to the duration of his youthful age". Then, lines 169 to 179: "Ati-vala, Naga-vala, Vidari, Satavari may be similarly taken for the same purposes, with this distinction that the (powders of) the Ati-vala should be taken with water, those of the Naga-vala, with honey, whereas Viddri and the Satavari powders should be taken with milk. The regimen of diet and conduct as well as the beneficial results produced therefrom should be the same (as from the use of Vala). The present remedies are recommended to persons seeking strength or suffering from an attack of Haematemesis or Haematochezia." (The OCR prints "Ati-valai" and "Ati-vald"; the final "i" is read as punctuation.) A footnote signed "D. R." glosses the last two words as "Suffering from consumption and hoemoptysis".

*The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa ch.26) and the other rasayana chapters (ch.28 to 30).* A search found no Ativala in any of them.

*Other chapters, read for the line only.* Ativala is named as one of the drugs "of sweet taste" for a medicated compound in `SS_Sutra_ch15.txt` line 664 ("Vala, Ativala, Nagavala"); in the list that "soothes" the deranged Vayu, `SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` line 88; and among the drugs "generally included within the Madhura group", `SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` line 297. It is in oils and plasters for Maha-Vata-Vyadhi in `SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt` lines 117, 140 and 316 (the oil "cooked with the decoction of ... Vala, Ati-vala and Trina-pancha-mula"; the five Payasas as a hot poultice; a plaster pasted with the milk of a she-goat); in an oil taken internally for goitre, `SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt` line 466; in plasters for Nyachchha, Vyanga and Nilika, `SS_Chikitsa_ch20.txt` line 230; in a Taila for a Vasti, `SS_Chikitsa_ch37.txt` line 109; in an oil in the chapter on diseases of the pupil and crystalline lens, `SS_Uttara_ch17.txt` line 269; in a ghrita for suppression of urine, `SS_Uttara_ch58.txt` line 306; in an Agada against the poison of a Visvambhara insect, `SS_Kalpa_ch08.txt` line 308 (spelled "Ati-bala", beside Bala); and in an Asthapana enema for a pregnant woman, `SS_Sharira_ch10.txt` line 78 ("a decoction of Vadara mixed with Vala, Ativala, Satapushpa, Palala (flesh), milk, cream of curd ..."). The sentence at Sutra 15 line 663 reads, as printed, "A case of patent obesity should be checked with a medicated compound", and follows an account of emaciation; the Codex reports it as printed. ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Sida* is a genus of the mallow family (Malvaceae), herbs and shrubs with mucilaginous tissues, and some species of the genus are reported to contain ephedrine-type alkaloids. That is a statement about the genus and not a finding of the Sushruta text, and which species the name Ativala stands for is open. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the name the chapters print, the drug (if it is this plant) is a root-powder tonic taken with water in the Sushruta rasayana chapter (Chikitsa 27), a drug of sweet taste in a medicated compound (Sutra 15), a member of a Vayu-soothing list (Sutra 39), and an ingredient in oils, plasters, enemas and an antidote (Chikitsa 5, 18, 20, 37, Uttara 17, 58, Kalpa 8, Sharira 10). The Codex gives no protocol and no dose, and none of these is offered as a recommendation.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparation the rasayana chapter gives for Ativala is the powder taken with water, "similarly" to the Vala preparation (Chikitsa 27, lines 169 to 172), under the regimen of the Vala-Kalpa. The only quantity in that passage belongs to Vala: "A Pala or half a Pala weight of the (powdered) roots of the Vala" in cow's milk (lines 153 to 155). The text does not restate it for Ativala. Elsewhere Ativala goes into oils, plasters and enemas without a quantity of its own. No dose of Ativala's own is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** no *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed under Ativala. The group lines carry the only qualities: it is among drugs "of sweet taste" (Sutra 15, line 664), in a list "possessed of the general virtue of soothing ... the deranged Vayu" (Sutra 39, lines 88 to 96) and "generally included within the Madhura group" (Sutra 42, lines 297 to 300).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(a) Attested for the Sanskrit name Ativala: named in the rasayana chapter as a drug with a stated use.* Chikitsa 27, the chapter on elixirs and rejuvenators (*Rasayana*), says (lines 169 to 179, page 519) that Ati-vala "may be similarly taken for the same purposes" as the Vala-Kalpa, with water, under the same regimen of diet and conduct, and recommends it "to persons seeking strength". The status rests on the Sanskrit name in that chapter. It does not rest on the Latin name, which comes from Bhishagratna's appendix by position. The chapter says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for the drug, and none is asserted here. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) does not name it. The text makes the course conditional on prior cleansing (Chikitsa 27, lines 11 to 22), and the Codex repeats that condition. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** In the Sushruta scheme the drug named Ativala is a rasayana taken under a physician, after cleansing, as a powder in water and with the regimen of the Vala-Kalpa, which the text spells out only under Vala. The Latin identification is a translator's appendix pairing and the chapters do not confirm it; nothing here supports gathering or taking a plant on the strength of the Latin name.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for Ativala. The text itself makes the rasayana course conditional on cleansing by emetics and purgatives, and speaks of seclusion in a room for the Vala-Kalpa; these are not minor steps and should not be skipped on the strength of this entry. The identity is open, and some species of the genus *Sida* are reported to contain stimulant alkaloids, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read, apart from the Asthapana enema for a pregnant woman at Sharira 10, which is a physician's procedure and is not recommended here. [USER: verify]
