# Multiflora rose hips — 营实 *Ying Shi* (Shennong bencao jing), also 蔷薇子 and 墙薇 *Qiang Wei*; Yang 1998 prints "Ying Shi (Semen Rosae Multiflorae)"
## *The fruit (hips) of the multiflora rose, Rosa multiflora Thunb., in the standard modern identification, of high confidence at species level because Tao Hongjing's note, as Sun quotes it, equates the drug with the seed of 墙薇 (the wall rose) and Yang prints the same Latin* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`) and, as a second witness attributed to him, Yang's 1998 English translation (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`). The identification is ours. (The spine carries only other roses, from other traditions, and a genus placeholder "Rosa sp.", CPU-02283, whose locus is Yang's printing of this same monograph, line 3158 below.)

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the upper (上) classic of Sun's arrangement, reads 味酸，温 ("sour, warm"). It governs 痈疽恶创 ("welling abscesses, deep-rooted abscesses and malign sores"), 结肉跌筋 ("flesh bound up, sprained sinews"), 败创 ("rotten sores"), 热气 ("heat qi"), 阴蚀不疗 ("genital erosion that does not heal"), and "frees the joints" (利关节). The aliases are 一名墙薇，一名墙麻，一名牛棘 ("wall rose", "wall flax", "ox bramble"), and the habitat 生川谷 ("grows in river valleys"). There is no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 营实 *ying shi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 813.)

**Wu Pu (3rd century).** 《吴普》曰 gives the plant, not the fruit: 蔷薇，一名牛勒，一名牛膝，一名蔷薇，一名山枣 ("rose; also called ox-rein, ox-knee, rose, mountain jujube"), with the source mark 《御览》 (Yulan). The repeated 蔷薇 and the odd 牛膝 are as printed. (Line 815.)

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰：一名牛勒，一名蔷蘼，一名山棘，生零陵及蜀郡，八月、九月采，阴干: aliases 牛勒, 蔷蘼 and 山棘 ("mountain bramble"), habitat Lingling and Shu commanderies, "gathered in the eighth and ninth months, dried in the shade". (Line 817.)

**Sun's note (案).** Sun quotes Tao Hongjing: 即是墙薇子, "it is just the seed of 墙薇". (Line 819.)

**Yang 1998 (second witness).** Yang files this drug under "Herbs: Inferior Class" (PDF page 85, line 3158), not the upper class. He prints: "Ying Shi (Semen Rosae Multiflorae) is sour and warm. It mainly treats welling and flat abscesses, malign sores, flesh binding, sprained sinews, putrefying sores, heat qi, and intractable genital erosion. It [also] disinhibits the joints. Its other name is Qiang Wei (Wall Rose). Another name is Qiang Ma (Wall Flax). It is also called Niu Ji (Cow Bramble). It grows in rivers and valleys." His footnote 192 (lines 3173-3175) adds: "Semen Rosae Multiflorae is used to treat thorns in the flesh, fish bones stuck in the throat, etc. The translator suspects that the indications explained in the text are those of Radix Rosae Multiflorae." The two witnesses therefore disagree on the class (upper in Sun's order, inferior in Yang's), and Yang doubts that the printed indications belong to the fruit.

**Identity.** The name 营实 does not itself say rose. The equation with the wall rose comes from Tao Hongjing, cited in Sun's note, and from the Wu Pu paragraph, whose heading is 蔷薇. The aliases "wall rose", "wall flax" and "ox bramble" all point to a thorny, climbing or hedge plant. The species level (*Rosa multiflora*) is the standard modern one and is not in the sources. ### Active Components

*Rosa multiflora* is a thorny Rosaceae shrub with small clustered white flowers and small red hips. Rose hips in general contain sugars, organic acids, vitamin C, carotenoids and flavonoids. The composition of this species' hips was not retrieved for this entry, and none is asserted. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records a sour, warm fruit for abscesses, sores, sprained sinews and genital erosion, and for the joints. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends none. Abscesses and non-healing sores need medical assessment, and the claims have not been tested in sources retrieved.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only handling in the sources is the Mingyi layer's harvest: gathered in the eighth and ninth months and dried in the shade (八月、九月采，阴干). No preparation, route, quantity or course is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Rose hips are a general food source of vitamin C and are eaten or made into syrup and tea in many places. *Rosa multiflora* is also known as a hedge and rootstock plant, and as an invasive weed where it has been introduced. Whether the hips relieve any of the conditions the Classic names has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* Sun lists 营实 among the upper-grade herbs (the upper herb list at line 45 includes it, ahead of 天名精), and the preface (line 43) says upper drugs nourish life and are taken long without harm. On the grading rule used for this series, placement in the upper classic is itself the ground for (a). The monograph itself, however, has no 久服 clause and no 轻身 or 不老 promise: it lists abscesses, sores, sprains and genital erosion, which are the complaints of a drug that treats disease. Yang places it in the inferior class, where the preface says drugs treat disease and are not for long use; that placement does not count toward the grade, and the Codex follows Sun. The attestation is of Sun's upper-class placement and not of a life-nourishing promise in the paragraph. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 营实 *ying shi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 813; Yang 1998 line 3158.)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A fruit for sores, treated as an upper-grade drug in one reconstruction and as an inferior one in the other. If used at all, it is as these texts use it, for a stated complaint and a short course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Abscesses, spreading sores and joint injuries.** Need medical assessment.
- **Identification.** Several roses and brambles bear similar hips; do not eat wild hips on a guess. The hairs inside rose hips can irritate the mouth and gut.
- **Foreign bodies.** Yang's note on thorns and fish bones is historical record only; a swallowed bone needs medical care.
- **Pregnancy.** No statement is made in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
