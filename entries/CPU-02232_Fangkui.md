# Fangkui — 防葵 *fangkui*, "Fang Kui"; also 梨盖 *ligai*, "Pear Cover"

## *An umbelliferous root drug of the Shennong upper classic, identified in modern works as Peucedanum japonicum Thunb.; the identification is ours, of medium confidence, and disputed, because the old sources describe the plant only loosely and Sun Xingyan records an old confusion with 野狼毒 (wild wolf-bane)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Fang kui* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The spine row it replaces was the placeholder "Peucedanum sp.", added on 13 October from Yang's English translation at genus level.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 防葵 in the upper classic (卷一 上经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 383, 味 paragraph; name line 381). The Shennong text proper reads: 味辛，寒。主疝瘕肠泄，膀胱热结，溺不下。咳逆，温疟，癫痫，惊邪狂走。久服，坚骨髓、益气、轻身。一名梨盖。生川谷。In English: "Flavour acrid, cold. Governs mounting-and-conglomeration (疝瘕) and intestinal diarrhoea; heat bound in the bladder, with urine not passing; counterflow cough, warm malaria (温疟), epilepsy (癫痫), fright-evil with manic running (惊邪狂走). Taken long (久服), it firms the bone marrow (坚骨髓), boosts the qi (益气) and lightens the body (轻身). Another name is Li Gai. It grows in rivers and valleys." These are translations of the classic's terms and are not rewritten into modern diagnoses.

**Wu Pu layer** (《吴普》, third century; line 385). Wu Pu gives the drug as 房葵 and lists a string of aliases: Li Gai, Jue Li, Fang Yuan, Chen Cao ("morning herb"), Li Ru and Fang Gai. He then sets out how the authorities he quotes judged the flavour: "Shennong: acrid, slightly cold; Tongjun and Bian Que: non-toxic; Qi Bo, Lei Gong and the Yellow Emperor: bitter, non-toxic" (神农：辛，小寒；桐君、扁鹊：无毒；岐伯、雷公、黄帝：苦，无毒). Wu Pu's quotation of "Shennong" has "slightly cold" where the classic as reconstructed has "cold"; the two are not reconciled here. Wu Pu is also the only layer that describes the plant: "stem and leaf like the mallow (葵), black-yellow above; in the second month the root is born, the root as large as the *jiegeng* (桔梗, balloon-flower root), red and white within; in the sixth month the flower is white, in the seventh and eighth months the fruit is white; the root is gathered on the third day of the third month" (茎叶如葵，上黑黄。二月生根，根大如桔梗，根中红白。六月，花白，七月、八月，实白，三月三日采根). The citation to the *Yulan* is Sun's.

**Mingyi bielu layer** (《名医》, later additions; line 387). More aliases (Fang Ci, Jue Li, Nong Guo, Li Ru, Fang Gai); the habitat, "grows at Linzi, and at Songgao, Taishan and Shaoshi" (生临淄，及嵩高太山少室); and the harvest: the root is gathered on the third day of the third month and dried in the sun (三月三日采根，曝干).

**Sun Xingyan's note** (案, line 389). One line only: the *Bowuzhi* says that 防葵 "is similar to wild wolf-bane" (防葵，与野狼毒相似). Sun adds no comment.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 1825) prints "Fang Kui (Radix Peucedani Japonici) is acrid and cold" and renders the indications, the long-taking clause and the alias in the same sense as above. The Latin is Yang's gloss. No footnote is attached to the entry.

**Identity.** The Codex's name, *Peucedanum japonicum*, rests on the later standard identification and on Yang's gloss. The old text does not settle it. What the sources on disk support is a drug whose root looks like the balloon-flower's (Wu Pu), with white flowers, and which in the *Bowuzhi*'s time was close enough to another plant, 野狼毒, to be confused with it. The Codex does not resolve the dispute.

### Active Components

No analytical source was retrieved for this plant, and no constituent is asserted. As a general fact, *Peucedanum* is a genus of the carrot family (Apiaceae), and plants of that family commonly carry coumarins and volatile oils. That is a statement about the family and not a finding about this drug, whose identity is itself uncertain. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic records an acrid, cold root for a list of internal complaints (mounting, diarrhoea, bound bladder heat, malarial fevers, epilepsy, frenzy) and, if taken long, for "firming the bone marrow", boosting the qi and lightening the body. The Codex gives no protocol and does not turn these terms into modern diagnoses. The identity of the plant is disputed, and a similar-looking plant is named as toxic in the same classic (see Contraindications). It should not be gathered or used on the strength of this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources give the harvest only: the root, gathered on the third day of the third month and dried in the sun (Mingyi layer, line 387). No part for use other than the root, no preparation, no quantity, frequency or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Divine Farmer's Classic).** 味辛，寒, "acrid, cold" (line 383).
- **Wu Pu layer.** Shennong: acrid, slightly cold; Tongjun and Bian Que: non-toxic; Qi Bo, Lei Gong and the Yellow Emperor: bitter, non-toxic (line 385). The authorities differ on the flavour, and Wu Pu records the difference without settling it.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the upper classic (上经), which the preface describes as "to nourish life, in correspondence with heaven; non-toxic; taken in quantity or taken long, it does not harm people" (上药...为君，主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人; line 43), and its 味 paragraph carries its own 久服 clause: "Taken long, it firms the bone marrow, boosts the qi, and lightens the body" (久服，坚骨髓、益气、轻身; *Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 防葵 *fangkui*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 383). 轻身, "lighten the body", is the classic's term and is not translated into a pharmacological effect. The Wu Pu layer calls it non-toxic on the authority of Tongjun and Bian Que (line 385), but records Shennong's flavour as "slightly cold". The classification is the classic's; the status is attached to the classic's 防葵, whose botanical identity is the open question above. The later layers add no cultivation claim.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade entry in the classic's own scheme, but an entry whose plant is disputed and whose neighbour in the *Bowuzhi* is a toxic root. Nothing here supports taking a plant on the name alone. A cultivator can read it as a record of what the classic promised for long taking, and should not act on it.

### Contraindications

- **Identity and look-alike.** Sun's note records that the *Bowuzhi* held 防葵 and 野狼毒 to be similar (line 389). In the same classic 野狼毒 is a lower-classic drug (line 3429) described as able to "kill flying birds and running beasts" (杀飞鸟、走兽), and a quoted passage lists it first among five poisons with their antidotes (line 4147). Sun gives the likeness alone; the sources read say nothing of how a mix-up arose or whether one was made. Misidentified root material is a real hazard. Do not collect or use roots of umbellifers on the strength of a name.
- **Toxicity statements disagree.** Wu Pu records some authorities as "non-toxic" and others as "bitter, non-toxic" (line 385); the classic itself prints no toxicity statement.
- **Serious conditions.** Epilepsy, malaria and frenzy need medical care. The classic's indications are historical record.
