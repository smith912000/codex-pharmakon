# Firefly — 荧火 *yinghuo* (Shennong bencao jing), "glowing fire"

## *The firefly, a soft-bodied beetle that makes light; our identification (medium confidence) is the family Lampyridae with Luciola as a candidate genus, the species not established, and Guo Pu's gloss quoted by Sun ("flies at night, with fire under the belly") is the only description in the entry* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Shennong bencao jing (SBJ).** The firefly is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The identification is ours, from the name and Guo Pu's gloss. The source is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`); the entry stands in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 荧火 *yinghuo*, same file, line 4009).

**The Shennong text proper** (line 4009): "Flavour pungent, slightly warm. It governs brightening the eyes; fire sores and wounds of children; heat qi, *gu* poison, *guizhu*; it *tong shen* (通神). One name, 'night light' (夜光). It grows in ponds and marshes." (味辛，微温。主明目，小儿火创伤，热气、蛊毒、鬼注，通神。一名夜光。生池泽。) Sun adds a bracketed note that the *Taiping yulan* quotes further names, "glittering" (熠耀) and "instant shining" (即照), and that the Daguan edition prints them in black characters. *Tong shen* is literally "to connect/communicate with the spirit" and the text does not explain it. I leave it untranslated beyond the literal sense. [USER: verify] It is unusual to find it in a lower-class entry, but it is not a *jiu fu* ("taken for long") clause and does not carry the status of one.

**The Wu Pu layer** (line 4011, from the *Yiwen leiju*): the names "night shining" (夜照), "glittering" (熠耀), "rescue fire" (救火), "*jingtian*" (景天), "holding fire" (据火) and "clasping fire" (挟火). The list includes *jingtian*, the same name as the stonecrop entry (I_1468) in this batch. No text read says why the two share a name.

**The Mingyi bielu layer** (line 4013): further names, "giving out light" (放光), "glittering" and "instant shining". "Grows on stepped ground (阶地). Collect on the seventh day of the seventh month; dry in the shade." (七月七日收，阴干。)

**Sun Xingyan's note (案, lines 4015 and 4017).** Sun quotes the *Shuowen* that *lin* (磷) is the ghost-fire from the blood of those who died in war and of cattle and horses; the *Erya*: "*yinghuo* is *jizhao*", with Guo Pu: "it flies at night, with fire under the belly" (夜飞，腹下有火); the *Mao shi*: "*yiyao* walks at night", with the commentary that *yiyao* is *lin* and *lin* is *yinghuo*; the *Yueling*: in the last month of summer "rotting grass turns into fireflies" (腐草化为荧), with Zheng Xuan's gloss that the firefly is the flying insect. Sun then argues about the characters and concludes that the *Yueling* phrase is a loan of sound for another character. In this note the firefly is also identified with ghost-fire, and Sun follows Mao Chang in doing so. I report this as a philological argument about the name, and it is Sun's own layer, not the classic's.

**Preface.** The lower class is "the left-hand envoy, mainly treating disease to correspond to earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken for long" (line 2935).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, a firefly's light comes from an enzyme reaction (luciferin and luciferase), which is cold light. Some fireflies carry defensive steroidal toxins; the species of this entry is not established, and none is asserted. Nothing more specific is stated in the texts. No analytical source was retrieved. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records the firefly for brightening the eyes, for burns or "fire sores and wounds" of children, for heat qi, *gu* poison and *guizhu*, and for *tong shen*. The Codex does not turn these into modern indications. Eye trouble and injuries to children need medical care. Wild fireflies are a declining group and are best left in place.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Mingyi layer gives the collection date (the seventh day of the seventh month) and shade-drying (line 4013). The Shennong paragraph gives no preparation and no dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Firefly light is a well-studied bioluminescent reaction and the enzymes are used in laboratory assays. That is a general statement. Whether a dried firefly acts on the eyes or on burns, as the text says, has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong).** One flavour and one nature are printed: pungent (辛), slightly warm (微温). Lower class by placement. No further quality is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry is in the lower classic, which the preface (line 2935) describes as mainly for treating disease, mostly toxic and not for long use. The paragraph has no *jiu fu* ("taken for long") clause. It does contain *tong shen* (通神), which I translate only literally ("connects with the spirit"); standing alone in a lower-class entry and without a long-taking clause, it is not graded as a cultivation status. The status is the lower class's: a remedy for a defined complaint, not for long use.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing for a cultivator here. The classic records a lower-class remedy with an unexplained *tong shen* clause, and gives no dose or course. Watching fireflies at night in a clean marsh is not a practice the text records either.

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity.** The lower class is "mostly toxic" and not for long use. Some fireflies carry defensive toxins. Do not swallow fireflies as a remedy. [USER: verify]
- **Eyes and children.** The uses named are for the eyes and for children; neither is a case for home treatment from a historical text.
- **Conservation.** Fireflies are declining in many regions because of habitat loss and light pollution. Do not collect them.
- **Pregnancy, medicines.** The text says nothing; no use is supported. [USER: verify]
