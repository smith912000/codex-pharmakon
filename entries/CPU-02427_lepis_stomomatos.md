# Lepis stomomatos (steel scale) — Lepis Stomomatos (LEPIS STOMOMATOS), Dioscorides 5-90

## *Scales or flakes of "stomoma", a material that Dioscorides says has the same strength as brass scales and is washed and stored the same way, but purges the bowels less; the translator offers no identification ("UNKNOWN: stoma is a little mouth"). The Codex working name "steel scale" comes from the list and from the first-pass check and is not in the chapter, so the identification is unresolved* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, marked "uncertain" in its curation. A search of `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` for "stomomatos", "stomoma" and "lepis" found this chapter, its parent (5-89) and the index entries, and nothing in another tradition on disk.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-90, *Lepis Stomomatos* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10267; name-index row in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` at row 863, translator identification "UNKNOWN: stoma is a little mouth", class guess mineral; list row in `_sources/dioscorides.csv` at line 817, "Lepis Stomomatos", mineral; the book index lists "LEPIS STOMOMATOS 787"). The whole chapter was read. It reads (line 10269):

"Scales [flakes] of stomoma have the same strength as scales [flakes] of brass, and their washing and storage are the same; but in purging the bowels they are less effective than that of brass."

The chapter thus gives: the material (scales or flakes of "stomoma"); a comparison (the same strength as brass scales); a method (their washing and storage are the same as for brass scales); and one difference (it is less effective than brass scales at purging the bowels). It gives nothing else.

**What it sends the reader to.** The comparison is to the chapter immediately before, 5-89, *Lepis* (translator's gloss "Brass Scales", line 10259). That chapter says that *squama* is also called *helitis*, is made from Cyprian brass nails, and is best when thick and shiny and "turns rusty when vinegar is sprinkled on it"; that it "is contractive and reduces the intensity of symptoms of noma", repressing putrefaction and forming new skins; that a decoction "taken as a drink with honey and water" expels fluids; that "some work it into a paste with meal and give it as a catapotium [pill]"; and that it is mixed with eye medicines "as it dries eyelids that are rough, removing rheum" (lines 10261-10265). It also gives a washing method (half a pound of dry brass scales stirred in a mortar with clear water, pressed, and the "flower of squama" collected; the remainder washed until no glueyness shows, covered with linen and left two days, then dried and put in a box; line 10266). These are cited here as the source of the comparison; the stomoma chapter itself says only that its washing and storage are "the same". It is not said which of the uses of brass scales the stomoma shares, apart from the bowel-purging, in which it is weaker. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The translator marks the material "UNKNOWN" and explains only that "stoma is a little mouth" (line 10268). The Codex working name "steel scale" and the first-pass note "iron scale" are not in the chapter. The text gives no indication that the scales are of iron or steel, only that they behave like brass scales. We do not adopt "steel scale" as an identification. ### Active Components

None can be stated from the chapter, because the material is not identified. Brass scales are, in general terms, flakes of copper alloy oxide formed on hot metal; copper compounds are irritant and toxic when swallowed. This is a general fact about brass scales, which the chapter makes the model, and it is not a claim about the identity of the stomoma.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** Dioscorides records scales of "stomoma" as a weaker version of brass scales for purging the bowels. The substance is unidentified, and the model it is compared to (brass scales) is a copper compound that is irritating to the gut. The Codex supplies no modern protocol and does not recommend swallowing metal scales.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation of its own is given. The chapter says its "washing and storage are the same" as for brass scales (line 10269). The brass-scale method is in 5-89 (line 10266), summarised above. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any quantity from this entry.** [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Metal scales:** copper and brass compounds are irritant and toxic when swallowed; do not ingest them.
- **Identity:** the material is unidentified; do not substitute iron, steel or any other scale on the strength of the Codex working name.
