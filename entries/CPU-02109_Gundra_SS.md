**Single-text entry: named only in the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, in a list.** The row's locus is Bhishagratna Vol. I line 15401 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`), which is also line 60 of `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`. **Sushruta (SS).** The line reads: "The Viratarvadi Group:— The drugs known as Virataru, the two kinds of Sahachara, Darbha, Vrikshadani, Gundra, Ela [OCR: I\'ala], Kusha, Kasha, Ashmabhedaka, Agnimantha, Morata, Vasuka, Vasira, Bhalluka, Kuruntaka, Indivara, Kapotavanka, and Shvadanstra enter into the composition of the group known as the Viratarvadi." The chapter opens by saying that the drugs "are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (the chapter heading, "Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam", is the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties). The group's metrical text (line 15409 on) reads: "The group subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and" and, after a page break in the OCR, "proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles." One word after "Vata and" is lost at the page break in this e-text. [USER: verify against the page.]

The drug is named in the group and nowhere else in the lines read. The text prints no description of the plant, no part used, no habitat and no property for Gundra on its own. No ancient commentary and no gloss from the translator is printed beside the name in the lines read, so the identification rests on the name alone.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The text puts the name into a group whose combined action is said to subdue deranged Vata and to be curative in stone (*ashmari*), gravel (*sharkara*) and difficult or obstructed urination. It says nothing of Gundra alone. The Codex does not translate these terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and recommends no use.
