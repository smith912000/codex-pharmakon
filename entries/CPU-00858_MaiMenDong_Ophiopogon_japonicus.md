# Mai Men Dong — Mondo Grass, Dwarf Lilyturf, Maidong, Lily-turf Tuber

## *Ophiopogon japonicus* (Thunb.) Ker Gawl. | Family: Asparagaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SBJ, SS
**Energetic Classification:** Cold and moist (2nd degree) — a moistening refrigerant that replenishes depleted humours
**Training Phase:** Restoration / Yin-Consolidation (the rehydrating, tissue-rebuilding phase after depletion or febrile burn-out)

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Shennong / Chinese Materia Medica (SBJ):** The pale, spindle-shaped tuberlets of Mai Men Dong ("wheat-gate winter," so named because the plant stays green through winter and the tubers resemble grains of wheat) are among the oldest continuously used Chinese tonics. The herb is classed in the earliest strata of the Chinese materia medica tradition attributed to Shennong as a superior-grade nourishing drug — one taken over long periods to "quiet the centre," moisten the Lung, and settle the Heart. Classical indication centres on *yin* depletion: dry unproductive cough, a parched mouth and throat, restlessness with a dry tongue, and the wasting-and-thirsting pattern (*xiao ke*) that maps onto what later physicians recognised as diabetes-like states. It is a component of enduring formulae such as Sheng Mai San ("Generate the Pulse Powder"), given for collapse of *qi* and *yin* with sweating and a feeble pulse.

**Avicenna / Canon of Medicine (CM):** Within the Greco-Arabic (Unani) frame that Avicenna systematised, a moistening, mildly cooling tuber of this character answers to remedies for *su'āl yābis* — the dry cough — and for the hectic dryness that accompanies wasting fevers. Ophiopogon itself is an East-Asian plant, so its appearance in Unani-influenced practice is via the trans-Asian materia medica exchange rather than the Mediterranean pharmacopoeia; the doctrinal logic, however, is identical: a cold-and-moist simple corrects a hot-and-dry distemper of the chest.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS):** In the Ayurvedic reading, a sweet (*madhura*), cooling, unctuous root of this type is a *rasāyana*-class rejuvenator and a *pitta*-pacifier, indicated for *shosha* (wasting/consumption) and *trishna* (morbid thirst). Ophiopogon is used in this role in the wider Himalayan and Sino-Indian herbal continuum, prized where the tissues are dry, the throat raw, and the fluids (*rasa* and *shukra*) depleted.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Every tradition read Mai Men Dong as a *fluid-restorer* — a plant that stores moisture in a swollen tuber through a dry winter and yields it back to a dry body. That symbolism is not idle: the tuber's pharmacological actives are precisely the water-holding steroidal saponins and the anti-inflammatory homoisoflavonoids that calm irritated, dry respiratory mucosa. The "winter-surviving evergreen" signature and the demonstrated protection of heart and vessel tissue converge on the same organ the classics assigned it — the Heart-Lung axis.

### Active Components

- **Steroidal saponins (ophiopogonins A–D and D′, ophiopojaponin C, liriopesides B):** the principal bioactive class, associated with cardioprotective, anti-inflammatory and vasoactive effects.
- **Homoisoflavonoids (methylophiopogonanone A and B, methylophiopogonone A and B):** a signature class for the genus, carrying anti-inflammatory, hypolipidaemic and cytoprotective activity.
- **Ruscogenin** and related sapogenins (variable, often low or absent depending on cultivar/origin).
- **Polysaccharides / fructans:** contribute the demulcent, moistening, mucosa-soothing quality.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Mai Men Dong is filed as a **Restoration-phase moistener**: the tool reached for when a training block, illness, or heat-stress has left the trainee dry, thirsty, hoarse, and hollow-pulsed. Its remit is the tail-end of depletion — rebuilding fluid and calming an over-fired system rather than stimulating. Practical use-cases: dry non-productive cough and throat rawness after respiratory illness; thirst and dryness following fever or exertional heat; and as a gentle cardiac-and-vascular support within *qi*-and-*yin* restoring formulae. It pairs conventionally with a *qi* tonic (ginseng-type) and an astringent (schisandra) — the Sheng Mai triad — rather than being used alone.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the chemistry and pharmacology of *Ophiopogon japonicus* substantially corroborate its classical fluid-restoring, chest-and-heart indications:

- Chen *et al.* (2023, *Molecules*) developed an HPLC-DAD-ELSD method quantifying nine constituents — four steroidal saponins (ophiopojaponin C, ophiopogonin D, liriopesides B, ophiopogonin D′) and four homoisoflavonoids (the methylophiopogonones/-nanones) — across Maidong from different origins, and showed the extracts and isolated homoisoflavonoids and ophiopogonin D′ exert cytotoxic (anticancer) activity against A2780 ovarian cancer cells, with composition varying markedly by geographic source [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules28031045).
- Li *et al.* (2020, *Braz J Med Biol Res*) reported that the homoisoflavonoid methylophiopogonanone A (MO-A) alleviated high-fat-diet-induced hyperlipidaemia in rats — reducing serum and hepatic lipids and improving lipoprotein enzyme activity — and noted in its rationale that MO-A had previously been shown to attenuate myocardial apoptosis and improve cerebral ischaemia/reperfusion injury, giving a mechanistic thread to the herb's traditional Heart-protecting reputation [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1590/1414-431X20199201).
- Hung *et al.* (2010, *Bioorg Med Chem Lett*) isolated new homoisoflavonoids from the roots and demonstrated anti-inflammatory activity via suppression of the chemokine eotaxin in bronchial epithelial (BEAS-2B) cells — a model of allergic airway inflammation — providing a direct biochemical correlate to the traditional use for irritated, dry airways [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bmcl.2010.03.043).

**Evidence grade: Moderate (preclinical).** Chemistry is well characterised and multiple in-vitro/animal studies support anti-inflammatory, cardioprotective and metabolic effects. Robust human clinical trials of the isolated herb are limited; most clinical data sit at the level of multi-herb formulae. Claims here are graded *traditional use with supportive preclinical mechanism*, not proven clinical efficacy.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Sweet, slightly bitter, cold. Enters Heart, Lung, and Stomach channels. Nourishes *yin*, moistens the Lung, clears Heart heat, generates fluids, relieves irritability.
- **Ayurveda (as used in the Sino-Himalayan continuum):** *Rasa* — sweet (*madhura*); *Guṇa* — heavy, unctuous (*guru, snigdha*); *Vīrya* — cooling (*shīta*); *Vipāka* — sweet (*madhura*); *pitta*-pacifying, *rasāyana*.
- **Greco-Arabic / Unani (Avicennan frame):** Cold and moist temperament, indicated against hot-dry distempers of the chest and the hectic dryness of wasting.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** *Ophiopogon japonicus* is an East-Asian plant and is not a simple of the Mediterranean or Perso-Arabic pharmacopoeia; it does not appear in the *Canon* corpus, and its use under a Greco-Arabic reading is a doctrinal mapping of a cold-and-moist tuber onto the treatment of *su'āl yābis* (dry cough) rather than an attested Unani drug. The tradition's strongest cultivation idea — the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture as a finite vital endowment, so that longevity is a matter of husbanding fluids rather than acquiring them — is a frame of the whole regimen and is not a status this tradition assigns to this species. No *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed. **(c) absent** as to any Unani cultivation status.

**Chinese — Bencao (SBJ).** Mai Men Dong 麥門冬 is one of the herbs of the earliest classical stratum, classed in the **upper grade** (上品) of the *Shennong Bencao Jing* — the grade of substances taken over long periods to nourish life (養生) rather than to attack disease, tied to "quieting the centre" and lightening the body (輕身) with prolonged use (Yang Shou-zhong, trans., *The Divine Farmer's Materia Medica / Shen Nong Ben Cao Jing*, Blue Poppy Press, 1998, lists 麥門冬 among the upper-class drugs). The upper-grade placement is itself the cultivation verdict: a conservational, long-term tonic within *yang sheng* 養生, not a short-course corrective. Note that the specifically *yin*-fluid-generating language (滋陰, 生津) belongs to the later, post-classical elaboration of the herb rather than to the terse *Bencao* stratum; the classical claim recorded here is the grade. **(a) attested** — upper grade 上品, per the named translation of the classic.

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Ophiopogon japonicus* is not a classical Sushrutan *dravya*; it is an East-Asian species worked in the wider Sino-Himalayan herbal continuum, where a sweet (*madhura*), cooling (*shīta*), unctuous root of this character is read as *pitta*-pacifying and handled in a *rasāyana*-like rejuvenating role for *shosha* (wasting) and *trishna* (thirst). *Rasayana* is a genuine Sushrutan cultivation category, but no named classical source assigns *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status to this species; the standing here is therapeutic and dietetic by analogy, not a sourced formal rejuvenative grade. **(b) framed** — used within the frame, no cultivation status attested for this species.

**Practitioner's note.** The one firm cultivation fact is Chinese: Mai Men Dong is an upper-grade herb, taken over time to moisten and settle rather than for a defined course against a defined complaint. A cultivator uses it as a restorative — rebuilding fluids and calming an over-fired Heart-Lung axis after depletion, heat, or febrile burn-out — conventionally inside the Sheng Mai triad with a *qi* tonic and an astringent, not alone. The Unani and Ayurvedic readings are doctrinal analogies laid over an East-Asian plant; they describe how a cold-moist rejuvenator *would* be classed, not a status those traditions actually recorded for this tuber, and should not be read as independent attestation.

### Contraindications

Contraindicated in patterns of Cold and Damp (loose stools, copious clear phlegm, poor digestion with a swollen pale tongue) — its moistening cold can deepen these. Use caution in early-stage colds with productive phlegm, where a moistening demulcent may retain pathogenic damp. As with any hypolipidaemic/cardioactive botanical, monitor when combined with lipid-lowering or cardiac medication. Not a substitute for prescribed therapy in diabetes or heart disease.
