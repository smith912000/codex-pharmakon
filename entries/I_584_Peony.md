# I-584 | Peony

**Codex Pharmakon Universalis — Volume I: Plant-Based Substances**

**Botanical name:** *Paeonia lactiflora* Pall. (White Peony); *P. suffruticosa* Andrews (Tree Peony / Moutan); *P. officinalis* L. (Common Peony / European)  
**Family:** Paeoniaceae (formerly placed in Ranunculaceae; now its own family)  
**Energetic Tier:** ◈ Jing (blood tonic/nourishing) | ◉ Qi (antispasmodic/anti-inflammatory)  
**Convergence Score:** 7/9 traditions  
**Entry type:** Medicinal Herb — Paeoniflorin Monoterpene Glucoside / Blood Nourishing Herb

## Section 1 — Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Primary names:**
- English: Peony, Chinese Peony, Common Peony
- Greek: *Paionía* (Παιωνία) — from *Paeon*, the physician of the gods in Greek mythology who used the plant to heal Pluto's wound; or from Paeonia (a region of ancient Macedonia)
- Latin: *Paeonia*; *lactiflora* = white/milky flower; *officinalis* = of the apothecaries; *suffruticosa* = shrubby/woody base
- Chinese: *Sháo Yào* (芍药) = White Peony root (*P. lactiflora* root without bark = *Bái Sháo*, 白芍); *Chì Sháo* (赤芍, Red Peony = *P. lactiflora* root with bark, or *P. veitchii*); *Mǔ Dān Pí* (牡丹皮, Tree Peony bark = *P. suffruticosa*)
- Japanese: *Shakuyaku* (芍薬; *P. lactiflora*), *Botan* (牡丹; *P. suffruticosa*)
- Sanskrit: *Ārogyaparaparā*, *Yashtimadhu-like* descriptions; Ayurvedic *Udra* (tentative)
- Arabic: *Ūdud*, *Fāwāniyā* (فاونيا; phonetic borrowing from Greek)
- German: *Pfingstrose* (Pentecost/Whitsun rose — blooms at Pentecost), *Wundrose*
- French: *Pivoine*
- Persian: *Ṣad-bar* (hundred petals), *Pīwōneh*

**Critical Chinese disambiguation — THREE different peony drugs:**

| Drug | Chinese name | Source | Primary properties | Key compound |
|------|-------------|--------|-------------------|--------------|
| White Peony Root | *Bái Sháo* (白芍) | *P. lactiflora* root, bark removed | Blood nourishing; antispasmodic; Liver Yin tonic | Paeoniflorin dominant |
| Red Peony Root | *Chì Sháo* (赤芍) | *P. lactiflora* root with bark, or *P. veitchii* | Clears heat; cools blood; invigorates blood; disperses stasis | Paeoniflorin + gallic acid |
| Tree Peony Bark | *Mǔ Dān Pí* (牡丹皮) | *P. suffruticosa* root bark | Clears heat; cools blood; invigorates blood | Paeonol (different compound!) |

These are three distinct TCM drugs with different properties, different indications, and different primary active compounds. Conflating them is a clinical error.

**Botanical identity (*P. lactiflora*):**
Perennial herbaceous herb, 50–100 cm; arising from thick, fleshy tuberous roots; stems multiple, erect. Leaves alternate, biternate to pinnate; leaflets ovate, entire or lobed. Flowers: large (8–15 cm diameter), fragrant, single or semi-double in wild form; white to pink/red; petaloid sepals; numerous yellow stamens. Fruit: follicle (3–5), beaked, containing large black seeds. Native to China, Mongolia, Siberia; cultivated globally as garden ornamental; commercially cultivated for root production in China, Japan, Korea, Eastern Europe.

**European *P. officinalis*:**
Similar morphology to *P. lactiflora* but with crimson-red flowers (wild form) and generally more robust stems. Distributed in Southern Europe (Mediterranean region through Turkey). Used in European medicine since antiquity; less important in modern phytopharmacology than *P. lactiflora*.

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**Epilepsy/convulsions — cross-Mediterranean convergence:**
The use of peony for epilepsy is one of the most consistent cross-traditional convergences in antiquity. Dioscorides, Ibn Sīnā, Pliny, Galen, and Hildegard von Bingen all document peony for seizure management. The mechanism proposed in antiquity (magical/sympathetic: peony seed worn as necklace to prevent nightmares and convulsions) is not pharmacological, but the consistent empirical observation of benefit may reflect actual antispasmodic activity at medicinal doses.

Modern pharmacology: paeoniflorin activates adenosine A1 receptors + modulates GABA-A receptors → anticonvulsant effects in animal models. The mechanism is plausible but not validated in human epilepsy RCTs (where valproate, levetiracetam, and other modern drugs perform clearly).

**Blood nourishing convergence:**
The Chinese concept of *Bái Sháo* as a "blood nourishing" herb (treats *blood deficiency* patterns: pale face, palpitations, menstrual irregularity, muscle cramps from inadequate blood perfusion) has cross-traditional parallels:
- Dioscorides: uterine conditions and menses
- Ibn Sīnā: menstrual regulation
- Tibetan: blood tonic
The common thread — peony root supporting female reproductive function and blood volume — likely reflects genuine pharmacological activity: paeoniflorin's estrogen-modulatory effects + smooth muscle antispasmodic action on uterine muscle.

**Divergence — bark vs. root pharmacology:**
The Chinese tradition's sophisticated distinction between *Bái Sháo* (root without bark = paeoniflorin dominant = nourishing) and *Mǔ Dān Pí* (bark of tree peony = paeonol dominant = blood-moving/cooling) represents a level of phytochemical precision that other traditions did not develop. European medicine treated peony as a single drug. The Chinese anatomical differentiation within a single plant family produces two pharmacologically distinct drugs — a rare example of traditional medicine identifying part-specific active compounds correctly.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Primary bioactive compounds:**

***Bái Sháo* (*P. lactiflora* root, bark removed):**

**Paeoniflorin** (C₂₃H₂₈O₁₁; MW = 480.5 g/mol):
- Most abundant monoterpene glucoside in *P. lactiflora*
- Content: 2–4% in *Bái Sháo* (Chinese Pharmacopoeia minimum 1.6% for standard quality)
- A monoterpene (pinane skeleton) with glucose attached at C-1 of the benzoate ester
- Primary active compound; responsible for most documented effects
- Pharmacological activities: antispasmodic, anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, neuroprotective, analgesic

**Albiflorin:**
- Isomer of paeoniflorin; also a monoterpene glucoside; ~0.5–1.0% in *Bái Sháo*
- Anti-anxiety effects in animal models; cognitive protection

**Oxypaeoniflorin, Benzoylpaeoniflorin:**
- Minor monoterpene glucosides; anti-inflammatory

**Gallic acid:** 
- Phenolic acid; antioxidant; astringent; higher in *Chì Sháo*

**Paeonol** (2'-hydroxy-4'-methoxyacetophenone):
- Primary active compound of *Mǔ Dān Pí* (tree peony bark)
- Simple phenolic; anti-inflammatory via COX inhibition; analgesic; antipyretic; antiarrhythmic
- Content in *P. suffruticosa* bark: 0.5–2%
- NOTE: Paeonol content is LOW in *Bái Sháo*; the two drugs have different primary active compounds

**Peonol C and related stilbenes:**
- Resveratrol-type stilbenes; antioxidant; anti-inflammatory

**Lactiflorin:**
- *P. lactiflora*-specific flavonoid; minor

**Benzoic acid:**
- Antimicrobial; present in *Bái Sháo* at low levels

**Sterols and fatty acids:**
- β-Sitosterol; various fatty acids

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**Paeoniflorin primary mechanisms:**

**Adenosine A1 receptor agonism:**
- Paeoniflorin → adenosine A1 receptor (Gi-coupled) activation → inhibition of adenylyl cyclase → reduced cAMP → various downstream effects:
  - Neuronal inhibition → anticonvulsant/sedative at CNS level
  - Smooth muscle relaxation → antispasmodic effect on uterus, GI tract, vascular smooth muscle
  - Anti-nociceptive effect via spinal cord A1 receptor activation

**GABA-A receptor modulation:**
- Paeoniflorin potentiates GABA-A receptor-mediated chloride influx → neuronal hyperpolarization → anticonvulsant/anxiolytic effect
- Explains traditional anti-epileptic use and modern anxiolytic data

**Hypothalamus-Pituitary-Ovary (HPO) axis regulation:**
- Paeoniflorin in combination with licorice root (Glycyrrhiza) glycyrrhizin: the classical formula *Sháo Yào Gān Cǎo Tāng* ("Peony-Licorice Decoction")
- Paeoniflorin → aromatase (CYP19A1) induction → androgen-to-estrogen conversion
- Glycyrrhizin → 5α-reductase inhibition → reduced androgen conversion to dihydrotestosterone
- Net in polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS): reduces LH surge frequency; reduces androgens; promotes ovarian follicle maturation
- This mechanism has Level B clinical evidence in PCOS patients (Takahashi 2004)

**NF-κB inhibition (anti-inflammatory):**
- Paeoniflorin → reduced IKKβ activity → reduced NF-κB nuclear translocation → reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression
- Demonstrated in rheumatoid arthritis models: reduced joint inflammation, reduced macrophage activation

**Osteoclast inhibition:**
- RANKL-induced osteoclast differentiation suppressed by paeoniflorin → bone-protective effect
- Relevant to PCOS-associated bone density concerns and RA bone erosion

**Neuroprotection:**
- Paeoniflorin → BDNF expression increase → neuroprotection
- Autophagy induction → clearance of damaged proteins
- Mitochondrial membrane protection
- Documented in Alzheimer's disease animal models

**Paeonol mechanisms (*Mǔ Dān Pí*):**
- COX-1 and COX-2 inhibition → PGE₂ reduction → anti-inflammatory, antipyretic, analgesic (NSAID-like)
- Platelet aggregation inhibition → antithrombotic
- Cardiac: paeonol reduces cardiac arrhythmia in animal models via multiple membrane-stabilizing mechanisms
- Antimicrobial: broad spectrum including *S. aureus*, fungi

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Level A:**

**Paeony-Licorice (*Sháo Yào Gān Cǎo Tāng*) for PCOS:**
- **Takahashi 2004** (*American Journal of Chinese Medicine*, n=34): *Sháo Yào Gān Cǎo Tāng* × 24 weeks in PCOS patients with hyperandrogenemia; significant reductions in LH levels, LH/FSH ratio, testosterone; improved menstrual regularity; mechanism: paeoniflorin + glycyrrhizin HPO axis normalization.
- Multiple Japanese RCTs and observational studies for *Sháo Yào Gān Cǎo Tāng* in PCOS: consistent positive signal; systematic review Takahashi/Kitazawa group.

**Muscle cramp (Peony-Licorice formula):**
- *Sháo Yào Gān Cǎo Tāng* for muscle cramp: **Niwa 2011** (*Journal of Smooth Muscle Research*): paeoniflorin + glycyrrhizin combination significantly more effective than either component alone for reducing muscle cramp frequency in cirrhosis patients.
- Mechanism: paeoniflorin → smooth muscle antispasmodic via A1 receptor; glycyrrhizin → potassium channel modulation → reduced muscle excitability.

**Level B — Chinese clinical observational:**

**Rheumatoid Arthritis:**
- *Bái Sháo*-containing formulas in RA: Multiple Chinese RCTs; consistent anti-inflammatory effect on ESR, CRP, joint count; methodological heterogeneity; not head-to-head with methotrexate.
- **Total Glucosides of Paeony (TGP)**: Standardized extract of *P. lactiflora* glucosides; used in Chinese clinical practice for RA, SLE; anti-inflammatory via NF-κB; Chinese product *Pafulin* (帕夫林).

**Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):**
- **Chen 2015** (*Journal of Ethnopharmacology*): TGP in SLE patients combined with hydroxychloroquine vs. hydroxychloroquine alone; TGP combination group lower disease activity scores; reduced prednisone requirement.

**Hepatitis/liver protection:**
- *Bái Sháo* formulas in Chinese hepatitis B management: multiple observational studies; anti-fibrotic activity of paeoniflorin via TGF-β1 pathway inhibition in hepatic stellate cells.

**Level B — Neurological:**
- **Anxiety**: Albiflorin (extracted from *P. lactiflora*): animal models; clinical data limited to traditional formula context
- **Alzheimer's disease**: Paeoniflorin preclinical data strong; single small human pilot studies; not validated in Phase III

**Level C — Traditional:**
- Dioscorides: peony seed for "nightmares of children" — historically documented anti-convulsant/anxiolytic use
- Chinese medicine classical formulas: *Sì Wù Tāng* (Four-Substance Decoction with *Bái Sháo* for blood deficiency); *Guì Zhī Fú Líng Wán* (for uterine blood stasis with *Chì Sháo*)

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**Paeon and the Divine Physician:**
In Greek mythology, *Paeon* was the physician to the gods. He healed Pluto (Hades) after Hercules wounded him in battle, and healed Ares (Mars) of wounds from the Trojan War. Zeus prevented Paeon from dying by transforming him into the peony plant — so the plant carries the physician's power. The myth specifically attributes healing to *peony root*, with Pluto grateful enough to protect the plant in his underworld domain. Whether this mythological account reflects genuine ancient Greek medical use (the myth preserving cultural memory of medicinal application in the form of divine narrative) or is purely etiological mythology is debated among classical scholars.

**The Peony in East Asian Culture — More than Medicine:**
*Paeonia lactiflora* and *P. suffruticosa* in Chinese culture have a status exceeding purely medical significance. The tree peony (*P. suffruticosa*; Chinese: *Mǔ Dān*; Japanese: *Botan*) is the "king of flowers" in Chinese tradition — symbol of wealth, prosperity, and feminine beauty. The empress Wu Zetian (624–705 CE) of the Tang dynasty so loved peonies that she had them planted throughout the imperial garden. The Tang dynasty represents the golden age of peony cultivation — thousands of cultivated varieties recorded, comparable to 18th–19th century European tulip mania in intensity of horticultural interest. The same plant whose bark (*Mǔ Dān Pí*) is a TCM cardiac and hematological drug was simultaneously China's supreme aesthetic flower. This dual role (utilitarian medicine + supreme cultural symbol) parallels the rose in European culture.

**Hildegard von Bingen and the Christian Peony:**
Hildegard von Bingen (1098–1179 CE) documented the European peony (*P. officinalis*) in *Physica* and *Causae et Curae*. Her treatment protocol for epilepsy involved peony preparation worn next to the skin during the new moon — combining pharmacological (skin absorption of paeoniflorin?) and magical elements in typical medieval medical synthesis. Hildegard called peony *beonia* and attributed its anti-convulsant properties to its "hot and moist" nature — the opposite of the "cold and dry" excess that, in Hildegard's Galenic system, produced epilepsy.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

**Chinese Medicine preparations (standardized):**

***Bái Sháo* (White Peony Root, processed):**
- Dose: 6–15g in decoction; up to 30g in some formulas for severe spasm
- Properties: bitter, sour, slightly cold; Liver, Spleen meridians
- Functions: nourishes blood, regulates menses, softens Liver, relieves pain, restrains Yin, stops sweating
- Primary indications: menstrual disorders (blood deficiency pattern); Liver-Spleen disharmony (abdominal cramps, IBS); muscle spasm/cramp; spontaneous sweating (Yin deficiency)
- Key formula: *Sháo Yào Gān Cǎo Tāng* (Peony-Licorice Decoction): *Bái Sháo* 20g + *Zhì Gān Cǎo* (processed licorice) 20g; for muscle cramp, abdominal pain, menstrual irregularity

***Mǔ Dān Pí* (Tree Peony Bark):**
- Dose: 6–12g in decoction
- Properties: bitter, pungent, slightly cold; Heart, Liver, Kidney meridians
- Functions: clears heat, cools blood, invigorates blood, disperses stasis, reduces deficiency heat
- Primary indications: blood heat conditions (nosebleed, skin eruptions, menstrual flooding); blood stasis (amenorrhea, painful menses, abdominal masses); deficiency heat (afternoon fever, bone-steaming fever in Yin deficiency)
- Contraindicated in pregnancy; with blood deficiency cold pattern; during heavy menstruation

**Western Herbal:**
- European peony (*P. officinalis*) standardized extracts: used in European herbal medicine for premenstrual tension, menstrual irregularity, anxiety
- Dose: dried root 1–3g/day; tincture (1:10, 45%) 3–6 mL/day
- Commission E: not approved (insufficient evidence); used in complementary practice

**TGP (Total Glucosides of Paeony) — Chinese pharmaceutical:**
- *Pafulin* (帕夫林, Zhejiang Jianfeng Pharmaceutical): 600 mg/capsule; 1–2 capsules TID
- Indications: RA, SLE (adjunctive)
- Evidence base: Level B; anti-inflammatory via NF-κB pathway
- GI side effects most common at therapeutic dose

**PCOS clinical application (*Sháo Yào Gān Cǎo Tāng*):**
- Prescribed by TCM practitioners; 8–12 week courses; monitor androgens and LH/FSH
- Not as standalone therapy; adjunct to lifestyle/conventional management
- Safe for most women; avoid with hypokalemia (glycyrrhizin increases aldosterone → potassium depletion)

**Drug interactions:**
- Warfarin: peony root has minimal anticoagulant effect at standard doses; caution with *Chì Sháo* (blood-invigorating) + anticoagulants; monitor INR
- Immunosuppressants: TGP immune modulation may interact with cyclosporine; avoid concurrent use or monitor carefully
- CYP interactions: paeoniflorin does not significantly inhibit major CYPs at therapeutic doses

**Safety:**
- *Bái Sháo*: generally safe at standard doses; GI upset at high doses; avoid prolonged high-dose use
- *Mǔ Dān Pí*: contraindicated in pregnancy; avoid in heavy menstrual bleeding
- European peony (*P. officinalis*): all parts mildly toxic in excess; seeds particularly
- Toxicity: large doses of *Bái Sháo* can cause hypotension (antispasmodic vascular effect); reduce dose
