### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was opened on 13 October from the extraction (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`), where the Latin line is kept as "not paired to a Sanskrit name (OCR column split)". **The pairing, by counting, and why it is doubtful.** On the page that carries this Latin line, the Sanskrit names run "Jambira", "Jambu", "Jata'-ma'nsi", "Ja'ti", "Jatila'", "Ja'ti-phala", "Javsi", "Jayapa'la", "Jhinti", "Jiraka", "**Jivaka**" (line 31810) and "Jayanti" (twelve names, lines 31795–31811), and the first twelve Latin entries run "Citrus medica", "Eugenia gambolana", "Valeriana Jatamansi", "Jasminum Grandiflorum.", "See Jatamansi", "Myristica Fragrans", "Hibiscus Rosa sinensis", "Croton tigliura", "Barlarea Cristata", "Cuminum Cyminum. Cumin seed", "**Pentaptera tomentosa**" (line 31861) and "Sesbania aculeata; S. Aegyptiaca". The eleventh Sanskrit name, Jivaka, thus stands against the eleventh Latin entry, and the neighbours agree (Jiraka with cumin, Jhinti with *Barleria*, Jayapala with *Croton*, Jambu with *Eugenia*). The pairing is therefore sound by count. But the same Latin species is printed once more as "Terminalia Tomentosa." (line 31334) against the Sanskrit name Asana, on the page of A-names (Arjuna, Arka, Arka-mula, Arka-pushpi, Arushkara, **Asana** ... line 31281); counting there gives "Terminalia Arjuna" for Arjuna, "Calotropis gigantea" for Arka, "See Bhallataka" for Arushkara, and "Terminalia Tomentosa" for Asana. So Asana is already tied to this tree by count, and the translator would then have given the same tree for two Sanskrit names. Jivaka is, in the Sushruta group lists, a member of the Vidarigandhadi and Kakolyadi drug groups and a leaf in a vegetable list, which are not what a tall timber tree would be. Either the translator gave the same species twice, or the line has been shifted by one in the OCR. The Codex cannot decide this from the file. [USER: verify against the page image]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say of each candidate name.* Neither name's description is a description of this Latin species; both are given so that the editor can see what the Latin line may refer to.

*Asana (the tree).* Sutra 38 (`SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 76–86): "The Salasaradi group of medicinal drugs consists of Salasara, Ajakarna, Khadira, Kadara, Kalaskandha, Kramuka, Bhurjja, Meshashringi, Tinisha, Chandana, Kuchandana, Shinshapa, Shirisha, Asana, Dhava, Arjuna, Tala, Shaka, Naktamala, Putika, Ashvakarna, Aguru and Kaliyaka." The metrical text that follows says the group "destroys the germ of Kushtha, absorbs the deranged fat and Kapham and proves beneficial in morbid discharges from the urethra (Meha), chlorosis or jaundice (Pandu)". Sutra 46 (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 1790–1795), on flowers: "The flowers of the Rakta-Vriksha, Nimva, Mushkaka, Arka, Asana and Kutaja trees subdue the Pittam and Kapham, and prove curative in skin diseases (Kushtham)." Sutra 6 (`SS_Sutra_ch06.txt`, line 376) names Asana among the trees that "flower in abundance" in the season of the rains. (The "Asana-Pushpaka" of Sutra 46 line 83 is a rice, a member of the Shashtika group, and is not the tree.) The Codex's spine has a separate Asana entry, CPU-00338 (`I_385_Asana_Pterocarpus_marsupium.md`), which identifies the Asana of Sutra 38 as *Pterocarpus marsupium*; that is a different identification from the appendix's, and the conflict is the editor's to settle.

*Jivaka (the group member).* Sutra 38 (lines 14–19): "Vidarigandha, Vidari, Sahadeva, Vishvadeva, Shvadanstra, Prithakparni, Shatavari, Sariva, black Sariva, Jivaka, Rishavaka, Mahasaha, Kshudra-Saha, Vrihati, Kantakari, Punarnava, Eranda, Hansapadi, Vrishchikah (as printed) and Rishavi, form the group known as the Vidari-gandhadi"; and (lines 249–251) "The Kakolyadi Group: The drugs known as Kakoli, Kshira-Kakoli, Jivaka, Rishabhaka, Mudgaparni, Mashaparni, Meda, Mahameda, Chhinna-ruha ..." Sutra 16 (`SS_Sutra_ch16.txt`, lines 458–460): a plaster of "the drugs which form the group of the Jivakadi Varga with a quantity of clarified butter" for a burning ulcer. Sutra 46 (lines 1728–1736): "The leaves of the Lonika, Jatuka, Triparnika, Pattura, Jivaka, Suvarchala, Kurmaka, Kathinjara, Kuntalika, Kurantika etc., are sweet in taste and digestion and cooling in their potency. They subdue the Kapham and do not inordinately generate the Pittam." The Codex's spine has a separate Jivaka entry, CPU-00307 (`I_356_Jivaka_Crepidium_acuminatum.md`), marked "id-uncertain".

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Terminalia* (including the older *Pentaptera*) is a genus of trees in the Combretaceae, whose bark and fruits are generally tannin-rich and astringent. This applies only if the identification stands. [USER: verify]
