# Sarsaparilla — Zarzaparrilla · "Blood-Purifier" · Root-Beer Root

## *Smilax officinalis* Kunth | Family: Smilacaceae

**Tradition Tags:** DMM (Dioscorides), CM (Avicenna), TCM (Chinese medicine), SS (Sushruta), BM (Badianus)
**Energetic Classification:** Hot and dry in the second degree (Galenic–Unani) — a warming, sudorific *depurative* / alterative "sweetener of the blood"
**Training Phase:** Phase III — Depuration & the Blood (alteratives, diaphoretics, and "cleansers")

---

### Origin & Tradition

Sarsaparilla is the long, whip-like root of climbing *Smilax* vines — the New World drug of commerce coming chiefly from *Smilax officinalis* Kunth and its Central American relatives, whose Spanish name *zarzaparrilla* ("little bramble vine") the English merely swallowed whole. Its story is a hinge between two worlds. Old World medicine already knew related *Smilax* — Mediterranean *S. aspera*, the East-Asian **China root** (*S. china* / *tu fu ling*) — as warming, sweat-provoking, blood-cleansing roots. Then, in the sixteenth century, Spanish traders carried the American root back across the Atlantic and it exploded into fame as one leg of the great **syphilis triad** — alongside **China Root** and **Guaiacum** (both already in this Codex) — the celebrated "depuratives" prescribed to sweat, cleanse, and "purify the blood" of the pox that was then ravaging Europe.

Sarsaparilla's afterlife is a study in how a failed drug becomes a flavour. Stripped of its medical pretensions, the root's sweet, vanilla-and-wintergreen character made it a foundation of nineteenth-century **root beer** and "sarsaparilla" sodas; and in the twentieth century, a persistent gym-culture **myth** recast its steroidal saponins as a "natural anabolic steroid" — a claim with no pharmacological basis, dismantled below.

**Avicenna & the Unani stream (CM).** Unani medicine adopted *ushba* (sarsaparilla) as a hot, dry, blood-cleansing *muṣaffī-yi dam* (blood-purifier) and diaphoretic, given for chronic skin eruptions, "corrupt humours," joint and venereal complaints — the archetypal depurative indication. Its incorporation postdates Avicenna's own life for the American species; the doctrinal slot, however, is squarely Galenic-Unani.

**Dioscorides & the Graeco-Roman root (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* describes *smilax* (the bramble-vine *Smilax aspera*), used as a warming, protective, and detoxifying root — including a reputation as an antidote against poisons. This is the classical seed of the "purifying/protective root" doctrine that the American species later inherited and amplified.

**Shennong / Chinese materia medica (TCM).** The Chinese analogue is **tu fu ling** (*Smilax glabra* / *S. china*), a sweet, bland, neutral root that "resolves toxicity, expels dampness, and unblocks the joints." Strikingly, Chinese medicine deployed it for exactly the same historical disease — it became a mainstay for **syphilis** ("*mei du*", the "bayberry poison") and mercury-poisoning sequelae, and remains a classic damp-heat and skin-disorder drug. The convergence with the European syphilis use is one of the Codex's cleanest cross-tradition echoes.

**Sushruta & Ayurveda (SS).** Indian medicine's *Smilax* (and the functionally parallel **sarivā**/*Hemidesmus*, the "Indian sarsaparilla") occupies the *raktaprasādana* / *raktaśodhana* niche — **blood-clearing** alteratives for skin disease (*kuṣṭha*), pruritus, and "heated blood." Whether the classical *dvīpāntara-vacā*/*Smilax* identifications are exact is philologically loose and should be flagged [USER: verify]; the *functional* depurative role is secure.

**Aztec — the Badianus Codex.** The *Libellus de Medicinalibus Indorum Herbis* (the 1552 Badianus manuscript, the earliest New World herbal) records native Mesoamerican use of *Smilax* (*zarzaparrilla*) roots among remedies for skin and bodily complaints — the indigenous American testimony that stands *behind* the root Europe adopted. (No single-letter Codex tag is assigned to the Badianus tradition.)

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** The unifying doctrine across all five streams is **depuration** — the belief that chronic skin, joint, and venereal disease reflects a *corrupted, over-heated, or impure blood* that the right root can cleanse, largely by promoting sweat and urine. Mapping this onto the chemistry is where honesty is required:

- **"Purifies the blood / cleanses corrupt humours" → steroidal saponins as surfactant, mild diuretic, and anti-inflammatory agents — but not as literal blood-cleansers.** Sarsaparilla's signature molecules are **steroidal saponins** (sarsasaponin/parillin and their aglycones **sarsasapogenin** and **smilagenin**) plus **phytosterols** (β-sitosterol, stigmasterol) and flavonoids. Saponins are amphipathic surfactants; historically it was proposed they might bind gut **endotoxins** and bile, and modern data confirm genuine **anti-inflammatory** activity (see below). A drug that damps inflammation, gently promotes diuresis/diaphoresis, and binds some gut toxins would *look*, to a humoral physician, like a "blood-sweetener" — visible improvement in inflamed skin. So the doctrine has a partial, mechanistic shadow: real anti-inflammatory chemistry dressed in the language of humoral purification.
- **The syphilis-cure doctrine → almost certainly a failure, exactly as with China Root.** Here the doctrine must be **deflated hard**. *Treponema pallidum* is killed by antibiotics, not saponins. The Renaissance "cures" ran alongside the disease's own natural remission into latency, alongside sweating regimens, and alongside (often) mercury; the apparent successes are best explained by spontaneous latency and regression to the mean, not by the root. The parallel to China Root and Guaiacum is precise — three celebrated depuratives, one shared and almost certainly spurious anti-venereal reputation.
- **The "natural anabolic steroid" myth → false on two grounds.** First, a *steroidal saponin* is not an *anabolic-androgenic steroid*: sarsasapogenin is a spirostane sapogenin, not testosterone, and there is no evidence the plant contains testosterone or that its sapogenins are converted to it in the human body. Second, saponins are **large, poorly absorbed** molecules — much of an oral dose stays in the gut lumen. The bodybuilding legend is a category error trading on the word "steroid." **Deflated.**

The doctrine-to-chemistry verdict, then, is split: a *real* anti-inflammatory/depurative kernel, wrapped in a *spurious* anti-syphilitic claim and a *false* anabolic myth.

### Active Components

- **Steroidal saponins** — sarsasaponin (parillin) and related spirostanol/furostanol glycosides; the defining constituents.
- **Sapogenins** — **sarsasapogenin** and **smilagenin** (the aglycones released on hydrolysis); historically of interest as steroid-synthesis feedstocks, *not* as endogenous hormones.
- **Phytosterols** — β-sitosterol, stigmasterol, pollinastanol.
- **Flavonoids and phenolics** — including astilbin and related compounds in *Smilax* species; contributors to antioxidant/anti-inflammatory activity.
- **Starch, resin, and the sweet-aromatic principles** responsible for the root-beer flavour.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Sarsaparilla is a Phase III depurative: the practitioner's tool for **chronic inflammatory skin disease** (psoriasis-type scaling, eczema, stubborn eruptions), rheumatic and "damp-heat" joint complaints, and as a gentle sudorific/diuretic "spring cleanse" adjunct. Its genuine, evidence-supported action is **anti-inflammatory**, best framed as a slow alterative taken over weeks, typically as a decoction, and classically **combined** (in the triad tradition) with other alteratives. What it is emphatically *not* used for in an honest Kenetica: curing syphilis (use antibiotics) or building muscle (it is not anabolic).

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the modern evidence for *Smilax* is almost entirely **preclinical**, and it lands squarely on the *anti-inflammatory* reading of the depurative doctrine — not on the syphilis or anabolic claims.

- **Anti-inflammatory chemistry is real (in vitro).** Furostanol saponins isolated from *Smilax china* rhizomes inhibited **TNF-α** mRNA expression in LPS-stimulated macrophages — several compounds cutting expression by 67–93% at 10 µM (Xie et al., *Steroids* 2018; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.steroids.2018.09.011)). Steroidal saponins from *S. china* also inhibited **COX-2** activity in enzyme assays (Shao et al., *Phytochemistry* 2006; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2006.10.026)), and furostanol saponins from the related *S. davidiana* suppressed IL-1β while promoting IL-10 in stimulated macrophages (Zhou et al., *Steroids* 2017; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.steroids.2017.08.013)). This is a coherent anti-inflammatory signature consistent with skin/joint use.
- **A traditional-use review that refuses to over-claim.** A comprehensive review of *Smilax china* — long used for inflammatory disorders, particularly pelvic inflammation — catalogues 134 constituents (steroidal saponins and flavonoids predominant) and a broad in-vitro/in-vivo pharmacology (anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, anti-diabetic, antibacterial), while explicitly stating that **some traditional uses are demonstrated and others still require confirmation** (Wang et al., *J Ethnopharmacol* 2023; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2023.116992)). That candour is the correct evidence posture.
- **No credible clinical evidence for the historical headline uses.** No retrieved trial supports sarsaparilla as a cure for syphilis (a bacterial infection curable only by antibiotics), and none supports an anabolic/ergogenic effect; the poorly-absorbed, non-hormonal nature of its saponins argues actively against the latter.

**Evidence grade:** the **anti-inflammatory/alterative** kernel of the depurative doctrine has genuine but *preclinical, cell-and-animal-level* support; the **syphilis cure** is graded a historical **failure**; the **"natural anabolic steroid"** claim is graded **false**. Human clinical data for sarsaparilla specifically are essentially absent — a large evidence gap the entry should not paper over.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic–Unani (AT):** *ushba* — hot and dry (≈second degree); a *muṣaffī-yi dam* (blood-purifier), diaphoretic and diuretic alterative.
- **Ayurveda (SS):** in the *raktaśodhana/raktaprasādana* (blood-cleansing) alterative class; for the parallel *sarivā*, *rasa* — sweet/bitter (*madhura, tikta*); *vīrya* — cooling (*śīta*); *vipāka* — sweet (*madhura*); precise grading for the American *Smilax* is not classically fixed [USER: verify].
- **TCM (CM):** *tu fu ling* — sweet, bland, neutral; resolves toxicity, dispels dampness, unblocks joints (Stomach/Liver channels).
- **Aztec / Badianus:** a skin-and-body remedy root; no formal humoral grade (non-humoral tradition).

### Contraindications

- **Not a treatment for syphilis or any STI.** Presenting sarsaparilla as an anti-venereal cure is dangerous; bacterial STIs require antibiotic therapy. This is the single most important caution.
- **Not an anabolic steroid.** Do not use as a "natural steroid"/ergogenic aid; the claim is false and marketing to that effect is misleading.
- **GI irritation.** Saponins can cause nausea, gastric irritation, or loose stools at high doses; large amounts of any saponin-rich root are irritant.
- **Drug absorption / diuresis.** Saponins may affect absorption of co-administered drugs; the mild diuretic action warrants caution with other diuretics and in renal impairment.
- **Pregnancy/lactation.** Insufficient safety data at therapeutic doses; restrict to culinary flavouring.
- **Allergy.** Possible hypersensitivity; discontinue on reaction.
