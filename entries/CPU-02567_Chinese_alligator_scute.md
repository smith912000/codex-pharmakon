# Chinese alligator scute — 鱼甲 *Yu Jia* (Shennong bencao jing), glossed 鼍甲 *Tuo Jia*
## *The armour of the Chinese alligator, Alligator sinensis Fauvel, in the standard modern identification, reached through Tao Hongjing's gloss of 鱼甲 as 鼍甲 (the 鼍 being traditionally read as that alligator); medium confidence, because the classic's text does not describe the animal and the Mingyi layer's habitat 南海 (the South Sea) does not match the alligator's range as we know it* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The pages of Yang's 1998 translation that carry the animal drugs are not readable on disk, so there is no second witness. The identification is ours. (The spine's crocodile rows, for example III_65 Crocodile, come from other traditions and are not this animal.)

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the middle (中) classic, reads 味辛，微温 ("acrid, slightly warm"). It governs 心腹症瘕 ("concretions and conglomerations of the heart and abdomen"), 伏坚 ("hidden hardness"), 积聚 ("accumulations and gatherings"), 寒热 ("cold and heat"), in women 崩中 ("flooding"), 下血五色 ("downward discharge of blood in five colours"), 小腹阴中相引痛 ("lower-abdominal pain pulling into the genitals"), and 创疥死肌 ("sores, scabies and dead flesh"). The habitat is 生池泽 ("grows in pools and marshes"). There is no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 鱼甲 *yu jia*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2735.)

**Wu Pu.** No Wu Pu paragraph is printed for this entry.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰：生南海。取无时: "grows in the South Sea; taken at any time." (Line 2737.)

**Sun's note (案).** The transcription of the note is damaged (it has dropped characters), and only parts can be read. It cites the *Shuowen* with the statement 皮可为鼓 ("the skin can be made into a drum") and the phrases 鼍…水虫似蜥，易长大 ("the 鼍 is a water creature like a lizard that grows large easily"), and then Tao Hongjing, 陶弘景云：蛇，即鼍甲也 ("[the drug] is the scute of the 鼍"). The character printed before 即 is 蛇 ("snake") and is probably an error for 鱼甲; that reading is ours. [USER: verify] (Line 2739.)

**Identity.** The Classic names only 鱼甲, literally "fish armour". Tao's gloss ties it to the 鼍, a large water reptile whose hide made drums; the standard modern identification of the 鼍 is the Chinese alligator, which is why the drug is placed here. The note's description ("like a lizard, grows large") fits a reptile but does not name the species. The Mingyi layer's "South Sea" suggests a southern coast, whereas the Chinese alligator is known from the lower Yangtze region, so the habitat line points away from the identification or reflects a trade source. ### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, an alligator's scutes are bony plates embedded in the skin (osteoderms), made mainly of bone mineral (calcium phosphate) and collagen. Nothing more specific can be said without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice, and for conservation reasons not a practice that should be revived. The Classic records an acrid, warm animal armour for lumps and hard masses in the abdomen, for women's flooding and abnormal bleeding, and for sores and scabies. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends none. Abdominal masses and abnormal bleeding need medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives only the harvest: taken at any time (Mingyi layer). It gives no preparation, route, quantity or course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

The Chinese alligator is a small alligator endemic to the lower Yangtze basin of China. It is critically endangered and listed in Appendix I of CITES, which prohibits international commercial trade in the wild species. Reptiles can carry *Salmonella* on skin and tissues. No medical property of alligator scutes is established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- Class: middle (中). No organ affinity is printed. No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 鱼甲 in the middle grade (中品); its preface (Sun ed. line 1677) says middle drugs "mainly nourish the nature to correspond with humanity", that some are toxic and some not, and that one should weigh them before use. The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise; it lists abdominal masses, bleeding and sores. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 鱼甲 *yu jia*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2735.)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A middle-grade animal armour for lumps and bleeding.

### Contraindications

- **Conservation and law.** The Chinese alligator is critically endangered; its parts must not be collected, bought or traded. Imitation or substitute "scute" in trade may come from other crocodilians, some also protected. [USER: verify local rules]
- **Abdominal masses, abnormal vaginal bleeding, sores.** Need medical assessment.
- **Hygiene.** Reptile material can carry *Salmonella* and other pathogens.
- **Identification.** The classic's animal may not be the alligator.
