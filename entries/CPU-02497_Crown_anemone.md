# Crown anemone — شقائق (*shaqāʾiq*, as printed)

## *Crown anemone: the red-flowered anemone of the Canon, shaqa'iq; modern identification Anemone coronaria L. (moderate confidence)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the Arabic texts on disk. The locus is *Canon* Book II, entry *shaqāʾiq* (شقائق), Arabic text line 18046 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; it runs to line 18070. It is Dioscoridean at the start: "the excellent sage Dioscorides said: some people call it *armiyūn* and also *ʿāmīnūn*" (lines 18047-18048), and the description that follows matches the English Dioscorides *anemone* (2-207, line 6374 on disk): two kinds, wild and cultivated; cultivated with red, milk-pale or purple flowers; leaves like coriander but thinner; flowers like poppy with black or blue-black centres; root the size of an olive; wild larger, with more small roots, and one black kind sharper (lines 18048-18054). The resemblance is mine, from reading both.

**Distinguishing it (CM).** Some people, Avicenna says, "do not separate wild *shaqāʾiq al-nuʿmān* from the drug called wild *ladaḥmūniyā* and from the poppy whose flowers resemble it in redness" (lines 18054-18056). The mark of the *shaqāʾiq*: it has "no tear, no poppy-head or pomegranate-like capsule", but something like asparagus tips (lines 18057-18059). The plant with a saffron-coloured tear is the *arghāmūnī* (the English Dioscorides calls it *argemone*).

**Identification.** The description (no latex, no poppy capsule, asparagus-like tops, red flower) is of an *Anemone*; *Anemone coronaria* is the usual modern reading of *shaqāʾiq*. The text does not fix the species, so confidence is moderate. Compare I_1003 (*Anemone alba*) and the other *Anemone* entries on the spine.

**Temperament and actions (CM).** "Hot in the second degree, moist" (حار في الثانية رطب; line 18059). Polishing and dissolving; Galen: "polishing, washing, drawing, ripening" (lines 18059-18060). Cosmetic: mixed with walnut husks it blackens the hair, and the leaf and stems improve the hair (lines 18060-18061). Swellings: cooked and applied to soft hot swellings and boils (lines 18061-18062). Wounds: the dry plant cleans and heals dirty ulcers, scaling and running scab (lines 18063-18064). Head: its juice as snuff cleans head and brain; the root chewed draws moisture from the head and removes ringworm (*qūbāʾ*) (lines 18066-18067). Eye: the juice with honey for dimness, whiteness and scars; boiled in wine as a poultice for hard swellings around the eye (lines 18067-18069). Chest: leaves and stems boiled with thyme "bring on milk as it should" (lines 18069-18070). Excretory: promotes menses as a pessary (line 18070).

### Active Components

Plants of the buttercup family (Ranunculaceae), anemones among them, are known to contain ranunculin, which breaks down to the irritant protoanemonin when the fresh plant is crushed. This is general phytochemistry; the Canon names no constituent.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The entry records a topical cleanser, hair darkener, eye and snuff remedy and a milk and menses promoter. Not a recommended practice; the fresh plant is an irritant.

### Preparation & Dosing

Forms in the text: juice as snuff; juice with honey for the eye; leaf and stem cooked, alone or with thyme; the root chewed; applied as a poultice in boiled wine. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General fact: crushed fresh anemone is a blistering irritant of skin and mucous membranes.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Hot in the second degree and moist; polishing and dissolving (*Canon* Book II, entry *shaqāʾiq*, line 18059). Galen is quoted for "polishing, washing, drawing, ripening" (lines 18059-18060).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* Anemone is a cleansing and drawing drug in the text, quoted from Dioscorides and Galen. It carries no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** A drawing and cleansing plant for sores, scalp, eye and breast milk. Nothing for a cultivator beyond reading the text.

### Contraindications

Fresh anemone is an irritant (blistering skin and mouth) and the whole Ranunculaceae family is regarded as poisonous when eaten fresh. The menses- and milk-promoting uses make pregnancy a general concern. No safe internal use can be derived from the text. [USER: verify]
