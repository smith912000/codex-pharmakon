# Ajamoda — Ajamoda, also Agnika (Sushruta; Pippalyadi group)

## *A minor herb used as an ingredient in many Sushruta compounds; the translator's Appendix 3 pairs the name by position with Pimpinella involucrata and, in the body of the text, glosses Agnika as Ajamoda; the Codex's first-pass guess, Trachyspermum ammi (ajwain), is a later-literature identification that is not made in the translation read, so ours is the translator's: a carrot-family herb (Apiaceae), genus Pimpinella, low confidence at species level* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The only attestation is the Sushruta Samhita in Bhishagratna's English translation. A search of the other texts on disk for *Ajamoda* and *Ajmod* found nothing outside it. The row's guess of *Trachyspermum ammi* is not stated in any line read; other identifications of Ajamoda are current in later literature and none was opened. [USER: verify.]

**Sushruta (SS).** The name is among the most frequent of the minor drugs in the translation.

- **Drug groups.** Sutrasthana ch. 38 lists it in the Pippalyadi group: "Pippali, Pippali mulam, Chavya, Chitraka, Shringavera, Maricha, Hasti-Pippali, Harenuka, Ela, Ajamoda, Indrayava, Patha, Jiraka, Sarshapa, Maha-Nimva-Phala, Hingu, Bhargi, Madhurasa, Ativisha, Vacha, Vidanga and Katurohini" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 148-153; Vol. I line 15493). The metrical text: "The present group acts as a good appetiser and is an absorbant of intestinal mucous and unassimilated lymph chyle. The range of its therapeutical application includes catarrh, deranged Kapham and Vatam, non-relish for food, abdominal glands, colic and gastralgia." This is the action of the group.
- **Appendix.** In "Appendix No. 3. Names of Drugs" (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`) the Sanskrit name *Ajamoda* (OCR "AjamodsC") is at line 31155, and the Latin line "Pimpinella Involucrata." at 31212 falls against it by position: *Ajagandha* is paired with "Ocymum gratissimum" (31210) and *Aja-s'ringi* with "Gymnema Sylvestre" (31214). The appendix also gives *Agnika* (line 31141) with the cross-reference "See Ajamodd" (line 31199). The pairing is by position. [USER: verify against the printed page.]
- **Agnika as synonym.** The translator glosses Agnika as Ajamoda in the body: "Agnika (Ajamoda)" (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch09.txt` line 107; `uttara/SS_Uttara_ch24.txt` line 238; `uttara/SS_Uttara_ch55.txt` line 323; `kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch02.txt` line 323).
- **Recipes.** Ajamoda is an ingredient in: the gruel given between the stages of poisoning (Kalpa ch. 2 line 323); the antidote paste Ajitagada, powdered with Vidanga, Patha, Triphala, Hingu, Tagara, Trikatu, the five salts and Chitraka, pasted with honey and kept a fortnight in a cow's horn (`kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch05.txt` line 387); the Hingvadi-vati with asafoetida, Trikatu, Vacha and others (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt` line 679); a drink with Shvadamshtra, Talamuli, Kadamba roots and Nagara against gravel (Sarkara) (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch07.txt` line 99); an oil of Nikumbha with Saindhava salt and powdered Ajamoda (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch14.txt` line 149); the Ninth Sneha, a paste with Patha, Pippali, Gaja-pippali and others (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch37.txt` line 195); an alkaline lambative with Kushtha, Saindhava, Yashti-madhu, Nagara and Vidanga (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch42.txt` line 277); a varti for the rectum with Danti, Kampilla and others (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch55.txt` line 323); and a fumigation of a child's body with white mustard, Vacha, Hingu, Kushtha, parched rice and Bhallataka (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch36.txt` line 27). Further occurrences are at `chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch08.txt` line 110, `ch37` lines 71 and 224, and `uttara/SS_Uttara_ch43.txt` line 74, `ch44` line 232 and `ch52` line 305, which were not read in detail.

### Active Components

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** Ajamoda is a supporting ingredient in digestive, antidotal, urinary, rectal and fumigation compounds. The Codex gives no protocol and does not map the drug onto a modern spice.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those above; the lines read give proportions in some ("taken in equal parts" with Kushtha, salts, Vacha and Hingu in a potion, `chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch08.txt` line 110; "one Pala weight" of each in the alkaline lambative, Uttara ch. 42) and none for Ajamoda alone. No dose is given in the sources retrieved for the drug by itself. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Seed spices of the carrot family are commonly used for flavour and for digestive complaints in the traditions that use them, and many contain volatile oils. The identification here is open, so nothing specific is claimed. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Ajamoda in the lines read. The Pippalyadi group's action (above) is stated for the group.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug sits in the Pippalyadi group, framed as an appetiser acting on Kapham and Vatam and on digestion, and in many compounds for poisoning, gravel and the rectum. That is a therapeutic frame. No *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter read names it, so no cultivation status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do. A common ingredient in compounds for digestion and poisoning, without a cultivation role in the text on disk.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The Latin name is the translator's and the pairing is positional; later sources may identify Ajamoda differently. Do not use a plant on the strength of this entry.
- **Antidote and rectal recipes.** Some recipes treat poisoning or are given by the rectum. They are historical record, not advice.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
