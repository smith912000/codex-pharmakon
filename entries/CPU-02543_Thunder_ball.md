# Thunder ball — Lei Wan 雷丸 (Shennong bencao jing); aliases Lei Shi 雷矢, Lei Shi 雷实, "thunder pellet"

## *Omphalia lapidescens Schroet. (the name varies in the modern literature, Polyporus being another), "thunder ball", a hard, dark, ball-like fungal sclerotium dug from the soil; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence, since the species name for the drug has been given differently and the texts read describe no organism* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The only text read for this entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk for *Omphalia* and for "Lei Wan" found only the Shennong extract file `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/sun_monographs.csv` (row 319).

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 雷丸 in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 雷丸 *lei wan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3677). The Shennong paragraph (the name is written 雷公丸 in the Yulan) reads: 味苦，寒 ("bitter, cold"); 主杀三虫 ("governs killing the three worms"), 逐毒瓦斯 (see below), 胃中热 ("heat in the stomach"), 利丈夫，不利女子 ("benefits men, does not benefit women"), and 作摩膏，除小儿百病 ("made into a rubbing ointment, it removes the hundred illnesses of children"); habitat 生山谷 ("grows in mountain valleys"). The words 毒瓦斯 are a modern word for "poison gas", which cannot belong to a Han text; the Codex takes them for a conversion fault in the transcription (for 毒气, "poison qi") and does not read them further. [USER: verify] The paragraph's parenthesis says the Yulan adds the alias 一名雷矢 and that the Daguan edition prints it in black, which means as Mingyi bielu text and not as the Shennong layer. There is no 久服 clause.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 3679) records a disagreement of authorities on the taste and toxicity: Shennong "bitter"; Huangdi, Qibo and Tongjun "sweet, toxic" (甘，有毒); Bianque "sweet, non-toxic" (甘，无毒); Li shi "very cold" (大寒). The Yulan quotation adds the alias 雷实 and "or it grows at Hanzhong; gathered in the eighth month".

The Mingyi bielu layer (line 3681) adds the aliases 雷矢 and 雷实, and: 生石城及治中土中。八月采根，曝干, "grows in Shicheng and in the soil of [the name is printed as 治中]; the root is gathered in the eighth month and dried in the sun". It calls the part gathered a "root". The Codex reads the object as a sclerotium from the modern identification only, and not from this text.

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 3683) is a single line: the Fanzi jiran says that lei shi comes from Hanzhong and that the white colour is good (范子计然云：雷矢，出汉中。色白者，善). The note before this entry in the transcription (on mang cao, a toxic plant) is a different monograph.

An appendix of drug pairings at the end of the volume gives: 雷丸 荔实、浓朴为使；恶葛根, "lei wan: li shi and hou po are its envoys; it dislikes ge gen" (line 4537). It is a later appendix and gives no further statement.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the sclerotium of the drug is reported to contain a protein-digesting enzyme (a protease) with polysaccharides, and its activity is described as lost on heating. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, cold sclerotium for killing "the three worms" (intestinal worms in the classic's sense), for heat in the stomach, and, as a rubbing ointment, for children's illnesses, and says it benefits men and not women. The Wu Pu layer records that several authorities called it toxic. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose, and states plainly that this is a lower-grade, possibly toxic drug: see Contraindications.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the paragraph is the rubbing ointment ("made into a rubbing salve", 作摩膏) for children, with no quantity. The Mingyi layer gives the harvest (the eighth month) and sun-drying. For toxic drugs generally the classic's preface gives a rule: begin with a dose like a grain of millet; if the disease leaves, stop; if not, double; if not, increase tenfold (若用毒药疗病，先起如黍粟，病去，即止。不去，倍之；不去，十之。取去为度) (line 4125). That rule is general, the paragraph itself does not apply it, and the Codex does not use it as a dose for this drug. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The sclerotium is used in modern Chinese pharmacy against intestinal worms; it is not a general nutritional food. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The classic's categories ("the three worms", "poison") are not modern diagnoses.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味苦，寒, "bitter, cold" (line 3677). The Wu Pu layer records that other authorities called it sweet, sweet and toxic, or very cold (line 3679); the Codex does not settle the disagreement.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph for 雷丸 reads 味苦，寒 and files the sclerotium in the lower grade (下品). The classic's preface (line 2935) says the lower medicines "are for treating disease, to correspond to earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to clear cold-and-heat evil qi, break accumulations and heal illness, base it on the lower classic" (主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经). The paragraph has no 久服 clause; it is an indication list that kills worms, drives out heat, and treats children with an ointment. The register is therefore (b): a lower-grade drug whose grade is the tradition's own warning against long use. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 雷丸 *lei wan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3677)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The tradition grades this as a lower-grade, possibly toxic drug against worms, with no long-use clause, and several authorities in the Wu Pu layer called it toxic. The honest use of the entry is as a historical record.

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity:** the Wu Pu layer records Huangdi, Qibo and Tongjun as calling it toxic, and the classic's lower grade is "mostly toxic, not to be taken long" (line 2935). Do not take it on the strength of this entry.
- **Women:** the paragraph says it does not benefit women (不利女子); no further safety information was read, including for pregnancy. [USER: verify]
- **Children:** the paragraph's ointment for children is historical record only; do not apply any preparation to a child on the strength of this entry.
- **Worms and parasites:** intestinal infestation needs medical diagnosis and treatment.
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given.
