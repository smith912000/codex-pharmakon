# Ficus cingalensis — Nandi, Nandi-vriksha (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Ficus cingalensis, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Ficus  Cingalensis.", line 32436) against the Sanskrit name Nandi (line 32376). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print Nandi-Vriksha in a drug group and Nandi with the translator's bracket "(Vata)" in one recipe; neither gives a Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The chapters name Nandi twice (one group list and one recipe); the appendix Latin and the chapter bracket "(Vata)" point to different figs, so the identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` (row 592) gives the name as "Nandi", the locus "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 32376; id line 32436)" and the Latin "Ficus  Cingalensis.", with the note that the pairing was proposed by an agent from the two OCR columns and checked by script.

**The pairing, checked by counting.** On the page of N-names (page number "74" at line 32338) the Sanskrit run ends "... The Latin run ends "See Usira", **"Ficus Cingalensis"**, "Cocos Nucefera. Cocoa-nut.", "Calamus rotang", "See Kantakari", "See Danti", "Indigofera Tinctoria", "Nymphea Stellata", "Melia Azadirachta Indica", "Citrus Medica", "Kyllingia Monocephala", "Vitex Negundo", "Curcuma Longa", "Vigna Catjang", "Wild variety of Oryzo sativa", "Ficus Bengalensis" (lines 32434 to 32456); the Ficus Cingalensis line is also fifteenth from the end. The neighbours fit: Nalada with "See Usira", Narikela with "Cocos", Nichula with *Calamus rotang*, Nirgundi with *Vitex negundo*, Nisi with turmeric and, at the end, Nyagrodha with *Ficus bengalensis*. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* A search of all chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) for Nandi found the following. Several other words begin the same way (Nandimukha and Nandimukhi, Sutra 46 lines 110, 165, 188 and 741, and Nandi-Varalaka, line 824, are different names; the appendix prints "Nandi-mukha" as a bird at line 30814) and are not this plant.

*The Nyagrodhadi group (Sutra ch.38).* `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 344 to 351: "The drugs known as Nyagrodha, Audumvara, Ashvattha, Plaksha, Madhuka, Kapitana, Kakubha, Amra, Koshamra, Chorakapatra, the two sorts of Jamvu, Piyala, Madhuka (Maula), Rohini, Vanjula, Kadamva, Vadari, Tinduki, Sallaki, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Bhallataka, Palasha, and Nandi-Vriksha, combinedly form the group known as the Nyagrodhadi Gana." The metrical text (lines 353 to 359): "This group proves beneficial in cases of ulcer, cures all disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones and all sorts of secretions of the body in addition to its astringent properties (Sangrahi) and proves curative in a case of haemoptysis. It is an anti-fat and assuages the burning sensation of the body." Nandi-Vriksha is the last member. The text speaks of the group, not of its members singly.

*Uses of the group.* The group is named again, without listing members, in `SS_Chikitsa_ch01.txt` lines 665 to 668 (its drugs "should be used in any of those seven forms, in the case of an ulcer due to the aggravated Pitta"), `SS_Chikitsa_ch20.txt` line 81 (an oil cooked with the decoction of "the drugs of the Kashaya (Nyagrodhadi) and Madhura (Kakolyadi) groups" to heal an ulcer after purification), `SS_Sharira_ch02.txt` lines 92 to 94 (a ghrita "prepared with the drugs included within the groups of Parushakadi and Vatadi (Nyagrodhadi) Ganas" for a pus-like appearance of the fluid, in the chapter on the purification of semen) and `SS_Sharira_ch10.txt` lines 780 to 783 (a plug soaked in "the expressed juice of the drugs of the Nyagrodhadi group", inserted into the vaginal passage, in the chapter on the management of pregnant women). These passages bear on Nandi-Vriksha only if the group's last name is the appendix's Nandi, which the text does not say.

*The recipe against Sita-putana (Uttara ch.34).* `SS_Uttara_ch34.txt` lines 10 to 12: "The decoction of Kapittha, Suvaha (Rasna), Vimbi, Vilva, Prachivala (?), Nandi (Vata) and Bhallataka should be used in sprinkling (the child's body)", in the chapter on the treatment of an attack by Shita-putana. The brackets are the translator's, including the question mark for Prachivala. The gloss "(Vata)" makes Nandi the banyan in this chapter.

**Identity doubt.** The appendix pairs Nandi with "Ficus Cingalensis" (line 32436) and pairs Nyagrodha with "Ficus Bengalensis" (line 32456). By the same kind of count on the page of V-names (lines 33214 to 33262), the appendix pairs Vata with "Ficus Bengalensis" as well (Vata is the second name from the end of its Sanskrit run, against the second Latin line from the end; the Latin line is 33261, listed in names.csv as unpaired). The chapter bracket "Nandi (Vata)" in Uttara 34 therefore names the banyan, which the appendix does not give to Nandi. The two readings cannot both hold, and the Codex does not choose. ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Ficus* is a genus of the mulberry family (Moraceae), whose members carry a milky latex. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. If the Nandi-Vriksha of the Nyagrodhadi group is this tree, it is one member of a group the text uses for ulcers, uterine disorders, fractures and haemoptysis; the text gives it no action singly. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters give these preparations of the group or the recipe: a decoction (Uttara 34, for sprinkling the child), an oil cooked with a decoction of the group (Chikitsa 20), a ghrita (Sharira 2), and a plug soaked in the expressed juice (Sharira 10), and the seven forms (decoction and the rest) for the group (Chikitsa 1). No quantity is given for Nandi in any of them. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Nandi-Vriksha only as the last member of the Nyagrodhadi group (Sutra 38) and as one ingredient of a sprinkling decoction (Uttara 34). Both are therapeutic contexts (ulcer, uterus, bone, a child's illness). The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with this tree. It is a group member and a recipe ingredient, and the Latin name behind it is a translator's appendix pairing that his own chapter bracket contradicts.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this plant. The identity is open between two figs, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Ulcers, bleeding in pregnancy and a child's illness need medical care. No conservation information was read.
