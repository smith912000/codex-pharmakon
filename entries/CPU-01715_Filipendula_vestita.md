# Filipendula vestita — hbam.po, Bhampo, Bampoe, "Meadow Sweet" (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Filipendula vestita (Wall ex G. Don) Maxim, the first of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for hbam.po (with Achillea setacea), under the English name "Meadow Sweet"; the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints "Heracluem millefolium" in the place that column order gives to "Bhampo"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT), and under this binomial only by Clark.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The genus *Filipendula* is named once elsewhere on disk, at genus level and as the translators' suggestion only: Dioscorides 3-135 ("OINANTHE") prints "SUGGESTED: Oenanthe, Filipendula [Fuchs], Filipendula vulgaris [Bauhin], Spiraea filipendula [Linnaeus], Filipendula hexapetala [in Sprague] — Dropwort [Mabberley]" and the heading "POISONOUS" (`Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, lines 7724 to 7726). That is a different species under a different Greek name, with its own entry (I_1197), and is not read as a mention of this plant. Yang 1998, Ebers, the Assyrian text and Sushruta name no *Filipendula*. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. Clark's preface (line 83) says: "Therefore more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance", and here the line prints two, of which the spine row takes the first (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, line 172). The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1074).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 ("The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines", line 792), within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The entry follows *rgud.drus* (*Senecio scandens*, line 1073) and precedes *mkhan.pa* (mugwort, line 1078). It reads:

"hbam.po: Meadow Sweet: Filipendula vestita (Wall ex G. Don) Maxim: Achillea setacea: [has two names and grows around meadows. It has a leaf, flower and stalk similar to fennel, is malodorous and has a thick taste (i.e., phlegm-like or hard to distinguish). But for the fact that it has fewer leaves it would easily be mistaken for rtsad. The male type has a flower with stalk, whilst the female or black type lacks these. Both types] reduce swelling, remove internal abscesses (ulcers) [and glandular growths]."

Clark's preface (line 82) says: "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". Inside the brackets (the commentary layer) are the two names, the habitat, the likeness to fennel, the smell and "thick taste", the warning about *rtsad*, the male and female types, and the clause "and glandular growths". Outside the brackets (the root-text layer, as far as Clark's brackets mark it) are the Tibetan name, the English name "Meadow Sweet", the two Latin names, which are Clark's identifications and not the root text's, and the clause "reduce swelling, remove internal abscesses (ulcers)". Clark's index of Latin names lists "Filipendula vestita" (line 1585), and the English index lists "Meadow Sweet" (line 1614).

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text on "Bampoe" (line 3448).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225), within the run headed "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (line 3428). The sentence reads: "Gudue treats open wounds, joins ruptured blood vessels and cures intestinal pains. Bampoe subsides swelling and dissolves cancer of the visceral organs." The names stand in the same order as Clark's *rgud.drus* and *hbam.po* (lines 1073 and 1074), and the action ("subsides swelling") agrees in kind with Clark's "reduce swelling". The text prints the name as "Bampoe", the glossary and index print it as "Bhampo" (the index at line 7269 gives "Bhampo 209"), and the Codex reads them as one name from the spelling, the order and the action. The text gives no Latin name in the sentence. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary.* The Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, the "B" block (the letter at line 5064), prints the phonetic names "Bhalu", "Bhampo" and "Bhashaka" (lines 5091 to 5093), the transliterations "ba lu", "’bam po" and "ba sha ka" (lines 5095 to 5097) and, in the scientific-name run, the lines "Rhododendron sp. Heracluem millefolium" (line 5099) and "Justicia Adhatoda" (line 5100). Counting in order, "Bhalu" stands against *Rhododendron* sp., "Bhampo" against "Heracluem millefolium" and "Bhashaka" against *Justicia adhatoda*. The transliteration "’bam po" is Clark’s *hbam.po*. The Codex therefore reads the glossary as pairing "Bhampo" with a binomial printed "Heracluem millefolium", by column position only. The Codex does not correct the printed genus. The epithet *millefolium* is the epithet of yarrow (*Achillea millefolium*), which is a general fact, and Clark's second binomial for *hbam.po* is an *Achillea* (*Achillea setacea*), so the two Tibetan texts may be pointing at the same genus. The glossary does not print *Filipendula* anywhere, and the binomial the spine row uses, *Filipendula vestita*, is Clark's first choice only. The Codex does not choose. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Filipendula*, *hbam.po*, *Bhampo* and *Bampoe* found no entry on this drug. I_1197 (*Spiraea filipendula*, tag DMM) is the Dioscorides entry on the dropwort of 3-135, and I_809 (Meadowsweet, *Filipendula ulmaria*, tag EF) is a European-herbal entry on another species; neither names the Tibetan drug. I_1224 (*Adhatoda vasica*) quotes the same glossary block (lines 5091 to 5100) for its neighbour "Bhashaka" and is an entry on another plant. I_1212, I_728 and I_832 mention *Filipendula* only in passing, in the prose of other plants. The spine has a row for yarrow (CPU-00552, *Achillea millefolium*, written as I_770) with Badianus and Dioscorides tags and no FMT tag, and a row for *Achillea ageratum* (I_977, Dioscorides); the glossary's "millefolium" and Clark's *Achillea setacea* are the reason to name them here, and neither entry was read as a source for this one. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Filipendula* is a genus of the rose family (Rosaceae) known as meadowsweets and dropworts, and that *Achillea* is a genus of the daisy family (Asteraceae) known as yarrows, so Clark's two names and the glossary's "millefolium" point to plants of two unrelated families. Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a plant that "reduce[s] swelling, remove[s] internal abscesses (ulcers)" (Clark, line 1074) and "subsides swelling and dissolves cancer of the visceral organs" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3448). These are the texts' claims, attached to this binomial only by Clark's first choice among two, and they are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no quantity, no frequency and no duration. Clark's commentary layer describes the leaf, flower and stalk, the male and female types, and does not say which part is taken. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Tibetan terms ("swelling", "internal abscesses", "glandular growths", "cancer of the visceral organs") are not modern diagnoses or measured outcomes, so no modern finding can be set beside them here. The identity of the drug is open between two genera, so a study of any *Filipendula* or *Achillea* species would not bear directly on it. It is a general, well-established fact that a swelling that persists, or any suspected cancer, needs medical assessment.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Clark's commentary layer (line 1074) gives the plant a "thick taste (i.e., phlegm-like or hard to distinguish)" and a bad smell ("malodorous"). Neither is one of the six tastes, and the parenthesis says that the taste is hard to place. No power or post-digestive taste is printed for it. The name does not appear in the Men-Tsee-Khang taste-group lists of chapter 19 (which begin at line 3204) that were read for this entry.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica gives this drug by what it does: it reduces swelling and removes internal abscesses, and the Men-Tsee-Khang text says the same in kind. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this name places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the sentences cited above were read in full and none mentions one. Its framing is therapeutic only, and the link from this binomial to that framing rests on Clark's first choice between two.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a drug for swellings and internal abscesses in the Tibetan texts, with no preparation and no dose, and its Latin name is one of two that Clark offers, with a third in the glossary. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. The identity is open between genera, and no preparation or dose is given, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Internal abscesses and growths need medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
