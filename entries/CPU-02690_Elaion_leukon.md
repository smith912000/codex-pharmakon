# Elaion leukon (white olive oil) — ELAION LEUKON, "To Whiten Oil" (Dioscorides 1-32)

## *Olive oil (Olea europaea, the plant of the neighbouring Dioscorides chapters) that has been worked by the method of 1-32 until it turns white; the chapter describes a preparation, not a plant, and the botanical link is ours, from the chapter's position among the olive oils, at medium confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** This material is attested only in Dioscorides, *De Materia Medica*, book 1, chapter 32, headed ELAION LEUKON and subtitled "To Whiten Oil" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 3812). No other text on disk was found to name a whitened oil under this name. The entry is a preparation of a plant product and is kept separate from the olive plant row (CPU-00034) because the chapter is about a procedure for changing the oil, and the oil it starts from is stated to be an ordinary clear oil.

**Where the chapter stands.** It follows 1-29 (new oil from unripe olives, *elaion omotribes*), 1-30 (old olive oil, *elaion koinon*) and 1-31 (wild olive oil, *elaion agrias elaias*), and precedes 1-33 (Sicyonian oil, *elaion sikuonion*), which calls for "the white oil [above]" as one of its ingredients (line 3815 onward). The translator's SUGGESTED lines for the neighbouring chapters give *Olea europaea* and related names; 1-32 carries no SUGGESTED line of its own.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter reads, in the English translation on disk: take oil "which is a clear colour and not over a year old" and pour it into a new broad-mouthed ceramic jar, "an amount of fifty pints". Place it in the sun and pour it back with a spoon every day around noon, "letting it fall down from on high, so that it may be altered by frequent rolling and beating, and foam". On the eighth day fifty teaspoonfuls of clean fenugreek, softened in warm water, are put into the oil "without straining out the water", together with "the same weight of pinewood" cut small; eight more days pass. The oil is stirred again and, "if it is white", poured into a new jar that has first been rinsed with old wine, with eleven teaspoonfuls of the corolla of melilot flowers (cross-referenced 3-48) and the same amount of iris scattered in. "If it is not (yet white) it must be set out again in the sun and treated until it becomes white." The chapter gives no property and no therapeutic use for the finished oil. Its use in the text is as an ingredient of the Sicyonian oil of 1-33, which Dioscorides says is "somewhat warming, suitable for fevers and affected nerves", with women using it "to have a clean skin" (that sentence belongs to 1-33 and not to the white oil itself).

**Other olive-oil statements nearby (context, not this entry).** Chapter 1-30 says that "commonly all oil is warming and softens flesh" (line 3803 onward); that is a statement about oil in general, which is the nearest the text comes to a quality for any oil, and it is not assigned to the whitened oil.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, olive oil is a fat made up mostly of triglycerides rich in oleic acid, with small amounts of free fatty acids, sterols and aromatic compounds that give oil its smell and colour. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is.** A method for making a clear oil white, by a sixteen-day routine of sunning, pouring, foaming, and steeping with fenugreek and pine wood, then storing with melilot flowers and iris in a jar rinsed with old wine.
- **What it is for.** The chapter does not say. The only use found is as an ingredient of Sicyonian oil (1-33).
- **What the Codex does with it.** Nothing. The Codex does not translate a first-century workshop method into a home protocol, recommends no oil preparation of this kind, and gives no modern use.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparation is the whole of the chapter, and the quantities it prints are quantities of materials for making the oil, not doses to be taken: fifty pints of oil (the translator's rendering), fifty teaspoonfuls of fenugreek, the same weight of pinewood, eleven teaspoonfuls of melilot corolla and as much iris, timed at eight days and eight days more (line 3812). The text gives a visible endpoint (the oil is white) and a rule to repeat the sunning if it is not. No dose and no route of use is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Oil exposed to sun, air and repeated agitation is oxidised and can go rancid, which changes its smell and taste; oil mixed with water-containing plant material can also grow moulds or bacteria if stored damp. Whether the whitening described is a colour change from bleaching, from emulsion, or from suspended solids is not stated and is not known to this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** No quality (warming, cooling, astringent) is printed for this oil in chapter 1-32. The general line at 1-30, that "all oil is warming and softens flesh", belongs to oil as a class and is not assigned here. None is imported.
- No other tradition attests this material.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter offers a cultivator is the discipline of attention that runs through his work: the starting oil is specified (clear, not over a year old), the process has a daily rhythm, the endpoint is judged by the eye ("if it is white"), and the failure case is named (return it to the sun). That is a method of provenance and sensory assay, recorded as a craft. It carries no cultivation status in the sources read.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to practise. This is an oil-working recipe whose product is an input to another oil. It is of interest for how the source describes a process judged by eye, and for nothing about the person's vital constitution; no source retrieved says otherwise.

### Contraindications

- **Ingestion and skin.** The chapter gives no use for the oil, so no safe use is established. Home-prepared oil held warm and wet for sixteen days is a hygiene risk (rancidity, mould, bacterial growth).
- **Allergy.** Fenugreek, melilot, iris and pine products can each cause reactions in some people; the text says nothing on this. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
