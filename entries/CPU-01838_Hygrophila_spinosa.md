# Hygrophila spinosa — Kokilaksha, Ikshuraka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Hygrophila spinosa, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Hygrophila  Spinosa.", line 32046) against the Sanskrit name Kokilaksha (OCR "Eokila'ksha", line 31974). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print Kokilaksha once, as the translator's bracket after the name Ikshuraka, in the vajikarana chapter; they give no Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. Of the ten plants in this batch it is the one whose Sanskrit name the translator ties, in a bracket, to a recipe in the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26); the Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Kokilaksha and its OCR variants (Kokilaksha, Kokildksha, Kokilikshaka, KokiMksha) and by fuzzy matching of every word. The name is printed in two chapters.

*The vajikarana recipe (Chikitsa ch.26).* The chapter opens (`SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` lines 1 to 6) as the one "on the medical treatment for increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons (Kshina-Valiyam Vaji-Karana)", and defines the class: "if duly taken, the Vaji-karana remedies make a man sexually as strong as a horse (Vaji), and enable him to cheerfully satisfy the heat and amorous ardours of young maidens, a fact which has determined the nomenclature of this class of (medicinal) remedies." Lines 173 to 177 read: "The use of pulverised Atmagupta and Ikshuraka (Kokilaksha) seeds mixed with sugar and taken with milk just milched enables a man to indulge in the pleasures of youth for the whole night without any sense of fatigue." The bracket "(Kokilaksha)" is the translator's. The text names Ikshuraka and the translator glosses it; the part used is the seed; no quantity, duration or course is given. The passage goes on to Uchchata powders, Satavari and Uchchata roots, a soup of Atmagupta seeds with pulse, and Atmagupta, Gokshura and Uchchata boiled with milk.

*A seed in a urinary recipe (Uttara ch.58).* `SS_Uttara_ch58.txt` lines 218 to 223, in the chapter on suppression of urine: "A compound consisting of Vala, Svadamshtra, seeds of lotus (Krouncha), Tandula (i.e., seeds) of Kokilaksha, roots of Shata-parvan, Devadaru, Chitraka and stone of Aksha, pasted together and dissolved in wine should be taken by a person under the circumstances inasmuch as it removes the defects of urine and eliminates the gravels from the bladder." A translator's note (line 252) says: "Some explain 'Kokilikshaka-tandula' as meaning Kokiliksha and Tandula (rice) which meaning may also be accepted."

*The bare name Ikshuraka.* The same Sanskrit name stands without the bracket in four more places, which the Codex lists without claiming that the bracket applies to them: a Ksharagada (alkaline antidote) of burnt woods (`SS_Kalpa_ch07.txt` line 13); an alkali of the ashes of Nila, Ikshuraka, Sarshapa, Yava, Palasha and Mulaka with the urines of five animals (`SS_Uttara_ch42.txt` lines 268 to 272); a decoction of roots in the chapter on Gulma, "Kshudra-saha (Mudga-parni) and Ikshuraka-roots" boiled with a Drona measure of water to a quarter, used with Yava-kshara against attacks of Shula of the Vataja, Pittaja, Kaphaja and Tridoshaja types (lines 631 to 640); and, in Uttara ch.58, a compound of honey, ghee churned from milk, sugar, grapes, Atmagupta seeds, Ikshuraka and Pippali (lines 268 to 281) and a ghrita whose Kalka and decoction include "seeds of Ikshuraka" (lines 302 to 304). A near spelling, "Kshuraka", stands in the Kalyanaka-Lavana drug list (`SS_Chikitsa_ch04.txt` line 281); the Codex does not tie it to this plant.

*Rasayana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Kokilaksha and Ikshuraka found nothing.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Hygrophila*, Kokilaksha and Ikshuraka found no entry on this plant or on the Chikitsa 26 passage. **I_1391** (*Barleria prionitis*, Koranta) names Kokilaksha only as the next name above its own in the pairing count. **I_1375** (*Panicum italicum*, Kangu) and **I_1380** (*Arum indicum*) print the Atmagupta Latin lines (31999, 31869) in other pairings; no entry covers Atmagupta itself. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapters print the seed in two recipes: a vajikarana powder with Atmagupta, sugar and milk (Chikitsa 26), and an ingredient in a wine-dissolved urinary compound (Uttara 58). The first is classed by the text as an aphrodisiac of the *vaji-karana* kind; the Codex does not recommend it, gives no protocol, and notes that the identity of the plant behind the name rests on a translator's bracket and an appendix pairing.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters give these preparations for the name: the seeds pulverised, mixed with sugar and taken with fresh milk (Chikitsa 26, lines 173 to 177); the seeds, with Vala, Svadamshtra, lotus seeds, Shata-parvan roots, Devadaru, Chitraka and Aksha stone, pasted and dissolved in wine (Uttara 58, lines 218 to 223). For the bare name Ikshuraka, a compound with honey, ghee, sugar and the rest of which "A Pani-tala (two Tolas) measure ... should be licked, followed by draughts of milk" (Uttara 58, lines 268 to 276). No quantity is given for the seeds in either Kokilaksha passage. No dose is given in the sources retrieved for the seeds themselves. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(a) Attested, in the vajikarana chapter.* Sushruta names Ikshuraka, glossed by the translator as Kokilaksha, in Chikitsa ch.26, the chapter on *vaji-karana* (the Sushrutan division for increasing strength and virile power), where a powder of its seeds with Atmagupta, sugar and milk is said to enable a man "to indulge in the pleasures of youth for the whole night without any sense of fatigue". The attestation is of the Sanskrit name in that chapter; the link to the Latin *Hygrophila spinosa* is the appendix's, not the chapter's. The chapter's stated aim is sexual vigour; the text does not say that the seed builds *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*, and none of the three is claimed here. The rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it. The same name in Uttara 58 is a urinary recipe. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** A single sentence in a vajikarana chapter, with no dose, course or duration. A cultivator has no practice to take from it: the text frames the recipe as an aphrodisiac for a defined purpose, and the plant's identity is a translator's gloss. Nothing here supports taking the seed.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for the seed. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Urinary retention, stones or blood in the urine need medical care, and a wine-based or aphrodisiac preparation is not a safe substitute. No conservation information was read.
