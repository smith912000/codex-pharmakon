# Vitis pedata — Godha-padi and Hamsa-padi (Sushruta, Bhishagratna's Appendix 3, as aligned by position)

## *A climbing plant named in the translator's appendix by the Latin synonym "Vitis pedata", apparently of the grape family (Vitaceae) from the genus name; the same Latin name is printed against two different Sanskrit names, Godha-padi and Hamsa-padi, so it is uncertain whether it is one plant or a translator's shared guess; the identification is ours by position, genus-level, low confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The only attestation is the Sushruta Samhita in Bhishagratna's English translation. A search of the other texts on disk for *Vitis pedata* found nothing. The Codex has a separate row for the grape (*Vitis vinifera*, CPU-00120), which is not this row.

**Sushruta (SS).** The Latin name stands twice in "Appendix No. 3. Names of Drugs (with Latin synonyms)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, lines 31714 and 31732), as ".  Vitis  pedata." and "..  Vitis  pedata.". The appendix prints the Sanskrit names and the Latin synonyms in separate columns, and the OCR has separated them. Counting down both lists:

- **Line 31714, G section.** The Sanskrit list runs *Gauri* (line 31660), *Gayatri* (31661), *Godha-padi* (31662, as OCR prints "Godhatpadi"), *Godhuma* (31663), *Gokshura* (31667). The Latin list runs "See Haridra" (31710), "See Khadira" (31712), "Vitis pedata" (31714), "Triticum Vulgare. Wheat." (31716), "Elephantopus Scaber", "Tribulus terestris" (31720). *Godhuma* against wheat and *Gokshura* against *Tribulus* are secure, so "Vitis pedata" falls against *Godha-padi*.
- **Line 31732, H section.** The Sanskrit list opens with *Hamsa-padi* (line 31677), then *Haridra* (31679), *Hari-mudga* (31681), *Haritaki* (31682). The Latin list opens with "Vitis pedata" (31732), "Curcuma longa" (31734, against *Haridra*), "Phaseolus mungo" (31736, against *Hari-mudga*), "Terminalia chebula" (31738, against *Haritaki*). So "Vitis pedata" falls against *Hamsa-padi*.

Both pairings are by position and rest on the translator's appendix. [USER: verify against the printed page.]

Neither Sanskrit name is explained in the translation. *Godha-padi* occurs nowhere else in the chapter files or volumes read. *Hamsa-padi* (printed "Hansapadi" in the body) occurs once, in Sutrasthana ch. 38, in the Vidari-gandhadi group: "Vidarigandha, Vidari, Sahadeva, Vishvadeva, Shvadanstra, Prithakparni, Shatavari, Sariva, black Sariva, Jivaka, Rishavaka, Mahasaha, Kshudra-Saha, Vrihati, Kantakari, Punarnava, Eranda, Hansapadi, Vrishchikah, and Rishavi" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 13-18; Vol. I line 15358). The metrical text says the group "subdues the action of the deranged Vayu and Pittam and proves beneficial in phthisis (Shosha), Gulma, aching of the limbs, Urdha Shvasa and cough." That action is stated for the group, not for one member. Whether the Hansapadi of that group is the plant the appendix glosses as *Vitis pedata* is the appendix's claim; the body text does not say.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The text offers a name in an appendix, and, if the Hamsa-padi pairing holds, one membership in a group of twenty drugs. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation and no dose is given in the sources retrieved. The Vidari-gandhadi group is named without quantities. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Vitis* in its older, wide sense included many climbers now placed in other genera of the grape family, so the Latin name by itself does not fix a plant. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* If the Hamsa-padi pairing is right, the plant sits in the Vidari-gandhadi group, whose stated action is on Vayu and Pittam and on wasting, Gulma, cough and breathlessness. That is a therapeutic frame. No *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter was read naming it, so no cultivation status is attested.
