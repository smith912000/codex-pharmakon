# Pushkarmool / Pushkaramula

## *Inula racemosa* Hook.f. | Family: Asteraceae (Compositae)

**Tradition Tags:** CM,SS,FMT

**Energetic Classification:** Warm · Pungent-Bitter · Drying · Downward-and-outward moving (chest-opening)

**Training Phase:** Phase II — Circulatory Consolidation (the cardiac-respiratory bridge)

---

### Origin & Tradition

Pushkarmool is one of the oldest cardiac and respiratory roots in the Himalayan pharmacopoeia — a stout, resinous Asteraceae rhizome gathered above 2,000 m from Kashmir to Himachal, so identified with wet mountain ground that its Sanskrit name binds it to standing water and the lotus.

**Ayurveda (Sanskrit: पुष्करमूल *Puṣkaramūla*, "root of the lotus-pool").** In the classical Ayurvedic corpus Pushkarmool is the named remedy for **hridroga** (हृद्रोग, heart disease) and for **parshvashula** (पार्श्वशूल, piercing pain in the flanks and chest) — a symptom-picture that maps with unusual precision onto exertional angina and pleuritic chest pain. It is simultaneously the go-to root for **shwasa** (श्वास, dyspnoea/asthma) and **kasa** (कास, cough). The dravyaguna doctrine classes it as *ushna* (heating), *tikta-katu* (bitter-pungent), *kapha-vata-hara* (reducing the cold, heavy, and windy humours that "obstruct the channels of the heart and lungs"). The therapeutic logic is explicit and mechanical: cold, sticky kapha congeals the **hridaya srotas** (channels of the heart); a hot, drying, pungent root melts and moves the obstruction. This is a genuine pre-modern ischaemia-and-bronchospasm doctrine, and Pushkarmool is its chief instrument — frequently paired with guggul (*Commiphora wightii*) in formulas aimed squarely at chest pain.

**Sowa Rigpa (Tibetan medicine; *ru rta* / *manu* group).** Across the Himalaya the same and closely allied *Inula* roots enter Tibetan formularies as warming, channel-clearing, "wind-and-phlegm"–dispersing agents used for stagnant chest and rlung (wind) disorders. The Tibetan reading rhymes with the Ayurvedic one: a warm aromatic root that mobilises cold obstruction in the trunk.

**Chinese Materia Medica (土木香 *tǔ mù xiāng*).** The dried root of *Inula racemosa* is an accepted source of *tu mu xiang*, classed as warm, pungent-bitter, entering the Liver, Spleen and Stomach channels to "move qi, relieve pain, and calm the middle." Here the emphasis slides toward the epigastrium and chest — qi-stagnation pain — but the shared thread with the Indian doctrine is unmistakable: a warming root that unblocks a stalled, painful thorax.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** Every tradition above encodes the same physical intuition in humoral language: *cold, heavy, sticky matter obstructs the vessels of the chest, and a hot, resinous, pungent root dissolves and moves it.* Translated into chemistry, the doctrine is a description of a lipophilic sesquiterpene-lactone resin. The "heat" and "pungency" the classical authors tasted are the electrophilic α-methylene-γ-butyrolactone warheads of alantolactone and isoalantolactone — the same reactive chemistry that irritates mucous membranes, stimulates secretion, and (as the modern data below show) engages redox and calcium-handling machinery in cardiac and airway tissue. The "melting of kapha obstruction in the heart-channel" is, read forward, an antioxidant-and-calcium-modulating action on ischaemic myocardium. The doctrine is not literally true — there is no cold sludge in the coronary arteries — but it is a serviceable folk-model of a real target: the oxidatively stressed, calcium-overloaded ischaemic heart. Where the doctrine over-reaches is in promising *cure* of established heart disease; the chemistry supports adjunctive protection, not reversal.

### Active Components

- **Sesquiterpene lactones** — **alantolactone** and **isoalantolactone** (eudesmanolides) are the signature and best-studied constituents; these two positional isomers are the resin's reactive core and have been chromatographically resolved from *I. racemosa* root.
- **Dihydro-derivatives** and related eudesmane sesquiterpenes.
- **Inulin** (the storage polysaccharide characteristic of the genus).
- **Essential-oil fraction**, phytosterols, and minor flavonoids/glycosides.

The α-methylene-γ-lactone of alantolactone is a Michael acceptor: it forms covalent adducts with protein thiols. This single structural fact underlies most of the pharmacology *and* most of the toxicology (contact allergy, mucosal irritation) — a recurring double-edge in the Asteraceae.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Pushkarmool is filed as a **Phase II Circulatory Consolidation** root: the bridge between respiratory opening and cardiac steadying. In the Kenetica frame it is invoked where the chest is described as *tight, cold, and gripping* — the somatic signature the classical texts called hridroga-with-shwasa. Its work is not stimulant excitation but obstruction-clearing: opening the breath while buffering the heart against oxidative and calcium stress. It sits alongside guggul-type resins as a chest-decongesting, angina-pattern ally, and is explicitly **not** positioned as a standalone treatment for diagnosed cardiac disease — it is an adjunctive, protective, breath-and-chest root.

### Scientific Correlation

Attribution: the following is based on articles retrieved from PubMed.

- **Whole-root cardioprotection (animal, moderate).** In an isolated-heart myocardial ischaemia–reperfusion model, *I. racemosa* root extract (100 mg/kg for 30 days) preserved haemodynamics and left-ventricular contractility, restored SOD/catalase/GPx/GSH, and prevented CK-MB and LDH leakage versus I-R controls (Ojha et al., 2010, PMID 21506500). A separate Langendorff study in hyperlipidaemic rats found root extract *restored* ischaemic-preconditioning cardioprotection, reducing infarct size, LDH, CK-MB and ventricular fibrillation, with the benefit abolished by an MPTP opener — implicating mitochondrial-permeability-transition inhibition (Tiwari et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3831/KPI.2022.25.4.369)). **Grade: MODERATE preclinical, consistent, no human trials retrieved.**
- **Isolated compound (mechanistic, moderate).** Alantolactone protected against isoproterenol-induced myocardial injury in vivo and cobalt-chloride hypoxic injury in vitro, lowering LDH/CK/cTnI, raising antioxidant enzymes, suppressing ROS and apoptosis, limiting calcium overload, and directly inhibiting the L-type Ca²⁺ current (IC₅₀ ≈ 17 µM) (Liu et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/20587384211051993)). This gives a plausible molecular route — antioxidant + calcium-channel modulation — for the anti-anginal doctrine. **Grade: MODERATE mechanistic.**
- **Direct cardiac electrophysiology (early, low).** An isolated frog-heart study of *I. racemosa* constituents reported a **negative inotropic and negative chronotropic** effect and blunting of adrenaline responses (Lokhande et al., 2006, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/j157v06n03_03)) — i.e. a rate- and force-lowering, β-adrenergic-dampening action consistent with reduced cardiac oxygen demand (the classical anti-anginal mechanism). **Grade: LOW (amphibian, single study), but mechanistically coherent.**
- **Placement in evidence-based herbal cardiology (review).** *I. racemosa* is catalogued among the better-characterised traditional cardiovascular botanicals for hyperlipidaemia and ischaemic heart disease (Mahmood et al., 2010, PMID 20067878). **Grade: review, context only.**

**Honest bottom line and nulls.** The ancient indication (chest pain / angina pattern) is *supported at the preclinical level* by convergent antioxidant, anti-apoptotic, mitochondrial-protective and calcium-modulating data — an unusually good doctrine-to-mechanism match. **However:** no randomised human trial of *I. racemosa* alone for angina was retrieved in this search. The **bronchodilator** claim implied by the shwasa/kasa indication is, in the retrieved literature, essentially unsupported at the compound level for this species — no direct airway-smooth-muscle study surfaced, so the anti-asthmatic reputation must be graded **UNSUPPORTED (data-absent)** here, however plausible by class. Doctrinal "cure of heart disease" is deflated to "adjunctive cardioprotection, animal-stage."

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Ayurveda:** *Rasa* — tikta (bitter), katu (pungent); *Virya* — ushna (heating); *Vipaka* — katu; *Karma* — kapha-vata-hara, hridya (cardiotonic), shwasahara (anti-dyspnoeic).
- **Sowa Rigpa:** warming, wind-and-phlegm dispersing, channel-opening.
- **TCM (tu mu xiang):** warm; pungent, bitter; Liver/Spleen/Stomach; moves qi, relieves pain.
- **Kenetica axis:** Warm / Drying / Downward-outward, chest-opening; Phase II consolidator.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The retrieved profile carries no Avicennan or Unani material for *Inula racemosa* at all; the "Chinese materia medica (土木香 *tǔ mù xiāng*)" section it does carry is Chinese, not Perso-Arabic, and cannot be read under the CM letter without repeating the cross-tradition drift the registry forbids. On Unani's own terms — the *ruh* and the natural, vital and animal faculties, and above all the finite endowment of innate heat and radical moisture whose conservation is the tradition's one genuine longevity idea — no source assigns this Himalayan root any status, no *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is attested for it, and the warming-drying description present belongs to other traditions' humoral language rather than to a sourced Unani classification. Cultivation status: **(c) absent** — and the CM tag itself reads as a mislabel for the Chinese (TCM) content actually in the entry.

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Puṣkaramūla* is a securely attested classical *dravya* — the named root for *hṛdroga* (हृद्रोग), *pārśvaśūla*, *śvāsa* and *kāsa*, classed *uṣṇa* in *vīrya*, *tikta-kaṭu* in *rasa*, *kaṭu* in *vipāka*, and *kapha-vāta-hara*, worked to melt cold obstruction from the *hṛdaya srotas*. Its recorded *karma* is *hṛdya* (cardiotonic) — a therapeutic, channel-clearing action, not a *rasāyana* or *vājīkaraṇa* assignment. No Sushrutan or Caraka rejuvenative/generative status for this species is recorded in the retrieved sources, so its standing is therapeutic and dietetic rather than a formal cultivation grade; it moves and warms rather than building *ojas*. **(b) framed** — richly attested in the materia medica, no *rasāyana* cultivation status sourced.

**Tibetan — Sowa Rigpa (FMT).** The same and allied *Inula* roots (the *ru rta* / *manu* group) enter Tibetan formularies as warming, channel-opening agents that disperse cold *rlung*-and-phlegm stagnation in the trunk. This is a therapeutic deployment against a *nyes pa* imbalance, not an assignment to *bcud len* (essence-extraction) or to any formal subtle-body-substance category — of which Sowa Rigpa has none. Medicine here is the supporting branch, not the root-level practice, and nothing in the retrieved material links this root to essence-cultivation. **(b) framed** — used, with no cultivation status recorded.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a longevity or essence-building substance in any of the three traditions actually in play. Pushkarmool is a warm, resinous, downward-moving chest root: Ayurveda works it against cold *kapha-vāta* obstruction of the heart-and-lung channels, Sowa Rigpa against cold *rlung*-phlegm stagnation, and the Chinese *tu mu xiang* stratum to move stalled qi in the epigastrium and chest. A cultivator uses it for a defined obstruction pattern — the tight, cold, gripping chest — as an adjunct, then stops; it is explicitly not a tonic taken long to build the constitution, and the reactive sesquiterpene-lactone chemistry (contact allergy, mucosal irritation) is itself the argument against casual long-term use.

### Contraindications

- **Sesquiterpene-lactone contact allergy.** Alantolactone/isoalantolactone are classic Asteraceae contact sensitisers; cross-reactivity within the Compositae is common. Avoid in anyone with known Asteraceae/parthenium dermatitis.
- **Pregnancy and lactation — avoid.** Warming, emmenagogue-leaning, reactive-lactone-bearing root with no reproductive-safety data; do not use.
- **Diagnosed cardiac disease — supervision only.** Given the negative inotropic/chronotropic and calcium-channel-blocking signals, do not combine with rate- or force-lowering cardiac drugs (β-blockers, calcium-channel blockers) without medical oversight; risk of additive bradycardia/hypotension.
- **Mucosal irritation / high dose GI upset** from the reactive lactones.
- Not a substitute for evidence-based angina therapy.
