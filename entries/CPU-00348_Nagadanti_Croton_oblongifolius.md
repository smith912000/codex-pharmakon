# Nagadanti — Nāgadantī, Dantī-bheda, Danti-related purgatives (Sanskrit); also Rāja-vṛkṣa group

## *Croton oblongifolius* Roxb. | Family: Euphorbiaceae
**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā)
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa — Kaṭu (pungent), Tikta (bitter); Vīrya — Uṣṇa (heating); Vipāka — Kaṭu (pungent post-digestive); Guṇa — Laghu (light), Tīkṣṇa (sharp, penetrating); Karma — Bhedana / Virecana (purgative-cathartic), Kapha-Vāta-hara
**Training Phase:** Purgative and channel-clearing preparations (Śodhana); advanced-practitioner use only — never a beginner's herb

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** In the surgical tradition of Suśruta the *danti* group of drugs occupies the shelf of the powerful drastic purgatives (*tīkṣṇa virecana dravya*) — agents reserved for stubborn Kapha- and Vāta-locked accumulations, ascites (*udara*), obstinate skin disease (*kuṣṭha*), abdominal masses, and the deliberate downward evacuation (*virecana*) that Śodhana therapy demands before the milder rebuilding phase can begin. *Nāgadantī* — literally "serpent's tooth," a name shared across several sharp-acting Euphorbiaceous purgatives — sits in this dangerous, respected register. *Croton oblongifolius* Roxb., the oblong-leaved croton of the deciduous forests of the Indian subcontinent, Thailand and mainland Southeast Asia, is the plant most often carried under this and cognate vernacular names in the eastern and Himalayan-foothill materia medica, where its root, bark and seed oil have long been used as a drastic cathartic. The Ayurvedic pharmacology of such agents is unambiguous: they are *bhedana* (that which breaks and splits obstruction) and *virecana* (that which carries downward), and they are handled with the caution owed to a scalpel.

**Wider folk and Southeast-Asian tradition:** Across Thailand, Myanmar and Bengal the stem bark and roots of *Croton oblongifolius* — known regionally by names such as *plao yai* — have been employed for dyspepsia, dysmenorrhoea, worm infestation, fever and as a purgative and emmenagogue. This is a shared feature of the genus *Croton*, whose most notorious member, *Croton tiglion* (croton oil), is one of the most violent cathartics and skin irritants known to classical pharmacy. The kinship matters: it is the reason the whole group is hedged with warnings.

> ⚠️ **Toxicity flag — Croton genus:** Members of *Croton* (Euphorbiaceae) contain phorbol-ester and diterpene constituents historically associated with severe gastrointestinal irritation, purgation, mucosal blistering and — in the case of *C. tiglium* oil — tumour-promoting activity. *C. oblongifolius* is a distinct species with a different (clerodane/cembranoid diterpene) profile, but it belongs to a family where drastic irritancy and toxicity are the default assumption. Treat every internal use as a controlled Śodhana intervention under a qualified practitioner. It is not a food, tonic, or self-administered herb.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Serpent's Tooth and the Logic of the Downward Path:**
The name *Nāgadantī*, the serpent's tooth, is a piece of pharmacological poetry that also encodes a warning. In the Indic imagination the serpent (*nāga*) is simultaneously venom and medicine, death and the shedding of skin — the creature whose bite kills and whose venom, rightly handled, heals. A "tooth" of that serpent is a controlled dose of a lethal edge. The whole doctrine of *virecana* in Ayurveda rests on this paradox: to clear a body clogged with morbid Kapha and inflamed Pitta you introduce, deliberately, an agent violent enough to force the system into downward evacuation — you use a small, timed poison to expel a larger, settled one. This is *homeopathy of the drastic* long before Hahnemann: like clears like, the sharp expels the stuck. In the Kenetica frame the danti-type purgatives sit at the doctrinal boundary between medicine and toxin, the place where the practitioner's judgement — dose, timing, patient strength (*bala*), season — is the only thing separating cure from injury. The serpent's tooth is not to be feared; it is to be respected, and never picked up carelessly.

### Active Components

*Croton oblongifolius* is a rich source of **clerodane and cembranoid diterpenoids**, the class that dominates its chemistry and its biological activity. According to PubMed, chemical study of the roots yielded two new *ent*-clerodane diterpenes together with seven known clerodanes, which showed mild cytotoxicity against several human tumour cell lines (Youngsa-ad et al., 2007, *Planta Medica*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2007-990247). From the stem bark a cembranoid diterpene, **neocrotocembranal**, was isolated and shown to inhibit thrombin-induced platelet aggregation and to be cytotoxic to P-388 leukaemia cells in vitro (Roengsumran et al., 1999, *Journal of Natural Products*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/np9900268). The genus more broadly contributes phorbol-type diterpene esters (the family's irritant/purgative principle), phenolics, and a fixed oil in the seed. It is the diterpene fraction — not any single "active" — that carries both the traditional cathartic action and the emerging cytotoxic interest.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Within Kenetica, Nāgadantī is filed as a **drastic downward-clearing agent (*virecana / bhedana*)** — a tool of the purgation phase, never of maintenance. Its classical indications map onto states of heavy Kapha-Vāta obstruction: sluggish, cold, congested abdominal conditions, obstinate accumulations, and the pre-treatment clearing that precedes deeper work. In the training-phase language of the Codex it belongs to the *śodhana* (purification) arc — the deliberate emptying-out that makes the vessel ready — and explicitly **not** to any tonic, nootropic or daily-practice register. Its cytotoxic and antiplatelet diterpenes (see Scientific Correlation) are of research interest only and do not license internal experimentation. The honest Kenetica use-case is narrow: a supervised purgative, understood as a scalpel, deployed once and withdrawn.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the documented pharmacology of *Croton oblongifolius* is at present **phytochemical and in-vitro**, not clinical:

- **Diterpene cytotoxicity:** Novel and known clerodane diterpenes from the roots showed *mild* cytotoxicity (IC₅₀ ≈ 10–50 µg/mL) against HuCCA-1, KB, HeLa, MDA-MB231 and T47D tumour cell lines (Youngsa-ad et al., 2007, *Planta Medica*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2007-990247).
- **Antiplatelet + cytotoxic cembranoid:** Neocrotocembranal inhibited thrombin-induced platelet aggregation (IC₅₀ ≈ 47 µg/mL) and was cytotoxic to P-388 cells (IC₅₀ ≈ 6.5 µg/mL) in vitro (Roengsumran et al., 1999, *J. Nat. Prod.*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/np9900268).

**Evidence grade: WEAK / preliminary.** These are isolated-compound, laboratory findings. There are no human trials of *C. oblongifolius*, no validated purgative dosing in the peer-reviewed record, and no safety data supporting internal self-use. The traditional cathartic reputation is not the same as demonstrated clinical efficacy, and the genus's known toxicity means the burden of proof sits firmly against casual use.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṭu (pungent), Tikta (bitter)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry), Tīkṣṇa (sharp/penetrating)
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha-Vāta-hara (reduces Kapha and Vāta); may aggravate Pitta and, in excess, provoke Vāta through over-depletion
- **Karma (actions):** Bhedana (breaks obstruction), Virecana (purgation), Krimighna (anthelmintic), Kaphaghna (Kapha-dissolving)
- **Gaṇa / group:** Among the *tīkṣṇa virecana* (drastic purgative) drugs of the surgical tradition; cognate in action with the *danti* (Baliospermum) group

### Contraindications

- **Absolute practitioner supervision** — a drastic cathartic of a toxic plant family; not for self-administration.
- Contraindicated in the depleted, elderly, very young, pregnant and lactating, and in anyone of low *bala* (strength).
- Genus contains irritant/tumour-promoting diterpene esters; mucosal contact and overdose can cause severe gastrointestinal injury.
- Antiplatelet diterpene activity means theoretical **bleeding-risk interaction** with anticoagulant/antiplatelet drugs.
- Not to be confused with, or substituted for, the milder *danti* (*Baliospermum montanum*) despite overlapping vernacular names — verify botanical identity before any use.
