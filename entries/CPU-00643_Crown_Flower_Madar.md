# Crown Flower / Madar — Arka, Alarka (Sushruta; Bhishagratna Appendix 3: "Calotropis gigantia")

## *Calotropis gigantea* (spine botanical; Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 prints "Calotropis gigantia" against both Arka and Alarka, while `_sources/sushruta.csv` gives *C. procera* for Arka; "Madar" is the spine's vernacular and is not printed in the Sushruta text read) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM, SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity warning.** This row was promoted from MASTER_INDEX (two traditions: Avicenna, Sushruta). Only the Sushruta tag has text behind it. Within Sushruta, the same name *Arka* is assigned to two different species by two project files: *C. procera* in `_sources/sushruta.csv` (Sutra ch.38 list row) and "Calotropis gigantia" in Bhishagratna's Appendix 3. A finished entry for Arka as *C. procera* already exists, I_393 (CPU-00347, tagged CM, SS), and the two entries overlap heavily. The Sushruta chapters also name *Arka* in many formulae without saying which species is meant. The Avicenna tag has no locus. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Chapter files are under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/`.

*Group membership (Sutra ch.38).* `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 102 to 111, reads: "The Arkardi Group:—The drugs known as the Arka, Alarka, the two kinds of Karanja, Nagadanti, Mayuraka, Bhargi, Rasna, Indrapushpi, Kshudrashveta Mahashveta, Vrishchikali, Alavana and Tapasha-Vriksha, enter into the composition of the group known as the Arkadi Gana." The metrical summary (lines 112 to 116) adds: "The group known as the Arkadi destroys Kaphani, fat, and the effects of poison. It acts as a vermifuge and a specific aseptic agent in the case of an ulcer and proves curative in diseases of the skin." The extraction (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, lines 90 and 91) lists both names against Vol. I line 15444. Ch.38 gives no identification here.

*Identification (Appendix 3).* Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`) prints, in the name column, "Arka" (line 31273), followed by Arka-mula and Arka-pushpi, and in the Latin column the entries "Calotropis gigantia.", "Aristolochia Indica." and "Gynandropsis Pentaphylla." in the same order (`names.csv`, lines 391 to 393). *Alarka* is paired in the same way with "Calotropis gigantia" (`names.csv`, line 364). These pairings were made from two OCR columns by position, and the OCR spelling is "gigantia".

*Other passages that name Arka (not an identification).* Sutra ch.39 lists *Arka* among drugs "possessed of purgative properties" (`SS_Sutra_ch39.txt`, lines 22 to 28) and among the errhines, where "the roots of plants commencing with Karavira and ending with Arka" are the part used (lines 48 to 61); *Alarka, Arka* appear in the Vata-pacifying group of the same chapter (lines 86 to 90). Ch.45 lists *Arka* among the seeds whose oils "are irritating, light, non-heat-making in their potency, and pungent in taste and digestion" (`SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`, lines 949 to 955). Ch.46 says "The Asavas (wines) of the Arka, Shelu, and Shirisha are beneficial to those suffering from the effects of any poison" (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 2818 to 2819). In Kalpa ch.6, in the treatment of an animal bite, "Clarified butter mixed with the milky exudation of an Arka plant" is prescribed "as an errhine" (`kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch06.txt`, lines 305 to 308; the chapter opens on rat-poisoning). The chapters 39, 45 and 46 passages are lists, and none states which species is meant.

*Rasayana or vajikarana.* A search of the Sutra, Chikitsa, Kalpa, Uttara and Nidana chapter files found *Arka* in many formulae and no named rasayana or vajikarana chapter.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** No locus was found. `_sources/avicenna.csv` has no *Calotropis*, "madar" or "akk" row, and no Canon chapter text is on disk. (I_393 also carries a CM tag for *C. procera*, and that tag was not examined here.)

### Active Components

The latex of *Calotropis* species is generally known to contain cardiac (cardenolide) glycosides, such as calotropin, and the latex is a recognised irritant to skin and eyes. This is a general statement about the genus, and no source read gives the composition. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The Sushruta passages above record *Arka* as a purgative, an errhine, a source of an irritating oil, a poison-antidote wine and a latex in butter for an animal bite, all as Sushruta's and none as a Codex protocol. The plant is a toxic one, the identification is unsettled, and the Codex gives no protocol for it.

### Preparation & Dosing

Sushruta ch.39 (lines 48 to 61) gives the part used (the root of *Arka* as an errhine); ch.45 gives oil from the seeds; ch.46 gives wines (*asava*); Kalpa ch.6 gives the milky exudation mixed with clarified butter as an errhine. No quantity is given in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The tag rests on a MASTER_INDEX row, not on a list row or a text read. No cultivation framing, and no attestation of any kind, was found. (The Canon carries no chapter references in this project.)

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* *Arka* heads the Arkadi group of ch.38 (a gana membership, which is a grading by therapeutic action and not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status), appears in the purgative, errhine and poison-antidote lists of chs.39, 45 and 46, and is a named ingredient in many formulae. It is not named in a rasayana or vajikarana chapter in the files searched, and nothing read ties it to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*. The Sushruta frame is therapeutic and purificatory (the errhine and purgative lists), and the claim here does not widen to a cultivation use. The identification with *C. gigantea* rests on an OCR-paired appendix. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a drug of purging and of poison treatment in Sushruta, and a toxic plant by general knowledge; the Unani tag is unsupported. The abstentive answer is the correct one: do not use it.

### Contraindications

*Calotropis* latex is generally recognised as toxic and irritating. It can injure the eye and skin and is dangerous if swallowed, and it is not a self-administration substance. None of the traditional uses above should be treated as advice. Anyone exposed to the latex, especially in the eye, or who has swallowed any part of the plant, should seek urgent medical care. Do not use in pregnancy.
