# Panicum italicum — Kangu, Kanguka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3)

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Tibetan text folders found "Panicum" only in Dioscorides' translators' SUGGESTED lines for 2-119 *KENCHROS* and 2-120 *ELUMOS* (lines 5511 to 5517: "Panicum chrus-galli", "Panicum miliaceum [Linnaeus] — Millet, Panic Millet", "Panicum [Fuchs], Setaria italica [in Sprague]"), which are separate chapters of another tradition and are not read for this entry. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. Quotations keep the translator's words; the OCR's garbled diacritics are normalised. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The name in the chapters.* The appendix prints "Kangu". The chapters print **"Kanguka"** in three places and "Kangu" in none. A search of the appendix text for "Kanguka" found nothing, so the equation of Kanguka with the appendix's Kangu is the Codex's inference and not a printed one. [USER: verify] The three chapter passages are read below.

*Food (Sutra 20, lines 43 to 50).* In the chapter on salutary and non-salutary regimen, the text lists "articles or substances which may be safely included within the food stuffs of all human beings": "the members of the group (Varga) known as the red Shali, the Shastika, the Kanguka, the Mukundaka, the Panduka, the Pitaka, the Pramodaka, the Kalaka, the Ashanaka, the Pushpaka, the Karddamaka, the Shakunahrita, the Sugandhaka, the Kalama, the Nivara, the Kodrava the Uddalaka, the Shyamaka, the Godhuma and the Venu, etc." In this list Kanguka stands among grains, between the Shastika rice and the Mukundaka.

*An ulcer powder (Sutra 36, lines 191 to 194).* In the chapter on miscellaneous remedies for swellings: "A pulverised compound consisting of Kanguka, Triphala, Lodhra, Kasisam, Shravana and the barks of Dhava and Ashvakarna powdered together, is possessed of a similar healing property," that is, a property of healing an ulcer, as the preceding recipe is said to have.

*An oil (Chikitsa 31, lines 70 to 73).* In the chapter on the Snehas (oils and fats): "The expressed oils of Shravana, Kanguka and of Tuntuka are used in imparting a yellow colour (to a cicatrix)."

*What the chapters do not say.* The grain section of Sutra 46 (food and drink) does not name Kanguka or Kangu. There, at lines 162 to 168, the *Kudhanya* (lesser grain) group is "Koradushaka, Shyamaka, Nivara, Shantanu, Varaka, Uddalaka, Priyangu, Madhulika, Nandimukhi, Kuravinda, Gavedhuka, Varuka, Todaparni, Mukundaka, and Venu-yava"; that group names "the four (black, red, yellow and white) varieties of Priyangus" (lines 182 to 185), and Priyangu is a separate name in the Codex's I_413. The three Kanguka passages put the name to three uses (a food grain in a list of safe foods, an ingredient of an ulcer powder with a mineral, and a source of an oil used to colour a scar), and the text does not say that they are one drug. The Codex does not decide it. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Kangu*, *Kanguka* and *Panicum italicum* found no entry on this name. I_643 (Foxtail Millet, *Setaria italica*; tags DMM, SBJ, SS) is the likely neighbour: its heading prints "Kangni / Kangani (Hin.)" and "Priyangu (Skt., contested)", and its SS paragraph says plainly that it asserts no Sushruta chapter or verse. The Latin *Panicum italicum* is, as a general botanical matter, an old name for *Setaria italica*, but the Codex has not checked its current standing, and it keeps this row separate until Smith decides. [USER: verify] I_465 (*Panicum miliaceum*) is another millet; I_413 (Priyangu, *Callicarpa macrophylla*) is the entry on the name Sutra 46 lists beside the lesser grains. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, the millets are small-seeded grasses (Poaceae) whose grain is mainly starch with some protein. The three Kanguka passages may or may not describe a grain, and nothing is stated here about the composition of any drug used for an ulcer powder or an oil. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Kanguka as a food in a list of safe foods, as an ingredient of an ulcer-healing powder and as the source of an oil for colouring a scar. These are the text's statements about a Sanskrit name, and the equation with the appendix's Latin is the translator's, by position. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives these preparations, each with its locus. Sutra 36, lines 191 to 194: Kanguka powdered with Triphala, Lodhra, Kasisam, Shravana and the barks of Dhava and Ashvakarna. Chikitsa 31, lines 70 to 73: the expressed oil of Kanguka (with Shravana and Tuntuka) for yellowing a cicatrix. Sutra 20: named as a food, with no preparation. No dose, part or quantity is given for Kanguka in any of the three passages. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Sushruta terms (a healing powder, an oil that yellows a scar) are not measured outcomes, so no modern finding can be set beside them. As a general fact, a cereal grain is a staple food and not a drug, and a mineral such as *kasisam* in an ulcer powder is not a food; the passages cannot all describe the same material, and the identification is open.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text names Kanguka as a safe food in a list of grains, and as an ingredient of an ulcer powder and of an oil. A search of the chapter files for "Kanguka" and "Kangu" found it in none of the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) or the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30), so the text assigns it no rejuvenative or generative status in the lines retrieved. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The popular sattvic food lists do not trace to Sushruta (CULTIVATION_REGISTER_SPEC) and are not used here. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. In the Sushruta scheme a name that may be a millet is food among safe foods, and the same name, in a different place, is an ingredient of a physician's ulcer powder. A millet eaten as a staple is a matter of diet, not of cultivation in the Codex's sense, and nothing should be taken as a drug on the strength of a Latin name the chapters do not give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read print no warning for the drug itself. The ulcer powder in Sutra 36 includes *kasisam*, a mineral substance and not a food; it should not be taken internally on the strength of this entry. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
