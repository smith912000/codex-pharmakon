## *The domestic goose and its fat, egg, guts and blood; the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary prints* Anser domestica *with the gloss "greese"; the Ebers names are Bryan's English renderings* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP, FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The English text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 *The Papyrus Ebers*, a translation with summaries; the Egyptian words are not printed. The goose heads the second half of Bryan's list of the animals drawn on for remedies, "Pigeon Goose Duck Stag Gazelle Ram Goat" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1398, page 34 of the book). The spine's list row is `_sources/ebers.csv` line 120, "Goose", source field "Ebers Papyrus; Ghalioungui 1987 trans., Glossary I (Medicaments)", a glossary not on disk and not read; line 67 of the same list has "Goose fat", *Anser* sp., source "Ebers; Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus", which was not relied on. The goose is the most frequent bird in the pages read, mostly as fat or oil:

- *Eye.* "To drive out fat-in-the-eyes" (xanthelasma): "Red-lead, Goose-grease. Smear the eyes therewith" (lines 2760-2766), and for cataract "Goose-grease and Honey was tried as a poultice" (line 2823).
- *Ear and mouth.* For a discharging ear, "Incense-in-Goose-Grease ground up with Cream-from-the-Milk-of-a-Cow" (line 3032). For the tongue and teeth, milk gargled and spat out, then, "If the disease persisted, the Milk is reinforced by Goose-grease and the gargle turned into a chewing-remedy", and a second chewing remedy of goose-grease, honey, incense, caraway seeds, fresh lead-earth and water, "chew it Nine Times" (lines 3128-3136).
- *Skin.* For scabs in every limb, a poultice of cyperus and red corn "crushed in Fresh Oil, Goose-oil, and Semen" (line 2644); and, in the diagnosis section, goose-fat among the poultice ingredients for a mattery tumour of the neck (line 3858). In the abscess called *uases*, a remedy of "Blood-of-a-Dove, Blood-of-a-Goose, Blood-of-a-Swallow, Blood-of-a-Vulture. Anoint therewith" (lines 2357-2358).
- *Anus.* For sores in the anus a poultice of "Egg-of-the-Goose, Guts-of-the-Goose. Clap on the anus" (lines 2080-2086).
- *Inside the body.* In a purgative, "a draught composed of Goosegrease and Lead-vitriol, flavoured though it be with Wine" (line 1686); in a drink of honey and goose-fat "let the man or woman drink it" (lines 1894-1895); in a "debility" remedy, rotten flesh, herbs-of-the-field and garlic "cooked in Goose-oil and swallowed for four days" (line 1377); and in a heart remedy, a drink of "Fat Flesh, Figs, Berries, Incense, Caraway, Nasturtium, Goose-grease, Writing-fluid, and Sweet Beer" (line 3434).
- *Other compounds.* Goose oil is an ingredient in a long compound in the nervous-system chapter (line 3185); the complaint heading is not printed in the lines I read.

Bryan's remarks on these ("tough is the only designation applicable to a 'drink'", line 3434) are his own. No recitation is reported with any goose compound in the lines read; since Bryan summarises, that is not proof the papyrus gives none. No deity is attributed.

### Active Components

The sources name none. In general: goose fat is mostly triglycerides, rich in unsaturated fatty acids compared with other animal fats; goose egg is yolk and white (protein, fat, cholesterol); guts are muscle and mucosa with the gut flora; blood is a fluid tissue of cells in plasma. No quantities are given here because none were read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice beyond goose as ordinary cooked food. The Egyptian goose items are a fat applied to eyes, ears, skin and mouth, an egg and guts for the anus, and fat, oil and blood in compounds taken by mouth. The Codex has no protocol for any of it and offers none. The reasons are specific: raw poultry fat, egg, guts and blood are infection routes, and smearing them on the eye, ear or open lesions is hazardous; lead-vitriol in the purgative and red lead in the eye salve are lead compounds that are toxic; the sources give no dose; and the Tibetan texts read give no goose medicine at all.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations Bryan gives are fat "made into one" with the other ingredients and smeared, ground with incense, used as a poultice with honey, chewed, or swallowed in a drink or draught; the chewing remedy is "nine times"; the debility remedy is taken "for four days". Bryan gives no quantities for the goose items. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Raw poultry, eggs and guts commonly carry *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*, and geese, like other waterfowl, are hosts of avian influenza viruses; fat from a cooked goose is a food fat. Red lead (lead tetroxide) and lead vitriol are lead compounds; lead is a cumulative poison, and ocular or ear application of any unsterile material risks infection. No effect of goose fat on xanthelasma, cataract, ear discharge, gum disease or purgation is established. No clinical studies of the traditional uses were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do. In Egypt the goose is a fat for dressings and a few drinks, and the Tibetan texts read give it no role. A cultivator may eat goose as food in the ordinary way, and need not follow the Egyptian eye, ear or anus remedies.

### Contraindications

- **Infection.** Raw goose fat, egg, guts and blood carry *Salmonella*, *Campylobacter* and other pathogens; never apply them to the eye, ear, anus or open lesions, and cook goose and eggs thoroughly.
- **Lead.** Red lead and lead vitriol, ingredients in the eye and purgative recipes, are poisonous.
- **Avian influenza.** Avoid contact with sick or dead waterfowl.
- **Egg allergy.** The goose egg poultice is unsuitable for people with egg allergy.
- **Ethics and diet.** The use means slaughter; unsuitable for vegetarians and vegans; geese and other waterfowl may be protected species in the wild, and domestic birds are regulated in some places.
- **Dose unknown.** No dose is given in the sources read.
