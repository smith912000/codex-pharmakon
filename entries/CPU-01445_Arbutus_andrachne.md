### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) printed on the chapter's SUGGESTED line, not Dioscorides' own identification, and this row was added from that line (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) without being verified against POWO. [USER: verify] The SUGGESTED line prints "Comaron, Comarus [Latin], Arbutus andrachne, Arbutus unedo", so the translators give two species for one Greek chapter. The plate caption nearby reads "Arbutus unedo after FAGUET — 1892" (line 4620, p.155), which names the other species and not this one. This row is therefore one of two species the translators allow, and the text does not choose. This is an identity doubt. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading at line 4799 (the chapter text follows at line 4801).

*1-175. KOMAROS* ("Fruit of Arbute Tree, Strawberry Tree, Cane Apples"). "Comarus is a tree similar to the cotoneae [1-160] fruit tree with a thin leaf, bearing fruit called memacyla, the size more or less of a prune, with no kernel. When ripe it is somewhat a yellowish or reddish colour, chaff. When eaten it is bad for the stomach and causes headaches." That is the whole chapter: it gives a description of the tree and fruit and a harm from eating it, and no medicinal use. The index prints KOMAROS at p.172. The preceding passage on the plum (the unlabelled chapter immediately above, ending "Rubbed on with vinegar it heals lichen") belongs to a different tree.

### Active Components

Plants of the heath family (Ericaceae) to which *Arbutus* belongs are generally known to contain the phenolic glycoside arbutin in their leaves. Whether the fruit or leaf of the tree Dioscorides describes does was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides records the fruit as bad for the stomach and a cause of headache. He gives it no therapeutic use. The Codex does not recommend it as a remedy, and the text itself warns against eating it.

### Preparation & Dosing

1-175 gives no preparation: the fruit is named, with the warning on eating it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter offers a cultivator is the discipline of attention that the cultivation spec describes for this tradition: how to know the plant by its parts, where it grows, what it is called, and what is claimed for it. 1-175 gives the tree's likeness (to the cotoneae fruit tree), the thin leaf, the fruit's name memacyla, its size, ripening colour and lack of a kernel, and a warning. It records no use for the person and assigns no status. The identification between the Greek tree and either *Arbutus* species is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. This is a chapter of recognition and warning, and the honest practice is to take the warning.

### Contraindications

Dioscorides records the fruit as bad for the stomach and a cause of headache. Whether the species is edible in general was not read. Do not forage from this entry. None of the traditional uses above is advice. [USER: verify]
