# Feldspar (Chang Shi) — Chang Shi, "Feldspar" (Yang 1998); other name Fang Shi (Rectangular Stone); list row 長石
## *Feldspar in the Divine Farmer's Classic; Yang 1998 prints "Feldspar" for Chang Shi with no footnote on the identification, while the spine marks the row id-uncertain* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Chang Shi is printed in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) on PDF page 30, under the heading "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (line 1168, the top of that page), as the fourth entry of the page. The text (lines 1186-1191): "Chang Shi (Feldspar) is acrid and cold. It mainly treats generalized fever and reversal cold of the limbs. It disinhibits urination, frees the flow of the blood vessels, brightens the eyes, eliminates screen causing blindness, removes the three [kinds of] worms, and kills gu toxins. Protracted taking may make one free of hunger. Its other name is Fang Shi (Rectangular Stone). It is produced in mountains and valleys." The OCR of the page is damaged in the entries above it but is clear for this one.

Yang prints no footnote on Chang Shi. The footnotes that follow on the page, 57, 58 and 59, belong to the entries above it (Ning Shui Shi, Ci Shi, Li Shi). Footnote 59, on Li Shi (Gypsum Fibrosum), says that "Although this medicinal's identity is not absolutely certain, it is probably either Gypsum or Calcitum" (lines 1199-1202). That footnote does not concern Chang Shi, but it shows that the stones of this page are treated by the translator as having uncertain Latin identifications. The list `_sources/shennong.csv` carries the row as 長石 (line 129), and `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` has the row "Chang Shi" (line 27) with the identification "Feldspar". The spine's note for this row is "middle; id-uncertain". The sources retrieved do not say why the spine marks the identification uncertain, and Yang does not print a doubt; the doubt is the spine's own. [USER: verify]

The other name "Fang Shi (Rectangular Stone)" is Yang's rendering of the name in the text; the Codex does not interpret it, and it is not a statement about the stone's crystal form in the sources read. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Feldspars are the commonest group of minerals in the Earth's crust: aluminium silicates of potassium, sodium or calcium. The spine's formula KAlSi3O8 is that of potassium feldspar (orthoclase or microcline). Feldspars are hard (about 6 on the Mohs scale), insoluble in water, chemically stable, and are an essential ingredient of ceramics and glass. The sources read give no composition for the stone Yang translates.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records the stone for fever and cold limbs, for urination and the blood vessels, for the eyes and "screen causing blindness", for worms and "gu toxins", and says that long taking may leave one "free of hunger". The Codex does not endorse these claims and offers no protocol. A ground silicate is not recommended for swallowing.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is printed in the monograph and no dose is given in the sources retrieved. The text says only "Protracted taking" (line 1189). [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Feldspar is chemically inert and insoluble, so it is not absorbed. It is an industrial mineral used in ceramics, glass and glazes. Feldspar dust, like other silicate dusts, is a nuisance to the lungs when inhaled; feldspar rock often contains some free quartz. No medical benefit of swallowing feldspar for the conditions the Classic names is established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- SBJ: "acrid and cold" (Yang line 1186). Class: Middle (heading line 1168). Yang's footnote 32 (lines 849-855) explains that his rendering omits the word "flavor" and that "balanced" means neither cold nor hot; this entry is "cold". No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: a middle-grade drug (中品).* Chang Shi stands under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (Yang line 1168). Yang's preface says the medium class medicinals "mainly nurture personality and correspond to humanity. They may or may not be toxic, and [therefore,] one should weigh and ponder before putting them to their appropriate use. If one intends to control disease, supplement vacuity, and replenish exhaustion, one should base [one's efforts] on the middle class" (lines 385-390). Yang's own note (lines 356-366) says this class "may be toxic" and that "their prescription requires care". The monograph prints "Protracted taking may make one free of hunger" (lines 1189-1190) and no longevity, lightness or immortality claim. The grade is a statement of the Classic as translated by Yang in 1998 and is not carried forward from later materia medica works. It attaches to the entry as printed; if the identification "Feldspar" is wrong, the grade stays with the Classic's stone and not with feldspar.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: not a "nourish life" drug for long use, but a drug for controlling disease, here a cold stone against fever. Feldspar is an inert silicate, so nothing in the sources supports taking it. For a cultivator the entry is a record of how the Classic ranked a stone Yang calls feldspar. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Powdered stone sold as a remedy may be a different mineral or may be contaminated; the spine's own doubt about the identification applies. Mineral medicines are unstandardised and their sale is regulated in many places. Silicate dust is a lung irritant. A cold stone given against fever is not a substitute for medical assessment of fever.
