### Origin & Tradition

Tree turmeric is a spiny Himalayan barberry whose bright-yellow root and stem wood are a major Ayurvedic drug — **Daruharidra** ("wood turmeric," for the turmeric-yellow colour of its inner wood, though it is unrelated to true turmeric). Its concentrated aqueous extract, boiled down to a solid, is **Rasanjana**, a classic eye medicine. It is the Indian counterpart to the European barberry (*Berberis vulgaris*, this volume) and the Oregon grape, all sharing the same vivid yellow **berberine** chemistry and the same broad therapeutic identity.

In Ayurveda Daruharidra is a **bitter, drying, *kapha-pitta*-reducing** drug used for a wide span of "hot," damp and infective conditions: as an eye remedy (Rasanjana for conjunctivitis and eye inflammation), for skin diseases and wounds (antimicrobial wash), for diarrhoea and intestinal infection, for the management of diabetes and metabolic ("prameha") conditions, for liver complaints and jaundice, and as a general antimicrobial bitter. The Unani tradition uses it similarly. Across all of these the active thread is **berberine** — the yellow isoquinoline alkaloid with broad antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, antidiarrhoeal and (much studied) glucose- and lipid-modulating activity.

Berberine is one of the most pharmacologically validated of all plant alkaloids, and Daruharidra is the principal Ayurvedic berberine source — a traditional drug whose chief constituent has a large and growing evidence base.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Ayurvedic mapping:** Daruharidra — *tikta-kashaya* (bitter-astringent), *ushna* (heating), *kapha-pitta* reducing; antimicrobial, *lekhana* (scraping/anti-obesity), eye and skin remedy.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Dāruharidrā* sits firmly on the therapeutic side of the *dravyaguṇa*: *tikta-kaṣāya* (bitter-astringent) in rasa, *uṣṇa* (heating) in vīrya, *kapha-pitta*-reducing, and *lekhana* (scraping) — worked against damp, hot and infective conditions and, as *rasāñjana*, as a classic eye medicine. It is a purgative-cleansing, *śodhana*-adjacent bitter, not a *rasāyana* or *vājīkaraṇa* drug: no rejuvenative or generative cultivation status is recorded for it in the sources retrieved, and it builds neither *ojas* nor generative essence. Its standing is corrective and therapeutic — the opposite pole from the tonic rasāyanas. **(b) framed.**

**Tibetan — Sowa Rigpa (FMT).** Barberry (*skyer pa*) is a genuine drug of the Tibetan materia medica, its bark and its boiled-down extract (*skyer pa'i khanda*) used for the same berberine-driven span — damp-heat, *tsha ba* (hot) disorders, eye complaints, wounds and gut infection. Within the Four Tantras medicine is the third of the four treatment branches, supporting rather than reaching the spiritual root, and there is no formal "substance for subtle-body practice" category into which this cooling bitter could be placed; none is claimed. It is used, with no cultivation status assigned. **(b) framed.**

**Practitioner's note.** This is a corrective, not a cultivator's tonic. In both traditions it is a bitter-cooling, drying, antimicrobial drug taken for a defined damp-heat or infective complaint — an eye wash, a gut antimicrobial, a skin decoction — and then stopped. Nothing here supports long-term self-administration to build vitality; a drying bitter over-taken depletes rather than nourishes, and the tradition's own cool-drying reading is the caution. Use it to clear a condition, then return to actual tonics for the rebuilding.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding — avoid.** Berberine is contraindicated in pregnancy (uterine-stimulant; can cause/aggravate neonatal jaundice — displaces bilirubin) and in nursing/newborns. [USER: verify.]
- **Neonatal jaundice.** Berberine-containing herbs are specifically contraindicated.
- **Drug interactions.** Berberine inhibits CYP enzymes/P-gp and can raise levels of co-administered drugs (e.g. ciclosporin); caution with many medications. [USER: verify interaction list.]
- **Hypoglycaemia.** Additive with antidiabetic drugs.
- **Dryness.** A drying bitter can aggravate very dry constitutions.
