# Caragana franchetia — gra.ma, Drama (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Caragana franchetia Kom, spelled as printed: the first of three binomials Clark 1995 prints for gra.ma (with Caragana brevifolia and Caragana pygmaea); the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text prints the name only as "Drama", without a Latin name in the running text* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** **Named only by the Tibetan texts, and under this binomial only by Clark.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. Both Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance", and here the line prints three, of which the row takes the first (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, row 185). The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1095).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The entry reads:

"gra.ma: Caragana franchetia Kom: Caragana brevifolia: Caragana pygmaea: [is a thorny bush with a dull yellow flower and white wood separated by a gap from the bark. It has bitter taste, cool power, and its] root cures flesh- and channel-fevers."

Clark's preface (line 82) says: "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". Inside the brackets (the commentary layer) are the description (thorny bush, flower, wood), the "bitter taste, cool power" and the opening words "and its". Outside the brackets (the root-text layer, as far as Clark's brackets mark it) are the Tibetan name, the three Latin names, which are Clark's own identifications and not the root text's, and the clause "root cures flesh- and channel-fevers". The sentence is cut across the bracket: its subject ("its") is in the commentary layer and its verb phrase is outside it. The line prints no number of names.

The next line, *mdzo.mo* (line 1096), says in its commentary layer that the tree is "larger than gra.ma", which ties the two drugs together in the commentary only. It is a separate drug and is treated in the *Caragana jubata* entry (I_1249).

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text (line 3457).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, chapter 20, in the section "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (it opens at line 3428). The sentence reads: "The root of the Drama cures hot disorder of the muscle tissues and the channels." The Codex reads "Drama" as the same name as *gra.ma* from the spelling and from the agreement in the part used (the root) and in the places named (the tissues and the channels, against Clark's "flesh" and "channel"); the text does not say so, and gives no Latin name in the passage. The Codex records each text's English as printed and does not set "hot disorder" equal to "fever". The printed index has no entry for "Drama" (the nearby entries are "Dramachen 135" and "Drak cham 210", lines 7346 to 7348). [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (lines 5191, 5209, 5246 and 5250).* In the "D" block of the Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients (the letter at line 5179), the phonetic names (lines 5181 to 5196) include "Drama" (line 5191, seventeenth) and the Tibetan transliterations (lines 5198 to 5213) include "bra ma" (line 5209, seventeenth). The seventeenth binomial in the scientific run is *Caragana gerardiana* (line 5246), and the seventeenth English name is "Pea shrub" (line 5250). So the glossary sets *Caragana gerardiana*, and not *Caragana franchetia*, against "Drama", by column position only. Clark prints *Caragana gerardiana* for the other drug, *mdzo.mo* (line 1096), as its second binomial. The two Tibetan texts therefore place *C. gerardiana* against different drugs, and the Codex does not resolve it. The glossary's Tibetan spelling is "bra ma" and Clark's is *gra.ma*; reading them as one name is an inference from the sound and from the Caragana binomials. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Caragana*, "Pea shrub", *Drama*, *gra.ma* and *Zhomo* found no entry on this plant. I_1172 (*Pterocephalus hookeri*) and I_1224 (*Adhatoda vasica*) quote Clark's line 1210 ("Caraganajubata" among the blood-disorder medicines) in their prose, and neither is an entry on a *Caragana*. I_1248 (*Caragana gerardiana*) and I_1249 (*Caragana jubata*) are the sibling rows in this batch. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Caragana* is a genus of thorny shrubs of the pea family (Fabaceae). Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained, which may be another species of the genus. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a drug whose root "cures flesh- and channel-fevers" (Clark, line 1095) or "cures hot disorder of the muscle tissues and the channels" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3457). These are the texts' claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give the root as the part used and no preparation. No quantity, frequency or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica classes this drug by what its root cures, which is flesh- and channel-fevers, and the Men-Tsee-Khang text says the same of the root of "Drama". The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this drug places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: a search of both Tibetan texts for *gra.ma*, "Drama", *Caragana* and "Pea shrub" returned only the lines above and the glossary entries. Its framing is therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a root drug for fevers of the flesh and channels in the Tibetan texts, and the two texts pair the name with different *Caragana* species. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. The identity is uncertain, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Fevers need medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
