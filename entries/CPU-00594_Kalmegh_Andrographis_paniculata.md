# Kalmegh — King of Bitters, Chuān Xīn Lián, Kariyat, Green Chiretta

## *Andrographis paniculata* (Burm.f.) Nees | Family: Acanthaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SBJ, SS
**Energetic Classification:** Cold and dry (bitter, draining) — a cooling, heat-clearing herb in both Galenic and East Asian schemes
**Training Phase:** Purification

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS):** In the Ayurvedic corpus, kalmegh is *bhunimba* — "neem of the ground" — a supreme bitter (*tikta*) used to clear *pitta*, kindle weak digestive fire, treat fevers and "clean the blood." Classical practice deployed it against jaundice, sluggish liver, worms and skin disease, and it remains the principal ingredient of the widely used compound *Kalmegh navayas loha* and related bitters. Its byname, "king of bitters," records how emphatically the tradition ranked its taste and its heat-clearing power.

**Shennong / Chinese medicine (TCM):** Entering Chinese practice as *chuān xīn lián* ("thread-the-heart lotus"), the herb is classed among the drugs that **clear heat and resolve toxicity** (*qing re jie du*). It is directed at "damp-heat" and "heat-toxin" patterns — sore throat, cough, febrile respiratory illness, dysentery, carbuncles and snakebite. Though it entered the Chinese materia medica later than the Han-era Shennong canon, it was fully absorbed into the heat-clearing tradition the *Bencao* lineage founded and is today one of the most-used cooling antivirals in that framework.

**Avicenna / Unani (CM):** The bitter chiretta-type herbs of the eastern trade reached Unani practice as cold, dry drugs of the bitter tonic class — febrifuge, hepatic, and blood-cleansing. Persian and later Unani physicians used the imported bitters to "cut" hot fevers and to open obstructions of the liver, aligning with the Galenic reading of intense bitterness as cold, drying and deobstruent.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Across all three traditions the plant is read through the door of **bitterness**: extreme bitter taste signals a cold, heat-clearing, liver-directed, "cleansing" drug. This is one of the cases where traditional sensory doctrine tracks real pharmacology closely — the same bitter diterpene lactone that defines the taste, **andrographolide**, is also the molecule carrying the immune-modulating, anti-inflammatory and antiviral activity now studied clinically. The symbolism of "cleansing the blood and clearing heat" maps onto measured effects on inflammatory signalling and on the resolution of respiratory infection.

### Active Components

- **Andrographolide** — the principal *ent*-labdane diterpenoid lactone; the marker compound and main bioactive, with anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory and antiviral activity.
- **Neoandrographolide, deoxyandrographolide, and related diterpenoid lactones** — contributing to the overall bitter/active profile.
- **Flavonoids** (e.g. polymethoxyflavones) and phenolic acids — antioxidant support.
- Standardised extracts are typically expressed as a percentage of andrographolide content.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Kalmegh is the archetypal **Purification-phase** herb of the Codex: an intense bitter used, in short courses, to support the practitioner through the heat-clearing, "reset" work of that phase. Its evidence-backed role is symptomatic support of **uncomplicated upper-respiratory infection** — shortening and softening cough and sore throat — which makes it a practical ally during the seasonal illnesses that interrupt training. Secondarily it carries the traditional liver-directed, digestive-bitter role: a small bitter dose before food to wake sluggish digestion. It is used in defined courses, not as a permanent daily tonic.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, *Andrographis paniculata* has relatively strong clinical evidence for acute respiratory symptom relief — unusually so for a traditional botanical.

- Hu et al. (2017), *PLoS One* — a systematic review and meta-analysis of 33 RCTs (7,175 patients) found *A. paniculata* improved cough (SMD −0.39, 95% CI [−0.67, −0.10]) and sore throat (SMD −1.13, 95% CI [−1.37, −0.89]) versus placebo, and shortened time to symptom resolution, with only minor (mainly gastrointestinal) adverse events; the authors caution that trial quality was generally poor. DOI: [https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0181780](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0181780).
- Poolsup et al. (2004), *Journal of Clinical Pharmacy and Therapeutics* — an earlier systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs concluded *A. paniculata*, alone or combined with *Acanthopanax senticosus*, was more effective than placebo for uncomplicated upper-respiratory infection on symptom-severity scores. DOI: [https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2710.2003.00534.x](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2710.2003.00534.x).
- Okonogi et al. (2023), *Drug Discovery & Therapeutics* — a randomised, double-blind trial (60 adults) found an *A. paniculata* throat spray produced faster symptom resolution than a chamomile positive control in acute viral pharyngitis (response 1.9 ± 0.7 vs 2.5 ± 1.2 days, p = 0.049), with no adverse events. DOI: [https://doi.org/10.5582/ddt.2023.01053](https://doi.org/10.5582/ddt.2023.01053).

**Evidence grade:** Moderate clinical evidence (multiple RCTs and meta-analyses) for symptomatic relief of acute upper-respiratory infection; the effect is consistent though tempered by variable trial quality and product heterogeneity. Immunomodulatory and antiviral mechanisms are supported preclinically.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Bitter, cold; enters Lung, Stomach, Large Intestine and Small Intestine channels; **clears heat, resolves toxicity, dries dampness**.
- **Ayurvedic:** *rasa* — tikta (bitter); *guna* — laghu (light), ruksha (dry); *virya* — shita (cooling); *vipaka* — katu (pungent). Pitta- and kapha-reducing; a *dipana/pachana* (kindling/digestive) and *yakrit-uttejaka* (hepatic-stimulant) bitter.
- **Galenic/Unani:** Cold and dry; bitter febrifuge and deobstruent of the liver.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy** — avoid; traditional caution regarding reproductive/uterine effects and insufficient safety data.
- **Autoimmune disease and immunosuppressive therapy** — theoretical caution due to immunostimulant activity; use only with clinical oversight.
- **Fertility** — some preclinical concern over male reproductive effects at high doses; avoid prolonged high-dose use.
- **Anticoagulant/antiplatelet and antihypertensive drugs** — possible additive effects; monitor.
- **Allergy** — discontinue if rash or hypersensitivity occurs (reported occasionally). Gastrointestinal upset and bitter-taste intolerance are the common minor effects. Use in short courses, not continuously.
