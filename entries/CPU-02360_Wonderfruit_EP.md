### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Ebers Papyrus among the texts on disk, under Bryan's English label.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from Bryan's plant list; this entry replaces it. The first-pass guess was "unresolved (Wonderfruit, a Bryan label)". That stands.

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The English text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 *The Papyrus Ebers*, a translation with summaries and not a transliteration (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). "Wonderfruit" is a head word of Bryan's plant list (Ch. VI, "Plant Remedies", line 1352; row 90 of `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`), with the forms "Wonderfruit" and "Seeds-of-the-Wonderfruit". The extract's README says its identifications are "Bryan's 1930 English renderings only; no Egyptian word and no binomial is given in these lists". Bryan says of the whole corpus that many substances are "completely hidden from us under the guise of meaningless words" (line 1233). Where Bryan coins an English label of this kind (compare "Excrement-of-the-adu-Bird", "What-is-in-the-ut'ait-fruit" in the same text, line 1362), the label records an Egyptian word he could not translate. Whether "Wonderfruit" is such a case or a real translation is not stated in the lines read. [USER: verify against a transliterated edition.]

Where the label occurs in prescriptions:

1. **Dyspepsia.** In the digestive section: "...one could essay a sort of sundae prepared by crushing Wonderfruit in cooked Cow's Milk, and adding Cream to it" (line 1787). Here the plant is crushed into cooked milk, which is the only use that names a preparation of the fruit itself.
2. **A cooling remedy for the anus.** In the chapter on the anus, a remedy headed "Another to cool after the other medicines" lists "Ox-bile", "Boiled Milk", "Honey" and "Wonderfruit", with numerals in a damaged column, and ends "Strain and pour on the anus for a day" (lines 2051 to 2070). It is an external application.
3. **Urinary troubles.** "Urinary troubles in the adult were corrected in three ways: a rectal injection of Olive Oil, Honey, Sweet Beer, Sea-salt, and Seeds-of-the-Wonderfruit was one way" (line 2415).
4. **To regulate menstruation.** "...a douche composed of Fennel, Wonderfruit, Honey and ..." (line 2507, continuing on the next page).

The part used is the fruit in the first use and the seeds in the third. No Egyptian name, no habitat, no description and no property of the plant is printed in the lines read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unresolved identity.**

- **What the text records.** The fruit appears as an ingredient in remedies for dyspepsia, for a cooling application in the anal section, for urinary troubles (the seeds, in a rectal injection) and for the regulation of menstruation (in a douche).
- **What the text does not say.** No identity, no dose, and no property or action ascribed to the plant by itself.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified. Do not substitute any fruit on the strength of the label.
- **Injections and douches.** The papyrus's rectal injection and douche are historical records. Do not use plant material this way; it risks irritation and infection.
