### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry: named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** This row was added on 2026-10-09 by the plant-gap run (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP). The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) on the SUGGESTED line of one chapter. The plant is named by no other text read. The chapter is brief and so is this entry.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`.

*3-129 AMBROSIA* (heading at line 7688; SUGGESTED lines 7689 and 7690: "Ambrosia maritima — Sea Ambrosia, Oak of Cappodocia, Oak of Jerusalem" and, on the next line, "Ambrosia artemisifolia — Common Ragweed"; text at line 7691, in the third book). The whole chapter reads: "Ambrosia is a little shrub three feet in height, full of branches, with small leaves like rue around the emergent stalk. It has small stems full of little seeds like little bunches of grapes, which never flower — smelling pleasant like wine. The root is slender, two feet long. In Cappadocia it is plaited into wreaths for the head. It is able to repress and repel, and is smeared on as an astringent for fluids that have come down. It is also called botrys, or botrys artemisia, the Romans call it caper sylvaticus, or apium rusticum, and the Egyptians, merseo." The chapter gives a description (height, branching, rue-like leaves, grape-bunch-like seed stems that never flower, a long slender root), a use as a wreath in Cappadocia, and a single medicinal use: smeared on as an astringent for "fluids that have come down".

**Identity doubts.** (1) The translators give two binomials for the chapter, *Ambrosia maritima* and *Ambrosia artemisifolia*; the second is the common ragweed, which is a plant of the Americas by general knowledge, and cannot have been the plant Dioscorides described. The row takes the first. (2) The chapter's alternative names include "botrys". The next chapter, **3-130 BOTRUS** (heading at line 7695, text at line 7699), carries a separate translators' suggestion (*Chenopodium botrys*, purple goosefoot, and *Teucrium botrys*) and states: "The Cappadocians call this ambrosia, and it is also called artemisa." That chapter's use is "A decoction (with wine) is used as a drink to ease difficult breathing." The ambrosia of 3-129 and the botrus of 3-130 thus share a name by Dioscorides' own report, and the text does not decide whether they are one plant. The uses of 3-130 are not attributed to this row. ### Active Components

*Ambrosia* species are generally known as aromatic members of the daisy family, and the pollen of common ragweed is a well-known allergen. The composition of the plant Dioscorides described, whose identification is uncertain, was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The chapter records a pleasant-smelling shrub plaited into head-wreaths in Cappadocia and used locally as an astringent. The identification is a translators' suggestion with a doubtful alternative, and the Codex gives no protocol for it.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in line 7691 is external: the plant "is smeared on as an astringent for fluids that have come down". No quantity or frequency is printed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
