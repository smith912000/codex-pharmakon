## Section 1 — Nomenclature

### Primary Scientific Name
*Chrysanthemum morifolium* Ramat. (Asteraceae); syn. *Dendranthema morifolium* (Ramat.) Tzvelev

### Nomenclature Table

| Tradition | Language | Name | Transliteration | Meaning |
|-----------|----------|------|-----------------|---------|
| Chinese (SBJ) | 文言文 | 菊花 | Jú Huā | "Chrysanthemum flower" — one of the Four Noble Plants of Chinese culture (along with plum, orchid, bamboo) |
| Chinese | 文言文 | 甘菊 | Gān Jú | "Sweet chrysanthemum" — the medicinal variety (white flowers) vs wild yellow |
| Arabic (CM) | العربية | أقحوان | Uqḥuwān | Standard Arabic for chrysanthemum; also *gul-e-daudi* in Persian |
| Persian (CM) | فارسی | گل داودی | Gul-e-Daudi | "David's flower" — popular Persian/Urdu name |
| Sanskrit (SS) | संस्कृत | सेवन्ती | Sevantī | Classical Sanskrit designation |
| English | — | Chrysanthemum / Mum | — | From Greek *chrysos* (gold) + *anthemon* (flower) — originally named for yellow wild species |

### Botanical Identity
Perennial herb 60–100cm; long history of cultivation in China (attested from at least 1st millennium BCE); enormous cultivar diversity — medicinal use focuses on white-flowered *Hang ju* (Hangzhou chrysanthemum) and *Chu ju* (Chuzhou chrysanthemum); cultivated primarily in Zhejiang and Anhui provinces. The species is a polyploid hybrid of uncertain wild parentage; the flower heads are the drug material.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ)

Listed in the upper class (superior medicines):
> *"Jú huā: sweet, bitter; slightly cold. Enters Liver and Lung channels. Clears Liver-Heat, brightens the eyes, subdues Liver Yang, dispels Wind-Heat. Treats red, painful, swollen eyes, headache from Liver Yang excess, dizziness, blurred vision. Prolonged use lightens the body, delays aging, extends life."*

This is the definitive Chinese chrysanthemum application — Liver-Heat affecting the eyes and head. The statement "brightens the eyes" for a cooling liver herb is one of the most consistent applications in the TCM pharmacopoeia. The longevity claim reflects upper-class drug classification.

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā:
> *"Uqḥuwān: temperament cold and dry in the second degree. Benefits the eyes, reduces inflammation of the eyes and head, calms febrile heat in the head, relieves headache from heat. The flowers dried and taken internally or applied externally to the eyes reduce redness and heat."*

The Islamic and Chinese traditions converge precisely on ophthalmological use — cooling inflammatory eye conditions. This is the pharmacological core of chrysanthemum: anti-inflammatory compounds acting on vasodilated, inflamed eye tissue.

### 2.3 Sushruta Samhita (SS)

*Sevantī* appears in Ayurvedic compound preparations for:
> *"Cooling Pitta in the head and eyes; brightening vision; treating inflammation of the eyes and head with excess Pitta."*

All three traditions converge on the eye-cooling, Liver/Pitta-Heat clearing indication.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Anti-inflammatory / Ophthalmological

**Luteolin**: Inhibits NF-κB → reduced TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β → anti-inflammatory. COX-2 selective inhibition → reduced prostaglandin E2 → reduces vascular inflammation. This is the pharmacological basis of the consistent cross-traditional eye application — luteolin reduces conjunctival and periorbital inflammatory mediators.

**Apigenin**: Inhibits 5-LOX → reduces leukotriene B4 → additional anti-inflammatory pathway.

### 5.2 Hepatoprotective / Liver-Heat Clearing

Luteolin + chlorogenic acid → hepatoprotective via Nrf2/ARE activation → antioxidant enzyme induction. Reduces hepatic triglycerides and oxidative stress. The TCM concept of "Liver-Heat" maps pharmacologically to hepatic oxidative stress and inflammatory dysregulation — chrysanthemum's luteolin content directly addresses this.

### 5.3 Antihypertensive

Chlorogenic acid + apigenin → ACE inhibition + endothelial NO enhancement → vasodilation → blood pressure reduction. Explains the "subdues Liver Yang" TCM application, where Liver Yang excess = hypertensive headache pattern.

### 5.4 Neuroprotective

Luteolin → inhibits neuroinflammation (microglial activation) → reduces neuroinflammatory cytokines → neuroprotective. Apigenin → GABA-A modulation → anxiolytic.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Eye inflammation**: Traditional use consistent with anti-inflammatory mechanism; limited human RCTs specifically for chrysanthemum ophthalmology.

**Hypertension**: Multiple small studies confirm BP reduction with chrysanthemum tea consumption; mechanistically supported.

**Antioxidant/metabolic**: Human studies confirm significant antioxidant effect; consistent evidence for lipid-lowering (luteolin, chlorogenic acid).

**Dry eye / conjunctivitis**: Clinical observation data from Chinese medicine practice consistent; chrysanthemum eyewash is a common Chinese traditional practice for conjunctivitis.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The cooling eye bath**: Chrysanthemum tea applied as an eyewash — a small cup of warm (not hot) chrysanthemum tea over the open eye — is one of the most widely used first-aid treatments in Chinese households for conjunctivitis and eye fatigue. The experience is immediately soothing: the mild tannins and flavonoids reduce vascular dilation in the conjunctiva within minutes. This is a functional application that has propagated continuously from classical pharmacology into modern Chinese domestic medicine.

**Yellow to white to medicine**: The original wild chrysanthemum is yellow — the genus name (Greek *chrysos* = gold) describes this colour. Chinese pharmacological practice over centuries selected for white-flowered varieties (*Hang ju*, *Chu ju*) for medicinal use, based on empirical observation that these had superior cooling and eye-clearing effects. This selection is pharmacologically founded — white *C. morifolium* varieties generally have higher luteolin content than yellow wild types.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Chrysanthemum tea | 3–5g dried flowers per 250ml, steep 5 min | Primary delivery; eye health, Liver-Heat |
| Eyewash | Strained cooled tea applied to eye | Conjunctivitis, eye fatigue |
| Combined with *gou qi zi* (wolfberry) | Equal parts | Classic Chinese longevity eye formula |
| Capsule extract | 250–500mg standardised extract | Antihypertensive, antioxidant |

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Bitter, sweet; slightly cold. Channels: Liver (肝), Lung (肺). Actions: Clear Wind-Heat, calm Liver Yang, clear Liver-Fire, brighten eyes. Key pair: *ju hua* + *gou qi* (wolfberry) for Liver-Kidney deficiency with eye symptoms.

**Ayurvedic**: Bitter, sweet; cooling. Reduces Pitta ↓↓. *Pittahara, Cakṣuṣya* (beneficial to eyes).

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Cold conditions / Spleen deficiency**: Cooling nature — avoid excessive use in cold-spleen diarrhoea patterns
- **Asteraceae allergy**: Cross-reactivity with ragweed, chamomile, arnica — screen for family allergy
- Generally very safe at tea doses
