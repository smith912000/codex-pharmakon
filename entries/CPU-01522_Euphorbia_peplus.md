# Euphorbia peplus — Peplos (Dioscorides 4-168)

## *Euphorbia peplus [Linnaeus], the third form on the translators' SUGGESTED line ("Peplos, Esula rotunda [Fuchs], Euphorbia peplus [Linnaeus], Tithymalus peplis, Euphorbia peplis — Wild Purslane, Purple Spurge, Petty Spurge")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant under this name. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) printed under the chapter heading, not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED line gives the Greek name and a pre-Linnaean name credited to Fuchs ("Peplos, Esula rotunda"), the Linnaean *Euphorbia peplus*, a second pair ("Tithymalus peplis, Euphorbia peplis") that is spelled differently and has no author in brackets, and three English names ("Wild Purslane, Purple Spurge, Petty Spurge"). The row takes the Linnaean binomial. The same second pair, with the spelling *peplis*, is also printed on the heading of the tithymal group (4-165, line 9519), so the translators use both forms. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-168. PEPLOS* at line 9576, SUGGESTED lines at 9577 and 9578, text at line 9579. The chapter stands in Book Four, "Other Herbs & Roots", after 4-167 *LATHUROS* and before 4-169 *PEPLION*. The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 746.

The whole chapter reads: "Peplos is a little shrub full of white juice, with a little leaf like rue [3-52, 3-53, 4-98], but broader. The whole clump is about twenty centimetres round, spread on the ground; and under the leaves is little seed, round, smaller than that of white poppy. The herb has much use although the single root (from which the whole shrub breaks out) is of no use. It grows in gardens and vineyards. It is gathered at harvest time, dried in the shade, and turned often. The seed is pounded, made clean, and stored. An acetabulum [vinegar cruet] of a decoction (taken as a drink with a half pint of honey water) expels phlegm and bile. Mixed in meats it disturbs the digestion. It is preserved in brine. It is also called syce, or papaver spumeum."

So the chapter has a description (a small spreading clump of about twenty centimetres across, full of white juice, with rue-like but broader leaves and small round seed beneath them), a habitat (gardens and vineyards), a handling procedure (gathered at harvest time, dried in the shade and turned often, the seed pounded, cleaned and stored), one action (the decoction expels phlegm and bile), and one warning (mixed in food it disturbs the digestion). It adds that the plant is preserved in brine, and gives two other names.

*Neighbouring chapters.* The next chapter, *4-169. PEPLION* (line 9580), describes a different, maritime plant (suggested *Peplis portula*, "Common Water Purslane") whose seed is "hot to the taste", and says it is "gathered, stored, and given like peplus preserved in brine, and it has the same properties". The chapter after that, *4-170. CHAMAISUKE* (line 9583), compares itself to peplus and prints the same alternative names *syce* and *papaver spumeum* (see I_1084). In the tithymal chapter (4-165d, line 9543) the leaves of the sun spurge are said to be "like portulaca [4-168]", and the translators' bracket points that name to this chapter; the same pointer is printed at line 7940 (a succulent "similar to portulaca [4-168]") and line 9582. And in *4-54. PARONUCHIA* (heading at line 8477, text at line 8480) the plant is described as "a small shrub similar to peplus". Peplos is thus used by Dioscorides as a standard for comparing other low plants. These are different chapters and the entry does not merge them. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

No analysis of the plant Dioscorides describes was read. It is well established that members of the genus *Euphorbia* have a milky latex that is irritant to skin and eyes, and the chapter itself says the shrub is "full of white juice". Nothing further is stated here about this plant's composition. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records one medical use of the seed or herb, a decoction drunk in honey water to expel phlegm and bile, and a caution about its use in food. The Codex gives no protocol. A purge from a spurge is a risk, spurge latex is an irritant, and the text itself says the herb disturbs the digestion in food.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives these preparations. The herb is "gathered at harvest time, dried in the shade, and turned often". The seed is "pounded, made clean, and stored". The drink: "An acetabulum [vinegar cruet] of a decoction (taken as a drink with a half pint of honey water) expels phlegm and bile." The herb may also be "preserved in brine". The acetabulum and the half pint of honey water are the only quantities printed (the translators gloss the acetabulum as a "vinegar cruet" and do not give a modern equivalent). The chapter does not say how much herb or seed the decoction is made from, nor how often it is taken. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints no hot, cold, moist or dry quality and no taste. It prints the action that the decoction "expels phlegm and bile", and that the herb "mixed in meats ... disturbs the digestion".

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. This chapter gives the most explicit handling instructions of the spurge chapters in this batch, and so fits the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition: where the plant grows (gardens and vineyards), when it is gathered (harvest time), how it is dried (in the shade, turned often), and how the seed is prepared and kept (pounded, cleaned, stored). He also notes that the root, from which the whole shrub breaks out, is useless. That is attention to season, drying and storage, and not a cultivation status. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The transferable lesson is a modest one: Dioscorides tells the reader when and how to gather and dry a plant and which part to ignore, and he says in the same chapter that it harms digestion in food. The text offers nothing more.

### Contraindications

The chapter itself warns that the herb "mixed in meats ... disturbs the digestion". Spurge latex is an irritant of skin, eyes and mucous membranes, and a purge made from spurge can cause vomiting and dehydration, so no part of this plant should be taken or applied. The identification is only the translators' suggestion, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No safety information for pregnancy was read.
