### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The list `_sources/sushruta.csv` has one row (line 126): "Laksha", *Laccifer lacca* Kerr, class animal, source reference "Sushruta Samhita Ch.38", not flagged id-uncertain. The chapter is *Sutrasthana* ch.38, the chapter of the groups (*gana*) of drugs, and the name is read in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`). In the line of ch.38 (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 451-454): "The Laksha'di Group :— The drugs known as the Laksha, Arevata, Kutaja, Ashvamara, Katphalam, Haridra, Daru-Haridra, Ximva [as the file reads, an OCR form], Saptachchhada, Malati, and Trayamana form the Lakshadi Gana." The metrical text that follows (lines 456-462) says: "This consists of astringent, bitter and sweet taste (Rasa) and acts as a good vermifuge and a purifying (aseptic) agent in cases of bad, malignant or indolent ulcers. Diseases due to the deranged Kapham and Pittam prove amenable to its curative properties, which extend to cases of cutaneous affections (Kushtham) as well." That statement is about the group as a whole, and the lines read do not say what in the group is lac. The translator does not gloss Laksha here.

Where Bhishagratna does gloss the name, it is as lac or shellac, and the uses are those of a resin: (1) *Sutrasthana* ch.14, on bleeding after opening a vein, the mouth of the wound "should be lightly touched with the powders of Laksha and Samudra-phena, and its edges should be similarly pressed together" (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch14.txt` lines 377-379). (2) *Sutrasthana* ch.27, on foreign bodies in the pharynx: "In the case of a bit of shellac being accidentally pricked into the pharynx" a metal tube is inserted, a heated rod is reached down to "the obstructing shellac", the melted shellac sticks to the rod and is condensed by cold water poured through the tube (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch27.txt` lines 224-233), and another foreign body may be withdrawn "with the help of a rod, soaked in melted wax or shellac" (lines 241-243). Here shellac is the hazard and the tool, not a remedy. (3) *Chikitsasthana* ch.2, on a fat lump protruding from an abdominal wound: "Instead of this Ghrita, milk prepared medicinally with Yashtimadchi, Ldkshd and Gokshura mixed with (a proper quantity of) sugar and castor oil (as Prakshepa) is equally commendable for the alleviation of the pain and the burning sensation" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch02.txt` lines 238-258); the translator's footnote gives Dallana's alternative readings of how many milk preparations the verse names (lines 281-293). (3b) Also in ch.2, "An oil cooked with" a long list of drugs that includes "Lodhra, Yashti-madhuka, Vidanga ... Ldkshd, and the tender leaves of Kshiri trees" (the OCR of the list is damaged), "or with as many of them as would be available, should be regarded as a good healing remedy in respect of all non-malignant traumatic sores or ulcers" (lines 487-495). (4) *Chikitsasthana* ch.3, on fractures: "A preparation of milk from a cow, delivered for the first time, boiled with the drugs of the Madhurddi group and mixed with powdered shellac and clarified butter (as an afterthrow) should be given (when cold) to a fracture-patient as a beverage every morning" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch03.txt` lines 72-74). (5) *Chikitsasthana* ch.18, on a fat-tumour (Medoja Granthi), the affected part is fomented "with a ladle, pasted with heated shellac (Ldksha)" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt` lines 167-169). (6) *Chikitsasthana* ch.20, on stricture of the urethra, "a tube open at both ends made of iron, wood, or shellac should be lubricated with clarified butter and gently introduced" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch20.txt` lines 280-285). (7) *Chikitsasthana* ch.12, in the directions for the iron-infused fermented preparation named Loha-rishta, the pitcher is "plastered inside with (an adequate quantity of) Pippali-powder and honey, the outer side being plastered with shellac" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch12.txt` lines 249-250). (8) *Chikitsasthana* ch.25, an oil for the ear-lobes is cooked with "Ldkshd-Rasa (decoction or infusion of Lakshi)" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch25.txt` lines 147-153). (9) *Uttaratantra* ch.19, among eye ointments, a pill ointment is "made up of Trikatu, Paldndu (onion), Yashti-madku, Saindhava, Ldkshd (lac) and Gairika (earth) pasted together" (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch19.txt` lines 113-114). (10) *Sharira* ch.2: healthy menstrual blood is "red like the blood of a hare, or the washings of shellac" (`sharira/SS_Sharira_ch02.txt` lines 143-145). (11) *Chikitsasthana* ch.13, on diabetes, the mineral exudate *Shilajatu* is called a "shellac-coloured substance" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch13.txt` line 30): a comparison of colour, not a use of lac.

**Identity and the plant reading.** The spine labels the row an insect product (*Laccifer lacca*). The name Laksha appears in the *Sutrasthana* in plant contexts. In ch.39 it is in the list of errhines ("Shala, Tala, Madhuka (Maula), Laksha and Hingu", `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` line 55), and the text then speaks of "the gummy exudation (Niryasa) of Hingu plants and Laksha trees" (lines 71-73). In ch.38 it heads a group whose other members (Arevata, Kutaja, Haridra and the rest) the translator also leaves unglossed there. The translator therefore calls the Laksha of ch.39 a tree and leaves the ch.38 name unglossed, while elsewhere he writes shellac and lac. Whether Sushruta means one substance throughout, and whether the resin is meant in the gana, cannot be settled from the English alone. ### Active Components

Lac is a resinous secretion that the lac insect (a scale insect) makes on the twigs of host trees. Shellac is lac refined into flakes. It is a natural polyester resin of hydroxy fatty acids, with some wax, and the crude lac carries a red dye (laccaic acid). The sources read give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. In the Sushruta text as translated, lac is a surgical and practical material: a styptic powder, a milk-drink additive for fractures, a heated fomentation, a tube material, a sealant and a colour. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation, and it recommends none of them. Shellac is today a food glaze and varnish.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted above: the powder dusted on a bleeding wound (Sutra ch.14), the powdered shellac mixed into milk for a fracture (Chikitsa ch.3), the heated shellac-pasted ladle (Chikitsa ch.18), the infusion in an oil (Chikitsa ch.25) and the pill ointment (Uttara ch.19). No quantity of lac is given in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Shellac is a resin used as a coating and glaze in food, pills and wood finish. Heated shellac sticks to skin and burns. No medical benefit of swallowing it is established, and it is not a substitute for a clean dressing or setting of a fracture. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The text claims the substance for surgery, fractures, the urinary passage and the eye, not for *rasayana* or *vajikarana*. A search of *Chikitsasthana* ch.26 (the chapter on increasing strength and virile power, *Vajikarana*) and ch.27-30 (the *rasayana* chapters, ch.27 headed "elixirs and rejuvinators of the human organism") found no Laksha, lac or shellac. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is printed for it. The Lakshadi group is described as a purifier of ulcers and as acting on *Kapha* and *Pitta* and on skin disease, which is a therapeutic frame and not a cultivation status. The sattvic and rajasic food lists, which come from later material, are not applied here.

### Contraindications

Heated shellac burns, and molten resin should not be put in the throat, urethra or a wound. Wound bleeding, fractures and urinary stricture need proper medical care; the Sushruta uses are not a guide. Lac and shellac can cause contact allergy, and lac dye and flakes sold as medicines are unstandardised. Lac is an insect product, which matters to people who avoid animal products. The identity of Laksha in the groups of ch.38 and ch.39 is uncertain. [USER: verify]
