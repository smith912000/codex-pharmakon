### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, and only as a member of one group.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is Sutrasthana chapter 38, "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam), in Bhishagratna's translation: `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15401, and `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 60.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS).** The chapter opens by saying that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)". The Viratarva'di group is one of them. Its members are printed as: "Virataru, the two kinds of Sahachara, Darbha, Vrikshadani, Gundra, I\'ala, Kusha, Kasha, Ashmabhedaka, Agnimantha, Morata, Vasuka, Vasira, Bhalluka, Kuruntaka, Indivara, Kapotavanka, and Shvadanstra enter into the composition of the group known as the Viratarvadi" (lines 15399-15405). The Metrical Text that follows reads: "The group subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles" (ch.38 file lines 66-70). That statement is made of the group as a whole, and the text does not say which member does what.

The chapter closes with a rule of use (ch.38 file line 558): the physician should prepare plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, ghritas or potions "according to the exigencies of each individual case", and "only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group ... or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety" are to be used as the physician decides. That is the only guidance on how a member of a group is taken.

The reading "I\'ala" is the OCR of the e-text, where the backslash and apostrophe are print or scan artefacts and the true spelling is not shown.
