### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` (Sutra, Chikitsa, Uttara, Kalpa folders) and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The chapters name the drug only by Sanskrit name. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The name in the chapters.* "Bakula" occurs once in the chapters and "Vakula" in eight places; each is read below from the chapter file.

*Astringent group (Sutra 42, lines 349 to 361).* In the chapter on the specific properties of flavours, the astringent group is made up of "the groups known as the Nyagrodhade-Gana, the Amvashtadi-Gana, and the Priyangvadi and the Rodhradi Ganas, Triphala, Shallaki, Jambu, Amra, Vakula, Timduka fruits, Katakha fruits, Shaka fruits ..." and others.

*The fruit group (Sutra 46, lines 1120 to 1130 and 1152 to 1155).* Among fruits "which are known as the Jamva, Rajadana, Todana, Tinduka, Vakula, Phanvana, Ashmantaka ..." the text says they "are cooling, and astringent in their effect. They subdue the Pittam and Kapham, tend to produce a condition of parchedness in the system, and have a sweet and astringent taste." Of Vakula in particular: "The Vakula fruit has a sweet and astringent taste, is demulcent and astringent in its properties, imparts a greater firmness to the teeth, and removes the viscid condition of the membranes."

*The flowers (Sutra 46, lines 1806 to 1811).* "The Vakulas, like the Patala flowers, are sweet smelling and pleasant, their pleasing and odoriferous property instantaneously permeates the whole system." In Sutra 6 (lines 318 to 324), the description of spring, the woods "are decked with the full-blown flowers of the Kinshuka, lotus, Vakula, mango and Ashoka trees."

*A use in the emetic chapter (Sutra 43, lines 10 to 19).* After saying that Madana is the best of the emetic fruits, the text gives "a potion consisting of the powders of raw Madana fruits, stirred in a decoction of Vakula and Ramyaka, and heated in fire," given "with the addition of honey and rock-salt."

*Recipes in the Chikitsa.* In Chikitsa 11 (the Prameha chapter, lines 185 to 193), Arishtas, Asavas and Yavagus "should be prepared ... with Priyangu, Anantā, Yuthikā, Padmā (Bhargi), ... Padma (lotus), Tunga, Kes'ura, Dhātaki, Vakula, S'almali, S'rivestaka and Mocharasa". In Chikitsa 9 (the Kushtha chapter), a plaster of "Saindhava, Chakra-marda seeds, treacle, Kes'ara (Vakula), and Tārkshya-s'aila (Rasanjana) pasted together with expressed Kapittha juice" is given for ringworm (Dadru; lines 238 to 242), and a medicated ghee in which the Kalka includes "Kes'atdhva (Vakula)" is given where the disease has attacked the Dhatus (lines 372 to 380; the "Nila-Ghrita"). The names "Kes'ara", "Kes'ura" and "Kes'atdhva" are the translator's equivalents inside parentheses, not the chapters' own identification.

*In the alcoholism chapter (Uttara 47, lines 395 to 405).* In the cooling regimen laid down for the patient, the chamber "should be scented and decorated with Mānsi, Tamāla, Musta, Kunkuma, Padma-leaves, Jāti flower, Utpala (blue-lotus), Priyangu, Kes'ara (Bakula), and Pundarika (red-lotus), Punnāga, Nāga-Kes'ara, and Karavira."

*Not in the group lists.* A search of the Sutra 38 group lists (extracted in `names.csv` for 31 of the 37 groups) found no Vakula or Bakula. The row's only entries in `names.csv` are the appendix rows above.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Mimusops*, *Bakula* and *Vakula* found I_1136 (*Mimusops schimperi*, Dioscorides 1-187; a different species and tradition) and three Sushruta fruit entries that quote the same Vakula line in a list: I_912 (Tinduki, Sutra 42 and 46), I_909 (Jambu, Sutra 42) and I_908 (Plaksha, Sutra 46). None is an entry on this plant, and nothing is merged.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Vakula as an astringent fruit, as a sweet-smelling flower, as a decoction used with Madana for emesis, and as an ingredient of fermented drinks, a plaster for ringworm and a medicated ghee. These are the text's statements about a Sanskrit name, and the Latin name that the appendix attaches to it is the translator's. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives these preparations, each with its locus. Sutra 43, lines 17 to 19: a decoction of Vakula and Ramyaka in which the powder of raw Madana fruits is stirred and heated, given with honey and rock-salt. Chikitsa 11, lines 185 to 193: Arishtas, Asavas and Yavagus made with it among many drugs. Chikitsa 9, lines 238 to 242: a plaster with Saindhava, Chakra-marda seeds and treacle, pasted with Kapittha juice. Chikitsa 9, lines 372 to 380: a medicated ghee from a decoction of Triphala and Asana with a Kalka that includes it. The measures printed in that recipe are for the Triphala and the Asana, not for this drug. No dose, part or quantity is given for Vakula itself. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** the chapters read print for the fruit: a sweet and astringent taste, cooling potency and subduing of Pittam and Kapham as a member of a fruit group (Sutra 46, lines 1120 to 1130); "demulcent and astringent" with a firming action on the teeth for the Vakula fruit itself (line 1152 to 1155); membership of the astringent group (Sutra 42, line 353). For the flower, the text prints only "sweet smelling and pleasant" (Sutra 46, line 1808). No *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Vakula alone in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text names Vakula as an astringent fruit, as a scented flower and as an ingredient of the recipes above. A search of the chapter files for "Bakula", "Vakula", "Bakul" and "Vakul" found none in the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) or in the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30), so the text assigns it no rejuvenative or generative status in the lines retrieved, and the Codex does not give it one. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for this drug. The Latin identification is the appendix's. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. In the Sushruta scheme the name is a fruit of the astringent group, a scented flower in a cooling regimen (Uttara 47), and an ingredient of recipes prescribed by a physician. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of a Latin name that the text does not itself give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read print no warning for the drug itself. The text uses it in a potion with Madana, which is an emetic, so that passage is a physician's procedure, not a household one.
