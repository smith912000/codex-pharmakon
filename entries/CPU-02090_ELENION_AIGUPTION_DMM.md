# ELENION AIGUPTION — "Helenium of Egypt" (Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 1-28)

## *A low spreading herb that Dioscorides, citing Crateuas, calls "another helenium that grows in Egypt"; the translator's heading gives "UNKNOWN" and no suggested species. The Codex does not identify it. It is not shown to be elecampane (Inula helenium), which is the separate chapter 1-27 immediately before it. Identification: unresolved. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Dioscorides (DMM).** The entry is *De Materia Medica* Book 1, chapter 28, "ELENION AIGUPTION", in the 2000 English edition on disk (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 3792; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 29, which reads "UNKNOWN"). The whole chapter reads, under the heading "UNKNOWN" (line 3794):

"Crateuas mentions another helenium that grows in Egypt. It is a herb with branches a foot long spreading on the ground like *serpyllum* [3-46], its many leaves around the branches like those of lenticule [?lentil] but longer; the roots a pale colour, the thickness of the little finger, thin below but thicker above, with a black rind. It grows in places bordering on the sea and on hillocks and rocks. One root of it (taken in a drink with wine) is able to help those bitten by snakes."

Four points follow from the text. (1) The account is second-hand: Dioscorides attributes it to Crateuas and does not say he saw the plant. (2) It is called "another helenium", set against the helenium of the previous chapter (1-27), which has narrow leaves like verbascum, a fragrant sharp root and a habitat of hilly, shady, moist places (line 3779); the two descriptions differ in leaf, root and habitat. (3) The one use is for those bitten by snakes, with one root taken in wine. (4) Nothing is said of taste, season of gathering, preparation or amount. The Latin name *Inula helenium* printed in the translator's picture caption at line 3795 belongs to the chapter before it and is not offered as an identification of this plant. The index of Greek names lists the entry at page 33 (line 12125).

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified; do not substitute elecampane or any other herb for it.
- **Snake-bite.** This is a medical emergency. Seek urgent medical care; do not rely on any plant.
