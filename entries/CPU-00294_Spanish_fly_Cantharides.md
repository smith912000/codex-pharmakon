### Active Components

The blister beetles contain cantharidin, a terpenoid compound (a cyclic anhydride) in the body fluid and especially in the male's reproductive glands, which the beetle uses as a defence. It blisters the skin on contact and is highly toxic when swallowed, damaging the lining of the gut and urinary tract and the kidneys. The sources read give no composition.

### Scientific Correlation

Cantharidin is a vesicant. In modern medicine a purified topical preparation, applied by a clinician, is used to remove warts and molluscum on the skin; taken by mouth it is a poison. It is not an aphrodisiac: the genital irritation it causes is an inflammation and not arousal. No clinical studies were retrieved for the traditional uses. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Cantharides and cantharidin are dangerous poisons. Contact blisters skin and mucous membranes; swallowing causes burning of the mouth and gut, vomiting, blood in the urine, kidney failure and death, and the poisoning is a medical emergency. Products sold as Spanish fly are unregulated and often adulterated. It must not be given to others without their knowledge, which is a crime. Possession and sale are restricted in many places; the legal status has not been checked here. [USER: verify]
