# Pyrrosia fern — Shi Wei 石苇 (Shennong bencao jing, as Sun Xingyan prints the name; usually written 石韦); alias Shi Pi 石皮, "stone skin"

## *Pyrrosia lingua (Thunb.) Farw., the tongue fern, a small rock-clinging fern whose leaf is the Chinese drug shi wei; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence at species level, since the classic describes no plant and more than one Pyrrosia species is used under the name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** The principal text read is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Its other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), also has the drug, as "Shi Wei (Folium Polypodii Linguae)" (line 3191), and is read here as a second witness, attributed to Yang. The row began as an unverified placeholder under that pharmaceutical-Latin name; *Polypodium lingua* is an old name of the same fern now placed in *Pyrrosia*. A different fern, the male fern, appears in I_982 (*Polypodium filix-mas*, Dioscorides 4-186); it is not this plant and that entry does not cite the Shennong text.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 石苇 in the middle classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 石苇 *shi wei*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2157). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，平 ("bitter, balanced"); 主劳热邪气 ("governs taxation heat and evil qi"), 五癃闭不通 ("the five dribbling blocks, closed and not passing"), 利小便水道 ("frees urination and the water passages"); alias 一名石 followed by a character lost in the digitised text; habitat 生山谷石上 ("grows on rocks in mountain valleys"). There is no 久服 clause.

There is no Wu Pu layer for this drug. The Mingyi bielu layer (《名医》曰, line 2159) adds the alias 石皮 ("stone skin"), the habitat 生华阴山谷 ("grows in the mountain valleys of Huayin"), a rule of quality, 不闻水及人声者，良 ("those that do not hear water or human voices are good"), and the harvest: 二月采叶，阴干 ("the leaf is gathered in the second month and dried in the shade"). The text does not explain the rule of quality. There is no 案 note by Sun for this entry.

Yang 1998 (line 3191) renders the paragraph as "Shi Wei (Folium Polypodii Linguae) is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats taxation heat evil qi and the five dribbling blocks. It disinhibits urination by [dredging] the water passageways. Its other name is Shi Zhe (Stone Skin). It grows over rocks in the mountains and valleys." The English alias "Stone Skin" agrees with the Mingyi alias 石皮. Yang's footnote 194 explains the dribbling blocks as retention of fluids; that is his commentary and not the classic's. The nearest class heading above Yang's paragraph reads "Herbs: Inferior Class" (line 3157), whereas Sun places the drug in the middle classic (entry under 卷二 中经; contents, line 1677). The Codex follows Sun and records the difference without resolving it. [USER: verify]

**Appendix.** An appendix of drug relations, later than the main text, says of it: 石苇 滑石、杏仁为使，得菖蒲，良 ("shi wei: hua shi and xing ren are its envoys; with chang pu it is good", line 4385). It also names shi wei as the envoy of mang xiao (line 4217) and of hua shi (line 4221), and says that ma huang "dislikes shi wei" (line 4367). The preface (line 4117) says that drugs in the "mutually needing" and "envoy" relations are good to combine and those in the "dislike" and "opposed" relations are not; the Codex draws no further conclusion.

### Active Components

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, balanced leaf for taxation heat and evil qi, for the five dribbling blocks and for freeing urination. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose. Retention of urine and burning or bloody urine need medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the sources read is the harvest: the leaf is gathered in the second month and dried in the shade (Mingyi layer, line 2159). The preface (line 4121) says that drugs differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Pyrrosia lingua* is an epiphytic and rock-growing fern of East Asia, and leaves of several *Pyrrosia* species are used in modern Chinese pharmacy. The classic's claims about urination are not translated into a modern pharmacological claim. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味苦，平, "bitter, balanced" (line 2157). There is no Wu Pu layer, so no disagreement among authorities is recorded.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The paragraph for 石苇 stands in the middle classic, whose preface says the middle medicines "nourish the nature, correspond to man; non-toxic or toxic, weigh what is fitting; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the lean" (主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经; line 1677). The paragraph has no 久服 clause, so no promise of lightening the body or extending the years attaches to it; it is a flavour, nature and indication list. The register is (b). (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 石苇 *shi wei*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2157)

**Practitioner's note.** The classic gives this fern leaf a urinary indication and no long-use promise. A cultivator has nothing to do with it beyond the record. The entry is no licence for any use.

### Contraindications

- **Urinary symptoms:** retention, pain or blood in the urine need medical assessment; do not treat them with this entry.
- **Identity:** other ferns and other *Pyrrosia* species are not interchangeable with the drug; the text describes no plant.
- **Appendix cautions:** the appendix says ma huang "dislikes" shi wei (line 4367); this is a historical pairing and not a modern interaction list.
- **Pregnancy and nursing:** no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given.
