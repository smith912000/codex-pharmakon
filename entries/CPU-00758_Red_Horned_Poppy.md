# Red Horned Poppy — glaukion (Dioscorides 3-100), horned poppy of Hierapolis

## *Glaucium corniculatum* (spine botanical; the translator's gloss for Dioscorides 3-100 reads "Chelidonium corniculatum, Glaucium corniculatum, Glaucium leiocarpum, Glaucium phoeniceum — Red Horned Poppy") | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP, AT, DMM, CM

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity warning.** The Dioscorides chapter is the only locus that names this plant, and even there it describes a *juice* made from a herb, not the herb at length. The translator offers four Latin names. The Assyrian tag has no readable locus.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 3-100, "GLAUKION" (text line 7446), is in full: "Glaucium is the juice of a herb that grows at Hierapolis in Syria. The leaves are similar to the horned poppy but fatter, scattered on the ground, with a strong scent, and more bitter to the taste. It has considerable quantities of saffron-coloured juice. The inhabitants throw the leaves into a pot, warm it in half-cold ovens until withered, and afterwards beat them to press out the juice. It is used for new eye sores because it is cooling." A cross-reference "see 4-64" (the wild poppy, "MEKON ROIAS", line 8559) follows the translator's gloss. The "horned poppy" the leaves are compared to is chapter 4-66 MEKON KERATITES (line 8602), glossed *Glaucium flavum*, the yellow-flowered sea poppy. That chapter adds: "Some have been deceived, thinking that glaucium [3-100] was made of this, because of the resemblance of the leaves", and warns that eating or drinking that horned poppy produces "the same symptoms as in the taking of opium". That chapter belongs to the separate spine row CPU-00759 (Yellow Horned Poppy), and is not attributed here.

**Egyptian — Ebers (EP).** Bryan's chapter VI list prints "Poppy-plant" with the forms Poppy-berries, Poppy-seeds, Poppy-grain and Stalk-of-the-Poppy (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1323). `_sources/ebers.csv` line 22 reads it as *Papaver somniferum* with `id_uncertain = yes`. No horned poppy is named, and nothing read ties the Egyptian poppy to *Glaucium*.

**Assyrian (AT).** No locus was found. The text on disk is cuneiform plates only (`Sources/AT_Assyrian/extract/README.md`), and `_sources/assyrian.csv` has no poppy row.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The attestation rests on a list row only: `_sources/avicenna.csv` line 26, "Opium / Poppy", *Papaver somniferum*, "Canon II". It names no horned poppy, and no Canon chapter text is on disk.

### Active Components

Poppy-family plants characteristically carry alkaloids, and the horned poppies are known for isoquinoline alkaloids; the alkaloid profile of this species was not read. Dioscorides' own warning, in the neighbouring chapter 4-66, is that the other horned poppy acts like opium. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The only sourced use is as an eye remedy ("new eye sores", "because it is cooling"), made from a hot-pressed juice. Plants of this group are alkaloid-bearing, and the Codex gives no protocol for any of them.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian — Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* The poppy lines concern the opium poppy, with an uncertain identification, and not this plant. The Ebers material is organised by complaint, and no *heka* recitation or placing against the person's *ka*, *ib* or *akh* attaches to it.

**Assyrian (AT).** *(c) Absent.* No attestation was found, and none is asserted. The blocking warning on Assyrian identifications applies; no tablet number or deity is given.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent* as to cultivation status. Dioscorides has no subtle-body system. What the chapter models for a cultivator is provenance and assay: he names the place (Hierapolis in Syria), the leaf, the smell and the taste, and the method of pressing. His neighbouring warning that glaucium is wrongly said to be made from the other horned poppy is an adulteration note, a discipline of attention.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The tag rests on a list row for the opium poppy. No cultivation framing was found for this species.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here supports cultivating this plant for any purpose of the Codex. It is a historical eye-juice from a Syrian plant, and the sibling horned poppy is recorded by Dioscorides as opium-like. The Egyptian, Assyrian and Unani tags should be treated as unverified inheritances from the word "poppy".

### Contraindications

Poppy-family alkaloid plants can be dangerous, and Dioscorides' own warning for the related horned poppy is that its effects resemble opium (4-66). Do not ingest. Do not put plant juices in the eye. Seek ordinary medical care for eye injuries. Wild collecting may be restricted locally, and no conservation data were read.
