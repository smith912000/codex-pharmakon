# Kshira-Kakoli — क्षीरकाकोली (Kṣīra-Kākolī); White Himalayan Lily; Kshirakakoli

## *Lilium polyphyllum* D.Don ex Royle | Family: Liliaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā) — Aṣṭavarga member; Jīvanīya (life-promoting) gaṇa; *endangered / routinely substituted — see Contraindications and Scientific Correlation*

**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Madhura (sweet) rasa · Guru–snigdha guṇa · Śīta vīrya · Madhura vipāka → Vāta–Pitta śāmaka (pacifying), Kapha-vardhaka (aggravating in excess)

**Training Phase:** Restoration · Deep Nourishment · Convalescence (post-depletion rebuilding)

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (Ayurveda) — the canonical source.** Kshira-Kakoli is one of the eight herbs of the **Aṣṭavarga** ("group of eight"), the elite rasāyana cluster that forms the potentiating heart of *Cyavanaprāśa* and the classical *Aṣṭavarga* ghṛtas and tailas. Within the pairing logic of the Aṣṭavarga, the herbs run in four couplets — Jīvaka/Ṛṣabhaka, Meda/Mahāmeda, Kākolī/Kṣīra-Kākolī, and Ṛddhi/Vṛddhi. Kshira-Kakoli ("milk-Kākolī") is the *snigdha*, more markedly lactogenic and demulcent sister of Kākolī proper; the name reads literally as the Kākolī "of milk," pointing both to its milky, mucilaginous juice and to its use as a *stanya-janana* (galactagogue) and *śukra*-building tonic. Suśruta places it in the **Jīvanīya gaṇa** (the life-promoting group headed by Kākolī and Kṣīra-Kākolī) and the **Kākolyādi gaṇa** — cooling, sweet, unctuous herbs deployed against Pitta and Vāta disorders, emaciation, and the burning, wasting states the classical texts call *dāha* and *kṣaya*.

Its therapeutic identity in the saṃhitās is that of a *brṃhaṇa* (bulk-and-tissue-building) and *rasāyana* (rejuvenating) agent: it is said to nourish all seven *dhātus*, and specifically to enrich *rasa*, *rakta*, *māṃsa*, and *śukra*. It is indicated in *rājayakṣmā* (consumptive wasting), *raktapitta* (bleeding disorders), general debility, and in the postpartum and convalescent states where the body must be rebuilt after loss.

**Later Nighaṇṭu and Unani reception.** The medieval materia medica (Bhāvaprakāśa, Rāja Nighaṇṭu, Dhanvantari Nighaṇṭu) preserve Kshira-Kakoli within the Aṣṭavarga and repeat its cooling, tonic, aphrodisiac reading. Because the true Himalayan source became scarce very early, the nighaṇṭus already discuss *pratinidhi dravya* (accepted substitutes) — a candour worth preserving in any honest modern account.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Milk of the High Meadows:** In the symbolic grammar of Ayurveda, *kṣīra* (milk) is the archetype of *ojas* — the refined essence that is the material substrate of vitality, immunity, and steadiness of mind. A herb named for milk, drawn from the cold alpine meadows where the air itself is thin and rarefied, is read as a condenser of that essence: it takes the subtle, cooling, sattvic quality of high places and offers it back to a depleted body as building substance. The Aṣṭavarga as a whole carries a lunar, *soma*-adjacent symbolism — nourishing, cooling, moistening, restorative — against the solar, drying, depleting logic of illness and overexertion. Kshira-Kakoli sits at the lunar pole of that scheme. In the Kenetica frame it is not a stimulant but a *reservoir*: the substance you draw on when the well has run dry, not when you want to run faster.

### Active Components

Rigorous, compound-level phytochemistry specific to authenticated *Lilium polyphyllum* is thin — a direct consequence of its rarity (see Scientific Correlation). Reported constituents, largely by analogy with the genus *Lilium* and from limited regional analyses, include steroidal saponins and glycosides, mucilage and polysaccharides (the basis of its demulcent *snigdha* action), phenolics and flavonoids, and trace alkaloids. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Kshira-Kakoli is a **restoration-phase** dravya — deployed when a practitioner has been emptied rather than merely tired. Its Kenetica role:

- **Post-depletion rebuilding:** convalescence after illness, hard training blocks, or systemic *kṣaya* (wasting) states where dry, hot, catabolic Vāta–Pitta dominates.
- **Cooling the burn:** for the *dāha* pattern — the feeling of being "burnt out," inflamed, sleepless, and dry. Its śīta vīrya and madhura vipāka pull the system back toward moist, anabolic ground.
- **Ojas-building:** as a lunar, *brṃhaṇa* tonic taken in the low, quiet phase of a cycle — not to push performance but to refill the reservoir that performance draws down.

It is almost never used alone; its classical home is the Aṣṭavarga compound, buffered and directed by the other seven.

### Scientific Correlation

**Evidence grade: traditional / preclinical — species-specific pharmacology is essentially absent; the retrievable literature is conservation-, cytogenetic-, and germination-focused, not clinical.** This must be stated plainly: there are, at the time of writing, no retrieved controlled human trials of *Lilium polyphyllum* for any classical indication, and compound-level pharmacology is scant. Its inclusion in the Codex rests on the classical record and genus analogy, not on modern clinical evidence.

According to PubMed, the substantive published work concerns the plant's **critically endangered status, karyology, and seed biology** rather than its pharmacology:

- Kumar, Singh & Kumar Singh (2021), *Microscopy Research and Technique* — karyomorphological and SEM study of newly discovered north-western Himalayan populations, confirming *L. polyphyllum* as **critically endangered** (2n = 24). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jemt.23784)
- Hussain Mir, Tyub & Kamili (2020), *Saudi Journal of Biological Sciences* — distribution mapping and conservation of critically endangered endemics of the Kashmir Himalaya, documenting shrinking *L. polyphyllum* populations under over-exploitation "for medicinal purposes." [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sjbs.2020.05.006)
- Dhyani, Phartyal, Nautiyal & Nautiyal (2013), *Journal of Biosciences* — epicotyl morphophysiological dormancy in *L. polyphyllum* seeds, relevant to why the plant is so slow to propagate and thus so easily depleted in the wild. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12038-012-9284-5)

The clear signal from the retrieved literature is a **conservation crisis**, not a pharmacological dossier. Any vitality or rasāyana claim here is attributed to the Ayurvedic tradition, not to modern trial evidence.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Madhura (sweet)
- **Guṇa (quality):** Guru (heavy), Snigdha (unctuous)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Madhura (sweet)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** Stanya-janana (galactagogue) and śukra-brṃhaṇa beyond what rasa/guṇa alone predict — the "milk" signature
- **Doṣa action:** Vāta–Pitta śāmaka; Kapha-vardhaka in excess
- **Karma:** Jīvanīya, Brṃhaṇa, Rasāyana, Vṛṣya (aphrodisiac/reproductive tonic), Balya (strength-giving), Dāhapraśamana (heat-relieving), Stanya
- **Gaṇa / group:** Aṣṭavarga; Jīvanīya gaṇa; Kākolyādi gaṇa (Suśruta)
- **Dhātu:** nourishes all seven, with emphasis on rasa, rakta, māṃsa, śukra
- **Srotas:** rasavaha, śukravaha, stanyavaha

### Contraindications

- **Āma and low Agni:** its heavy, unctuous, cooling nature can deepen *āma* (metabolic congestion), aggravate Kapha, and burden weak digestion. Avoid in high-Kapha, high-āma, or sluggish-digestion states.
- **Conservation ethics:** *L. polyphyllum* is critically endangered (see Scientific Correlation). Wild-harvested material should be avoided; use validated substitutes or cultivated/verified sources.
- **Substitution reality:** commercial "Kshira-Kakoli" is very frequently an accepted *pratinidhi* (e.g. tuberous substitutes) rather than authentic *L. polyphyllum*. Identity should never be assumed.
