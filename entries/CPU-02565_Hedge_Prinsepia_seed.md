# Hedge Prinsepia seed — 蕤核 *ruihe*, "Rui He"

## *The stone (seed) of a small, clustered, thorny shrub of the rose family, Prinsepia uniflora Batal.; the identification is ours, of high confidence, made from Guo Pu's gloss on the Erya's 白桵 ("a small tree, growing in clusters, its fruit purple-red and edible") and the modern standard reading* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Rui he* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The spine had no row for it before this one (CPU-02565); it was found in the monograph run of Sun Xingyan's text. Yang's English translation was not found to carry the entry on the readable pages (a search of the readable pages of `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` for the drug's name and Latin gloss found nothing), so Sun's text is the only witness read.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 蕤核 in the upper classic (卷一 上经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1315, 味 paragraph; name line 1313). The paragraph reads: 味甘，温，主心腹邪气，明目，目赤痛伤泪出。久服，轻身、益气、不饥。生川谷。In English: "Flavour sweet, warm. Governs evil qi of the heart and abdomen; brightens the eyes; [and] red, painful eyes with injury and tearing. Taken long (久服), it lightens the body (轻身), boosts the qi (益气) and [one is] not hungry (不饥). It grows in rivers and valleys." The part used is not named; "核" in the name means stone or kernel, and the Codex reads the entry as the seed.

**Wu Pu layer** (《吴普》, third century; line 1317). One name is given, lost to a missing glyph in the file. The authorities "Shennong and Lei Gong" are quoted as "sweet, balanced, non-toxic", the habitat as "pools and marshes" (生池泽), and the harvest as the eighth month. The functions are given as "supplements the centre, strengthens the will, brightens the eyes; taken long, one is not hungry" (补中，强志，明目，久服不饥); the citation to the *Yulan* is Sun's. Note that Wu Pu's "Shennong" gives "balanced" where the classic as reconstructed has "warm".

**Mingyi bielu layer** (《名医》, later additions; line 1319). "Grows at Hangu and Baxi" (生函谷及巴西).

**Sun Xingyan's note** (案, line 1321). Several of the characters in this note are lost in the file, and the passage can only be read in part. The *Shuowen* is quoted for a pair of related plant names; the *Erya* says "[lost glyph], 白桵 (*bairui*)"; and Guo Pu glosses: "a small tree, growing in clusters, with a fruit like an [lost glyph] of the ear, purple-red, and edible" (小木，丛生有实如耳[ ]，紫赤可啖). The *Yiqie jing yinyi* is quoted: "The *Bencao* has 蕤, which is today's [lost] 核" (本草作蕤，今[ ]核是也). This is the only description of the plant in the sources read, and it fits a small shrub with a red-purple edible fruit.

**Identity.** The Codex's name, *Prinsepia uniflora*, rests on Guo Pu's gloss and the modern standard reading. The row was first matched by an automatic pass to a *Prunus* species and to *Polygonatum*; the job notes for this entry reject both. The name 蕤 resembles 萎蕤 *weirui*, another drug, which Yang prints as "Wei Rui (Rhizoma Polygonati Odorati)" (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 1500). That is a different drug, and this entry does not describe it.

### Active Components

No analytical source was retrieved for this plant, and no constituent is asserted. As a general fact, the seeds of rosaceous shrubs are often oil-rich. That is a statement about the family and not a finding about this drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic records a sweet, warm seed for evil qi of the heart and abdomen and for red, painful, tearing eyes, and, if taken long, for lightening the body, boosting the qi and not being hungry. The Wu Pu layer adds supplementing the centre and strengthening the will. The Codex gives no protocol. Eye pain, redness or injury needs medical assessment; do not treat the eyes with any preparation drawn from this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation-related statement is the Wu Pu layer's harvest, in the eighth month (line 1317). The classic names no preparation. No quantity, frequency, duration or form is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Divine Farmer's Classic).** 味甘，温, "sweet, warm" (line 1315).
- **Wu Pu layer.** Shennong and Lei Gong: sweet, balanced, non-toxic (line 1317). The temperature differs from the classic's "warm", and Wu Pu records it without comment.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the upper classic (上经), which the preface describes as "to nourish life, in correspondence with heaven; non-toxic; taken in quantity or taken long, it does not harm people" (line 43), and its 味 paragraph carries its own 久服 clause: "Taken long, it lightens the body, boosts the qi, and [one is] not hungry" (久服，轻身、益气、不饥; *Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 蕤核 *ruihe*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1315). 轻身, "lighten the body", is the classic's term and is not translated into a pharmacological effect. The Wu Pu layer repeats "taken long, one is not hungry" and adds "strengthens the will" (强志; line 1317), which is a Wu Pu addition and not the classic's.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade entry with a plain life-nourishing clause, a secure plant and no dose. A cultivator can read it as a record of the classic's promise for long taking, and should not take it as a recipe: nothing in the sources says how much, in what form, or for how long.

### Contraindications

- The classic does not warn, and the preface calls the upper class non-toxic (line 43). The Codex does not infer safety for any preparation from this.
- **Eyes.** The eye uses are historical record only. Red, painful or injured eyes need medical assessment.
- **Identity.** Do not confuse this drug with 萎蕤 or with other rosaceous seeds. The Codex gives no identification guidance for gathering.
