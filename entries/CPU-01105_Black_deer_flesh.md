# Black deer (ena) flesh — Ena (Bhishagratna: "black deer"), venison of the Janghala group
## *The meat of the deer Bhishagratna renders "Ena (black deer)"; the text itself says "Deer which are black are called Ena"; no binomial is printed* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The English is Bhishagratna's translation of the *Sushruta Samhita*. The animal is named in the meat group of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 4-6; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 143, "Ena (black deer)"). The chapter opens the group by saying that the flesh of aquatic, marsh, village, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoof and high-ground animals "is generally used as food. Of these each succeeding kind is superior to the one immediately preceding it" (lines 385-394), so the high-ground (*Jangala*) kind, named last, ranks first. It divides that kind into eight, of which the *Janghala* ("large-kneed") is one of the two most important, and lists the *Ena* first among the *Janghala* (lines 403-415).

The medicinal statement that admitted the entry (`_nonplant/meats.csv`, row "black deer (ena) flesh", locus line 433) is the verse on this animal (lines 431-436): "The venison of the Ena species is sweet and astringent in taste, and palatable, and proves curative in diseases due to the deranged condition of the Pittam, blood and Kapham. It is astringent in its effect, imparts strength to the system, improves a relish for food and is a febrifuge." The text also defines the name (lines 440-443): "Deer which are black are called Ena, while those which are red are called Harina. Those, that are neither red nor black, are designated as Kuranga." The group statement before it (lines 426-429) says that "these species of venison have a sweet and astringent taste, are light, keen, pleasant (palatable), laxative, and diuretic in their effect. They subdue the Vayu and the Pittam." The Ena is named again in the same chapter and in several others:

- *Ranked first.* "The flesh of the Lava, Tittiri, Saranga, Kuranga, Ena, Kapinjala, Mayura (peacock), Varmi and Kurma (tortoise) should be regarded as the best of those of all other animals respectively belonging to their own species" (ch.46 line 2162).
- *Safe for all.* Among the foods "which may be safely included within the food stuffs of all human beings" are grains and "the flesh of the Ena, the Harina (copper coloured deer), the Kuranga ..." (Sutrasthana ch.20, "the salutary and non-salutary effects of regimen", line 51).
- *Fever diet.* "The meat of Lava, Kapinjala, Ena, Prishata, S'arabha, Kala-puchchha, Kuranga, Mriga-matrika ... or S'asa (hare) may be prescribed as diet for a fever-patient accustomed to the use of animal food" (Uttarasthana ch.39, line 905).
- *After emetics and purgatives.* Cooked Shashti grain or S'ali rice, Mudga pulse "as well as (the soup of the flesh of) an Ena, Lava, hare, peacock, Tittiri, or deer, and such other light food should be given" (Chikitsasthana ch.39, line 240).
- *Alcoholism.* In the Pittaja type, "Meat-juice (Rasa) of Lava, Ena and Tittira unmixed with any acid, or Mudga soup should be taken with sugar and clarified butter" (Uttarasthana ch.47, line 184).
- *Cough and asthma.* A soup of Kulattha "properly cooked with the heads of Ena deer, etc." destroys cough and asthma (Uttarasthana ch.51, line 156); ghee cooked with a decoction and the essence of Kulira (crab), Sukti, Chataka, "Ena-deer" and Lava is curative in cough from an ulcer or wasting (ch.52, line 352).
- *The eye.* "The flesh of a vulture and of a deer of the Ena species, Saindhava salt, honey and clarified butter should be prepared and applied (to the affected eye) in the manner of Puta-paka" (Uttarasthana ch.17, line 280).

The Ena's blood is a separate item: after a bleeding-too-freely accident in blood-letting, the patient's ordinary drink is to be "the blood of the Ena or common deer, or of a sheep, hare, or buffalo" (Sutrasthana ch.14, line 399). Bhishagratna glosses the Ena there as "common deer", not "black deer". Deer blood in the Ebers and Tibetan texts is in `III_32_Deer_blood.md`; other venisons are in `III_42_Antelope_flesh.md`.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, venison is lean muscle: mostly water and protein, with little fat, and a source of iron, zinc and B vitamins.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text records black-deer venison as a food: palatable, strengthening, a relish-improver and a febrifuge, safe for all, and prescribed in the soups for fever, cough, asthma and alcoholism listed above. It is food and regimen. Which deer Bhishagratna's "black deer" is cannot be settled from the text, and deer are wild animals, often protected. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no one hunt or buy one for this.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the text gives are all in the loci above: soup or meat-juice (*Rasa*) of the flesh, "unmixed with any acid" and taken with sugar and ghee in alcoholism (Uttara ch.47); soup of the flesh after emetics and purgatives (Chikitsa ch.39); soup with Kulattha cooked with the heads (Uttara ch.51); ghee cooked with the essence (Uttara ch.52); the flesh with salt, honey and ghee applied to the eye as *Puta-paka* (Uttara ch.17). (The "moderate quantity" of Uttara ch.39 lines 910-917 is said of the heavier birds named there, not of the deer.) No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Lean game meat is a source of complete protein and iron. Undercooked wild game can transmit bacterial and parasitic infection; deer can carry chronic wasting disease in some regions. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** ch.46 line 431-436: sweet and astringent in taste, palatable, curative in diseases from deranged *Pittam*, blood and *Kapham*, "astringent in its effect", strengthening, a relish-improver and a febrifuge. The group statement (lines 426-429) adds light, keen, pleasant, laxative and diuretic, subduing *Vayu* and *Pittam*. The English prints no separate *virya* or *vipaka* for the Ena in the passage read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The Ena's flesh is attested as a food of the first-ranked high-ground group and among "the best" of its kind (ch.46, lines 385-394 and 2162), as safe for all (ch.20), and as a diet in fever, alcoholism, cough, asthma, and after cleansing (Uttara and Chikitsa chapters above). That is a regimen statement. In the passages read, it is not placed in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and the English prints no claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The popular *sattvic*/*rajasic*/*tamasic* food lists belong to *Samkhya*-era and later material, not to Sushruta, and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes black-deer venison a cultivation substance. Sushruta places it in the first-ranked group of meats and uses it as a light, strengthening diet for the sick. That is dietary advice, not practice. A cultivator takes ordinary food from this entry, or nothing. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Conservation and law:** wild deer are protected or regulated in many places; killing, possessing or trading in them can be restricted or illegal. The modern identity of the *Ena* is not settled by the text. [USER: verify]
- **Hygiene and infection:** wild-killed meat carries parasitic and bacterial risk and must be cooked through; handling carcasses carries zoonotic risk.
- **Fever (Sushruta):** the fever diet is for a patient "accustomed to the use of animal food" (ch.39).
- **Ethics and diet:** many people abstain from meat; the Codex recommends no animal food.
- **Pregnancy:** ordinary food hygiene applies; nothing is stated in the sources.
