# Kaolin — Bai Wu (白堊) "Kaolin, Chalk" (Yang 1998)
## *A white earth in the Divine Farmer's Classic; Yang 1998 prints "Kaolin, Chalk" for Bai Wu, a double rendering that leaves open whether the drug was kaolin clay or chalk (calcium carbonate)* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Bai Wu is printed in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) on PDF page 34, directly under the heading "Jades and Stones: Inferior Class" (line 1311). The text (lines 1312-1315): "Bai Wu (Kaolin, Chalk)70 is bitter and warm. It mainly treats females' cold and heat, concretions and conglomerations, menstrual block [i.e., amenorrhea], accumulations and gatherings, genital swelling and pain, leaking, and infertility. It is produced in mountains and valleys." The words in square brackets are the translator's. Yang's footnote 70 (lines 1323-1326) adds a use that is not in the Classic's line: "Chalk is also used to treat vacuity heat stomach reflux, diarrhea, nosebleed, hemorrhoids, and, externally, sores on the lower leg, genital itching, and head sores." That is the translator's commentary from later practice and is not the Classic's text. `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` has the row (line 39) "Bai Wu, Jades and Stones: Inferior Class, PDF p.34 (line 1312), Kaolin, Chalk, mineral".

The list `_sources/shennong.csv` carries the row 白堊 (line 244), in characters only, between 鹵鹹 (line 243), 鉛丹 (245) and 粉錫 (246). Those neighbours follow the same order as Lu Xian (Alkali), Qian Dan (Minium) and Fen Xi on Yang's page 33-34, which supports reading 白堊 as Yang's Bai Wu. The match is by order and not by a printed character, because Yang's text layer is English only. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The spine's note is "low; id-uncertain", and the doubt is real. Yang prints two English names for the one drug, "Kaolin" and "Chalk", and his footnote speaks of "Chalk" only. Kaolin is a clay (hydrated aluminium silicate); chalk is soft limestone (calcium carbonate). The sources read do not decide between them. The project's spine labels the row with the kaolinite formula; that label is the project's and is not printed by Yang. Elsewhere in the book the translator says of five coloured stones: "The identities of these medicinals are controversial. Many people believe that all except for Hei Shi Zhi are variously colored Kaolinite clays, while Hei Shi Zhi is a kind of Graphite" (footnote 45, lines 1043-1045, on the entry "Qing Shi, Chi Shi, Huang Shi, Bai Shi, Hei Shi", line 1022 ff.). That entry lies on page 26, inside the run headed "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (headings at lines 985 and 1060). It is a different entry and is named here only so that the two are not confused. Another entry of this Codex (II_113, Calcium hydrate / lime) is a Dioscorides chapter for a different tradition and is not part of this one.

### Active Components

Kaolin is hydrated aluminium silicate, Al2Si2O5(OH)4, a soft white clay mineral that is insoluble in water and chemically inert in the gut. Chalk is calcium carbonate, CaCO3, a soft fine-grained limestone that neutralises acid. Because the Classic's drug may be either, the composition of the substance meant is unknown. The sources read give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records Bai Wu for women's complaints: cold and heat, masses and lumps, absent menses, genital swelling and pain, abnormal discharge and infertility. The Codex does not endorse any of these uses and offers no protocol, dose or preparation. Swallowing earths is not recommended, and the identity of the material cannot be settled from the sources read.

### Scientific Correlation

Kaolin is an industrial and pharmaceutical-grade clay used in ceramics, paper and as an excipient and adsorbent; calcium carbonate (chalk) is a common antacid and calcium supplement. Neither is established as a treatment for amenorrhoea, genital swelling or infertility. Clay eaten in quantity can bind other drugs and cause constipation, and unrefined earths may carry lead and other contaminants. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: a lower-grade drug (下品).* Bai Wu stands under "Jades and Stones: Inferior Class" (Yang line 1311). Yang's rendering of the Classic's preface says the inferior class "mainly treat disease and correspond to earth. They are usually toxic and cannot be taken for a long time. If one intends to eliminate cold and heat and evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and cure disease, one should base [one's efforts] on the inferior class" (lines 391-395), and his note says the class "specifically treat disease", is "usually at least slightly toxic" and "cannot be taken in large amounts or for prolonged periods of time without developing negative side effects" (lines 362-366). Under this grade the drug is for a defined complaint and a short course and has no nourishing-life role. The monograph itself contains no phrase about protracted taking, lightness of body or long life. The grade is a statement of the Classic as translated by Yang in 1998 and is not carried forward from later materia medica.

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: the Classic ranks it as something taken against a complaint and then stopped, and the tradition's own grading is the warning. Nothing here supports taking a white earth for cultivation, and the identity of the earth is itself uncertain. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Eating clay or chalk can cause constipation and bowel obstruction, interferes with the absorption of other medicines and minerals, and may carry heavy-metal or microbial contamination. The Classic's own grade says the lower class is for short use. The material is unstandardised, and the Classic's uses for amenorrhoea and infertility are not claims of fact; a missed period or failure to conceive needs proper medical assessment, including for pregnancy. The identity of the drug (kaolin or chalk) is uncertain, so no safety statement for the one holds for the other. [USER: verify]
