# I-224 — Terminalia arjuna (Roxb. ex DC.) Wight & Arn. (Arjuna / White Murdah)

## Section 1: Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Canonical Name:** *Terminalia arjuna* (Roxb. ex DC.) Wight & Arn.  
**Family:** Combretaceae  
**Common Names:** Arjuna (Sanskrit/Hindi — named after the mythological archer-warrior of the Mahabharata epic); White Murdah, White mudar (English); Arjun (Hindi); Arjunan, Vella Marudu (Tamil/Malayalam); Neer Marudu (Tamil); Kumbuk (Sinhala/Sri Lanka); Koha (Sanskrit alternate)

**Etymological note:** Named for Arjuna, the greatest archer in Hindu mythology (Mahabharata), son of Indra the storm god. Arjuna the warrior is associated with: white color (*arjuna* = white/silver in Sanskrit), strength of heart (literal and metaphorical — in the Mahabharata, Arjuna faces the moral crisis of war and receives the Bhagavad Gita from Krishna), physical endurance. The herb's association with cardiac health perfectly maps the mythological namesake's qualities: heart (courage + the physical organ), whiteness (white bark), strength. This is one of the most elegant naming conventions in Ayurvedic botanical nomenclature — the herb IS the metaphor.

**Botanical identity:** Large deciduous tree; 20–30 m; buttressed trunk with smooth grey bark (distinctive: bark peels in smooth flakes revealing cream/white inner bark — the characteristic that prompted the "white" in common names and possibly the Sanskrit "arjuna" association); leaves alternate, oblong-elliptic; conical spikes of small white flowers; drupes with 5 prominent longitudinal wings (characteristic of *Terminalia* genus); found along riverbanks (riparian habitat) across the Indian subcontinent; also grown ornamentally

**Terminalia genus context:** Three Terminalia species appear in this Codex:
- *T. chebula* (Haritaki, I-203): Digestive, anti-microbial, "King of Medicines"
- *T. bellerica* (Bibhitaki, I-205): Respiratory, HMG-CoA inhibition
- *T. arjuna* (this entry): Cardioprotective specialist
All three are in Combretaceae; all three share some polyphenol chemistry; each has distinct primary indication reflecting different tannin/triterpenoid profiles.

**Primary bioactives:** Arjunolic acid (triterpenoid saponin); arjunetin; arjunetosides I–IV; tannins (arjungenin, castalagin); beta-sitosterol; calcium, magnesium, zinc

**Historical clinical significance:** Arjuna bark's documentation as a specific *hridaya* (heart) medicine in Charaka Samhita (ca. 300 BCE) represents one of the earliest formal cardiac pharmacology entries in world medicine — predating the development of Western cardiological concepts by over two millennia. The traditional prescription to specifically target the heart as an organ (rather than treating systemic conditions with secondary cardiac effects) is pharmacologically notable.

## Section 3: Convergence Analysis

**Why primarily in the Ayurvedic tradition:**

*Terminalia arjuna* is geographically restricted to the Indian subcontinent and Sri Lanka's riparian zones. Unlike spices or aromatics that traveled trade routes, bark medicine from a large native tree did not become a trade commodity. Its pharmacological properties were discovered and systematically documented only within the traditions that had direct access to the tree.

**The cardiac-specific specialization:**

Among approximately 100+ Ayurvedic herbs with cardiovascular effects, Arjuna emerged as the one specifically classified as *hridaya* (heart-specific) — a pharmacological precision that was empirically derived without understanding myocardial tissue mechanics, left ventricular ejection fraction, or cardiac biomarkers. That modern RCTs document specific left ventricular function improvement, anti-ischemic effects, and cardiac enzyme protection validates the ancient classification through mechanism-independent convergence: traditional physicians observed a clinically distinct cardiac response that led to "heart specialist" classification.

**Comparison with Western cardiac pharmacology:**

Arjuna's documented mechanisms (positive inotropic effect, anti-ischemic, diuretic, anti-platelet) overlap with standard cardiology approaches (digoxin for positive inotropy, nitrates for ischemia, diuretics, antiplatelets). The heart failure RCT evidence (Bharani 1995) suggesting Arjuna achieved effects comparable to isosorbide mononitrate is remarkable — this class of overlap (natural compound equivalent to a standard drug) is among the strongest validations in the Codex.

## Section 4: Phytochemical Composition

### Triterpenoid saponins (primary bioactives):

**Arjunolic acid (principal triterpenoid — 0.15–0.4% of bark):**
- Chemical name: 2α,3β,23-trihydroxyolean-12-en-28-oic acid; oleanane-type pentacyclic triterpenoid
- Mechanism: Cardiac glycoside-like positive inotropic effect (but via different mechanism than digoxin); anti-oxidant; anti-ischemic; hepatoprotective

**Arjunetin (oleanane triterpenoid glycoside):**
- Cardiac protective; anti-inflammatory; structurally related to arjunolic acid

**Arjunetosides I, II, III, IV (glycosides of arjunolic acid):**
- Pro-drugs converted to arjunolic acid by intestinal bacteria
- Water-soluble; good oral absorption

**Terminolic acid (minor triterpenoid):**
- Related structure; synergizes with arjunolic acid

### Tannins (secondary bioactives — significant fraction, 10–25% of bark):

- **Arjungenin:** Ellagitannin; anti-oxidant; anti-HIV preclinical
- **Castalagin:** Ellagitannin; also in Haritaki/Amalaki — cross-reference to urolithin pathway (I-203, I-204, I-206)
- **Punicalagin:** Most potent ellagitannin antioxidant; also in pomegranate
- **Chebulinic acid:** Also in Haritaki; anti-microbial

**The Terminalia-ellagitannin family:** Three Terminalia entries in this Codex (Haritaki I-203, Bibhitaki I-205, Arjuna I-224) all contain significant ellagitannin fractions — a phytochemical family connection reflecting common Combretaceae chemistry. The urolithin pathway (ellagitannins → gut microbiome → urolithin A → mitophagy) documented for Haritaki and Triphala (I-206) is potentially active for Arjuna bark as well, representing a mechanistic family connection across multiple Codex entries.

### Phenolic acids:
- **Gallic acid:** Broad-spectrum antioxidant, anti-tumor, anti-viral
- **Ellagic acid:** Anti-tumor, anti-inflammatory (see Amalaki I-204)
- **Caffeic acid, ferulic acid:** Standard anti-inflammatory phenolics

### Minerals (clinically relevant quantities in bark):
- **Calcium (734 mg/100g bark):** Relevant to cardiac function; contributes to the bark's cardiac-specific effect
- **Magnesium (388 mg/100g):** Critical for cardiac rhythm and myocardial relaxation
- **Zinc (198 mg/100g):** Immune, reproductive, wound healing
- The mineral content of Arjuna bark is unusually high compared to most herbs — a unique phytochemical feature that may contribute to its cardiac-specific action (both calcium and magnesium are essential for normal cardiac contractility and rhythm)

### Phytosterols:
- **Beta-sitosterol:** Anti-inflammatory; cholesterol-lowering (blocks intestinal cholesterol absorption via NPC1L1 competition — same mechanism as ezetimibe)
- **Beta-sitosterol-3-O-beta-D-glucoside:** Cardiac protective; anti-arthritic

## Section 5: Mechanisms of Action

### 5a. Positive Inotropic Effect (Cardiac Contractility Enhancement)

The most clinically significant mechanism for heart failure:

- **Arjunolic acid:** Increases intracellular calcium availability in cardiac myocytes → enhanced actin-myosin cross-bridge cycling → increased contractile force
- Mechanism differs from digoxin (Na+/K+-ATPase inhibition) — arjunolic acid does NOT inhibit Na+/K+-ATPase at therapeutic doses
- **Possible mechanism:** Arjunolic acid may enhance sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium release (ryanodine receptor 2 modulation) → increased systolic calcium transient → stronger contraction
- Net clinical effect: Improved left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), reduced dyspnea in heart failure

### 5b. Anti-ischemic / Cardioprotective Mechanism

**Free radical scavenging:**
- High tannin + ellagitannin content → powerful antioxidant capacity; reduces reactive oxygen species (ROS) during ischemia/reperfusion
- Prevents oxidative damage to mitochondrial membrane in cardiac cells during ischemic events

**Anti-peroxidative:**
- Inhibits lipid peroxidation of cardiac membranes → preserves membrane integrity during ischemia

**Cardiac enzyme protection:**
- Reduces elevation of cardiac enzymes (CPK-MB, LDH) after ischemic events in animal models → cardioprotective/anti-infarct action

**Mitochondrial protection:**
- Arjunolic acid stabilizes cardiac mitochondria during oxidative stress → prevents the mitochondrial permeability transition (MPT) — a key step in ischemia-induced cardiomyocyte death

### 5c. Anti-hypertensive Mechanism

- **Diuretic activity (mild):** Arjuna bark has mild diuretic properties → reduced preload → reduced cardiac workload → reduced blood pressure in fluid-overloaded states
- **Vasodilatory:** Arjunolic acid → prostaglandin E2 elevation in vascular smooth muscle → vasodilation
- **ACE inhibition (mild):** Some tannin fractions → angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibition → reduced angiotensin II → vasodilation

### 5d. Lipid-Modifying Mechanism

- **Beta-sitosterol:** Competes with cholesterol for NPC1L1 intestinal absorption transporter → reduces dietary cholesterol absorption (ezetimibe-like mechanism)
- **Tannins:** Bile acid binding in gut (similar to cholestyramine, but weaker) → increased bile acid excretion → compensatory conversion of cholesterol to bile acids → LDL reduction
- **Arjunolic acid:** Possible HMG-CoA reductase inhibition (mild; less potent than Bibhitaki's belleric acid — I-205)

### 5e. Anti-platelet Mechanism

- Platelet aggregation inhibition (ADP and thrombin pathways)
- Reduces thromboxane B2 (the pro-aggregatory eicosanoid)
- Increases prostacyclin:thromboxane ratio → net anti-thrombotic effect
- Important in coronary artery disease context: anti-platelet + anti-ischemic together address both the occlusion risk and the ischemic damage

## Section 6: Clinical Evidence

### 6a. Heart Failure / Cardiac Function — STRONG EVIDENCE

**Bharani 1995 RCT** (N=40, established stable angina and ischemic cardiomyopathy):
- Arjuna bark decoction 500 mL/day (prepared as *Arjuna Ksheerarpaka* — milk decoction) vs. isosorbide mononitrate 40 mg/day vs. placebo
- **Arjuna vs. isosorbide mononitrate:** No significant difference in symptom relief (chest pain frequency, severity, exercise tolerance) — both superior to placebo
- Arjuna: Improved echocardiographic left ventricular function
- Significance: Equivalent clinical efficacy to a standard cardiac drug (isosorbide mononitrate) is among the most striking herbal medicine RCT findings in any tradition

**Dwivedi & Jauhari 1997 RCT** (N=58, ischemic mitral regurgitation):
- Arjuna bark 500 mg powder 3× daily × 3 months
- Significant improvement in LVEF (left ventricular ejection fraction) by echocardiography
- Reduction in angina frequency
- Improved exercise capacity (treadmill test)

**Joshi 2009** (N=30, heart failure NYHA class II-III):
- Arjuna extract + standard therapy vs. placebo + standard therapy
- Arjuna group: Significantly improved LVEF and reduced BNP (brain natriuretic peptide — heart failure severity biomarker)

**Overall assessment:** Multiple positive RCTs for cardiac function improvement; evidence level = STRONG for angina; MODERATE for systolic heart failure

### 6b. Hypertension — MODERATE EVIDENCE

**Multiple small RCTs:** Arjuna bark preparations → modest but significant systolic and diastolic BP reductions (typically -5 to -10 mmHg systolic)

**Singh 1995** (N=57): Arjuna extract → significant reduction in systolic BP and total cholesterol

Not a primary antihypertensive agent but potentially useful adjunct

### 6c. Dyslipidemia — MODERATE EVIDENCE

**Multiple Indian RCTs:** Total cholesterol reductions 9–15%; LDL reductions 8–12%; triglyceride reductions 10–15%; HDL increases 4–8%  
**Mechanism:** Beta-sitosterol + tannin bile acid binding + mild HMG-CoA inhibition  
Not as potent as statins but clinically meaningful, especially for mild dyslipidemia or as adjunct

### 6d. Chronic Stable Angina — GOOD EVIDENCE

- Consistent improvement in angina frequency and severity across multiple trials
- Anti-ischemic mechanism well-supported
- Comparable benefit to Bharani 1995 isosorbide mononitrate comparison
- Current position: Useful adjunct to standard angina therapy; not replacement for guideline-directed medical therapy in high-risk patients

## Section 7: Phenomenological Record

### The warrior's heart and Ayurvedic cardiology

The association of Arjuna (herb) with Arjuna (the archer-warrior of the Mahabharata) is one of the most culturally rich naming traditions in the Codex. In the Bhagavad Gita — the philosophical discourse embedded in the Mahabharata — Arjuna the warrior faces a profound crisis of courage as he prepares to fight his own kinsmen. His heart fails him at the crucial moment, and it is Krishna's teaching (the Gita) that restores his cardiac courage.

In Ayurvedic practice, the herb Arjuna is prescribed for conditions understood as *hridaya daurbalya* (heart weakness) — but this phrase encompasses both physical weakness (heart failure, angina) and what we might call emotional-cardiac conditions (anxiety, grief, palpitations from emotional distress). The herb named for the warrior who doubted his heart is the herb that strengthens the heart — physical and metaphorical convergence of the most elegant kind.

The phenomenological tradition of Arjuna consumption is inseparable from this metaphorical dimension: to drink Arjuna bark decoction is (in the traditional understanding) not only to support myocardial contractility but to cultivate the heart-quality of the great warrior — clarity, courage, steadiness.

### Clinical phenomenology in heart failure patients

The patient experience documented in Bharani 1995 and Dwivedi 1997: reduced chest pain, increased exercise tolerance, reduced dyspnea on exertion, better sleep (reduced nocturnal dyspnea) — these functional improvements in daily life quality represent a phenomenological record of cardiac rehabilitation. Heart failure patients on Arjuna bark described (in clinical observation reports) the ability to perform activities they had been unable to do for months or years — the phenomenological equivalent of reclaiming life from a progressively debilitating condition.

## Section 8: Practical Application

### Part A: Forms and Preparation

**Traditional Ayurvedic preparation (gold standard):**
- **Arjuna Ksheerarpaka (milk decoction):** The classical preparation used in most RCTs
  - Recipe: 5 g Arjuna bark powder + 250 mL water + 250 mL milk → boil gently until 250 mL remains → strain → drink warm once or twice daily
  - Milk serves as: lipid carrier for lipophilic triterpenoids; cardiac tonic (calcium/magnesium from milk) + cardiac tonic (herb minerals and saponins); traditionally: milk enhances absorption and palatability
- **Arjuna churna (powder):** 1–3 g twice daily with warm water or milk

**Standardized extract (supplement):**
- Arjuna bark extract standardized to: 5–15% arjunolic acid; 15–25% total polyphenols
- Typical product: 500–750 mg extract twice daily

**Arjunarishta (traditional fermented preparation):**
- Arjuna bark + multiple herbs fermented; 15–25 mL after meals; classical cardiac tonic formulation

**Arjuna tea:**
- 1–2 tsp bark powder simmered in water 10–15 minutes; strain; drink daily; traditional "heart tea"

### Part B: Dosage Guidelines

**Heart failure / angina (adjunct to conventional cardiology care):**
- Arjuna Ksheerarpaka: 250–500 mL once daily (equivalent ~5–10 g bark powder)
- Standardized extract: 500 mg 2–3× daily
- Duration: 3–6 months minimum; continue with monitoring

**Preventive cardiac support / hypertension:**
- Lower dose: Arjuna churna 1–2 g daily in warm milk
- Long-term traditional Rasayana-adjacent use

**Lipid management:**
- Standardized extract 500 mg twice daily; assess lipid panel at 3 months

### Part C: Safety Profile

**Overall: GOOD SAFETY PROFILE — generally well-tolerated**

**Common adverse effects:**
- Constipation (high tannin content; reduce dose or increase water intake)
- Headache (mild, initial use — related to vasodilatory effect)
- Mild nausea (with large doses; take with food or milk)
- Gastrointestinal heaviness

**Significant safety notes:**

**Cardiac monitoring essential:**
- Given direct cardiac effects (positive inotropic, blood pressure effects), patients with significant cardiac conditions (heart failure, arrhythmia, already on multiple cardiac drugs) should use only under physician supervision with ECG monitoring
- Not a replacement for guideline-directed medical therapy (ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, diuretics, SGLT2 inhibitors for heart failure)

**Calcium content:**
- High calcium bark content → relevant in patients with hypercalcemia or calcium-sensitive conditions; theoretical concern with excess supplemental calcium risk (arterial calcification); unlikely at typical doses

**Thyroid interaction (minor):**
- Possible mild thyroid hormone modulation; lower concern than Ashwagandha or Guggul; monitor in thyroid-disease patients

**Pregnancy:**
- Insufficient safety data; traditional texts generally list as safe in moderation; AVOID high-dose preparations pending adequate data
- Classify as: "Traditional use with caution in pregnancy; formal contraindication not established but no confirmatory safety data either"

### Part D: Drug Interactions

| Drug/Class | Interaction | Clinical Action |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Digoxin** | Arjuna has cardiac glycoside-like positive inotropic activity → possible additive effect → increased risk of digoxin toxicity (bradycardia, arrhythmia) | Use with extreme caution; monitor heart rate and digoxin levels; consider avoiding combination |
| **Antihypertensives** | Arjuna has modest anti-hypertensive effect → additive blood pressure lowering | Monitor BP; dose adjustment of antihypertensives may be needed |
| **Warfarin** | Anti-platelet activity + possible tannin effects → may enhance anticoagulation | Monitor INR; caution in anticoagulated patients |
| **Aspirin / Antiplatelet drugs** | Additive anti-platelet effect | Increased bleeding risk; monitor; combination probably acceptable at usual doses in low-bleeding-risk patients |
| **Calcium channel blockers** (amlodipine, diltiazem, verapamil) | Possible additive vasodilatory and cardiac effects | Monitor BP and heart rate |
| **Beta-blockers** | Both reduce heart rate and cardiac workload; usually complementary but monitor | HR monitoring; generally safe combination |
| **Statins** (mild HMG-CoA inhibition) | Possibly additive lipid-lowering; generally complementary and safe | Generally acceptable combination; monitor for myopathy at higher statin doses |
| **Diuretics** | Arjuna's mild diuretic effect → additive fluid/electrolyte effects | Monitor electrolytes (especially potassium); hypokalemia risk with combined diuretics |
| **Tannin-mineral absorption** | High tannin content → may reduce iron, zinc, calcium absorption of co-administered supplements | Separate Arjuna from mineral supplements by 2 hours |

### Part E: Contraindications

- **Severe heart failure (NYHA Class IV):** Only under specialist cardiology supervision; may be appropriate adjunct but clinical instability requires medical control first
- **Significant cardiac arrhythmia:** Consult cardiologist (positive inotropic effect could theoretically worsen certain arrhythmias)
- **Concurrent digoxin therapy:** High caution; avoid unless cardiologist supervises
- **Hypercalcemia:** Caution given high bark calcium content
- **Active bleeding / hemorrhagic conditions:** Anti-platelet effect → increased risk
