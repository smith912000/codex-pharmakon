# Arsenolite (Yu Shi) — Yu Shi, "Arsenolitum" (Yang 1998); other names Qing Fen Shi, Li Zhi Shi, Gu Yang Shi; list row 礜石
## *An arsenical stone of the Divine Farmer's Classic that Yang glosses "Arsenolitum" (arsenolite, arsenic trioxide); his footnote 66 says the identity is controversial, one suggestion being Mispickel, which others reject* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yu Shi is printed in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) on PDF page 32, directly under the heading "Jades and Stones: Inferior Class" (line 1240, the top of that page), and runs over onto page 33. The text (lines 1250-1253 and 1274-1275): "Yu Shi (Arsenolitum)66 is acrid. It is toxic, treating mainly cold and heat, mouse fistulas, erosion sores, dead muscles, wind impediment, hardness in the abdomen, and evil qi. It eliminates heat. Its other name is Qing Fen Shi (Green-blue Breakable Stone). Yet another name is Li Zhi Shi (Instantly Ready Stone). It is also called Gu Yang Shi (Fasten Goat Stone). It is produced in mountains and valleys." Yang's rendering of the third name is damaged in the OCR ("Fasten (;o.JI Stollt'"); "Fasten Goat Stone" restores it from the damaged print. Yang's footnote 66 (lines 1265-1269): "The identity of this medicinal is controversial. Some people suggest it is really Mispickel, while others reject this notion. In many other versions, there is 'and greatly hot' after the word 'acrid.'" Mispickel is the old name of arsenopyrite, an iron arsenic sulfide, so the dispute is between an oxide (arsenolite) and a sulfarsenide. The footnote also records a textual variant: other versions of the Classic add "and greatly hot" to the quality, which this version does not.

One detail of the printed text is worth setting down. The name "Li Zhi Shi (Instantly Ready Stone)" is also the other name printed for Li Shi (Gypsum Fibrosum) at line 1184, so the same alias is given to two entries in Yang's text. Yang prints no comment, and the sources retrieved do not say which is the earlier use. [USER: verify]

The list `_sources/shennong.csv` carries the row as 礜石 (line 241), and `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` has the row "Yu Shi" (line 34) with the identification "Arsenolitum". The spine's note for this row is "low", with no identity doubt marked, although Yang's footnote 66 gives one. DMM and the other texts were checked: the Dioscorides entry ARSENIKON (5-121) is glossed in the 2000 edition as orpiment and realgar, arsenic sulfides, so it is not the same substance as arsenolite and is not applied here.

### Active Components

Arsenolite is arsenic trioxide, As2O3, a white crystalline compound that occurs as a rare secondary mineral formed by the weathering of arsenic ores and is also the main commercial arsenic compound; in the form of "white arsenic" it is the classical poison. It is sparingly soluble in water. If the stone were mispickel (arsenopyrite), it would be an iron arsenic sulfide, FeAsS, a different and much less soluble compound. The sources read give no composition for the stone Yang translates.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records the stone, which it calls toxic, for cold and heat, fistulas, erosive sores, dead muscles, wind impediment and abdominal hardness. The Codex does not endorse these uses and offers no protocol. Arsenic compounds are poisonous, and nothing in the sources read supports taking this stone.

### Scientific Correlation

Arsenic trioxide is acutely toxic, and inorganic arsenic compounds are classified as human carcinogens; chronic intake causes skin, nerve and organ damage. A pharmaceutical preparation of arsenic trioxide is used in modern medicine under specialist supervision for a particular leukaemia; that is a fact about a purified, dosed drug and supports nothing about the stone of the Classic. ### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: a lower-grade drug (下品), toxic and for a short course.* Yu Shi stands under "Jades and Stones: Inferior Class" (Yang line 1240). Yang's preface says the inferior class medicinals "mainly treat disease and correspond to earth. They are usually toxic and cannot be taken for a long time. If one intends to eliminate cold and heat and evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and cure disease, one should base [one's efforts] on the inferior class" (lines 391-395). Yang's own note on the classes (lines 356-366) says inferior class medicinals "specifically treat disease. They are usually at least slightly toxic" and "cannot be taken in large amounts or for prolonged periods of time without developing negative side effects". The monograph itself says "It is toxic" and prints no longevity, lightness or immortality claim. The grade is a statement of the Classic as translated by Yang in 1998. It carries a cultivation meaning only in the negative: the class is the one the Classic sets apart from the drugs "that nourish life".

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: a toxic drug against disease, to be stopped when the disease is hit, and the tradition's own grading is the warning. Arsenic is a poison, and nothing here supports taking it. For a cultivator the entry is a record of a drug the Classic kept out of the "nourish life" class. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Contraindications

Arsenic compounds are poisonous by mouth, by skin absorption and by inhalation; the Classic itself says "It is toxic". Arsenic is cumulative and carcinogenic, and exposure in pregnancy and childhood is of particular concern. Powdered stone sold as a remedy may be a different mineral or may be heavily contaminated.
