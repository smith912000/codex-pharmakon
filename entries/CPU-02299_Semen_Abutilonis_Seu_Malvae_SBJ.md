# Semen Abutilonis Seu Malvae — Gu Huo 姑活 (Shennong bencao jing); alias Dong Kui Zi 冬葵子 (in this entry)

## *Gu huo 姑活, an herb of the Shennong bencao jing whose botanical identity is not established. Yang 1998 prints the gloss "Semen Abutilonis Seu Malvae" (seed of Abutilon or mallow), taken from the alias Dong Kui Zi; Tao Hongjing's note in Sun Xingyan's edition says that this Dong Kui Zi is not the seed of the vegetable mallow, and Yang's own footnote says the drug has long been out of clinical use. Identification: unidentified. The first-pass guess (Abutilon theophrasti) is not adopted. Basis: the two editions and Tao's note. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY AND TOXICITY CAUTION.** Tao Hongjing's note ties a "Gu huo pill" to a name of a different, poisonous drug (see below), and the plant here is not identified. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather any plant under this name.

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; the other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), is read as a second witness and attributed to Yang. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Semen Abutilonis Seu Malvae [SBJ]", taken from Yang's English; this entry replaces it, and gives it its Chinese name 姑活. A search of the other texts on disk for the Chinese and pinyin names found nothing for this drug. The Codex already has an entry for the vegetable mallow seed, 冬葵子, in its own upper-classic entry (Malva verticillata, CPU-01750, Sun line 1623); that is a different entry in the classic and is not merged with this one (see below).

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** In Sun's edition the entry is 姑活 *gu huo* in the **upper classic** (卷一 上经; Sun's text, heading at line 1085, with 卷一 上经 printed at lines 1083 and 1093). The paragraph (line 1087) reads: 味甘，温 ("sweet, warm"); 主大风邪气，湿痹寒痛 ("governs great wind and evil qi, damp impediment, cold pain"); 久服，轻身、益寿、耐老 ("taken long: lighten the body, increase the years, bear old age"); 一名冬葵子 ("one name: *dong kui zi*"), followed by Sun's own bracketed note 旧在《唐本草》中，无毒，今增 ("formerly in the *Tang bencao*; non-toxic; now added"). That note says that Sun took the entry from the *Tang bencao* and placed it here; the placement is therefore Sun's reconstruction, not a reading of an older complete text. The Mingyi bielu layer (line 1089) adds only the habitat 生河东 ("grows in Hedong").

Sun's note (案, line 1091) gives the quotation and the warning that govern the identity. It says: the *Shui jing zhu*, under Xie county, quotes the *Shennong bencao* as saying that "the earth has the kinds of *gu huo*, *nü shu*, *tong yun* and *zi yuan*" (固活、女疏、铜芸、紫苑之族). Then it gives Tao Hongjing: 方药亦无用此者，乃有固活丸，即是野葛一名 ("no prescription uses this; there is a *Gu huo pill*, which is a name of *Ye ge*"). Then: 此又名冬葵子，非葵菜之冬葵子，疗体乖异 ("this is also named *dong kui zi*, but it is not the *dong kui zi* of the mallow vegetable; its treatment-body, i.e. its therapy, differs"). The text therefore warns, in the same breath, against taking the alias for the vegetable mallow seed and ties a pill of similar name to *Ye ge*. In Sun's own text 野葛 is an alias of 钩吻 *gou wen* (lower classic, line 3201), a drug of which the Wu Pu layer records the opinion of Lei Gong as "toxic, kills people" (line 3203), and Yang's footnote 204 calls Gou Wen (Herba Gelsemii Elegantis) "fatally poisonous" (line 3332). Whether Tao's remark means that the herb *gu huo* is *ye ge*, or that a pill of that name is made from it, or only that the name has been confused, cannot be settled from the lines read, and the Codex draws no conclusion. **Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 3758): "Gu Huo (Semen Abutilonis Seu Malvae) is sweet and warm. It mainly treats great wind, evil qi, damp impediment, and cold pain. Protracted taking may make the body light, increase life span, and slow aging. Its other name is Dong Kui Zi (Winter Big Flower Seed). It grows in rivers and swamps." Two differences from Sun need stating. First, Yang files it in his **Inferior Class** (PDF page 101, headed "Herbs: Inferior Class") while Sun files it in the upper classic; the register below follows Sun. Second, Yang's habitat ("rivers and swamps") differs from the Mingyi "Hedong"; the Mingyi layer is not part of Yang's text. Yang's footnote 237 (line 3776) is modern commentary: "This medicinal has long been out of clinical use." The Latin gloss, "Abutilon or mallow seed", is Yang's, taken from the alias; it is not in the classic.

The row therefore leaves three things unresolved: which plant *gu huo* is; whether the alias *dong kui zi* ever pointed to Abutilon or to any mallow; and what Tao's equation with *ye ge* means. The first-pass guess *Abutilon theophrasti* has no line behind it in the sources read, and is not adopted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unidentified; a poisonous namesake is flagged in the sources.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Great wind and evil qi, damp impediment and cold pain; taken long, it lightens the body, increases the years and bears old age (Sun, line 1087).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no part used in Sun's text, no duration, and no description of the plant. Tao Hongjing states that no prescription uses it.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend any plant for the conditions named.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. Tao Hongjing's note (line 1091) says that prescriptions do not use it. Yang's Latin gloss ("seed") names a part, but the classic does not. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Because the plant is unidentified, no pharmacology can be stated for it. The mallow family (Malvaceae) contains mucilaginous plants, of which both mallow and *Abutilon* are members, but the sources do not tie this drug to them. The Gou Wen of Yang's footnote is a different plant (*Gelsemium elegans*) that is generally regarded as highly poisonous; the Codex cites it only because the sources bring its alias into the discussion. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The paragraph stands in the upper classic (上经) in Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction. The preface (Sun line 43) reads 上药一百二十种，为君，主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经: the upper drugs are "the lords, mainly nourishing life to correspond with heaven; non-toxic; taking much or taking long does not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body and boost the qi, not age and extend the years". The drug's own paragraph carries a 久服 clause that promises 轻身 (lighten the body), 益寿 (increase the years) and 耐老 (bear old age). That is the life-nourishing status of the *san pin* scheme; 輕身 has no pharmacological equivalent and is not weight loss. Three cautions apply. Yang files the drug in the **inferior** class, and where Sun and Yang place a drug in different grades, Sun's grade decides, and the difference is stated here. Sun's own note records that he added the entry from the *Tang bencao*. Tao Hongjing's note, finally, says that no prescription uses the drug and links its name to a poisonous plant's alias. The grade is therefore attested by Sun's reconstruction, but the drug is not usable. The text does not mention *jing*, *qi* or *shen* as acted on by this drug, and none is claimed.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade entry on paper whose plant nobody can name, whose placement is Sun's reconstruction (Yang puts it in the lowest class), and whose name Tao Hongjing links to a poison. A cultivator should read the grade as a fact about a text and not act on it.

### Contraindications

- **POSSIBLE TOXIC NAMESAKE.** Tao Hongjing's note ties the name to *Ye ge* (an alias of the poisonous *Gou wen*). Do not ingest anything sold under the name *gu huo* or *dong kui zi* on the strength of this entry.
- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified. Do not substitute mallow seed, *Abutilon* seed or any other herb for it. Tao states the *dong kui zi* here is not the vegetable's.
- **The conditions named.** Wind, damp impediment and cold pain are named in the classic's terms; they are not modern diagnoses. Joint and pain conditions call for proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Text variants.** The grade (upper in Sun, inferior in Yang) and the habitat differ between editions.
