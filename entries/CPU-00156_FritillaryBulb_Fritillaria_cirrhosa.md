### Origin & Tradition

*Fritillaria cirrhosa* is the alpine fritillary of the high Sichuan-Tibetan borderland, its small white-to-yellow bulb the source of **Chuān Bèi Mǔ** — "Sichuan fritillary" — one of the most esteemed cough medicines in the Chinese pharmacopoeia and the premier "moistening" antitussive. It is distinguished from the cheaper lowland *Zhè Bèi Mǔ* (*F. thunbergii*): Chuān Bèi is the sweeter, more moistening, more expensive bulb, reserved for dry, chronic, and deficiency-type coughs, and it is the fragrant heart of the famous loquat-and-fritillary cough syrups.

**Chinese Tradition (Chuān Bèi Mǔ):**
Chuān Bèi Mǔ is sweet, slightly bitter, and cool, entering the Lung and Heart. Its two classical actions: it **clears heat and transforms phlegm** while **moistening the Lung** to stop cough — making it the specific remedy for dry coughs, coughs with scanty sticky phlegm, chronic Lung-heat and Lung-dryness coughs, and the cough of Yin deficiency (where warming, drying expectorants would worsen matters); and it **clears heat and dissipates nodules** (for phlegm-fire lumps, scrofula, and Lung abscess). Its sweet, moistening, gentle nature — unusual among phlegm herbs, most of which are warm and drying — is its defining virtue.

**Tibetan / Himalayan Tradition (FMT):**
Native to the Tibetan cultural region, the alpine fritillary is used in Tibetan and Himalayan practice as a cooling remedy for hot lung disorders and cough, consistent with its Chinese profile.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the moistening cool that stills the dry cough:**
Chuān Bèi Mǔ embodies a precise pharmacological principle the tradition articulated with great subtlety: that the dry, heat-parched, deficiency cough must be treated not by *drying* expectorants (which scatter phlegm but injure the fluids and worsen dryness) but by a *moistening, cooling* agent that resolves the sticky phlegm while restoring the Lung's fluids. It is the herb of **moist coolness** — the small white bulb from the snow-line, gathering the cold mountain's moistening quality to soothe the burning, dry chest. In the Codex's energetic scheme it sits at the Yin-moistening, phlegm-transforming node, and is filed to RESET/SLEEP as the gentle cool remedy for the dry, unproductive night-cough.

### Active Components

- **Isosteroidal alkaloids — verticine (peimine), verticinone (peiminine), imperialine, imperialine-3-β-D-glucoside, delavine, peimisine** — the characteristic and pharmacologically central constituents, responsible for the **antitussive, expectorant, anti-asthmatic and anti-inflammatory** actions. Several also show **antioxidant (Nrf2-activating) cytoprotection** against cigarette-smoke-induced oxidative stress in macrophages (Liu et al., 2019). These steroidal alkaloids are the marker compounds for quality control of Chuān Bèi.
- **Non-steroidal alkaloids and saponins** — supporting expectorant and anti-inflammatory constituents.
- **Polysaccharides and mucilage** — contribute the soothing, demulcent, moistening quality to the airway.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Primary domain: RESET/SLEEP — the dry, irritable, heat/deficiency cough. A gentle, moistening cool antitussive.**

1. **Dry/irritable cough and airway soothing:** the specific Kenetica role — the dry, hacking, unproductive cough (post-viral, exercise-airway-irritation, dry-environment) that drying expectorants would worsen. Its demulcent-plus-alkaloid action both soothes and gently resolves scanty sticky phlegm.
2. **Night-cough / sleep (SLEEP):** the classic use in soothing syrups for the dry night-cough that disrupts sleep and recovery.
3. **Lung-heat / oxidative airway load:** the Nrf2-antioxidant activity of its alkaloids is a mechanistically interesting fit for airway oxidative stress (e.g. smoke/pollution exposure), though this remains preclinical.

### Scientific Correlation

*Attribution: findings below are from an article retrieved from PubMed; DOI linked.*

The bulb's **isosteroidal alkaloids** are the documented basis of its actions. Liu et al. (2019, *Fitoterapia*) confirmed the traditional antitussive/expectorant/anti-asthma/anti-inflammatory reputation and showed, for the first time, that six isosteroid alkaloids from *F. cirrhosa* bulbus (verticine, verticinone, imperialine and its glucoside, delavine, peimisine) protect macrophages against **cigarette-smoke-induced oxidative stress** by reducing reactive oxygen species, raising glutathione, and activating the **Nrf2/HO-1 antioxidant pathway**, with a structure-activity relationship among the alkaloids ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fitote.2019.104434)).

*Evidence-grade note: the antitussive/expectorant use is long-established (pharmacopoeial) and the alkaloid pharmacology is well characterised; the antioxidant/Nrf2 findings are preclinical (cell-based). Whole-herb human RCTs of Chuān Bèi specifically are limited (it is generally used within compound syrups). Claims graded accordingly.*

### Contraindications

- **Wind-cold cough with copious watery/clear phlegm; cold-damp phlegm:** contraindicated — Chuān Bèi is for dry/heat/deficiency coughs; drying-warming coughs need warm herbs.
- **Aconite incompatibility (十八反):** classically **not** combined with aconite (Aconitum — Wū Tóu, Fù Zǐ, Cǎo Wū). A firm traditional prohibition.
- **Spleen deficiency with damp / poor digestion:** the sweet moistening nature can burden a damp-encumbered Spleen.
- **Species/authenticity + conservation:** wild alpine *Fritillaria cirrhosa* is over-harvested and expensive, and Chuān Bèi is frequently adulterated with cheaper *Fritillaria* species; identity and sustainable sourcing matter.
- **Evidence honesty:** antioxidant/Nrf2 activity is preclinical; core antitussive use is pharmacopoeial/traditional.
