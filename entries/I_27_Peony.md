### Botanical Identity
Perennial herb 60–100cm; large multi-petalled flowers (white in medicinal species); complex root system; native to northern China, Mongolia, Siberia. *Bai shao* (white peony root, bark removed) vs *chi shao* (red peony, bark intact) are classified as distinct drugs in TCM with opposite energetic valence. Both are roots of *P. lactiflora*; the processing determines the pharmacological character.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ)

*Bái Sháo* (White Peony):
> *"Bitter, sour; slightly cold. Enters Liver and Spleen channels. Nourishes Blood and Yin, astringes Yin, alleviates pain through softening the Liver, restrains sweating. Used for Blood deficiency patterns: pale complexion, dizziness, menstrual irregularity, uterine spasm, abdominal pain from Liver invading Spleen, spontaneous and night sweating."*

*Chì Sháo* (Red Peony):
> *"Bitter; slightly cold. Invigorates Blood, disperses stasis, relieves pain, clears Heat from the Blood. Used for Blood stasis patterns with pain, inflammatory conditions, and heat entering the Blood level."*

The bai/chi distinction is pharmacologically real — bark removal and vinegar processing (bai shao) removes paeonol and alters paeoniflorin ratios, shifting activity from anti-inflammatory/Blood-moving toward anti-spasmodic/Blood-tonifying.

### 2.2 De Materia Medica (DMM)

Dioscorides (*Book III, Chapter 140*):
> *"Paiōnía: root used for conditions of the liver, stomach, and uterus. It helps women with menstrual irregularity and uterine pain. The seeds and root taken in wine promote sleep and benefit the kidneys. Applied externally it reduces swellings."*

This is a remarkably precise convergence with the SBJ application — specifically the uterine and menstrual applications of the root. The "promotes sleep" observation may reflect anxiolytic/antispasmodic mechanisms.

### 2.3 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā:
> *"Fāwūniyā: temperament warm and dry. The root benefits the uterus, treats epilepsy, promotes urine, reduces liver inflammation, relieves headache. The seeds hung around the neck protect children from the falling sickness (epilepsy)."*

The epilepsy application across DMM and CM has a pharmacological basis: paeoniflorin's GABAergic activity and anticonvulsant properties.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

| Compound | Content | Activity |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Paeoniflorin** | 2–5% of root | Primary active; GABAergic; antispasmodic; anti-inflammatory (NF-κB); immunomodulatory; neuroprotective |

| **Benzoylpaeoniflorin** | Minor | Anti-inflammatory; Blood-activating |

**Paeoniflorin** is the pharmacological anchor — a monoterpene glucoside unique to peony genus with multi-target CNS and anti-inflammatory activity.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Antispasmodic / Uterine

**Paeoniflorin**: Smooth muscle relaxant via calcium channel antagonism → reduces uterine spasm → relieves dysmenorrhoea. This is the primary mechanism explaining the pan-traditional uterine application. Calcium entry blockade in smooth muscle is the same mechanism as verapamil for cardiac and vascular smooth muscle.

### 5.2 GABAergic / Anticonvulsant

**Paeoniflorin**: Positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptor → enhanced inhibitory neurotransmission → anticonvulsant, anxiolytic, sedative. This validates the classical epilepsy applications (CM, DMM) and the "promotes sleep" observation (DMM).

### 5.3 Anti-inflammatory / Immunomodulatory

**Paeoniflorin**: NF-κB and MAPK pathway inhibition → reduced TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 → anti-inflammatory. TLR modulation → regulatory T-cell induction → immunomodulatory (used in autoimmune conditions).

**Paeonol** (from tree peony bark, *mu dan pi*): COX-2 selective inhibition → prostaglandin reduction → anti-inflammatory + analgesic.

### 5.4 Hepatoprotective / Liver-Softening

Gallic acid + paeoniflorin → hepatoprotection via Nrf2/ARE → increased antioxidant enzyme activity. Anti-fibrotic: reduces hepatic stellate cell activation → prevents hepatic fibrosis. The TCM "softens the Liver" action maps to this anti-fibrotic mechanism.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Dysmenorrhoea**: Multiple RCTs in Chinese medicine tradition — peony-containing formulas (especially *Shao Yao Gan Cao Tang* — peony + licorice) significantly reduce dysmenorrhoea pain; NSAID-comparable effect in some trials.

**Autoimmune conditions**: Paeoniflorin immunomodulatory effect — clinical studies in rheumatoid arthritis show significant improvement in symptoms and inflammatory markers.

**Muscle cramps / spasm**: *Shao Yao Gan Cao Tang* (peony-licorice decoction) is among the most clinically replicated antispasmodic formulas in TCM — significant evidence for muscle cramp relief in multiple RCTs.

**Anxiety/neuroprotection**: Mechanistically well-founded; limited large-scale clinical trials.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**Named after a god's physician**: *Paeonia* is named after Paeon — the physician of the gods in Greek mythology who healed the wounds of Ares in the Iliad. The attribution of the genus to a divine healer reflects the plant's deep integration into classical Greek medicine before the Dioscorides era. This is one of the few plant genera named for a mythological figure rather than a botanical descriptor, signalling its exceptional pharmacological reputation.

**White vs red — processing as pharmacology**: The transformation of the same root from *chi shao* (red, Blood-moving, anti-inflammatory) to *bai shao* (white, Blood-tonifying, antispasmodic) through bark removal and vinegar processing is a classical example of pharmaceutical processing creating two distinct drugs from one source. The distinction is pharmacologically real — bark-on preparations have higher paeonol and more pro-inflammatory resolution (anti-stasis), bark-removed preparations have higher paeoniflorin in the antispasmodic/tonic direction.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| *Bai shao* decoction | 6–15g | Blood tonic, antispasmodic, Liver-Spleen |
| *Shao Yao Gan Cao Tang* | Peony 12g + Licorice 12g | Classic antispasmodic formula; cramps, dysmenorrhoea |
| *Si Wu Tang* | With Dang Gui + Rehmannia + Ligusticum | Blood tonic formula |
| *Chi shao* decoction | 6–12g | Blood-moving, anti-inflammatory, Blood-stasis pain |
| *Mu dan pi* | 6–12g | Heat in Blood, inflammatory/autoimmune |

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM (Bai Shao)**: Bitter, sour; slightly cold. Channels: Liver (肝), Spleen (脾). Actions: Nourish Yin and Blood, soften Liver, relieve pain, restrain sweating. Synergy: *bai shao* + *gan cao* (licorice) — supreme antispasmodic combination.

**Classical Western**: Warm and drying (DM/CM classification) — opposite to TCM "slightly cold" designation. This energetic difference reflects different assessment systems, but the clinical application (uterine, anticonvulsant, hepatic) is consistent.

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Damp-cold patterns / loose stools**: Cold nature of *bai shao* — avoid in cold-damp spleen deficiency
- **Anticoagulants**: Paeonol antiplatelet activity — caution with warfarin
- **Pregnancy**: *Chi shao* (Blood-moving form) — avoid; *bai shao* generally considered safer but use caution
- **Aconite combinations**: Classical warning against combining peony with aconite (*wu tou*) due to potential interaction
