# I-236 | Panax ginseng — Asian Ginseng / Korean Ginseng / Ren Shen

## Section 1 | Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Accepted name:** *Panax ginseng* C.A.Mey.  
**Family:** Araliaceae (same family as Eleutherococcus I-235, Schefflera)  
**Common names:** Asian Ginseng / Korean Ginseng / Chinese Ginseng (English), Ren Shen / 人参 (Chinese — "human root" — for humanoid root shape), Insam / 인삼 (Korean), Ninjin / 人参 (Japanese), Ginseng (international — from Korean/Chinese ginseng/renshen), Radix Ginseng (pharmacopoeial), Sang (Vietnamese), Jinseng (Mandarin pronunciation), Shen (abbreviated Chinese), Asian root; cultivated form: Yuan Shen (garden ginseng); wild form: Shanshen (mountain ginseng)

**Panax species complex — CRITICAL TAXONOMY:**
- *Panax ginseng* C.A.Mey. — Asian/Korean/Chinese ginseng (this entry): warming, stimulating; Korea, northeast China, Russia
- *Panax quinquefolius* L. — American Ginseng (I-237 next entry): cooling; ginsenoside profile distinct; Wisconsin, Ontario, Appalachia
- *Panax notoginseng* (Burkill) F.H.Chen — San Qi / Tienchi ginseng (I-xxx): hemostatic; Yunnan, China
- *Panax japonicus* C.A.Mey. — Japanese Ginseng: chikusetsu saponins; distinct from P. ginseng
- *Panax trifolius* L. — Dwarf ginseng (North America): minor medicinal use

**The Panax etymology:** Greek *panax* = "panacea" / "all-healing" — reflects the traditional pan-systemic therapeutic claim, parallel to and likely inspired by Western panacea concept reaching Asian trade routes. "All-healing" = the most ambitious pharmacological claim in the Codex, and arguably the most comprehensively studied herb in history.

**Botanical description:** Small deciduous perennial 30–60cm; palmately compound leaves with 5 leaflets (quinqueleaf number diagnostic); solitary terminal umbel of greenish-white flowers; red drupe berries. Root is the medicinal part: 2–10 years to maturity, deeply forked (anthropomorphic shape), 5–15cm length at harvest. Fresh = "white ginseng"; steamed/heated = "red ginseng" (increased Rg₃, Rh₂ content through processing). Wild mountain ginseng (Shanshen) is extraordinarily rare and valuable (100-year-old roots sell for tens of thousands of dollars); commercial cultivation is 4–6 year cycle.

**Distribution:** Native to montane forests of northeastern China (Manchuria), Korean peninsula, Russian Far East (Primorsky). Wild populations critically endangered from over-harvesting over 2,000+ years. Commercial cultivation: South Korea (primary global producer — 75%+ of market), China, Japan, Russia, Canada (Ontario). South Korean Jilin and Gyeonggi provinces maintain highest quality cultivation standards.

**Authenticated part(s):** Root — primary; rhizome; occasionally leaves and berries (contain different ginsenoside profiles). Processing determines product type:
- *White ginseng:* peeled, sun-dried root; ginsenoside profile preserved
- *Red ginseng:* unpeeled, steamed then dried root; heat creates new ginsenosides (Rg₃, compound K, Rh₂) and concentrates others
- *Korean Red Ginseng (Goryeo):* 6-year root, specific steam-dry processing protocol; most RCT-validated preparation

**Standardization:** Ginsenoside content by HPLC: Korean Pharmacopoeia requires minimum 0.5% total ginsenosides; European Pharmacopoeia ≥4% total ginsenosides (Rg₁ + Rb₁). Korean Red Ginseng standardized to ≥0.4% Rg₃ in addition.

## Section 3 | Convergence Analysis

**Pan-Asian master tonic convergence:** CM, SBJ, Korean Hanbang, and FMT all independently arrive at ginseng as the supreme tonic for vital energy deficiency. The consistency across these four traditions, spanning 2,000+ years and multiple distinct medical systems, represents the deepest single-tradition convergence in Asia for any substance. The CM statement in the *Shennong Bencao Jing* — "great tonic of the five viscera" — anticipates by two millennia the modern finding that ginsenosides modulate multiple organ systems simultaneously.

**The panacea claim and its pharmacological basis:** "Panax" (all-healing) is the boldest traditional claim in the Codex. Modern pharmacology has not validated ginseng as literally healing everything, but has identified an unusually broad multi-system mechanism: HPA axis modulation (adaptogenic), nitric oxide pathway modulation (cardiovascular), insulin signaling modulation (metabolic), immune modulation, neurotrophic factor upregulation (cognitive), and anti-tumor properties. The breadth, if not the universality, of ginseng's pharmacological activity is consistent with the "all-healing" traditional designation.

**The warming-cooling distinction (P. ginseng vs P. quinquefolius):** CM tradition sharply distinguishes Asian ginseng (warm/yang-tonifying) from American ginseng (cool/yin-tonifying). Modern pharmacology supports this energetic distinction: *P. ginseng* is richer in stimulatory ginsenoside Rg₁ (CNS-stimulating, warming); *P. quinquefolius* is richer in calming ginsenoside Rb₁ (CNS-sedating, cooling). The traditional energetic distinction precisely maps onto the differential ginsenoside profiles — a pharmacological convergence discovered empirically and confirmed molecularly.

**Wild vs. cultivated pharmacological distinction:** Classical CM texts carefully distinguish wild mountain ginseng (Shanshen) from garden-cultivated ginseng. Modern analysis shows wild ginseng has significantly higher total ginsenoside content, more diverse ginsenoside profile, and higher malonyl-ginsenoside content than commercial cultivated root. The traditional quality hierarchy (wild > long-cultivated > short-cultivated) is pharmacologically justified by phytochemical analysis.

## Section 4 | Phytochemical Composition

**Ginsenosides (saponins) — primary bioactives (primary standardization markers):**
Classified into three structural groups:
1. **Protopanaxadiol (PPD) group** (sedating/anti-proliferative tendency):
   - **Rb₁**: highest abundance (~25% of total ginsenosides); anti-anxiety; sedating; memory; phytoestrogenic; anti-tumor; intestinal bacteria convert → compound K
   - **Rb₂**: anti-diabetes; anti-obesity; anti-tumor
   - **Rc**: immune modulating; anti-inflammatory
   - **Rd**: neuroprotective; anti-inflammatory; anti-tumor
   - **Rg₃**: formed mainly in red ginseng processing; anti-tumor; anti-angiogenic; immunomodulatory; the most-studied anti-cancer ginsenoside
   - **Rh₂**: formed in red ginseng; anti-tumor; apoptosis induction; cancer cell differentiation
   - **Compound K** (20-O-β-(D-glucopyranosyl)-20(S)-protopanaxadiol): metabolite produced by gut bacteria from Rb₁; highly bioavailable; anti-tumor; anti-diabetic; anti-inflammatory; may be the "true" bioactive form of PPD ginsenosides after metabolism

2. **Protopanaxatriol (PPT) group** (stimulating/pro-proliferative tendency):
   - **Rg₁**: second most abundant; CNS-stimulating; memory; anti-fatigue; immune modulating; phytoestrogenic; increases dopamine/norepinephrine
   - **Re**: anti-diabetic; anti-inflammatory; antioxidant
   - **Rg₂, Rh₁**: memory; neuroprotective; anti-fatigue

3. **Oleanolic acid group:**
   - **Ro**: anti-hepatotoxic; anti-tumor; anti-inflammatory; distinct from panaxadiol/triol

**Total ginsenoside content:** White ginseng: 4–7%; Red ginseng: 4–8% (with unique Rg₃/Rh₂ from processing); Wild ginseng: 7–12%

**Polysaccharides:**
- Panaxans (A–U): hypoglycemic; immunomodulatory; primary water-soluble fraction
- Ginsenan (PA, PB): hepatoprotective

**Polyacetylenes:**
- Panaxynol, panaxytriol: anti-tumor; antibacterial; anti-inflammatory

**Phenolic compounds:**
- Maltol: antioxidant; neuroprotective; iron chelator
- Ginsenoside phenols: caffeic acid, vanillic acid, p-coumaric acid

**Essential oil:** β-Elemene, β-farnesene, panasinsene (sesquiterpenes); low concentration but contributes to characteristic ginseng odor

## Section 5 | Mechanisms of Action

**1. Adaptogenic / HPA Axis Mechanism**
Ginsenosides (primarily Rg₁ and Rb₁ acting antagonistically on HPA):
- **Rg₁:** Stimulates CRH/ACTH release at low stress levels → prepares system for stress response
- **Rb₁:** Suppresses excessive cortisol at high stress levels → prevents cortisol dysregulation
- Net: biphasic HPA modulation — increases capacity to respond to stress while preventing pathological cortisol excess. This bidirectional action is the molecular basis for the "adaptogenic" property: neither pure stimulant nor pure sedative but a regulatory compound

**2. Nitric Oxide (NO) Pathway — Cardiovascular**
- Ginsenoside Rg₁/Re: eNOS upregulation → increased NO production → vasodilation, improved endothelial function
- Reduces ET-1 (endothelin-1) → vasoconstriction reduction
- Anti-platelet: reduces thromboxane A₂ / increases prostacyclin → anti-thrombotic
- Cardioprotective: reduces ischemia-reperfusion injury
- This NO mechanism validates CM "tonifies blood" documentation for cardiovascular benefit

**3. Insulin Signaling / Metabolic Mechanism**
- Compound K (Rb₁ gut metabolite): GLUT4 translocation → improved insulin-independent glucose uptake
- Ginsenosides Rb₁, Re: PPARγ modulation → insulin sensitization; adipogenesis modulation
- Panaxans: direct hypoglycemic effect (independent of insulin pathway)
- GLP-1 secretagogue activity (some ginsenosides): enhances insulin secretion
- Combined: multi-mechanism antidiabetic action

**4. Neurotrophic / Cognitive Mechanism**
- Ginsenoside Rg₁: NGF (nerve growth factor) and BDNF upregulation
- Ginsenoside Rb₁: GABAergic modulation (anxiolytic component); protects against excitotoxicity
- Compound K: anti-neuroinflammatory; reduces Aβ aggregation (Alzheimer's relevance)
- AChE inhibition (Rg₁ primary): enhanced cholinergic tone → improved memory, attention
- Dopamine/norepinephrine modulation: improved executive function, working memory

**5. Immunomodulatory Mechanism**
- Polysaccharides (panaxans): macrophage activation, NK cell enhancement, B-cell proliferation
- Ginsenoside Rg₁: enhances Th1 response (IFN-γ, IL-2)
- Ginsenoside Rb₁: enhances Th2 response (IL-4, IL-10) — together: balanced Th1/Th2
- Dendritic cell maturation enhancement

**6. Anti-tumor Mechanism**
- Ginsenoside Rg₃ (red ginseng-specific): anti-angiogenic (VEGF suppression); apoptosis induction (Bcl-2/Bax); cancer cell invasion inhibition (MMP suppression)
- Ginsenoside Rh₂: differentiation induction in cancer cells; most potent ginsenoside for cancer
- Compound K: cancer cell apoptosis; autophagy induction; cancer stem cell inhibition
- Polyacetylenes (panaxynol): direct anti-proliferative
- Anti-tumor immunity enhancement (ginsenoside-mediated NK + CTL activation)

**7. Phytoestrogenic Mechanism (dual Rg₁/Rb₁)**
Both ginsenosides bind ERα and ERβ as partial agonists — SERMs:
- Explains CM use for menopausal symptoms
- CRITICAL: same concern as other phytoestrogens — hormone-receptor-positive cancer patients AVOID

## Section 6 | Clinical Evidence

**Fatigue / Quality of Life (Level A)**
*Barton et al. (2013) — RCT:* N=364 cancer patients with fatigue; American + Asian ginseng (Wisconsin) 2g/day × 8 weeks. MFSI fatigue score significantly improved vs. placebo (p=0.003). Well-powered multicenter RCT.

*Arring et al. (2018) — Systematic review:* 10 RCTs examining ginseng for fatigue; consistent evidence for fatigue reduction in both cancer-related and idiopathic chronic fatigue.

**Type 2 Diabetes / Blood Sugar (Level A)**
*Vuksan et al. (2000, 2001) — landmark RCTs:* N=12 T2DM patients; American ginseng (different from Asian ginseng but comparable ginsenoside content) 6g pre-meal vs. placebo. Fasting blood glucose reduced -10%; post-prandial glucose significantly reduced. Time-dependent effect (40 min pre-meal optimal). Multiple replications.

*Shishtar et al. (2014) — Meta-analysis:* 16 RCTs (N=770 participants); ginseng supplementation (various Panax species); fasting glucose significantly reduced (WMD: -0.31 mmol/L, p=0.001); HOMA-IR significantly improved. Robust meta-analytic evidence.

**Cognitive Function (Level A)**
*Kennedy et al. (2001, 2004) — RCTs:* Korean Red Ginseng (KRG) and *P. ginseng* extract; serial subtraction, word recall, spatial memory; significant improvements vs. placebo in single-dose and chronic-dosing studies.

*Oh et al. (2011) — systematic review:* 9 RCTs on cognition; 8/9 showed significant improvement with ginseng; quality varied but overall positive evidence.

**Erectile Dysfunction (Level A)**
*Jang et al. (2008) — Meta-analysis:* 7 RCTs (N=349); Korean Red Ginseng vs. placebo for erectile dysfunction. IIEF (International Index of Erectile Function) significantly improved (p=0.0007). Effect size moderate but consistent. NO pathway mechanism (eNOS) directly explains erectile function improvement.

**Immune Function / Influenza (Level B)**
*McElhaney et al. (2004, 2006) — RCTs:* Cold-FX (North American ginseng extract) vs. placebo in elderly nursing home residents. Influenza/respiratory illness incidence significantly reduced; antibody titers to influenza vaccine significantly higher in ginseng group.

**Cancer Adjunct (Level B)**
*Multiple Korean and Chinese RCTs:* Korean Red Ginseng as adjunct to chemotherapy in gastric, colorectal, lung cancers: improved NK cell activity, reduced fatigue, improved quality of life. Tumor response rates in some small trials suggest additive anti-tumor effect.

*Yun & Choi (1995, 1998) — Case-control:* Ginseng consumption associated with significantly reduced cancer incidence in Korean population (OR 0.44 for 5+ years use vs. non-users). Epidemiological evidence only; confounded.

**Hypertension (Level B)**
Inconsistent evidence: some RCTs show BP reduction (Rg₁ eNOS mechanism); others show no significant effect. Population baseline BP, ginsenoside profile, and dose all affect outcome. Not recommended as primary antihypertensive.

**Menopausal Symptoms (Level B)**
*Kim et al. (2012) — RCT:* N=72 menopausal women; KRG 3g/day × 12 weeks. Hot flash frequency, insomnia, fatigue: all significantly reduced. Phytoestrogenic mechanism.

## Section 7 | Phenomenological Record

**The "human root" and morphological pharmacology:** The Chinese name *Ren Shen* (人参 — human root) reflects the genuine morphological resemblance of the mature ginseng root to a human body — arms, legs, trunk — a resonance so striking that it likely contributed to the pan-systemic tonic belief ("a root shaped like a human body heals the human body"). This doctrine of signatures logic, while not pharmacologically valid as a principle, generated the sustained investigation that ultimately revealed genuinely pan-systemic ginsenoside pharmacology.

**2,000+ years of continuous clinical documentation:** No other plant in the Codex has a continuous, uninterrupted written medical history of comparable length and depth. From the Shennong Bencao Jing (ca. 100 CE) through the Bencao Gangmu (1596) through the Modern Chinese Pharmacopoeia (2020), ginseng occupies 40+ generations of consistent clinical attention in East Asia. This accumulation of clinical observation — representing arguably the largest body of pre-scientific human pharmacological data for any single plant — is a resource that modern medicine has only begun to properly engage.

**The wild ginseng economy:** A 100-year-old wild mountain ginseng root (Shanshen) sells for $50,000–$100,000+ at Korean/Chinese auction houses. The price reflects scarcity (millennia of harvest have virtually eliminated wild populations), pharmacological reputation (the most valuable medicine in the Asian tradition), and cultural significance (gift for emperors, longevity symbol). The extraordinary price is also a data point: East Asian cultures, with 2,000+ years of clinical experience, placed ginseng's value at the apex of their pharmacopoeias. This collective judgment, expressed in economic terms across centuries, is itself a form of evidence.

**The Korean national identity:** South Korea's global reputation as the world's highest-quality ginseng producer is embedded in national identity. The Goryeo dynasty gave ginseng its English name through trade networks, and Korea invested centuries in cultivation refinement (4-year minimum cultivation, specific soil preparation, shade cultivation with custom wooden frames). This national investment in ginseng quality is a unique case of a country's economic and cultural identity being tied to a specific medicinal plant — generating quality control practices that have produced the most consistently reliable ginseng in global commerce.

**Red vs. white ginseng transformation:** The steaming process that converts white to red ginseng was discovered empirically — likely as a preservation technique — and produces new ginsenosides (Rg₃, Rh₂) not present in white ginseng. The discovery that thermal processing could enhance specific anti-tumor ginsenosides (Rg₃) while creating compound K precursors represents an ancient empirical pharmaceutical technology that modern chemistry has only recently decoded. Traditional red ginseng processing is a 500-year-old biotransformation protocol that anticipates modern pharmaceutical glycoside processing techniques.

## Section 8 | Practical Application

**Standardized preparations:**
- **Korean Red Ginseng (KRG) extract (standardized ≥4% total ginsenosides):** 1–3g/day; most RCT-validated preparation; best anti-tumor ginsenoside profile (Rg₃)
- **White ginseng root extract (standardized ≥5% ginsenosides):** 500mg–2g/day; milder stimulatory profile
- **Raw dried root (Radix Ginseng):** 1–5g/day as tea/decoction (boil 30 min); traditional Chinese/Korean preparation; full-spectrum including polyacetylenes
- **High-potency standardized extract (≥7% ginsenosides):** 300–500mg 2×/day for specific clinical indications (ED, diabetes, immune)
- **Liquid extract (tincture 1:5, 60% ethanol):** 5–10 mL/day; traditional Korean/European preparation

**Timing protocols:**
- Morning or early afternoon dosing (stimulatory; may disrupt sleep if taken late)
- With or after meals (reduces GI irritation)
- Cycling: 4–8 weeks on → 1–2 week break → resume (traditional CM and Korean guidance; reduces tachyphylaxis)

**Clinical protocols:**
- *Fatigue/burnout:* KRG 2–3g/day × 8 weeks; monitor fatigue scales; combine with lifestyle interventions
- *Type 2 diabetes (adjunct):* 2–3g/day; take 40 min before meals (Vuksan protocol); monitor HbA1c/fasting glucose; may allow metformin dose reduction under medical supervision
- *Erectile dysfunction:* 900mg–1g KRG 3×/day × 8–12 weeks; IIEF assessment; works via NO mechanism, not hormone
- *Cognitive enhancement:* 200–400mg extract × 8 weeks; single-dose effect also documented (200–400mg 30 min before cognitive demand)
- *Cancer adjunct (under oncologist supervision):* KRG as adjunct to chemotherapy; typical protocol: 3g/day throughout chemotherapy cycles; NK cell monitoring

**Drug interactions — IMPORTANT:**
- **Warfarin:** Ginsenoside anti-platelet activity + mild CYP3A4 modulation; INR monitoring; case reports of reduced warfarin effect and of enhanced bleeding — both reported, direction inconsistent; monitor closely
- **MAO inhibitors (phenelzine etc.):** Case reports of headache, tremulousness, mania; AVOID combination; mechanism: Rg₁ dopamine/norepinephrine effects
- **Antidiabetic medications (insulin, sulfonylureas):** Additive glucose-lowering → hypoglycemia risk; monitor blood glucose closely
- **Stimulants (caffeine, methylphenidate):** Additive CNS stimulation; insomnia/anxiety risk at high ginseng doses
- **Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus):** Immunostimulatory activity may reduce immunosuppressive efficacy; AVOID in transplant patients
- **Digoxin:** Case reports of assay interference (similar to Eleutherococcus I-235); notify prescriber

**Contraindications:**
- **Pregnancy:** Ginsenoside phytoestrogenic activity + uterotonic potential at high doses; AVOID pharmacological doses; culinary amounts in Korean cuisine (ginseng chicken soup) likely safe in moderation
- **Hormone-receptor-positive breast/uterine/ovarian cancer:** Phytoestrogenic mechanism; AVOID without oncologist guidance
- **Acute infection with fever:** Traditional CM caution — warming herb may exacerbate hot/febrile conditions; use adaptogens outside acute illness
- **Hypomania/mania (bipolar disorder):** Stimulatory ginsenosides may trigger manic episode; AVOID without psychiatric supervision
- **Autoimmune conditions:** Immunostimulatory activity; caution; may exacerbate some autoimmune conditions (distinguish: most autoimmune benefit from immunomodulation, not raw immunostimulation)

**Safety:** Generally excellent at standard doses. Most common adverse effects: insomnia, palpitations, headache, GI upset — dose-dependent and transient. "Ginseng abuse syndrome" (high dose continuous use): hypertension, insomnia, nervousness — Korean/Chinese traditional warning documented and validated clinically. Avoid doses >3g/day continuously. Rare: gynecomastia (phytoestrogenic at high dose), vaginal bleeding in postmenopausal women (same mechanism).

**Energetic tier:** ◉ Qi (master qi-tonifying herb — the definitive Qi tonic in CM tradition) primary; ◈ Jing (constitutional strengthening, longevity, essence tonification — long-term root tonic) secondary; ☿ Shen (calms spirit, opens heart-orifice, benefits intelligence — CM documentation; cognitive effects pharmacologically validated) tertiary.

**Quality hierarchy by type:**
1. Wild mountain ginseng (Shanshen) — highest potency, highest price, extremely rare
2. Korean Red Ginseng (6-year cultivated, specific processing) — best clinical evidence
3. White ginseng root (4–6 year cultivated) — standard medicinal quality
4. Ginseng powder/capsules (undefined origin) — lowest quality assurance; most common commercial product

**Cross-references:** I-237 (American Ginseng — *P. quinquefolius* — cooling yin-tonifying complement), I-235 (Eleutherococcus — Araliaceae family adaptogen; "Siberian Ginseng" naming confusion clarified), I-220 (Ashwagandha — Ayurvedic functional analog; western/Ayurvedic adaptogen comparison), I-233 (Rhodiola — adaptogen pair; Rhodiola + Ginseng documented synergy)
