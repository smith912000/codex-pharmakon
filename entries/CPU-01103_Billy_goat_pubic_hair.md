# Billy-goat pubic hair — ra.thug.rmongs.spus, "Pubic hair of billy-goat" (Clark 1995) / "The pubic hair of billy goat" (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011)
## *Pubic hair of the male domestic goat; the translations print no binomial for it, and the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary lists "Billy Goat" without one* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

This entry is one line of one text, in two translations. The goat as an animal (flesh, milk, blood, brain, and this hair among them) is in `III_35_Goat.md`, which already prints the hair line; it is given here as its own material and not repeated at length.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The locus is the Explanatory Tantra, chapter 20, in Clark's 1995 translation (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, line 1195). Section "(8) Medicines of Animal Origin" opens (line 1148): "The thirteen medicines of animal origin consist of horn, bone, meat, blood, bile, fat, brain, skin, nails, hair, urine, droppings and whole body." The hair is the tenth of the thirteen, and the section under the heading "Hair" runs through peacock, heron, owl, fruit-bat and burrhel-sheep hair or feathers. It ends: "ra.thug.rmongs.spus: Pubic hair of billy-goat cures infectious tissue degeneration." Clark prints the Tibetan name before the English, as he does for other animals in the chapter. The same name appears in his index of Tibetan names (line 1754). The spine's source row is `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv`.

The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text of the Root and Explanatory Tantras (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, lines 3559-3560) prints the same item under the hair and feather section: "The pubic hair of billy goat cures inflammation of muscle tissues." The two translations render the disease differently ("infectious tissue degeneration" and "inflammation of muscle tissues"). I did not check the Tibetan, so which rendering is closer is unknown. [USER: verify]

Neither text gives a method, a dose, the age or colour of the goat, or how the hair is to be taken or applied. The glossary of animals at the end of the Men-Tsee-Khang volume lists "Billy Goat" (line 7102) with no binomial printed beside it; the OCR layout of that glossary does not show which of the neighbouring binomials, if any, belongs to it ("Goat / Capra hircus Linnaeus" stands at lines 7094-7096). The identity of the material is therefore the plain English: hair from the pubic region of a male goat.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, hair is keratin, a structural protein rich in sulphur-containing amino acids, with small amounts of lipid and water. Nothing about hair from this body site is known to be pharmacologically active in the sense the Tantra claims.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources record one action for this material in a list of animal parts, and no outcome. There is no protocol here, and the Codex invents none. Hair from a goat's body is an unhygienic and unstandardised material, and the tissue-degeneration or inflammation it is said to cure needs ordinary medical care.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources give no preparation, no form (burnt, powdered, or applied whole) and no amount. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Hair is keratin and is not a source of any known medicine; it can carry skin bacteria, fungi and mites from the animal. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT):** none printed. Clark and Men-Tsee-Khang give an action only ("cures infectious tissue degeneration"; "cures inflammation of muscle tissues"). Neither prints a taste, a post-digestive taste or a potency for the hair, and the passages read do not assign it to one of the three *nyes pa* (*rlung*, *mkhris pa*, *bad kan*).

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed.* The hair sits inside the Explanatory Tantra's section on medicines of animal origin, one of the thirteen kinds Clark lists (line 1148), as a drug with a named action. No passage read assigns it a cultivation status, and no *bcud len* (essence-extraction) use or subtle-body role is given for it.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes this a substance for cultivation. It is a single line in a catalogue of animal parts, with an action and no method. A cultivator takes nothing from this entry. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** hair from the genital region of an animal can carry bacteria, fungi and parasites; it should not be put on broken skin or swallowed.
- **Medical care:** the conditions named (tissue degeneration, muscle inflammation) can be serious and need proper diagnosis.
- **Ethics and law:** the plain goat is a domestic animal; no conservation issue arises from the hair, but the Codex recommends no animal-part therapy from this line alone.
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** nothing in the sources.
