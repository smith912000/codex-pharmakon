# Himalayan Mayapple — Indian Podophyllum · Bankakri · Ban Kakri · Papra · Giant Mayapple

## *Podophyllum hexandrum* Royle (syn. *Sinopodophyllum hexandrum* (Royle) T.S. Ying) | Family: Berberidaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SBJ, SS
**Energetic Classification:** Hot and Dry in the third degree (Unani); intensely purgative, downward-draining
**Training Phase:** Advanced — Purgation & Excision (restricted; supervised use only)

---

> ⚠️ **SEVERE HAZARD ENTRY.** *Podophyllum hexandrum* rhizome yields podophyllotoxin, a potent antimitotic cytotoxin. This plant is a documented cause of severe systemic poisoning. It is included here for scholarly completeness and because its lignans seeded a genuine class of modern chemotherapy drugs — **not** as a home remedy. Read the Contraindications section before anything else.

### Origin & Tradition

The Himalayan mayapple is a low, umbrella-leaved perennial of the alpine and sub-alpine Himalaya, from Afghanistan and Kashmir across to south-west China, growing at 2,400–4,500 m. Its stout underground rhizome, harvested in autumn, is the medicinal part. Across the traditions that touched the mountain trade routes, the same paradox recurs: a drug prized for its violent cathartic power and feared for its toxicity.

**Ayurveda & the Himalayan folk pharmacopoeia (SS / AT):** Known in Sanskrit-derived and Hindi vernaculars as *Bankakri*, *Papra* or *Giri-parpat*, the rhizome (and the resinous extract "Indian podophyllum resin") was employed as a drastic purgative, an emmenagogue, and a caustic application to warts, condylomata, and indolent skin growths. Folk practitioners of Kashmir and Himachal used minute doses for chronic constipation and to expel intestinal worms, and topical pastes for skin tumours and "morbid growths." The plant sits at the outer, most dangerous edge of the materia medica — a *tikshna* (sharp, penetrating) drug reserved for conditions where gentler agents had failed.

**Unani / the Avicennan inheritance (CM):** The Podophyllum group entered the Graeco-Arabic stream through the broader category of drastic resinous purgatives comparable to scammony and colocynth — agents classed as hot and dry, acting by forceful downward evacuation of "morbid humours." The Himalayan species, native to the eastern reach of the Persianate medical world, was folded into this logic: a resin to be corrected, diminished, and combined with milder drugs to blunt its griping.

**Chinese medicine (TCM):** The closely allied *Dysosma* / *Sinopodophyllum* rhizomes appear in Chinese and Tibetan mountain practice ("Gui Jiu," 鬼臼, "demon's mortar") as a toxic, dispersing agent used externally for snakebite, swellings, and stubborn skin lesions, and internally — with great caution — as an anti-parasitic and tumour-dispersing drug. The name itself signals its dangerous reputation.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Every tradition that used this rhizome coded it as a *downward, expelling, cutting* force — and modern pharmacology explains why the symbolism was accurate. Podophyllotoxin arrests cell division by binding tubulin and freezing the mitotic spindle; it literally halts proliferating tissue. The traditional "excises morbid growths" is a phenomenological description of an antimitotic. The symbol and the mechanism converge: this is medicine as controlled destruction.

### Active Components

- **Podophyllotoxin** — the principal aryltetralin lignan; a microtubule-assembly inhibitor and the pharmacological heart of the plant. *P. hexandrum* rhizome is the richest natural source, with reported contents around 2.5% in high-yielding accessions.
- **Picropodophyllotoxin, deoxypodophyllotoxin, podophyllotoxone** and related lignans.
- **4′-demethylpodophyllotoxin** — the semi-synthetic gateway to **etoposide** and **teniposide**.
- **Flavonoids** — quercetin, kaempferol; and podophyllotoxin glucosides.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Within Kenetica, *Podophyllum hexandrum* is filed as a **restricted excision agent**, not a general tonic or purgative. Its legitimate contemporary relevance is threefold: (1) as the botanical origin of the etoposide/teniposide chemotherapy lineage; (2) as the source of purified podophyllotoxin used topically, under prescription, for anogenital warts (podofilox/podophyllotoxin 0.5% preparations); and (3) as a teaching specimen for the doctrine that the most powerful drugs are the most dangerous. Kenetica does **not** endorse any unsupervised internal use.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, *P. hexandrum* is well documented as the premier natural source of podophyllotoxin and its antitumour lignans. Giri & Lakshmi Narasu review the plant as an endangered Berberidaceae whose rhizome lignans possess antitumour activity, with podophyllotoxin acting as an inhibitor of microtubule assembly and serving as the starting compound for the anticancer drugs **etoposide and teniposide** (used against lung cancer, testicular cancer, neuroblastoma and hepatoma), while noting antiviral activity and the collection pressure threatening wild populations ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008138230896), PMID 19003377). Kumar et al. quantified podophyllotoxin at up to ~2.51% in high-yielding rhizome accessions in a comparative transcriptome study of the biosynthetic pathway ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00709-015-0938-7), PMID 26733390). Kumari et al. further document the rhizome as the principal PTOX-accumulating tissue and confirm the plant's status as a cultivated medicinal source under conservation pressure ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11274-024-04245-y), PMID 39815146).

**Evidence grade:** The *mechanism* (tubulin-binding antimitotic) and the *derivative drugs* (etoposide, teniposide, topical podophyllotoxin for warts) are firmly evidence-based and clinically established. The *traditional crude uses* (drastic purgation, folk tumour pastes) are historically attested but **not** supported as safe practice; the therapeutic window of the crude drug is dangerously narrow. Graded: **strong for derivatives / traditional for crude use, with a heavy safety caveat.**

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Unani/Galenic:** Hot and dry, drastic evacuant of the "downward" type; requires correction (*islah*) with mucilaginous or cooling adjuvants.
- **Ayurveda:** *Rasa* — bitter/pungent (tikta/katu); *Guna* — sharp, penetrating (tikshna); *Virya* — heating (ushna); *Vipaka* — pungent (katu); action *adhobhagahara* (downward-expelling). A drug of *visha* (toxin) class, requiring purification (*shodhana*) before any use.
- **Chinese/Tibetan:** Acrid, bitter, warm, **toxic**; disperses stasis and masses, expels parasites — used mainly externally.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The rhizome enters the Graeco-Arabic stream not on its own account but as one of the drastic resinous evacuants — the company of scammony (*saqmūniyā*) and colocynth (*ḥanẓal*) — classed hot and dry and acting by forceful downward expulsion of morbid humours. Such a drug is precisely what the tradition insists must be *corrected* (*iṣlāḥ*): diminished in dose, blunted with mucilage or cooling adjuvants, given only against a defined excess. The strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus is the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture — a finite vital endowment that ageing depletes — and a violent evacuant is the antithesis of that conservational project, not an instrument of it. No *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed, and the identification of this particular Himalayan species within the classical resin category is a later folding-in rather than a *Canon*-era assignment. The plant is attested therapeutically; it carries no cultivation status. **(b) framed.**

**Chinese — Bencao (SBJ).** In Chinese mountain practice the allied rhizome is *Gui Jiu* 鬼臼, "demon's mortar" — a toxic, acrid, dispersing agent worked mainly externally against snakebite, swellings and stubborn masses, and internally only with extreme caution. The name broadcasts its dangerous, lower reputation, and the classical *Gui Jiu* of the *Bencao* stratum is conventionally the eastern-Chinese *Dysosma*/*Sinopodophyllum* group rather than the Himalayan *Podophyllum hexandrum* of this entry; the sources retrieved record no *san pin* 三品 grade that can be securely attached to *this* species. It sits inside *yang sheng* 養生 — conservational, nourishing life — only as its opposite: a *treats-disease* toxin, never a substance taken long-term to lighten the body (*qing shen* 輕身). Because the grade cannot be sourced for the species named here, no cultivation status is asserted. **(b) framed**, with a species-identity caveat.

**Ayurveda (SS).** In the Himalayan folk pharmacopoeia the rhizome is *Bankakri* or *Papra*, a *tikshna* (sharp, penetrating) and frankly *visha* (toxin-class) drug of the *adhobhagahara* (downward-expelling) action — reserved for the outermost, most dangerous edge of practice where gentler agents have failed. Its defining Ayurvedic requirement is *shodhana*, the purification a toxin must undergo before any use; that is a discipline of detoxification, not a rejuvenative status. No *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) assignment is recorded for it in the sources retrieved, and its attestation is folk-Himalayan rather than a classical *Samhita* placement. A depleting purgative-caustic is the categorical opposite of an *ojas*-building drug. **(b) framed.**

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing tonic with this plant, and every tradition on the tag line says so in its own voice: a corrected drastic in Unani, a lower "demon's mortar" toxin in the Chinese scheme, a *visha* requiring *shodhana* in Ayurveda. There is no longevity, no life-nourishing, no generative use here — only controlled destruction of proliferating or morbid tissue, which is exactly why its lignans seeded modern chemotherapy rather than any elixir. The honest cultivation instruction is abstention: this is a supervised excision agent and a poison monograph, not a substance one takes to build vitality.

### Contraindications

⚠️ **PODOPHYLLOTOXIN IS A POTENT ANTIMITOTIC CYTOTOXIN. TREAT THIS ENTRY AS A POISON MONOGRAPH.**

- **ABSOLUTE PREGNANCY CONTRAINDICATION.** Podophyllotoxin is **teratogenic and embryotoxic**. Never use in pregnancy, in women who may become pregnant without contraception, or during breastfeeding. Fetal death and malformation are documented risks of systemic exposure.
- **Systemic toxicity is severe and can be fatal.** Reported poisoning from crude rhizome or over-applied resin includes: profuse vomiting and bloody diarrhoea, abdominal pain; **peripheral and central neurotoxicity** (sensorimotor neuropathy, confusion, seizures, coma); **bone-marrow suppression** (pancytopenia); hepatic and renal injury; and cardiovascular collapse. Onset may be delayed by hours.
- **Topical crude resin is strongly caustic** and absorbs systemically — historical deaths followed application to large or mucosal surfaces. Never apply to bleeding, friable, mucosal, or extensive skin areas; never occlude; keep strictly to the small treated lesion and within labelled limits for purified products.
- **Do not self-administer internally under any circumstances.** There is no safe home dose of the crude drug.
- **Narrow therapeutic index** — even purified podophyllotoxin products carry area/volume ceilings; exceeding them causes systemic poisoning.
- **Drug interactions / vulnerable groups:** avoid with other myelosuppressive or neurotoxic agents; extreme caution in hepatic or renal impairment, in children, and in the debilitated.
- **Overdose is a medical emergency** — seek immediate poison-control/hospital care; management is supportive (no specific antidote).

**Conservation note:** *P. hexandrum* is **endangered** and listed under **CITES Appendix II**; wild rhizome harvest is heavily restricted. Sustainable/cultivated sourcing and semi-synthetic routes are ethically and legally preferred.
