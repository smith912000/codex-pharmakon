# Dhava — Dhava, Dhurandhara, Axlewood, Indian Gum Tree, Gum Ghatti tree

## *Anogeissus latifolia* (Roxb. ex DC.) Wall. ex Guillem. & Perr. | Family: Combretaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā), CS (Caraka Saṃhitā), AH (Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya), Nighaṇṭus (Bhāvaprakāśa, Dhanvantari)
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa — Kaṣāya (astringent), Tikta (bitter) · Vīrya — Śīta (cooling) · Vipāka — Kaṭu (pungent) · Guṇa — Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry) · Kapha–Pitta-hara; a **Sthāpana/Grāhī** astringent, **Kuṣṭhaghna**, **Vraṇaśodhana–Ropaṇa** (wound-cleansing and healing), and companion anti-prameha heartwood.
**Training Phase:** Tissue-firming / consolidation (astringent) · Wound & mucosal repair · Clearing / Purification

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** Dhava is a named member of Suśruta's **Sālasārādi gaṇa** — the heartwood-and-bark group for *prameha*, *kuṣṭha*, *medoroga*, and *kṛmi* — where it stands alongside Asana (*Pterocarpus marsupium*) as one of the astringent, drying, Kapha-clearing woods. It also belongs conceptually to the great astringent–haemostatic cluster the surgical tradition relied on for **vraṇa** (wound) management: bark decoctions to wash and contract wounds, pastes to arrest bleeding and check discharge. Suśruta the surgeon valued exactly this — a tannin-dense bark that cleans, contracts, and consolidates raw tissue.

**Caraka Saṃhitā (CS) & AH:** Caraka and Vāgbhaṭa retain Dhava within the anti-prameha and skin-disease pharmacology and among astringent, *saṅgrāhī* (fluid-binding) agents useful in diarrhoea, bleeding disorders, and weeping dermatoses. The tree is also the botanical source of **gum ghatti** (Indian gum), an economically important exudate, and of a bark rich in the tannin used in traditional tanning and dyeing — a fact the tradition read directly as *pharmacological* astringency.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Tannin That Contracts the Breach:** Dhava's doctrine is the doctrine of the *closing wound*. Where Asana in the same gaṇa is the vessel that corrects the water, Dhava is the astringent that draws a torn edge back together — its very name-cluster and its industrial use as a tanning bark encode the action: it firms what is loose, seals what is leaking, tans the raw into the durable. In Kenetica this is the **consolidation** principle in its purest botanical form: after clearing and purification comes contraction — the firming of over-loose channels, the closing of the breach through which Pitta-heat and fluid escape. The cooling virya keeps the contraction from becoming brittle; the tree consolidates without scorching. Dhava is the trainer of integrity-under-repair.

### Active Components

According to PubMed, phytochemical review of *Anogeissus latifolia* documents a rich profile across plant parts: **hydrolysable tannins and ellagic acid** (the marker constituent of the stem bark), **flavonoids, phenols, terpenoids, sterols, saponins, coumarins, and alkaloids**, plus the polysaccharide gum ghatti of the exudate (arabinose, galactose, mannose, xylose, rhamnose, glucuronic acid, with magnesium and calcium salts of ghattic acid). Ellagic acid and the tannin–flavonoid fraction are the components most associated with the antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and tissue-protective actions (Khan et al., 2024, *Chemistry & Biodiversity*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbdv.202401378)).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Dhava is Kenetica's **astringent consolidator and repair bark**. It is placed in the tissue-firming track wherever the goal is to contract loose, weeping, or bleeding tissue — mucosal laxity, chronic loose stool, slow-healing or discharging wounds and skin. As a Sālasārādi member it doubles into the metabolic-regulation and Clearing work in damp, Kapha-heavy pictures, and its ellagic-acid antioxidant/anti-inflammatory profile supports organ-protective use. It is the natural partner of Asana (heartwood glycaemic anchor) and of the other tannin astringents (Arjuna, Lodhra) in the Codex's astringent cluster.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the modern evidence for *Anogeissus latifolia* is preclinical (in-vitro and rodent) — evidence grade **preliminary**, but coherent with the traditional astringent–protective use.

- Khan et al. (2024), *Chemistry & Biodiversity*, is a comprehensive review documenting antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, antidiabetic, antioxidant, hepatoprotective, wound-healing, and hypolipidaemic activities across *A. latifolia* preparations, alongside a dose-dependent toxicity note (particularly intraperitoneal). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbdv.202401378)
- Sharma et al. (2021), *Journal of Ethnopharmacology*, demonstrated that an ellagic-acid-standardised ethanolic **stem-bark extract of *A. latifolia* (Dhava)** was **nephroprotective** against gentamicin-induced kidney injury in rats — attenuating serum creatinine/urea rises, oxidative-stress and inflammatory markers (TNF-α, IL-6), and histological damage — explicitly validating the traditional use of the bark in kidney disorders. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2021.114001)

The nephroprotective and antioxidant/anti-inflammatory findings map onto the classical Vraṇaśodhana–Ropaṇa and Grāhī reputation. No human clinical trial for Dhava was retrieved in this run; claims should remain **traditionally grounded, preclinically supported**. `[USER: verify before any clinical claim]`

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa:** Kaṣāya, Tikta
- **Vīrya:** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka:** Kaṭu
- **Guṇa:** Laghu, Rūkṣa
- **Doṣa action:** Reduces Kapha and Pitta; may aggravate Vāta if overused (drying/cooling)
- **Karma:** Grāhī / Saṅgrāhī / Stambhana (astringent-binding, haemostatic), Vraṇaśodhana & Vraṇaropaṇa (wound-cleansing and healing), Kuṣṭhaghna (anti-dermatosis), Pramehaghna (adjunct anti-diabetic), Raktastambhaka (haemostatic)
- **Gaṇa membership:** **Sālasārādi gaṇa** (Suśruta) — with Asana and the heartwood/bark astringents
- **Srotas:** Raktavaha (blood), Mūtravaha (urinary), Māṃsavaha (muscle/wound), Purīṣavaha (colon)
- **Prabhāva:** The tanning-astringent contraction of raw and leaking tissue — consolidation of the breach

### Contraindications

- Strongly drying/astringent — caution in Vāta constitutions, constipation, dryness, and depletion.
- **Dose-dependent toxicity** has been reported preclinically (notably by intraperitoneal route); keep to enteral/topical traditional forms and conservative doses. `[USER: verify]`
- Pregnancy, lactation, and paediatric safety not established — avoid without supervision. `[USER: verify]`
- Tannin content may impair absorption of iron and certain drugs taken concurrently — separate dosing. `[USER: verify]`
