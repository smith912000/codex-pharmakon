### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not identified. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The names.csv guess was "unresolved (unknown)"; that stands. A search of all the Sushruta text on disk (the chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and Bhishagratna's three volumes) for the name found the group list below, and a separate drug named Phanji, which is a different printed name and is not taken to be the same plant.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Chapter 38 of the *Sutrasthana* is the chapter "which deals with the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam)" and says that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (Vol. I lines 15343 to 15350; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3 to 9). The drug is placed there by group, and a group's virtue is stated for the group as a whole.

1. **Group list.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38, the Surasa'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15461 to 15466; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 120). Bhishagratna prints: "The drugs known as Surasa, white Surasa, Fainjjhaka, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika form the group known as the Surasadi Gana." It is the third name, after the two Surasas. The initial "F" and the doubled "jj" are as printed and may be a transliteration variant or an OCR error; the name is kept as the spine has it. [USER: verify against the printed page]
2. **The group's virtue.** The metrical text gives the virtue of the group as a whole: "acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough" (lines 15468 to 15472). This is the group's virtue, not a statement about Fainjjhaka alone.

**Not Phanji.** The same list prints "Phanji" separately (line 15464), as do the Rodhrardi group (line 15432), a leaf list in ch.46 ("Phanji leaves are slightly tonic", Vol. I line 21259) and the Uttara ch.40 text, where Bhishagratna glosses Phanji as "a kind of Patha" (Vol. III line 9706). Those places are about Phanji. The text read does not say that Fainjjhaka is another name for it, and no link is drawn. [USER: verify]

No description of Fainjjhaka, no part used, and no rasa or potency was found, and the glossary of Bhishagratna's Vol. III did not return the name.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in a chapter 38 group list, which classifies drugs by the use of the whole group (*Dravya-Sangrahaniya*). Chapter 38 is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns this drug no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only, and only at the level of its group. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it. The remark in ch.46 that Phanji leaves are "slightly tonic" is about a different printed name and is not carried over.

**Practitioner's note.** An unidentified name in a group list. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without an identification nothing can be gathered or used. A cultivator reads it as a name in a record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified; Phanji is a separate name and is not assumed to be the same drug. Do not substitute any herb for it.
- **The conditions named.** Worm infestation, catarrh, asthma and cough are named for the group. These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Group membership.** The drug is one of many in its group, and the group's virtues are not safety statements about any one member.
