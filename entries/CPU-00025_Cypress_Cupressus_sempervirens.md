### Origin & Tradition

The Mediterranean cypress is among the most culturally saturated trees of the classical world — the dark, columnar evergreen of cemeteries and sacred groves from Greece and Rome to the gardens of Persia. Its association with death and mourning is ancient: sacred to Hades/Pluto, planted at tombs, its branch a sign of grief. The same evergreen persistence that made it a symbol of the eternal also marks it as *sempervirens*, "ever-living." In Persian culture the cypress (*sarv*) is, conversely, an emblem of upright grace and endurance, woven through poetry and the paradise-garden.

Dioscorides records the cypress among the astringent woods and resins of Book I, valuing especially the **cones ("nuts" / galbuli)** and leaves as powerful binding agents. The dominant medicinal note throughout the tradition is **astringency**: cypress is the tree that draws tissues together and stops fluxes. The cones, leaves and oil were used to staunch bleeding, to check diarrhoea and excessive sweating, to firm spongy gums and prolapsed tissues, and — in a use that runs from antiquity to modern phytotherapy — to tone slack veins and ease the discomfort of haemorrhoids and varicosities.

In Unani and Mediterranean folk medicine the cypress cone decoction remains a classic astringent-styptic for haemorrhoids, varicose veins, excessive menstruation, night sweats and loose bowels; the essential oil distilled from the foliage is used (diluted) for the same venous-toning, antiperspirant and respiratory purposes. The plant's whole therapeutic identity is unusually unified: it is, before anything else, an astringent.

### Active Components

- **Condensed tannins (proanthocyanidins) — chiefly in cones and bark** — the principal astringent constituents; they precipitate surface proteins, tightening tissues, checking minor bleeding and fluxes, and giving the cone decoction its haemostatic/antidiarrhoeal action.
- **Essential oil (monoterpenes — α-pinene predominant, with δ-3-carene, cedrol and others)** — distilled from foliage/twigs; contributes venotonic, antiseptic and mild bronchial actions, and the resinous scent. **Cedrol** is a characteristic sesquiterpene alcohol of the oil. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Astringent for fluxes.** The core use: checking diarrhoea, excessive sweating (including night sweats), excessive menstruation and other "fluxes" by tightening relaxed tissues.
2. **Venous tonic.** Cone decoction and diluted oil to tone slack veins — haemorrhoids, varicose veins, the heavy-leg complaints — a use with continuous traditional pedigree.
3. **Styptic / haemostatic (external).** Applied to staunch minor bleeding and to firm spongy gums and prolapsed tissues.
4. **Respiratory antiseptic (oil).** Diluted essential oil used by inhalation/chest rub for coughs and bronchial congestion — the resinous monoterpenes.
5. **Antiperspirant / deodorant.** The astringent-aromatic combination used to reduce excessive perspiration.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM / Galenic humoral degree:** Cold (or temperate) and Dry — fundamentally a **binding, drying astringent**; the drying-binding quality is the constant across all sources.
- **Unani/Persian mapping:** *sarv* — cold-dry temperament; qabid (astringent), habis (styptic/anti-flux); used for fluxes, bleeding and relaxed tissues.
- **Kenetica Qi/Shen tier:** ◉ **Qi** — squarely on the astringent/consolidating axis (the "gather and hold" function), the archetypal tissue-binding tree of the volume.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy — caution.** Astringent-aromatic medicinal use and the essential oil are traditionally avoided in pregnancy. [USER: verify.]
