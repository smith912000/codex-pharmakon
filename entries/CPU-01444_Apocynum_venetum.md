# Apocynum venetum — Apokunon (Dioscorides 4-81)

## *Apocynum venetum (the translators' suggestion; "Venetian Dog's-bane")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) printed on the chapter's SUGGESTED line, not Dioscorides' own identification, and this row was added from that line (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) without being verified against POWO. [USER: verify] The translators mark the chapter POISONOUS.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading at line 8763.

*4-81. APOCUNON*, SUGGESTED: "Apocynum venetum — Venetian Dog's-bane", POISONOUS. "Apocynon is a shrub with long willow-like stems, hard to break; leaves similar to cissus [2-210] but softer and sharper towards the top; with a heavy scent; full of yellow juice, somewhat viscous. The fruit is like a pod of beans, about the thickness of a finger, similar to a bladder, in which are little seeds — hard, small and black. Put into bread and put out for them, the leaves of this kills dogs, wolves, foxes, and panthers, and immediately dissolves their lips. It is also called cynanchon, pardalianches, cynomoron, cynocrambe, cynoctonon, phaleos, cynanche, oligoros, hippomanes, onistis, ophioscorodon, cynarice, or elaphoscordon; the Magi call it paralysis, the Romans, brassica rustica, and some, canina." The chapter gives no medicinal use for people. The index prints APOCUNON at p.632. It follows 4-80 (4-80 Milax, a passage ending "This is mentioned as a warning", line 8762) and precedes 4-82 Nerion (oleander; see the existing entry I_971).

One of the names printed in 4-81, *cynoctonon*, is also printed in the wolf's-bane chapter 4-78 (line 8734; see I_903), and the translators' line for 4-77 uses the binomial *Aconitum pardalianches*, so the dog-and-wolf-killer names are shared between chapters and are not unique to this plant. ### Active Components

The dogbane family (Apocynaceae) is generally known for toxic cardiac glycosides and milky latex in many members. The constituents of the plant described by Dioscorides were not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides records the plant solely as an animal poison: leaves in bread kill dogs, wolves, foxes and panthers. He gives no use for a person. The Codex gives no protocol, and the plant is poisonous.

### Preparation & Dosing

4-81 gives only the leaves "put into bread and put out" as a bait for animals, which is not a human preparation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Cardiac glycosides in the dogbane family are well-established poisons in general. No study of this species was opened. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** none is printed beyond the translators' "POISONOUS" and the chapter's "heavy scent".

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter offers a cultivator is the discipline of attention that the cultivation spec describes for this tradition: how to know the plant by its parts, where it grows, what it is called, and what is claimed for it. 4-81 gives the shrub's look (long willow-like hard-to-break stems, cissus-like leaves, yellow viscous juice, a bean-pod-like fruit with small hard black seed), its effect on animals, and a long list of names including a name used by "the Magi". It records no use for the person and assigns no status. The identification with *A. venetum* is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a bait poison in Dioscorides, and the correct practice is to learn it so as not to be harmed by it.

### Contraindications

Plants of the dogbane family can be dangerously poisonous, and the chapter records the leaves as killing dogs, wolves, foxes and panthers. Do not ingest it. Do not use in pregnancy. None of the traditional uses above is advice. [USER: verify]
