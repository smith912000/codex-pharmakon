# Euphorbia neriifolia — Snuhi, Sudha, Maha-vriksha (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3)

## *Euphorbia neriifolia, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Euphorbia Neriifolia.", line 33050) against the Sanskrit name Snuhi (line 33025), with a second printing (line 32874) that falls against Sehunda by count. The Appendix also prints "See Snuhi" against the names Sudha (line 33054) and Maha-vriksha (line 32286). The pairing of each Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The chapters name the drug by Sanskrit name only, and in two places the translator himself glosses one name with the other in brackets: "Sudhd (Snuhi)" (`SS_Chikitsa_ch08.txt` line 278) and "Mahd-vriksha (Snuhi plant)" (`SS_Chikitsa_ch14.txt` line 199; also "Mahd-vriksha (Snuhi)" in `SS_Uttara_ch41.txt` line 294). A search of the name lists of the Dioscorides and Shennong sources for the genus found other *Euphorbia* species under other names, which are not this plant and were not read for this entry. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The names in the chapters.* The chapters print "Snuhi" on thirty lines of the chapter files, "Sudha" in Sutra 44 and Chikitsa 8, and "Mahavriksha" in Sutra 40, Sutra 44, Chikitsa 14, Uttara 41 and Kalpa 7. The text calls the milky exudation of the plant the part used. A search of the chapter files for Sehunda and Sehundi found nothing.

*The strongest purgative (Sutra 44, lines 519 to 563).* In the chapter on the choice of purgatives, after the fruit purgatives, the text turns to "similar milky exudations of plants and trees": "The milky juice of a Sudha plant is the strongest of all purgatives, which being imprudently used by a medical ignoramus, may be attended with dangerous consequences, while the same in the hands of a judicious physician proves strong enough to disintegrate a mighty accumulation of deranged humours and to successfully combat many an irremediable distemper." The chapter then gives forms: the juice taken as one eighth of a compound with the decoction of the major Panchamulam and Vrihati groups, boiled, and taken with two Tola of an acid liquid "in the manner of Trivrit compounds"; a gruel of rice saturated with the milky exudation of a Mahavriksha, or a sweetened porridge made with treacle; an electuary of sugar, clarified butter and the milky juice of a Snuhi plant; powdered Pippali soaked in the juice and taken with rock salt; Kampillaka boluses made with Snuhi juice; and powders of Saptala, Shankhini, Danti, Trivrit and the kernel of Aragvadha, saturated with cow's urine and soaked in Snuhi juice "successively for seven consecutive days", after which "a smell of the powder thus prepared and strewn over the flower-garlands, and clothes worn by a man whose bowels are easily moved, acts as a mild purgative".

*A drug whose virtue is in one part (Sutra 40, lines 54 to 59).* In the chapter on the flavours, virtues and potencies of drugs, the ninth reason given for holding a drug to be more important than its taste is that "a drug may prove curative through the efficacy of one of its component parts or principles as in the case of Mahavriksha, the milky exudations of which are possessed of therapeutical virtues, which cannot be said of its taste."

*Dropsy of the abdomen (Chikitsa 14).* In the chapter on Udara, clarified butter "cooked with the milky juice of Snuhi plants" is used as a purgative in the Kaphaja type (line 79). A thousand Pippali soaked twenty-one times in Snuhi juice are consumed gradually (lines 163 to 166). The *Mahavriksha Ghrita* is made by boiling a quantity of the juice, "one fourth of the cow's milk in quantity", with cow's milk, churning the butter and cooking it again with the juice, to be given "for a month or a fortnight in adequate doses and with proper vehicles" (lines 196 to 205). A second ghrita takes two Pala of the juice, eight Pala of cow's milk and eight Pala of cow's urine cooked with a Prastha of clarified butter, "given in convenient doses to the patient for the period of a month or a fortnight" (lines 220 to 225). The juice is also added to the paste for plugs (lines 282 to 287) and, in the commentator Dallana's note, to Asavas, Arishtas and Suras prepared with urine and the juice (lines 255 to 259).

*Other uses.* The milk of Snuhi and Arka is in an oil for fistula-in-ano (Chikitsa 8, line 270, and "Sudhd (Snuhi)" at line 278). It is in a depilatory with the oil of Bhallataka (Chikitsa 1, lines 851 to 853); a plaster of turmeric for haemorrhoids (Chikitsa 6, lines 245 and 246); a paste for a sinus (Chikitsa 17, lines 272 to 275); a ghrita for the Sannipataja type of swelling (Chikitsa 23, lines 109 to 112); a lambative with Trivrit root against a rat bite (Kalpa 6, lines 112 to 114); the juice applied to the scalp of a newly delivered woman whose placenta is retained (Sharira 10, lines 322 to 340); and a ghrita in phthisis (Uttara 41, lines 291 to 297). The plant's twigs go into a medicated salt (Chikitsa 4, line 263) and its wood and leaves into an alkali, which is a burnt-ash preparation (Sutra 11, line 126). In the chapter on ear diseases a paste of Arka sprouts is placed in a hollow made in a branch of a Snuhi tree and wrapped up (Uttara 21, lines 119 to 122). The names Snuhi and Mahavriksha also appear in lists of ingredients in Chikitsa 9, 10 and 12 and Kalpa 7, where no separate property is given.

*What the chapters do not say.* A search of the chapters for the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) found none of the three names. No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for the plant in the passages read. The Latin identification is the appendix's. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Euphorbia* is a genus of the spurge family (Euphorbiaceae), and the white latex of spurges is an irritant to skin and eyes. The Sushruta text calls the milky juice the active part. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records the milky juice of Snuhi, Sudha and Mahavriksha as the strongest of purgatives, to be used only by a judicious physician, and as an ingredient of ghritas, plugs, plasters and a depilatory. These are the text's statements about Sanskrit names, and the Latin name the appendix attaches to them is the translator's, by position. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose, and it recommends none: the text's own warning is that the juice is dangerous in untrained hands.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives these preparations, each with its locus. Sutra 44, lines 524 to 563: the juice as an eighth part of a compound with a Panchamulam and Vrihati decoction; a rice gruel or treacle porridge saturated with the juice; an electuary with sugar and clarified butter; Pippali soaked in the juice; Kampillaka boluses; powders soaked in the juice for seven days. Chikitsa 14, lines 196 to 225: two ghritas, with the quantities quoted above, to be given "for a month or a fortnight". Chikitsa 14, lines 79 and 163 to 166: a ghrita cooked with the juice, and a thousand Pippali soaked twenty-one times. Chikitsa 8, lines 267 to 281: oils for fistula-in-ano. No dose per day is given for any of these beyond "adequate doses" and "convenient doses". No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** the passages read print for the drug its action as a purgative ("the strongest of all purgatives", Sutra 44, line 524) and the part used, the milky exudation. No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Snuhi, Sudha or Mahavriksha in the lines read, and Sutra 40 says the drug's virtue "cannot be said" to be that of its taste (lines 57 to 59).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text names the plant by three Sanskrit names as a strong purgative and as an ingredient in ghritas, plasters and plugs, and says the same juice that heals in a physician's hands is dangerous in an ignoramus's. A search of the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) found none of its names, so the text assigns it no rejuvenative or generative status in the lines retrieved. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The one cleansing discipline the text records is the *shodhana* the physician performs with it on the patient, which is a therapeutic procedure and not a practice for a cultivator. The Latin identification is the appendix's. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. In the Sushruta scheme the milky juice is a purgative whose strength is the reason it is reserved for the trained physician, and the text says so itself. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of a Latin name that the chapters do not give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text warns that the milky juice, "imprudently used by a medical ignoramus, may be attended with dangerous consequences" (Sutra 44, lines 524 to 528). Spurge latex is a general irritant to skin, mouth and eyes, and the juice should not be handled or swallowed. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read, although the text itself applies the juice to the scalp of a newly delivered woman with a retained placenta in one passage (Sharira 10, lines 322 to 340).
