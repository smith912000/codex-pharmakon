### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā & the Aṣṭavarga:** Kākolī (and its pair Kṣīrakākolī) is one of the celebrated **Aṣṭavarga** — the "group of eight" rare Himalayan *jīvanīya* (life-giving) herbs (Jīvaka, Ṛṣabhaka, Meda, Mahāmeda, Kākolī, Kṣīrakākolī, Ṛddhi, Vṛddhi) that form the core of the classical rejuvenative and nutritive pharmacopoeia. Suśruta places Kākolī among the *jīvanīya*, *bṛṃhaṇa* (building), *śukrala* (reproductive-tissue-promoting), *stanyajanana* (galactagogue), and *rakta-pitta-hara* actions. It is a paradigmatic **rasāyana** and **balya** (strengthening) dravya, used in states of depletion, emaciation, chronic illness, convalescence, and the building of ojas (vital essence).

The Aṣṭavarga herbs are the nutritive heart of great classical formulations such as **Cyavanaprāśa** and various *Aṣṭavarga* / rasāyana ghṛtas and pākas.

**⚠️ ENDANGERMENT & SUBSTITUTION — critical practical flag:**
> The Aṣṭavarga species — *Fritillaria roylei/cirrhosa* among them — are **critically endangered** high-altitude Himalayan plants. They are scarce, slow-growing, over-harvested, and frequently **adulterated or substituted** in commerce. In classical and modern practice, the true Aṣṭavarga members are very often replaced by **accepted *pratinidhi dravya* (substitutes)** — commonly *Roscoea* spp., *Malaxis/Microstylis* (orchids), *Withania somnifera* (Aśvagandhā), *Asparagus* spp. (Śatāvarī), or *Vidārī* — because authentic material is essentially unavailable and its wild harvest is ecologically and legally fraught. Any "Kākolī" in the modern supply chain should be assumed to be a substitute unless a verified botanical voucher proves otherwise. **Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Milk of the Mountain:**
Kākolī, and especially its "milk" twin Kṣīrakākolī (*kṣīra* = milk), is Kenetica's emblem of the **deep restorative — the substance that rebuilds what has been consumed**. Its signature is sweet, cool, unctuous, heavy: everything that *nourishes and constructs* rather than moves or clears. Where Devadāru burns away stagnation and Priyaṅgu seals loss, Kākolī performs the slowest and most patient work of all — it *replaces the lost substance itself*, feeding the tissues back to fullness, rebuilding ojas, semen, milk, and flesh from the ground up.

Its rarity is part of its meaning. The Aṣṭavarga sit at the summit of the pharmacopoeia — high-mountain, hard-won, life-giving — and their scarcity encodes a doctrinal truth: **the deepest nourishment is precious and not to be squandered.** In Kenetica, Kākolī governs the **rasāyana / deep-rebuilding phase** — the long convalescent restoration after depletion, illness, or over-expenditure, when the task is not to act but to *be fed back into wholeness*. Its endangerment adds a further, sober symbolic layer: the reminder that some restorative resources are finite, and that true rejuvenation must be sustainable, not extractive.

### Active Components

The medicinal bulbs of *Fritillaria roylei / cirrhosa* are characterised by **isosteroidal and steroidal alkaloids** — the compound class for which *Fritillaria* is pharmacologically famous. Marker alkaloids identified in the primary literature include **peimine (verticine), peimisine, puqiedinone, and puqiedine**. The bulbs also contain **saponins, polysaccharides, and sterols** consistent with the traditional sweet-nutritive (madhura, bṛṃhaṇa) character. The isosteroidal alkaloid fraction underlies the antitussive, antiplasmodial, and cholinesterase-inhibitory activities documented for the species.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

- **Deep nourishment / rebuilding (bṛṃhaṇa, jīvanīya):** the flagship role — restoring depleted, emaciated, convalescent states.
- **Rasāyana / vitality (ojas-building):** long-term rejuvenation and strengthening.
- **Reproductive & lactation support (śukrala, stanyajanana):** classical galactagogue and reproductive-tissue nourisher.
- **Cooling of Pitta / rakta-pitta:** sweet-cooling support in heat-and-bleeding depletion.
- **Respiratory (traditional Fritillaria use):** the alkaloid-bearing bulb is antitussive/expectorant in the wider Fritillaria tradition (kāsa/jvara).

Phase role: the **deep-restorative / rasāyana** agent — nourishing, cooling, building, patient. Used with a strong conservation caveat (prefer sanctioned substitutes).

### Scientific Correlation

*According to PubMed* (note: current taxonomy treats *Fritillaria roylei* Hook. as a synonym of *Fritillaria cirrhosa* D.Don, under which the primary literature is indexed):

- **Bora et al., 2024, *Fitoterapia* — new isosteroidal alkaloids as acetylcholinesterase inhibitors.** Biochemometric-guided isolation from *F. cirrhosa* (syn. *F. roylei*) bulbs yielded isosteroidal alkaloids with in vitro acetylcholinesterase (AChE) inhibition (lead compounds IC₅₀ ≈ 24.7–33.0 µM) and favourable in silico ADMET/binding profiles, advanced as leads for neurodegenerative (Alzheimer's) research. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fitote.2024.106279)
- **Bora et al., 2023, *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* — antiplasmodial activity.** Bulb extracts/fractions of *F. cirrhosa* (syn. *F. roylei*) showed antiplasmodial activity against chloroquine-sensitive (Pf 3D7) and chloroquine-resistant (Pf INDO) strains (IC₅₀ ≈ 1.76–21.52 µg/mL), with peimine, peimisine, puqiedinone and puqiedine identified as marker constituents — providing nascent evidence for the ethnomedical use in *jvara* (fever-related conditions). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2023.116389)

Evidence grade: **preclinical (in vitro enzyme inhibition and antiplasmodial assays; in silico support).** These studies validate the alkaloid chemistry and some bioactivities of the species, but they do **not** directly test the classical *rasāyana / jīvanīya* (rejuvenative, nutritive) claims, and there is no cited human clinical trial. Rejuvenative/vitality claims remain traditional and must not be presented as clinically proven; no human-efficacy percentages should be attached.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa:** madhura (sweet) predominant
- **Guṇa:** guru (heavy), snigdha (unctuous), sometimes picchila (slimy/nourishing)
- **Vīrya:** śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka:** madhura (sweet)
- **Doṣa action:** Vāta-śāmaka and Pitta-śāmaka; Kapha-vardhaka (nourishing-heavy — caution in Kapha excess / āma)
- **Karma:** jīvanīya (life-promoting), bṛṃhaṇa (building/anabolic), balya (strengthening), rasāyana (rejuvenative), śukrala (reproductive-tissue-promoting), stanyajanana (galactagogue), raktapittahara, vṛṣya (aphrodisiac/vitalising)
- **Srotas / dhātu:** nourishes rasa, rakta, māṃsa, śukra, and stanya; a builder of ojas; acts across the nutritive srotas
- **Gaṇa / group:** **Aṣṭavarga** ("group of eight"); *Jīvanīya* and *Bṛṃhaṇīya* classical groups; core of Cyavanaprāśa-type rasāyanas

### Contraindications

- **Kapha excess / āma / obesity / sluggish digestion:** the heavy, sweet, unctuous, cooling nature can aggravate Kapha, mucus, and undigested-toxin states; use with dīpana support or avoid.
- **Weak agni:** heavy nutritive dravyas burden a low digestive fire.
- **Conservation / legality:** wild *Fritillaria roylei* is critically endangered — **do not source wild material**; prefer sanctioned substitutes. Ethical and legal risk is a genuine contraindication to the authentic drug.
- **Substitution uncertainty:** because commercial "Kākolī" is very often an unlabelled substitute, both identity and dosing are uncertain — verify. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy / lactation / paediatric:** although classically used as a nutritive/galactagogue, alkaloid content and adulteration risk mean verified, supervised sourcing is essential; no validated modern human dose exists.
