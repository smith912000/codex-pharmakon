# Pig dung — "Pig's stool" in Clark's translation of the rGyud-bzhi; "Pig feces" in the Men-Tsee-Khang edition
## *The droppings of the domestic pig; named in the Tibetan materia medica of animal origin* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan Tradition (FMT).** The text is Barry Clark's 1995 translation of the rGyud-bzhi, file `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`. The locus is the Explanatory Tantra, chapter 20, "The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines" (heading at line 792), section "(8) Medicines of Animal Origin" (line 1147), sub-group "Droppings" (line 1198). The line (1199) is a long run of dungs, and the pig entry reads: "Pig's stool: cures indigestion, septic disorders, infectious fevers and bile tumors." It follows "Vulture's droppings: increase digestive heat, disperse tumors and suppurate swellings" and precedes "Human excreta: cure gall stones, neutralize poisons, reduce swellings [and subdue spirits]", horse dung, rabbit droppings and dog stool. The pig entry carries no bracket, so it is not marked as interpolated; Clark says (line 80) that he "interpolated liberally from the Blue Aquamarine commentary" and that the brackets mark such matter, which is an inference about the convention. `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 376 ("Pig feces", animal, id_uncertain "yes") points to this chapter; the row's name follows the Men-Tsee-Khang wording. The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 edition (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, line 3568) has the parallel sentence: "Pig feces cure indigestion, epidemic and cholelithiasis." The two translations agree on indigestion. For the rest, Clark's "septic disorders, infectious fevers and bile tumors" appears as "epidemic and cholelithiasis" (gallstones) in the other edition; "bile tumors" and "cholelithiasis" are not the same term and I do not reconcile them. [USER: verify] Neither translation gives a Tibetan name for the substance in this line.

Only the pig's dung is relevant here; Clark's other pig statements concern different parts: bone "cures brown phlegm" (line 1160), blood "collects [scattered types of] poisoning and brown phlegm" (1176), fat "collects poison, heals psoriasis" (1180), and the meat "is cool and light" (677). None of these is carried over to the dung.

The "Droppings" group contains many animals and ends, in Clark's text, with insects and small creatures (beetle, crab, ant, cockroach, dung beetle, slug; lines 1199-1202). The pig's place in it is that of one item among several.

### Active Components

Pig faeces consist of undigested plant fibre, bacterial biomass, bile pigments and water, and carry a heavy microbial load, including intestinal bacteria and parasite eggs. The text names no active compound.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The Tibetan text says pig's stool "cures indigestion, septic disorders, infectious fevers and bile tumors". The Codex does not recommend using faeces of any animal as a remedy, and gives no protocol. The substance is a vector for infection, and every condition named is better served by medical care. The line is retained for the record only.

### Preparation & Dosing

The line gives no preparation (dried, ash, decoction, external application) and no dose, and does not say whether the stool is taken by mouth or applied. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General facts: pig faeces can carry *Salmonella*, pathogenic *E. coli*, *Ascaris suum* and other parasites, and pig-associated viruses; ingestion is a recognised route of infection. Faecal microbiota transplantation from screened human donors is a medical procedure in specific indications, and is unrelated to this entry.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Tibetan: the line prints actions only, with no taste, power or post-digestive taste. None is given in the sources retrieved. No other tradition is tagged.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan (FMT).** *(c) Absent.* Pig's stool appears once in the animal-origin materia medica, in the "Droppings" group (line 1199), with therapeutic actions only. No cultivation status, no essence-extraction (*bcud len*) role and no rejuvenation role is printed for it in the lines read. In the same group several items carry spirit-related notes (human excreta "[and subdue spirits]", horse dung "[beneficial against spirits]", dog stool "[especially beneficial against spirits]"); the pig entry has none.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. A cultivator does not use pig dung. The text records it against indigestion and fevers, and the Codex keeps the record without carrying it into practice.

### Contraindications

Do not ingest or apply pig or any animal faeces: the risk of bacterial, viral and parasitic infection is high. Fevers, sepsis ("septic disorders") and gallstones are conditions that need medical assessment. Pig products are prohibited in some religious traditions; respect them. This entry gives no medical advice.
