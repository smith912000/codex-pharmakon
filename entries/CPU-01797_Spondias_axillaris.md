# Spondias axillaris — snying.zho.sha, Nying shho sha, "Hog plum" (rGyud-bzhi; Clark 1995 prints Spondias axillaris Roxb., the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary prints Choerospondias axillaris)

## *Spondias axillaris Roxb., as Clark 1995 prints it for the Tibetan name snying.zho.sha (line 948); the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary sets Choerospondias axillaris and the English "Hog plum" against "Nying shhosha" by column position (line 5568), and the Codex keeps Clark's genus as the entry name because the spine row is keyed to it* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi, read in two modern editions.** The editions are Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, both in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. They are two translations of one tradition's text and are not two independent traditions. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Sushruta text folders on disk for *Spondias* found no hit in the primary texts. The row was added from a printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (line 87) the row is keyed by the **Tibetan name** *snying.zho.sha*, with the identification "Spondias axillaris Roxb." and the locus "Clark 1995 (line 948)". The string prints a single Latin name, so the spine's binomial is the only name Clark gives, and it is neither a first nor a later one of several. The Men-Tsee-Khang glossary has no row of its own in `names.csv`, so the glossary passage below was found by following the Tibetan name.

**Identity doubt.** The two editions print the plant under two genus names. Clark prints *Spondias axillaris* Roxb.; the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary, in the position that column order assigns to "Nying shhosha", prints *Choerospondias axillaris* with the English name "Hog plum". The shared epithet and the English name point to one tree, and it is the Codex's understanding (not checked against POWO or a flora) that *Choerospondias axillaris* is the name under which *Spondias axillaris* Roxb. is now placed; the entry therefore treats the two spellings as one plant and records the difference. Clark's preface says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance" (line 83) and that "the data given in some of these is occasionally suspect" (line 68). The Men-Tsee-Khang editors' note (line 686) says that its botanical terms rest on the information of its own Materia Medica Department. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 948).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 ("The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines", line 792). Clark's list of the eight sections (line 802) names "(6) plants"; the text read prints no "(6)" heading, and the entry stands between "(5) Resins" (line 879) and "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). It follows *skyu.ru.ra*, emblic myrobalan (line 947), and is followed by *mkhal.zho.sha* (line 949) and *gla.gor.zho.sha* (line 950). It reads:

"snying.zho.sha: Spondias axillaris Roxb.: [has two names. hKhrungs.dpe text says, “It is the type of Spondias that cures heart diseases and grows in forested valleys. (The tree) has a large trunk and thick leaves. The flower is white and extremely attractive, whilst the fruit is heart-shaped, black and coarse]. It cures heart fevers.”"

Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries. The habitat, the trunk, the leaves, the flower and the fruit, and the clause "cures heart diseases", are therefore the commentary layer, and the commentary here quotes a text it calls "hKhrungs.dpe" (a source the Codex has not seen; it appears in Clark's name index, line 1678, and in other entries, lines 908, 915 and 949). Outside the brackets, as Clark's marks stand, are the Tibetan name, the Latin name, which is Clark's identification, and the clause "It cures heart fevers." The quotation marks do not agree with the brackets: the quotation opens before the square bracket and closes after "heart fevers", so the closing clause is both outside the brackets and inside the quotation attributed to the hKhrungs.dpe text. The Codex cannot say, from the printed line, whose words the clause is. The line gives no taste, no power, no part used and no preparation. Clark's name index lists the Latin name as "Spondias axillans" (line 1647, so printed), and a search of the text for the Tibetan name *snying.zho.sha* found line 948 only.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text (line 3417).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225; colophon at line 3583), in the run that begins "The efficacy of these medicinal substances for healing disorder is as follows" (line 3380) and ends where "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" begins (line 3428): "Nying shho sha, Khelma shho sha and Laagor shho sha cure hot disorders of the heart, kidneys and spleen respectively." The index lists "Nying shho sha 205" (line 7454). The three names agree in order and in organ with Clark's three neighbouring entries: *snying.zho.sha* "cures heart fevers" (948), *mkhal.zho.sha* "kidney fever" (949) and *gla.gor.zho.sha* "spleen fever" (950). "Hot disorders" and "fever" are the two translators' words and the Codex does not treat them as one term.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (the "N" block, lines 5539 to 5570).* The Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients prints its columns in separate runs. Nine phonetic names are printed (lines 5539 to 5541): "Ngonbu", "Nheu", "Nimpa", "Nyalo", "Nyangtse trae", "Nye shing", "Nyiga", "Nying shhosha" and "Nyiwa". The transliterations (lines 5543 to 5546) end "... nyi dga' snying zho sha snyi ba". The scientific names (lines 5548 to 5570) are, in order, "Cyananthus lobatus", "Chenopodium album", "Azadirachta indica", "Aconogonom campanalatum", "Coptis teetoides", "Asparagus adscendens", "Malva botka", **"Choerospondias axillaris Hog plum"** (line 5568) and "Codonopsis convolvulacea Bell flower" (line 5570); a lone "." is printed at line 5550 and is not counted as a name. Counted in order, "Nying shhosha" is the eighth phonetic name and *Choerospondias axillaris* the eighth scientific name. The count is checked at three points: "Nimpa" is the third name against *Azadirachta indica* and "Neem"; "Nyangtse trae" is the fifth against *Coptis teetoides* and "Gold thread"; and "Nyiwa" is the last, against *Codonopsis convolvulacea*, which Clark prints for *snyi.wa* (line 1061). The glossary does not print the pairing in one row, so it is by position. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Spondias*, *Choerospondias*, *snying.zho.sha* and "Nying shho" found no entry on this plant. The glossary block is read by I_1236 (*Asparagus adscendens*), I_1313 (*Malva botka*), I_1306 (*Lactuca dissecta*) and I_1054 (*Codonopsis convolvulacea*), which count the same "N" block for other binomials; I_1272 (*Entada scandens*), I_1246 (*Canavalia gladiata*) and I_1321 (*Mucuna monosperma*) are the entries for the two sibling drugs of the same three-name sentence (lines 949 and 950 of Clark; line 3417 of Men-Tsee-Khang). Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Spondias* and *Choerospondias* are genera of the cashew family (Anacardiaceae), a family that includes the mango and whose members range from fruit trees to plants with skin-irritant resins; that is a statement about the family and not about this tree. The lines read give no composition for the Tibetan drug, and nothing is stated here about what it contains. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a tree drug that cures "heart fevers" (Clark, line 948, a clause whose speaker is unclear) and a drug that cures "hot disorders of the heart" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3417, grouped with two other drugs). These are the texts' claims, and they are attached to this binomial only through a glossary count and an agreement of order. They are not Codex protocols. Heart disease, and any fever with chest symptoms, needs medical care. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

The sources read give no preparation, no part used, no quantity, no frequency and no duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Clark's line 948 prints no taste, power or post-digestive taste. It prints, inside the square brackets and attributed to the hKhrungs.dpe text, that the fruit is "heart-shaped, black and coarse"; "coarse" there describes the fruit and is not a power. The Men-Tsee-Khang lines read (3417, 5541, 5546, 5568) print no taste, power or post-digestive taste for *Nying shho sha*.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica gives this drug by what it does: it cures heart fevers, and the Men-Tsee-Khang text, which names it with two other drugs, says it cures hot disorders of the heart. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. A search of both Tibetan texts for the drug's names (*snying.zho.sha*, "Nying shho sha", *Spondias*, *Choerospondias*) found it only in the lines cited above, the glossary and the indexes. It is not in Men-Tsee-Khang's rejuvenation chapter (chapter 23, colophon at line 3950) or in Clark's chapter 23, "Normal health" (lines 1264 to 1276, which include his rejuvenation passage), and the herb entries of Clark's chapter 20 that mention essence extraction (lines 971, 1043, 1078, 1103, 1121 and 1144) do not include it. Its framing is therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this tree. It is a heart-fever drug with no preparation and no dose, whose genus name differs between the two editions. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. Heart disease and fever need medical care; the identity is open between two genus names, no preparation or dose is given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. It is a general fact that some members of the cashew family contain resins that irritate the skin, which is a further reason to leave the plant alone while its identity is unsettled. No conservation information and no safety information for pregnancy were read. [USER: verify]
