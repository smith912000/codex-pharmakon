# I-268: Allium ascalonicum / cepa var. aggregatum (Shallot)

**Codex Pharmakon Universalis — Volume I: Plant-Based Substances**
**Entry I-268 | Amaryllidaceae (formerly Alliaceae)**

---

## Section 1: Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Primary Binomials:**
- *Allium ascalonicum* L. — classical designation (now largely deprecated as species; reclassified)
- *Allium cepa* var. *aggregatum* G.Don — current accepted nomenclature (aggregate/multiplier onion group)
- Some authorities: *Allium oschaninii* — distinct Central Asian species occasionally used as "true shallot"

**Family:** Amaryllidaceae

**Common Names:**
- English: Shallot, Eschalot (formal), French Shallot
- French: *Échalote* — the French culinary identity
- Arabic: *Basal al-`Asqalān* (بصل العسقلان) — "onion of Ascalon"; origin of the English name
- Thai: *Hom daeng* (หอมแดง) — "red fragrant"
- Indonesian/Malay: *Bawang merah* — "red onion"; the primary Allium of SE Asian cuisine
- Hindi: *Chhoṭī pyāz* (small onion)
- Hebrew: *Batzal ha-`Asqaloni* — onion of Ascalon (ancient Philistine/Canaanite city on Mediterranean coast)

**Ascalon Etymology:**
*Ascalon* (modern Ashkelon, Israel) was a major Canaanite and Philistine port city. The Latin botanical name *ascalonicum* and the English "shallot" both derive from this city. This does not mean shallots originated there — the name reflects their transit trade route or their association with Levantine merchants. Actual origin: Central Asia or Afghanistan.

**Taxonomy Note:**
The shallot's taxonomy is complicated: it is so closely related to onion (*A. cepa*) that many authorities classify it as a variety rather than a species. However, shallots have distinctive characteristics:
- **Aggregating habit:** multiple bulbs form from one planted bulb (onion forms one bulb)
- **Higher quercetin concentration:** Per gram of dry weight, shallots contain more quercetin than yellow onion
- **Distinct flavor:** More complex, wine-like, garlicky-sweet; preferred by French classical cuisine for sauces

**SE Asian Shallot:**
In Southeast Asia (Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, Vietnam, Philippines), shallot is THE primary Allium — equivalent in cultural and culinary centrality to onion in Western cuisine. The small red-purple Thai/Indonesian shallot is distinct in appearance from the large French grey or Jersey shallot but shares pharmacological profile.

**Part Used:** Bulb (fresh, dried, fried); outer skins (highest quercetin).

5. **Respiratory / anti-cough** — DMM, CM, FMT

**Unique Pharmacological Emphasis — Quercetin Concentration:**
Shallot's primary pharmacological distinction is its **exceptional quercetin content** — the highest among commonly consumed *Allium* species per gram:
- Shallot: 1,190–2,400 mg quercetin/kg fresh weight
- Red onion: 250–350 mg/kg
- Yellow onion: 200–250 mg/kg
- Garlic: ~30 mg/kg (allicin dominant; low quercetin)
- Leek: ~110 mg/kg

This concentration differential means shallot is pharmacologically the "onion intensified" — the same quercetin mechanisms as onion (I-262) but at 5–10× the concentration per gram.

## Section 4: Phytochemical Composition

### Flavonoids (Primary Active Class — Highest Concentration in Common Allium)

**1. Quercetin-3,4'-diglucoside**
- Primary glycoside form; well-absorbed
- 1,190–2,400 mg/kg fresh weight — highest in common Allium

**2. Quercetin-4'-monoglucoside (spiraeoside)**
- Shallot-characteristic glycoside; excellent oral bioavailability

**3. Quercetin aglycone**
- In outer dry skins primarily; concentrated 100× vs. inner flesh

**4. Isorhamnetin glycosides**
- Methylated quercetin; anti-inflammatory; good bioavailability

**5. Kaempferol glycosides** — minor

**6. Anthocyanins (SE Asian red shallots)**
- Cyanidin-3-glucoside; cyanidin-3-laminaribioside
- Antioxidant; anti-inflammatory; gives red-purple pigmentation

### Organosulfur

**7. Alliin → allicin** — present at onion-comparable concentrations; responsible for pungent character
**8. APDS (allyl propyl disulfide)** — anti-platelet; vasodilatory
**9. SMCS (S-methyl-L-cysteine sulfoxide)** — anti-diabetic

### Other

**10. Ascalonicoside (Allium-specific saponin)** — named after shallot's Ascalon association; minor anti-inflammatory
**11. Prostaglandin A₁** — vasodilatory; anti-hypertensive (as in onion)

## Section 5: Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Cardiovascular (Quercetin Primary — Higher Than Onion)
- **eNOS upregulation (quercetin):** NO production → vasodilation; more potent than onion at equivalent dose due to concentration
- **ACE inhibition (quercetin):** anti-hypertensive
- **Anti-platelet (APDS):** TXA₂ inhibition; reduces arterial thrombus risk
- **Quercetin LDL oxidation inhibition:** directly prevents LDL oxidation → reduces atherosclerotic plaque formation

### 2. Anti-Inflammatory (Quercetin, Isorhamnetin)
- **COX-1/COX-2 dual inhibition** (IC₅₀ ~30-100 μM quercetin)
- **5-LOX inhibition:** anti-leukotriene; anti-allergic
- **NF-κB suppression:** reduces systemic inflammatory cytokine production
- **NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition:** quercetin recently confirmed as NLRP3 inhibitor — relevant to metabolic inflammation, gout, cardiovascular inflammatory cascades

### 3. Anti-Diabetic
- **α-Glucosidase inhibition (quercetin):** slows carbohydrate absorption → reduced postprandial glucose spike
- **GLUT4 translocation (quercetin, isorhamnetin):** improved peripheral glucose uptake
- **SMCS:** direct insulin-mimetic

### 4. Anti-Cancer (Quercetin Anti-Proliferative)
- Quercetin: induces apoptosis in multiple cancer cell lines (caspase-3 activation; p53 stabilization)
- Quercetin: anti-angiogenic (inhibits VEGF receptor signaling)
- Epidemiological: high quercetin dietary intake inversely associated with several cancer types (lung, colorectal — same data as onion)

### 5. Antimicrobial
- Allicin: standard thiol-reactive mechanism
- Quercetin: topoisomerase inhibition; membrane disruption
- Dual mechanism provides broader spectrum than allicin alone

## Section 7: Phenomenological Record

### Qualitative Experience

**The French Classical Culinary Context:**
French haute cuisine elevates shallot to a foundational status that distinguishes it from onion: *beurre blanc* (butter sauce), *béarnaise*, *mignonette* for oysters, *bordelaise* sauce — all depend on shallot's distinct flavor profile. The shallot is not a weaker onion but a qualitatively different ingredient with more complexity, subtler sweetness, and wine-compatible acidity. This culinary reverence has sustained 500+ years of documented French shallot cultivation and medicinal use.

**The SE Asian Identity:**
For Indonesian, Thai, and Malaysian cooks, the small red shallot is as fundamental as garlic and ginger — the "holy trinity" of SE Asian cooking. Fried shallots (*bawang goreng*) are the universal garnish across SE Asian cuisines — thinly sliced, deep-fried to golden-brown crisp, sprinkled over rice, soups, noodles, and curries. The frying converts allicin to less volatile compounds while caramelizing the quercetin-anthocyanin profile, creating a sweet-savory-aromatic flavor that is simultaneously deeply familiar and distinctly complex.

**Wound Poultice (Traditional SE Asian):**
Traditional Thai/Indonesian wound care: fresh shallot halved and rubbed on minor cuts, insect bites, skin irritation. The allicin provides antimicrobial; the quercetin anti-inflammatory; the moisture maintains wound humidity. The burning/stinging from allicin contact is understood as a sign of "cleaning" the wound — consistent across cultures with allicin-based wound care.

**Fever (Traditional Thai):**
*Nam tarn nom* (boiled shallot bath) for fever in children — shallots boiled in water, the warm liquid used to bathe a feverish child; the allyl volatile compounds provide diaphoretic-adjacent effect; the warmth of the bath promotes perspiration. The practice mirrors Chinese *Cōng Chǐ Tāng* (scallion; I-267) at the household level.

## Section 8: Practical Application

### Formulation and Dosing

**Dietary (primary):**
- 20–50 g fresh shallot: provides 24–120 mg quercetin (significantly higher than equivalent onion serving)
- Raw preferred for maximum quercetin and allicin retention
- Thin-sliced: increased surface area → more allicin formation per gram

**French macerated shallot (mignonette):**
- Shallot + red wine vinegar + black pepper
- Enzymatic allicin formation in the acidic medium; maintained for hours; quercetin preserved
- As condiment on oysters or fish: medicinal-culinary overlap

**Fried shallots (SE Asian *bawang goreng*):**
- Deep-fried until golden; crisp; intense flavor
- Reduced allicin (volatile; largely evaporated during frying); concentrated quercetin + caramelized sugars + anthocyanin degradation products
- Still flavonoid-rich; antioxidant; but mechanism shifts from allicin-dominant to quercetin-caramelized-dominant

**Shallot tincture / extract:**
- Standardized quercetin supplement from shallot/onion skin extract
- Same as onion quercetin discussion (I-262); shallot skin provides highest quercetin source

### Safety Profile

**Very safe at dietary doses.** Widely consumed globally.

**Drug Interactions:**
- **Warfarin:** Anti-platelet (APDS) + potential quercetin CYP3A4 inhibition → monitor INR at supplemental doses; culinary amounts minor effect
- **Antidiabetic drugs:** Additive glucose-lowering (SMCS + quercetin); monitor blood glucose with supplemental doses
- **Cyclosporine:** Quercetin is P-glycoprotein inhibitor; theoretical increased cyclosporine bioavailability at high supplemental quercetin doses; clinical significance at dietary amounts unclear
- **Anti-hypertensive drugs:** Additive BP lowering; monitor with supplemental use

**Contraindications:**
- Allium family allergy (garlic, onion, leek cross-reactive; rare)
- FODMAP: Small amounts of FOS; may trigger IBS symptoms in sensitive individuals

**Animal Toxicity:**
*Allium* thiosulfate compounds toxic to dogs, cats, horses.
