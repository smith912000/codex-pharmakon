## *A wild mountain goat; the Ebers material is its fat, named in hair remedies* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The attestation rests mainly on a list row. The project list `_sources/ebers.csv` (line 126) gives "Ibex", class animal, source "Ebers Papyrus; Ghalioungui 1987 trans., Glossary I (Medicaments)", with the identification flagged uncertain. I did not have Ghalioungui's glossary. The same list has a second row (line 75), "Ibex fat, Capra nubiana", citing "Ebers (baldness pomade)" and a popular web source; I do not use the web source and I treat the binomial as unverified. [USER: verify]

The Bryan 1930 text on disk (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`) does not use the word "ibex". Bryan's list of animals whose identity is not in doubt (line 1385 onwards) names, among others, Lion, Hippopotamus, Crocodile, Ox, Cow, Serpent, Stag, Gazelle, Ram and Goat (lines 1393-1398), and no ibex. What he does print, in his chapter on the hair, is a remedy "ANOTHER FOR THE GROWTH OF THE HAIR ON A HEAD WHICH IS BECOMING BALD" (line 4023). Its ingredients are "Fat-of-the-Lion, Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus, Fat-of-the-Crocodile, Fat-of-the-Cat, Fat-of-the-Serpent, Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat", with the direction "Make into one and rub the head of the Bald One therewith" (lines 4024-4029). "Egyptian Goat" fat also occurs in other remedies he summarises (lines 2273, 3170). Whether Bryan's "Egyptian Goat" and the ibex of the list row are the same animal is not stated in the lines I read, and I do not equate them. [USER: verify]

The project's own file `Sources/EP_Ebers/EP_Original_Hieroglyphic.md` (lines 368-371) prints, as "Prescription 302 ... Ebbell Translation", a hair-loss remedy that lists "fat of a hippopotamus; fat of a crocodile; fat of a serpent; fat of an ibex; fat of a wild goat", and `EP_Overview.md` (line 190) prints a growth remedy of "fat of a hippopotamus; fat of a crocodile; fat of a serpent; fat of an ibex; fat of a lion". These are project-compiled files, not the printed translation. I could not check the quotations or the prescription numbers against Ebbell's book, and the "sympathetic magic" commentary in them is interpretation, not text. [USER: verify]

So what is supported is this: an ibex (or ibex fat) is a hair-remedy ingredient in the Ebers corpus according to a list row and to two project files, and the same kind of remedy is printed in Bryan with other animals' fat. The entry is thin on that account.

### Active Components

Animal fat is chiefly triglycerides with small amounts of cholesterol and fat-soluble compounds. Nothing about the specific composition of ibex fat is stated from the sources, and no figures are given.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The Ebers hair remedies are an ancient practice for baldness. The Codex does not offer a protocol for them. The ibex species of the Egyptian region are protected in many places, and trade in wild-animal parts is restricted (see Contraindications). There is no reason to source ibex fat today.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Bryan hair remedy (lines 4023-4029) says to mix the fats and rub the head with them; no quantity is given in the lines read, and I read no ibex-specific preparation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The only general statement made is that animal fat is a lipid mixture that works as an ointment base. That is not evidence that any fat restores hair.

No cultivation framing is attested for the ibex, and the identification itself is uncertain. The project's *ka*/*ba*/*akh*/*ib* framework and *heka* are not tied to this substance in any line read. **Practitioner's note.** Nothing. A cultivator has no use for this substance; it is a record of one hair remedy, and the identification is open.

### Contraindications

Ibex are wild animals, and in many regions they are protected, so taking or trading them or their parts can be illegal. Unprocessed animal fat can carry bacteria and parasites. Hair loss has many causes, some medical; see a clinician rather than applying animal fat. There is no safety information for the ibex in the sources retrieved.
