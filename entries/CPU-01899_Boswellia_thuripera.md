# Boswellia thuripera — "Gum of Boswellia Thuripera" (Bhishagratna, Appendix 3)

## *The gum-resin of the Indian frankincense tree; our identification: Boswellia thurifera (Colebr.) Roxb., usually treated as a synonym of Boswellia serrata Roxb. ex Colebr., read from the printed Latin "Thuripera" (a misprint of "thurifera"); confidence medium-high for the genus, medium for the species, which the Samhita text does not state* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Sushruta (SS).** One line of Appendix No. 3 of Bhishagratna's translation, headed "Names of Drugs (with Latin synonyms)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, heading at lines 31119-31121). The translator's note at the end of the appendices (lines 33351-33354) says he drew on *Vaidyaka-Sabdasindhu*, Wilson's *Sanskrit and English Dictionary*, *Indian Plants and Drugs* and *Kavirsiji S'iksha*. The Latin names are therefore the translator's glosses from those reference works. They are not words of the Sushruta Samhita itself. In this e-text the Sanskrit-name column and the Latin-name column have been separated into different blocks, so the Sanskrit name that stands against this Latin gloss cannot be read reliably from the file. [USER: verify against the printed page.]

The line is 32992: "Gum of Boswellia Thuripera." It stands in a run of glosses that includes "Resin of Pinus longifolia", "Aegle Marmellos", "Mucuna pruriens" and "Oroxylum Indicum". The same appendix prints "Boswellia Serrata" twice (lines 32828 and 32972). Whether those and this are the same Sanskrit drug is not stated. Because the gloss says "Gum", the drug named is the exuded resin, not the leaf or bark. The text of the Samhita proper was searched for the Latin name only and does not print it; no indication, preparation or dose is available from Sushruta.

### Active Components

The appendix names no constituent. In general terms, Boswellia gum-resins are aromatic mixtures of resin acids, volatile oil and gum, the resin acids including the boswellic acids of the genus. General, well-established facts only. Frankincense (olibanum) is the dried gum-resin of *Boswellia* trees, burned as incense since antiquity and still used in perfumery and traditional medicine. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]
