# Polygala oppositifolia — Polugalon (Dioscorides 4-142)

## *Polygala oppositifolia, as printed in the plate caption "Polygala oppositifolia after FAGUET — 1888" beside the chapter; the translators' SUGGESTED line gives a different name, Polygala vulgaris ("Common Milkwort, Milkweed")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is not a suggestion on the SUGGESTED line. It is the caption of a plate printed with the chapter, and the SUGGESTED line (line 9274) reads "Polygala vulgaris — Common Milkwort, Milkweed". The row's name is therefore not the translators' identification of the chapter's plant, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The names.csv row for this chapter also carries the caption as its identification. Whether the plate shows the plant of the chapter, or only an illustration of a plant of the genus, is not stated in the text read. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is *4-142. POLUGALON*, heading at line 9270, the plate caption at line 9272, the SUGGESTED line at line 9274 and the text at line 9275, with the page number 688 at line 9277. It stands in Book Four, after *4-141. GLAUX* (heading at line 9266, the subject of entry I_1093). The translators' list of illustrations (line 11832) prints "Polygala oppositifolia 686", while the translators' index prints "Polygala vulgaris 688" (line 17059). The page for the plate (686) and the page of the caption in the text (688) therefore differ in the two lists. [USER: verify]

The whole chapter reads: "Polygalon is a little shrub twenty centimetres long, with leaves similar to lentils, harsh to the taste. A decoction (taken as a drink) is thought to cause more milk [breastfeeding]."

That is all the chapter prints. It gives a size ("a little shrub twenty centimetres long"), a leaf comparison (lentils) and a taste ("harsh"), then one use in a hedged form: a decoction "is thought to cause more milk", which the translators bracket as breastfeeding. The words "is thought to" report an opinion and are not Dioscorides' assertion. The chapter prints no habitat, no further name and no closing list of names. The chapter does not explain its own name, and the Codex does not add a gloss the text lacks.

**Neighbouring chapter.** The preceding chapter, 4-141 *GLAUX* (entry I_1093), also compares its leaves to lentils and is also used for milk: "These (boiled with barley meal, salt, and oil) are sipped to restore milk that has stopped flowing [breastfeeding]." The two plants are separate chapters, with separate texts and preparations, and they are not read as one.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for the chapter number, *Polugalon* and *Polygala vulgaris* found only I_1093, where 4-142 is named as the next chapter. I_322 (*Polygala tenuifolia*) is an entry on a different species and is not on this chapter.

### Active Components

The constituents of the plant Dioscorides describes were not read. Nothing is stated here about the composition of any *Polygala* species. It is a general fact that *Polygala* is the milkwort genus (Polygalaceae). [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records, as an opinion, that a decoction of the shrub increases milk. That is the text's claim and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol for breastfeeding, and a lactating person should not take an unidentified plant decoction on the strength of this entry. The identification is uncertain.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives one preparation: "A decoction (taken as a drink)". No quantity, frequency or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints the leaves as "harsh to the taste". It prints no warming, cooling, drying or moistening quality.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter records no amuletic use. What it offers is the shortest form of the discipline of attention that the cultivation spec describes for this tradition: a size, a leaf compared with a lentil, a taste. The one use is hedged ("is thought to"). The plant is given no status beyond that. The identification is uncertain, since the row's name and the translators' suggestion differ.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It offers a size, a leaf, a taste and one reported use, so it is history and not a practice. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The chapter prints no warning. A plant decoction taken during breastfeeding may pass to the infant, and the plant is not securely identified, so none should be taken on the strength of this entry. Problems with milk supply need assessment by a midwife or physician. [USER: verify]
