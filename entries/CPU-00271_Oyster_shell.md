# Oyster shell (Mu Li) — Mu Li 牡蠣 (project Shennong list) / Shukti, oyster-shells, "Kshirapakas" (Bhishagratna) / burnt shells and ostrakites lithos (Dioscorides)
## *The shell of the oyster, a bivalve mollusc, in the sources mostly burnt or calcined; no text on disk prints a binomial for it, Yang's Mu Li monograph is on unreadable pages, and Dioscorides has no oyster chapter* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM, CM, SBJ, SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** There is no oyster chapter in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`: the word "oyster" occurs there only in the names of plants (oyster plant, 3-15 and 3-16, and a salsify gloss). The Greek text does treat the shells of shellfish, mostly burnt, in Book 2: the shell of the sea urchin, "roasted well" for psoriasis washes and "Burnt, it cleans foul ulcers and represses abnormal growths on the flesh" (2-1, lines 4911-4914); purple-shell (2-4), whelks (2-5; "Quicklime is made of them as we will show when we come to discuss calx"), mussels (2-7; "Washed like lead they are good with honey for eye medicines") and *tellinae* (2-8; burnt with salt and cedria so the plucked eyelid hairs do not grow again), and snail shells (2-11, line 4959), whose burnt shells "are able to heat and burn, and clean leprosy, vitiligines [form of leprosy] and the teeth". In Book 5, 5-165 OSTRAKITES LITHOS, glossed "Fossilised Oyster Shells" (lines 10688-10690): "Ostracites stone is like a shell, crusty and easy to break. Women use it instead of pumice stone for taking off hair. A teaspoonful (taken as a drink with wine) stops the menstrual flow. If anyone drinks two teaspoonfuls after the menstrual flow it prevents conception. Applied with honey it soothes inflamed breasts, and stops gangrenous ulceration of the cheeks." That fossil-shell chapter is the closest Greek text; it is a stone, not the shell of a living oyster. The tag rests on these. A separate draft exists for it (see the report). [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) lists "Animals: Superior Class", "Middle Class" and "Inferior Class" in its contents (lines 770-776), but the text layer is readable only to PDF p.104 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/README.md`), so the Animals monographs, including Mu Li, could not be read. **Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Oyster is claimed in several places, always as shell, ash or alkali or as an animal group, and not as a food-drug of its own. In *Sutrasthana* ch.46 *Shukti* is named, with *Shankha*, *Shankhanakha* ("a species of small bivalve molluscs"), *Shambuka* and *Bhalluka*, in the *Koshastha* (conchiferous) species (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 758-761); the flesh of the *Shankha* and tortoise orders "is sweet in taste and digestion, cooling in its potency, demulcent, and beneficial to stool and the Pittam. It destroys the deranged Vayu and produces Kapham" (lines 775-779). In *Chikitsasthana* ch.14 a patient with an enlarged spleen, after cleansing, is advised "to take the alkali of marine oyster-shells through the medium of milk" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch14.txt` lines 313-316). In ch.20, a non-suppurated *Aja-gallika* is, after leeches, plastered with "the alkalies (Kshara) of oyster-shells, *Svarjika* and of *Yava*" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch20.txt` lines 11-16), and Gayadasa's note puts "Yava-kshara, oyster-shells and Saurashtri" first and leeches after (line 41). In *Chikitsasthana* ch.1 a paste for a black cicatrix takes "The shell of a hen's egg, Kataka, Madhuka, (Yashti-madhu), sea-oysters and crystals (pearls according to Jejjata and Brahmadeva)", with the note "Burnt ashes of sea-oysters, and pearls etc., should be used" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch01.txt` lines 808-817). In *Sutrasthana* ch.11 "Kshirapakas (fresh water oysters)" and *Sankhanabhi* are burnt red hot and immersed in alkaline water in making the caustic alkali (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch11.txt` lines 150-162). An oyster shell is also named as a vessel for pouring errhines in ch.40 (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch40.txt` lines 359-361). In *Sutrasthana* ch.14 *Shankha* and *Shukti* are among powders rubbed on a wound to stop bleeding (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch14.txt` lines 367-369). In ch.38 the *Eladi* group contains a *Shukti* (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 167), where the sense (a plant or the shell) is not stated.

### Active Components

Oyster shell is mostly calcium carbonate (calcite), with a small organic matrix of protein and trace minerals. Burned at high heat it becomes calcium oxide (quicklime) and, with water, calcium hydroxide, which is strongly alkaline; this is what the Sanskrit "alkali of oyster-shells" and the Greek burnt shell are. Shell from polluted waters can carry traces of heavy metals.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. In the sources the shell is a calcined alkali (Sanskrit), a burnt dental and ulcer remedy (Greek, for shells in general), and an unreadable Chinese monograph. Calcium carbonate is an antacid and a calcium source, but the Codex gives no protocol, dose or preparation from these texts, and a calcined (burnt) shell is caustic.

### Preparation & Dosing

SS: the shell is burnt and its ash filtered as an alkali (*kshara*) and given in milk (Chikitsa ch.14); used burnt in ash form or as shell with other ingredients in a paste (ch.1, ch.20); burnt red hot with other shells and immersed in alkaline water to make a caustic (Sutra ch.11). DMM: shells of shellfish are burnt, sometimes packed with salt first (2-5, 2-8); the fossil oyster stone is taken as "A teaspoonful (taken as a drink with wine)" and "two teaspoonfuls after the menstrual flow" (5-165). SBJ: not read. No other dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Calcium carbonate neutralises stomach acid and is a source of calcium; burnt shell (calcium oxide) is a caustic that burns skin and gut. The Greek contraceptive and menstrual claims for the fossil shell have no established basis, and a contraceptive claim is not a safe guide. No clinical studies were retrieved for the traditional uses. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Calcined shell and its alkali are caustic and can burn the mouth, gut and skin; raw shell powder is not sterile and can carry contaminants from the water it grew in, including heavy metals. High calcium intake can cause kidney stones and interfere with some medicines. The Greek menstrual and contraceptive uses are unsafe to follow. Wild oyster reefs and shell middens may be protected or regulated in some places; the status of any given source has not been checked here. [USER: verify]
