# Rakkyo — 薤 *xie* (the head glyph is lost in the file)

## *A leek-leaved bulb vegetable of the Shennong middle classic, identified as Allium chinense G.Don (A. macrostemon Bunge) in modern works; the identification is of high confidence at genus level and is ours at species level, made from the preface's list of middle-class vegetables and Sun Xingyan's note that the Shuowen calls it a vegetable "with leaf like the leek"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Xie* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The spine row it replaces is not a placeholder; the entry had no row before CPU-02571, and the spine holds other *Allium* (onion, garlic, leek, shallot and others), none of them this species.

**The name.** In the file the head glyph of this entry is lost and prints as a placeholder (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2909; the same glyph recurs through Sun's note). The reading as 薤 rests on two things: the middle-classic preface lists the vegetables of that class as "蓼实、葱实、薤、水苏" (line 1677), and Sun's note describes the plant as a vegetable "with leaf like the leek" (see below). The modern drug name 薤白 *xiebai* ("white of the xie", the bulb) is a later name and is not printed in the text read; the Codex names the entry by the classic's name alone.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files the entry in the middle classic (卷二 中经; line 2911, 味 paragraph; name line 2909). The paragraph reads: 味辛，温。主金创，创败，轻身、不饥、耐老。生平泽。In English: "Flavour acrid, warm. Governs incised wounds (金创) and rotting sores (创败); [it] lightens the body (轻身), [one is] not hungry (不饥) and endures ageing (耐老). It grows in level marshes." The part used is not named. There is no 久服 clause.

*Wu Pu layer.* None.

**Mingyi bielu layer** (《名医》, later additions; line 2913). "Grows at Lushan" (生鲁山). Nothing more.

**Sun Xingyan's note** (案, line 2915). The *Shuowen*: the plant is "a vegetable; leaf like the leek" (菜也，叶似韭). The *Guangya*: "the leek, [the xie], the 荞 (*qiao*); its flower is called 菁 (*jing*)." The *Erya*: "[xie] 鸿荟 (*honghui*)"; Guo Pu: "that is the [xie] vegetable"; the *Erya* adds "劲山贲". Tao Hongjing: "the scallion (葱) and the [xie] are different things, yet are now in one entry; the *Bencao jing* has no leek (韭), and because they are the same kind, it is so." The note thus describes the plant only as leek-like, and it records Tao's remark that the classic had no separate leek entry.

The file has no Yang witness for this entry: the readable pages of `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` do not carry it (a search of the readable text for the drug's name, Latin gloss and the bulb name found nothing).

**Identity.** The Codex's name, *Allium chinense* (*A. macrostemon*), rests on the later standard reading of 薤. As a general fact, current Chinese pharmacopoeial practice accepts both *Allium chinense* and *Allium macrostemon* as sources of 薤白. [USER: verify] The classic gives no description beyond "leaf like the leek", so a species cannot be chosen from the sources read. The Codex names the genus-level plant with high confidence and the species with less.

### Active Components

No analytical source was retrieved for this plant, and no constituent is asserted. As a general fact, *Allium* bulbs contain sulfur-bearing compounds that give them their sharp smell and taste. That is a statement about the genus and not a finding of the classic. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic records an acrid, warm vegetable for incised wounds and rotting sores and, in the same paragraph, as lightening the body, leaving one not hungry and enduring ageing. The Codex gives no protocol. Wounds and sores need hygienic care and, where deep or infected, medical care. The Codex does not recommend rakkyo or any *Allium* as a wound dressing.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources give no preparation: no part, form, quantity, frequency or duration. The Mingyi layer names only the habitat. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the middle classic (中经), which the preface describes as "to nourish the nature, in correspondence with man; non-toxic or toxic, to be weighed as fit; for those who wish to hold off disease and make good the weak" (line 1677). The grade therefore does not come from the class. It comes from the drug's own 味 paragraph, which promises, without a 久服 prefix, "lightens the body, not hungry, endures ageing" (轻身、不饥、耐老; *Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 薤 *xie*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2911). That is a life-nourishing wording, quoted here, and on the grading rule used for this series it is sufficient for (a) with or without the long-taking clause. The cautions stand: the entry is in the middle class and not the upper, the head glyph of the entry is lost in the file, the entry's primary indication is wounds, and 轻身 is the classic's term and is not translated into a pharmacological effect. The Mingyi and 案 layers add no cultivation claim.

**Practitioner's note.** A vegetable that the classic files in the middle class with a short, promising paragraph and no dose. A cultivator has no preparation to follow here. If the vegetable is eaten, it is eaten as food.

### Contraindications

- The classic does not warn, and the preface says middle-class drugs may be toxic or not (line 1677).
- **Wounds and sores.** The incised-wound and rotting-sore uses are historical record only; deep, dirty or spreading wounds need medical care.
- **Identity.** The entry's head glyph is lost in the file, so the name rests on the preface and Sun's note. Do not identify wild *Allium* for use on this entry; some bulbs that look alike are not food.
- No pregnancy, interaction or allergy information was read, and none is asserted. [USER: verify]
