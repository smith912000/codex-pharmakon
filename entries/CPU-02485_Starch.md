# Starch — نشا (*nashā*, as printed; also *nashāstaj*, نشاستج)

## *Starch: the white starch prepared from grain and boiled as a paste or gruel (the Canon does not say which grain)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Starch is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the Arabic texts on disk. (Dioscorides has a chapter on starch, *amylon*, 2-123, in the English text on disk, line 5540; it is not cited in this entry and the entry is not tagged DMM here.) The locus is *Canon* Book II, entry *nashā* (نشا), Arabic text line 15591 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; the entry runs to line 15597.

**What the text says (CM).** Temperament: "cold and dry in the first degree" (بارد يابس في الأولى; line 15592). Action: "in it is a strengthening and a softening, and starch must be cooked with three times its water" (فيه تقوية وتليين ويجب أن يطبخ النشا بثلاثة أمثاله ماء; lines 15592-15593). Cosmetic: with saffron it removes the facial blotch (*kalaf*) (line 15593). Wounds: it heals and corrects ulcers (line 15594). Eye: it prevents flowing of matter to the eye (lines 15594-15595). Chest: "it softens the chest, and the gruel (*ḥasw*) made from it prevents catarrhs from the chest" (lines 15595-15596). Last: "*nashāstaj* alone and with lentils binds the nature and prevents the passing of bile" (النشاستج وحده وبالعدس يعقل الطبيعة ويمنع اختلاف المرار; lines 15596-15597). The text prints the Persianised form *nashāstaj* here, which we take as the same material; that is our reading.

The source plant is not stated, so no species is given.

### Active Components

Starch is a polysaccharide of glucose, a mixture of amylose and amylopectin, and swells and forms a paste when heated in water. That is general chemistry and explains the soft, binding gruel the text describes.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. In the *Canon* starch is a plain food-medicine: a cooked gruel for the chest and the bowel, a paste for the skin and eye. Nothing here is a recommended practice or a protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

One preparation is given: cook the starch in three times its weight of water (line 15593). The cosmetic use adds saffron; the bowel use is alone or with lentils. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General fact: cooked starch is a bland carbohydrate food and a classical demulcent base. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Cold and dry in the first degree (*Canon* Book II, entry *nashā*, line 15592). No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* Starch is given only external and gentle internal uses. The text assigns it no cultivation status and speaks of no vital faculty.

**Practitioner's note.** Starch is food and plaster, not a cultivation substance. A cultivator reading this entry learns how the Canon thickened a soothing gruel, and nothing more.

### Contraindications

Ordinary food starch is safe for most people. The text's own use for the bowel (binding) would be unwise in constipation. Gluten-containing starches (wheat) may matter to people with coeliac disease; the Canon does not say which grain was meant. Wound dressings with unsterile starch carry infection risk.
