### Origin & Tradition

*Urtica laetevirens* is an East-Asian/Himalayan stinging nettle — a perennial of the Urticaceae distributed through China, the Himalaya and adjacent montane Asia, distinguished within the genus by its bright, fresh-green (*laetevirens* = "vividly green") foliage. Like its better-known cousin *Urtica dioica*, it is armed with **stinging trichomes**: fine, silica-tipped hairs that break on contact and inject a cocktail of irritant mediators into the skin, producing the genus's signature urticating sting. The nettles are a globally convergent medicine — wherever they grow, people have eaten the young cooked greens as a mineral-rich spring tonic and have used the fresh stinging plant deliberately, lashing aching joints to relieve pain (**urtication**).

In the **Himalayan and Sowa Rigpa (Tibetan) world**, nettle (*sisnu* / *zhu-mar*-type usage) is both a staple wild food and a medicine: the cooked greens as a nourishing, blood- and strength-building vegetable in lean seasons; the plant in formulas and applications for cold-type joint and *rlung* (wind) disorders, where its warming, circulation-rousing sting suited cold, painful, stiff conditions. In **Chinese and broader Himalayan folk medicine**, local *Urtica* species (including *U. laetevirens*) are used for rheumatic and arthritic pain, as a diuretic and blood-tonic, and topically. The **Ayurvedic** genus-relative *Vrishchikali* / *Bichhu-buti* ("scorpion plant," for the sting) carries the same anti-rheumatic and detoxifying associations.

The medicine has two distinct faces: the **sting** (the trichome irritant complex, used counter-irritant/anti-rheumatically) and the **cooked herb** (a dense nutritive — once the formic-acid-bearing hairs are neutralised by heat or drying, the greens are simply food-as-medicine, rich in minerals and chlorophyll).

### Active Components

- **Trichome (stinging-hair) irritant complex — formic acid, histamine, serotonin (5-HT), and acetylcholine** — the injected mediators responsible for the sting and for the counter-irritant, locally circulation-rousing effect exploited in urtication; this is the genus-characteristic chemistry of the fresh stinging plant
- **Chlorophyll and carotenoids** — the dense green-pigment fraction of the leaf
- **Minerals (notably iron, calcium, magnesium, potassium, silica)** — the nutritive basis of the "blood-building" and connective-tissue reputation; nettle greens are a classically mineral-dense wild food

### Contraindications

- **Hot/inflamed (acute, swollen, red) joints:** urtication and warming use are for *cold* stiff joints; do not apply the counter-irritant sting to acutely inflamed, hot, or gouty joints.
- **Sting reactions / allergy:** the fresh plant causes urticaria; rare individuals have stronger allergic/anaphylactoid responses — patch-test before deliberate urtication.
- **Anticoagulant interaction:** nettle's vitamin K content can antagonise warfarin; caution with anticoagulant therapy. [USER: verify.]
