### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is Sutrasthana chapter 38, "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam), in Bhishagratna's translation: `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15665, and `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 324.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS).** The chapter opens by saying that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)". Jojanvalli is printed in the Priyangvardi (the text also spells it Priyangvadi) group: Priyangu, Samanga, Dhataki, Naga-pushpa, Chandana, Kuchandana, Mocharasa, Rasanjana, Kumbhika, Srotohnjana, Padma-keshara, Jojanvalli and Dirghamula (Vol. I lines 15661-15665). No Metrical Text is printed for this group on its own. The one that follows the next group, the Amvashthadi, covers both: "The two medicinal recipes or groups prove beneficial in a case of deranged Pitta, favour the healing of ulcers, bring about the adhesion of fractured bones and prove curative in cases of dysentery where the stools are found to consist of lumps of thick and matured mucous (Pakvatisara)" (ch.38 file lines 336-341). That statement is made of the group as a whole, and the text does not say which member does what. The chapter closes with a rule of use (ch.38 file line 558): the physician should prepare plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, ghritas or potions "according to the exigencies of each individual case", and "only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group ... or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety" are to be used as the physician decides. That is the only guidance on how a member of a group is taken.

A search of Bhishagratna Volumes I to III and of the chapter files found the name only here. The spelling "Jojanvalli" is that of the e-text, and may differ from the printed page. [USER: verify]
