# Cynomorium — ṭarāthīth (طراثيث)

## *Cynomorium coccineum L., the Mediterranean and Arabian parasitic "fungus" of the desert; the identification is the modern one, matched from the Arabic name and a short description, and it is fairly but not fully secure* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** The spine's other Cynomorium is I_801_SuoYang_Cynomorium_songaricum (*C. songaricum*, tagged SBJ and MON); it is a different species of the same genus, from Central Asia, and the Canon does not mention it. Book II, entry *ṭarāthīth*, Arabic text lines 13518 to 13524.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** What it is: "pieces of wrinkled wood, the thickness of a finger, and of a length less or more [than that], astringent in taste, dusty grey (*aghbar*), and its power is like the power of *julnār* (pomegranate flower); and it is said that it is brought from the desert (*al-bādiya*)" (قطع خشب متغضنة في غلظ أصبع وطولها أقل وأكثر قابض الطعم أغبر وقوته كقوة الجلنار ويقال أنه يجلب من البادية). Properties: "astringent, it prevents the movement of the blood in all the organs, as is said" (قابض يمنع حركة الدم في الأعضاء كلها فيما يقال). Joints and nerves: "it strengthens the slackened joints." Digestive organs: "it benefits slackness of the stomach and liver." Excretory organs: "binding, it holds back haemorrhage, and for dysentery and [excess] menstrual flow (*al-iʿrās*, a word the edition leaves unclear), drunk in cooked goat's milk" (عاقل يحبس نزف الدم ولاختلاف الدم والاعراس شربا في لبن الماعز المطبوخ). Substitutes: "half its weight of burnt, washed eggshells, a sixth of its weight of oak gall, and a tenth of its weight of gum" (line 13523 to 13524). No temperament degree is given, and no dose. The entry quotes no other author, and where it passes on a claim it adds "as is said".
The name occurs in other places of the Canon, in compound recipes (lines 29392, 31323, 33509, 34640, 35864, 37365). Those lines were found by search and were not opened for this file.

**Identity doubt.** The entry describes wrinkled woody pieces, finger-thick, astringent, grey, from the desert; that fits the dried stems of *Cynomorium coccineum*, a leafless parasite of desert shrubs, but the text does not say it grows on other plants. The text says "wood". The modern identification is therefore by the name and the general look. [USER: verify with an Arabic materia medica glossary.]

### Active Components

The texts give none. *Cynomorium coccineum* is a leafless root parasite; its tissues are generally described as tannin-rich, which would fit the astringent taste the text reports. [USER: verify.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The text records an astringent drunk in goat's milk for bleeding and dysentery, and used for slack joints, stomach and liver. Uncontrolled bleeding and dysentery need medical care, and no protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation is the one in the entry: drunk in cooked goat's milk (line 13523). Substitute ratios are given (line 13523 to 13524). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

Tannin-rich plant materials are astringent. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Avicenna: astringent in taste and power, like pomegranate flower; no degree of heat or dryness is printed in this entry.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The entry lists astringent, binding uses only, and nothing on the vital faculties. **(c) Absent: no cultivation framing is attested for ṭarāthīth.**

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing here to practise beyond reading. The entry is a clear example of Avicenna's use of "as is said" for claims he reports but does not own.

### Contraindications

- Bleeding or bloody dysentery is a reason to seek care, not to self-treat.
- Constipation: an astringent drug can aggravate it (a general point, not in the text).
