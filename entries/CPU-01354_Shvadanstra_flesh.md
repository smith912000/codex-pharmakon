# Shvadanstra flesh — Shvadanstra (as printed in Bhishagratna's English, Sutrasthana ch.46 and ch.20)
## *The flesh of an animal that Sushruta's meat chapter lists by the Sanskrit name Shvadanstra among the high-ground (Janghala) game. The translation gives no English gloss for the name in either place, so the plain identity of the animal is not known from the sources read* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The text is Bhishagratna's 1907 English *Sushruta Samhita*, Sutrasthana ch.46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink (Annapana-Vidhi-madhyayam)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`). The only locus for this entry is the text itself: line 419. There is no row for an animal Shvadanstra in `_sources/sushruta.csv`; the entry was added from the ch.46 text alone, and the name is printed there without a gloss. The chapter opens (lines 9-12) with the claim that food "determines the origin of beings and forms the chief source of their bodily strength and complexion, as well as of the albuminous (Ojas) principle in their organisms". That is said of food in general.

The meat section begins at line 385: "Now I shall describe the properties of the different species of edible meats". The high-ground animals (Janghala) are divided into eight sub-species, which are named in lines 403-410, and "of these the Janghala and the Viscira are the most important" (lines 409-410). The first of the eight is then described (lines 411-421): "The following animals belong to the Janghala (large-kneed group — viz. the Ena (black deer), the Harina (red deer), the Rishya (blue deer), the Kuranga (antelope), the Karala, the Kritamala, the Sharabha, the Shvadanstra, the Prishata, the Chitrila (Spotted deer), the Charushka, the Mriga-matrika, etc." The translator's glosses cover only the Ena, Harina, Rishya, Kuranga and Chitrila. The Shvadanstra has none.

The group's properties come next (lines 426-429): "These species of venison have a sweet and astringent taste, are light, keen, pleasant (palatable), laxative, and diuretic in their effect. They subdue the Vayu and the Pittam." Individual sentences follow for the Ena, the Harina, the Kuranga and the Mriga-matrika (lines 431-449). None of them is for the Shvadanstra, so the group statement is all that the chapter says of its flesh.

The name appears in one more place as an item of food. In *Sutrasthana* ch.20 (the effects of regimen), lines 43-56 list "articles or substances which may be safely included within the food stuffs of all human beings", including rice and pulse varieties, and "the flesh of the Ena, the Harina (copper coloured deer), the Kuranga, the Mriga, the Mriga-matrika, the Shvadanstra, the Karala, the Krakara, the Kapota (pigeon), the Lava, the Tittiri, the Kapinjala, the Varttira, and the Varttika, and such like beasts and birds". Here it again stands in a run of game and birds.

A caution on the name. "Shvadanstra" is printed in the same translation as a drug (*Sutrasthana* ch.38, line 15, in the *Vidarigandhadi* group of drugs, and ch.46, line 2877, "The Asava of Shvadanstra or of Vasuka should be taken after Pippalis"). The project's own list row `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 5, "Gokshura / Shvadamshtra", ties a plant drug to ch.38. Those are plant uses and are not this entry. I did not find where the text itself says that the animal in the ch.46 game list and the drug in ch.38 are, or are not, the same word. The identity of the animal rests on its place in the Janghala list. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent, and the animal is not identified in the sources, so no composition can be stated beyond what is true of any lean game meat: mainly water and protein, with little fat.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Sushruta lists the Shvadanstra with other game as a meat whose group properties are given, and as food that may be included in anyone's diet. The Codex cannot recommend, or even describe a source for, an animal that the translation does not identify, and wild-animal meat carries the usual hygiene and legal difficulties (see Contraindications).

### Preparation & Dosing

The texts give no preparation of the Shvadanstra's flesh and no quantity. The ch.46 passage describes its properties as a food. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The terms *vayu* and *pittam*, and qualities such as "keen" and "light", belong to the Ayurvedic frame and have no direct counterpart in modern physiology. Because the animal is not identified, nothing more can be said. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** From *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 426-429), for the flesh of the Janghala group to which the Shvadanstra belongs: taste, "sweet and astringent"; qualities "light, keen, pleasant (palatable), laxative, and diuretic"; effect on the humours, it subdues *vayu* and *pittam*. No potency (*virya*) or taste after digestion (*vipaka*) is printed for this group in the passage read, and none is printed for the Shvadanstra alone.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The flesh is named in a food-and-drug chapter (*Sutrasthana* ch.46), which opens by saying that food is the chief source of strength, complexion and *ojas* in beings (lines 9-12), and in the ch.20 list of foods that may be safely included in anyone's diet (lines 43-56). Both are statements about food. I searched the *Chikitsasthana* and *Uttaratantra* chapter files for the name, and the animal does not appear in any *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter; the only other occurrences are the drug uses above. The Janghala group's text does not say "spermatopoietic". No passage read says that this flesh builds *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The sattvic and rajasic food lists are *Samkhya*-era material and not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The text names the Shvadanstra in lists of game and gives one group statement for it; it does not identify the animal for the reader. The Codex does not recommend the meat. ### Contraindications

- **Identity:** the animal is not identified in the sources read; do not substitute any animal or any plant drug of the same name.
- **Hygiene and infection:** wild-animal meat can carry bacteria, parasites and viruses.
- **Legal and conservation status:** unknown, because the animal is not identified; hunting and trade in wild game are regulated in most places.
- **Ethics:** the Codex does not recommend taking an animal's life for these uses.
