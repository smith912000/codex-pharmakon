# Catalpa bark — 梓白皮 *zibaipi* ("white bark of the zi tree")

## *The bark of a Catalpa; Catalpa ovata is the modern identification, which is ours and of medium confidence, with Catalpa bungei as an alternative. The classic's own commentary shows that the old names 梓 and 楸 were already confused* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on one text in one edition, Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). A search of the other texts on disk for *Catalpa* found nothing.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The name line is 梓白皮 (line 3697). The classic's own paragraph is at line 3699: 味苦，寒。主热，去三虫。叶：捣，传猪创，饲猪，肥大三倍。生山谷。 In English: "Flavour bitter, cold. Governs heat (主热) and removes the three worms (去三虫). The leaf: pounded and applied to sores of pigs; fed to pigs, it makes them fat and big threefold (叶：捣，传猪创，饲猪，肥大三倍). Grows in mountain valleys." "The three worms" is a classical category of intestinal worms that the Codex keeps in the text's words. The paragraph is unusual in giving its second use to an animal, the pig, and the leaf, not the bark, carries it. The paragraph has no 久服 clause.

The classic places the entry in the lower class (下经; heading 卷三 下经 at line 3707). The preface (line 2935) describes the lower class as for treating disease, mostly toxic, not to be taken long (主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服).

**Mingyi bielu (《名医》, later additions) layer.** Line 3701: "grows in Henei" (生河内). No Wu Pu layer and no gathering time is given.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案).** Lines 3703 and 3705, a chain of glosses on the tree name, whose point is the muddle of 梓, 楸 and 椅 (several characters in the transcription are lost). The *Shuowen*: "梓 is 楸" (and, in the lost characters, "also written ... 椅梓"); "楸 is 梓". The *Erya* and Guo Pu are quoted on 槐 and 楸 ("槐，小叶曰[ ]") in a passage too damaged to read in detail; Guo Pu also says of 椅梓 that it "is 楸" (即楸). The *Mao shi*: "椅, 桐, 梓, 漆"; the commentary: "椅 is of the 梓 kind". Lu Ji (line 3705): "梓 is the 楸 with sparse grain, white, that bears seed; ... the bark of the 桐 is called 椅". The note makes clear that 梓 and 楸 were separate trees or one tree depending on the authority, and that the old scholars could not settle it. The modern split of *Catalpa ovata* and *C. bungei* is a modern attempt to name them, and the Codex does not claim to know which the classic meant. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None is stated in the text. As a general fact, *Catalpa* belongs to the trumpet-creeper family (Bignoniaceae), and the genus is known to contain iridoid glycosides and naphthoquinone-type compounds in various species. The Codex retrieved no analytical source for this species, and names no compound. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The classic gives a bitter, cold bark for "heat" and for the three worms, and a veterinary use of the leaf. The Codex gives no protocol and does not turn "heat" into a modern diagnosis. Worm infestation needs medical assessment. The pig-feeding line is an agricultural claim of the classic, which the Codex records and does not endorse.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic names one preparation: the leaf is "pounded" (捣) and "applied" (传) to pigs' sores, and is fed to pigs. No quantity is given. For the bark, no preparation is named. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower class (下经), which the preface describes as for treating disease, mostly toxic, and not for long use. Its 味 paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 梓白皮 *zibaipi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3699). The frame is a warning and not a promise. The Mingyi and 案 layers add none.

**Practitioner's note.** There is no cultivation practice here. The entry is a bitter, cold bark for heat and worms, with a leaf use for pigs; the tradition's own grading counsels a short course if any.

### Contraindications

The preface warns that lower-class drugs are mostly toxic and not for long use. The classic's paragraph for this bark says nothing of toxicity, and no safety information for pregnancy, children or other medicines was read. The identification is not settled between *Catalpa* species. No conservation information was read. [USER: verify]
