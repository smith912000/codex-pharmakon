# Mang cao — 莽草 *Mangcao* (Shennong bencao jing), also 春草 *chuncao*; Illicium lanceolatum
## *A highly toxic star-anise relative: Illicium lanceolatum A.C. Sm. (Schisandraceae), the identification we give to 莽草, of medium confidence; Illicium anisatum is the alternative, and the classic gives no description. A POISONOUS plant: historical record only* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Toxic substance: historical record only.** The classic's own later layers call this plant poisonous, and the genus is dangerous. Nothing in this entry is a recommendation. **Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The identification is ours.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the lower (下) classic, reads 味辛，温 ("acrid, warm"). It governs 风头痈肿 ("abscesses and swellings of the head with 'wind'"), 乳痈 ("breast abscess") and 疝瘕 ("hernia and masses"); it "removes bound qi" (除结气) and "scabies and itching" (疥瘙; the Yulan adds the two characters 痈疮, "abscess, sore"); and it **杀虫鱼**, "kills insects and fish". The habitat is 生山谷. There is no 久服 clause, and the Shennong paragraph states no toxicity. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 莽草 *mangcao*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3667)

**Wu Pu (3rd century).** 《吴普》曰 gives the alias 春草 and the flavours of different authorities: "Shennong: acrid; Leigong and Tongjun: bitter, **toxic**" (雷公、桐君：苦，有毒). It gives the habitat in the valleys of Shanggu or at Yuanju, the gathering month (the fifth), and the use "treats wind" (治风; Yulan).

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 adds an alias (its first character is lost), 春草, the habitat 生上谷及冤句 and the harvest: 五月采叶，阴干, "leaves gathered in the fifth month, dried in the shade". The part named is therefore the leaf, and only in this later layer.

**Sun's note (案).** Sun cites the Shanhaijing, Zhongshan jing: "on Zhaoge mountain there is a plant called *mangcao*, which can be used to poison fish (可以毒鱼)", and, on another mountain, a tree "like the *tang*, red, whose leaves can poison fish". He adds the Erya and Guo Pu (an alias 芒草), the Zhouli passage in which the officer Jianshi uses a fumigant of 薰草莽 against pests, the Fanzi jiran (best from Sanfu), and Tao Hongjing, who says the character is also written 两. The Shanhaijing's two entries, a plant and a tree, are not distinguished by Sun.

**Identity.** The Shennong clause "kills insects and fish" (杀虫鱼) and the Shanhaijing's fish-poison use point to a poisonous plant; this is consistent with *Illicium* species, several of which are toxic. The text gives no features, so *I. lanceolatum* versus *I. anisatum* cannot be decided from it. ### Active Components

The text prints no constituent. In general knowledge the toxic *Illicium* species contain neurotoxic sesquiterpene lactones (for example anisatin) in the fruit and other parts; these cause seizures and are dangerous. [USER: verify the species before attributing any compound.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records a plant used, in its own words, for swellings of the head, breast abscess, hernia-like masses and itching skin sores, and to kill insects and fish. The Codex gives no protocol. The plant is poisonous; do not ingest or apply it.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives the leaf as the part (Mingyi layer) and the gathering month (the fifth), dried in the shade. The Zhouli passage in Sun's note shows it used as a fumigant against pests, and the Shanhaijing as a fish poison. The Shennong paragraph gives no route, preparation, quantity or course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved, and none is supplied. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

In general knowledge, *Illicium verum* (Chinese star anise) is a safe spice, but other *Illicium* species, such as Japanese star anise, are poisonous, and contamination or confusion of the two has caused poisonings. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong text proper).** 味辛，温: acrid; warm. The Shennong paragraph states no toxicity. Wu Pu records Leigong and Tongjun as saying bitter and "有毒" (toxic).
- No organ or channel affinity is printed. No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 莽草 in the lower grade (下品); its preface says lower-grade drugs treat disease, are mostly toxic, and are not for long use. The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise, and its last indication is that it kills insects and fish. Wu Pu's "toxic" belongs to a later layer, not to the Shennong text. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 莽草 *mangcao*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3667)

**Practitioner's note.** There is no practice here. A lower-grade drug whose later commentary calls it poisonous and whose own text says it kills fish. A cultivator has nothing to do with it except to avoid it.

### Contraindications

- **Highly toxic.** Do not eat, drink, inhale smoke from, or apply this plant. Species of *Illicium* other than the culinary star anise cause seizures and can be fatal. Seek emergency care after any ingestion.
- **Identification.** Culinary star anise and the poisonous species can be confused; the Codex does not recommend any use.
