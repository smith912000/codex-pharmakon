# Fossil muscovite (manjira) — man(y)jira / Manzira (Tibetan, as transliterated by Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011)
## *A stone medicine glossed by Clark (1995) as "Fossil Muscovite: Ophicalcite"; the text describes a stone of no fixed colour that bears the design of a goat's or sheep's horn, and the two glosses name different minerals* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan Tradition (FMT).** The main locus is the Explanatory Tantra materia medica (ch.20) in Clark's 1995 translation (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 825; list row `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 135, "Manzira, , mineral, rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Efficacy of Medicinal Substances)", id_uncertain "yes"). The entry stands under "(2) The Powers of Stone Medicines" (heading at line 816), between *be.snabs* (yellow halloysite, line 824) and *phag.mgo* (pig's head fossil, line 826). The whole entry reads:

"man(y)jira: Fossil Muscovite: Ophicalcite: [Zur.mkhar asserts this to be a hard black stone required to retain mercury (during processing), whereas Gong.smanpa and sKyem.ts(h)e.dwang assert it to be a shiny liver-colored stone. It has no fixed color and bears the design of the horn of a goat or sheep. The male type is coarse and the female type is smooth. The medium types are regarded as natural. All types of] muscovite cure bone fevers."

**Reading the entry.** (1) The square-bracketed part is the commentarial description inside Clark's translation; it names three authorities who disagree. Zur.mkhar is said to hold it a hard black stone "required to retain mercury (during processing)"; Gong.smanpa and sKyem.ts(h)e.dwang are said to hold it a shiny liver-coloured stone. (2) "It has no fixed color" and "bears the design of the horn of a goat or sheep". (3) A "male type" (coarse) and "female type" (smooth) are distinguished, and "medium types are regarded as natural"; the text does not say what the medium types are medium between. (4) The action given is one line: the stone "cure[s] bone fevers".

**Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text.** The same drug appears in the Explanatory Tantra of the Men-Tsee-Khang edition as "Manzira" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3356), in the passage that opens "To reveal the efficacy of stone medicines" (line 3352): "Manzira cures hot disorders of the bone." The same edition's glossary has a row "Manu pata Manzira" (line 5464) and an index entry (line 7431); the glossary row is OCR-scrambled and no further information was read from it.

**Identity.** The two glosses in Clark's heading are different minerals. Muscovite is a mica (a potassium aluminium silicate); ophicalcite is a marble in which serpentine veins run through calcite. "Fossil" suggests a fossil form, and the horn-shaped "design" in the text could fit a fossil impression, but the text does not say what it is a fossil of, and no identification is chosen here. The description (a stone that may be black or shiny liver-coloured, with a horn pattern) does not by itself settle between the glosses. The Codex's name-index `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` has no row for this stone. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Stated generally. If the stone is muscovite it is a layered potassium aluminium silicate and is chemically inert in the body in the ordinary sense; if it is an ophicalcite it is mostly calcium carbonate with magnesium silicate (serpentine). The text gives no composition and the identity is unsettled (above), so no composition can be assigned to *manjira* from the sources read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Tibetan text records one action (bone fever) for a stone of unsettled identity, with no preparation and no dose. A cultivator has no sourced protocol to follow, and ingesting an unidentified powdered stone is not supported by anything retrieved.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only processing mentioned is in the Zur.mkhar comment: the stone is "required to retain mercury (during processing)" (Clark line 825). That is a role in mercury preparation as the commentary describes it, not a preparation of the stone as a medicine. No preparation of the stone itself is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* *Manjira* is attested in the materia medica (Clark 1995 line 825; Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 line 3356) with a therapeutic action only: it cures bone fevers or hot disorders of the bone. In the Tibetan frame the three *nyes pa* arise from the three poisons and medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root. No *bcud len* (essence extraction) use of this stone was found in the lines read, and no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition. None is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator to take. The sources give a stone of unsettled identity, one disorder it was said to cure, and a commentary on its role in mercury processing; no preparation or dose is printed. What carries over is the way the text records disagreement among named authorities about what the material looks like. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Unidentified mineral:** the identity is unsettled between a mica and a marble; an unidentified stone should not be ingested.
- **Mercury processing:** the commentary ties the stone to retaining mercury; mercury and its compounds are toxic, and nothing here endorses handling them.
- **Dust:** powdered mica and carbonate rock are respiratory irritants.
- **Fossil material:** collecting or trading fossils is regulated in many places; the legal status was not retrieved. [USER: verify]
