# Cow flesh — "cows" (Bhishagratna) in the Gramya (domestic animal) group; Go (cow/bullock) in the source list
## *The meat of the cow. Bhishagratna renders the flesh as "Beef" and lists "cows, bullocks"; the source list `_sources/sushruta.csv` (line 232) gives the Sanskrit as "Go (cow/bullock)". No binomial is printed in the lines read* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The English is Bhishagratna's translation of the *Sushruta Samhita*. Cow flesh is treated with the other domestic meats in *Sutrasthana* ch.46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink (Annapana-Vidhi-madhyayam)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 4-6). The chapter lists the kinds of flesh by habitat, aquatic, marshy, village-dwelling (*Gramya*), carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed and high-ground, and says "each succeeding kind is superior to the one immediately preceding it" (lines 385-394). The domestic kind is the third of the six in that order.

The cow is named in the definition of the domestic group (lines 614-619): "Animals such as horses, mules, cows, bullocks, asses, camels, goats, sheep, and Medapuchhas (fat tailed or Turkish sheep) etc., belong to the group of domestic animals (Gramyas)." The group statement follows (lines 621-625): "The flesh of domestic animals is possessed of constructive, tonic and appetising properties, is sweet in taste and digestion. It destroys the deranged Vayu and produces the Kapham and Pittam." After lines for the goat, the sheep and the Medapuchcha, the beef statement comes (lines 643-646): "Beef is holy and refrigerant, proves curative in dyspnoea, catarrh, cough, chronic fever and in cases of a morbid craving for food (Atyagni), and destroys the deranged Vayu." The word "holy" is the translator's English, and the Sanskrit word it renders is not printed in the passage. [USER: verify] The statement says "beef" and does not separate cow from bullock; it is taken here, as in the sibling entry III_54 Bullock flesh, to cover the cattle listed at line 616. This entry and III_54 therefore rest on the same line; the Codex files them separately because the source list has two rows.

Two further statements are about habitat and not about the cow: flesh of beasts or birds living "remote from a village or pool" slightly increases "the secretions of the internal organs", and the flesh of one "living in a village or near a reservoir of water, excessively increases that secretion" (lines 653-657). They may bear on a domestic animal, but the text does not apply them to the cow by name. [USER: verify]

A different animal must be kept apart: "the wild cow" is named in the marsh-animal group of shore-dwellers, the *Kulacharas* (lines 671-680), with the deer, the boar and the rhinoceros. The flesh of that group is described separately as "spermatopoietic and destroys the deranged Vayu and Kapham... sweet in taste and digestion, cooling, tonic, demulcent and diuretic" (lines 682-686). That is the wild cow's group, not the domestic cow's, and the group statement is not carried over to the cow here.

Other places where the cow or beef is named, which I opened:

- *Eye disease.* After a puncture to drain matter in a case of *Ajaksi*, "the incidental ulcer should be filled with powdered beef mixed with clarified butter" (*Uttarasthana* ch.12, line 232).
- *Collyrium.* Collyriums for the *Pitta-vidagdha* and *Sleshma-vidagdha* kinds of vision are made "with Gairika, Saindhava, Pippali, and the charred remains (Masi) of cow's teeth, or with beef" (*Uttarasthana* ch.17, line 35). The cow's teeth are a different material from the flesh.
- *Insanity.* "Fumigation with burnt putrid beef or dog's flesh should be constantly employed" (*Uttarasthana* ch.62, the chapter on *Unmada*, line 99).
- *Pregnancy.* A mother's longing for beef during gestation "indicates the birth of a strong and vigorous child capable of sustaining any amount of fatigue and physical pain" (*Sharirasthana* ch.3, on pregnancy, lines 232-235); the same passage reads a longing for buffalo meat, boar flesh and venison as signs of other temperaments. This is a sign read in the mother's wish and not a use of the meat.
- *Milk and ghee.* In the same chapter, "Cow's milk is the best of milks, and similarly, clarified butter made from the milk of a cow should be considered as pre-eminently the best and the most efficacious of clarified butters" (ch.46, lines 2180-2184). That is a statement about milk and ghee, not about the flesh, and the Codex's other entries cover them.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, beef is skeletal muscle: complete protein, iron (largely as haem), zinc, vitamin B12 and other B vitamins, with a variable fat content depending on the cut and the animal's feeding.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text records cow flesh as a food: constructive and tonic as one of the domestic meats, "refrigerant", and curative in breathlessness, catarrh, cough, chronic fever and a morbid craving for food. It also records powdered beef as an eye dressing and burnt putrid beef as a fumigant in insanity. Beef is an ordinary food in many cuisines and needs no protocol from this Codex; the Codex does not recommend the eye or fumigation uses, which are unsafe by modern standards. In some communities and jurisdictions the cow is sacred or protected, and the meat is not eaten at all.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the text gives: the flesh as food; powdered beef mixed with clarified butter to fill an eye ulcer after puncture (Uttara ch.12); beef as an ingredient of a collyrium (Uttara ch.17); burnt putrid beef as a fumigation (Uttara ch.62). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Beef is a source of complete protein, iron and vitamin B12. Undercooked beef can transmit bacteria such as *Salmonella* and *E. coli*, and parasites. Powdered or putrid meat placed in an eye or inhaled as smoke is a route to infection or irritation. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** ch.46 lines 621-625 (domestic group): constructive, tonic, appetising; sweet in taste and in digestion; destroys deranged *Vayu*; produces *Kapham* and *Pittam*. Lines 643-646: beef "holy and refrigerant", curative in dyspnoea, catarrh, cough, chronic fever and *Atyagni*, and destroying deranged *Vayu*. The English prints no separate *virya* or *vipaka* for beef. "Refrigerant" is the nearest word to a potency, and is the translator's.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Cow flesh is attested in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 as one of the domestic meats and by its own beef line (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 616 and 643), and in the eye, collyrium, fumigation and pregnancy passages above. Those are food and drug statements, and "tonic" is a property listed in a chapter on food and drink. No passage read puts the flesh in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and the English prints no claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The text does single out cow's milk and cow's ghee as "the best", but that is a statement about those products in the food chapter and is not extended to the flesh. The popular *sattvic*/*rajasic*/*tamasic* food lists belong to *Samkhya*-era and later material, not to Sushruta, and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes cow flesh a cultivation substance. Sushruta classes it as a tonic, cooling, wind-subduing domestic meat, ranks its group third of six, and gives it a short list of uses. That is dietary advice, not practice. A cultivator who eats beef eats it as food; one whose community holds the cow sacred takes nothing from it, and the Codex does not ask otherwise. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** raw, undercooked or putrid meat carries bacterial and parasitic risk; powdered meat in an eye, or fumigation with putrid meat, is unsafe.
- **Prion disease:** bovine brain and spinal tissue can carry the agent of bovine spongiform encephalopathy; the Codex does not recommend raw or unregulated bovine tissue.
- **Allergy:** some people react to red meat (alpha-gal sensitisation after tick bites).
- **Diet:** people with cardiovascular disease or on a lipid-lowering regimen should treat beef as any dietary fat and protein source.
- **Ethics and law:** the cow is sacred to many communities, and cattle slaughter and the trade in beef are restricted or prohibited in some places.
- **Pregnancy:** ordinary food hygiene applies; the longing for beef in pregnancy is, in Sushruta, a sign and not a prescription.
