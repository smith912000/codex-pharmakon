# Feronia elephantum — Kapittha (as printed "Kapittha", "Kapittham", "Kaplttha" in Bhishagratna's Sushruta Samhita)

## *Kapittha, a fruit tree of the Sushruta fruit and acid groups, which Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 lists against the botanical name "Feronia Elephantum" (the wood apple, elephant apple); the identification rests on the translator's two-column appendix, in which the OCR separates the Sanskrit and botanical columns, so the pairing is read by position and is of medium-to-high confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Kapittha is named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk. The text is Bhishagratna's English translation (1907-1916); the chapter files are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the whole translation is in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol{1,2,3}_djvu.txt`. The row began on 8 November as an unverified residual from the botanical-name column of Appendix 3 ("Feronia Elephantum [SS]"), with loci "SS Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31916; id line 32001)". This entry replaces it, and gives the plant under its Sanskrit name because that is the name the text uses.

**The identification (Appendix 3).** In `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` the Sanskrit names of the appendix are printed as one column (line 31916: "Kaplttha ..."; the neighbours are Kapi-kachchhu at line 31914 and Karanja at line 31918, then Ka'ravella) and the botanical names as another column far below (line 32001: ". Feronia Elephantum."; the neighbours are ". See Atma-Guptd." at line 31999, ". Pongamia Glabra." at line 32003 and ". Momordica charantia ; M. Muricata." at line 32005). Counting down both columns, the third botanical line sits opposite Kapittha: Kapi-kachchhu is given as "See Atma-Gupta", Kapittha as Feronia Elephantum, Karanja as Pongamia glabra and Karavella as Momordica charantia. The pairing is the translator's, and it is read here by position because the OCR separates the columns. [USER: verify against the printed page.]

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS), the fruit.** The name occurs in the lists of fruit. Sutrasthana ch.46, the fruit group (*phala-varga*), names it in the opening list: "such as the Dadima, Amalaka, Kapittha, Vadara, Kola, Karkandhu, Sauvira, Shimvitikaphala, Matulunga, Amra ..." (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 988). The properties are given at lines 1038-1044: "The Kapittham in its unripe stage produces hoarseness. It is astringent and subdues the Kapham and increases the Vayu, while in its ripe stage it subdues the Vayu and Kapham, is heavy, and has a sweet and acid taste." Sutrasthana ch.42, in the classification by taste, lists it among the "Acid Groups": "The fruits known as Dadima, Amalaka, Matulanga, Amrutaka, Kapittha, Karamarda, Vadra, Kola, Prachina-Amalaka, Tintidhi ..." (`SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` line 303).

**The exception (Sutra ch.40).** Chapter 40 uses Kapittha as an example of a drug whose effect overrides its taste. It says that "Kapittham, though acid, soothes" the deranged Kapham, as honey, though sweet, "tends to pacify the deranged Kapham owing to the dry character of its potency" (`SS_Sutra_ch40.txt` line 152). The passage is an illustration among several (the same sentence gives radish and the bitter *kakamachi* as other examples), not a statement about Kapittha alone.

**Uses in treatment.** The fruit, its juice, bark and sprouts recur in the surgical and local chapters:

- *Skin colour of a scar* (*Chikitsa* ch.1, line 801). To bring back the natural colour of a scar that has healed black, the hollow rind of the Kapittha fruit, with the pulp removed, is filled with goat's urine together with *kasisa*, rock salt, *tuttham*, *haritala*, *manahsila* and other materials, and buried for a month under the roots of an *Arjuna* tree; the result is applied to the scar (`SS_Chikitsa_ch01.txt` lines 796-810).
- *Hair* (*Chikitsa* ch.1, line 839). A plaster of sulphate of iron and tender *Karanja* leaves pasted with the expressed juice of Kapittha is named as one of the applications for the growth of hair (`SS_Chikitsa_ch01.txt` lines 836-841).
- *Skin blemishes* (*Chikitsa* ch.20, line 235). Kapittha and *Rajadana* pasted together are among the plasters for the blemishes treated at that place (`SS_Chikitsa_ch20.txt` lines 232-236).
- *Ear* (*Uttara* ch.21, line 247). In *karna-sraba* the ear cavity is filled with the powders of the *Pancha-kashaya* drugs mixed with honey and "the expressed juice of Kapittha" (`SS_Uttara_ch21.txt` lines 244-248).
- *Poison* (*Kalpa* ch.1, line 300). A potion of drugs with a cow's urine "and the expressed juice of Kapittha" is named in the treatment of poisoning (`SS_Kalpa_ch01.txt` lines 296-301).

Further uses exist (the name is found in about forty other lines of the chapter files, including *Chikitsa* chapters 5, 6, 9, 11, 17 and 31, *Uttara* chapters 9, 11, 30, 34, 40, 47, 49 and 50, and *Kalpa* chapters 6 and 7), and were not all read for this entry. ### Active Components

The Sushruta text names no constituent. In general terms, the fruit of the wood apple (*Feronia*, family Rutaceae) is a hard-shelled fruit with an acid-sweet pulp; fruit pulps of this kind contain organic acids, sugars and tannins. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a food, the fruit is graded by ripeness: the unripe fruit is astringent and brings hoarseness, the ripe is heavy, sweet and acid (ch.46). In treatment, the juice, rind and bark enter applications to scars, hair, the skin and the ear and a potion in poisoning (see above).
- **What the text does not say.** It gives no dose for the fruit, and it gives no use of it in a rejuvenating chapter in the lines read.

The Codex does not translate these uses into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend any of the compound recipes, several of which contain mineral and animal ingredients (goat's urine, cow's urine, copper and iron salts, arsenical and other minerals).

### Preparation & Dosing

Only the preparations printed are recorded: the expressed juice (*Chikitsa* ch.1, line 839; *Uttara* ch.21, line 247; *Kalpa* ch.1, line 300), the hollow rind filled and buried for a month (*Chikitsa* ch.1, line 801), and the paste with *Rajadana* (*Chikitsa* ch.20, line 235). The amounts of the single ingredients are not given in the lines read. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Feronia limonia* (the wood apple, also known by the synonym *F. elephantum*) is a tree of South Asia in the citrus family whose fruit is eaten and used in preserves. The fruit pulp is acid and astringent when unripe. The classic's terms (*Kapham*, *Vayu*) have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Sutra ch.46, lines 1038-1044).** Unripe: astringent, increases *Vayu*, subdues *Kapham*, produces hoarseness. Ripe: sweet and acid, heavy, subdues *Vayu* and *Kapham*.
- **SS (Sutra ch.42, line 303).** Placed with the acid-taste fruits.
- **SS (Sutra ch.40, line 152).** "Though acid, soothes" the *Kapham*; an example of potency over taste.
- No *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Kapittha in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* Kapittha is a fruit of the *phala-varga* (ch.46) and an acid-group fruit (ch.42), and an ingredient of applications to scar, hair, skin and ear and of an anti-poison potion. In the lines read it does not appear in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no text read assigns it a status in *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The *sattva/rajas/tamas* food lists are not Sushrutan and are not applied. Nothing is therefore claimed for the cultivation of vital essence.

**Practitioner's note.** A fruit graded by its ripeness, and an ingredient in local applications. Nothing in the lines read supports a cultivation use. A cultivator who wants the one thing the text does teach about it can use the contrast in chapter 40: a drug is judged by its potency, and not by its taste alone.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Per Sushruta's fruit lists and treatment chapters, kapittha acts on the skin, the hair, the outer ear, the throat and the digestion. The unripe fruit is astringent, increases Vayu, subdues Kapham and produces hoarseness; the ripe fruit is heavy, sweet and acid, and subdues Vayu and Kapham. Its juice or rind enters applications to a scar that has healed black (to restore its colour), a hair-growth plaster, skin blemishes, the ear cavity in karna-sraba, and an anti-poison potion. Chapter 40 cites it as an acid drug that soothes Kapham despite its taste. The entry records no virya or vipaka and no rasayana or vajikarana status. Several recipes also contain copper and iron salts, arsenical minerals and animal urine, which the entry does not recommend. The systems mapped are the integument and scars, hair follicles, the ear, the upper airway (hoarseness) and the gut through the ripe and unripe fruit.

### Contraindications

- **Compound recipes.** Several of the recipes that name Kapittha include copper and iron salts, arsenical minerals and animal urine; none of these is recommended here.
- **Poisoning, ear disease, scars, hair loss.** These are medical conditions; use proper care. The ear and anti-poison applications are historical.
- **Allergy and fruit.** The unripe fruit is described by the text itself as astringent and as producing hoarseness (ch.46).
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the lines read. [USER: verify]
