**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *nārmushk* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text line 15572, to line 15584). Description: هو فقاح وقشور وأقماع تشبه البسباسة بل أقل حمرة إلى الصفرة عطرة ولها قليل عفوصة يقارب الناردين في القوة ويقال له ناغبشت (15573-15575): "it is flower-buds (*fuqqāḥ*), husks and calyces resembling *basbāsa* (mace; our gloss, [USER: verify]), but less red, towards yellow, aromatic, with a little astringency; it approaches nard in power; and it is called *nāghbusht*."

Powers: لطيف محلل, "subtle, dissolving" (15575). Temperament: حار يابس في الأولى, "hot, dry in the first degree" (15577). "In it is cleansing, softening and much purging, but it does not reach *kirsanna* [bitter vetch]; and it dissolves winds and phlegm" (15577-15578). Digestive organs: good for the cold stomach and liver, "and it benefits with the benefit of *sunbul*" (15575-15576). Swellings: with strong vinegar at the start of a hot swelling; moistened with wine and applied to hot breast swellings; it disperses phlegmatic and windy swellings (15578-15580). Wounds: with strong vinegar on ulcerated scab, applied hot (15580-15581). Chest: "softens the chest by its cleansing", especially a sip of its water with sugar and almond oil; moistened with wine it helps breast swellings (15581-15583). Excretion: it moves the bowels to expel their contents; its sip softens the belly (15583-15584). Poisons: helps scorpion and viper stings as a poultice (15584).

**Substitute.** الأبدال: بدله ربع وزنه زنجبيل ونصف وزنه فستق وسدس وزنه سنبل (15576-15577): "its substitute is a quarter of its weight in ginger, half its weight in pistachio, and a sixth of its weight in *sunbul*" (the reading of *fustuq* as pistachio and *sunbul* as spikenard is ours; [USER: verify]). A stray word (*nukhāla*, "bran") follows in the text and is left untranslated.

**Other places in the Canon.** The name occurs in compound recipes (for example lines 27230, 32754, 59025, 59148, 60162 by search); those were not read for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this material. The terms of the *Canon* (hot, cold, dry, moist, the humours) are the tradition's own and are not translated into modern diagnoses. The Canon uses it as a warming, dissolving aromatic for the stomach and for hot or cold swellings.

### Preparation & Dosing

Preparations printed: vinegar poultice, wine-moistened poultice, a sip of its water with sugar and almond oil, a poultice for stings, and the substitute mixture above. No dose is given in the passage. [USER: verify]
