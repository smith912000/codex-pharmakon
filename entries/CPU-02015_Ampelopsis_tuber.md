# Ampelopsis tuber — Bai Lian 白蔹 (Shennong bencao jing); aliases Mian He 免核 (Yang gives it as Tu He) and Bai Cao 白草, "white weed"

## *Ampelopsis japonica (Thunb.) Makino, a climbing vine of the grape family whose tuberous root is the Chinese drug bai lian; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence for the modern drug, with the text's only description of the plant (a commentator's, in Sun's note) saying it resembles the gua lou vine with abundant, fine leaves* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** The principal text read is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Its other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), also has the drug, as "Bai Lian (Radix Ampelosis Serjaniaefaliae)" (line 3550), and is read here as a second witness, attributed to Yang.

**The placeholder's name was wrong.** The spine row began as an unverified placeholder, "Ampelodesma sp.", an optical-recognition garble of Yang's "Ampelosis". *Ampelodesmos* is a Mediterranean grass genus and has nothing to do with this vine; I_975 (*Ampelodesma tenax*, Dioscorides 5-2) is that grass and is a different plant from a different tradition. The vine genus here is *Ampelopsis*. The Dioscorides "wild vine" (*ampelos agria*, I_998) is likewise a different tradition's plant, and neither cites the Shennong text.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 白蔹 in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 白蔹 *bai lian*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3273). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，平 ("bitter, balanced"); 主痈肿疽创 ("governs abscesses and swellings, deep sores"), 散结气 ("disperses bound qi"), 止痛除热 ("stops pain, removes heat"), 目中赤 ("redness within the eyes"), 小儿惊痫 ("fright epilepsy in children"), 温疟 ("warm malaria"), 女子阴中肿痛 ("swelling and pain inside the female genitals"); aliases 一名免核 (as printed by Sun; Yang gives it as "Tu He", which suggests the form 菟核 that appears in the *Erya* passage Sun quotes; [USER: verify]) and 一名白草 ("white weed", line 3275); habitat 生山谷 ("grows in mountain valleys"). The paragraph does not print "toxic" (有毒) and has no 久服 clause.

There is no Wu Pu layer for this drug. The Mingyi bielu layer (《名医》曰, line 3277) adds the aliases 白根 ("white root") and 昆仑, the habitat 生衡山 ("grows on Mount Heng"), and the harvest: 二月、八月采根，曝干 ("the root is gathered in the second and eighth months and sun-dried").

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 3279) is philological and is the commentator's, not the Shennong text's. It cites the *Shuowen* for 蔹 as the white one (the character before it is lost in the digitisation); the *Mao shi* (the Odes) line 蔹蔓于野, "the lian creeps in the wilds"; and Lu Ji's commentary on the Odes: 蔹，似栝蒌，叶盛而细, "the lian resembles the gua lou vine; the leaves are abundant and fine". The *Erya* line Sun quotes is garbled in the digitised text. Sun's last words are 即此矣, "this is it" (line 3281).

Yang 1998 (line 3550) renders the paragraph in English: "bitter and balanced. It mainly treats welling abscesses and flat abscesses, disperses bound qi, relieves pain, eliminates heat, and [treats] red eyes, fright epilepsy in children, warm malaria, and, in females, genital swelling and pain. Its other name is Tu He (Cuscuta Kernel). Another name is Bai Cao (White Weed)." The English gloss "Cuscuta Kernel" for the alias is Yang's own and is not in Sun's text. His footnote 219 (line 3553 onward) is modern commentary: "an important medicinal for draining fire and dispersing binding", used "mainly to treat various kinds of abscesses and sores". It is not the classic's wording.

**Appendix.** An appendix of drug relations, later than the main text, says of it: 白蔹 代赭为使；反乌头 ("bai lian: dai zhe is its envoy; it is opposed to wu tou", line 4459). Wu tou is in turn listed as "opposed to" bai lian (line 4439). Fang feng and fu ling are said to dislike it (lines 4321 and 4477). The preface (line 4117) says that drugs standing in "dislike" or "opposed" relations should not be combined.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, balanced root for abscesses and sores, for bound qi, pain and heat, for red eyes, for children's fright epilepsy, for warm malaria and for female genital swelling and pain. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose. Abscesses, eye redness, fits and malarial fevers each need medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the sources read is the harvest: the root is gathered in the second and eighth months and sun-dried (Mingyi layer, line 3277). The preface (line 4121) says that drugs differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Ampelopsis japonica* belongs to the grape family (Vitaceae), and its tuberous root is used in modern Chinese medicine, chiefly for skin sores. The classic's claims about malaria, children's epilepsy and the eyes are not translated into modern pharmacological claims. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味苦，平, "bitter, balanced" (line 3273). There is no Wu Pu layer, so no disagreement among authorities is recorded.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph for 白蔹 stands in the lower classic, whose preface says the lower medicines "treat disease, correspond to the earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to remove cold and heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and cure illness" (主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经; line 2935). The paragraph has no 久服 clause and does not itself mark the drug toxic. The register is (b): the lower grade is not a life-nourishing frame. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 白蔹 *bai lian*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3273)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. This is a lower-class drug for sores, fevers and fits that the classic's own preface says is not for long use. The honest use of the entry is as a record.

### Contraindications

- **Lower-class drug:** the classic's preface says the lower medicines are mostly toxic and not for long use (line 2935). The paragraph itself does not mark this root as toxic; the safety of the root is not established by any source read here.
- **Eye redness, children's fits, malarial fever, abscesses:** each needs medical assessment; do not treat them with this entry.
- **Appendix pairings:** the appendix says bai lian is "opposed to" wu tou (aconite root), a very poisonous drug, and wu tou to it (lines 4439 and 4459); these are historical pairings and not a modern interaction list. [USER: verify]
- **Identity:** other vines of the grape family are not interchangeable with this root.
- **Pregnancy, nursing, children:** no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given.
