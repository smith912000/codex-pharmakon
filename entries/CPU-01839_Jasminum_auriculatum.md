# Jasminum auriculatum — Balga (as the OCR prints it); Yuthika (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Jasminum auriculatum, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints in two places: OCR "Jasaminum curiculalum." (line 31447) against the Sanskrit name the OCR gives as Balga (line 31389), and OCR "Jasminum Auriculatum." (line 33349) against Yuthika (OCR "YutWka'", line 33336). Both pairings of a Sanskrit name with its Latin line are positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; neither is printed on one line. The chapters do not print Balga; they print Yuthika in four places, with no Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The name the row was keyed on, Balga, is not in any chapter file and may be a damaged print or OCR form; the second appendix name, Yuthika, is named in four chapters, in an illness diet, a drink recipe and an oil. The Latin pairings are positional and agent-proposed, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 420 gives the name as "Balga", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31389; id line 31447)" and the Latin as "Jasaminum  curiculalum." (the batch note records the merged printed form "Jasaminum curiculalum"), with the note that the pairing was proposed by an agent from the two OCR columns and checked by script. The same file lists a second Latin line, "Jasminum  Auriculatum." (row 820, appendix line 33349), as "not paired to a Sanskrit name (OCR column split)". The count below pairs it with Yuthika, which is not a row of the batch and is the Codex's own reading.

**First pairing, Balga, checked by counting.** On the page headed "67" (line 31367) the Sanskrit names run from Bahu-mulak (line 31370) to Briddha-daraka (31433). The OCR brackets three pairs (Bakula with Vakula, Bala-bhadra with Bala, Barbara with Barbula), so the forty printed lines are thirty-seven entries, and the Latin lines (31436 to 31497) are also thirty-seven. Balga is the ninth Sanskrit entry (Bahu-mulak, Bahu-vara, Bahu-vija, Baka, Batkuchi, Bakula/Vakula, Bala-bhadra/Bala, the damaged "Bskla", **Balga**) and the OCR Latin line "Jasaminum curiculalum." is the ninth Latin entry. The neighbours agree: Bakula and Vakula with "Mimusops Elengi" (31442), Bala-bhadra and Bala with "Sida Cordifolia" (31444), the damaged "Bskla" with "Pavonia Odorata" (31446), and the next two names, Bandhu-jiva and Bandhuka, with the two "Pentapetes phoenicea" lines (31448, 31449). The OCR damages other names on this page ("Bskla", "Bhsindi", "Bbantaki"), so "Balga" is not a reliable spelling of any Sanskrit word and the Codex does not guess one. [USER: verify]

**Second pairing, Yuthika, checked by counting.** The last page of the appendix prints seven Sanskrit entries under Y: Yajnodumvura (line 33327), Yamani with Yamanika (33328, 33329, bracketed), Yashti-madhu (33330), Yava (33332), Yavakshara (33334), Yavasaka with the gloss "(Yam)" (33335) and **Yuthika** (OCR "YutWka'", 33336). The Latin lines are seven: "Ficus Glotnerata." (33341), "Lingusticum Ajwan ; Carum Copticum" (33343), "Glycyrrhiza Glabra. Liqucorice." (33345), "Hordeum Hexastichum. Barley." (33346), "Carbonate of Potash." (33347), "Alhgi Maurorum." (33348) and **"Jasminum Auriculatum."** (33349). Yuthika is the seventh and last name and the Jasminum line the seventh and last Latin line; each neighbour fits (Yava with barley, Yavakshara with carbonate of potash, Yavasaka with Alhagi). The appendix thus prints the Jasminum Latin twice, against two Sanskrit names. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Balga and its variants (Balga, Balaga, Balja, Baliga) and by fuzzy matching of every word; no plant of that name was found. They were also searched for Yuthika with its OCR forms (Yuthikd, Yuthi, Juthika).

*A drink recipe in the Prameha chapter (Chikitsa ch.11).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch11.txt` lines 187 to 191: "Likewise Arishtas, Ayaskritis, lambatives and Asavas should be prepared (in the manner hereinbefore described) with Priyangu, Ananta, Yuthika, Padma (Bhargi), Trayantika, Lohitika, Ambashtha, bark of pomegranate, Sala-parni, ... Padma (lotus), Tunga, Kesura, Dhataki, Vakula, Salmali, Srivestaka and Mocharasa, should be administered to the patient." The chapter is on the diseases of the urinary tracts (Prameha), and the translator's bracket after Padma is "(Bhargi)". An alternative list of drugs follows. This passage is also quoted in the entry on Mimusops elengi (I_1371).

*A diet for diarrhoea (Uttara ch.40).* `SS_Uttara_ch40.txt` lines 578 to 583: "The diet of the patient suffering from Atisara should be prepared with the admixture of Kapittha, Salmali, Phanji (a kind of Patha), Vana-Karpasi (D. R. Vata and Karpasi), Dadima, Yuthika, Kachchhura, Selu, Sana, Chuchchu, Dadhi (curd), Sala-parni, Prishniparni, Vrihati, Kantakarika, Bala, Svadamshtra, Vilva, Patha, Nagara and Dhanyaka and may also consist of the pastes of sesamum and Mudga pulse or of Mudga soup." (spellings normalised from the OCR).

*A diet in haemorrhage (Uttara ch.45).* `SS_Uttara_ch45.txt` lines 109 to 131, in the chapter on haemorrhage (Rakta-pitta), under "Articles of Fare": "The use of milk, the drugs of cold-producing potency (e.g. the drugs of the Utpaladi group), essence of the meat of an animal of the Jangala group, soup of Satina (cereals), Sali rice, Shashtika grains, leaves of Patola, Sehi, Sunishanna, Yuthika and Sindhuvara (Nirgundi) as well as the tender sprouts of Vata and Atimukta (Tinduka) as pot-herbs and cooked with clarified butter are recommended as diets."

*An oil, in a footnote (Uttara ch.56).* `SS_Uttara_ch56.txt` lines 173 to 175, in the translator's footnote to the treatment of Visuchika: "Oil should be duly cooked with Chitraka, Yuthi-flower, oil-cakes (of sesamum), Bhallataka, the two Ksharas, Saindhava and two parts of Kushtha. This should be prescribed by an expert to be rubbed, or used as a plaster over the body of the patient." The text names the flower, not a leaf or other part, here.

*A spelling that may be the same name (Sutra ch.46).* `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1584 to 1586, among the leaves of plants in the chapter on food and drink: "Chuchchu, Juthika, Taruni, Jivanti, Vimvitika, Nandi, Bhallataka, ... Karvudara and Kovidara etc., have an astringent, sweet and bitter taste, and prove curative in haemoptysis. They subdue the Kapham, generate the Vayu and are astringent in their action and light of digestion". The spelling is Juthika, not Yuthika; the Codex lists it and does not assume it is the same plant. The text then speaks of Chuchchu, Jivanti, Vrikshadani and Phanji singly and says nothing of Juthika singly.

*Other jasmines.* The appendix pairs other jasmine Latin lines with other Sanskrit names (Kunda with "Jasminum MuUiflorum", line 32111; Mallika with "Jasminum Samboc", line 32293; an unpaired "Jasminum Grandiflorum", line 31847), and the chapters name Jati flowers (for example Sutra 39, the errhines; Uttara 11, an eye-salve). None of these is tied to Yuthika or Balga here.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Yuthika, Juthika and Balga found nothing.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Jasminum* is a genus of the olive family (Oleaceae), and jasmine flowers are known for a strong fragrance. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the name Yuthika the chapters use the plant as one ingredient among many in an illness drink (Prameha), two illness diets (diarrhoea and haemorrhage) and an external oil (Visuchika, in a footnote); the text gives it no action singly. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters give these preparations that include the name: Arishtas, Ayaskritis, lambatives and Asavas (Chikitsa 11); a diet "prepared with the admixture of" the listed articles (Uttara 40); pot-herb leaves cooked with clarified butter as a diet (Uttara 45); and an oil cooked with the flower, to be rubbed on or used as a plaster (Uttara 56, footnote). No quantity is given for Yuthika in any of them. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** none is given for Yuthika singly. The chapters read print no *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* under that name. The only qualities nearby are the astringent, sweet and bitter taste and the Kapham-subduing, Vayu-generating action printed for a group of leaves that includes the spelling Juthika (Sutra 46), which the text does not apply to it by name.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Yuthika only in therapeutic contexts: a drink in the Prameha chapter, a diet in diarrhoea, a diet in haemorrhage and an oil for Visuchika. The row's own key name, Balga, is not in any chapter. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name either, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for the plant. The grade rests on the Codex's second pairing (Yuthika), not on the row's key name. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with this plant; it is an ingredient in illness regimens, and the Latin behind the name is a translator's appendix pairing, one half of which rests on a damaged name.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this plant under either name. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. The conditions in the recipes (Prameha, diarrhoea, bleeding, cholera-like illness) need medical care. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read.
