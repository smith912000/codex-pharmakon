# Nakula flesh — Nakula, the mongoose ("mungoose"), flesh of a hole-dwelling (Vileshaya) animal, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the animal Bhishagratna prints as "Nakula" in ch.46 and as "mungoose" in Kalpasthana ch.1; his Vol. 3 glossary reads "Nakula (Mungoose vivetra ichntumon) A mongoose" in damaged OCR* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Nakula is named in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 228 lists "Nakula (mongoose)", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", id_uncertain "no"). The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The high-ground (*Jangala*) animals fall into eight sub-groups; the seventh is the *Vileshaya* (lines 403-409). The Nakula is named in it: "Animals such as, the Shvavit, Shalyaka, Godha, Shasha, Vrishadansha, Lopaka, Lomasha-Karna, Kadali, Mriga-Priyaka, Ajagara, Sarpa, Mushika, Nakula and Maha-Vabhru belong to the group of Vileshaya (hole-dwellers)" (lines 574-578).

The group text: "The general properties of the flesh of animals belonging to this species are to increase the consistency of stool and urine. They are heat-making in potency, sweet in digestion as those of the preceding group. They subdue the Vayu and generate the Pittam and Kapham. They are demulcent and beneficial in cough, dyspnoea and cachexia" (lines 580-587). The text then takes some members singly (Shasha, Godha, Shalyaka, Mriga-Priyaka, Ajagara, Sarpa); the Nakula is not among them, so ch.46 gives it only the group's properties. In the after-drinks passage, "Phalasara wine should be taken after the flesh of any of the hole-dwelling (Vileshaya) animals" (lines 2847-2849).

Other places. *Kalpasthana* ch.1, on protecting food and drink from poison, includes in a king's regular diet "the meat and soup of the flesh of a peacock, mungoose, Godhi (a species of lizard), or Prishata deer", and gives the recipe: "The flesh of a Godha, mungoose, or deer should be cooked and spiced with pasted Palindi (Trivrit), Yashtimadhu and sugar" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch01.txt`, lines 445-449 and 453-457). That is the only passage read in which the flesh of the animal is a stated item of diet outside the group definition. Other parts of the animal appear in two places in the *Uttaratantra*: oil cooked "with the biles of a lizard, a mongoose, an elephant (or snake), a deer of the Prishata class, a bear and a cow", for draughts and embrocations in the chapter on *Apasmara* (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch61.txt`, lines 119-124), and the "dungs, hairs, skin, Vasa, urine, blood, bile, nails, etc. of lions, tigers, bears, cats, elephants, horses, cows, dogs, Salyakas, lizards, camels, mongooses, etc." for oil and ghee, among drugs said to have "the efficacy of exorcising evil spirits" (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch60.txt`, lines 296-303). These are not the flesh and are not the subject of this entry. The animal also supplies a name and an omen: *Nakulandhya*, the eye disease "in which the colour of Drishti (pupil) of a man affected by the Doshas resembles (and is found to emit (luminous) flashes like) that of a mungoose", with external objects appearing multi-coloured by day (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch07.txt`, lines 217-224), which *Uttaratantra* ch.1 counts among the incurable *Tridoshaja* eye diseases (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch01.txt`, lines 262-265); and in *Sutrasthana* ch.29 a mongoose seen on the left is a happy omen for a physician on the way to a patient (`SS_Sutra_ch29.txt`, lines 229-231). No Dioscorides, Tibetan or other text is attached to this entry; the tag is SS only.

**Identity.** The translator glosses "mungoose" in *Kalpasthana* ch.1, and the Vol. 3 glossary reads "Nakula (Mungoose vivetra ichntumon) A mongoose" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, line 30813). The binomial is OCR-damaged and is not relied on; the English "mongoose" is consistent across the places read and with the spine list. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Mongoose flesh is muscle protein and fat, with the general composition of carnivore meat. No analysis specific to this entry was read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The mongoose is a wild carnivore, many mongoose species are protected or regulated, and in several countries introduced populations are controlled as pests. Nothing in the sources supports eating or sourcing it. As a medicine, the sourced record is a dietary one in a king's poison-protection regimen, and the Codex does not recommend the flesh as a therapy. It gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The one preparation the text gives is the king's soup: the flesh of a mongoose (with Godha or deer) "cooked and spiced with pasted Palindi (Trivrit), Yashtimadhu and sugar" (*Kalpasthana* ch.1). Phalasara wine is the after-drink for the *Vileshaya* group (ch.46). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Wild carnivore flesh must be cooked through and handled hygienically; mongooses are known reservoirs of rabies and leptospirosis in several regions. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 580-587), for the *Vileshaya* group of which the Nakula is a named member: increases the consistency of stool and urine; heat-making in potency; sweet in digestion; subdues *Vayu*; generates *Pittam* and *Kapham*; demulcent; beneficial in cough, dyspnoea and cachexia. No quality is printed for the Nakula by name. In *Kalpasthana* ch.1 the flesh is part of a diet of "poison-destroying properties" (lines 465-469). No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Nakula is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food in general, not about this flesh. The text gives it no property of its own in ch.46 and, in *Kalpasthana* ch.1, a place in a king's anti-poison diet, which is protective and not a cultivation claim. No *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter read names it. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta the mongoose is a hole-dwelling animal whose flesh shares the group's properties and appears once in a king's protective diet, and whose name is borrowed for an incurable eye disease. A cultivator has nothing to take from it, and the animal is not an ethical or practical source of food.

### Contraindications

- **Food safety and zoonoses:** wild carnivores can carry rabies, leptospirosis and parasites; cook through and avoid handling wild animals.
- **The text's own cautions:** the *Vileshaya* group generates *Pittam* and *Kapham* (ch.46, lines 584-585).
- **Identity:** the binomial in the glossary is damaged; do not rely on it. [USER: verify]
- **Legal and conservation status:** hunting, possession and trade of mongooses are regulated or prohibited in many countries; some are protected and some are invasive. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food is a moral question for many traditions, and meat is restricted in some; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
