# Ushira (Vetiver / Khus) — उशीर (Uśīra), Vīraṇa, Sugandhimūla, Abhaya, Naladā, Khas-khas

## *Vetiveria zizanioides* (L.) Nash (≡ *Chrysopogon zizanioides* (L.) Roberty) | Family: Poaceae (Gramineae)

**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā) — cooling fragrant root; Pittahara; dāhapraśamana (heat-quelling), tṛṣṇāhara (thirst-relieving), stambhana (checking)

**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa — tikta (bitter), madhura (sweet), kaṣāya (astringent) · Vīrya — śīta (intensely cooling) · Vipāka — kaṭu (pungent) · Doṣa — pacifies Pitta and Kapha; may increase Vāta if overused

**Training Phase:** Cooling & De-escalation · Grounding / Fragrant-Stabilisation · Recovery from heat-exhaustion phases

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā:** Uśīra — the fragrant root of the grass *Vīraṇa* — is one of the most celebrated cooling dravyas of the classical canon. It is a keystone of *dāha-praśamana* (relief of burning), *tṛṣṇā-nigrahaṇa* (thirst control), *rakta-pitta* (haemorrhagic/heat disorders), *mūrcchā* (fainting from heat), and *jvara* (fever), especially the *pitta-jvara* of summer. Suśruta places it among the cooling, thirst-quenching, and haemostatic groupings, and it is a standard ingredient of *ṣaḍaṅga-pānīya* (the six-herb cooling water) and of *Uśīrādi* type formulations used to bring down internal heat.

**Bṛhat-trayī and pharmacopoeial continuity (Caraka / Vāgbhaṭa):** Uśīra recurs across the *Pittahara*, *tṛṣṇānigrahaṇa*, *dāhapraśamana*, *stanyaśodhana* (breast-milk-purifying), and *varṇya* (complexion) uses. It is worn, infused into drinking water, woven into cooling screens (*khas* mats hung and wetted in hot season), and applied as *lepa* for burning skin, prickly heat, and inflammatory swelling. As a fragrant root (*Sugandhimūla*) it also serves *saumanasya* — gladdening of mind — and is used in incense, cosmetics, and calming preparations.

**Ethnobotanical breadth:** Beyond Āyurveda, vetiver root and its steam-distilled essential oil are used pan-tropically as perfumery fixatives, coolants, and topical antimicrobials.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Cool Root Beneath the Fire:**
Uśīra is Kenetica's emblem of *cooling without deadening*. Fire (Pitta) is the transforming principle — necessary, but ruinous in excess: it burns the blood, parches the fluids, scatters the mind into irritability and haste. Uśīra is the substance that meets fire not with more force but with *depth and moisture* — it is a root, and roots draw coolness and water from below. Symbolically it is the *hidden reservoir*: the calm, damp, fragrant ground beneath the surface heat.

Its double signature — deeply cooling yet fragrant and mind-gladdening — makes it the archetype of *composed coolness*, distinct from a merely sedating chill. Where a suppressant simply dampens, Uśīra *re-composes*: it quells the burning while lifting the mood, the way a scented, water-cooled room in the peak of summer restores not just the body but the temper. In the training arc it governs the **de-escalation phase** — the deliberate downshift from heat, overexertion, and inflammatory intensity back to a stable, fragrant baseline. It teaches that true cooling is not collapse but *conservation of moisture and equanimity*.

### Active Components

The steam-distilled **essential oil of vetiver root** is a complex mixture dominated by **sesquiterpenes and sesquiterpene alcohols/ketones**, characteristically including **khusimol**, **khusimone**, **vetiselinenol/isovalencenol**, **α- and β-vetivone**, **vetivenes**, and related **zizaene/khusinol-type** constituents — the profile responsible for the deep, woody-earthy fragrance and the reported bioactivities. The root also carries **triterpenoids, phenolics/flavonoids, and tannins** consistent with the astringent, antioxidant character.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

- **Deep cooling of Pitta heat (dāha-praśamana):** the flagship use — burning sensations, prickly heat, inflammatory heat, heat-fever.
- **Thirst and fluid conservation (tṛṣṇāhara):** infused cooling water for dehydration and heat-thirst.
- **Haemostatic / heat-in-blood (rakta-pitta):** cooling-astringent support in bleeding-heat states.
- **Fragrant calming (saumanasya):** the aroma supports downshift, calm, and composure — vetiver oil in aromatic and topical calming preparations.
- **Topical antimicrobial / skin-cooling:** vetiver oil in formulations for skin and cooling care.

Phase role: the **cooling / de-escalation** substance par excellence, and a fragrant grounding agent.

### Scientific Correlation

*According to PubMed:*

- **Kurrimboccus et al., 2021, *Planta Medica* — antimicrobial anti-acne formulation.** *Chrysopogon zizanioides* (vetiver) essential oil showed notable antimicrobial activity (mean MIC ≈ 0.14 mg/mL) against acne-associated organisms, and an emulsified oil-in-water lotion at optimised HLB 9 remained stable and antimicrobially effective over an 84-day test period — supporting the traditional topical/antimicrobial use of vetiver root oil. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-1699-3284)
- **Sivakumar et al., 2020/2021, *Z. Naturforsch. C* — vasorelaxation.** Vetiver root essential oil (as a nanoemulsion) produced significant relaxation of phenylephrine-precontracted rat thoracic aortic rings, acting through a muscarinic pathway and as a calcium-channel blocker — offering a preclinical mechanistic correlate to the traditional use of the root in hypertension and heat/circulatory disorders. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/znc-2020-0143)

Evidence grade: **preclinical (in vitro antimicrobial; ex vivo vascular pharmacology).** These are laboratory and tissue-level findings, not human clinical trials — they lend mechanistic plausibility to the traditional cooling/antimicrobial/circulatory uses but do not establish clinical efficacy or dosing in people. No human-efficacy percentages should be claimed.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa:** tikta (bitter), madhura (sweet), kaṣāya (astringent)
- **Guṇa:** laghu (light), rūkṣa (dry), sometimes cited snigdha in oil form
- **Vīrya:** śīta (intensely cooling) — a defining feature
- **Vipāka:** kaṭu (pungent)
- **Doṣa action:** strongly Pitta-śāmaka; Kapha-śāmaka; Vāta-vardhaka if overused (cooling-drying)
- **Karma:** dāhapraśamana (heat-quelling), tṛṣṇāhara (thirst-relieving), jvaraghna (antipyretic), raktapittahara, stambhana (checking/astringent), varṇya (complexion), saumanasya-kara (mind-gladdening), dīpana (mildly kindling despite coolness, per some texts)
- **Srotas / dhātu:** rakta and rasa primarily; used in raktavaha and udakavaha (fluid) srotas; supports twak (skin)
- **Gaṇa / formulary:** ingredient of *ṣaḍaṅga-pānīya*, *Uśīrādi*, and cooling Pittahara compounds

### Contraindications

- **Cold / Vāta-predominant constitutions and conditions:** the strong cooling-drying nature can aggravate cold, dryness, and Vāta symptoms; avoid or balance with warming, unctuous anupāna.
- **Deep-cold digestive weakness:** excessive cooling may further dampen an already weak agni.
- **Essential oil:** never apply undiluted; avoid internal use of the concentrated oil without qualified supervision; standard essential-oil cautions in pregnancy and for infants apply. [USER: verify]
- **No validated human oral dose** for the isolated oil — root infusions are the traditional internal form.
