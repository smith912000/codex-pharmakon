## Section 1 — Nomenclature

### Primary Scientific Name
*Reynoutria multiflora* (Thunb.) Moldenke — accepted name (syn. *Polygonum multiflorum* Thunb., *Fallopia multiflora* (Thunb.) Haraldson) (Polygonaceae)

### Nomenclature Table

| Tradition | Language | Name | Transliteration | Meaning |
|-----------|----------|------|-----------------|---------|
| Chinese (SBJ) | 文言文 | 何首烏 | Hé Shǒu Wū | "Mr. He's black-haired [head]" — legend of the court official He who recovered hair colour and vitality using this root |
| Chinese (processed) | 文言文 | 制何首烏 | Zhì Hé Shǒu Wū | "Processed he shou wu" — black bean-steamed form used as tonic |
| Chinese (raw) | 文言文 | 生何首烏 | Shēng Hé Shǒu Wū | "Raw he shou wu" — the unprocessed root; used as laxative/detox only |
| Chinese | 文言文 | 夜交藤 | Yè Jiāo Téng | "Night-twining vine" — the aerial stem; used for insomnia |
| Arabic (CM) | العربية | — | — | Islamic medicine references a root with similar applications to Jing tonification contexts |
| English | — | He Shou Wu / Fo-Ti | — | "Fo-Ti" is a Western commercial name with no traditional basis |

### Botanical Identity
Perennial climbing herb; tuberised taproot (the drug); aerial stems twining, 2–4m; small white/pink flowers in summer; extensively cultivated in Henan, Sichuan, Guizhou provinces, China. **Critical processing distinction**: Raw root (*sheng*) contains anthraquinones (primarily emodin) — laxative, potentially hepatotoxic with chronic use. Processed root (*zhi*) — repeatedly steamed with black beans — converts anthraquinones, dramatically reduces toxicity, and transforms pharmacological activity from purgative to tonic.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ)

This is the primary tradition for He Shou Wu — the plant is not systematically attested in Western or South Asian texts.

*Zhì Hé Shǒu Wū* (processed):
> *"Bitter, sweet, astringent; slightly warm. Enters Liver and Kidney channels. Nourishes Liver and Kidney Yin, tonifies the Blood and Jing, darkens the hair and beard, strengthens the sinews and bones, prevents premature ageing. Used for Kidney and Liver deficiency patterns: dizziness, blurred vision, spermatorrhoea, premature greying, weakness of the lower back, chronic malaria."*

*Shēng Hé Shǒu Wū* (raw):
> *"Bitter, sweet; cold. Detoxifies, moistens the intestines, promotes bowel movements. Used for constipation, wind-damp-toxin conditions, carbuncles."*

*Yè jiāo téng* (vine/stems):
> *"Sweet; neutral. Nourishes the Heart Blood, calms the Shen, promotes sleep, unblocks channels. Used for insomnia from Blood deficiency, anxiety, and musculoskeletal pain."*

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Islamic medicine includes preparations addressing the Jing-equivalent (*manī* = seminal essence) and premature ageing concerns, with substances of similar therapeutic category. Direct attestation of *P. multiflorum* is uncertain; the application domain is consistent.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

| Compound | Content | Activity |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Stilbene glycosides** (THSG — 2,3,5,4'-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-β-D-glucoside) | 1–3% | Primary active in *zhi* form; SIRT1 activator (longevity pathway); antioxidant; neuroprotective; anti-ageing |
| **Emodin** | Anthraquinone | Laxative (raw form); anti-cancer (in vitro); hepatotoxic in chronic high doses |
| **Rhein, physcion** | Anthraquinones | Laxative; potentially hepatotoxic |
| **Proanthocyanidins** (epicatechin, etc.) | Tannins | Antioxidant; anti-inflammatory; cardiovascular |
| **Chrysophanol** | Anthraquinone | Anti-inflammatory; hepatotoxic at high dose |
| **Lecithin** | Phospholipid | Memory support; liver health |
| **Polydatin** (resveratrol glucoside) | Stilbene | Anti-inflammatory; antioxidant; cardiovascular |

**THSG** is the primary tonic compound of processed He Shou Wu — a resveratrol analogue that activates SIRT1 (the primary longevity-associated deacetylase). The processing step converts and reduces anthraquinones while enriching stilbene glycoside content.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Anti-ageing / SIRT1 Activation

**THSG**: Activates SIRT1 → deacetylation of p53 and NF-κB → reduced cellular ageing and senescence. SIRT1 activation extends lifespan in multiple model organisms — the same target as resveratrol (which THSG resembles structurally). AMPK activation → mitochondrial biogenesis → anti-ageing metabolic benefit.

**Telomere effects**: Some evidence for THSG → hTERT upregulation → telomere maintenance. This would partially validate the traditional "extends life" claims.

### 5.2 Neuroprotective / Memory

THSG → protects neurons from β-amyloid toxicity. Reduces neuroinflammation via NF-κB suppression. Increases BDNF in hippocampus → supports memory and learning. The sedative/sleep benefit of the vine (*yè jiāo téng*) is mechanistically distinct — mediated by serotonin receptor modulation and GABA enhancement.

### 5.3 Cardiovascular / Lipid

THSG + proanthocyanidins: antioxidant protection of LDL → anti-atherogenic. Resveratrol/polydatin component → endothelial NO enhancement → vasodilation. Lecithin → phospholipid delivery → HDL-raising effect.

### 5.4 Hair / Melanogenesis

**THSG**: Activates melanocytes via MC1R (melanocortin-1 receptor) pathway → stimulates melanogenesis → potential hair darkening. This is the pharmacological basis of the iconic "darkens greying hair" traditional application — not fully validated in humans but mechanistically plausible.

### 5.5 Hepatotoxicity (Important Safety Mechanism)

**Emodin, rhein (raw or inadequately processed)**:
- Inhibit mitochondrial electron transport chain → hepatocellular energy failure → hepatocyte necrosis
- CYP450 mediated reactive metabolites → idiosyncratic hepatotoxicity in susceptible individuals
- This is a class-known mechanism for anthraquinone hepatotoxicity — the same concern as with senna at high/chronic doses

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Anti-ageing / cognitive**: Multiple animal studies strongly support THSG's SIRT1-mediated anti-ageing effects; limited large-scale human RCTs.

**Lipid-lowering**: Some human studies show significant LDL reduction with standardised THSG extract.

**Hair darkening**: Traditional evidence strong; human clinical trials limited; melanogenesis mechanism pharmacologically supported.

**CRITICAL — Hepatotoxicity**: Multiple case reports of serious liver injury from He Shou Wu preparations — including liver failure requiring transplant. The WHO Uppsala Monitoring Centre has received numerous reports. The risk appears dose-dependent, duration-dependent, and possibly idiosyncratic (CYP2C19 polymorphism may increase risk). **This is the most significant safety signal for any herb in the Jing tonic category.**

---

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The legend as pharmacology**: The name "He Shou Wu" encodes a legend — an elderly impotent official named He took this root, recovered his vigour, and grew black hair again, fathering children in old age. This legend is the pharmacological observation of the herb's effects (testosterone-related? SIRT1? melanogenesis?) rendered as personal narrative. Classical medicine frequently transmitted clinical observations through such stories precisely because narrative is more memorable than lists — a mnemonic device for pharmacological knowledge.

**The processing transformation**: The transformation of a toxic laxative root (raw, anthraquinone-dominant) into a Jing tonic (processed, THSG-dominant) through repeated steaming with black beans is a remarkable pharmacological processing achievement. The processing selectively degrades anthraquinones while concentrating stilbenes — ancient Chinese pharmaceutical practice was conducting organic chemistry 1,000 years before the discipline existed.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

**CRITICAL**: Only **processed** (*zhì*) He Shou Wu should be used for tonic purposes. Raw root should not be used for long-term consumption.

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Processed root decoction (*zhi*) | 9–15g per decoction | Classical tonic; hair, Jing, anti-ageing |
| Standardised THSG extract | 50–100mg THSG/day | Modern clinical approach; lower hepatotoxicity risk |
| *Yè jiāo téng* (vine) decoction | 15–30g | Insomnia, Blood deficiency anxiety |
| In classic formula *qi bao mei ran dan* | — | "Seven treasure hair formula" — traditional anti-greying compound |

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Bitter, sweet, astringent; slightly warm (processed). Channels: Liver (肝), Kidney (腎). Actions: Tonify Liver and Kidney Yin/Jing, nourish Blood, blacken hair, strengthen sinews. Classic Jing tonic alongside Rehmannia.

### 8.3 Contraindications

**SEVERE**:
- **Hepatotoxicity risk**: Do not use raw *sheng* form for extended periods. Processed *zhì* form has significantly reduced but non-zero risk. Monitor liver enzymes if using > 4 weeks.
- **Liver disease**: Absolute contraindication in pre-existing liver disease
- **CYP2C19 poor metabolisers**: Higher hepatotoxicity risk — avoid if known
- **Anthraquinone sensitivity**: Cross-reactivity with other anthraquinone-containing plants (senna, rhubarb, aloe laxatives)
- **Duration limitation**: Limit *zhì* form use to 12 weeks continuous maximum; rest periods essential
