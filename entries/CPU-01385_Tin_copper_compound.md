# Tin-copper compound — Xi Tong Jing Bi (printed in Yang 1998's OCR as "Xi Tong ling Bi", "Compound of Tin & Copper")
## *A tin-and-copper material in Yang's gloss; Yang's own note 74 calls it "a compound of lead and copper from which mirrors were made in olden times" and adds that tin was then often confused with lead. No Chinese characters are printed in the text read* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The locus is Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 English translation of the *Divine Farmer's Materia Medica Classic* (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, page marker [[p35]], line 1350). The page is badly damaged in the OCR text on disk. Line 1350 reads: "Xi Tong ling Bi (Compound of Tin &t Copper)74 mainly tn."ats fenlales' blood block, concretions and conKlomerations lying in the intpstines, and infertility." Reading through the damage, and marked as my reading of corrupted text, the entry says the material mainly treats females' blood block, concretions and conglomerations lying in the intestines, and infertility. The next lines (1352-1355) continue: "[Galenite] is also named Tie Xi (Disinte~ratable l'in). It is produced in the mountains and valleys of Gui Yang." Yang's note 75 gives Gui Yang as "present-day Chenzhou, Hunan Province". The spelling "jing" in this entry's heading is the project's rendering; the OCR reads "ling", and the Chinese characters are not printed in the file read, so the romanisation could not be checked against the original. **Yang's note 74** (lines 1365-1367): "This was a compound of lead and copper from which mirrors were made in olden times. In those times, tin was often confused with lead." The text therefore carries two descriptions of one drug: the body text and heading say tin and copper, the note says lead and copper, and the bracketed "[Galenite]" in the line after is a further gloss whose relation to either is not explained. All three are the translator's. Which metals the original meant is a question the sources read do not settle. [USER: verify]

**Position in the classic.** The entry stands in the minerals section between the Inferior Class "Jades and Stones" run (page 34 carries the running head "Jades and Stones: Inferior Class", line 1311, with *Bai Wu*, *Qian Dan* and *Fen Xi* before it) and the entries *Shi Hui* (limestone) and *Dong Hui* (ash) that follow it on the same page; the "Herbs: Superior Class" begins on the next page (line 1382). No class label is printed on page 35 itself in the OCR, so the grade given below rests on position.

### Active Components

Stated generally, not from the text: an alloy of copper with tin is bronze; an alloy of copper with lead is a leaded bronze, and mirror metal in the ancient world was commonly a copper-based alloy. The text does not give the metal proportions or any preparation, so no composition can be asserted for this drug. Copper and lead compounds are both toxic in sufficient amount, and lead has no biological function.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records the material for women's blood block, abdominal concretions and conglomerations, and infertility, as a drug of the lower grade. It is a metallic material whose identity is uncertain even in the translator's notes, and one of the possible identities is a lead alloy. Nothing here supports ingesting it, and no modern protocol is supplied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested by position: lower grade.* In Yang's preface the *san pin* three-grade scheme is defined, and the inferior class (下品) are the "125 inferior class medicinals which are used as assistants and envoys. They mainly treat disease and correspond to earth. They are usually toxic and cannot be taken for a long time" (lines 391-393; the preface is at lines 352-395). This entry falls inside the run of minerals that the running head on page 34 places in the "Jades and Stones: Inferior Class" and ends before the "Herbs: Superior Class" begins (line 1382), so it is a drug for a defined complaint and not one taken to nourish life. The grade is by position in a damaged OCR text, not from a printed label on the entry. Nothing is claimed about *jing*, *qi* or *shen* for this drug, and the *Qian Dan* entry's remark about "the spirit light" (line 1320) belongs to that drug and is not carried across. Neidan overlays are not a *bencao* category and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: for a defined complaint, for a short course, and then stopped, if it were used at all. The identity is uncertain (tin, lead or both, with copper), and the tradition's own grading is the warning. Nothing here supports taking it for cultivation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Contraindications

- **Toxic metals:** lead and copper compounds are toxic; the possibility that the material was a lead alloy alone is a reason not to ingest it.
- **Pregnancy and conception:** the classic's own indication is infertility; lead is harmful to a developing pregnancy and to fertility.
- **Course length:** the classic's preface says that inferior-class drugs are usually toxic and not to be taken for a long time.
- **Antiques:** ancient mirrors are archaeological objects; collecting or damaging them may be regulated. [USER: verify]
