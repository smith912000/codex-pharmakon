# Ghee (clarified butter) — Ghritam (as printed in Bhishagratna's English), "Clarified Butter"
## *Clarified butter, the milk fat of the animal whose milk it is made from (cow's ghee is called the best in the Sushruta text)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Ghee is described in the *Sushruta Samhita*, *Sutrasthana* chapter 45, the chapter "on liquid substances in general" (*Drava-Dravya-Vidhi*), in Bhishagratna's English translation (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`, lines 775-796). The entry opens "Clarified Butter (Ghritam)": it "is Saumya or cooling in its essence and potency, and is mild and sweet. It slightly increases the slimy secretions of the organs, and acts as a lubricating moistener". The text names the complaints it is "efficacious in" (rendered by Bhishagratna as *Udavarta*, insanity, epilepsy, colic, chronic fever, and distention of the abdomen from suppression of stool and urine), says it is "appetising and subdues the Vayu and the Pittam", and that it "improves memory, intelligence, complexion, voice, personal beauty, amiability of features and the principle of strength (albumen, Ojas) in the body". It is "vitalising, rejuvenating, spermatopoietic and heavy", "improves the eyesight, increases the quantity of bodily Kapham and the duration of life". The same passage says it "is sacred and is regarded as an appeaser of adverse fate. It eliminates poison from the body and wards off the invasions of monsters and demons." Those last lines are the text's own and are recorded, not endorsed. The English words "rejuvenating" and "spermatopoietic" are Bhishagratna's renderings; the Sanskrit terms are not printed in the file read, so which formal term each translates is not confirmed. The chapter then separates ghee by the animal whose milk it is made from (lines 797-848). Cow's ghee is "sweet in digestion, and cool in its potency", subdues deranged *Vayu* and *Pittam*, eliminates poison, improves eyesight and has "excellent tonic and invigorating properties"; "Cow-butter, in its clarified state, is the best of all kinds of butter." Goat's ghee is appetising, eye-invigorating and strength-increasing, wholesome in cough, dyspnoea and consumption, and light in digestion. Buffalo's ghee is sweet, heavy and remedial in haemoptysis. Camel's is anti-toxic and pungent in digestion. Ewe's is light and does not enrage *Pittam*. Mare's is light, heat-making in potency and astringent. Woman's milk ghee "should be regarded as the prototype of divine ambrosia on earth". She-elephant's ghee is astringent in taste and curative in skin affections and worms. Butter "churned out of thickened milk and clarified" (*Kshira Ghritam*) is astringent and beneficial in eye diseases, haemoptysis, epileptic fits and vertigo. The "condensed upper stratum of clarified butter" (*Ghrita-manda*) is a laxative, cures aching pain in the vagina, ears, eyes or head, and is used as an errhine, enema or eye-drops (lines 849-862). These are the text's own differentiations and are listed here so that nothing in the locus is lost; the folded variety the batch lists is old ghee, treated below.

Ghee also appears as a vehicle in the *Chikitsasthana*. *Medhayushkamiya Rasayana*, ch.28 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch28.txt`, line 1-9, "elixirs and remedial agents which tend to improve the memory and invigorate the mental faculties as well as to increase the duration of human life"), gives *Brahmi Ghrita* (line 146) and *Sata-paka Vacha-Ghrita* (line 209). In the *Vajikarana* chapter, ch.26 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt`, line 1-6, "increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons"), ghee is an ingredient or accompaniment in several recipes (for example *Vidari* bulb powder "taken with milk and clarified butter", about lines 142-145).

### Varieties

**Old clarified butter** (SS; *Sutrasthana* ch.45, line 863, with the verses at lines 872-890). Under cow's-milk ghee and the other ghees above, the text gives an aged form. "Old clarified butter is laxative and pungent in digestion. It subdues the three deranged bodily humours, and proves curative in epileptic fits, obesity, insanity, abdominal dropsy, fever, chemical poisoning, oedema, hysteria, and in aching pain in the vagina, ears, eyes or head. It is appetising and is recommended to be used as eye-drops and enema, and for sternutatory purposes." The verses add that old or matured ghee "proves curative in *Timira* (Gutta Serena), dyspnoea, catarrh, fever, cough, epileptic fits, and *Kushtam*, in cases of poisoning, mental aberration, and hysteria ascribed to the influence of malignant planets". Ghee "matured from eleven to a hundred years is called the *Kumbha Ghritam* (Pitcher clarified butter)", and that older than this is *Maha Ghritam* (the great clarified butter). *Kumbha Ghritam* "is said to be possessed of the mystic potency of warding off the invasions of monsters", while *Maha Ghritam* "is highly efficacious, sacred, and specifically curative" in *Timira*, "acts as a prophylactic against the malignant influences of all evil spirits and baneful planets, and should be taken by men in whom Vayu predominates. It subdues the deranged Kapham, and improves the strength and intellect." (The translation's wording "from eleven to a hundred years" is quoted as printed.)

*Synthesis of the varieties.* The Sushruta text separates ghee on two grounds only: the source animal (cow, goat, buffalo, camel, ewe, mare, woman, she-elephant) and the stage of treatment or age (fresh clarified butter, the condensed upper layer *Ghrita-manda*, butter clarified from thickened milk, and old ghee with its named grades *Kumbha* and *Maha*). It ranks cow's ghee first among butters. It gives old ghee a different digestive effect from fresh (pungent where cow's ghee is sweet in digestion) and a wider range of uses including the grade-names. The text does not rank the aged grades against the animal-source ghees on a single scale. Only the Sushruta text is tagged for this entry, so no cross-tradition comparison is made.

### Active Components

Ghee is milk fat from which water and milk solids have been removed by heating and straining. It is therefore overwhelmingly triglycerides (a mixture of saturated, monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fatty acids), including the short-chain fatty acid butyric acid that is characteristic of ruminant milk fat. It also carries small amounts of the fat-soluble vitamins found in milk fat (A, D, E, K) and some cholesterol, in amounts that depend on the animal's feed and the method of making. Because most water and protein are removed, it contains little lactose or milk protein, though not none. No figures are given here, because none were read in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

The record is Ayurvedic and textual. Sushruta's ghee is both a food-like substance and the base ("*Sneha*") into which other drugs are cooked, and the Codex's task here is to report that record. As an ordinary cooking fat, ghee is a lawful, commonplace food in many cuisines and needs no special statement. As a therapeutic or *rasayana* practice, the Codex gives no protocol: the sources retrieved supply recipes for medicated ghritas with named ingredients, but no dose for plain ghee, and nothing here is a recommendation to take ghee in quantity for any condition. The medicated ghritas, in particular, depend on authenticated plant ingredients and a stated method, and several Sushruta recipes also add animal parts (see Preparation & Dosing); they are not recommended practices in this entry. The text itself attributes mystical effects (warding off monsters, demons and baneful planets, appeasing adverse fate); these are the text's own claims and the Codex records them without endorsing them.

### Preparation & Dosing

- **Plain ghee:** no dose is given in ch.45 (lines 775-796). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- ***Brahmi Ghrita*** (ch.28, line 146): "Two Prastha measures of the expressed juice of the Brahmi and one Prastha measure of clarified butter should be cooked with one Kudava measure of Vidanga seeds, two Pala weight of each of Vacha and Trivrit, and twelve (in number) of each of Haritaki, Amalaka and Vibhitaka well pounded and mixed together." It is kept "in a covered pitcher" and "taken in adequate doses as in the preceding manner"; after the medicine is digested the patient takes "boiled rice, clarified butter and milk". The preceding recipe's dosing is not reproduced here.
- ***Sata-paka Vacha-Ghrita*** (ch.28, line 209): "Clarified butter should be cooked a hundred times in succession with an adequate quantity of Vacha. The use of a Drona measure of this medicated Ghrita (taken every day in an adequate dose) extends the earthly career of its user to five centuries, and proves beneficial in cases of scrofula, goitre, elephantiasis and hoarseness." The text's claim of five hundred years is quoted, not endorsed. "Adequate dose" is the text's phrase; no per-day quantity is given.
- ***Vajikarana* (ch.26):** one recipe has ghee boiled with "the eggs or the testes (as the case might be) of alligators, mice, frogs and sparrows", with the soles of the feet anointed (about lines 152-156). It is recorded as the text's content and is not a recommended practice.
- **Sneha cooking degrees** (ch.31, about lines 192-196): the text distinguishes mild (*Mridu*), middling (*Madhyama*) and hard (*Khara*) cooking of a *Sneha* (oil or ghrita). The criteria were not read in detail. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only: ghee is a concentrated dietary fat, high in saturated fat, and so high in energy; it can be heated to higher temperatures than butter before smoking because the milk solids are removed; and the fat-soluble vitamins it carries are carried by the fat. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Qualities printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.45: ghee is *Saumya* "cooling in its essence and potency, and is mild and sweet" (line 775); cow's ghee is "sweet in digestion, and cool in its potency" (about line 797-799); old ghee is "laxative and pungent in digestion" (line 863). It "subdues the Vayu and the Pittam" (fresh), and old ghee "subdues the three deranged bodily humours". Other animals' ghees carry their own listed qualities (for example mare's ghee is "heat-making in its potency"; camel's "pungent in digestion"). No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* (Graded (b), not (a), on spot-read: the rasayana and vajikarana chapters give the title to compound ghrita recipes, and ch.45 gives ghee qualities without assigning it a status.) Ghee is named in Bhishagratna's English of *Sutrasthana* ch.45 (line 775-796) as improving "the principle of strength (albumen, Ojas)", and as "vitalising, rejuvenating, spermatopoietic" and increasing "the duration of life"; old ghee grades (*Kumbha*, *Maha*) are given a further set of uses (lines 863-890). It is the base of *Brahmi Ghrita* and *Sata-paka Vacha-Ghrita* in the *Medhayushkamiya Rasayana* chapter (*Chikitsasthana* ch.28, lines 146 and 209), where the medicine is taken with ghee-and-milk meals afterwards, and it appears in recipes in the *Vajikarana* chapter (ch.26). The qualification is that the *rasayana* and *vajikarana* chapters attach those titles to compound recipes, not to plain ghee by itself; the status attaches to the ghrita as a carrier, and the text itself ascribes ojas and long life to ghee in ch.45. The words "rejuvenating" and "spermatopoietic" are the translator's renderings, and the Sanskrit terms were not read. [USER: verify] *Ojas* is used here only in the sense Bhishagratna's gloss prints it in ch.45. *Sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas* are *Samkhya*-era categories and are not used: Sushruta's text does not assign ghee to them in any passage read.

**Practitioner's note.** In the Sushruta record, ghee is a food and a medicinal base, and the cultivation claims attach to specific medicated ghritas taken as courses under a physician, and to ghee's general ranking as a nourishing, cooling, ojas-improving fat. For a practitioner today the practical content is modest: ghee is an ordinary cooking fat with a long Ayurvedic reputation, and the Codex offers no dose, course or long-life protocol for it. The five-hundred-year and ward-off-demons claims are not a basis for any practice.

### Contraindications

- **Dairy sensitivity:** clarification removes most milk protein and lactose, not all of it (general fact); people with a milk allergy should take medical advice.
- **Dietary fat and heart disease:** ghee is a concentrated saturated fat; a person with a lipid disorder or advised to limit saturated fat should ask a clinician. [USER: verify]
- **The text's own cautions:** it states ghee "increases the quantity of bodily Kapham", and the text's heavy, moistening qualities suggest caution where that is unwanted; the chapters read give no formal contraindication list.
- **Spoilage and storage:** old ghee in the text is aged deliberately; ordinary ghee that smells rancid should not be eaten (general fact). Aging ghee for years is a traditional preparation, not a recommendation.
- **Medicated ghritas and animal additions:** the plant and animal additions in the recipes read have their own toxicity, legal and ethical issues, handled in their own entries; they are not endorsed here.
