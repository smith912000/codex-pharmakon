### Origin & Tradition

**Identity warning.** This row was added 2026-10-08 by an extraction ruling (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §TTT, "Two-text finds"): the same binomial is printed in both texts. *Chapters naming Putika and Puti-karanja.* These were found by search and are not identified in the chapters themselves:

- **Sutra ch.46** (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 1760 to 1763): "The Putikaranjas are heat-making in their potency and alleviate oedema and anasarca. They are pungent in digestion, easily digestible and subdue the Vayu and the Kapham."
- **Sutra ch.39** (`SS_Sutra_ch39.txt`, line 27 and line 43): *Putika* is in the list of drugs "possessed of purgative properties" (lines 20 to 29), and "the leaves of Putika and Aragvadha" are named among the parts used (lines 40 to 44).
- **Sutra ch.38** (`SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, line 46 and line 80): *Putika* is a member of the Varunadi group and of the Salasaradi group.
- **Chikitsa ch.14** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch14.txt`, lines 321 to 323): in the chapter that opens on *Udara*, "the alkali of Puti-karanja, filtered with Kanjika and mixed with a copious quantity of Vid salt (black salt) and powdered Pippali should be administered". **Chikitsa ch.6** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch06.txt`, lines 317 to 318): "take the alkaline water of Putika mixed with a Kalka of Chitraka, Putika and Nagara". **Chikitsa ch.19** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch19.txt`, line 421): a translator's note records that Chakradatta reads for this passage that the juice of *Puti-Karanja* leaves is taken with mustard oil.
- **Separation from Karanja.** The commentary note in `SS_Chikitsa_ch12.txt` (line 80) reads: "Udakirya and Prakirya are the two kinds of Karanja". The two kinds of *Karanja* are therefore named as *Udakirya* and *Prakirya* in that note, and *Putika* is not named in that note. *Karanja* is the subject of I_375.

No named rasayana or vajikarana chapter was found to name *Putika* or *Puti-karanja*.

### Active Components

*Caesalpinia bonducella* seeds are generally known to contain bitter cassane-type diterpenoids. The composition of the trees named in the texts was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Sushruta records *Putika* and *Puti-karanja* as purgative, as a leaf, and as an alkali (*kshara*) in formulas, with a heating potency in ch.46, and the Tibetan glossary records *Karanza* as raising digestive heat. These are the texts' own claims and not a Codex protocol. The identification is unsettled, and the Codex gives no protocol for the plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

Sushruta Chikitsa ch.14 (lines 321 to 323) gives an alkali of *Puti-karanja* filtered with *Kanjika* and mixed with black salt and *Pippali* powder; Chikitsa ch.6 (lines 317 to 318) gives the alkaline water of *Putika*; Sutra ch.39 gives the leaves. No quantity for the plant is given in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** Sutra ch.46 (lines 1760 to 1763): the *Putikaranjas* are "heat-making in their potency", "pungent in digestion" and "easily digestible", and they "subdue the Vayu and the Kapham". The group is tied to *C. bonducella* only by the appendix reading above.
- **FMT:** Men-Tsee-Khang line 3394 says that *Karanza* "generates digestive heat". Clark line 917 prints for *karany.ja* (a *Pongamia* entry) "extremely bitter natural and post-digestive tastes".

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* *Putika* appears in purgative lists and in the Varunadi and Salasaradi groups (ch.38 and ch.39), *Putikaranja* has a property line in the dietary chapter (ch.46), and both appear as ingredients in formulas, with *Puti-karanja* as an alkali in Chikitsa ch.14. Group membership in ch.38 is a grouping by therapeutic action. It is not named in a rasayana or vajikarana chapter in the files searched, and nothing read ties it to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*. The Sushruta frame is therapeutic and purificatory. The identification with *C. bonducella* rests on an OCR-paired appendix. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The tradition's text names *Karanza* by a power (it generates digestive heat), and Clark's ch.20 entry for the same phonetic name is a different tree. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice and not reaching the root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. Nothing read assigns the plant a place in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice. The glossary pairing with *C. bonducella* is by column order only. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. In Sushruta it is a purgative and an alkali ingredient and in the Tibetan texts a digestive-heat medicine, under names that two of the texts apply to other trees. The abstentive answer is the correct one: take the identification as unsettled and do not use it on the strength of this entry.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. The seeds of some *Caesalpinia* species are bitter and are used as traditional medicines, and purgative and alkali preparations can irritate the gut. None of the traditional uses above is advice. Seek a qualified practitioner, and seek ordinary medical care for abdominal swelling, fluid retention or digestive illness. Do not use in pregnancy. Wild plants of this thorny climber should not be collected by amateurs.
