# Dandelion — 蒲公英 (Pú Gōng Yīng) / Mongolian Dandelion

## *Taraxacum mongolicum* Hand.-Mazz. — the bitter rosette that clears the fire of swelling and toxin

**Energetic Classification (TCM):** ◉ Qi

**Training Phase:** `[GENERAL]`

### Origin & Tradition

*Taraxacum mongolicum* (Asteraceae) is a perennial rosette herb of northern China, Mongolia, and adjacent temperate Asia — the species that supplies the great bulk of the Chinese drug *pú gōng yīng* ("dandelion"). Like its cosmopolitan relative *T. officinale*, it is a low, lactiferous plant with deeply toothed leaves, a hollow scape, a yellow ligulate head, and a milky latex; the whole plant, root and aerial parts together, is gathered in flower and dried as the medicinal material.

Where the European tradition prized dandelion chiefly as a bitter hepatic and diuretic — the "piss-a-bed" of the apothecaries — the Chinese tradition foregrounds a different action: dandelion is one of the principal herbs that **clear heat and resolve toxin** (*qīng rè jiě dú*), and above all the classical remedy for *rǔ yōng* — acute mastitis, the hot, swollen, suppurating breast abscess. Its bitter-cold profile, combined with a capacity to disperse hard, hot swellings and to move the milk-channels, made it the sovereign herb for inflammatory breast disease, applied both internally and as a fresh poultice.

**SBJ (Bencao tradition):** Dandelion is classified as bitter, sweet, and cold, entering the Liver and Stomach channels. Its primary actions are recorded as clearing heat and resolving fire-toxin (*qīng rè jiě dú*), reducing swelling and dissipating nodules (*xiāo zhǒng sàn jié*), and — the action for which it is most famous — treating *rǔ yōng* (mastitis / breast abscess) and other hot sores and swellings. A secondary action, promoting urination and resolving damp-heat (*lì shī tōng lín*), draws heat downward and out through the urine; and through its Liver entry it is used for damp-heat affecting the liver and gallbladder, with a cholagogue, jaundice-clearing reputation that parallels the Western hepatic-bitter use.

**CM / Western correlate:** The bitter, latex-bearing dandelion is documented across Eurasian folk medicine as a cholagogue and diuretic bitter for sluggish digestion, hepatic congestion, and water retention — a phytochemically reasonable convergence with the Chinese Liver-Stomach damp-heat indication.

**BM (Mongolian / steppe folk medicine):** As a native steppe plant, dandelion is a familiar folk bitter and heat-clearing food-medicine, eaten as a spring potherb and used for hot inflammatory complaints and digestive sluggishness.

### Active Components

- **Sesquiterpene lactones** — taraxinic acid and related germacranolide-type lactones characteristic of *Taraxacum*; the principal bitter principles, contributing the cholagogue/digestive-bitter action and anti-inflammatory activity
- **Triterpenes and sterols** — taraxasterol, taraxerol, ψ-taraxasterol, β-sitosterol; anti-inflammatory triterpene fraction
- **Phenolic acids** — chicoric acid (dicaffeoyltartaric acid), chlorogenic acid, caffeic acid; antioxidant and hepatoprotective phenylpropanoids
- **Flavonoids** — luteolin and luteolin glycosides, and related flavones; anti-inflammatory and antioxidant
- **Inulin** — fructan storage carbohydrate concentrated in the root; prebiotic; the basis of the root's gentle digestive action
- **Potassium** — the leaf is notably potassium-rich, which is relevant to its diuretic profile (potassium-sparing relative to many diuretics)

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Acute inflammatory swelling, hot sores, and localised infection** `[GENERAL]` — Dandelion's defining action — clearing fire-toxin and reducing hot swellings — translates directly to acute inflammatory presentations: hot, red, swollen tissue, infected sores, and inflamed lymph nodes. The luteolin and sesquiterpene-lactone anti-inflammatory fractions provide a mechanistic correlate. The classical fresh poultice for hot swellings remains a practical topical use.

2. **Mastitis and hot breast/pectoral swelling** `[GENERAL]` — The single most established traditional indication; for the breastfeeding population this is its sovereign use, internally and as a poultice.

3. **Hepatobiliary support — cholagogue and damp-heat clearing** `[GENERAL]` — Through its Liver-Stomach entry and bitter sesquiterpene/phenolic profile, dandelion stimulates bile flow and supports hepatic clearance, applicable to the sluggish-digestion, damp-heat, "congested" presentation; the phenolic fraction (chicoric, chlorogenic, caffeic acids) underlies the hepatoprotective/antioxidant reputation.

4. **Gentle diuresis without potassium depletion** `[GENERAL]` — The potassium-rich leaf supports fluid clearance and damp-heat urinary patterns; useful where mild diuresis is wanted without the potassium loss of pharmaceutical loop/thiazide diuretics.

5. **Digestive bitter and prebiotic** `[GENERAL]` — The bitter principles prime digestive secretion; the root's inulin provides a prebiotic fibre supporting gut flora.

### Preparation & Dosing

**Decoction (classical Chinese preparation — primary method):**
- 9–15g dried whole herb (or up to 30g of the fresh-equivalent for acute hot swellings) simmered in water; taken warm in divided doses
- Classically combined for mastitis with herbs such as *jīn yín huā* (Lonicera flower) and *lián qiào* (Forsythia) in heat-toxin-clearing formula

**Fresh poultice (topical, for hot swellings):**
- Fresh whole plant crushed and applied directly over the hot, swollen area; the traditional external treatment for *rǔ yōng* and hot sores

**Infusion / tea (food-tonic and bitter):**
- The dried leaf as a daily bitter tea; the roasted root as a coffee-substitute decoction for digestive and hepatic support

**Culinary:**
- Young leaves eaten as a spring bitter potherb — the gentlest preparation, appropriate for indefinite use

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Primary Tier:** ◉ Qi — a bitter-cold, draining and dispersing substance acting on the functional (Qi) level to clear heat, resolve fire-toxin, move stagnant damp-heat, and dissipate hot swellings and nodules; not a deep tonic (it drains rather than builds)

**TCM Channel:** Liver (肝, Gān), Stomach (胃, Wèi) — primary

**Energetic Action:** Clears heat and resolves fire-toxin (*qīng rè jiě dú*); reduces swelling and dissipates nodules (*xiāo zhǒng sàn jié*) — especially *rǔ yōng* (mastitis); promotes urination and drains damp-heat (*lì shī tōng lín*); clears Liver-Gallbladder damp-heat and benefits jaundice (cholagogue action)

**Western / Galenic correlate:** Cold and dry; a hepatic-bitter cholagogue and diuretic of the "deobstruent" class, for hot, congested, damp states of liver and digestion

### Contraindications

- **Cold/deficiency-cold of the Spleen-Stomach:** As a bitter-cold draining herb, dandelion can aggravate cold-deficient digestion (cold abdominal pain, loose stools from cold, no heat signs); use cautiously or combine with warming herbs
- **Asteraceae sensitivity:** Latex and sesquiterpene lactones carry contact- and ingestion-allergy potential; avoid in known Compositae/daisy-family allergy
- **Biliary obstruction:** As a cholagogue, dandelion is contraindicated in complete bile-duct obstruction or acute gallstone-with-obstruction; use only with diagnosis
- **Potassium-altering / diuretic / lithium co-medication:** Diuretic activity may interact with lithium clearance and with other diuretics; monitor where relevant
- **Pregnancy / lactation:** Widely used and food-grade, including in lactation for mastitis; nonetheless medicinal-dose use in pregnancy warrants the usual caution
