# Marrubium pseudodictamnus — Pseudodiktamnos (Dioscorides 3-38, "Pseudodictamnus"); "White Horehound, Bastard Dittany" on the translators' line

## *Marrubium pseudodictamnus, the first of three binomials on the translators' SUGGESTED line for chapter 3-38 (Marrubium pseudodictamnus, Berringeria pseudodictamnus, Ballota pseudodictamnus), glossed "White Horehound, Bastard Dittany"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk names this plant under this name. The entry rests on one chapter of one text, and that chapter is two sentences long and defined by its comparison with the chapter before it. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed under the chapter heading, and not Dioscorides' own identification. The three binomials on the line are names for one suggestion under three genera (*Marrubium*, *Berringeria*, *Ballota*), and the entry takes the first. The English gloss "White Horehound" is also the gloss of a different chapter, 3-119 *PRASION* (see below). The Codex does not state the identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The heading *3-38. PSEUDODIKTAMNOS* is at line 6857, the SUGGESTED lines at lines 6858 and 6859 and the chapter text at line 6860. The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, row 444. The chapter stands in Book Three ("Roots of Akanthoda or Prickly Plants" in the running head), after 3-37 *DIKTAMNON* (line 6840) and before 3-39 *DIKTAMNOS ALLO* (line 6864). The printed page number 407 follows the chapter, and the translators' indexes list *pseudodictamnus*, *Pseudodictamnus*, *Ballota pseudodictamnus* and "Bastard Dittany" at page 407 (lines 16497, 17162, 14879 and 14884).

The whole chapter reads: "That which is called pseudodictamnus grows in many places and is similar to the one above but less sharp. It does the same things as dictamnus, but is not similarly effective."

So the chapter says three things: that the plant "grows in many places", that it is "similar to the one above but less sharp", and that it "does the same things as dictamnus, but is not similarly effective". It gives no description of its own, no use of its own, no part used, no preparation, no season and no other names. The translators' English name for it is "Bastard Dittany".

**What the chapter points to.** Everything in 3-38 depends on 3-37 *DIKTAMNON* (line 6840), whose translators' identification is *Dictamnus albus* or *Dictamnus fraxinella* with *Origanum dictamnus* ("Dittany of Crete"). That is a different chapter, and it is not folded into this entry. It describes dictamnus as "a Cretian herb — sharp, smooth, similar to pulegium [3-36]", with larger downy leaves and "neither flower nor seed". It "does all the things that the cultivated pulegium does but much more forcibly", and expels dead embryos, "taken as a drink" or "applied and inhaled as smoke". It is "effective for a painful spleen". The root "warms those who taste it", and the juice is taken for the bites of poisonous beasts and dropped into wounds from iron. It is gathered "in the summer and the fall". The chapter also records, with "They say", that Cretan goats that have fed on the herb reject arrows. The text of 3-37 adds a parenthetical paragraph with a different list of gathering seasons ("the spring, the hot seasons, and in the autumn"). The "same things" that 3-38 says the bastard plant does are those. The Codex does not carry any of them over to this plant as a finding. Dioscorides does not say which of the uses the bastard form shares, only that its effect is weaker. [USER: verify]

**An odd cross-reference.** Five other chapters compare a plant to "marrubium" and cite the number 3-38 after the word: 3-109 *KLINOPODION* (line 7515, whose flowers are said to be "similar to marrubium [3-38]"), 3-117 *BALLOTE* (line 7585, "marrubium [3-38, 3-42]"), 3-120 *STACHUS* (line 7627), 4-33 *SIDERITIS* (line 8293) and 4-176 *PUKNOKOMON* (line 9634). The text's own horehound chapter is 3-119 *PRASION* (line 7607; SUGGESTED *Marrubium vulgare*, "Common White Horehound"), and it is not the one these cross-references name. So the translators' [3-38] after "marrubium" points at this chapter, whose SUGGESTED line contains the genus name *Marrubium*. That reads as a cross-reference by name rather than a statement by Dioscorides that pseudodictamnus is the horehound he means. The Codex does not resolve it. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** The chapter 3-38 is not the subject of any existing entry. I_1068 (*Dictamnus albus*, the 3-37 chapter) names 3-38 in its paragraph "Other chapters on the same name (not merged)", summarises it and notes that it has no entry of its own. The nearest entries by genus are I_597 (White Horehound, *Marrubium vulgare*) and I_524 (Black Horehound, *Ballota nigra*), neither of which cites 3-38. The sister chapter of this batch is 3-105 *ALUSSON* (I_1128, *Marrubium alysson*). 3-39 *DIKTAMNOS ALLO* has no entry. None is merged. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The text prints no constituent, and no analysis of the plant Dioscorides describes was read. As a general fact, the horehound genus *Marrubium* belongs to the mint family and is known for bitter diterpenoid lactones (marrubiin is the best known, from white horehound). That is a statement about the genus. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the plant the translators call *Marrubium pseudodictamnus*. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records no use of its own. It records only a ranking: the bastard dittany does "the same things" as dictamnus but less effectively. The uses of dictamnus in 3-37 include the expulsion of a dead embryo, which is a medical matter of the gravest kind. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is two sentences that rank it below dittany of Crete, so the honest answer is that there is nothing here to take up.

### Contraindications

Chapter 3-38 prints no warning. The drug it is compared with in 3-37 is recorded as expelling a dead embryo and as a "birth-hastener", so the chapter gives a reason for caution in pregnancy, and no one should take a plant on that basis. The identification is only the translators' suggestion, and the chapter gives no dose, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. [USER: verify]
