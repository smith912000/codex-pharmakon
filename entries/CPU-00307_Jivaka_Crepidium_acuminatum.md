# Jivaka — Sanskrit: Jīvaka, Jīvana; a Himalayan Aṣṭavarga orchid

## *Crepidium acuminatum* (D.Don) Szlach. (syn. *Malaxis acuminata* D.Don) | Family: Orchidaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SS
**Tags (previous line, retained for reference):** Suśruta Saṃhitā (member of the *Aṣṭavarga* octad; jīvanīya / rejuvenative context, constituent of *rasāyana* and *cyavanaprāśa*); Caraka Saṃhitā (jīvanīya gaṇa association); classical *ghṛta*/*rasāyana* formulae; modern conservation concern (threatened, routinely substituted)

**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Tridoṣaghna with special Vāta-Pitta pacification; *madhura-vipāka*, *śīta-vīrya*, *guru-snigdha* — a bṛṃhaṇa (bulk-and-strength building), jīvanīya (life-promoting) dravya

### Origin & Tradition

**The Aṣṭavarga and Suśruta:** Jīvaka is one of the *Aṣṭavarga* — the "group of eight" Himalayan drugs (Jīvaka, Ṛṣabhaka, Meda, Mahāmeda, Kākolī, Kṣīrakākolī, Ṛddhi, Vṛddhi) that classical Ayurveda prizes as premier *jīvanīya* (life-giving) and *bṛṃhaṇa* (nourishing, tissue-building) agents. In the Suśruta tradition the Aṣṭavarga are core constituents of the great restorative preparations — *rasāyana ghṛta*, medicated milks, and above all *cyavanaprāśa* (Chyavanprash), the flagship immune-and-vitality confection. Jīvaka's very name — from *jīv*, "to live" — marks it as a drug of vitality restoration: it is deployed to rebuild depleted tissue (*dhātukṣaya*), counter emaciation and debility, support ojas and reproductive strength, and quiet Vāta-Pitta heat and dryness.

**Botanical identity & the pairing:** Jīvaka is a small terrestrial orchid of the temperate-to-tropical Himalaya (roughly 1200–2000 m), the dried **pseudobulb** being the officinal drug. It is classically paired with **Ṛṣabhaka** (*Malaxis muscifera*) — the two orchids form one of the four Aṣṭavarga couples, and are so alike and so often confused/co-collected that they are frequently treated as a functional pair. According to PubMed, its role as an ingredient of Aṣṭavarga and Chyavanprash and its status as a threatened orchid are documented by Bose et al. (2017), *J Photochem Photobiol B* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphotobiol.2017.07.010).

**Endangerment & substitution (a living doctrinal problem):** Because the true Aṣṭavarga orchids are slow-growing, wild-harvested, and now threatened, they are among the most heavily **adulterated and substituted** drugs in the Ayurvedic trade. Authentic Jīvaka is scarce; commercial "Aṣṭavarga" is commonly reconstituted with accepted substitutes (*abhāva pratinidhi dravya*) — tuberous plants such as *Malaxis*/other orchids, *Roscoea*, *Habenaria*, or, per classical substitution rules, *Vidārī* (*Pueraria*) and *Aśvagandhā* standing in for the jīvanīya-bṛṃhaṇa action. Any modern use must foreground this identity/sourcing problem.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Orchid of Life in the Octad:** The Aṣṭavarga are Ayurveda's clearest expression of *bṛṃhaṇa-rasāyana* logic: sweet, cool, heavy, unctuous drugs that "fill" a body worn thin by heat, dryness, age, or overexertion. Jīvaka sits at the head of this logic — its name *is* the doctrine (*jīvana* = enlivening). That the two Aṣṭavarga orchids come as a male–female-styled pair (Jīvaka/Ṛṣabhaka, the latter's name evoking the bull, *ṛṣabha*) encodes the Ayurvedic idea that vitality is restored through complementary, mutually completing forces — the octad as a small cosmology of nourishment, four couples building the eight *dhātu*-supporting powers. The mountain provenance is itself symbolic: these are drugs gathered from high, cold, pure ground and brought down to rebuild the depleted lowland body — the Himalaya as the reservoir of ojas. The modern substitution crisis gives the symbol a shadow: the "life-giver" is itself endangered, its identity now partly a matter of faithful surrogacy.

### Active Components
Pseudobulb and aerial parts yield **dietary fatty acids**, **α-hydroxy acids**, **phenolic acids and polyphenols**, **flavonoids**, **sterols (β-sitosterol and related)**, **alkaloids**, sugars and glycosides, and antioxidant secondary metabolites. According to PubMed, GC-MS profiling of *M. acuminata* is reported by Bose et al. (2017) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphotobiol.2017.07.010), and *M. acuminata* is noted as the most chemically-characterised *Malaxis* species (≈103 compounds) in the genus review by Rautela et al. (2024), *Chem Biodivers* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbdv.202301830).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica
Jīvaka is a **rejuvenative / tissue-building (jīvanīya-bṛṃhaṇa)** drug for Kenetica's Rejuvenation and deep-Recovery phases: indicated where training or illness has produced depletion, dryness, low body-mass, and Vāta-Pitta heat — states calling for a sweet, cooling, nourishing rasāyana rather than a stimulant. It belongs to the Consolidation → Rejuvenation arc, ideally as part of an Aṣṭavarga/Chyavanprash-style compound rather than alone. **Because authentic material is threatened and widely adulterated, Kenetica treats Jīvaka as a compound-context / substitute-aware entry** — use validated substitutes where genuine drug is unavailable.

### Scientific Correlation
According to PubMed:
- **Metabolite profile & bioactivity** — Bose et al. (2017), *J Photochem Photobiol B*, report antioxidant capacity, skin-aging-enzyme inhibition (anti-collagenase, -elastase, -tyrosinase), anti-inflammatory (5-LOX, hyaluronidase) and photoprotective activity of *M. acuminata* extracts, with GC-MS identification of bioactive metabolites — offering a mechanistic reading of its "rejuvenator/anti-aging" reputation [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphotobiol.2017.07.010).
- **Genus review** — Rautela et al. (2024), *Chem Biodivers*, systematically compile *Malaxis* phytochemistry (189 compounds across the genus; *M. acuminata* the richest) and traditional uses, and stress conservation/domestication needs [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbdv.202301830).

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical (in vitro antioxidant/enzyme-inhibition and phytochemical profiling) plus a systematic review. No clinical trials on the isolated drug; classical jīvanīya-rasāyana use is traditional, with in-vitro antioxidant/anti-aging data as partial mechanistic support.

### Traditional Energetic Classification
- **Rasa (taste):** Madhura (sweet)
- **Guṇa (quality):** Guru (heavy), Snigdha (unctuous)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Madhura (sweet)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** *Jīvanīya* / *bṛṃhaṇa-rasāyana* — a life-promoting, tissue-building rejuvenative effect central to the Aṣṭavarga
- **Doṣa action:** Pacifies Vāta and Pitta (sweet-cool-unctuous); heavy/unctuous quality can increase Kapha and burden weak digestion — *tridoṣaghna* with a Vāta-Pitta lean
- **Karma (actions):** Jīvanīya (life-promoting), bṛṃhaṇa (nourishing/bulk-building), rasāyana (rejuvenative), balya (strength-giving), vṛṣya/śukrala (reproductive-tonic), dāhapraśamana, ojovardhaka (ojas-building)
- **Gaṇa / group:** *Aṣṭavarga* octad (paired with Ṛṣabhaka)

### Contraindications
Heavy, sweet, unctuous — may aggravate Kapha, low digestive fire (*mandāgni*), *āma* (undigested-toxin) states, obesity, and diabetes/metabolic sluggishness; not for congested or high-Kapha conditions without processing. Safety in pregnancy/lactation not established `[USER: verify]`. **Overriding caution: conservation status** — genuine *Crepidium acuminatum* is a threatened wild orchid; use only sustainably/legally sourced material or classically-sanctioned substitutes, and beware widespread commercial adulteration.
