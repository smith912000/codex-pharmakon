### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda (Suśruta & Caraka):** Śāla is the archetypal hard-timber, resin-yielding tree of the classical forest, and it names one of Suśruta's most important dravya-clusters. Suśruta heads the **Sālasārādi gaṇa** with it — a gaṇa of dense heartwood ("sāra") trees (Śāla, Aśvakarṇa, Khadira, Dhava, Tiniśa, Kramuka and others) prescribed collectively against **kuṣṭha (skin disease), medas (fat/obesity), prameha (urinary/diabetic disorders), kṛmi, kaṇḍū and kapha disorders**. The heartwood decoction is lekhana (scraping) and kaphamedohara. Distinct from the wood, the tree's exuded oleoresin — **Rāla / Sarja-rasa / dhūpa** ("sal dammar") — is a major officinal in its own right: a fragrant, astringent, cooling resin used in wound dressings (vraṇaropana), skin disease, dysentery/atisāra, and as the classical incense (dhūpa) burned for fumigation and ritual. Sarja-rasa is a standard ingredient of medicated ghees and ointments for ulcers.

**Folk & material culture:** Sal dammar is a subcontinental workhorse resin — a caulking and varnish material, a cosmetic and pharmaceutical base, and one of the principal temple incenses of northern and eastern India. The tree itself is sacred in Buddhist and Hindu tradition (the Buddha's mother is said to have grasped a sāla branch at his birth; the twin sal trees at his parinirvāṇa), which anchors its dhūpa/incense role in devotional as well as medical practice.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Heartwood and the Smoke:** Śāla carries a double symbolism. As **sāra** — heartwood, the densest, most durable core of the tree — it is the emblem of structural integrity, of what remains when the soft and perishable is stripped away; Suśruta's whole Sālasārādi gaṇa is, doctrinally, a **scraping-to-the-core** class that reduces excess (medas, kapha) back toward essential form. As **dhūpa** — the resin given to fire — it is the medicine of *ascent and purification*: smoke that cleanses the air, the wound and the space. Sal thus binds together two `[RESET]` gestures: paring back to the durable core, and purifying by fire-and-smoke. In Kenetica it reads as a restoration-of-structure agent — clearing the accumulated soft excess and re-establishing the sound frame.

### Active Components

The oleoresin (Rāla/dammar) is a **triterpenoid** complex — dammarane- and ursane-/oleanane-type triterpene acids (including asiatic-acid-related and ursolic/oleanolic-type compounds) with essential-oil sesquiterpenes conferring the aroma. The bark and heartwood are rich in **tannins** (catechins, procyanidins) accounting for the astringent, wound-drying action. Exact triterpenoid marker profiles vary by sample.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Salasara sits in `[RESET]` as a **wound-and-tissue restoration and purification** agent. The resin's classical vraṇaropana (wound-healing) and skin-disease use maps onto barrier repair and tissue closure; the Sālasārādi heartwood's lekhana/kaphamedohara action maps onto clearing soft metabolic excess. As dhūpa it also carries the space-purification/aromatic role. In the Kenetica frame it is used topically (resin dressings, ointments) and as a cleansing incense rather than as a systemic tonic.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the modern evidence is **preclinical (animal)** and centres on the resin.

- Wani et al. (2012), *Indian Journal of Biochemistry & Biophysics* 49(6):463–7, **PMID 23350282** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23350282/)), showed a 70% ethanolic extract of *Shorea robusta* resin produced significant, dose-dependent anti-inflammatory activity (carrageenan-induced paw oedema and cotton-pellet granuloma) and antipyretic activity (Brewer's-yeast pyrexia) in rats at 100–300 mg/kg, supporting the traditional use of the resin in painful inflammatory conditions and fever.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṣāya (astringent), Madhura (sweet)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Guru (heavy), Snigdha (unctuous) — resin; heartwood tends Laghu–Rūkṣa
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** Vraṇaropana (wound-healing) resin; Sālasārādi gaṇa head as kapha-medohara/lekhana
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha–Pitta śāmaka (resin); heartwood is kapha-medohara
- **Karma (actions):** Vraṇaropana-śodhana (wound cleansing/healing), kuṣṭhaghna (anti-dermatosis), grāhī/stambhana (astringent, anti-diarrhoeal), śothahara (anti-inflammatory), medohara/lekhana (heartwood), dhūpana (fumigation)

### Contraindications

Resin dressings: patch-test; discontinue on irritation. Incense/dhūpa smoke — avoid in asthma and respiratory sensitivity. Internal use of heartwood/bark decoctions only under qualified supervision; high tannin content can cause constipation and gastric upset. Pregnancy/lactation: internal use best avoided without guidance.
