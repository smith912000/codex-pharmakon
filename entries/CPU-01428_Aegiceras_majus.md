### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry: named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** This row was added on 2026-10-09 by the plant-gap run (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP). The binomial *Aegiceras majus* appears once on disk, on the SUGGESTED line of one chapter of the English *De Materia Medica* (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), and the plant is named by no other text read. The identification is the translators' and is doubtful, for the reasons below. This is an honest short entry.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`.

*1-113 AIGEIROS* (heading at line 4378; SUGGESTED line 4379: "Aegiceras majus— River Mangrove ALSO: Astragalus aegiceras"; cross-reference line 4380: "see 4-62"; text at line 4381, in Book One among the gums of trees). The whole chapter reads: "The leaves of aigeiros applied with vinegar help gouty pains, and its resin is mixed with softening medicines. The seed (taken as a drink with vinegar) is good for epilepsy. It is also reported that the oozing which trickles down from them near the river Padus grows hard and becomes amber, called chrysophorum by some. This yields a sweet smell when rubbed and looks like gold. Ground finely and taken as a drink it stops excessive discharges of the stomach and bowels."

**Identity doubts.** (1) The chapter gives no description of the plant at all: no leaf shape, height, habitat or root. It gives uses, and a report about an oozing near "the river Padus". Nothing in it describes a mangrove, and nothing places the plant on a tidal shore. (2) The translators' own line offers two names, *Aegiceras majus* and, after ALSO, *Astragalus aegiceras*, and then refers the reader to 4-62. That chapter is headed ASTRAGALOS and describes a small ground shrub with chickpea-like leaves and a radish-like root with horn-like growths (line 8543 onwards; its SUGGESTED line is *Astragalus gummifera* and *A. glycyphyllos*). The chapters are therefore two different plants, and the cross-reference marks the translators' uncertainty. (3) The text itself speaks of "them" in the oozing ("trickles down from them"), which is the language of trees. The Greek word is the classical name of a tree, but this was not established from anything read in this session, and the Codex does not assert it. ### Active Components

*Aegiceras* is a genus of mangrove shrubs of Indo-Pacific shores. Their chemistry was not read for this entry, and because the plant Dioscorides meant is not established, no composition can be attached to it. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The chapter records leaves for gouty pains, a resin for softening medicines, a seed drink for epilepsy, and a hardened exudate said to become amber and taken to stop discharges of the stomach and bowels. The plant is not securely identified, the uses are not tied to any recommended practice in the Codex, and the Codex gives no protocol for it.

### Preparation & Dosing

Dioscorides gives, in line 4381: leaves "applied with vinegar"; resin "mixed with softening medicines"; seed "taken as a drink with vinegar"; and the hardened oozing "ground finely and taken as a drink". No quantity, duration or number of doses is printed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
