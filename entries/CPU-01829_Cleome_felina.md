### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The chapters do print the Sanskrit name (in spellings Suvarnakshiri, Suvarnakhiri and Suvarna-kshiri), in three places, but give no Latin for it; the Latin comes from the appendix pairing alone.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` (row 625) gives the name as "Suvarnaksbiri", the locus "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 33045; id line 33063)" and the Latin "Cleome  felina.", with the note that the pairing was proposed by an agent from the two OCR columns and checked by script. The OCR spelling "Suvarnaksbiri" (with b for h) is read as Suvarnakshiri.

**The pairing, checked by counting.** In the block of the appendix that holds Soma-raji to Swarna-makshika (Sanskrit lines 33023 to 33046) the Sanskrit run is Soma-raji, Snuhi, Srotonjana, Sthala-padma, Sthulaila, Sudha, Sukshmaila, Sunnishannaka, Sura-daru with Sura-druma (bracketed), Surya-varta, Sushavi, Suvarchika, **Suvarnakshiri** and Swarna-makshika: fourteen entries. The Latin run (lines 33049 to 33064) is "Vernonia Anthelmintica", "Euphorbia Neriifolia", "Antimony", "Hibiscus Mutabilis", "Amomum Subulatum", "See Snuhi", "Elettaria Cardamomum", "Marsilea quadrifolia", "See Devadaru", "Cleome Viscosa", "Momordica Charantia", "Carbonate of Soda", **"Cleome felina"** and "Iron Pyrites": fourteen entries. Suvarnakshiri is the thirteenth in each run, second from the end. The neighbours fit: Snuhi with *Euphorbia neriifolia*, Srotonjana with antimony, Sudha with "See Snuhi", Sushavi with *Momordica charantia*, Suvarchika with carbonate of soda and Swarna-makshika with iron pyrites. The names.csv rows count Sura-daru and Sura-druma as two entries, which shifts one row (the Surya-varta row) by a place; the count above treats them as one, as the OCR brackets them, and with that reading both runs are fourteen long. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* The name occurs in three chapters, always in a list of purgative drugs.

*The Shyamadi group (Sutra ch.38).* `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 203 to 209: "The drugs known as Shyama, Maha-Shyama, Trivrit, Danti, Shankhini, Tilvaka, Kampillaka, Ramyaka, Kramuka, Putrashroni, Gavakshi, Rajavriksha, the two kinds of Karanja, Guduchi, Saptala, Chhagalantri, Sudha and Suvarnakhiri, form the group known as the Shyamadi Gana." The metrical text (lines 211 to 225) says that "this group is possessed of the therapeutic virtue of curing abdominal glands and acts as an anti-toxic", names Anaha, abdominal dropsy and diarrhoea (part of the line is lost in the OCR at the page break), and calls the group "one of the most reliable purgatives in cases of obstinate constipation of the bowels with suppression of urine and distention of the abdomen (Udavarta)". Nothing is said of the group's members singly.

*The purgative drugs (Sutra ch.39).* `SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` lines 21 to 30: "Purgative Drugs: The trees, plants, herbs and creepers, etc. known as Trivrita, Shyama, Danti, Dravanti, Saptala, Shankhini, Vishanika, Gavakshi, Chhagalantri, Snuk, Suvarnakshiri, Chitraka, Kinihi, Kusha, Kasha, Tilvaka, Kampillaka, Ramyaka, Patala, Puga, Haritaki, Amalaka, Bibhitaka, Nilini, Chaturangula, Eranda, Putika, Mahavriksha, Saptachchhada, Arka, and Jyotishmati, etc. are possessed of purgative properties." The text goes on: "Of these the roots of plants, which precede Tilvaka in the above list, should be used for purgative purposes." Suvarnakshiri stands before Tilvaka in the list, so on the text's own rule its root is the part used; the Codex draws that from the list order and the rule, and the text does not say it of this plant by name.

*The Tilvaka-ghrita (Chikitsa ch.4, the chapter on nervous disorders, Vata-vyadhi).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch04.txt` lines 172 to 183: "A paste (Kalka) of the following drugs, viz, Trivrit, Danti, Suvarna-kshiri, Saptala, Shankhini, Triphala and Vidanga (OCR "Vidariga"), each weighing an Aksha (two tolas), and Tilvaka-roots and Kampillaka, each weighing a Vilva (eight tolas), a decoction of Triphala and curd, each weighing two Patras (thirty-two seers) and clarified butter, weighing sixteen seers, should be duly cooked together. Medical authorities recommend this Tilvaka Ghrita as an oily purgative in cases of Vata-roga." Asoka-ghrita and Ramyaka-ghrita are prepared the same way with those drugs in place of Tilvaka. The weights are the translator's English for the text's measures and are given here as printed. [USER: verify]

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Suvarnakshiri, Suvarna-kshiri and Suvarnakhiri found nothing.

**Identity doubt.** The chapters print a purgative drug of this name and the appendix prints one Latin name against it. The Codex has read no second source for the identity, and does not say that *Cleome felina* is the drug the chapters use as a purgative. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Cleome* is a genus of flowering plants (Cleomaceae). Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta chapters use the drug named Suvarnakshiri as a purgative, one of a long list of purgatives, and as one ingredient of a purgative ghrita for Vata-roga. Whether that drug is the Latin species of the appendix is open. Purgatives can do real harm, and the Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters give one preparation with quantities: the Tilvaka-ghrita (Chikitsa ch.4, lines 172 to 183), in which Suvarna-kshiri is one of seven drugs pasted at an Aksha (two tolas) each, cooked with Tilvaka and Kampillaka roots, a Triphala decoction, curd and sixteen seers of clarified butter. The text gives no dose of the finished ghrita in the lines read. Sutra 39 says the root is used. No other dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** no *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Suvarnakshiri in the three passages read. The only quality the text prints is the action of the group lists: a purgative (Sutra 39) and, for the Shyamadi group as a whole, a cure for abdominal glands and an anti-toxic action (Sutra 38).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Suvarnakshiri only in purgative contexts: the Shyamadi group (Sutra 38), the list of purgative drugs (Sutra 39) and a purgative ghrita for Vata-roga (Chikitsa 4). These are therapeutic contexts, not rasayana or vajikarana ones, and none of the three passages gives the drug a cultivation status or speaks of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. A purgative drug in a list of purgatives gives a cultivator no long-term practice, and purging is a therapy to be taken under care for a defined complaint. The identity behind the Latin name is a translator's appendix pairing.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this drug in the passages read. Purgatives are unsuitable for self-treatment, and the identity here is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No conservation information was read.
