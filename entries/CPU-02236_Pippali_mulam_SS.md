# Pippali mulam [SS] — Pippali-mula, Pippali mulam, "root of long pepper" (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna tr.)

## *The root of the long-pepper plant, listed in the Sushruta Samhita as a drug separate from Pippali (the fruit); the identification as the root of *Piper longum* L. is ours, from the name (*mula*, "root") and the standard Ayurvedic identification of Pippali, and is of medium confidence: Bhishagratna's English does not print a botanical name for it* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Pippali mulam [SS]"; this entry replaces it. The text is Bhishagratna's English, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, with Vols. 2 and 3. The fruit, Pippali, is *Piper longum* and has its own entry (CPU-00065); this entry is the root only.

**Ayurveda (SS).** The group. Sutrasthana ch. 38, the Pippalyadi group (Vol. I line 15490): "Pippali, Pippali mulam, Chavya, Chitraka, Shringavera, Maricha, Hasti-Pippali, Harenuka, Ela, Ajamoda, Indrayava, Patha, Jiraka, Sarshapa, Maha-Nimva-Phala, Hingu, Bhargi, Madhurasa, Ativisha, Vacha, Vidanga and Katurohini." The text lists the root as a member in its own right, after the fruit. Its stated effect (line 15498): "The present group acts as a good appetiser and is an absorbant of intestinal mucous and unassimilated lymph chyle. The range of its therapeutical application includes catarrh, deranged Kapham and Vatam, non-relish for food, abdominal glands, colic and gastralgia." A translator's note (Vol. III line 12920) gives the drugs of "the group known as" the Pippalyadi, "viz. Pippali, Pippali-mula, Chavya, Chitraka and Nagara", as Dallana's reading of a shorter set.

Uses of the root. It is named in compounds in Vol. II (lines 14316, 14730, 26444, 27506) and Vol. III (lines 5939, 6847, 9425, 10714). Three examples. In a powdered compound treated with the juice of Matulunga it stands among Hingu, Trikatu, Vacha and others (Vol. II line 14316). A medicated Takra is prepared "with Pippali, Pippali-mula, Chavya, Chitraka, Vidanga, Sunthi and Haritaki" (Vol. II line 14730). A medicated ghrita for a child is cooked with the Kalka and decoction of "Pippali, Pippali-mula, the drugs of the Madhura group, Shalaparni and the two kinds of Vrihati" (Vol. III line 6847). I did not read these in full chapter context. [USER: verify.]

The fruit, for contrast. In ch. 46 the dried Pippali (fruit) "subdues the Vayu and Kapham, acts as a spermatopoietic and slightly soothes the Pittam" (Vol. I line 21095). That passage is about the fruit and is not transferred to the root.

### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms, the long-pepper plant is known for its pungent amide alkaloids (piperine and related compounds), found chiefly in the fruit; the root is a separate part and its chemistry is not given by the sources. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Through the Pippalyadi group: appetiser, catarrh, deranged Kapha and Vata, abdominal glands, colic and gastralgia; and as an ingredient in compounds.
- **What the text does not say.** No effect is printed for the root alone and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

Powdered, in compounds, in decoction and kalka, and in medicated takra and ghrita (lines above). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The long-pepper plant is a climber of South Asia grown for its fruit spike, which is used as a spice; the fruit contains piperine. The root is a traditional drug in Indian medicine but the sources read do not describe it. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for the root alone. The Pippalyadi group as a whole is an appetiser that absorbs intestinal mucus and unassimilated chyle (line 15498). The ch. 46 qualities belong to the fruit.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The root sits in the Pippalyadi gana, with a digestive and Kapha-Vata therapeutic account. The spermatopoietic remark in ch. 46 is made of dried Pippali, the fruit, and is not attached to the root; no rasayana or vajikarana assignment for the root alone was found. The status is unattested.

**Practitioner's note.** A pungent root listed with the digestive kindlers, and used as a compound ingredient. Nothing here makes it a cultivation substance.

### Contraindications

- **Pungent, heating drug.** The group is for Kapha and Vata conditions; the text itself places the unripe fruit as heavy and Kapha-generating, so the parts are not interchangeable.
- **Identity.** The species is our identification, of medium confidence. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Colic, gastralgia, catarrh.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
