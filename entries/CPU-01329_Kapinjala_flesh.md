# Kapinjala flesh — Kapinjala, flesh of a bird of the scatterer (*Vishkira*) group, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the bird Bhishagratna prints as "Kapinjala"; his Vol. 3 glossary reads "(the francoline partridge ?)" and, by column order, "A brown partridge"; the chapter gives no gloss and no binomial* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Kapinjala is described in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 156 lists "Kapinjala", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", flagged identity-uncertain). The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The high-ground (*Jangala*) animals fall into eight sub-groups, "Of these the Janghala and the Viscira are the most important" (lines 403-411). The Kapinjala is in the second: "Birds such as the Lava, Tittiri, Kapinjala, Vartira, Vartika, Vartaka ... Chakora ... Mayura, Krakara, Upachakra, Kukkuta ... Kutittiri, Kuruvahuka and Yavalaka belong to the Vishkira species" (names omitted where the OCR is damaged), so called "from the fact of their picking up their food after scattering it first with their bills and claws" (lines 450-463). The group text reads: "The flesh of a bird of this group is light, cooling, sweet and astringent in taste and tends to pacify the deranged humours of the body" (lines 463-466). Of the Kapinjala by name: "The flesh of the Kapinjala is light and cooling, and proves curative in cases of haemoptysis, and is recommended in diseases brought about through the deranged condition of the Kapham or Vayu (Manda-vata)" (lines 478-482).

It recurs elsewhere. *Sutrasthana* ch.20, on wholesome and unwholesome regimen, lists the flesh of the Kapinjala with other game among articles that "may be safely included within the food stuffs of all human beings" (lines 43-56, Kapinjala at line 55), and, in the list of incompatible preparations, says the flesh of Kapinjala, peacock, Lava, Tittira and Godha "boiled with castor oil and on a fire of the twigs of castor plants, should not be eaten" (lines 179-182). The after-drinks passage of ch.46 says "Kola and Vadara wines should be used after having taken the cooked flesh of the fowls of the Vishkira species" (lines 2828-2840). *Uttaratantra* ch.39 (fever) gives "The meat of Lava, Kapinjala, Ena, Prishata, Sarabha, Kala-puchchha, Kuranga, Mriga-matrika (different kinds of deer) or Sasa (hare)" as diet "for a fever-patient accustomed to the use of animal food" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt`, lines 904-908). *Uttaratantra* ch.40 (diarrhoea) says root preparations "may be advantageously prescribed with the soup of the flesh of any Jangala bird (such as Tittira, Kapinjala, etc.)" (`SS_Uttara_ch40.txt`, lines 442-445).

**Identity.** Ch.46 does not say what bird the Kapinjala is. The glossary of Bhishagratna's Vol. 3 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, line 30631) prints "Kapinjala (the francoline partridge ?) A sort of bird", with its own question mark, and a second row, by OCR column order, "A brown partridge". The spine list flags the identity as uncertain. The bird is not fixed here. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Game-bird flesh is mostly water and muscle protein with little fat, and supplies iron and B vitamins. No analysis of the bird Sushruta calls Kapinjala was read, and its species is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced record is dietary: a game bird's flesh with stated qualities, named as a permitted diet in a fever and with a diarrhoea preparation, and with one preparation forbidden. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no use for haemoptysis, fever, diarrhoea or any other condition. The bird is not identified with confidence; wild partridges and similar game birds are subject to hunting seasons, permits and protection in many countries. A reader who eats no meat has nothing to adopt.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the texts give are: the flesh as an ordinary food (*Sutrasthana* ch.20, line 55); the flesh as a fever diet "in moderate quantity" (*Uttaratantra* ch.39, where the same passage says some authorities do not advise certain heavy birds, naming the Sarasa, Krauncha, Mayura, Kukkuta and Tittira, and not the Kapinjala; lines 908-917); the soup of "Jangala" birds with root preparations in diarrhoea (ch.40, lines 442-445); and the prohibition on boiling it with castor oil on a castor-twig fire (ch.20, lines 179-182). No quantity or course is printed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Wild game birds can carry enteric bacteria such as *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter* and parasites, and thorough cooking with hygienic handling is the standard precaution. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 463-482). For the *Vishkira* group: light; cooling; sweet and astringent in taste; pacifies the deranged humours. For the Kapinjala by name: light; cooling; curative in haemoptysis; recommended in disorders of *Kapham* or *Vayu* (*manda-vata*). The text prints no separate potency or post-digestive taste for the Kapinjala. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Kapinjala is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food, not about this flesh. Its other places are a fever diet, a diarrhoea adjunct and a list of permitted foods, none a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter; no line read gives it that status. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta the Kapinjala is an ordinary light, cooling game bird listed as acceptable diet in illness, with one preparation to avoid. A cultivator can take only the caution about castor oil, and with the bird unidentified there is no reason to seek it out.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** wild game birds can carry bacteria and parasites; cook through.
- **The text's own caution:** not boiled with castor oil on a fire of castor twigs (*Sutrasthana* ch.20, lines 179-182).
- **Identity:** the bird is not fixed in the text; francolin and partridge are the translator's tentative glosses. Do not substitute on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Legal and conservation status:** game-bird hunting is regulated in many countries; status for any candidate species was not checked. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food or medicine is a moral question for many traditions; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
