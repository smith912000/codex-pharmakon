# Butter — BUTURON (Dioscorides 2-81) / Navanita (Sushruta, "fresh butter")
## *Butter: the fat separated from churned milk; Osbaldeston's edition prints "SUGGESTED: Butter" and Bhishagratna renders Navanita as "fresh butter"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM, SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

This entry is fresh butter, the churned fat of milk. Clarified butter (*ghritam*, ghee) is a different material with its own entry, `III_02_Ghee.md`; cattle and their milk are in `III_17_Cattle.md`. Where a source speaks of both, the lines are kept apart below.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 2-81 *BUTURON* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 5264-5269, in the Osbaldeston and Wood 2000 English version). Source and making: "Good butter is made of the fattest milk such as ewes' milk. It is also made from milk of goats, the milk being stirred around in jars until the fat is separated." Qualities: "It is softening and has the qualities of oil." Internal use: "Taken by itself it loosens the intestines, and when oil is not available it is an antidote against poison." Mixed with honey and rubbed on, it "helps teething and itching of the gums in children, and ulcers of the mouth [thrush, candidiasis]" (the bracket is the translator's). Rubbed on externally it "preserves the body and prevents pustules from breaking out." The text then gives a condition on freshness: "Butter that is neither stinking nor old is good against inflammations and hard lumps of the womb", is "given as a suppository for dysentery and ulceration of the colus [? possibly colon]", and is added to suppurating medicines, "especially for wounds around the nerves, neural membrane, bladder and neck. It fills and cleans and encourages new flesh." It is also applied "to one bitten by an asp", and as food "New butter is put in sauce for meats instead of oil, and in cakes instead of fat." The chapter ends with a second product, the soot of butter: butter is burnt in a new lamp covered by a pierced ceramic jar, more butter being added until enough soot is collected, which is scraped off with a feather; it is "useful in eye medicines, to dry, and as an astringent. It stops discharges and quickly brings ulcers to a scar."

Butter turns up twice more as a vehicle. In 2-84 (lanolin, line 5282ff) wool fat "applied in wool with melilot and butter" is recorded as an abortifacient, and in 2-85 (rennet, line 5289) rennet of hare "applied after the cleansing ... to the vulva with butter ... causes inconception." These are historical records of contraceptive and abortifacient uses; this Codex gives no protocol for them.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Sutrasthana* ch.45, the milk-products section (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 732-749, Bhishagratna's English). "Fresh butter (Navanita) is an albuminous substance, and is light, sweet, cooling, demulcent, pleasant, appetising, slightly acid and astringent. It subdues the deranged Vayu and Pittam. It is spermatopoietic, antacid in its reaction, and conduces to the improvement of one's memory and intellectual capacities. It proves beneficial in cases of consumption, cough, dyspnoea, ulcer, piles and facial paralysis" (the OCR prints "fecial"). Butter "of a few days standing" is heavy, "increases the quantity of fat and Kapham, and imparts strength and rotundity to the body, and proves especially wholesome to children." Butter made of thickened milk "is the best of all oily or (Kshira) substances. It is sweet, cooling and astringent; and imparts softness to the body, improves the eye-sight, and proves curative in haemoptysis and eye-diseases." A rule is added for other animals: the properties of preparations from the milk of other animals "should be regarded as identical with those of the milk of the animal out of which they have been prepared" (lines 766-772). Cream precedes this rule (line 761), then clarified butter (*Ghritam*, line 775ff), which carries the *ojas*, "vitalising, rejuvenating" wording and belongs to the ghee entry.

**Tibetan (FMT) is not on this entry's tag line but has butter text** that the editor may want to add: the Explanatory Tantra says "Fresh butter has cool power, increases semen, improves the complexion and eliminates bile fever. Old butter [nine or more months] is effective for mental disturbance, loss of memory and fainting [and for wounds]" (Clark 1995, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 685; Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 line 2948). ### Active Components

Butter is an emulsion of water in milk fat. The fat is mostly triglycerides, with a large share of saturated fatty acids including short-chain ones such as butyric acid; it carries cholesterol and the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K in amounts that vary with the animal's feed; it holds small amounts of milk protein, lactose and salts. Salted butter adds sodium chloride. The sources name no active component, and no figures are given here because none were read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice beyond butter as ordinary food. The sources record butter as a softening, oil-like fat used to loosen the bowels, rub on children's gums, protect the skin, and carry other medicines, and in the Sushruta text as a light, cooling food with benefit for memory. The Codex has no protocol that depends on these uses. The reasons are plain: butter is a dairy product with the usual cautions (below); the Greek text's suppository, poison-antidote and snakebite uses are no substitute for medical care; and the abortifacient and contraceptive mentions in 2-84 and 2-85 are recorded history and not a practice this Codex supports. For a cultivator the useful part of the record is the freshness rule: both texts treat fresh butter as the good material and say stale butter changes character.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the texts give are these. Dioscorides: butter made by stirring fatty milk in jars until the fat separates (2-81); butter mixed with honey for the gums; butter as a suppository; butter in suppurating medicines; and butter soot, made by burning butter in a covered lamp inside a pierced jar and scraping the soot off with a feather (2-81). Sushruta: fresh butter, butter of a few days' standing, and butter made of thickened milk, distinguished by their qualities (ch.45, lines 732-749). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Butter is a concentrated source of milk fat and is energy-dense; its saturated fat content is the main dietary consideration, and raised dietary saturated fat is associated with raised LDL cholesterol. Butyrate is a short-chain fatty acid also produced by gut bacteria from fibre, but the amount in butter is small and no benefit to the gut can be inferred from it here. Fats have long been used as emollient bases, but butter is not sterile and turns rancid, so it is not a suitable dressing for open wounds. Soot is finely divided carbon; no eye-medicine use of it is advised, since soot is not sterile and eye infection is the risk. No clinical studies of the traditional uses were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The lines read print qualities in plain terms: "softening" and "has the qualities of oil" (2-81); the soot is "to dry, and as an astringent". No four-quality scheme is printed for butter in the chapter, and none is supplied.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Fresh butter (*navanita*) is "light, sweet, cooling, demulcent, pleasant, appetising, slightly acid and astringent"; it subdues *vayu* and *pittam*; it is "spermatopoietic" and "conduces to the improvement of one's memory and intellectual capacities". Butter of a few days' standing is heavy and increases fat and *kapham*. The text gives taste and a cooling quality for fresh butter, but no separate *vipaka* statement for *navanita* in the lines read (the "sweet in digestion" wording belongs to cow-milk clarified butter, line 798ff).

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is supplied. Butter is a material with a therapeutic and culinary record in 2-81 and nothing more. What the chapter does give a cultivator is a discipline of attention: the best butter comes from the fattest milk, ewes' milk first; the making is described (milk stirred in jars until the fat separates); and the condition "neither stinking nor old" is set for the internal and womb uses. The soot recipe adds a second craft detail. These are rules of provenance and freshness, not claims about the person's constitution.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Fresh butter is described in the *Sutrasthana* ch.45 milk-products passage as "spermatopoietic" and as conducing "to the improvement of one's memory and intellectual capacities". Those are properties listed for a food, not a placement in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so they are not read as either status. The *ojas* wording and the "rejuvenating" wording in the same chapter attach to *ghritam* (clarified butter), not to *navanita*, and are not carried over to butter; clarified butter in the *rasayana* meal instructions (*Chikitsasthana* ch.28, lines 96-98, rice with milk and clarified butter after the medicine) is likewise ghee. No claim is made for *tejas* or *prana*. The sattvic food lists are Samkhya-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Butter is food, and both texts treat it as good when fresh. Nothing sourced here makes it a cultivation substance in either tradition: Dioscorides gives a plain materia medica entry, and Sushruta saves the vitality language for ghee. A cultivator can take the freshness rule and the plain-food status, and look to the ghee entry for the clarified product. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Dairy allergy and lactose.** Butter retains traces of milk protein and lactose; people with a milk-protein allergy should avoid it.
- **Cardiovascular and lipid disorders.** Dietary saturated fat is a consideration; follow medical advice in raised cholesterol or heart disease.
- **Spoilage.** The Greek text itself limits the good material to butter "neither stinking nor old"; rancid butter should not be eaten or applied.
- **Not for poisoning, snakebite or eye use.** The antidote, asp-bite and soot-for-eye uses are historical; these are emergencies or sterile-field matters for medical care.
- **Pregnancy.** Do not use the 2-84 and 2-85 abortifacient or contraceptive records as guidance.
- **Ethics and diet.** A dairy product; unsuitable for vegans.
