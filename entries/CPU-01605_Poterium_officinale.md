# Poterium officinale — Poterion (Dioscorides 3-17)

## *Poterium officinale, the first of three groups of names on the translators' SUGGESTED line for 3-17 ("Poterium officinale — Great Burnet"; "Poterium sanguisorba, Sanguisorba minor — Salad Burnet"; "Astragalus poterium, Astragalus arnacantha — Small Goat's Thorn")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant under this name. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED line (lines 6678 and 6679) names three different plants, in three groups, with no choice among them. The row takes the first, *Poterium officinale*. The identity is especially open here: the first group's English name is a burnet, the second is also a burnet, and the third is a thorny *Astragalus*. The row was added from the line (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is *3-17. POTERION*, heading at line 6676, SUGGESTED lines at 6678 and 6679, text at line 6680, with the page number 383 at line 6682. It stands in Book Three, before *3-18. AKANTHION* (heading at line 6685, with the translators' label UNKNOWN). The translators' index gives *Poterium officinale* and *Poterium sanguisorba* at page 383.

The whole chapter reads: "Poterium is a large shrub with long branches — soft, flexible like a bridle, thin, similar to tragacanth — the leaves little, round. The whole shrub is surrounded with a thin woolly down and is prickly; the flowers are small and white. The seed (to one who tastes it) has a sweet scent and is sharp with no use. It grows in sandy and hilly countries. The roots are underneath, two or three feet long, strong and sinewy. When cut close to the ground they send out a fluid similar to gum. The roots (cut and smeared on) heal cut-apart sinews and wounds, and a decoction of it (taken as a drink) is good for disorders of the strength. It is also called phrynion, or andidotum, and the Ionians call it neurada."

Dioscorides gives a long description for a short list of uses. The plant is a "large shrub", the branches "soft, flexible like a bridle", the leaves "little, round", the whole "surrounded with a thin woolly down" and "prickly", with small white flowers. The seed is tasted ("sweet scent", "sharp") and judged to have "no use". The root is "two or three feet long, strong and sinewy" and when cut near the ground gives "a fluid similar to gum". The root, "cut and smeared on", heals "cut-apart sinews and wounds", and a decoction "taken as a drink" is "good for disorders of the strength". The phrase "disorders of the strength" is as printed, and the chapter does not explain it. [USER: verify] The chapter prints three other names: *phrynion* and *andidotum*, and the Ionian *neurada*. They are names only. The comparison with tragacanth is the chapter's own, and it suits the translators' third group of names (an *Astragalus*) better than a burnet, which the Codex notes but does not settle.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for the chapter number, *Poterion* and *Poterium* found no existing entry on 3-17. I_638 (*Sanguisorba officinalis*, Great Burnet) shares the English words "Great Burnet" with the translators' first group of names, but it is a different binomial, carries CM, DMM and SBJ tags, and its Dioscorides paragraph does not cite 3-17 in the lines read. It is not merged with this chapter, and no tag is changed.

### Active Components

The constituents of the plant Dioscorides describes were not read. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the translators' *Poterium*, *Sanguisorba* or *Astragalus*. It is a general fact that tragacanth gum is obtained from certain thorny *Astragalus* shrubs, which is the plant the chapter compares its shrub to; the chapter does not say so. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records a thorny shrub whose cut root is smeared on cut sinews and wounds and whose decoction is drunk for "disorders of the strength". These are the text's claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol for wounds or severed sinews, which need surgical care, and the identification is open between three plants.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives two preparations: the roots "cut and smeared on", and "a decoction of it (taken as a drink)". No quantity, frequency or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints no warming, cooling, drying or moistening quality. It prints the seed as having "a sweet scent" and being "sharp".

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter records no amuletic use. What it offers is the discipline of attention that the cultivation spec describes for this tradition: a plant compared with tragacanth, hairy down and prickles noted, the seed tasted and judged useless, the root measured and its exudate observed. Recording that the seed has "no use" is itself a statement of discernment. The plant is given no status beyond a wound remedy and a drink. The identification is open.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It offers a detailed description and two remedies, so it is history and not a practice. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The chapter prints no warning. Deep wounds and cut sinews need urgent medical care, and a plant smear must not replace it. The identification rests on a translators' suggestion that spans three plants, so no wild plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. [USER: verify]
