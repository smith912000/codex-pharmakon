# Tephrosia purpurea — Sara-punkha (Sharapunkha) (Sushruta, Bhishagratna's text and Appendix 3)

## *Tephrosia purpurea (L.) Pers., a small leguminous herb (purple tephrosia, wild indigo type); the binomial is printed by the translator in the Appendix and in a footnote-style gloss in the text, and the Sanskrit name Sara-punkha is paired with it by position, so the identification rests on the translator's gloss: genus and species high confidence as the translator's, low as an independent check* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The only attestation is the Sushruta Samhita in Bhishagratna's English translation. A search of the other texts on disk for *Tephrosia* found no other row.

**Sushruta (SS).** Two places carry the name.

First, in "Appendix No. 3. Names of Drugs (with Latin synonyms)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, line 32851): "Tephrosia purpurea." The Sanskrit list in the S section runs *Sarala* (line 32770), *Sarala'* (32772), *Sara-pumkha* (32774, as OCR prints it), *Sariva*, *Sarjikshara* (32778), and so on; the Latin column runs "Pinus Longifolia" (32847), "See Trivrit", "Tephrosia purpurea" (32851), "Hemidesmus Indicus" (32853), "Carbonate of Soda" (32855). The pairing is confirmed at both ends: *Sarala* against pine, *Sarjikshara* against carbonate of soda, *Sariva* against *Hemidesmus*. So the appendix equates Sara-punkha with *Tephrosia purpurea*. The pairing is by position, because the OCR has separated the columns. [USER: verify against the printed page.]

Second, in the translation of Sutrasthana ch. VII, the chapter on surgical appliances (Yantra-vidhi-adhyaya): of the probes or directors, "the mouths of the two types of these directors respectively resemble those of a Gandupada earthworm and of a Sharapunkha Tephrosia Purpurea, Pers" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 5899-5900; chapter file `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch07.txt` lines 152-153). That is a comparison of shape, and the botanical name is the translator's. The text does not use the plant as a drug in this sentence.

No drug group of Sutra ch. 38 lists it in the translation read, and a search for *Sarapunkha* and *Sharapunkha* in the chapter files found only the ch. VII line. The Sushruta text on disk therefore names the plant but does not state an indication, a part used or a preparation for it.

### Active Components

No source on disk names a constituent. In general terms, *Tephrosia* species belong to the pea family (Fabaceae), and several are known to contain rotenoid and flavonoid compounds; some species have been used as fish poisons. Which applies to *T. purpurea* is not stated by any text read. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Tephrosia purpurea* is a widespread herb of tropical and subtropical regions, a legume, and some members of the genus are known to be toxic to fish. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The Latin name is the translator's, and the Sanskrit pairing is positional. Do not use a plant on the strength of this entry.
- **Toxicity.** Some *Tephrosia* species are used as fish poisons, so ingestion should not be assumed safe. [USER: verify]
