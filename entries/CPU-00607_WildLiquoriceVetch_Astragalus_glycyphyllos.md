# Wild Liquorice — Milk-vetch, Liquorice Milk-vetch, Sweet-leaved Vetch (Eng.)
## *Astragalus glycyphyllos* L. | Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)

**Tradition Tags:** AT, DMM, CM
**Energetic Classification:** Warm and Moist (temperate, gently tonic) — a mild, nourishing, restorative drug in the Galenic reading, without the acrid heat of true liquorice.
**Training Phase:** Foundation (a gentle building tonic; safe entry-level restorative)

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Astragalus glycyphyllos* is a sprawling perennial legume of European and western-Asian woodland margins, hedgerows and grassy slopes. Its species name — from Greek *glykys* (sweet) and *phyllon* (leaf) — records the faintly sweet taste of its foliage, the origin of the folk name "wild liquorice." It is botanically and pharmacologically **distinct from *Astragalus membranaceus* (Huang Qi)** of Chinese medicine and from *Glycyrrhiza glabra* (true liquorice); the shared common name reflects taste, not chemistry.

**Assyrian (cuneiform tablets):** The Mesopotamian herbals record sweet-leaved fodder legumes among nourishing and strengthening simples, given in decoction to convalescents and nursing mothers. The milk-vetches sit in this class of gentle building agents rather than among the purging or heating drugs.

**Dioscorides (De Materia Medica):** Dioscorides describes *astragalos*, a leguminous root, used as an astringent and a mild strengthening remedy — its decoction taken for loose bowels and to support recovery. The genus name itself descends from his usage. His astringent-tonic framing survives in the European folk use of wild liquorice root and leaf.

**Avicenna (Qanun / Canon of Medicine):** In the Unani tradition the sweet vetches are read as temperate and nourishing, gently moistening and restorative, suited to depletion, dryness and weakness. Avicenna's system places such mild leguminous tonics among agents that "thicken" and rebuild rather than attenuate.

Across European folk medicine *A. glycyphyllos* is best known as a **galactagogue** (to promote milk flow), a mild diuretic, and a general convalescent tonic — used in gentle decoctions where a strengthening rather than a purging action was wanted.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** The "sweet leaf" is the doctrine made legible: sweetness in the Galenic and Unani systems signals a temperate, nourishing, humour-building virtue, aligned with the plant's role as a soft tonic. The plant's low, spreading, ground-holding habit and its nitrogen-fixing roots — literally enriching the soil it grows in — mirror its symbolic reading as a quiet restorer of depleted ground, whether field or body.

### Active Components

- **Triterpenoid (cycloartane-type) saponins** — the principal characterised constituents, including rare saponins such as astrachrysoside A; the pharmacologically distinctive class of the genus.
- **Flavonoids** (including camelliaside A and related glycosides) — antioxidant constituents.
- **Sugars / sweet-tasting leaf compounds** — the basis of the common name.
- **Astragalus-type polysaccharides** (genus-typical, less characterised in this species than in *A. membranaceus*).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica, *A. glycyphyllos* is a **Foundation-phase tonic**: a gentle, low-risk restorative used to rebuild depleted, dry or convalescent states early in a training arc, before stronger agents are introduced. Its traditional galactagogue and convalescent uses place it in the "nourish and hold" category — restoring baseline vigour rather than driving change. It functions as the mild European counterpart to the stronger qi-tonic *A. membranaceus*, and is taught alongside it to distinguish a temperate building tonic from a more actively immuno-stimulating one.

### Scientific Correlation

The modern evidence base for *A. glycyphyllos* is **preclinical**, focused on its saponin chemistry and neuroprotective/antioxidant activity. According to PubMed, Stambolov et al. (2023), *Metabolites*, isolated cycloartane saponins (a new triterpenoid saponin and the rare astrachrysoside A) from the plant and showed statistically significant, dose-dependent in-vitro neuroprotective and antioxidant activity on rat brain sub-cellular fractions (synaptosomes, mitochondria, microsomes), with weak inhibition of human monoamine oxidase B. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/metabo13070857.

Earlier, Shkondrov et al. (2018), *Natural Product Research*, characterised a new tetracyclic saponin together with the flavonoid camelliaside A from *A. glycyphyllos*, reporting significant neuroprotective and antioxidant effects in a 6-hydroxydopamine model on isolated rat brain synaptosomes, comparable to silibinin. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2018.1491040.

**Evidence grade: PRECLINICAL / TRADITIONAL.** There are no human clinical trials for *A. glycyphyllos*, and no controlled evidence for its traditional galactagogue or tonic claims. The saponin and flavonoid antioxidant/neuroprotective data are in-vitro/ex-vivo only.

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian (AT).** In the Mesopotamian material the sweet-leaved fodder legumes sit among nourishing and strengthening simples, given in decoction to convalescents and nursing mothers — a therapeutic, dietetic role rather than any graded standing. Assyrian medicine classifies a substance partly by the causal agency it opposes (a divine hand, a ghost, a broken oath), but no such etiological role is recorded here for this vetch. Under the standing blocking warning in force for every Assyrian substance identification and tablet reference, no cultivation status is asserted; the attestation is that of a gentle building simple only. Cultivation status: **(c) absent**.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides describes *astragalos*, an astringent leguminous root taken in decoction for loose bowels and to support recovery, and the genus name descends from that usage — but the identification of his *astragalos* with this exact species is not secure, and in any case Dioscorides carries no subtle-body doctrine into which a cultivation status could be assigned. What the tradition offers a cultivator is his discipline of attention: the correct root, gathered and dried, assayed by taste and astringency, guarded against confusion with the many other leguminous roots that share the name. That is a real practice of provenance and sensory judgement, not a vital-body category. Cultivation status: **(c) absent**.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The sweet vetches are read as temperate, warm-and-moist, gently moistening and restorative, suited to depletion, dryness and weakness — an agent that "thickens" and rebuilds rather than attenuates. The strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus is the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture, the finite vital endowment whose depletion *is* ageing; but that is a longevity frame of the whole regimen, not a status the tradition assigns to this particular nourishing legume, and no *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed for it. Attested as a temperate restorative, with no cultivation grade sourced for this species: **(b) framed**.

**Practitioner's note.** A gentle building tonic across all three registers, worked short-to-medium term to rebuild a depleted, dry or convalescent state — the sweet leaf as a soft daily restorative, the root as an astringent-tonic decoction. Nothing here marks it as a longevity substance in any of the three traditions; its virtue is to nourish and hold depleted ground, not to drive a cultivation project. It is taught as the temperate European counterpart to the stronger *A. membranaceus*, precisely so the cultivator can tell a mild building tonic from an actively immuno-stimulating one and not mistake the sweet name for the greater plant's reputation.

### Contraindications

- **Generally regarded as a gentle, low-toxicity tonic**, but its saponin content warrants moderation — high or prolonged intake of saponin-rich material may irritate the gut.
- **Do not confuse with locoweed / selenium- or swainsonine-accumulating *Astragalus* species**, some of which are seriously toxic to livestock and humans; correct botanical identification is essential.
- **Lactation / galactagogue use:** although traditionally used to promote milk flow, there is no controlled safety data in breastfeeding; use only under qualified supervision. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy:** insufficient safety data — avoid unsupervised use.
- **Autoimmune conditions / immunosuppressant therapy:** *Astragalus* species can have immunomodulatory activity; caution and professional advice are advisable if immune-modulating drugs are being taken.
- Not a substitute for medical treatment of any condition.
