# Curcuma zedoaria — Pagoe, dpa' rgod (rGyud-bzhi, Men-Tsee-Khang glossary)

## *Curcuma zedoaria, as the binomial printed in the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary directly after the name "Pagoe" and its transliteration "dpa' rgod"; Clark 1995 prints no line for this binomial or this name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT), and by the Men-Tsee-Khang text alone.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. Clark 1995 (`Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`) prints no line for *Curcuma zedoaria* and none for the name Pagoe or *dpa'.rgod*; its only *Curcuma* is *Curcuma longa*, turmeric, under *yung.wa* (line 960, and in the index at line 1573). The entry therefore rests on one glossary line for the binomial and on one running-text sentence for the Tibetan name. The extraction note reads: "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, line 331). The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (lines 5576 to 5584).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, the "P" block (the letter at line 5576). The glossary prints, one under another with only blank lines between: "Pagoe" (line 5578), "dpa' rgod" (line 5580) and "Curcuma zedoaria" (line 5582). The next row begins at line 5584: "Pang gyen karpo spang rgyan dkar po Gentiana algida". The rows that follow take the same form (line 5586 "Pangpoe", line 5588 "spang spos", line 5590 "Nardostachys jatamansi"; line 5592 "Pang tsi dowo spang rtsi do po Pterocephalus hookeri"). No English name is printed for Pagoe at this place. In this stretch the glossary prints the phonetic name, the transliteration and the binomial in turn, and not in separate columns, so the pairing is closer to a printed row than the column-run pairings met elsewhere in the glossary. The Codex still reads it as the glossary's pairing and not as a verified identification. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text on "Pagoe".* The name is printed once in the text and once in the index. In chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225), the sentence at lines 3420 and 3421 reads: "Pushel is anti-emetic and cures hot disorders associated with baekan disorders. White and brown Khyungdher cures hot disorders associated with poisoning. Pawo karpo, Pawo serpo and Pagoe have the same potency as Khyungdher." The index (line 7459) lists "Pagoe 205". The sentence gives Pagoe no action of its own: it says that Pagoe, with Pawo karpo and Pawo serpo, has "the same potency as Khyungdher", and the sentence before it says what Khyungdher does. The word "potency" is the translator's, and the Codex reads it here as pointing to that action; the line does not spell the action out for Pagoe. [USER: verify]

*Where the sentence stands.* Chapter 20 names eight categories of substance (line 3324): precious, soil, stone, wood, aromatic, shrub, herbal and animal-product medicines. The sentence is not in the run that is headed "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (line 3428); it falls earlier, in the run that opens at line 3380 ("The efficacy of these medicinal substances for healing disorder is as follows"), after the passage that names wood, aromatic and shrub medicines (line 3375). The Codex reports that position and does not say which of the categories the text means for Pagoe.

*Neighbouring names.* The glossary sets "Uncaria" with the English "Hook" against "Khyungdher" by position (lines 5382 to 5389), and the Codex draws nothing from that for the *Curcuma* identification. Chapter 21 lists, among the medicines that cure poisoning, "Khungdher karpo and mukpo, Pawo karpo and serpo" (line 3604), and Pagoe is not in that list. A search of both Tibetan texts for "Pagoe", "zedoaria" and "Curcuma" found no other line (Men-Tsee-Khang lines 3421, 5578, 5582 and 7459; Clark line 960 for *Curcuma longa* only).

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Curcuma zedoaria*, "zedoary" and "Pagoe" found one entry that names the species: I_240 (turmeric, *Curcuma longa*) mentions *Curcuma zedoaria* in its prose (line 23), as the Chinese drug E Zhu, in a comparison with turmeric and its relatives. It is an entry on another plant and was not read as a source here. No entry on this plant exists. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Curcuma* is a genus of the ginger family (Zingiberaceae) grown for its rhizomes, and that *Curcuma* rhizomes are generally known to carry aromatic essential oils. Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained, because its Latin name rests on a glossary pairing. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Men-Tsee-Khang text records that Pagoe has "the same potency as Khyungdher" (line 3421), and Khyungdher "cures hot disorders associated with poisoning" (line 3421). These are the text's claims, attached to this binomial only by a glossary pairing, and they are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no part used, no quantity, no frequency and no duration for Pagoe. The glossary gives no English name, so even "rhizome" is not printed for it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Men-Tsee-Khang text places Pagoe in its list of the efficacy of medicinal substances, by comparison with a drug that cures hot disorders associated with poisoning. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this name places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the sentence and the glossary row cited above were read in full and none mentions one. Its framing is therapeutic only, and the link from this binomial to that framing rests on a glossary pairing.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a drug that the Men-Tsee-Khang text compares to another poison-related drug, with no action of its own and no dose, and its Latin name rests on a glossary pairing. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. The identity rests on a glossary pairing and the text names the drug beside poison-related medicines, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Poisoning needs urgent medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
