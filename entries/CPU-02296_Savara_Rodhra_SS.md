# Savara-Rodhra — Savara-Rodhra, also printed Savararodhra (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *A drug named three times in chapter 38, each time directly after Rodhra, which the same translation treats as Lodhra (Symplocos racemosa, CPU-00341). The name reads as a qualified Rodhra, but no line read says what the qualifier means or whether it is a second tree. Identification: unresolved, with Symplocos (a Rodhra-like tree) as the nearest candidate and no stated basis. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not securely identified. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group lists in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The first pass on 30 October called it a duplicate of CPU-00341 (Lodhra, *Symplocos racemosa*); the audit disagreed and the row was kept. That ruling stands here, for the reason given below: in all three group lists the name stands as a separate item after Rodhra, and a list that names Rodhra and then Savara-Rodhra is not using one name twice. A search of all the Sushruta text on disk (the chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and Bhishagratna's three volumes) for *Savara* found only these three places. A search of the appendix of names (Vol. III) for the name found no line.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Chapter 38 of the *Sutrasthana* is the chapter "which deals with the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam)" and says that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (Vol. I lines 15343 to 15350; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3 to 9). The drug is placed there by group, and a group's virtue is stated for the group as a whole.

1. **Rodhrardi group.** (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15430 to 15435; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 89 to 92). "The group of medicinal drugs known as the Rodhradi consists of Rodhra, Savararodhra, Palasha Kutannata, Ashoka, Phanji, Katphala, Elabaluka, Sallaki, Jingini, Kadamva, Sala and Kadali." The group is named for Rodhra, and Savararodhra is the second name. The metrical text says that "the group is antidotal to the deranged Kapham and fat, is astringent in its properties, removes vaginal and uterine disorders, neutralises the effects of poison (anti toxic) and acts as a styptic and purifying agent in a case of ulcer and arrests all secretions and excretions of the body" (lines 15436 to 15441).
2. **Amvashtha'di group.** (Vol. I lines 15668 to 15676; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 326 to 335). "Drugs known as Amvashtha, Dhataki flowers, Samanga, Katvanga, Madhuka, Vilva-peshika, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Palasha, Nandi-Vriksha and Padma keshara, enter into the composition of the group known as the Amvashthadi Gana." The metrical text is said of "the two medicinal recipes or groups" together: they "prove beneficial in a case of deranged Pitta, favour the healing of ulcers, bring about the adhesion of fractured bones and prove curative in cases of dysentery where the stools are found to consist of lumps of thick and matured mucous (Pakvatisara)" (lines 15677 to 15683).
3. **Nya'grodha'di group.** (Vol. I lines 15686 to 15694; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 346 to 352). "The drugs known as Nyagrodha, Audumvara, Ashvattha, Plaksha, Madhuka, Kapitana, Kakubha, Amra, Koshamra, Chorakapatra, the two sorts of Jamvu, Piyala, Madhuka (Maula), Rohini, Vanjula, Kadamva, Vadari, Tinduki, Sallaki, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Bhallataka, Palasha, and Nandi-Vriksha, combinedly form the group known as the Nyagodhradi Gana." The metrical text says that this group "proves beneficial in cases of ulcer, cures all disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones and all sorts of secretions of the body in addition to its astringent properties (Sangrahi) and proves curative in a case of haemoptysis. It is an anti-fat and assuages the burning sensation of the body" (lines 15695 to 15702).

**What the appendix does with the neighbouring name.** In Bhishagratna's Vol. III, Appendix No. 3, the line standing against *Rodhra* reads "See Lodhra" (line 32718), by a column count; no appendix line was found for *Savara-Rodhra*. The Codex entry for Lodhra (I_388, CPU-00341) records Rodhra as the translation's name for Lodhra and lists Savara-Rodhra only as a companion in the Rodhrardi, Amvashtha'di and Nyagrodhadi groups. Whether Savara-Rodhra is a second kind of the same tree, a different tree or a different part is not stated in any line read. The name Savara is not explained by the translator. [USER: verify]

The three group lists are the only places found. No description of the plant, no part used, and no *rasa* or potency was found.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Identity unresolved.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Rodhrardi group: acting against Kapha and fat, astringing, easing vaginal and uterine disorders, countering poison, and stopping bleeding and discharges from ulcers. As a member of the Amvashtha'di group: easing deranged Pitta, healing ulcers, knitting broken bones and stopping a mucous dysentery. As a member of the Nyagrodhadi group: healing ulcers, easing uterine and vaginal disorders, knitting broken bones, stopping secretions and haemoptysis, and cooling burning (all group-level).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no taste or potency for this drug on its own, and no identification.

The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no plant for any of the conditions named.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in three chapter 38 group lists, which classify drugs by the use of the whole group (*Dravya-Sangrahaniya*). Chapter 38 is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns this drug no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only, and only at the level of its groups. A search of the Sushruta chapter files found the name nowhere else, so the *rasayana* and *vajikarana* chapters do not carry it. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it. The groups' virtues are astringent and wound-closing, not life-nourishing.

**Practitioner's note.** A name that follows Rodhra in three group lists and is explained nowhere. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without an identification nothing can be gathered or used. A cultivator reads it as a name in a record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is not securely identified. Do not substitute any herb, including Lodhra, for it on the strength of the name.
- **The conditions named.** Ulcers, bleeding, dysentery, fractures, uterine and vaginal disorders and poisoning are named for the groups. These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. The groups' virtue includes uterine and vaginal disorders and secretions. [USER: verify]
- **Group membership.** The drug is one of many in its groups, and the groups' virtues are not safety statements about any one member.
