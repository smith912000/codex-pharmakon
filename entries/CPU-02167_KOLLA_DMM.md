# KOLLA — Kolla (glutinum, xylocolla, taurocolla), Dioscorides *De Materia Medica* 3-101

## *Kolla, glue made from bull hides (Latin glutinum, also xylocolla or taurocolla); the translator's gloss is "Glue from the hides of Bulls"; identification confident (high) as bull-hide gelatin glue, an animal product* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk under this name.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the name index (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`: "KOLLA, 3-101 (line 7450), Glue from the hides of Bulls"; the index lists it as a plant, which is a mistake, since the material is an animal product). This entry replaces it and files it in Volume III. The neighbouring chapters are separate substances and are not covered here: 3-99 *Sarkokolla* (a tree resin, "sarcocol"), 3-100 *Glaukion* (a herb juice) and 3-102 *Ichthuokolla* (fish glue, made from a whale fish's intestines). Chrysocolla and lithocolla (5-104, 5-164) are minerals.

**Dioscorides (DMM).** Book 3, chapter 101, `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 7450 to 7456 (a page break and two plates interrupt the paragraph in the e-text). The translator's gloss is "Glue from the hides of Bulls". The chapter reads: "The best glutinum (also called xylocolla or taurocolla) is that from Rhodes made from bull hides. It is white and transparent, but the black glue is bad. Dissolved in vinegar it is able to take away impetigo [skin infection] and leprosy on the outside of the skin. Diluted with warm water and smeared on, it prevents burns from blistering. Diluted with honey and vinegar it is good for wounds." The bracketed gloss "[skin infection]" is the translator's.

**What the text tells a reader about the material.** Provenance (Rhodes, named as the best), the raw material (bull hides), a sensory assay (white and transparent is good, black is bad), and three external uses. The alternative names *xylocolla* ("wood glue") and *taurocolla* ("bull glue") are printed in the chapter. This is the assay-and-provenance discipline that runs through Dioscorides.

### Active Components

General composition only. Hide glue is made by boiling animal hide so that its collagen is broken down into gelatin; it consists mostly of protein (gelatin, i.e. denatured collagen peptides) with water and traces of minerals. Dioscorides' "white and transparent" best grade and the "black" bad grade agree with this in kind, since darker glue is generally less refined. The sources name no constituent. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** All external: dissolved in vinegar, to take away impetigo and (the translator's word) leprosy of the outer skin; diluted with warm water and smeared on, to stop burns from blistering; diluted with honey and vinegar, "good for wounds" (3-101).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no internal use, no duration.

The Codex gives no protocol. Skin infection, burns and wounds are medical matters, and an old glue is not a dressing. The material is an animal product; it is unsuitable for anyone who avoids animal products, and its source (beef hides) should be considered for ethical and religious reasons.

### Preparation & Dosing

Three preparations are named, all for external use: dissolved in vinegar (impetigo and skin leprosy); diluted with warm water and smeared on (burns); diluted with honey and vinegar (wounds). The text gives no proportions. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Gelatin is hydrolysed collagen, and animal hide glue has long been used as an adhesive. Impetigo is a bacterial skin infection and today is treated with proper medical care. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek, Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is manufactured here. The chapter is a description of a plant (or material) and of its uses in medicine, with no cultivation status. What the text does offer a cultivator is a discipline of attention: how the thing looks, where it grows, what it is called and what it is mixed with.

**Practitioner's note.** A glue of bull hides, graded by sight and by place of origin, put to external use. Nothing in the record supports a cultivation use, and Dioscorides' only teaching here is how to tell good material from bad. A cultivator does nothing with this entry.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection.** Old hide glue is a growth medium for bacteria; applying it to open skin or a burn risks infection. Do not use craft or carpentry glue on skin.
- **Skin disease.** Impetigo, burns and leprosy (Hansen's disease) need medical care.
- **Ethics and diet.** An animal product (bovine hide); unsuitable for vegetarians and vegans and for those who avoid beef.
- **Allergy.** Gelatin can provoke allergy in some people. [USER: verify]
