# Kyanos (blue copper mineral) — Kuanos (κυανός), "Cyanus" in the translation (Dioscorides 5-106)
## *A blue material "formed from brass minerals" and found in Cyprus; the translator's suggestion is "Cyanochroite — blue hydrous Sulphate of Copper and Potassium"* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek Tradition (DMM).** The main locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-106 *KUANOS* in the 2000 English translation (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 10376-10379; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 879; book index "KUANOS 798", line 12202, and "Cyanochroite 798", line 15534). The translator's heading is "SUGGESTED: Cyanochroite — blue hydrous Sulphate of Copper and Potassium."

Dioscorides' account, read in full, is one paragraph:

"Cyanus, formed from brass minerals, is found in Cyprus. The largest quantity is made from sand at the shore that is found near some hollow places of the sea, like burrows. This is also the best. That which has a full colour must be chosen. It is burnt like chalcitis [calcium carbonate] and washed like cadmia [5-84]. It has the ability to repress, is a little corrosive, forms scabs, and is ulcerating." (line 10379)

**Reading the chapter.** (1) Source: formed from "brass minerals", in Cyprus; the largest quantity "is made from sand at the shore" near sea hollows, and that is the best. (2) Choice: "that which has a full colour". (3) Preparation: "burnt like chalcitis" and "washed like cadmia", both by reference to other chapters; the chalcitis chapter 5-115 (line 10426) was read and prints no burning method, and the cadmia chapter 5-84 (line 10220) was not read for its washing method. (4) Actions: "repress", "a little corrosive", "forms scabs", "ulcerating". No indication in a named complaint is given. The translator's bracket "[calcium carbonate]" after "chalcitis" is a gloss, not Dioscorides' description (he says chalcitis "looks like brass", line 10426).

**Neighbouring chapters.** 5-105 *Armenion* (the Armenian stone, "an azure [blue] colour", "does the same things as chrysocolla" but "not as strong"; line 10375) and 5-104 *Chrusokolla* (marked TOXIC by the translator, line 10366) are the blue and green copper materials just before it. Whether *kuanos* is a pigment, a mineral or a prepared product is not stated in 5-106. In the cadmia chapter (5-84, line 10223) a list of materials found together in a hill over Solis includes "coeruleus"; the text does not say that this is *kuanos*, and no equation is made here.

**Identity.** The translator's "Cyanochroite" is a named copper-potassium sulphate mineral. The chapter says nothing of the composition: it gives a colour (a "full colour", implied blue by the name), a copper-working origin ("brass minerals"), a Cyprian source, and corrosive actions. The identification therefore rests on the translator's suggestion and on those clues. The Codex's list row `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 832 reads "Kuanos,Copper and,plant,DMM V,no": the glossary gloss has been cut at its comma and the class mis-entered as "plant". [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Stated generally. Blue copper minerals and copper sulphates are copper compounds. Soluble copper salts are astringent and corrosive to tissue and toxic if swallowed; that fits the chapter's "repress", "a little corrosive", "forms scabs" and "ulcerating". The text gives no composition, and the identity above is a suggestion, so no specific composition is asserted for *kuanos*.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The chapter records a burnt and washed copper-bearing mineral with caustic actions and names no indication. A caustic copper material is not a self-care substance, and the sources give no protocol. No modern protocol is supplied.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only preparations of the mineral itself are given (loci above): gathering the material formed from sand at the Cyprian shore, burning "like chalcitis" and washing "like cadmia" (line 10379). The methods are by cross-reference and are not described in the chapter. No dose and no vehicle are given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. What he offers is a discipline of attention, and this short chapter shows it: a place of origin and of best quality (the shore sand of Cyprus), a rule of choice by depth of colour, and a stated preparation. The chapter is therapeutic and caustic in its actions; no cultivation status is assigned, and no amuletic use is recorded in the lines read.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator to take. What carries over is the habit of asking where a material was found, how it was made, and how it is chosen. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity:** copper salts are corrosive and poisonous if swallowed; the chapter's own words are "corrosive" and "ulcerating".
- **Skin and wounds:** caustic copper material can burn and scar; it is not a safe wound application.
- **Identity:** the chapter does not fix the composition and the translator's suggestion is not certain; the name cannot guarantee what a material contains.
- **Occupational:** burning copper-bearing minerals gives off irritant fumes, and the ores can carry arsenic or lead.
