# Hedysarum crinitum — Edusaron (Dioscorides 3-146; called pelecinus by the ointment makers)

## *Hedysarum crinitum (the first of four plants on the translators' SUGGESTED line: "Hedysarum crinitum — Crook-podded Hedysarum", then "Hedysarum coronarium — French Honeysuckle, Soola Clover", "Hedysarum alhagi, Alhagi maurorum, Alhagi mannifera — Camel Thorn, Egyptian Manna Plant" and "Biserrula pelecinus — Hatchet Vetch")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk names this plant. The entry rests on one chapter of one text. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed under the chapter heading, and not Dioscorides' own identification. The Codex does not state the identity as certain. Here the doubt is large: the SUGGESTED line gives four candidates in at least three genera (*Hedysarum*, *Alhagi*, *Biserrula*), and the entry takes the first only because the spine row does. The existing entry I_892 (Dioscorides 4-14, honeysuckle) remarks that "French Honeysuckle" under 3-146 is *Hedysarum coronarium*, which is the second candidate here. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *3-146. EDUSARON* at line 7800, SUGGESTED line at 7801, text at line 7802. The chapter stands in Book Three, "Roots", after 3-145 *ORMINON EMERON* and before 3-147 *ONOSMA*, both different plants. The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 552.

The whole chapter reads: "Hedysarum (called pelecinus by the ointment makers) is a shrub with little leaves similar to cicer [2-126], but pods like little horns in which is the red seed similar to an axe that has two edges (from which it is named). It is bitter to one who tastes it; a decoction (taken as a drink) is good for the stomach, and it is also mixed with antidotes. Taken as a pessary with honey before sexual intercourse it is thought to hinder conception. It grows among barley and wheat."

So the chapter gives a description (a shrub, little leaves like *cicer*, horn-like pods with a red axe-shaped seed), the origin of the name, a taste (bitter), three uses (a stomach drink, an ingredient of antidotes, and a contraceptive pessary), and a habitat (among barley and wheat). It prints no other names beyond *pelecinus*. The cross-reference to *cicer* [2-126] is the translators' and was not followed.

### Active Components

No analysis of the plant Dioscorides describes was read. The text prints no constituent, and the four candidate plants belong to different genera, so no composition can be stated for the plant of the chapter. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records a bitter stomach drink, an antidote ingredient and a pessary said to prevent conception. The Codex gives no protocol for any of these. The identity is a translators' suggestion among four, and a contraceptive pessary is not something to rely on from a translated herbal.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives these preparations: a decoction drunk (no vehicle named); the plant mixed into antidotes; and a pessary "with honey before sexual intercourse". No dose, quantity or duration is printed for any of them. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** chapter 3-146 prints one quality, a taste: the plant "is bitter to one who tastes it". It prints no hot, cold, moist or dry quality.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a description for recognition (horn-like pods, a red seed shaped like a double-edged axe), a sensory assay by taste (bitter), a habitat (among barley and wheat) and a trade name (*pelecinus*, "called ... by the ointment makers"). It is not a cultivation status. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The transferable point is the habit of tasting and describing a plant and of noting what trade names it. The text offers nothing more, and the abstentive answer is the right one.

### Contraindications

Chapter 3-146 prints no warning. The identification is only the translators' suggestion among four candidates, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. A pessary "thought to hinder conception" is not a reliable or safe contraceptive and is not advice. The decoction is recorded without a dose. [USER: verify]
