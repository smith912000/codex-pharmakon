# Calculi from a woman (mo.rdo) — mo.rdo, "female stone" (Clark 1995); "Modhe" (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011)
## *Kidney stones or gallstones removed from the body of a woman, which Clark's rendering of the Explanatory Tantra glosses under mo.rdo, literally "female stone"; the Tantra files it in its list of stone medicines* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The *mo.rdo* is entered in the Explanatory Tantra (*bshad rgyud*), chapter 20, the chapter on the powers of medicinal substances, in Clark 1995 (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). The chapter's "specific section" gives eight kinds of substance: "(1) precious [metal] medicines (2) stone medicines (3) earth medicines (4) tree medicines (5) resins (6) plants (7) herbs (8) animal products" (line 802), and *mo.rdo* is the last entry of "(2) The Powers of Stone Medicines" (heading at line 816). The line is 856:

"mo.rdo (lit. 'female stone'): [For example, kidney- or gallstones removed from the body of a woman. Such female stones] are beneficial for curing stone disorders [in men and vice versa. A certain similar smooth stone found on river banks is beneficial for blood clots and stone poisoning when used as a (cold) fomentation]."

So the verse itself, in Clark's rendering, appears to give only the name and the action "beneficial for curing stone disorders", and the brackets carry the explanation. Clark's brackets mark wording not in the main verse, and his introduction says he interpolated liberally from the *Blue Aquamarine* commentary (line 80); I did not confirm his exact convention. [USER: verify] On the face of Clark's text, three things follow. First, the substance is a calculus (a kidney or gall stone) removed from a woman. Second, a stone from a woman is used for the stone disorders "in men and vice versa", which reads as the rule that a stone from one sex is used against stones in the other. Third, the commentary adds a second, non-bodily material: a smooth river-bank stone that is similar and is used as a cold fomentation for blood clots and "stone poisoning". Which of the two the verse itself means, or whether the verse means both, is not stated in the lines read. [USER: verify]

*The Men-Tsee-Khang text.* In the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`) the stone medicines are given in one run of names with their actions (lines 3352-3357), and the last sentence of the run reads: "Dho thal dissolves consolidated mucous inside the stomach. Ha shik cleanses disorders of the channels and Modhe cures calculus." This translation transliterates *rdo* as "dho" elsewhere in the same passage (*Dho thal* for *rdo.thal*, *Dhosol* for *rdo.sol*), so "Modhe" is not an exact transliteration of *mo.rdo*. The match to Clark's *mo.rdo* rests on the position of the name after *rdo.thal* and *ha.shig* (Clark lines 854-856, in the same order) and on the shared action, stones. The translation gives no explanation of "Modhe", and so it does not say that the stone is from a woman. The classing of the material as mineral in the list, and as a stone in the Tantra's own list, against its filing as an animal-derived material, is recorded as an identity doubt for the editor.

*Related lines in the same chapter.* Other stone-dissolving materials sit nearby: coal (*rdo.sol*), which "dissolves stones [gall stones, kidney stones, etc.] and constricts the mouths of the channels" (Clark line 844), and saltpetre (*ze.tsha*, "Mirabilite"), which "dissolves stones [for example in the kidneys, gall bladder, etc.], removes calculi and stone tumors [and dispels urinary retention]" (line 860). In the section on animal products, "Human excreta" are said to "cure gall stones" (line 1199). Those are different materials with their own entries or none, and they are not folded in.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, urinary stones are crystalline aggregates most often of calcium oxalate or calcium phosphate, and less often uric acid, struvite or cystine, bound in a protein matrix; gallstones are mostly cholesterol or calcium bilirubinate. A stone removed from a person is a pathological deposit and not a medicine, and nothing links its composition to any action the Tantra gives.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Tibetan text records a stone removed from a woman's body as a medicine for "stone disorders", with the rule, in the bracketed commentary, that a stone from one sex serves for the other. The Codex does not recommend any use of a human calculus, for reasons of hygiene, of law and consent over human tissue, and of ethics. Kidney stones and gallstones are medical conditions with effective treatment, and the entry exists to record the text.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation of the stone and no way of taking it: whether it is ground, burnt or worn is not stated in the lines read, and the one named mode of use is the cold fomentation, which is given for the river-bank stone and not for the calculus. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Kidney stones form from substances in the urine that crystallise, and gallstones from bile; both are diagnosed and treated medically, by fluids, drugs, shock-wave or endoscopic treatment, or surgery as needed. No established pharmacology supports the use of a stone from another person to dissolve a stone, and a retained stone can cause severe pain, infection and kidney damage. Human tissue can carry blood-borne and other infections. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT):** The Materia Medica line gives the action "beneficial for curing stone disorders" and, for the river-bank stone in the bracket, a use as a cold fomentation. It gives no taste, potency or quality for the *mo.rdo*, and the Men-Tsee-Khang line adds no quality ("Modhe cures calculus").

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(c) Absent.* Neither translation read gives the *mo.rdo* any cultivation framing. It is a listed stone medicine for stone disorders (Clark line 856; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3357), which is a therapeutic placement and not a status. The wider frame is the tradition's own: the three humors are rooted in attachment, hatred and obscuration (Clark line 51), these are "the three causes" of wind, bile and phlegm disorders (line 147), so disease is at root a spiritual condition and medicine is one support. The chapter on routine behaviour calls for refraining from the ten negative actions (line 616). Those lines say nothing about this material. The rejuvenation passage in chapter 23 (lines 1262-1277) does not name it, and no *bcud len* use was found. The Tibetan tradition has no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice, and none is asserted here.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this. It is a list entry for a human calculus used against stones, with no preparation and no dose, and the Codex does not recommend using human tissue. The honest use of the entry is as a record of what the Tantra says. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Infection:** human tissue can carry blood-borne and other infections; a calculus from another person should not be handled or taken.
- **Law and consent:** the use, sale and transfer of human tissue are regulated in most countries, and the Codex does not recommend it.
- **Stone disease:** severe flank or abdominal pain, blood in the urine, fever with pain, or inability to pass urine needs urgent medical care; the text's "beneficial for curing stone disorders" is a historical statement and not a treatment.
- **Identity:** the Tantra files the substance among the stones, and the list marks it mineral; the Men-Tsee-Khang "Modhe" is matched to *mo.rdo* by position, not by exact spelling.
- **Ethics:** a stone from a living or dead person is human remains; any use needs consent and respect.
