# Antelope dung — "Antilope dung" (printed "Antilopedung" in the text on disk; Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus)
## *The droppings of an animal Bryan calls "the Antilope"; no Egyptian word, species or binomial is given in the sources retrieved, and the spine row's "Gazella / Antilope sp." is the list-maker's gloss* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The spine row reads "Antelope dung, Gazella / Antilope sp." with the note "Ebers; Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus" (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 77). Wikipedia was not used. The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). The animal list in Chapter VII (lines 1390-1404) has "Gazelle" (line 1398) but no "Antilope"; `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` has no row for the antelope. The word "Antilope" occurs only twice in the text on disk, at line 2026 (the fat, a separate item) and at line 2735 (the dung).

**The one passage.** The text was searched for "antelope", "antilope" and "oryx". The dung is in Chapter XVI "Diseases of the Eyes" (heading lines 2720-2721), in Bryan's account of remedies for bleary eyes, line 2735: "For Bleary Eyes one beat up Myrrh, Onions, Verdigris, and Cyperus-from-the-North, with Antilopedung, Clear Oil, and Entrails-of-the-qadit-animal. This could be painted on with a Vulture's feather." The sentence continues with Bryan's own comment that for those "whose means did not run to Antilopes and Vultures (not to mention qadit-animals) the homely Ass [...] stepped into the breach". The run-together word is an OCR join in the text on disk; it is read here as "Antilope dung", and Bryan's "Antilopes" in the next sentence supports that reading. The passage is Bryan's summary and gives no quantity. The Egyptian name of the *qadit*-animal is printed untranslated.

**Related items, not counted as the dung.** The fat of the same animal is a separate item (line 2026, in the anal remedies). "Gazelle's dung" is also a separate item (see its entry). The text on disk was searched for other antelope-dung passages and none was found.

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say which animal is meant, why the dung was chosen, whether it was dried, or how much was used. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated; the animal is not identified. In general terms, herbivore dung is mostly undigested plant fibre with bacteria and may carry intestinal parasites and pathogens.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show antelope dung once, as one ingredient of a paint-on mixture for bleary eyes. Putting animal dung, verdigris and other compounds on the eye is hazardous. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

---

### Preparation & Dosing

Only what Bryan reports: the ingredients are "beaten up" together and the mixture is "painted on with a Vulture's feather" (line 2735). No quantity is printed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Eye safety:** dung, verdigris and other raw ingredients applied to the eye risk infection and injury; this mixture must not be reproduced.
- **Hygiene:** animal dung can carry bacteria and parasites.
- **Conservation:** many antelope species are protected; the animal is not identified in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
