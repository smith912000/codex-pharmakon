# Spanish Garlic / Allium scorodoprasum — Skorodoprason (Dioscorides 2-183)

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry: named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** This row was added on 2026-10-09 by the plant-gap run (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP). The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed on the SUGGESTED line of the chapter, and the plant is named by no other text read. The chapter is three sentences long. This is a short entry.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`.

*2-183 SKORODOPRASON* (heading at line 6130; SUGGESTED line 6131: "Allium scorodoprasum — Spanish Garlic, Spanish Shallot"; text at line 6132, in the book on herbs with a sharp quality). The whole chapter reads: "Scordoprasum grows to the size of a leek, sharing the qualities of both garlic and leek (from which it has a mixed kind of strength), performing things that the garlic and the leek do, but with fewer efficacies. It is used as a vegetable, eaten boiled like leek and thus made to become sweet." The name is compounded of the Greek words for garlic (*skorodon*) and leek (*prason*), and the chapter treats it as a plant between the two.

**The chapters it refers to.** The chapter points to garlic and leek by comparison, so the two chapters just before it were read for this entry. Their contents are not attributed to this row.

*2-179 PRASON* (leek; heading at line 6086): the headed leek "is inflative, has bad juice, causes troublesome dreams, is uretic and good for the stomach, reduces the intensity of symptoms, causes dullness of sight, expels the menstrual flow, and hurts ulcerated bladders and kidneys" (line 6089). *2-182 SKORODON* (garlic; heading at line 6115): "It has a sharp, warming, biting quality. It expels flatulence, disturbs the belly, dries the stomach, causes thirst and puffing up, breeds boils on the outside of the body, and dulls vision" (line 6117). Both chapters go on to list many further uses. Their translator-suggested identities are *Allium porrum* (leek) and *Allium sativum* and relatives (garlic). The text says that 2-183 does "things that the garlic and the leek do, but with fewer efficacies", and it names no one of them. Which of the garlic and leek uses are meant for this plant cannot be read from the chapter. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Plants of the genus *Allium* are generally known to contain sulphur-bearing compounds (the source of the characteristic smell and sharpness). Whether the plant in 2-183 is *A. scorodoprasum* was not established, and no analysis of that species was read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The one thing the chapter states of its own accord is that this plant is a boiled vegetable, with the qualities of garlic and leek in a weaker form. Any medicinal use is only by reference. The Codex gives no protocol for it.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation given is in line 6132: it is "eaten boiled like leek and thus made to become sweet". No quantity is printed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Garlic and leeks are well-established food plants of the onion family, and eating them is an ordinary dietary use. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** 2-183 prints "a mixed kind of strength" from garlic and leek, and "fewer efficacies". The garlic chapter (2-182) prints "sharp, warming, biting"; that is not stated of this plant.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system. The chapter gives a size ("the size of a leek"), a relation to garlic and leek, and a way of eating. It gives no habitat, season or test of quality, and assigns the plant no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice. The plant is a boiled vegetable of weaker garlic and leek character in the text, and that is all the chapter states. The abstentive answer is the correct one.

### Contraindications

No safety information is printed for this plant. Garlic and leek relatives are generally known as food plants, but concentrated preparations of garlic can irritate the stomach and affect bleeding. None of the uses above is advice, and those uses belong to the chapters cited, not to this one.
