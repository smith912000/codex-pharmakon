### Origin & Tradition

Beet is an ancient Mediterranean cultivated plant of the goosefoot family, grown first for its leaves (the chard/spinach-beet forms) and only later bred for the swollen, sugar- and pigment-rich taproot familiar today. Dioscorides treats the beet in Book II among the pot-herbs, distinguishing the white and the dark (red) beets and noting both nutritive and medicinal virtues. The leaf was the older food and medicine; the deeply coloured root, with its staining crimson juice, gathered its own reputation as a blood-associated, "opening" and cleansing food by the doctrine of signatures and by genuine effect.

The classical and later traditions valued beet as a **mild aperient, deobstruent and "opener" of the liver and spleen**, a soother of the digestive tract, and a nutritive food for the depleted; the juice was used in the nose for headache and catarrh, and the leaves applied externally to inflammations and burns. The white beet was reckoned the more laxative/cleansing, the red the more nutritive and astringent. As a humble, cheap, sustaining vegetable beet was food and medicine together across the Mediterranean and European world.

Modern interest has transformed beetroot's profile through one constituent class: the **dietary nitrates**, which the body converts (via oral bacteria and tissues) to nitrite and then nitric oxide, a vasodilator. This underlies the now well-studied effects of beetroot juice on blood pressure and exercise performance — a genuinely modern chapter giving the old "blood" associations an unexpected vascular footing. The red pigments (betalains) are themselves antioxidant and the source of the harmless red urine/stool colouring (beeturia) that beet can cause.

### Active Components

- **Dietary nitrate (NO₃⁻)** — abundant in beetroot; reduced in the body to nitrite and nitric oxide (NO), a vasodilator; the basis of the studied blood-pressure-lowering and exercise-performance effects.
- **Betalains — betacyanins (betanin, the red-violet pigment) and betaxanthins (yellow)** — nitrogen-containing antioxidant pigments; betanin causes beeturia (harmless red urine) in susceptible people and is a natural food colourant.
- **Folate, potassium, manganese and fibre** — nutritive constituents underpinning the food value.
- **Sugars (sucrose — concentrated in sugar beet)** — the energy content; sugar beet is a major commercial sucrose source.
- **Oxalates** — present (especially in the leaves), relevant to the oxalate caution below.

### Contraindications

- **Oxalate / kidney stones.** Beet (especially leaves) is relatively high in oxalate; caution in calcium-oxalate stone formers.
- **Beeturia reassurance.** Red urine/stool after beet is harmless, not blood — but should not be confused with true bleeding.
- **Sugar (sugar-beet products).** Refined beet sugar is relevant to glycaemic control.
- **Hypotension / nitrate interactions.** The blood-pressure-lowering effect is relevant for those on antihypertensives or nitrate medication (theoretical additive effect). [USER: verify.]
- **Pregnancy.** Ordinary food amounts are fine and folate-rich.
