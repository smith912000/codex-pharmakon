# Honey stone (mellite) — ḥajar ʿasalī (حجر عسلي)

## *A stone "of honey", so called in the Canon for its excessive sweetness when scraped; "mellite" is the modern guess and is not supported by the text* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** Book II, entry *ḥajar ʿasalī*, Arabic text lines 13425 to 13428, in the same series of stones as the milk stone (*al-ḥajar al-labanī*, lines 13408 to 13414).

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** What it is: "a stone with a scraping (*ḥukāka*) of excessive sweetness, but it is like the milk stone in all its actions, and it has the power of *shādanaj* (hematite), and in it is some heat; and they count it among the medicines" (حجر له حكاكة مفرطة الحلاوة ولكنه كالحجر اللبني في جميع أفعاله وله قوة الشادنج وفيه حرارة ما ويعدونه من الأدوية). That is the whole entry. It has no dose and no other author. The actions are those of the milk stone, which the Canon describes in the entry before: a grey, sweet stone that gives a milk-like substance when scraped with water, kept in a lead box; it benefits the beginning of hot swellings, and as a kohl of its scrapings with water "prevents the flow of superfluities to the eye and the ulcers that befall it" (lines 13408 to 13414). The "power of *shādanaj*" is a comparison with a stone treated elsewhere in the Canon. That entry was not opened for this file; *shādanaj* is not the entry here.

**Identity doubt.** The spine's "mellite" is the modern mineral name for a honey-coloured organic crystal (aluminium mellitate), first described in the 18th century. Nothing in the entry says honey-coloured; it says only that the scraping is very sweet. The match to mellite is therefore a guess. The Arabic may mean a stone named for honey in colour or in taste; the entry says taste. A stone whose scraping is sweet may be a salt of lead or of an alkaline earth; the text does not say.

### Active Components

None is given. If the sweetness is real the substance is not a silicate rock, but the text does not say what it is. [USER: verify.] Lead salts are sweet and toxic; this is a general fact, not an identification, and the Codex does not identify the stone as such.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The text records a sweet stone that acts like the milk stone, as an eye and swelling remedy. The Codex gives no protocol, since the stone cannot be identified.

### Preparation & Dosing

The entry gives none. For the milk stone, which it follows: grind with water and keep what dissolves in a lead box; use scrapings with water as a kohl. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Avicenna: "in it is some heat" (line 13427); the "power of *shādanaj*"; like the milk stone in all actions. No degree is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** A comparative entry on a sweet stone; nothing on the vital faculties. **(c) Absent: no cultivation framing is attested.**

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing to practise here. The entry is an example of a Canon drug known only by its likeness to another.

### Contraindications

- Identity unknown; do not infer safety from the word "honey."
- Possible lead or other toxic content if the sweetness is a metal salt (see Active Components).
- Eye complaints need medical care.
