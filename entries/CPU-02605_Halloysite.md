# Halloysite — rdo.klad (lit. "stone brain"), rGyud-bzhi Explanatory Tantra ch.20

## *A white "pimply" stone that "looks like a brain", which "maintains the brain and stimulates flesh growth"; "Halloysite" is the translator's (Clark 1995) gloss for the Tibetan name. In mineralogy halloysite is a white clay mineral of the kaolin group, but Clark uses the same English word for several different Tibetan stones, so the identification is the translator's and of medium confidence; the white "brain-like" description does not by itself prove it* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

### Origin & Tradition

This is a single-text entry. The Codex list row is `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` row 15: "rdo.klad (lit. 'stone brain'), Clark 1995 (line 837), Halloysite, mineral". The row was entered on 8 November 2026 as an unverified placeholder because the spine held only the coloured halloysites; this entry replaces it.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The text is in Clark's 1995 translation of the Explanatory Tantra, chapter 20 (materia medica), under the heading "(2) The Powers of Stone Medicines" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 816). Line 837 reads: "rdo.klad (lit. 'stone brain'): Halloysite: [is a white 'pimply' stone that looks like a brain]. It maintains the brain and stimulates flesh growth." In Clark's typography the square brackets carry the commentarial description, and the sentence after the bracket is the tantra's own statement of power. So the description (white, pimply, brain-like) is commentary, and "maintains the brain and stimulates flesh growth" is the power the tantra assigns.

The Tibetan name is a compound of *rdo* (stone) and *klad* (brain), and Clark prints that literal gloss, so the name itself carries the likeness. The name is also listed in a run of Tibetan names at line 1708 of the same file (a glossary strip beginning "rdo.klad. rdo.mkhris. rdo.rgyus. rdo.chhu. rdo.thal ..."), which agrees with the order of the stones in the chapter. That line gives no further information. The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text on disk was searched for the name and for "stone brain" and nothing was found, so this is Clark alone.

**Neighbouring halloysites, kept apart.** Clark glosses three other stones as halloysite. be.snabs, "Yellow Halloysite", "of two types: pale yellow and yellowish brown", "cures fractures and glandular growths, and stimulates flesh growth" (line 824; Codex rows CPU-02617 and CPU-02455). btsag, "Red Ochre: Halloysite", found as a red stone, and yug, "a blue type of Halloysite", which together "cure eye diseases and bone fever and dry lymph" (line 852; row CPU-02405 for the blue). rdo.klad is the white, brain-like stone and is a different Tibetan name with a different power. The only power it shares with yellow halloysite is the stimulation of flesh growth.

### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms, halloysite is a hydrated aluminium silicate of the kaolin group, usually white, and is known as a clay. Whether the tantra's stone is that mineral is not established by the text. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the tradition says it is for.** To "maintain the brain" and to stimulate flesh growth (Clark line 837).
- **What the tradition does not say.** No preparation, no vehicle, no dose, no duration, no combination, and no stated disease. "Maintains the brain" is the translator's rendering of a Tibetan phrase and is not translated here into a modern claim about the brain.

The Codex does not recommend swallowing or applying a stone on the strength of a likeness in its name. A stone that "looks like a brain" and "maintains the brain" is the kind of correspondence that sounds persuasive and has no support in the retrieved sources beyond the single sentence.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Halloysite is a kaolin-group clay mineral that can form tubes at the microscopic level, and kaolin clays are used in pharmacy as inert carriers or adsorbents. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT (Clark 1995).** The stone is listed by power only. No taste, potency, or post-digestive taste is printed for it at line 837, and none is imported here. In this section of the chapter, stones are generally arranged by action on a condition, not by the three *nyes pa*.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The stone sits among the stone medicines of the chapter and is given a brain-related and a flesh-related action and nothing more (Clark line 837). No *bcud len* (essence extraction) use is attached to it in the text read. The one substance of this section that the chapter calls "unsurpassed as an essence-extraction substance" is *brag.zhun* (Clark line 870), not this stone. The tradition's frame is that medicine is the third of four treatment branches and supports practice without reaching its root; no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists here, and none is asserted.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Little to map: the line names two sites and one non-site. It is said to maintain the brain, which in modern anatomy is the brain and central nervous system, and to stimulate flesh growth, which maps to the soft tissues and flesh of the body. The Tibetan name, stone brain (rdo.klad), carries the likeness, and the commentary's white, pimply appearance is description, not an organ claim. The chapter arranges the stone medicines by action on a condition rather than by the three nyes pa, and the entry says no taste, potency or post-digestive taste is printed for it at this line. No channel, organ system or humour beyond the brain is named, and the stone is not described for use in essence extraction. The mapped claim is therefore the brain and the soft flesh tissue only.

### Contraindications

- **Ingestion of mineral material.** Unknown mineral stones can carry heavy metals or other contaminants. The text gives no preparation. Do not swallow any stone on the strength of this entry.
- **Identity.** The Tibetan name, the English gloss and the mineral are three different things here. A stone sold under either name cannot be taken to be the one in the tantra. [USER: verify]
- **Brain and wound conditions.** These are medical matters; use proper care. The Codex draws no treatment from this line.
