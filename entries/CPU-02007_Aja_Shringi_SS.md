# Aja-Shringi — Aja-s'ringi (Sushruta; Valli-Panchamulam group)

## *A creeper whose root belongs to the "five roots of creepers" group of the Sushruta Samhita; the translator's Appendix 3 pairs the name by position with Gymnema sylvestre (R. Br.), a climbing plant of the milkweed family, so the identification is the translator's: Gymnema sylvestre, medium confidence (the group is described as creepers and the climber fits), species not stated in the body of the text* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The only attestation is the Sushruta Samhita in Bhishagratna's English translation. A search of the other texts on disk for *Aja-shringi* and *Gymnema* found nothing. The Codex's "check" guess, "Unresolved (Aja-shringi)", is replaced here by the translator's own appendix equation.

**Sushruta (SS).** The name occurs in three places.

First, in the drug groups of Sutrasthana ch. 38: "The group consisting of the roots of the five medicinal creepers known as Vidari, Sariva, Rajani, Guduchi and Aja-Shringi, is called the Valli-Panchamulam" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 512-516; Vol. I line 15857). The group is followed by the Pancha-kantakam group (Karamradda, Trikantaka, Sairiyaka, Shatavari, Gridhranakhi) and a metrical text: "The two preceding groups prove curative in Haemoptysis and in all the three types of anasarca or oedema (Shopha). Moreover, it has the incontestable virtue of arresting all sorts of urethral discharges and is a potent remedy in all cases of seminal disorders." At the close of the list of Panchamulas the chapter adds that those standing third and fourth in order, "the Valli and Kantaka Panchamulas, subdue the deranged Kapham" (lines 546-552). Both statements are made of the group, not of Aja-Shringi alone.

Second, in a recipe of Chikitsasthana ch. 17, in the treatment of the Kaphaja type of Visarpa (erysipelas): "a medicinal plaster (Pradeha) composed of Aja-gandha, As'va-gandha, Sarala, Kala, Ekaishika and Aja-s'ringi pasted with the urine of a cow" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt` lines 118-122). The text prints the name there as *Aja-s'ringi*.

Third, in "Appendix No. 3. Names of Drugs (with Latin synonyms)" at the end of Vol. III: the Sanskrit name *Aja-s'ringi* (printed "Aja-^ringi") is at line 31157 (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`). The appendix's two columns were separated by the OCR. Counting down the A section, the Latin line "Gymnema Sylvestre." (line 31214) falls against it: *Ajagandha* (line 31153) is paired with "Ocymum gratissimum" (31210), *Ajamoda* with "Pimpinella Involucrata" (31212), and *Aja-s'ringi* with "Gymnema Sylvestre" (31214), followed by "Cassayta filiformis" (31215) against *Akasa-valli*. The same count holds for the pairs I checked before it (for instance *Ahigandha* against "Aristolochia Indica", line 31207). The pairing is by position and rests on the translator's appendix. ### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Gymnema sylvestre* is a climber of the milkweed family (Apocynaceae) known to contain triterpenoid saponins called gymnemic acids, a fact that is generally known and is stated here as background only. Whether the plant meant by the Sushruta name is this species is not established. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** For Sushruta, the root is one of five creeper roots in a group named for what it acts on (Kapham, haemoptysis, oedema, urinary and seminal discharges), and an ingredient in a plaster for a skin disease. The Codex gives no protocol and offers no modern use.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations in the text: the root as one of five in a group (no quantities stated in the lines read), and a plaster of six drugs pasted with cow's urine (Chikitsa ch. 17). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Gymnema sylvestre* is a woody climber of tropical Asia and Africa, and its leaves contain gymnemic acids, which in laboratory studies interfere with the taste of sweetness; whether the same plant is meant here is the translator's equation. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Aja-Shringi in the lines read. The group's stated action (above) is on Kapham, with the particular effects named, and applies to the group.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug sits inside the Valli-Panchamulam, whose stated action is against Kapham, haemoptysis, oedema and discharges of the urinary and seminal tracts. "Seminal disorders" there are disorders to be cured, a therapeutic frame; the group is not placed in a *vajikarana* or *rasayana* chapter in the lines read. No cultivation status (no *ojas* claim, no rejuvenation) is attested for the drug itself.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do. A root in a group for Kapham and discharges, and in a plaster for erysipelas, is a treatment record. The tradition's own words give a reader no cultivation use.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The Latin name is the translator's and the pairing is positional. Do not use a plant on the strength of this entry.
- **Blood sugar.** *Gymnema* is generally regarded as lowering blood sugar, so anyone on diabetes medication should not take it unsupervised. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Haemoptysis, oedema, skin disease.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
