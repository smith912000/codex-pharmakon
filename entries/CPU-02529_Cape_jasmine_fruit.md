# Cape jasmine fruit — 卮子 *zhizi* (栀子), also 木丹, 樾桃

## *The fruit of Gardenia jasminoides J.Ellis, the cape jasmine; the identification is ours, made from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence. Sun Xingyan writes the name 卮子 and notes that older texts wrote 栀* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk as a monograph.** The entry rests on one text in one edition, Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The only other trace on disk is one sentence in a modern book on the same tradition, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, line 2482), which says that yang jaundice requires Artemisia capillaris "in combination with Rhubarb and Gardenia". That is a modern formula remark and not a classical entry, and it is not used below as a source for the drug's classical use. Two existing entries mention the drug as a formula partner (I_1235 *Artemisia capillaris*, I_203 *Rheum*) and do not treat it.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The name line is 卮子 (heading in the 中经 section), followed by Sun's note "旧作栀，《艺文类聚》及《御览》引作支，是", i.e. "formerly written 栀; the *Yiwen leiju* and *Yulan* quote it as 支, which is right". The classic's own paragraph is at line 2399: 味苦，寒。主五内邪气，胃中热气，面赤、酒泡、[a character lost]鼻，白赖、赤癞，创疡。一名木丹。生川谷。 In English: "Flavour bitter, cold. Governs evil qi in the five interiors (五内邪气), hot qi in the stomach (胃中热气), red face, wine-blisters (酒泡), [the nose, one character lost], white *lai*, red *lai* (白赖、赤癞; skin conditions that the Codex keeps in the text's words), and sores and ulcers (创疡). Also called 木丹 ('wood cinnabar'). Grows in river valleys." There is no 久服 clause.

The classic grades the entry in the middle class (中经; heading 卷二 中经 at line 2407). The preface (line 1677) says the middle class "nourishes the nature to correspond to man, may be non-toxic or toxic, and the choice is weighed as fit" (主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜), and is for those who want to hold off illness and replenish the lean (欲遏病补羸者).

**Mingyi bielu (《名医》, later additions) layer.** Line 2403: "also called 樾桃; grows at Nanyang; the fruit is gathered in the ninth month and dried in the sun" (一名樾桃。生南阳。九月采实，曝干). The part, the fruit (实), is named by this layer and not by the classic's paragraph.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案).** Line 2405. The *Shuowen*: "栀 is a yellow wood that can be used to dye" (栀，黄木可染者). The *Guangya*: "栀子 is 樾桃" (the transcription has a lost character before 桃). The *Shiji* "Huozhi" chapter says "the land of Ba and Shu is rich in 卮" (巴蜀地饶卮), and the commentary *Jijie* quotes Xu Guang for the sound 支 and for the colour (as printed, "紫，赤色也"). Sun concludes that "according to the *Shuowen* it should be 栀" (据《说文》当为栀). The note is about writing and sound and adds nothing on use, except the dyeing remark in the *Shuowen*.

### Active Components

None is stated in the text. As a general fact, the fruit of *Gardenia jasminoides* contains iridoid glycosides (geniposide is the best known) and yellow carotenoid pigments of the crocin type, and has been used as a yellow dye and food colourant in East Asia. That agrees with the *Shuowen*'s "yellow wood that can be used to dye". The Codex retrieved no analytical source for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The classic records a bitter, cold fruit for evil qi in the interiors, hot qi in the stomach, a red face and wine-blisters, certain skin conditions, and sores. The Codex gives no protocol and does not translate "hot qi in the stomach" into a modern pattern or diagnosis. The fruit remains a standard drug in modern Chinese materia medica, which is a general fact. The Codex does not describe that use further.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic gives no preparation, no part and no quantity. The Mingyi layer gives the part (the fruit), the gathering month (the ninth) and the drying (in the sun). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

The identification is ours. Pigment and iridoid chemistry of the fruit is general knowledge. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] Terms such as "evil qi in the five interiors" and "white *lai*" are not modern diagnoses, so no modern finding can be set beside them.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Divine Farmer's Classic).** 味苦，寒, "bitter, cold" (line 2399). No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle class (中经), the class the preface describes as nourishing the nature, sometimes toxic, and fit for holding off illness. Its 味 paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 卮子 *zhizi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2399). Its cultivation status is therefore not attested; only its therapeutic one is. The Mingyi and 案 layers add none.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator has no practice here. The classic offers a bitter, cold fruit for heat and skin complaints in the middle class, with no long-use claim, no preparation and no dose.

### Contraindications

The classic does not warn. No safety information for pregnancy, children or other medicines was read, and none is asserted. The fruit is a dye plant, and material sold as a dye may differ from material sold as a drug. Sores, ulcers and skin conditions need medical assessment. No conservation information was read.
