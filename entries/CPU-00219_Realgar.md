**Assyrian (AT).** No Assyrian locus was found. `_sources/assyrian.csv` has no realgar row, and the only Assyrian text on disk (Thompson 1923, `Sources/AT_Assyrian/text/Thompson_1923_Assyrian_Medical_Texts.txt`) is OCR of cuneiform copy-plates that cannot be searched for a substance (`Sources/AT_Assyrian/extract/README.md`: "nothing extractable"). **Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 5-121 ARSENIKON (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10456) is headed by the translator "SUGGESTED: Orpiment — Yellow Arsenic, Realgar — Arsenic disulphide" and "POISONOUS". The text says it is found in the same mines as *sandaracha*; the best is "crusty, gold in colour and with crusts like fish scales", unmixed, from Mysia; a second kind comes "in clumps and like sandaracha in colour" from Pontus and Cappadocia. It is roasted in a new ceramic jar over burning coals, stirred until it is red-hot and has changed colour, then cooled, beaten fine and stored. Its strength is "antiseptic, astringent, and scab forming with a burning, strong, biting strength", and it is among the medicines that "repress abnormal growths and make the hair fall out". The next chapter, 5-122 SANDARACHE (line 10461), describes a mineral "fully red, brittle, easily pounded into small pieces, and pure — looking like cinnabar in colour, and also having a sulphurous smell", with "the same properties and method of roasting as arsenicum". The translator ties 5-121 to realgar and orpiment together; he does not tie 5-122 to realgar, and neither do I. [USER: verify]

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No Avicenna locus was found. `_sources/avicenna.csv` has no realgar row (its mineral rows are Borax, Iron, Mercury and Salt) and no Avicenna text is on disk. No *Canon* chapter reference exists to give. The tag rests on the spine row alone. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Xiong Huang is entry 雄黄 in `_sources/shennong.csv` line 18 and appears in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 1063, p.27) under "Jades and Stones: Superior Class": "Xiong Huang (Realgar) is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats cold and heat, mouse fistulas, malign sores, flat abscesses, hemorrhoids, and dead muscles. It kills spiritual matters, vicious demons, evil qi, and hundreds of toxic worms and insects. It overpowers the five weapons. Taking it after it is sublimated may make one an immortal with a light body." Other name: Huang Shi Shi (Yellow Edible Stone). Yang's note 50 explains "spiritual matter" as a spirit or ghost causing sudden fulminant disease with mental disorder; note 51 reads "overpowers the five weapons" as a protective power when worn as an amulet (lines 1085-1092).

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Manahshila* is named by Bhishagratna as "Realgar". *Sutrasthana* ch.1 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch01.txt` lines 448-451) lists "Metals and minerals such as gold, silver, gems, and Manahshila (Realgar)" with pearls, clay and bones among the earthy substances. It is an ingredient of the plug-lint and medicated oil in ch.36 (`SS_Sutra_ch36.txt` lines 99, 136), of a smoke for cough in ch.40 (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch40.txt` line 30), of plasters in *Chikitsasthana* ch.18 line 432, ch.19 lines 135 and 285 and ch.25 line 234, and of the scarifying collyrium in *Uttarasthana* ch.12 (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch12.txt` line 145, "metallic substances (Manah-s'ila, Gairika, etc.)"). *Sutrasthana* ch.13 (`SS_Sutra_ch13.txt` line 92) uses its colour as a simile for the *Kapila* leeches. The two mineral poisons named in *Kalpasthana* ch.2 (`kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch02.txt` line 49) are white arsenic and *Haritala*, glossed "yellow orpiment"; *Manahshila* is not named there. No properties for it are printed in the ch.46 groups.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** In the Explanatory Tantra ch.20 materia medica, stone medicines (Clark 1995, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 842): "ldong.ros: Realgar: Red Orpiment: [has three names and is an orange mineral with (its own peculiar) odor.]" It is followed by *ba.bla*, yellow orpiment, with "Both types of orpiment arrest malignant glandular growths and necrosis [and protect from infectious fever caused by spirits]" (line 843); the realgar line has no action of its own, so whether the phrase covers it rests on Clark's gloss "Red Orpiment". The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3356, p.199) prints "Dong roe and Bhala heal gangrene of an inflammed lymph node"; I read these as the phonetic forms of *ldong.ros* and *ba.bla*. ### Active Components

Realgar is a mineral of arsenic and sulphur, red to orange, and arsenic compounds are toxic. Heating arsenic sulphides in air (roasting, "sublimating") gives volatile arsenic oxides, which are more hazardous than the mineral. The texts above do not describe the composition; this paragraph is general chemistry only.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Realgar is an arsenic compound. The Greek text itself heads the chapter "POISONOUS" and lists the hair falling out among its effects, and the Chinese classic's promise of long life and immortality from the sublimated mineral belongs to a Daoist elixir literature (Yang preface, lines 348-400, and note 6) that no present-day practice can responsibly follow. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation. The entry records what the eight texts say and where they are silent.

### Preparation & Dosing

DMM 5-121 gives a roasting method (above). SBJ says only "taking it after it is sublimated" (line 1063) and gives no method. The Sushruta uses are external plasters, lint, oil and smoke, with no quantities in the lines read. Clark gives no preparation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Arsenic is a recognised poison and carcinogen, and chronic exposure damages skin, nerves, liver and other organs. Arsenic trioxide, a different compound, is used under specialist supervision in modern oncology; that does not make realgar a medicine. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No Egyptian locus for realgar was found (above). The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. No deity is attributed.

**Assyrian (AT).** *(c) Absent, no locus.* Nothing was read. The blocking warning is in force, and whether the substance was classed by the agency it opposes cannot be said. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What 5-121 and 5-122 offer is a discipline of attention: the best kind is crusty, gold in colour, in scales, unmixed; the place of origin is named (Mysia, Pontus, Cappadocia); the roasting is given step by step; the chapter is marked poisonous by the translator. No cultivation status is assigned.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent, and the attestation rests on a spine row.* No Avicenna text or list row was found. Innate heat and radical moisture cannot be tied to realgar without a source and are not tied to it here. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: upper grade.* Xiong Huang stands under "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (Yang p.27, line 1063). The preface (lines 348-400) defines the superior class as medicinals that "mainly nourish life", "nontoxic" and not harmful "in large amounts and for a long time", and says that to make the body light, boost the qi, prevent aging and prolong life "one should base [one's efforts] on the superior class". The entry itself says that taken after sublimation it "may make one an immortal with a light body". Yang's preface note 6 ties such passages to Daoist elixir practice. The preface's "nontoxic" is at odds with the mineral's chemistry, so the grade records what the classic says, not a safe practice. No *jing* action is asserted.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* *Manahshila* is classed among the earthy substances (ch.1) and used externally and as smoke; it is not named in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter in the passages read, and no *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Shodhana* (purification) for it is not described in the lines read.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed.* The entry sits in the stone medicines of ch.20, which are therapeutic: orpiment, with realgar as its "red" kind, checks malignant glandular growths and necrosis and protects from spirit-caused infectious fever (Clark lines 842-843). Medicine is the third of the four treatment branches in this tradition and supports practice. No *bcud len* use and no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice was found for it.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing sourced here supports taking realgar for cultivation. The one tradition that grades it highly, the Chinese classic, does so inside an elixir literature whose own preface says the superior class is nontoxic, which the mineral is not. A cultivator can read the record for what it shows (a feared poison used by the ancients externally and in alchemy) and leave it alone. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Realgar is an arsenic compound: toxic by mouth and by inhaled fumes, with long-term risks including cancer. Roasting or sublimating it releases arsenic oxides. Not for internal use, not for children or in pregnancy. Sale and use of arsenic-containing products is restricted in most jurisdictions; check the local rules. Skin contact with preparations can irritate and be absorbed.
