### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, and only in the index.** This is a single-text entry, and the whole of the attestation is one line of a name index. The text is Bhishagratna's English translation, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it.

**Ayurveda (SS).** Appendix No. 3 of Volume III (heading at line 31119) is a list of the Sanskrit plant names that occur in the translation, each with a Latin or English equivalent or a cross-reference. The names are printed in one column and the equivalents in another, and the OCR has separated the two. Reading the two columns together, the names "Bandhu-jiva", "Bandhuka", "Bandhyakotika", "Barbara / Barbula", "Bhadra-kali", "Bhallektaka" (lines 31391-31399) fall against the equivalents "Pentapetes phoenicea", "Pentapetes phoenicea", "See Katkali", "Accacia Arabica", "See Prasarani", "Semecarpus Anacardium" (lines 31448-31455). So **Bhadra-kali is printed at line 31398 and answered by "See Prasarani" at line 31454**. The alignment is ours, from the order of the two columns, and is supported by the neighbouring pairs, in which "Barbula" is paired with the Acacia (the babul) and "Bhallektaka" with the marking nut. *What the index does not give.* The index has no entry of its own for Prasarani that could be found (a search of Vol. III for "Prasar" finds only the cross-reference line, 31454), so the chain stops there. The one place in the translation where "(Prasarani)" appears as a gloss is in Vol. II (line 18919): "Apehivdtd (Prasarani) and Das'a-mula cooked with the four kinds of lardacious or emollient substances (oil, clarified butter ...)". That is Bhishagratna's gloss of another Sanskrit name; whether it is the plant the index means is not stated, and we do not assume it.

*Where the name is not found.* A search of the three Bhishagratna volumes and of the chapter files (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/{sutra,chikitsa,uttara,kalpa,nidana,sharira}/`) for "Bhadra-kali", "Bhadrakali" and "Bhadra kali" finds no occurrence outside the index. So no text of the Samhita is known to us that uses this name for a drug, gives it a taste, a use or a group, or places it in a chapter.
