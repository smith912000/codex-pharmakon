# Rhinoceros hide — "Rhino hide" (Clark 1995, Explanatory Tantra ch.20); "the hide of the rhinoceros" (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011); Tibetan *Semuk*, *Seru* in the glossary
## *The hide (skin) of the rhinoceros. Both Tibetan texts print the English only; the species meant is not stated in the medicinal passage* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The only tradition attesting this material in the sources retrieved is the Tibetan *Four Tantras*. In Clark's 1995 translation (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`; "Clark" below) the animal medicines close chapter 20 of the Explanatory Tantra: "The thirteen medicines of animal origin consist of horn, bone, meat, blood, bile, fat, brain, skin, nails, hair, urine, droppings and whole body" (line 1148). The hide is filed under the eighth, "Skin" (heading at line 1183), which reads: "Snake skin: cures leucoderma, glang.shu (a skin disease) [and is beneficial against bhutaspirits]. Rhino hide and Ox hide: cure smallpox. Rat and Mouse skin: drains all types of pus accumulations" (lines 1184-1185). The statement about the hide stands outside Clark's square brackets, which mark interpolations from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries (line 82), so it is the root-text claim as Clark renders it. The spine's locus is that line 1185.

The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 edition of the Root and Explanatory Tantras (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`; "MTK" below) has the same claim in the same section (lines 3554-3555): "The hide of the rhinoceros and the ox cure small pox." The two translations agree in substance: one disease, shared with the ox hide. MTK's Glossary on Animals gives the Tibetan as *Semuk Seru* with the English "Black Rhinoceros Rhinoceros" (lines 7123, 7125-7127) and the Latin names "Rhinoceros bicornis L." and "Rhinoceros imus Burchell" (line 7130). Which of these the physicians meant for the hide is not said. Clark's horn section has a separate entry for "Rhino" (*bse.ru*, line 1152: "[has four names and is of three colors, white, variegated and black in descending order of quality]... It dries [accumulations of] pus, blood and lymph in the stomach [and is beneficial in cases of poisoning]"). That is another material, the horn, and it is not treated here. The list row `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` line 222 (*bse.ru*, "Rhino") is class-labelled "plant" in the extract, which appears to be an error in the extract for an animal entry.

### Active Components

Rhinoceros hide is the thick dermis of a large mammal, mainly collagen, with keratin in the outer layer. The Tibetan texts name no constituent and no mechanism for the claim, and this entry supplies none.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The single claim is that the hide cures smallpox. Smallpox was declared eradicated in 1980, so the claim has no present use, and no source retrieved says how the hide was used (applied, burnt, taken). Rhinoceros species are protected animals, and their hide, like their horn, is a traded wildlife product (see Contraindications). Clark adds that "nowadays only very few animal products are used" (line 57). The Codex's application layer has nothing to add, and no protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

Neither text gives a preparation (raw, burnt, powdered), a form or a dose for the hide. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] The compounding chapter (Clark ch.21, from line 1204) lists medicines by the disease they cure, and the rhinoceros hide is not named in the lists read.

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] Smallpox is caused by a virus and was eradicated by vaccination; nothing retrieved supports an effect of any animal hide on it.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT).** Clark and MTK print no taste, potency or post-digestive taste for the hide. Clark's preface says that detailed texts such as the *Crystal Orb* record for each substance "which of the six tastes, eight natural potencies, three post-digestive 'tastes,' seventeen secondary qualities and individual uses each possesses" (line 57); those texts were not read. The dietary chapter lists the rhinoceros among the larger hill and game animals whose flesh is eaten (Clark line 672; MTK line 2907), but that is about the meat of the animal and says nothing about its hide. The one attribute the sources give for the hide is its action on smallpox.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The hide is attested in the Explanatory Tantra's materia medica as one of the skins in the animal-medicines list, with one disease named (Clark lines 1183-1185; MTK lines 3554-3555), shared with the ox hide. The tradition has a formal division of rejuvenation, "essence-extraction" (Clark line 128, "the division of essence-extraction (rejuvenation, etc.)"; also line 136), and no animal substance in the section read (lines 1147-1202) is assigned to it; the hide is not named in the compounding lists of chapter 21. Clark's own statement of what is rejuvenating is about plants (line 69). In this tradition medicine is one branch of treatment and the root of disease lies in the three poisons; no passage read ties the hide to that frame. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in the Tibetan sources, and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here supports a cultivation use, and a cultivator has two reasons to leave it alone: the disease it names is gone, and the animal is endangered. The honest entry is a record of what the Tibetan materia medica listed.

### Contraindications

- **Legal and conservation status:** all rhinoceros species are protected, and international trade in rhinoceros parts is prohibited or tightly restricted under CITES. Possessing or trading hide, horn or derivatives can be a serious offence, and the demand drives poaching. [USER: verify]
- **Hygiene and infection:** untreated hide from any large mammal can carry bacterial spores and parasites.
- **Smallpox:** the disease has been eradicated; the claim cannot be tested, and no animal material is a substitute for vaccination or care in any serious skin or viral illness.
- **Ethics:** use of a threatened animal's body has no support in the sources and a heavy conservation cost.
