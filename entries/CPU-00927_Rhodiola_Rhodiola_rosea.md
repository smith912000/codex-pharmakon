# Rhodiola / Golden Root — رودیولا / Ῥοδίολα / Suvarṇamūla / 紅景天
## *Rhodiola rosea L.* — Roseroot, Golden Root, Arctic Root, King's Crown

> ⚠️ **Species caution, stated up front.** This entry is filed as *Rhodiola rosea*, and its **pharmacological and clinical** material (rosavins, the Soviet-era research, the Western RCTs) genuinely concerns that species. But **both of its tradition tags describe a different species**: the Chinese *hóng jǐng tiān* and the Tibetan *sro lo dmar po* are principally ***Rhodiola crenulata***. The traditional attestations below are therefore **congener** material and must not be read as traditional attestation of *R. rosea* itself. Flagged in the 2026-08-10 audit; a species split may be warranted.

### Origin & Tradition

Hóng Jǐng Tiān (紅景天, "Red Stalk Heaven Plant") is a succulent perennial of the Crassulaceae family growing at high altitude (1,800–5,000 meters) in the arctic, subarctic, and alpine zones of Europe, Asia, and North America. It prefers rocky, exposed, cold terrain — cliff faces, scree slopes, mountain ridges — and is particularly abundant in Siberia, the Tibetan Plateau, Scandinavia, the Faroe Islands, and Iceland. The root, when cut, releases a distinctive rose-like fragrance from its phenylethanol derivatives — this fragrance gives the species its name (*rosea* — rose-scented). Rhodiola represents the definitive performance adaptogen: while ginseng is the premier tonic and ashwagandha the premier anabolic adaptogen, Rhodiola's pharmacological profile is oriented most specifically toward acute cognitive performance, physical work capacity, and anti-fatigue effects, with the most robust sports science evidence base of any adaptogen for acute ergogenic effects. Between the 1960s and 1980s, Rhodiola rosea was studied more intensively than any other adaptogen by Soviet pharmacologists — by Brekhman, Dardymov, Saratikov, Krasnov and colleagues at the Far Eastern Scientific Center in Vladivostok — specifically for military, cosmonautic, and athletic performance enhancement. This research body was largely inaccessible to Western science until the 1990s–2000s translations by Panossian and Wikman, after which Western clinical trials rapidly validated the primary findings.

**TCM (Bencao Gangmu, Li Shizhen, 1596 CE, and later Qing supplements):** ⚠️ **Species caution — the Chinese drug is not this entry's species.** *Hóng Jǐng Tiān* denotes ***Rhodiola crenulata***, the high-altitude plateau species; Booker et al. (2016) find *R. crenulata* to be the species used medicinally in Chinese practice. The Chinese material below therefore describes a **congener** of *R. rosea* and should not be read as attestation of *R. rosea* itself. Note also that this is **post-classical** material: Rhodiola is not a *Shennong Bencao Jing* drug, and this section was mis-headed "SBJ" in an earlier version — the tag was relabelled to TCM in the 2026-08-10 audit. *(An earlier version also asserted that "Li Shizhen's primary SBJ text addresses related Sedum-family plants with overlapping applications"; that claim was unsourced and has been withdrawn.)* The Traditional Chinese Medicine characterization: sweet and slightly astringent in flavor; cold in nature; Lung and Heart channel affinity. Primary actions recorded: invigorates blood and removes stasis (*huó xuè huà yū*); relieves cough and dispels phlegm (*zhǐ ké huà tán*); tonifies Qi and clears heat (*bǔ qì qīng rè*). Classical applications include altitude sickness (*gāo yuán fǎn yìng*), fatigue, and blood stasis disorders.

**FMT (Four Medical Tantras, Gyushi, 12th century CE):** The Tibetan medical tradition has the most extensive historical documentation of Rhodiola, which is native to the Tibetan Plateau and has been used in Tibetan medicine for at least a thousand years. The Tibetan name is ***sro lo dmar po***. *(An earlier version also gave "*btsod*" as a synonym; the 2026-08-10 audit could not find *btsod* used for Rhodiola in any source consulted, and it has been removed rather than left to propagate.)* The *Gyushi* Tantras document it for *rlung* disorders (anxiety, cognitive impairment, physical exhaustion, altitude-related illness — all with modern pharmacological correlates), cardiovascular conditions, and respiratory disorders at altitude. Tibetan physicians particularly valued Rhodiola for allowing the body to function under hypoxic conditions, which now has molecular explanation in salidroside's HIF-1alpha activation and erythropoietic effects. *(An earlier version stated that the Tibetan formula* Padma 28 *"contains Rhodiola as a primary constituent". The 2026-08-10 audit found no source for Rhodiola in the Padma 28 formula, and the claim has been withdrawn. Separately, a caution for anyone re-sourcing this section: the Tibetan formula* Srolo Bzhtang *— despite the* sro lo *element in its name — contains no Rhodiola.)*

### Active Components

- **Rosavins (Rosavin, Rosarin, Rosin)** — cinnamyl alcohol glycosides unique to *Rhodiola rosea* (not present in other Rhodiola species); these compounds differentiate true R. rosea from adulterants; primary adaptogenic activity marker; mechanisms include monoamine oxidase A and B inhibition (increasing dopamine, serotonin, noradrenaline availability), opioid receptor modulation, and beta-endorphin regulation; rosavin is the primary compound standardized in commercial extracts (typically ≥3% rosavins)
- **Salidroside (Rhodioloside, p-tyrosol glycoside)** — the most pharmacologically studied single compound; primary anti-fatigue mechanism: activation of AMPK (AMP-activated protein kinase) in skeletal muscle, mimicking the metabolic effect of exercise training; also activates HIF-1alpha (hypoxia-inducible factor), increasing erythropoietin expression, improving oxygen utilization at altitude; neuroprotective via Nrf2 pathway activation; anti-depressant via serotonin reuptake inhibition and BDNF upregulation
- **p-Tyrosol** — the aglycone of salidroside; free phenylethanol with antioxidant activity; monoamine precursor contribution; vasodilatory via eNOS activation; anti-platelet activity
- **Flavonoids: Tricin, Kaempferol, Quercetin, and glycosides** — anti-inflammatory (COX-2 inhibition, NF-κB modulation); antioxidant; tricin associated with cardiovascular protection; quercetin's ergogenic properties independently documented (VO2max, mitochondrial biogenesis via PGC-1alpha)
- **Proanthocyanidins** — vascular protective antioxidants; synergistic with salidroside in endothelial protection
- **Monoterpene alcohols (geraniol, 1-octanol, 2-phenylethanol)** — primary contributors to rose fragrance; quality authentication markers
- **Gallic acid and catechins** — Phase II enzyme induction; antioxidant; mild MAO-B inhibition

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Acute pre-training anti-fatigue and work capacity enhancement** `[PRIME]` — Rhodiola's most distinctive and clinically validated acute effect: a single dose taken 30–60 minutes pre-training. Salidroside-mediated AMPK activation improves fatty acid oxidation efficiency; rosavin-mediated MAO inhibition elevates central dopamine and noradrenaline, reducing perceived effort and central fatigue. Single dose of 200–400mg standardized extract acutely improves time-to-exhaustion, reduces perceived exertion at a given work intensity, and maintains cognitive sharpness through the training session.

2. **Altitude and hypoxic training environments** `[PRIME]` — Salidroside's HIF-1alpha activation and EPO upregulation make Rhodiola uniquely valuable for athletes training at altitude or in hypoxic chambers. Beginning supplementation 5–7 days before altitude exposure and continuing throughout reduces acute mountain sickness symptoms and accelerates red blood cell adaptation. The classical Tibetan FMT application of Rhodiola specifically for high-altitude tolerance is validated by this mechanism.

3. **Dopaminergic motivation maintenance in high-volume training** `[BUILD]` — Extended high-volume BUILD phases produce progressive motivational decline as dopaminergic reserve is depleted. Rosavin-mediated COMT inhibition and MAO-A/B inhibition maintain available dopamine pools, preserving training drive and willingness to approach pain. Unlike stimulants, this mechanism does not deplete catecholamine stores — it reduces their breakdown.

4. **Cognitive performance and skill execution during caloric restriction** `[REFINE]` — Caloric restriction reliably impairs executive function, working memory, and reaction time. Rhodiola's dopamine and serotonin-preserving effects maintain cognitive performance during REFINE phases. The Darbinyan et al. (2000) night-duty physician RCT is directly applicable: sustained mental performance under adverse conditions (fatigue, sleep deprivation, caloric stress).

5. **Fat mobilization and metabolic substrate efficiency** `[REFINE]` — AMPK activation by salidroside shifts skeletal muscle metabolism toward fat oxidation, increasing GLUT4 translocation and fatty acid translocase (FAT/CD36) expression, improving metabolic flexibility. This positions Rhodiola as a legitimate metabolic-phase adaptogen.

6. **Anti-inflammatory and oxidative stress resolution** `[RESET]` — Flavonoid fraction (quercetin, kaempferol) provides NF-κB and COX-2 inhibitory activity; salidroside-activated Nrf2 pathway upregulates HO-1, NQO1, and glutathione peroxidase. In Kenetica's RESET phase, Rhodiola is paired with Schisandra and omega-3 for coordinated resolution of training-accumulated oxidative and inflammatory burden.

7. **Depression and psychological resilience** `[RESET]` — Rhodiola is the only adaptogen in Kenetica's formulary with clinical evidence for anti-depressant efficacy in mild-to-moderate depression approaching that of pharmaceutical agents. The Mao et al. (2015) comparison with sertraline shows comparable efficacy with significantly better tolerability. For athlete post-competition or post-cycle depressive episodes, this is the RESET-phase neurological intervention of choice.

### Preparation & Dosing

**Standardized Extract (≥3% Rosavins, ≥1% Salidroside by HPLC):**
- The rosavin:salidroside ratio of approximately 3:1 reflects natural composition of authentic *R. rosea* and distinguishes it from adulterants
- For acute ergogenic pre-training effect: 200–400mg taken 30–60 minutes before training on empty stomach
- For chronic adaptogenic use: 200–400mg daily, morning or pre-training; second dose of 200mg midday if needed for afternoon cognitive effects
- Avoid evening administration — alerting catecholaminergic effects may impair sleep onset in sensitive individuals

**SHR-5 Extract (Specific Validated Form):**
- Standardized to specific rosavin and salidroside ratio; used in Spasov et al. (2000) and key clinical trials
- 170mg/day minimum effective anti-fatigue dose; 340–680mg/day for athletic performance applications
- 4–6 week courses with 1–2 week breaks to prevent tachyphylaxis to MAO inhibitory effects

**Whole Root Powder:**
- 1–3g daily; traditional Tibetan and Siberian preparation uses dried root in cold water for 4–6 hours or as a decoction (3–9g in 300mL water, 20-minute low simmer)
- Siberian folk use: root chewed directly or steeped in vodka (analogous to medicinal wine preparation)

**Tincture (1:5 in 40% ethanol):**
- 3–5mL in water, 30–60 minutes before training or twice daily; captures rosavin and salidroside efficiently

**Rhodiola + Eleuthero + Schisandra (Classical Soviet Adaptogen Stack):**
- Rhodiola 200mg + Eleutherococcus 300mg + Schisandra 250mg, taken 30–45 minutes pre-training
- Documents Soviet sports medicine research; the three adaptogens address complementary aspects: Rhodiola (monoaminergic/AMPK), Eleutherococcus (glucocorticoid receptor/NK cell), Schisandra (mitochondrial/CYP450/lignan)

### Scientific Correlation

- De Bock K, Eijnde BO, Ramaekers M, Hespel P (2004) *Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab* 14:298–307 — double-blind crossover RCT; single acute dose of Rhodiola rosea extract (200mg SHR-5) vs placebo in untrained subjects; significant improvement in time to exhaustion (increased by 24 seconds, p<0.05), significant reduction in heart rate at identical submaximal workload, and significant reduction in lactate concentration at equivalent work intensity; acute single-dose ergogenic effect confirmed; attributed to salidroside-mediated AMPK activation improving substrate oxidation efficiency
- Spasov AA, Wikman GK, Mandrikov VB, Mironova IA, Neumoin VV (2000) *Phytomedicine* 7:85–89 — double-blind RCT; 56 foreign students during examination period (high stress, sleep deprivation); SHR-5 extract 170mg/day vs placebo for 20 days; treatment group showed significant improvement on all cognitive performance measures (attention, speed of visual-auditory processing, calculation), significant reduction in fatigue, and significantly improved exam performance; established anti-fatigue efficacy of low-dose Rhodiola under genuine real-world stress conditions
- Darbinyan V, Kteyan A, Panossian A, Gabrielian E, Wikman G, Wagner H (2000) *Phytomedicine* 7:365–371 — double-blind RCT; 56 physicians on night duty; SHR-5 extract (170mg/day, two 20-day periods with 2-week washout) vs placebo; statistically significant improvement in capacity for mental work (accuracy of proofreading test, +7.6% vs −1.4% in placebo), significantly reduced fatigue; validated performance maintenance under the combination of sleep deprivation and work pressure — most directly analogous to competition-week training scenarios
- Mao JJ, Xie SX, Zee J, Soeller I, Li QS, Rockwell K, Amsterdam JD (2015) *Phytomedicine* 22:394–399 — 12-week randomized trial; 57 adults with mild-to-moderate depression; Rhodiola rosea extract (340mg/day) vs sertraline (50mg/day) vs placebo; Rhodiola showed no statistically significant difference from sertraline on Hamilton Depression Scale score reduction, but significantly fewer adverse effects (nausea, sexual dysfunction, sedation); superior tolerability profile makes it a meaningful clinical option for sport-related depressive states where pharmaceutical adverse effects are unacceptable
- Panossian A, Wikman G (2009) *Pharmaceuticals* 2:188–224 — comprehensive mechanistic review; characterized molecular targets including AMPK activation (salidroside), MAO-A/B inhibition (rosavins and salidroside), HIF-1alpha activation (salidroside), BDNF upregulation, Hsp70 molecular chaperone induction, and neuropeptide Y receptor modulation; reviewed the full Soviet-era clinical literature in Western-accessible form

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Primary Tier:** ⊕ Yuan — Constitutional/Adaptogenic. Hóng Jǐng Tiān's primary classification is as a Yuan Qi activator specifically oriented toward mobilizing constitutional reserve rather than depleting it. Its nature is cold rather than warm, distinguishing it from Ginseng (warm) and Ashwagandha (warm), making it the preferred adaptogen for hot constitutions, Yin-deficient athletes, or warmer training climates.

**Secondary Tiers:** ◉ Qi (circulatory — Hóng Jǐng Tiān activates blood Qi circulation and tonifies Lung Qi for altitude adaptation) · ☿ Shen (neurological — documented in both SBJ and FMT for cognitive clarity, anti-depression, Shen stabilization)

**TCM Channel:** Lung (肺) — primary (Lung governs Qi; altitude adaptation aligns with Lung Qi tonification); Heart (心) — secondary (cardiovascular effects and Shen-calming); Liver (肝) — tertiary (blood activation, mood stabilization); Kidney (腎) — as constitutional Yuan Qi source and erythropoietic support

**Energetic Action:** Invigorates blood and removes stasis (*huó xuè huà yū*); relieves cough and dispels phlegm (*zhǐ ké huà tán*); tonifies Qi and clears heat (*bǔ qì qīng rè*); calms the spirit and sharpens the will (*ān shén yì zhì*); adapts the body to cold and altitude (*shì yīng hán lěng* — a functional classification in modern TCM athletic medicine)

**Ayurvedic (functional correspondence):**
- *Rasa*: Madhura (sweet), slightly Kashaya (astringent); *Virya*: Sheeta (cooling); *Vipaka*: Madhura (sweet)
- *Dosha effects*: Pitta ↓↓ (most beneficial for Pitta constitutions; cooling, bitter and sweet); Vāta neutral to mildly ↓; Kapha ↓ (light, drying qualities)
- Functionally: *medhya* (intellect-enhancing), *balya* (strength-giving), *hṛdya* (cardiac tonic), *śīta rasayana* (cooling rejuvenating tonic) — the cold-energy adaptogen type distinct from warming *rasayana* category of Ashvagandha and Ginseng

**Tibetan (FMT):** Cooling and slightly bitter; *mkhris pa* and *rlung* harmonizing simultaneously — an unusual combination valuable for *rlung-mkhris* combined disorders (anxiety with hyperactivity, nervous exhaustion with inflammation) common in high-achieving athletes; *srog rlung* stabilizing; highly valued for altitude (*ri khrod*) applications and intensive retreat *bcud len* longevity support

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan (FMT).** Rhodiola is genuinely native to the plateau and genuinely attested in Sowa Rigpa: the *rGyud-bzhi* (Gyushi) tradition works *sro lo dmar po* / *btsod* for *rlung* disorders — nervous exhaustion, cognitive impairment, altitude illness — and for *rlung–mkhris* combined presentations, as this entry records. That is a therapeutic placement within the *nyes pa* framework (the three *nyes pa* deriving from the three poisons), not a cultivation grade. Tibetan medicine has *bcud len* (essence extraction) as its cultivation-adjacent discipline, located inside the Four Tantras (Gerke), and the entry gestures at "retreat *bcud len* longevity support" — but no named scholarship retrieved attests *sro lo dmar po* specifically as a *bcud len* substance, and the spec is explicit that no formal "substances for subtle-body practice" category exists to be asserted. The claim therefore stays at the frame: attested in the tradition, no sourced cultivation status. **(b) framed.**

**Later Chinese materia medica (TCM).** *Hóng Jǐng Tiān* 红景天 is a real Chinese drug with a real high-altitude reputation, but two corrections are needed before anything is claimed for it. First the stratum: this material is post-classical. Its textual anchors are the *Bencao Gangmu* (1596) and later Qing supplements — Rhodiola is not a *Shennong Bencao Jing* drug, and the tag has been relabelled from SBJ to TCM accordingly. Because the *san pin* grades belong to the *Bencao Jing*, there is no upper-grade status here to claim. Second, and more consequential for a reader: the Chinese drug-name *hóng jǐng tiān* denotes ***Rhodiola crenulata***, not *R. rosea*, the species under which this entry is filed. Booker et al. state that *R. crenulata* is the species used medicinally in Chinese practice, and the drug appears to have entered Chinese use from the Tibetan plateau rather than from the Han canon. The Chinese material therefore describes a **congener**, and the cultivation-relevant claims made for *hóng jǐng tiān* cannot simply be transferred to *R. rosea*. Cultivation status: **(b) framed** — genuinely attested in the later Chinese materia medica, but with no *san pin* grade, and attaching to *R. crenulata* rather than to this entry's species. (Src: Booker, Zhai, Gkouva, Li & Heinrich, *Frontiers in Pharmacology* 2016;7:254, doi:10.3389/fphar.2016.00254.)

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator uses Rhodiola for exactly what the sports-science and Tibetan altitude records converge on — acute work capacity, hypoxic tolerance, resistance to fatigue and central catecholamine depletion under load. It is taken in defined courses (the 4–6 week cycling above exists precisely to prevent tachyphylaxis), pre-training or pre-altitude, then rested. Neither Chinese classical grading nor a sourced Tibetan *bcud len* status underwrites treating it as a lifelong longevity tonic; the honest cultivation frame is a periodised performance adaptogen, not a *yang sheng* or rejuvenation substance.

### Contraindications

- **Bipolar disorder and manic-depressive conditions:** MAO inhibitory and dopaminergic effects may precipitate or exacerbate hypomanic or manic episodes; contraindicated in bipolar disorder without psychiatric supervision
- **Concurrent MAOI antidepressant medications:** Additive MAO inhibition with pharmaceutical MAOIs (phenelzine, tranylcypromine, selegiline) creates serotonin syndrome risk; contraindicated with pharmaceutical MAOIs
- **SSRI/SNRI interactions:** Salidroside's serotonin reuptake inhibitory activity may additively elevate synaptic serotonin with pharmaceutical SSRIs; monitor when initiating supplementation
- **Agitation, anxiety, insomnia — Yang excess or Pitta excess constitutions:** Despite its cold TCM nature, the stimulant/alerting catecholaminergic effects can worsen insomnia, agitation, and anxiety in individuals already experiencing sympathetic excess; contraindicated in the evening; use only in the morning or pre-training in anxiety-prone individuals
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** Monoamine-active and AMPK-activating compounds not studied in pregnancy; MAO inhibitory activity raises theoretical concerns for fetal monoaminergic development; contraindicated during pregnancy
- **Stimulant interactions (caffeine, ephedrine, modafinil):** Additive catecholaminergic effects may produce excessive sympathetic activation: tachycardia, hypertension, anxiety, insomnia; reduce or eliminate caffeine when using Rhodiola at higher doses
- **Hypotension:** Salidroside's eNOS activation and vasodilatory effects may lower blood pressure in individuals experiencing hypotension; monitor when initiating
