# Asana — Asana, Bījaka, Pītaśāra, Vijayasāra, Indian Kino, Malabar Kino

## *Pterocarpus marsupium* Roxb. | Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae; subfamily Faboideae/Papilionoideae)

**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā), CS (Caraka Saṃhitā), AH (Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya), Nighaṇṭus (Bhāvaprakāśa, Dhanvantari)
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa — Kaṣāya (astringent), Tikta (bitter) · Vīrya — Śīta (cooling) · Vipāka — Kaṭu (pungent) · Guṇa — Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry) · Kapha–Pitta-hara; the classical **Meha-hara / Pramehaghna** (anti-diabetic) heartwood, and a **Kuṣṭhaghna** (anti-dermatosis) and **Kṛmighna** agent.
**Training Phase:** Metabolic regulation (glycaemic conditioning) · Clearing / Purification · Tissue-firming (astringent consolidation)

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** Asana appears in Suśruta's **Sālasārādi gaṇa** — the group headed by Sāla-sāra (heartwood drugs) that is the canonical Ayurvedic cluster for *prameha* (the polyuric / metabolic-urinary disorders that encompass what we now call diabetes) and for *kuṣṭha* (obstinate skin disease), *kṛmi* (parasites), *pāṇḍu* (anaemia), and *medoroga* (disorders of fat). Within this gaṇa the **heartwood (sāra)** is the medicinal part — hence *Pītaśāra*, "yellow heartwood," and *Bījaka*. Suśruta's placement of Asana at the head of the anti-*prameha* pharmacology is the single most important classical fact about the drug: it is the archetypal wood-of-the-water-vessel, the heartwood carved into tumblers (the "vijayasāra glass") in which water is steeped overnight and drunk to blunt sweet urine.

**Caraka Saṃhitā (CS):** Caraka likewise counts Asana among the drugs for *prameha* and *kuṣṭha*, exploiting the astringent-bitter, drying, Kapha-and-Meda-reducing profile. The heartwood's kino-tannin exudate ("Malabar/Indian Kino") is itself a materia medica item — a powerful astringent used in diarrhoea and bleeding.

**Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya & Nighaṇṭus:** Vāgbhaṭa and the later nighaṇṭus retain Asana in the anti-prameha and anti-kuṣṭha registers. *Vijayasāra* ("victorious heartwood") becomes the market name; Bhāvaprakāśa records both the internal decoction and the water-vessel practice.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Vessel That Corrects the Water:** No Codex entry embodies its doctrine as literally as Asana. *Prameha* is, at root, a disorder of the body's *water* — the sweet, over-abundant, dysregulated flow of the urinary and fatty channels (medovaha and mūtravaha srotas) drowning in unmetabolised Kapha. The classical response is not to add heat or purge, but to pass the water through a *correcting membrane*: the heartwood vessel. Water sits overnight against the yellow, tannin-dense wood, is taken up by it, is changed, and is returned to the body altered. The wood is a filter that teaches the water discipline. This is the doctrinal heart of Kenetica's **metabolic-regulation** phase — the principle that dysregulated flux is corrected not by force but by repeated passage through a firm, astringent, consolidating boundary. Asana is the trainer of the sweet tide. Its cooling virya matters doctrinally: prameha runs with a smouldering metabolic heat and inflammatory oxidation, and Asana cools that fire while its astringency binds and firms the leaking channels.

### Active Components

According to phytochemical literature the heartwood of *Pterocarpus marsupium* carries a distinctive polyphenol suite: the stilbenes **pterostilbene** (the dimethyl-ether of resveratrol) and its relatives; the benzofuranone **(−)-epicatechin**; the C-glucosidic chromene **marsupsin** and **pterosupin**; **liquiritigenin** and other flavonoids; and the astringent kino-tannins (kino-tannic acid, kino-red). Pterostilbene and the flavan-3-ols are the components most associated with the antidiabetic and antioxidant activity; the kino-tannins account for the frank astringency.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Asana is Kenetica's **glycaemic-conditioning heartwood** — the anchor of the metabolic-regulation track. Where the training goal is to firm a leaking, over-sweet metabolism and to reduce the oxidative and inflammatory background of dysregulated glucose handling, Asana is first-line. Its astringency doubles it into the tissue-firming/consolidation work (chronic loose stool, mucosal laxity, weeping skin), and its bitter-drying Kapha–Meda action carries it into the Clearing phase for damp, heavy, over-nourished constitutions. It is a slow, structural drug, not a fast corrector — used steadily, in the classical vessel-and-decoction manner.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the antidiabetic pharmacology of *Pterocarpus marsupium* and of its stilbene pterostilbene is genuine and mechanistically detailed, though clinical evidence in humans remains limited — evidence grade **preclinical-to-emerging**.

- Dar et al. (2022), *Metabolites*, showed a methanolic heartwood extract of *P. marsupium* reduced oxidative stress and **improved glucose uptake in HepG2 hepatocytes**, with metabolomic profiling identifying quercetin and other antidiabetic phenolics. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/metabo12100947)
- Kosaraju et al. (2013), *Pharmaceutical Biology*, demonstrated that a *P. marsupium* extract is a potent **DPP-4 inhibitor** in vitro (IC50 ≈ 274 µg/mL) and raised active GLP-1 levels in diabetic rats — a mechanism shared with a modern class of oral antidiabetics. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3109/13880209.2013.823550)
- On the lead molecule: Estrela et al. (2013), *Critical Reviews in Clinical Laboratory Sciences*, review **pterostilbene**'s biomedical profile, noting its higher in-vivo bioavailability than resveratrol and documented effects on insulin sensitivity, glycaemia, and lipids. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3109/10408363.2013.805182)
- Shen et al. (2022), *Phytomedicine*, showed pterostilbene alleviated non-alcoholic fatty liver disease in mice via AMPK/mTOR signalling and Nrf2-dependent autophagy — supporting a metabolic/anti-oxidative mechanism relevant to the prameha picture. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phymed.2022.154561)

The heartwood-water and DPP-4/GLP-1 findings give an unusually coherent modern rationale for the classical Sālasārādi anti-prameha use. Human clinical confirmation for standardised *P. marsupium* preparations is still thin. `[USER: verify before any clinical glycaemic claim]`

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa:** Kaṣāya, Tikta
- **Vīrya:** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka:** Kaṭu
- **Guṇa:** Laghu, Rūkṣa
- **Doṣa action:** Reduces Kapha and Pitta (and Meda); may aggravate Vāta if used unbalanced due to drying/cooling
- **Karma:** Pramehaghna / Meha-hara (anti-diabetic), Kuṣṭhaghna (anti-dermatosis), Kṛmighna (antiparasitic), Medohara (fat-reducing), Grāhī/Stambhana (astringent-binding), Raktaśodhaka (blood-cleansing)
- **Gaṇa membership:** **Sālasārādi gaṇa** (Suśruta) — the heartwood group for prameha, kuṣṭha, medoroga
- **Srotas:** Mūtravaha (urinary), Medovaha (fat), Rasavaha, Raktavaha
- **Prabhāva:** The specific anti-prameha effect of the heartwood-steeped water — the drug's signature, doctrinally the "vessel that corrects the water"

### Contraindications

- Drying and cooling: use cautiously in Vāta constitutions, dryness, constipation, and depletion.
- **Additive hypoglycaemia:** because it genuinely lowers glucose in preclinical models and inhibits DPP-4, it may compound the effect of insulin, sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, and other antidiabetics — monitor blood glucose closely and coordinate with the prescriber. `[USER: verify]`
- Pregnancy, lactation, and paediatric safety not established — avoid without supervision. `[USER: verify]`
