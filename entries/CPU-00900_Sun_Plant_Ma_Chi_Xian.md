### Origin & Tradition

**Identity warning.** This row was promoted from MASTER_INDEX (two traditions: Shennong, Sushruta; idx 2174). Its two halves point at different plants. "Ma Chi Xian" is a Chinese drug name that the Codex already files under purslane, *Portulaca oleracea*, in the finished entry I_440 (CPU-00901). "Sun Plant" and *P. grandiflora* name a different species of the same genus. The Sushruta locus found below also prints *P. oleracea* and not *P. grandiflora*. Neither tag therefore has a locus for the species in the spine. The row probably duplicates I_440 and may need to be merged or retired. [USER: verify]

**Chinese — Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ).** The text is Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`; herbs begin at line 1383). A search for "Ma Chi", "Chi Xian", "Portulac", "purslane", "horse-tooth" and "amaranth" found no hit, and the Shennong extraction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv`) and list (`_sources/shennong.csv`) have no row for *Portulaca* or Ma Chi Xian. No entry for this drug was found in the classic as printed in Yang 1998, so no printed class heading applies to it. The tag has no locus. The nearest printed names containing "Xian" (Lu Xian, line 1281; Bai Xian, line 2878; Ma Xian Hao, line 3063) are different drugs.

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Three places were read.

*Appendix 3 (Bhishagratna Vol. III).* The two-column name and identification list (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`) prints the Sanskrit name "Loni" at line 32149 and, in the Latin column of the same L block, "Porlulaca oleracea." at line 32181 (OCR spelling). The extraction (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, row 720) lists the Latin line as "not paired to a Sanskrit name (OCR column split)". Counting down the two columns, the L block runs from Lajjalu to Loni on the name side and ends with "Symplocos racemosa" and "Portulaca oleracea" on the Latin side, so that Lodhra pairs with the first and Loni with the second. That reading is positional and was made in this session, and it was not script-checked. [USER: verify] The appendix does not print *P. grandiflora*.

*Sutra ch.46 (Annapanavidhi).* `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 1730 to 1738, reads: "The leaves of the Lonika, Jatuka, Triparnika, Pattura, Jivaka, Suvarchala, Kurmaka, Kathinjara, Kuntalika, Kurantika etc., are sweet in taste and digestion and cooling in their potency. They subdue the Kapham and do not inordinately generate the Pittam. They leave a saline after-taste in the mouth and produce a condition of dryness in the organism. They are alkaline and laxative, and generate the Vayu." This is a list of potherbs. The chapter gives no identification, and the link from *Lonika* to *Portulaca* depends on the appendix reading above.

*Rasayana or vajikarana.* The Sushruta chapter files were searched for *Lonika* and *Loni*, and no hit was found in a rasayana or vajikarana chapter.

**Related entry.** I_440 Purslane (*Portulaca oleracea*, CPU-00901) carries both tags (and five others) and is the natural home for the Chinese and Sushruta material. Its text was not re-examined here. *P. grandiflora* is a separate species in the same genus. The Dioscorides *Portulaca* chapters (2-150 ANDRACHNE and 2-151 ANDRACHNE AGRIA) are not tagged on this row and are not used.

### Active Components

*Portulaca* species are generally known to contain betalain pigments, and *P. oleracea* is a known source of oxalates and of omega-3 fatty acids in its leaves. Nothing was read about the composition of *P. grandiflora* specifically. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The sources read give no use for *P. grandiflora*. The Sushruta list above treats the *Lonika* leaves as a cooling, laxative potherb, and the identification of that name with any *Portulaca* is unsettled. The Codex gives no protocol for this plant.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. Both tags rest on a name and a genus and not on a text about *P. grandiflora*. The honest answer is to do nothing with it on the strength of this entry, and to look to I_440 for the purslane material.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read for *P. grandiflora*. Do not eat an ornamental *Portulaca* because a potherb of the same genus is eaten. Species and cultivars differ, and the plants called "Ma Chi Xian" and "Sun Plant" are not the same species. Purslane leaves are known to be rich in oxalate, which matters for people prone to kidney stones. Seek a qualified practitioner before any medicinal use.
