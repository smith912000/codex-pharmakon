# Hippopotamus — "Hippopotamus" (Bryan's English rendering; no Egyptian word is given in the list)
## *The hippopotamus, a large river animal; in the Ebers material its fat, oil, hide and hoof-fat appear as ingredients* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 translation, Ch. VII, Organic Remedies (animals): in the list of "those animals whose identity is not in doubt, and who were called upon to sacrifice themselves in the cause of the Healing Art", the second name after Lion is "Hippopotamus" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1391; `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 93: "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). `_sources/ebers.csv` line 124 cites "Ghalioungui 1987 trans., Glossary I (Medicaments)"; that translation is not on disk and was not read. Line 70 of the same list file has "Hippopotamus fat" with a binomial, but its sources are given as "history.com; crocodile-oil.com", which are not relied on here.

The text itself, as the English prose and prescriptions in the Bryan file give them, names the animal's products in these places:

- Fat, as a poultice for a wound: "Mix in Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus, or of the Hog, and apply as a poultice" (line 2374), introduced as a way to dry a wound (line 2371).
- Hide: "an unguent composed of Hide-of-the-Hippopotamus and Poppy-seeds" smeared on the head for a stubborn headache (line 2110).
- Oil, in a lotion for the same headache: "Mint-of-the-Mountains dissolved in Hippopotamus Oil along with Copper and Lead-vitriol" (line 2112).
- Oil, on the third day of a four-day course for "Eczema-of-the-Head" (line 2718; the course is read at lines 2711-2718).
- Oil with honey, as one of the salves for the "woodlike uha-disease" (line 3293).
- Oil among the oils of a liniment for suppleness of the limbs (line 2181).
- Fat, one of six fats (lion, hippopotamus, crocodile, cat, serpent, Egyptian goat) "made into one" and rubbed on the head of "the Bald One" (lines 4022-4028).
- Fat with writing-fluid and gazelle's dung, which Bryan calls a "vile concoction" among hair remedies (line 4030).
- Fat "from the hoof", crushed with tortoise-shell and rubbed on to remove superfluous hair (line 4075).

These are Bryan's English summaries, with the prescriptions set out in his running prose. The papyrus's own wording, the Egyptian name of the animal and the count of papyrus columns were not available in the files read. Whether "oil" and "fat" in the English are one product or two is not settled by the passages read. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

An animal fat is mainly triglycerides, the esters of glycerol with fatty acids, with smaller amounts of cholesterol and fat-soluble material. A rendered fat or oil is a greasy base that holds other ingredients and keeps a dressing on the skin. No analysis of hippopotamus fat was retrieved for this entry, and no composition figures are given. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The Ebers passages above use the animal's fat, oil and hide as a base or ingredient for dressings and ointments on the skin and scalp. They do not describe a use of the animal as a dietary or internal medicine, and nothing in them is a protocol for the present day.

It is not a recommended practice, for three plain reasons. First, the common hippopotamus is a protected species: it is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN and appears in the CITES appendices, and trade in its products is regulated. [USER: verify the current status] Second, the animal is dangerous to handle and carries the ordinary infection risks of raw animal tissue. Third, the Ebers identification is Bryan's English reading of an indication-organised text, so the evidence that any ingredient did what the passage claims is not available from the sources.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give the forms only: a poultice of fat (line 2374), an unguent of hide with poppy-seeds (line 2110), oil dissolved with mint, copper and lead-vitriol (line 2112), oil with honey (line 3293), and fat crushed with tortoise-shell (line 4075). No dose or quantity for the hippopotamus ingredient is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The general facts are these. Animal fats and oils act as emollients and occlusives: they soften the skin surface and slow water loss. Poppy seeds, copper salts and lead salts, which the same passages combine with it, are not harmless; lead compounds are toxic, and a lead-containing dressing on broken skin is hazardous. The headache and hair claims of the passages are traditional and have no modern evidential basis in the sources retrieved.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Egyptian / Ebers (EP): the Ebers material is organised by complaint and records no qualities (hot, cold, wet, dry), taste or potency for this animal. None is given in the passages read.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint (a headache, a wound, eczema of the head, a skin disease, baldness, superfluous hair), not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. The Egyptian practice of treating spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*) is not shown by the hippopotamus passages in Bryan's summary: none of them carries a recitation in the lines read, though Bryan summarises and that is not proof that the papyrus has none. No deity is attributed. The identity of each named substance is a modern reading, and here the English form is Bryan's.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here supports a cultivator's use. The sources give a greasy base for dressings and hair preparations, in a protected species, with no cultivation claim. The honest practice is abstention, and an interest in the entry as a record of how the Ebers scribes used the fats of large animals.

### Contraindications

- **Not a recommended practice.** This entry records a historical use of a wild animal's products. Nothing here is advice to use them.
- **Conservation and law:** the hippopotamus is a protected, declining species; collecting or trading its products is regulated and often unlawful. [USER: verify]
- **Infection:** raw animal fat, hide and hoof carry bacterial and parasitic risk if applied to wounds.
- **Toxic co-ingredients:** the recorded lotions combine the oil with lead-vitriol and copper; do not reproduce them.
- **Safety of the animal:** the live hippopotamus is among the most dangerous large mammals to people.
