The one Ebers text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). I searched it for "perch", "Lates" and "Nile fish" and found no such fish named. Bryan does print several fish in his translated recipes, none glossed by him as a perch: the "Bile-of-the-Black-abdu-fish" (lines 2112 and 2804, in a poultice for the head and in an eye "paste" of collyrium), the "Shadfish" (for example "Flesh-of-the-Shadfish and Honey beaten up in Yeast-of-Sweet-Beer", line 2182, and "Skull-of-the-Shadfish, cooked in Oil" for splinters, line 2344), the "Bone-of-the-Sword-fish" (lines 2102 and 2515), the "Redfish" and "Fish-oil", and the "mehit-fish" (line 2504). His chapter VII animal list names "Eel Electric Eel Crab Swordfish" among the fishes and crustaceans (line 1404). Whether any of these Egyptian fish names corresponds to *Lates niloticus* is not stated in what I read; the list row's identification could belong to any one of them or to a name Bryan did not print. I do not equate any of them with the Nile perch. Bryan's book is a summary with selected quotations, not a full translation, and the papyrus is organised by complaint, not by substance, so the identity of any animal named in it is a modern hypothesis. General, well-established facts only. Fish is a recognised source of protein and omega-3 fatty acids. The Nile perch is an introduced species in Lake Victoria whose spread is associated with the decline of many native fishes; it is also a major commercial fishery, so provenance matters. ### Contraindications

- **Food safety:** eat fish only when fresh and fully cooked; raw or poorly cooked freshwater fish can carry parasites and bacteria. Do not eat or apply raw fish bile or viscera.
- **Contaminants:** large predatory fish can accumulate mercury and other pollutants; pregnant people and young children are advised in many countries to limit them.
- **Identification:** the identification is uncertain even in the source list; do not infer a use from it.
- **Conservation:** fishery sustainability varies by water body; the Codex does not recommend taking wild fish for medicinal use.
