# Umbrella polypore sclerotium — Zhu Ling 猪苓 (Shennong bencao jing); "pig's ling", also written Shi Ling 豕零 in the Zhuangzi

## *Polyporus umbellatus (Pers.) Fr., the umbrella polypore, here its dark underground sclerotium (zhu ling), a fungus and not a plant; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence, supported by the Wu Pu's comparison with fu ling (a sclerotium) and by Sima Biao's "root like a pig's testicle"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The only text read for this entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Searches of the other texts on disk for *Polyporus* and for "zhu ling" found only the Shennong extract file `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/sun_monographs.csv` (row 216). The sister drug fu ling is the separate spine entry I_194 (*Wolfiporia extensa*, Poria) and is a different fungus; the Wu Pu layer below compares the two.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 猪苓 in the middle classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 猪苓 *zhu ling*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2487). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味甘，平 ("sweet, even"); 主 疟 (a character is missing before 疟, "intermittent fevers"), 解毒蛊注不祥 ("dissolves poison, gu-zhu and the inauspicious"; the Yulan reads 蛀 for 注) and 利水道 ("frees the water passages"); 久服，轻身、耐老 ("taken long: lightens the body, endures old age"; the Yulan reads 能老, "can age", for 耐老); alias 一名 [a character missing] 猪尿 ("also called [?] pig's urine"); habitat 生山谷 ("grows in mountain valleys").

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 2489) says: 猪苓，神农，甘；雷公：苦，无毒, "Shennong: sweet; Leigong: bitter, non-toxic". The Yulan quotation adds: 如茯苓，或生冤句，八月采, "like fu ling; or it grows at Yuanju; gathered in the eighth month".

The Mingyi bielu layer (line 2491) adds: 生衡山及济阴冤句。二月、八月采，阴干, "grows on Mount Heng and at Yuanju in Jiyin; gathered in the second and eighth months, dried in the shade".

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 2493) is short: the Zhuangzi names "shi ling" (豕零), and Sima Biao's commentary writes it as 豕囊, "pig's bag", and says: 一名猪苓，根似猪卵，可以治渴, "also called zhu ling; its root resembles a pig's testicle; it can treat thirst". Of everything in the entry, this is the only description of the object. Sima Biao's "root" is the commentator's word, and the Wu Pu comparison with fu ling is the better pointer to a sclerotium.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Polyporus umbellatus* sclerotium is a fungal body containing polysaccharides and ergosterol-type steroid compounds (polyporusterones). [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a sweet, even sclerotium for intermittent fevers, for dissolving "poison, gu-zhu and the inauspicious", and for freeing the water passages, with a long-use clause of lightening the body and enduring old age. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Mingyi layer gives the harvest months and the drying (in the shade); the Shennong paragraph names no part and no preparation. The preface (line 4121) says medicines differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The sclerotium is used in modern Chinese pharmacy as a diuretic, which agrees in kind with the classic's "frees the water passages". No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The classic's claims (lightening the body, enduring old age) have no modern pharmacological equivalent and are not translated into one; "intermittent fevers" are not a modern diagnosis.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味甘，平, "sweet, even" (line 2487). The Wu Pu layer records "sweet" for Shennong and "bitter, non-toxic" for Leigong (line 2489), a disagreement of flavour between two named authorities that the Codex does not settle.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The paragraph for 猪苓 reads 味甘，平 and files the sclerotium in the middle grade (中品). The classic's preface (line 1677) says the middle medicines "nourish the nature, to correspond to man; non-toxic and toxic, weigh what is fitting" (主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜). The paragraph carries its own 久服 clause: 久服，轻身、耐老, "taken long: lightens the body, endures old age". The clause promises lightening of the body, which the Codex treats as a life-nourishing promise, so the register is (a), with the note that the status is attached to the drug by its own words and not by its grade, since the middle grade does not by itself promise it. This records the classic's wording and not a claim that the sclerotium does these things. "Lighten the body" (qing shen) has no pharmacological equivalent and is not translated into one. The Wu Pu and Mingyi layers add flavour, place and season only. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 猪苓 *zhu ling*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2487)

**Practitioner's note.** The classic gives this sclerotium a long-use clause even though it sits in the middle grade, which its preface ties to weighing a drug's strength. A cultivator reading the entry has the wording and no dose, no preparation and no course length. Take it as a record of how the classic framed the drug.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** zhu ling is not fu ling (I_194); do not substitute on the similarity the Wu Pu draws.
- **Fluid balance:** a drug said to free the water passages should not be taken for oedema, thirst or intermittent fever without medical assessment.
- **Pregnancy and nursing:** no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given; "taken long" is not a basis for a regimen.
