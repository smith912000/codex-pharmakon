# Potter wasp / mud-dauber — heading lost in the source; the 案 and the Mingyi alias 土蜂 ("earth bee"); the entry is filed as 蠮螉 *yiweng*

## *A solitary earth-nesting wasp; Eumenes pomiformis is the modern identification, which is ours and of medium confidence (Eumenes and Sceliphron are both candidates). The heading of the classic's entry is lost in the transcription, and the identification rests on the Mingyi alias 土蜂 and on Sun Xingyan's note* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on one text in one edition, Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The existing entries III_208 (*Wasp*, tag EP) and III_294 (*Wasp nest*, 露蜂房, tag SBJ) are different records: the first is an Egyptian list item, the second a different Shennong monograph on a nest. Nothing is merged.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** In the transcription the heading line reads only 下经 (line 3875), and the name of the entry is not printed. The classic's own paragraph is at line 3877: 味辛，平。主久聋、咳逆、毒瓦斯，出刺出汗。生川谷。 In English: "Flavour acrid, level. Governs long-standing deafness (久聋), cough with counterflow (咳逆), [毒瓦斯], draws out thorns (出刺), brings out sweat (出汗). Grows in river valleys." The term 毒瓦斯 means "poison gas" in modern Chinese and cannot belong to a classic. It is probably a machine conversion of 毒气, "toxic qi", but that is the Codex's guess and builds nothing on it. [USER: verify] The paragraph has no 久服 clause. It names no part.

The classic places the entry in the lower class (下经; heading 卷三 下经 at line 3883). The preface (line 2935) describes the lower class as for treating disease, mostly toxic, not to be taken long.

**Mingyi bielu (《名医》, later additions) layer.** Line 3879: "also called 土蜂 ('earth bee'); grows at Xiong'er and [a place name lost], or in people's houses" (一名土蜂。生熊耳及[ ]柯，或人屋间). This alias is the only direct support for the identification as an earth-nesting wasp.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案).** Line 3881 is a chain of glosses, in which several characters are lost in the transcription. The *Shuowen*: "[ ], [ ]蠃, 蒲卢, the thin-waisted earth bee" (细要土蜂也), also written 螺蠃. The *Guangya*: "土蜂 is [ ]". The *Erya*: "土蜂". The *Mao shi*: "The caterpillar has young; the 螺蠃 carries them on its back" (螟蛉有子，螺蠃负之), with the commentary "螺蠃 is 卢". The *Liji*: "Government is 蒲卢", and Zheng Xuan explains that 蒲卢 (果蠃) "means the earth bee". The *Fangyan*: of bees "the small ones are called [ ], or 蚴蜕". Sun's last sentence is that the *Shuowen* has no [lost] character, "or it should be 医" (或当为医), apparently about the character of the heading. The Codex cannot resolve that sentence from the transcription. The glosses all describe a thin-waisted, earth-dwelling wasp whose proverbial habit is to carry caterpillars to its young. That habit is general knowledge about solitary wasps, and the Odes line is the old text's reflection of it.

### Active Components

None is stated in the text. The Codex has no source on the chemistry of the material the classic meant (the whole wasp, its nest or its mud, none of which the text specifies). No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic records long-standing deafness, cough, and the drawing out of thorns and of sweat, without a preparation or part. The Codex gives no protocol. Deafness and cough need medical assessment. Handling wasps or their nests carries sting and allergy risks, which are general facts.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic names no preparation, no part and no quantity. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

The identification is ours and weak at species level. The Chinese names cover solitary wasps of more than one genus. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower class (下经), which the preface describes as for treating disease, mostly toxic, and not for long use. Its 味 paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry heading lost, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3877). The frame is a warning and not a promise. The Mingyi and 案 layers add none.

**Practitioner's note.** There is no cultivation practice here. The entry is a short lower-class record for deafness, cough and thorns, in a material whose name the transcription has lost.

### Contraindications

The preface warns that lower-class drugs are mostly toxic and not for long use. No safety information for this material in particular was read. Wasps sting, and stings can be dangerous to people with allergy; nests may harbour other insects and moulds. No conservation information was read. [USER: verify]
