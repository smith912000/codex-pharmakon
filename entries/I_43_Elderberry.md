### Botanical Identity
Deciduous shrub/small tree 3–7m; compound pinnate leaves; large flat umbels of small white flowers (elderflower); clusters of small dark purple-black berries (elderberry); native to Europe and western Asia; cultivated and naturalised globally. **Toxicity note**: raw berries, bark, and leaves contain sambunigrin (cyanogenic glycoside) → raw consumption causes nausea/vomiting. Cooking destroys cyanogenic activity. The flowers are non-toxic raw.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 De Materia Medica (DMM)

Dioscorides (*Book IV, Chapter 173*):
> *"Aktea/Sambukos: the bark promotes urine and purges dropsy. The leaves applied warm reduce swellings and promote tumours to ripen. The berries (ripe) taken in wine promote urine and sweating. The root bark is a purge. The flower, dried, is good for the eyes."*

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā on elder:
> *"Elder: warming and drying. The berries relieve headache, promote sweating and urine, warm cold conditions. The flowers benefit the eyes and relieve head pain when inhaled. The bark purges phlegm and water from the body (dropsy)."*

### 2.3 Ebers Papyrus (EP)

*Sambucus ebulus* (dwarf elder) preparations in Egyptian texts for inflammatory and dropsical conditions — consistent with the diuretic/anti-inflammatory application.

### 2.4 Assyrian Medical Tablets (AT)

Elder-type tree preparations in cuneiform texts for fever and inflammatory conditions — consistent with the antipyretic applications.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Antiviral (Primary Modern Application)

**Cyanidin-3-glucoside + cyanidin-3-sambubioside**: Bind to influenza hemagglutinin (H1N1, H3N2) → prevent viral attachment to host cell → antiviral. Also: inhibit neuraminidase (the same target as oseltamivir/Tamiflu) → prevent viral release from infected cells.

**Sambucine (lectins)**: Additional antiviral and immunostimulatory activity.

This dual mechanism (hemagglutinin binding + neuraminidase inhibition) explains why elderberry has demonstrated clinical antiviral efficacy against influenza — it hits both the viral entry and exit stages.

### 5.2 Immunostimulatory

**Anthocyanins + quercetin**: Activate macrophages and NK cells → improved innate immunity. Increase interferon production → antiviral state induction. The combination of direct antiviral activity + immune enhancement creates a dual protection profile.

### 5.3 Anti-inflammatory

**Quercetin + rutin**: COX-2 and 5-LOX inhibition → reduced prostaglandins and leukotrienes → anti-inflammatory, antipyretic. Explains the traditional fever-reducing and inflammatory applications.

### 5.4 Diuretic

Elderflower: increases renal blood flow → diuretic. Potassium-sparing (unlike pharmaceutical diuretics that cause potassium loss).

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Influenza**: Zakay-Rones et al. (2004, *J Int Med Res*): elderberry extract vs placebo in confirmed influenza — 4 days faster recovery, significant reduction in fever, mucus, and symptom scores. Tiralongo et al. (2016): elderberry before/after transatlantic flight — significant reduction in cold duration and severity. Hawkins et al. (2019): meta-analysis — elderberry substantially reduces upper respiratory symptom duration.

**Antiviral (in vitro)**: Consistent evidence across HSV, influenza A/B, SARS-CoV (in vitro), adenovirus.

**Immunostimulatory**: Multiple human studies confirm cytokine elevation and NK cell activity.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The elder tree spirit**: In European folklore, the elder tree was inhabited by a spirit — the *Elder Mother* (*Hylde-Moer* in Danish) who had to be asked permission before cutting branches or harvesting. This animistic respect for a pharmacological resource is consistent across northern European cultures: a tree that heals fevers, reduces inflammation, and fights infections would quickly acquire protective spiritual status in communities dependent on it for survival.

**The instrument and the medicine**: The use of elder wood for musical instruments (*sambuke*) alongside its medical applications reflects the elder tree's total integration into human culture — its hollow stem makes it naturally useful for pipes, its berries for medicine and wine, its flowers for elderflower cordial. Plants embedded so deeply into multiple aspects of culture (musical, medicinal, culinary) tend to be the ones most thoroughly observed pharmacologically.

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Sweet, slightly bitter; cool. Clears Wind-Heat, promotes sweating, benefits the eyes. Pattern: Wind-Heat invasion (early cold/flu with fever and sore throat).

**Ayurvedic**: Sweet; cooling. Reduces Pitta; anti-inflammatory. (Minor Ayurvedic tradition; predominantly European/Islamic herb.)

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Raw berries**: Cyanogenic — always cook; nausea/vomiting from raw; fatal cases documented with bark/root preparations
- **Autoimmune conditions**: Immunostimulatory — may exacerbate MS, lupus, RA
- **Immunosuppressant drugs**: Counteracts immunosuppression — avoid after organ transplant
- **Children under 1**: As with any strong immune stimulant — consult pediatric guidance
