# Common Honeysuckle — Periklumenon (Dioscorides 4-14), "Woodbine, Perfoliate Honeysuckle, Caprifoly" (translator's gloss)

## *Lonicera periclymenum* (spine botanical; the translator's gloss for Dioscorides 4-14 lists "Lonicera periclymenum [Linnaeus]" and "Lonicera caprifolium") | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** AT, DMM, CM

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity warning.** Only the Dioscorides tag has a locus. The identification of Dioscorides' *periklumenon* with the honeysuckle is the translator's list (it names two species, *L. periclymenum* and *L. caprifolium*), and Dioscorides' description is of a small climbing plant with a hard round seed and a thick round root, and the chapter itself names no *Lonicera*. The extraction file `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` (row for 4-14) also classes the chapter as "animal", which is a data error: the chapter is a plant. [USER: verify] The Assyrian and Avicenna tags have no locus.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 4-14, "PERIKLUMENON" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, text line 8114; the translator's gloss at lines 8115 and 8116). The description: "Periclymenon is a single little shrub with small whitish leaves circling it at distances similar to cissus [2-210], and by the leaves' emergence are seeds similar to cissus. On top is a white flower similar to the bean, a somewhat round hard seed (in a way) lying on the leaf and hard to pluck out; the root is thick and round. It grows in fields and hedges and winds itself around the neighbouring shrubs." The seed is "gathered when it is ripe and dried in the shade". Uses: "A teaspoonful (taken in a drink for forty days) reduces the spleen, dissolves weariness, and is good for difficult breathing and the hiccups. After the sixth day it makes one urinate blood. It is also birth hastening, and the leaves have the same strength. A decoction (taken as a drink for thirty seven days) is said to make men unfit for generation [birth control]. Rubbed (with oil) on those who have fever fits that recur, it drives away the shivering." Other names printed include aegine, clymenon, carpathum, splenium, hepatitis, helxine major, clematitis, myrsine and calycanthemon; the Magi call it poliom veneris, the Egyptians turcum, the Romans volucrum majus and the Africans lanath. The word "periclymenon" also appears in the closing names of the preceding chapter (line 8113), which belongs to another plant. The only other honeysuckle in the translator's lists is "French Honeysuckle" under 3-146 (line 7801), which is *Hedysarum coronarium* and not a *Lonicera*.

### Active Components

Honeysuckles are generally known to contain iridoid glycosides, flavonoids and phenolic acids, and the berries of many *Lonicera* species are generally regarded as unfit to eat. The content of *L. periclymenum*, and of Dioscorides' plant, was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides' uses (a seed drink for the spleen, breathing and hiccups, a decoction said to make men infertile, an oil rub for recurrent fever) are recorded as his, and the Codex does not recommend them. The plant is a hedgerow climber, and the Codex gives no protocol for it.

### Preparation & Dosing

Dioscorides gives "a teaspoonful (taken in a drink for forty days)" of the seed and "a decoction (taken as a drink for thirty seven days)" (4-14, line 8114), with a note that it provokes bloody urine after the sixth day. These are his figures for his plant and are not offered as doses. No other dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent* as to cultivation status. Dioscorides carries no subtle-body system. What he offers a cultivator is attention: he records the habitat ("fields and hedges"), how the plant winds on neighbouring shrubs, the seed's hardness, the gathering of ripe seed and drying "in the shade", and the length of a course (forty days, thirty-seven days) together with a warning of an adverse effect. **Practitioner's note.** There is nothing to cultivate here in the Codex's sense. The one Greek account is therapeutic and descriptive, and it comes with an adverse-effect warning and a long fixed course. The Assyrian and Unani tags are unsupported.

### Contraindications

Dioscorides himself records that the seed drink makes one "urinate blood" after six days and that a decoction is said to make men unfit for generation. These are reasons for caution and not recommendations. The berries of honeysuckles are generally not eaten. Do not use any part of the plant in pregnancy or for children on the strength of this entry, and seek ordinary medical care for spleen, breathing or fever complaints.
