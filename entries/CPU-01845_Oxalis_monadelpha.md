# Oxalis monadelpha — Changeri; Amla-lonika (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Oxalis monadelpha, the first of two species the Latin line of Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Oxalis  Monadelpha  ;  O.  corniculata.", line 31549) against the Sanskrit name Changeri (OCR "OhaCageri", line 31514). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print Changeri in a potherb passage and two recipes, with no Latin name; the appendix also cross-refers a second name, Amla-lonika, to Changeri* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The chapters give Changeri a taste, a potency and an action (Sutra 46) and name it in two recipes (Uttara 40); they give no Latin. The Latin line prints two species, *O. monadelpha* and *O. corniculata*, and the batch takes the first; the pairing is positional and agent-proposed, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 453 gives the name as "OhaCageri", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31514; id line 31549)" and the Latin as "Oxalis  Monadelpha  ;  O.  corniculata.", with the note that the pairing was proposed by an agent from the two OCR columns and checked by script. The OCR reads the C of this page as O throughout ("Ohakramarda" for Chakramarda, "Ohanaka" for Chanaka, "Ohandana" for Chandana), so the Codex reads "OhaCageri" as Changeri, the spelling the chapters print.

**The pairing, checked by counting.** On the page headed "68" (line 31500) the Sanskrit names run from Chakramarda (line 31506) to Chuta (31534): fifteen printed lines, of which Choraka and Chora-pushpi are bracketed and Chavya, Chavika and Chavyaka share one Latin line, so that the Latin lines (31542 to 31560) are twelve and the Sanskrit entries twelve on that reading. From the start, Chakramarda stands against "Cassia Tora" (31542), Champaka against "Michelia Champaca" (31544), Chanaka against "Cicer Arietinum" (31546), Chandana against "Santalum Album" (31547) and **Changeri against "Oxalis Monadelpha ; O. corniculata"** (31549). From the end, Chuta stands against "See Amra" (31560), Choraka with Chora-pushpi against "Andropogon Aciculaiis" (31558), Chitraka-phala against "See Indra-viruni" (31556), Chitraka against "Pleumbago Zeylanica" (31555), Chira-vilva against "Pongamia Glabra" (31554) and Chhatraka against "Agaricus Campestris" (31553), leaving "Piper Chaba" (31551) for the three Chavya names. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Changeri, its OCR forms (Chdngeri, CJidngeri) and Amla-lonika, and by fuzzy matching of every word. Changeri is printed in three places.

*The potherb (Sutra ch.46).* `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1724 to 1728, in the chapter on food and drink: "The species called the Changeri has an acid, astringent and sweet taste. It is appetising and heat-making in its potency, proves beneficial in cases of deranged Vayu and Kapham and curative in cases of mesenteric disorders (Grahani) and piles." The passage stands among the potherbs (the leaves of edible plants); the text does not name the part used.

*A ghrita for diarrhoea (Uttara ch.40).* `SS_Uttara_ch40.txt` lines 291 to 296, in the chapter on diarrhoea (Atisara): "The Ghrita duly cooked with Nagara, Changeri and Kola (Vadara) and with milk, curd (Dadhi) and Amla (Kanjika), or simply the transparent part of clarified butter should be taken as a relief for Atisara attended with Sula (pain)." A translator's note (lines 321 to 323) says the recipe is in the Charaka Samhita too, but that there "Kshara" (alkali) stands in place of "Kshira" (milk) "and that appears to be the better reading", and sends the reader to "Changeri Ghrita" in the Charaka Samhita, chapter XIX. The Sushruta text does not itself describe the Charaka recipe. This passage is also quoted in the entry on Badari (I_911).

*A ghrita for Grahani (Uttara ch.40).* Lines 1001 to 1006, in the treatment of Grahani in the same chapter: the Hingvadi-churna, "or the medicated clarified butter described as remedial in a case of enlarged spleen, or the one duly cooked with the expressed juice of Changeri and with the drugs of the Magadhadi (Pippalyadi) group as Kalka may be likewise prescribed with beneficial results."

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Changeri and Amla-lonika found nothing.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Oxalis* is a genus of the wood-sorrel family (Oxalidaceae), whose species taste sour because they contain oxalates. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. In the Sushruta text the herb is a sour-astringent-sweet potherb said to help Vayu and Kapham disorders, Grahani and piles, and an ingredient in two ghritas for diarrhoea and Grahani. The Codex does not recommend any of these, gives no protocol and no dose, and notes that the plant behind the name rests on a translator's appendix pairing.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters give these preparations that include the name: a ghrita cooked with Nagara, Changeri and Kola and with milk, curd and Kanjika (Uttara 40, lines 291 to 296); a ghrita cooked with the expressed juice of Changeri and the Magadhadi drugs as Kalka (Uttara 40, lines 1001 to 1006). No quantity is given for Changeri in either. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this herb. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or eaten on the strength of this entry. As a general fact, wood-sorrels are rich in oxalates; people with kidney stones, gout or renal impairment are usually advised to avoid oxalate-rich plants. Diarrhoea that is persistent, bloody or in a child needs medical care. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
