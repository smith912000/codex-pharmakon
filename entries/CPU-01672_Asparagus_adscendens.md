### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** **Named only by the Tibetan texts, and under this binomial only by the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. Both Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The extraction note says: "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, row 329). The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (Explanatory Tantra, glossary).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. The Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients prints its columns in separate blocks. In the "N" block (the letter at line 5500; page number 312 at line 5572) the phonetic names are at lines 5539 to 5541: "Ngonbu", "Nheu", "Nimpa", "Nyalo", "Nyangtse trae", "Nye shing", "Nyiga", "Nying shhosha" and "Nyiwa" (nine). The Tibetan transliterations are at lines 5543 to 5546 (nine again: "sngon bu", "sne'u", "nim pa", "mya lo", "myang rtsi spras", "nye shing", "nyi dga'", "snying zho sha", "snyi ba"). The scientific names (lines 5548 to 5570) are, in order, "Cyananthus lobatus", "Chenopodium album", "Azadirachta indica", "Aconogonom campanalatum", "Coptis teetoides", "Asparagus adscendens" (line 5563), "Malva botka" (line 5564), "Choerospondias axillaris" and "Codonopsis convolvulacea" (nine). The English names include "Fat-hen", "Neem", "Gold thread", "Wild asparagus Marsh mallow" (line 5566), "Hog plum" and "Bell flower". "Asparagus adscendens" is the sixth of the nine scientific names, and "Nye shing" is the sixth of the nine phonetic names. The glossary does not print them on one row, and the Codex does not state that it pairs them. The pairing is by column position only, and the English "Wild asparagus" (which stands on one line with "Marsh mallow") is placed beside the binomial by order as well. The ends of the block agree with other lines: the last name, "Nyiwa" (*snyi ba*), is the name that Clark prints as *snyi.wa* for *Codonopsis convolvulacea* (line 1061), and "Nimpa" falls against "Azadirachta indica" and "Neem". *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, the name "Nye shing" in the running text.* The Explanatory Tantra text uses the name in three places. In the first part (the Root Tantra, "Basic Tantra", therapeutic methods) a note (line 1144) reads: "The five roots are Ra nye, Nye shing, Chawa, Bhatu and Zema", to the passage on medicinal butters (line 1116). In chapter 19 (tastes), line 3204 lists "Shingngar, Gundrum, Gurkum, Chugang, Dongga, Ra nye, Nye shing, Chawa, sugar, molasses, honey, meat, butter and other similar substances" as "the group of medicinal substances having a sweet taste". In chapter 20, in the efficacy of herbal medicines (the section begins at line 3428), line 3502 reads: "Nye shing and Ra nye prolong life and cure chhuser disorders." That the glossary's "Nye shing" is the name in these lines is a reading from the identical spelling. The text does not say that the plant in these lines is *Asparagus adscendens*. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, the same Tibetan name with other binomials (line 1143).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The entry reads: "nye.shing: Asparagus filicinus: Asparagus racemosus Willd: Polygonatum falcatum: [has eighteen names. It has a tall, thin, thorny stalk and a seed resembling an iron pea. The root has bitter and astringent tastes and] cures disorders of the xiphoid process." The next entry, *ra.mnye* (line 1144), ends with a sentence that names both: "Both Asparagus racemosus and Polygonatum promote longevity and cure lymph disorders." Clark's preface (line 82) says "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". So the names, the description and the tastes are the commentary layer, and what stands outside the brackets is Clark's own identifications (editorial, not root text) and the root-text clause "cures disorders of the xiphoid process". The sentence on longevity stands outside the brackets and names *Asparagus racemosus*, the second binomial; it does not name *Asparagus adscendens*. Clark does not print *Asparagus adscendens*. The spine holds a separate row for the first Clark binomial (CPU-01673, *Asparagus filicinus*).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Men-Tsee-Khang text records, under the name "Nye shing", a drug that belongs to the sweet-tasting group and to the "five roots", and that "prolong[s] life and cure[s] chhuser disorders". These are the text's claims, under a name that the glossary pairs with this binomial only by position, and they are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Men-Tsee-Khang materia medica says of "Nye shing and Ra nye" that they "prolong life and cure chhuser disorders" (line 3502), and lists the name among the "five roots" and the sweet-tasting medicines. A life-prolonging action is a claim about what the drug does, and the line does not use the term *bcud len* (essence extraction) or assign a place in a practice, so it is graded (b). The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. The Clark line that comes nearest, "Both Asparagus racemosus and Polygonatum promote longevity" (line 1144), names *Asparagus racemosus* and not this species, and the phrase "highly recommended in essence-extraction compounds" in the same entry is inside the brackets and belongs to *ra.mnye* (*Polygonatum*). The rejuvenation chapters (Clark chapter 23, Men-Tsee-Khang chapter 23) were read for names and do not name "Nye shing". The link from this binomial to the life-prolonging line rests on a column position and a spelling.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The Tibetan name behind the binomial is a "five roots" drug that the Men-Tsee-Khang text says prolongs life, and the link to *Asparagus adscendens* rests on a column position. The abstentive answer is the honest one.
