# Nalina — Nalina (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *A drug of the Anjanadi group, and a name that chapter 13 lists among aquatic plants beside Padma, Utpala, Kumuda and Pundarika; no text or glossary read gives a botanical name. Identification: an aquatic plant of the lotus and water-lily kind, species unresolved. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not identified to species. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The first-pass guess was "possibly *Nelumbo*". The text read supports "an aquatic plant of the lotus kind" and no more. A search of the three Bhishagratna volumes for the name found two places only.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

1. **Group list.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38, the Anjanadi group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15641 to 15645; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 300 to 303). Bhishagratna prints: "The group known as the Anjanadi Gana consists of Anjana, Rasanjana, Nagapushpa, Priyangu, Nilotpala, Nalada, Nalina, Keshara and Madhuka."
2. **The group's virtue.** The metrical text gives it for the group as a whole: "An attack of haemoptysis readily yields to the curative virtue of the group under discussion. It is anti-toxic in its character and allays the internal burning sensation of the body" (Vol. I lines 15646 to 15650). This is the group's virtue, not a statement about Nalina alone.
3. **Chapter 13, on leeches.** *Sutrasthana* ch.13 (`text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch13.txt` lines 129 to 133; Vol. I line 7244) says the origin of the non-venomous leeches "is ascribed to such decomposed vegetable matter, as the petrified stems of the several aquatic plants known as Padma, Utpalam, Nalina, Kumuda, Pundarika, and the common zoophytes which live in clear waters". Here the name stands in a list of aquatic plants. This is the only evidence of what kind of plant it is, and it is a list, not a description.

The translator's glossary in Vol. III (Appendix 3) did not return this name, and no part used, taste or potency was found. [USER: verify against the printed pages]

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in a chapter 38 group list, which classifies drugs by the use of the whole group (*Dravya-Sangrahaniya*). Chapter 38 is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns this drug no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only, and only at the level of its group. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it.

**Practitioner's note.** A name in a group list, and in a list of aquatic plants. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without an identification nothing can be gathered or used.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is not identified to species. Do not substitute any lotus or water lily for it.
- **The conditions named.** Haemoptysis, poisoning and burning sensations are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Group membership.** The drug is one of nine in its group, and the group's virtues are not safety statements about any one member.
