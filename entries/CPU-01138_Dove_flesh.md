# Dove flesh — "the dove, pigeon" (Bhishagratna), in the Pratuda group of the Sushruta meat chapter; Kapota (the Sanskrit word in the source list)
## *The meat of the dove or pigeon. Bhishagratna's English prints "the dove, pigeon" at the head of the Pratuda list and "Kana Kapota (wild dove)" in the verse that follows; the source list `_sources/sushruta.csv` (line 173) gives the Sanskrit as "Kapota (dove/pigeon)". No binomial is printed in the lines read* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The English is Bhishagratna's translation of the *Sushruta Samhita*. The dove is named in the meat group of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink (Annapana-Vidhi-madhyayam)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 4-6). The chapter lists the kinds of flesh by habitat, and says that "each succeeding kind is superior to the one immediately preceding it in the order of enumeration" (lines 387-394). A second classification (lines 396-409) divides the high-ground (*Jangala*) class into eight sub-species: the *Janghala*, the *Viscira*, the *Pratuda*, the *Guhashaya*, the *Prasaha*, the *Parnamriga*, the *Vileshaya* and the *Gramya*; of these the *Janghala* and *Viscira* "are the most important". The dove belongs to the third, the *Pratuda*.

The Pratuda definition (lines 506-511): "Birds such as the dove, pigeon Bhringaraja, cuckoo, Koyashtica, Kulinga, the domestic Kulinga, Gokshada, Dindimanaka, Shatapatraka, Matrinindaka, Bhedashi, Shuka, Sharika, Valguli, Girisha, Alahva, Dushaka, Sugrihi, Khanjaritaka, Harita, Datyuha, etc. belong to the group known as the Pratuda." The group verse follows (lines 513-518): "The Pratudas live on fruit, and their flesh has a sweet and astringent taste. It generates Vayu and produces a parched condition in the organism. It is cooling in its potency and reduces the Pittam and Kapham. It suppresses the discharge of urine and reduces the quantity of stool."

Two statements then single out members (lines 519-527): "Of these the flesh of the Bhedashi tends to vitiate the humours and to derange the three excrements of the body. The flesh of the Kana Kapota (wild dove) is heavy and has a palatable, saline and astringent taste. It proves beneficial in haemoptysis and is sweet of digestion." The next sentences concern the *Kulinga*. So the English gives the *Kana Kapota*, the wild dove, a line of its own, while the plain *Kapota* is named only in the list at the head of the group. What the chapter says of the domestic or ordinary dove is therefore the group statement; whether the wild dove's line holds for the other doves is not said.

In the chapter on liquids, "The fat, marrow, etc. of animals of the Pratuda and Vishkira species (doves, pigeons, etc.) reduce the bodily Kapham" (*Sutrasthana* ch.45, lines 1068-1070).

Other places where Sushruta names the dove or pigeon, which I opened:

- *As a food listed.* In the chapter on the salutary and non-salutary effects of regimen (*Hitahitiya*, *Sutrasthana* ch.20; lines 4-6 give the title), the *Kapota* (pigeon) is named in the list of meats, with the *Lava*, *Tittiri* and others, that may be eaten by all people (lines 50-56). The same chapter lists incompatible preparations, and names among them "Flesh of pigeon fried with mustard oil should not be eaten" (line 178).
- *Hiccough.* In the chapter on hiccough (*Uttaratantra* ch.50, lines 182-189), a soup made of the meat of the porcupine and other animals, "as well as of Kapota and Pardvata (different kinds of pigeon), Lava and other birds" is to be taken lukewarm with the expressed juice of acid fruits, rock salt and clarified butter.
- *After certain medicines.* In the chapter on diabetes (*Madhu-Meha*, *Chikitsasthana* ch.13, lines 100-105), a person using *Shilajatu* and *Makshika* in the manner prescribed "should refrain from taking pigeon-flesh and Kulattha pulse (during his life-time)".
- *Dung.* The dung of a pigeon is an ingredient in a plaster for the papillary growths around an insect bite (*Kalpasthana* ch.8, lines 807-808), and "the dung of a Kapota and of Pardvata" is in a plaster for a tumour (*Chikitsasthana* ch.18, lines 390-392).
- *Wine after the meat.* After a meal of the flesh of the Pratuda species, the wine of *Kshira-Vriksha* is to be used (*Sutrasthana* ch.46, lines 2840-2842).

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms dove and pigeon meat is skeletal muscle: protein, with a low to moderate fat content, iron and B vitamins. Nothing more specific can be said without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text records the flesh of the Pratuda birds as sweet and astringent, cooling, reducing *Pittam* and *Kapham*, generating *Vayu*, and suppressing urine and stool, and records the wild dove's flesh as beneficial in haemoptysis. It also records pigeon flesh as something to refuse after *Shilajatu* and *Makshika*, and not to fry in mustard oil. Dove and pigeon are eaten in some regions and avoided in others; the Codex gives no regimen built on them. Haemoptysis, coughing of blood, is a reason for urgent medical assessment, not a diet. The non-meat uses (the dung) are not recommended.

### Preparation & Dosing

The food chapter gives no preparation for dove flesh. The preparations named elsewhere are a soup with the juice of acid fruits, rock salt and clarified butter for hiccough (ch.50), and the warning against frying pigeon flesh in mustard oil (ch.20, line 178). ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Dove and pigeon meat is a source of protein and iron. Raw or undercooked poultry and game birds carry bacterial and parasitic risk. Wild and feral pigeons can carry psittacosis (*Chlamydia psittaci*), salmonella and cryptococcus, and their droppings are an infection risk. Cooling, astringent and *Pittam*-reducing properties are Ayurvedic categories and are not established by clinical evidence. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** ch.46 lines 513-518 (Pratuda group): *rasa* sweet and astringent; cooling in potency (*virya*); generates *Vayu*; reduces *Pittam* and *Kapham*; produces a parched condition; suppresses urine and reduces stool. Lines 521-527 (*Kana Kapota*, wild dove): heavy; palatable, saline and astringent in taste; sweet in digestion (*vipaka*); beneficial in haemoptysis. The English prints no *vipaka* for the group as a whole, and no separate statement for the plain *Kapota*.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dove flesh is attested in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 as one of the Pratuda birds (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 506), with a group statement of tastes and effects that does not use the words "tonic" or "constructive". No passage read puts dove flesh in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and the English prints no claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. Pigeon flesh appears in the diabetes chapter as a food to be refused for life by a person who uses *Shilajatu* and *Makshika* (ch.13, line 105); that is a rule for a user of those two drugs, and not a general restriction or a cultivation status. The popular *sattvic*/*rajasic*/*tamasic* food lists belong to *Samkhya*-era and later material, not to Sushruta, and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes dove flesh a cultivation substance. Sushruta files it with the Pratuda birds, gives the wild dove a line of its own, and elsewhere names the pigeon as a food to avoid in two situations. For a cultivator the honest use is ordinary diet where dove is an ordinary food, and nothing more. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** raw or undercooked game birds carry bacterial and parasitic risk; wild and feral pigeons can carry psittacosis, salmonella and cryptococcus, and handling their droppings is a risk.
- **Coughing blood:** haemoptysis needs urgent medical assessment.
- **Allergy:** some people react to poultry and game-bird meat.
- **Non-meat uses:** the dung plasters recorded by Sushruta are not recommended.
- **Ethics and law:** wild doves and pigeons are protected by law in many places and many are protected species, and the hunting season and the capture of wild birds are regulated. The Codex does not recommend harming any animal for these uses.
