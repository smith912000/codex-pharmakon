# Black chameleon thistle — kamālyūn / khāmālyūn (كماليون)

## *In the Canon, "a kind of mazariyun, black and deadly"; the spine's Atractylis gummifera is the modern match for Dioscorides' black chameleon and the Canon's own words point to a different plant group (see identity doubt)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** Book II, entry *kamālyūn*, Arabic text lines 14066 to 14068. The entry is a cross-reference of one sentence.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** What it is: "a kind of *māzaryūn*, black, deadly (*qattāl*), and it is also the one known as *khāmālyūn*, and we have spoken about that previously" (صنف من المازريون أسود قتال وهو أيضا المعروف بخاماليون وقد تكلمنا في ذلك فيما سبق). That is the whole entry: no temperament, no use, no dose.
The entry points to the *māzaryūn* entry (heading at line 14965), read in full for this file. It says: "*māzaryūn* is a large spurge (*yatūʿ*), and it is of two kinds: one with large thin leaves, and the other small in the leaf and thick, which is the worse of the two; and what is black is deadly" (lines 14966 to 14967). Its best is the one with many leaves "resembling the leaf of the olive" (lines 14967 to 14968). It is "hot and dry in the fourth [degree]" (lines 14969 to 14970); all its kinds are used as an external paint for vitiligo, leucoderma and freckles (lines 14970 to 14972); it "harms the liver greatly" (lines 14974 to 14975); and it purges water, with a soaked dose of "six drachms" boiled down (lines 14976 to 14978). On poison: "it is given with wine for the bites of vermin, and the black in particular is deadly: mixed with *sawīq*, gathered with water and oil, it kills mice, dogs and pigs, and the deadly dose for people is two dirhams; it kills with distress, vomiting and purging" (lines 14985 to 14988). In a later part of the Canon (the book is not identified here) a passage is headed "on *māzaryūn* and *khāmālāʾūn*": "the deadly draught of it is two dirhams; vomiting and excessive purging come on, and the black, called *khāmālāʾūn*, is the more deadly; there comes a severe burning in the bowels and pain in the whole body, a tickling, hiccup, then phlegmy and frothy vomiting, and it leads to tetanus (*kuzāz*) and loss of the voice" (lines 54871 to 54875). All of this is a historical record of a poison and not a recommendation.

**Identity doubt.** The spine gives *Atractylis gummifera* L., the thistle that modern authors identify with Dioscorides' black chameleon (*chamaileon melas*) [USER: verify]. The Canon says something else: it calls *kamālyūn* a kind of *māzaryūn*, which Avicenna describes as a large spurge, a plant of fourth-degree heat with olive-like leaves in its best kind. Spurge-type plants and thistles are different groups. Avicenna's text does not describe a thistle. Nothing here is therefore said about *Atractylis*, and the "thistle" in the entry name is the spine's, not the text's. ### Active Components

The Canon names none. As a general fact, *Atractylis gummifera* contains atractyloside, a toxic glycoside (the plant is a well-known cause of poisoning in the Mediterranean); this is stated for the spine's plant and not for the Canon's, whose identity is in doubt. [USER: verify.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and as a poison. There is no use here and no protocol. The Canon records the black kind as deadly.

### Preparation & Dosing

The entry gives none. The *māzaryūn* entry gives the deadly dose as two dirhams (lines 14987 to 14988). No therapeutic dose for the black kind is given. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify.] Poisoning by plants of both groups is a known toxicological subject; nothing is added here beyond the line above.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

The entry gives none. The *māzaryūn* entry says hot and dry in the fourth degree (lines 14969 to 14970); that classification is not Avicenna's word for *kamālyūn* itself.

### Contraindications

- Deadly poison by the Canon's own word. Do not handle, prepare or ingest.
- Thistle and spurge plants are skin and mucosal irritants; their identification is uncertain here.
