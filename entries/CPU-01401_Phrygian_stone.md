# Phrygian stone — Phrugios lithos (the dyers' stone of Phrygia, found in Cappadocia), Dioscorides 5-141
## *A pale, mildly heavy stone with white partitions "like cadmia", burnt three times in wine over coals and used with stiff ointment for binding, cleaning, forming scabs and healing burns; the translator gives no identification ("UNKNOWN")* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-141, *Phrugios lithos* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10568; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 914, translator identification "UNKNOWN", class guess mineral; list row `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 867, "Phrugios Lithos", mineral, flagged as an uncertain identification; the book index sends the reader to page 816). The whole chapter was read. It reads (line 10571):

"The Phrygian stone the dyers in Phrygia use (hence its name) is found in Cappadocia. The best is pale and mildly heavy, not firm from compaction, with partitions of white in-between like cadmia [5-84]. The stone is burnt as follows. Moisten it with the best wine, cover it with live coals, and blow on it continuously. When it changes colour to a more deep yellow, take it out, quench it with the same wine, place it into the coals again, then do the same things and burn it a third time, taking care that it does not crumble and vanish into soot. Both raw and burnt it is effective with stiff ointment for binding, cleaning, forming scabs, and healing burns. It is washed like cadmia [5-84]."

The chapter thus gives a name-origin (the stone the dyers in Phrygia use), a place of finding (Cappadocia, not Phrygia), a rule for choosing the best (pale, mildly heavy, not firm from compaction, with white partitions "like cadmia"), a full burning method, a use (with stiff ointment, raw or burnt, for binding, cleaning, scab formation and burns), and a washing instruction by reference. It says nothing of season and gives no adulterant. It states that the dyers use the stone, but not for what dyeing purpose.

**The cadmia comparison and the washing.** The "partitions of white ... like cadmia [5-84]" refers to the first cadmia chapter, which describes cadmia in several kinds (for example "an azure [blue] colour on the outside and whiter within with veins like an onyx stone") (line 10222). The washing "like cadmia" refers to the end of the same chapter: "Being pounded with water in a mortar, the water then poured away until no filth remains on it washes it; then it is made into balls and put in jars" (line 10227). The Phrygian-stone chapter only points to that method and does not itself describe it.

**Other chapters that refer to it.** In 5-144 (*aimatites lithos*, haematite) the burning of haematite is described as "burnt like Phrygian [dyer's] stone, only the wine omitted. It is properly burnt when it is mildly light and swollen up with bubbles." (line 10589). That chapter is about another substance, and its bracket "[dyer's]" is the translator's. It shows the wine-and-coals burning of the Phrygian stone cited as the method for another stone.

**Identity.** The translator gives no identification ("UNKNOWN"), and the Codex list marks the identification uncertain. The chapter's clues (pale, mildly heavy, not firm from compaction, white partitions, turns deeper yellow on burning, used by dyers) do not name a mineral. Which material Dioscorides meant is not settled by the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated from the chapter. The stone is unidentified, so no composition is asserted. The chapter reports that it deepens to yellow when burned in wine and coals and that it can crumble to soot if overburned; no constituent is inferred from these observations.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records an unidentified dyers' stone, prepared by thrice burning in wine and coals, as an ingredient of stiff ointments for binding wounds, cleaning them, forming scabs and healing burns. The identification is uncertain, the composition unknown, and the method involves open coals, wine and fine mineral dust. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

---

### Preparation & Dosing

The burning method in 5-141 is, step by step: "Moisten it with the best wine, cover it with live coals, and blow on it continuously. When it changes colour to a more deep yellow, take it out, quench it with the same wine, place it into the coals again, then do the same things and burn it a third time, taking care that it does not crumble and vanish into soot." The stone, raw or burnt, is used "with stiff ointment"; the chapter does not name the ointment base, the proportion of stone to ointment, how long it stays on, or how often it is renewed. Washing is "like cadmia [5-84]" (see above). Haematite (5-144) is said to be burnt in the same manner without wine. No quantity is given for any step. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What the herbal offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention (provenance, season of gathering, sensory assay, adulteration detection), and this chapter shows much of it: the trade context of the stone (the dyers' use), the place of finding (Cappadocia, although the name says Phrygia), a rule for the best specimen by colour, weight and structure, and a staged burning method with colour as the sign of completion and a warning against overburning. It gives no season and no adulterant. The chapter is therapeutic and carries no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation beyond the discipline the chapter shows: choose by appearance, process by stages, and use the colour as the sign to stop. The stone itself is unidentified and the method uses coals, wine and mineral dust. It is not a recommended practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Unidentified material:** the identification is uncertain; an unknown stone should not be burnt, ground or applied to wounds or burns.
- **Burning and dust:** the method uses live coals blown continuously; fumes and dust are a risk to the lungs and eyes.
- **Burns:** burns need cooling and medical assessment; mineral ointments on open burns risk infection.
- **Serious conditions:** large or deep burns and wounds need urgent medical care.
