# Mulberry mistletoe — 桑上寄生 *Sang shang jisheng* (Shennong bencao jing), with 寄屑, 寓木, 宛童
## *Taxillus chinensis (DC.) Danser, the Chinese loranthaceous mistletoe that grows on mulberry and other trees, the modern identification of 桑上寄生; our identification, of medium confidence: Taxillus sutchuenensis is also used, and the Chinese text names only a parasite on mulberry* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** (The European mistletoe, *Viscum album*, is a different plant in a different family and is treated in I_668 from other traditions; it is not this plant.) This entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The identification is ours.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph reads 味苦，平 ("bitter, balanced"). It governs 腰痛 ("pain in the lower back"), 小儿背强 ("stiff back in children"), 痈肿 ("abscesses and swellings"), and it "steadies the fetus" (安胎), "fills the flesh and skin" (充肌肤), "firms the hair and teeth" (坚发齿) and "lengthens beard and eyebrows" (长须眉). A separate clause follows for the fruit: 其实，明目、轻身、通神, "its fruit: brightens the eyes, lightens the body, puts one in communication with the spirit". The paragraph gives the aliases 一名寄屑, 一名寓木, 一名宛童 and the habitat 生川谷, "grows in stream valleys". There is no 久服 ("taken long") clause. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 桑上寄生 *sang shang jisheng*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1271)

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 adds the alias 茑, the habitat 生宏农桑树上 ("grows on mulberry trees in Hongnong") and the harvest: 三月三日，采茎，阴干 ("stems gathered on the third day of the third month, dried in the shade"). The Shennong layer names no part for the first group of indications; the Mingyi layer names the stem. The text does not say that the fruit is gathered at that time.

**Sun's note (案).** Sun cites Shuowen (茑, "a parasite"), the Shijing line 茑与女萝 ("niao and nüluo"), the Guangya (宛童, "a parasite"), the Shanhaijing Zhongshan jing ("on Longshan there is much 寓木"), and Guo Pu glossing 寓木 as a parasite plant and 茑 as its other name. The note breaks off in the transcription at the Mao commentary ("Lu…"), so its end is not on the page.

**Identity.** The text says a parasite on mulberry (桑), not a species. *Taxillus chinensis* is the species most often given for the modern drug 桑寄生, and it grows on a range of host trees, not only mulberry. [USER: verify] Our confidence is medium.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, balanced drug for back pain, abscesses and the care of the fetus, with a further clause for the fruit. The Codex gives no protocol. The use "for the fetus" in particular is the classic's claim, and the Codex does not recommend any plant in pregnancy on the strength of a classic.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The classic files 桑上寄生 in the upper grade (上品); its preface says upper-grade drugs nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic, and may be taken for a long time. There is no 久服 clause as such. The grade rests instead on the clause for the fruit, 其实，明目、轻身、通神 ("its fruit: brightens the eyes, lightens the body, puts one in communication with the spirit"), which promises *qing shen* ("lighten the body", a term with no pharmacological equivalent) and *tong shen* ("communicate with the spirit") to the fruit, together with the upper-grade placement. This is a like life-nourishing status, not the 久服 wording the register usually requires; the Codex records it as (a) on that ground and says so. The Mingyi bielu, not the Shennong text, gives the stem as the part gathered. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 桑上寄生 *sang shang jisheng*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1271)

**Practitioner's note.** The cultivation promise belongs to the fruit, but the later layer names only the stem as the part gathered, and the plant is a parasite whose host may change what it is. No practice follows from the entry. Treat it as a record of the classic's claim.

### Contraindications

- **Identification.** Several mistletoes share the name and the host matters; do not gather or use any on the strength of this entry. Some mistletoes (for example *Viscum album*) are toxic if eaten; the Codex has not read a source on this plant. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy.** The classic claims it "steadies the fetus"; the Codex gives no recommendation and has retrieved no safety information. [USER: verify]
