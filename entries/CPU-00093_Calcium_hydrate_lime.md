# Calcium hydrate / lime — ASBESTOS (5-133), "Calx viva", "Quicklime" (Dioscorides, 2000 English)
## *Quicklime (calcium oxide, CaO) made by burning shell, flint or marble, which becomes calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)2, when quenched in water; the spine's binomial "Calcium hydroxide" is the slaked form, and the Dioscorides chapter describes the quicklime* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter is 5-133 "ASBESTOS" in the English text on disk (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 10525-10532; name index `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, row for 5-133, line 10525). The translator's gloss reads "SUGGESTED: Quicklime — Calx Viva" and "Asbestos refers to `the unquenchable stone' — the action of cold water on quicklime" (lines 10526-10527). The method (line 10528): "Calx viva is made as follows. Take the shells of sea creatures called buccinae [whelks], cover them in the fire or place them into a thoroughly hot oven, and let them remain there all night and the next day. If they have turned very white take them out, but if not, repeat until they are white. Afterwards dip them in cold water and put them into a new pot, corked carefully with cloths. Leave them in there for one night and in the morning take them out, and after it has been refined, put it in jars. It is also made from flints or pebble stones that are burnt, and from common marble, which is also preferred." The properties (lines 10528-10532): "All calx in common is fiery, biting, burning and scab forming. Mixed with certain other things such as grease or oil, it becomes digestive, softening and dissipating, forming new skins. They regard the newest and that which was never wet to be the most effective." The chapter gives no further indication, no dose and no internal use. Two other places in Book 5 and Book 2 refer back to it. In 2-5 KUREKES, on the burnt shells of the *buccina* (whelks; line 4934) Dioscorides writes "Quicklime is made of them as we will show when we come to discuss calx". In 5-142 *Asios lithos* (line 10579) the stone is "used in plasters with bean meal, and for the spleen with vinegar and unslaked lime [quicklime — calx viva]" (the bracket is the translator's). Name-index glosses for 5-114 (*Chalkanthon*, "Calcanthum — Limestone"), 5-115 (*Chalkitis*, "Calcite ... Calcium Carbonate"), 5-152 (*Morochthos lithos*, "Phosphate of Lime — Calcium Oxide"), 5-171 (*Eretrias*) and 5-172 (*Samia*, both "Alkaline Earths; ... Lime — Calcium oxide ...") use the word lime, but those chapters describe a vitriol, a brassy mineral, a soft white stone and two earths, and they are not lime in the sense of this entry. The chapter 5-114 describes "azure coloured [blue]" crystals, so its "Limestone" gloss does not fit the description; it is not used here.

### Active Components

Quicklime is calcium oxide, produced by heating calcium carbonate (shell, limestone, marble). With water it reacts, giving off heat, to form calcium hydroxide (slaked lime), which dissolves slightly to a strongly alkaline solution (limewater). Both are caustic. Marble and shell are calcium carbonate with a small amount of other minerals. The source read gives no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. In the Greek text lime is a caustic and drying material, made by burning, to be handled as such. Dioscorides records no use for lime by mouth. The Codex does not recommend any therapeutic or cultivation use of lime. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation.

### Preparation & Dosing

DMM 5-133 gives the preparation, not a medicine: burn whelk shells (or flints, or marble) overnight and through the next day, repeating until white; dip in cold water; keep in a stoppered new pot overnight; refine and store in jars. No quantity is given. 5-142 uses "unslaked lime" with vinegar for the spleen, with no quantity. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Quicklime and slaked lime are strong alkalis. They burn skin and eyes and the gut when swallowed. Lime is used today in building (mortar and plaster), in agriculture and in water treatment. The old description that lime "burns" the tongue and forms scabs matches its caustic action. No clinical studies were retrieved for traditional medicinal uses. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- DMM: "fiery, biting, burning and scab forming" (5-133, line 10528); mixed with grease or oil it becomes "digestive, softening and dissipating, forming new skins". Dioscorides prints no hot-cold-dry-moist classification for it.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, thinly.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. Lime has a chapter in the materia medica but no cultivation status; the pneuma and the Galenic three spirits are later and post-Dioscoridean. What 5-133 offers a cultivator is the discipline of attention: how the material is made (repeat the burning until the shells are white), how it is kept (a new pot, corked with cloths, then jars), which is best ("the newest and that which was never wet") and what it does to the tongue and skin.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator to take. If the chapter teaches a practice it is the attention to making, keeping and testing a caustic material, and the warning that it burns. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Quicklime reacts violently with water and slaked lime is a strong alkali: both cause severe burns to skin and eyes, damage the gut and airway if swallowed or inhaled as dust, and should not be taken by mouth. Eye contact is an emergency. Lime dust is a respiratory irritant. Burning shell or stone releases dust and heat. The Dioscorides recipes using lime with vinegar or grease are not recommended. Purchase and use of lime are regulated in some places for safety. The translator's glosses with "lime" for other chapters (5-114, 5-115, 5-152, 5-171, 5-172) do not make those substances lime.
