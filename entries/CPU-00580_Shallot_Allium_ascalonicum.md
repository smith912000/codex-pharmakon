# Shallot — Eschalot, Ascalon Onion, Tree Onion (Aggregatum group)

## *Allium ascalonicum* L. (= *A. cepa* Aggregatum group) | Family: Amaryllidaceae (Alliaceae)

**Tradition Tags:** EP (Ebers), AT (Assyrian), DMM (Dioscorides), CM (Avicenna)
**Energetic Classification:** Hot and dry in the third/fourth degree — a strongly heating, penetrating, "opening" bulb in the Galenic-Unani scheme.
**Training Phase:** Foundation

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ebers Papyrus (EP):** Onions and their kin are among the most frequently invoked remedies of Egyptian medicine, and the Ebers Papyrus repeatedly enlists *Allium* bulbs in prescriptions for the chest, digestion, worms, and topical applications. The shallot — a small, clustering, sharp onion of the eastern Mediterranean — belongs to this same heavily-used culinary-medicinal bulb group that ran through Nile-Valley diet and pharmacy alike.

**Assyrian (AT):** Cuneiform administrative and medical texts record onions and garlic as staple foods and remedies; pungent *Allium* bulbs appear in the Mesopotamian pharmacopoeia as warming, "opening," worm-expelling and appetite-provoking agents. The clustering shallot sits within this Assyrian class of hot, sharp kitchen-bulbs pressed into medical service.

**Dioscorides (DMM):** Dioscorides treats the onion (*krommyon*) in *De Materia Medica*, distinguishing long and round, sharp and mild kinds; the Ascalon onion — named for the Levantine city of Ascalon, the root of the word "shallot" — is the small, biting sort. He describes it as sharp, heating, opening the bowels, provoking urine, thinning and moving thick humours, and applied with vinegar or honey to skin and wounds.

**Avicenna (CM):** In the *Qanun*, the Unani tradition classes onion as hot and dry in a high degree — an incisive, attenuating, "opening" food-medicine that cuts thick phlegm, stimulates appetite and digestion, provokes urine, and is applied externally to draw and cleanse. The shallot inherits this profile as a milder, more concentrated culinary member of the group.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** The shallot's signature is *heat that moves and opens*. It clusters — one planted bulb multiplying into a knot of many — and tradition read this multiplying, generative habit alongside its fierce pungency as the mark of a vitalising, quickening agent: a bulb that stirs stagnation, thins what is thick, and warms a cold interior. Modern chemistry grounds the symbolism: the bite is organosulfur chemistry (the same allyl/cysteine-sulfoxide family as garlic and onion), and the yellow-gold skin is quercetin-rich flavonol chemistry. The plant's "opening, moving" reputation maps onto documented antioxidant, antimicrobial and vasoactive organosulfur/flavonoid actions.

### Active Components

- **Organosulfur compounds** — cysteine sulfoxides (alliin-type) and their enzymatically released thiosulfinates and downstream sulfides, generated on crushing; the source of pungency and much of the antimicrobial/cardiovascular activity, shared with garlic and onion.
- **Flavonols — quercetin and glycosides** — HPLC of shallot shows quercetin 3,4'-diglucoside, quercetin 4'-glucoside and free quercetin, a profile nearly identical to onion; carriers of antioxidant and antiallergic activity.
- **Anthocyanins and phenolics** — in the pigmented outer scales, contributing antioxidant capacity.
- **Fructans / prebiotic carbohydrates** — inulin-type fibres typical of *Allium* bulbs.
- **Saponins, essential-oil sulfur volatiles, vitamins and minerals** — rounding out the nutritional-medicinal matrix.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In the Foundation phase, Shallot is a *warming, opening, groundwork* food-medicine — a daily culinary agent that stirs digestion, thins stagnation, and supplies antioxidant and antimicrobial support at nutritional doses. Its evidenced actions cluster around three areas: antioxidant and antimicrobial capacity relevant to general resilience and food-as-medicine practice; cardiometabolic support (adjuvant in cardiovascular and glycaemic contexts, per systematic review); and antiallergic action, with a controlled trial showing added symptom benefit in allergic rhinitis. As a Foundation material it is worked in through the kitchen rather than the dispensary — the base-layer bulb whose job is to keep the terrain warm, moving, and defended.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- Moldovan et al. (2022), *Frontiers in Nutrition* — a PRISMA systematic review (116 studies) concluding that shallot extracts exert potent antioxidant activity in vitro and in vivo and strong inhibitory capacity on various pathogens, and may serve as adjuvant therapy in cardiovascular disease, diabetes, cancer prevention, and other inflammation/oxidative-stress-linked conditions, while calling for more mechanistic work. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2022.903686
- Arpornchayanon et al. (2022), *Asian Pacific Journal of Allergy and Immunology* — a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial: shallot extract at 200 µg/mL gave ~97% β-hexosaminidase inhibition in vitro (vs 73% for onion), shared quercetin compounds by HPLC, and 3 g/day oral shallot added to cetirizine improved overall allergic-rhinitis symptoms and significantly improved ocular symptom scores versus cetirizine alone. DOI: https://doi.org/10.12932/AP-300319-0529

**Evidence grade:** Traditional use is of very high historical confidence. Antioxidant and antimicrobial activity is well supported preclinically; the antiallergic finding rests on a single small RCT (16 patients) and warrants confirmation; cardiovascular/antidiabetic/anticancer roles are "adjuvant" and largely preclinical or associational per the systematic review. Best read as a food-medicine with promising but not definitive clinical evidence.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic / Unani:** Hot and dry (high degree); incisive, attenuating, opening; cuts thick phlegm, provokes urine and appetite, applied externally to draw and cleanse.
- **TCM (by analogy via *Allium*):** the *Allium* bulbs (e.g. cōng, xiè) are warm, acrid, surface-releasing and yang-moving, dispersing cold and moving qi/blood; shallot maps to this acrid-warm dispersing category.
- **Ayurvedic (by analogy):** *rasa* — katu (pungent); *virya* — ushna (heating); *vipaka* — katu (pungent); kapha- and vata-reducing in cold-stagnant states, potentially pitta-aggravating in excess. Onions are classically *rajasic/tamasic* foods, used medicinally with that caveat.

### Contraindications

- **Anticoagulant/antiplatelet interaction:** organosulfur *Allium* compounds can inhibit platelet aggregation; caution with warfarin, aspirin, or other blood-thinners, and around surgery, at high or supplemental intake.
- **GERD, IBS, and FODMAP-sensitive individuals:** shallots are high in fructans and can trigger reflux, bloating and IBS symptoms.
- **Allium allergy / sensitivity:** contact dermatitis and, rarely, oral/respiratory allergy occur.
- **Hypoglycaemia risk:** possible additive glucose-lowering with antidiabetic drugs at therapeutic intakes; monitor.
- **Pregnancy/lactation:** culinary amounts are considered safe; concentrated therapeutic doses are not established — [USER: verify].
- Toxic to dogs and cats — keep *Allium* preparations away from pets.
