# Tuntuka — Tuntuka (Sushruta Samhita)

## *Unresolved: a tree named Tuntuka in the Mahat Panchamula (the major group of five roots) and in the oil chapter of the Sushruta Samhita; the translation prints no Latin name, and a later Sanskrit equation with another tree name is a lead only; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The name is printed *Tuntuka* in Bhishagratna's translation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder with the locus "SS Sutra 38, IVlahat Panchamula group (Vol. I line 15827)"; this entry replaces it. A search of all the chapter files and of the three Bhishagratna volumes found the name in the Sutra 38 group and in one passage of the *Chikitsasthana*. Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 does not list it.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The major five roots (Sutra ch. 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 484 to 492; Vol. I line 15827).* "The Mahat Panchamula Group: — The one known as the great or the major group of five medicinal roots (Mahat-Pancha-Mula) consists of the roots of such trees as Vilva, Agnimantha, Tuntuka, Patala and Kashmari." The metrical text: "It is bitter in taste and subdues the deranged Kapham and Vatam. It is light (easily digestible) and appetising, and acquires a subsequent sweet taste in its reaction (Anurasa)." The text then says that the two five-root groups together form the Dashamula, "possessed of the virtue of destroying the deranged Vata, Pittam and Kapham ... beneficial in cases of asthma and difficult respiration". These are properties of the group of roots; the passage says the roots are "of such trees", which makes Tuntuka a tree and the part used the root in this group.

*The oils (Chikitsasthana ch. 31, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch31.txt` lines 66 to 72).* The chapter lists the oils expressed from many plants and the uses of each. "The expressed oils of Sravana, Kanguka and of Tuntuka are used in imparting a yellow colour (to a cicatrix)." Here the oil of Tuntuka is expressed, so the plant bears a seed or fruit that yields oil. This is the only use of the drug alone.

**What the sources do not give.** No Latin name, no description of the tree, no habitat. In later Sanskrit usage the name is reported to be a synonym of another named tree of the standard five roots, but this was not found in any text opened for this entry, and the translator does not equate them. The idea is a lead only. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a root of the major five roots: bitter, subduing Kapha and Vata, light, appetising (group action, ch. 38); in the Dashamula combination, Vata, Pitta, Kapha, asthma and difficult respiration. As an oil: giving a yellow colour to a scar (ch. 31).
- **What the text does not say.** Everything about the tree itself, and the way the oil is applied.

The Codex gives no protocol. Colouring a scar is a cosmetic procedure and is not recommended.

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Tuntuka alone. The Mahat Panchamula group is "bitter in taste" with a "subsequent sweet taste in its reaction (Anurasa)", light and appetising, subduing Kapha and Vata (Sutra ch. 38, lines 488 to 492).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Tuntuka is named in the Sushruta Samhita as a member of a therapeutic group (*gana*) (the Mahat Panchamula, Sutra ch. 38) and in the oil list of *Chikitsasthana* ch. 31. A *gana* is a grouping by therapeutic action. The group is a classification by action, and the oil list gives a cosmetic use; neither is a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status, and the text read assigns Tuntuka neither. The chapters of the *Chikitsasthana* on *rasayana* and *vajikarana* (ch. 26 to 31 on disk) were searched for the name and it does not occur in them. Nothing links it to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The status is unattested; only a therapeutic one is recorded.

**Practitioner's note.** A tree whose root is in the major five roots and whose oil colours a scar. A cultivator has nothing to use from it until it is identified.
