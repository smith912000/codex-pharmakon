# byi.rug — byi.rug (Clark 1995, Explanatory Tantra ch.20, "Elscholtzia sp.")

## *A herb of the genus Elsholtzia (Lamiaceae, the mint family), which Clark prints "Elscholtzia sp."; the black type is glossed in his bracket as Elscholtzia calycocarpa and Stachys baicalensis. Identification: genus level, from Clark's gloss; medium confidence for the genus, low for the species. **Rig.pa tradition, the Four Tantras (FMT).** Clark's chapter 20 sets out the materia medica in sections. This entry sits in section (7) "Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (`Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 997), and the entry reads at line 1099:

> "byi.rug: Elscholtzia sp.: [is said to be of yellow and black types (black type: Elscholtzia calycocarpa; Stachys baicalensis). The superior type has thin, flexible petioles, an elongated, green leaf, an inflorescence similar to a golden stupa and a pungent smell. The types with black, or yellowish-black ears have round leaves. All three types have hot and astringent tastes and] heal wounds and sores infected by parasites, worms, etc."

In Clark's convention (introduction, line 82) the words in square brackets are taken from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries, and the unbracketed words are the root text. So the root text itself says only that it heals wounds and sores infected by parasites, worms and the like; the types, the botanical description and the tastes come from the commentaries.

The name occurs once in the chapter. A search of Clark for the name found only this entry, a place in the book's index of Tibetan names (line 1722), and the index words "Elscholtzia" and "Elscholtzia calycocarpa" (line 1580). The name is not used in any compound formula in the lines read.

**Notes on the identification.** "Elscholtzia" is a variant spelling of *Elsholtzia*, a genus of aromatic herbs of East Asia. Clark's bracket gives two names for the black type, one in *Elsholtzia* and one in *Stachys*, which shows that the commentary tradition itself, as Clark renders it, does not fix a single species.
