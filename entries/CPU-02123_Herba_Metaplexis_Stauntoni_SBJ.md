# Herba Metaplexis Stauntoni — Nu Qing (Yang 1998); in the classic 女青 *nü qing*, alias 雀瓢 *Que Piao* "Bird Gourd Ladle"

## *A climbing herb whose identification is unsettled in the texts themselves: Yang 1998 prints "Herba Metaplexis Stauntoni" (Metaplexis stauntonii), while Sun Xingyan's commentary, citing Lu Ji, links 雀瓢 to 萝摩, and the Mingyi layer calls 女青 "the root of 蛇衔"; the species is of low confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** This is a lower-class drug, and the lower classic's preface says its drugs are "mostly toxic, not to be taken long". Nothing in the paragraph marks this one as non-toxic. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation.

**Single-text entry: named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The row was filed from Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 English translation, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, "Herbs: Inferior Class, PDF p.101 (line 3750)". The principal text for the Chinese is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; Yang is read as a second witness and is attributed to Yang.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun's entry is 女青 in the lower classic (line 3469; in the lower-class list at line 2937; 卷三 下经 at line 3479). Line 3471 reads 味辛，平。主蛊毒，逐邪恶气，杀鬼温疟，辟不祥。一名雀瓢 ("acrid, balanced; governs gu toxin, drives out evil and malign qi, kills 鬼 and warm malaria, wards off the inauspicious. One name 雀瓢"; a variant 闾 is noted from the *Yulan*). The Wu Pu layer (line 3473) gives the name 霍由祗 and flavour "acrid" for Shennong and Huang Di. The Mingyi layer (line 3475) reads 蛇衔根也。生朱崖，八月采，阴干 ("it is the root of 蛇衔; grows at Zhuya; gathered in the eighth month and dried in the shade"), so the part named there is the root.

Sun's note (line 3477) gathers philology: the *Guang ya* says 女青 is 乌葛; the *Erya* has 芄兰; Lu Ji says its other name is 萝摩, called 雀瓢 in You Province; and the *Bielu* says the white sap of 雀瓢 treats worm and snake poison and is "the sap of the 女青 seedling". The note then records that the *Tang bencao* listed 萝摩 as a separate entry. Sun's own note at line 3233 (under another drug) cites the *Bencao tujing* that 雀瓢 is a name of 萝摩. These notes make the identification uncertain, and this entry does not resolve it.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 3750): "Nu Qing (Herba Metaplexis Stauntoni) is acrid and balanced. It mainly treats gu toxins, expels evil and malign qi, kills ghosts and warm malaria, and keeps off ill matters. Its other name is Que Piao (Bird Gourd Ladle). It grows in mountains and valleys." Yang's footnote 234 (line 3767) is modern commentary: "In olden times, the root (Radix Metaplexis Stauntoni) may have been used instead."

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Metaplexis* belongs to the milkweed subfamily (Apocynaceae, Asclepiadoideae), whose members characteristically carry a white latex, which agrees with the "white sap" of the commentary; some members of the family carry cardiac glycosides. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Treat as potentially toxic.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Gu toxin, evil and malign qi, "killing ghosts" and warm malaria, and warding off "ill matters".
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation beyond the harvest and shade-drying of the root (Mingyi), no duration.

The Codex does not translate these terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest: the root, gathered in the eighth month and dried in the shade (Sun, line 3475). The preface's general rule of method for toxic drugs (Sun line 4125, begin as small as a millet grain and stop when the illness goes) is not a dose for this plant. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Metaplexis* are twining herbs of East Asia with milky sap, in the milkweed group of the dogbane family. The classic's "gu toxin", "ghosts" and "warm malaria" have no modern equivalents and are not translated into any. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic, whose preface (line 2935) reads 下药，一百二十五种，为左使。主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经: the lower drugs are "the left envoys, mainly treating disease to correspond with earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long". The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no promise of lightening the body or of not ageing. Yang also prints it in the inferior class. The register is therefore (b): framed by the lower class, whose own preface is the warning, with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug taken, on the classic's own terms, for a defined complaint and not for long. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use, and the plant cannot be named with confidence. A cultivator should not use it.

### Contraindications

- **Possible toxicity.** The class preface warns that lower drugs are mostly toxic, and milkweed-group plants can be poisonous. Do not ingest or gather. [USER: verify]
