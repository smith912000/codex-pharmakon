# Greater Celandine — Swallow-wort, Tetterwort, Bai-Qu-Cai (白屈菜), Chelidonium

## *Chelidonium majus* L. | Family: Papaveraceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, DMM, CM, SBJ, EF
**Tags (previous line, retained for reference):** AT, DMM, CM, SBJ
**Energetic Classification:** Hot and Dry (third degree) — an acrid, incising, deobstruent herb in the Galenic reckoning
**Training Phase:** Purification (external use only — see Contraindications)

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Assyrian (AT):** The cuneiform materia medica of the Nineveh tablets records numerous acrid, latex-bearing herbs employed as caustics for growths of the skin and clouding of the eye. Greater celandine, native across the Fertile Crescent and the Anatolian uplands, belongs to this class of "burning" plants applied to blemishes rather than swallowed — a distinction the ancient scribes drew carefully between remedies for the surface and remedies for the belly.

**Dioscorides (DMM):** In *De Materia Medica*, Dioscorides names the plant *chelidonion* and preserves the etymological legend that it flowers when the swallows (*chelidones*) return and withers when they depart. He reports that swallows use its orange juice to restore sight to their fledglings — the ancient charter for celandine's long career as an eye remedy. He describes the caustic latex as sharpening vision and cleansing the skin, always as a topical.

**Avicenna (CM):** The *Qanun* places *māmīrān*/celandine among the hot, incising simples that open obstructions of the liver and clear the sight. Avicenna's system classes it as a deobstruent — a remover of stagnation — which in later European hands became the justification for internal "liver-cleansing" preparations that modern pharmacovigilance has shown to be hazardous.

**Shennong tradition (SBJ):** In Chinese medicine the aerial parts, *bai qu cai*, are used to move stagnant qi, relieve pain, and stop cough; the herb is classed as toxic (*you du*) and reserved for short courses. This toxicity note in the oldest strata of the tradition anticipates the modern hepatotoxicity signal.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Every tradition fixes on the same signature — the vivid orange latex that bleeds from a broken stem. To the doctrine of signatures this yellow-orange sap marked the plant as a remedy for the yellow humour (bile) and for jaundice, binding celandine forever to the liver in the symbolic imagination. The pharmacological irony is exact and cautionary: the very organ the plant was believed to cleanse is the organ its isoquinoline alkaloids can injure. The "swallow that restores sight" is a myth of regeneration; the clinical record is one of idiosyncratic damage. Kenetica keeps celandine as a teaching-stone of the Purification phase precisely because it embodies the lesson that a purgative signature is not a guarantee of safety.

### Active Components

Greater celandine is rich in benzylisoquinoline and related alkaloids concentrated in the latex and root: **chelidonine** (the principal, spasmolytic, morphinan-type alkaloid), **sanguinarine** and **chelerythrine** (quaternary benzophenanthridines, cytotoxic and irritant), **berberine**, **coptisine**, **protopine**, and **allocryptopine**. The plant also carries chelidonic acid, phenolic acids, and flavonoids. Sanguinarine and chelerythrine account for much of the caustic action of the fresh latex on skin and mucosa; the alkaloid fraction as a whole is implicated in the idiosyncratic liver injury reported after oral use.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Within Kenetica, celandine is a **strictly external Purification agent**. Its traditional and defensible use is the topical application of fresh latex to warts, corns, and verrucae — a folk practice paralleling the caustic keratolytics of formal dermatology. It is never taken internally in the Kenetica protocol. It is retained in the corpus as a demonstration of a caustic deobstruent and as a case study in how a compelling symbolic signature (orange sap → bile → liver) can license a dangerous internal use that the evidence does not support. The practitioner meets celandine to learn discernment, not to consume it.

### Scientific Correlation

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

- Pantano et al. (2017), *European Review for Medical and Pharmacological Sciences* — a review of the literature concluding that *Chelidonium majus* produces a distinct, idiosyncratic, metabolic-type form of herb-induced liver injury (HILI), and that given the absence of substantial proven benefit the risk-to-benefit ratio of internal use is negative. PMID: 28379595 (no DOI registered in PubMed).
- Teschke et al. (2011), *Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology* — a liver-specific CIOMS causality assessment of 22 spontaneous German case reports found causality for greater celandine ranging from highly probable to possible in the majority, describing a hepatocellular pattern of injury with female predominance, mean latency ~30–36 days. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yrtph.2011.08.008).

**Evidence grade:** The hepatotoxicity signal is clinically documented (case series with structured causality assessment) — this is the strongest, most actionable body of evidence for the plant. Claimed internal benefits (hepatoprotective, antitussive, eye-regenerative) remain traditional/preclinical and are not established. Topical antiviral/keratolytic use for warts rests largely on tradition and small studies.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM (SBJ lineage):** *Bai qu cai* — bitter, acrid, cool, and **toxic**; enters Lung, Stomach; moves qi, relieves pain, stops cough. Reserved for short courses because of its recognised toxicity.
- **Galenic / Unani (CM):** Hot and dry in the third degree; incising, deobstruent, attenuant.
- **Ayurvedic:** Not a classical dravya; by profile it would read as *tikta–katu rasa* (bitter–pungent), *ushna virya* (heating), *katu vipaka* — but this is an inference, not an attested Ayurvedic listing.

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian (AT).** The Nineveh materia medica records a class of acrid, latex-bearing "burning" herbs applied as caustics to skin growths and clouded eyes, kept carefully apart from remedies swallowed for the belly — and celandine belongs to that surface-caustic class. Under the standing blocking warning in force for every Assyrian substance identification and its causal-agency framing, no cultivation status is claimed; the attestation is a topical-therapeutic one, and even that at the level of class. **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides names *chelidonion*, preserves the swallow etymology, and reports the caustic orange latex as sharpening vision and cleansing the skin — a topical throughout. He carries no subtle-body system to assign a cultivation status, and the swallow-restores-sight story is a legend he records rather than a doctrine he asserts (recording is not endorsing). What the tradition offers a cultivator is his discipline of attention — the season the plant flowers and withers, the assay of the fresh latex, the strict separation of a surface caustic from anything taken inward. That is a real practice of provenance and judgement, not a vital-body status. **(c) absent** as to cultivation.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The *Qanun* classes *māmīrān*/celandine among the hot, incising simples that open obstructions of the liver and clear the sight — hot and dry in the third degree, a deobstruent and attenuant. This is a securely named therapeutic entry, but an incising remover of stagnation is curative, not conservational; the strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus, the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture, is a frame of the whole regimen and is not a status the tradition assigns to this caustic deobstruent. No *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed. Attested therapeutically, with no cultivation status sourced. **(b) framed.**

**Chinese — Bencao (SBJ).** *Bai qu cai* (白屈菜) is recorded as bitter, acrid, cool and toxic (*you du*), moving stagnant qi, relieving pain and stopping cough, and explicitly reserved for short courses because of that toxicity. A toxic herb held to a short course is the treat-disease pole of the tradition — the opposite of *yang sheng* 養生, which is conservational, not curative — and no *san pin* 三品 grade is recorded for it in the sources retrieved. Its standing here is therefore therapeutic, not a cultivation status; no *qing shen* 輕身 or life-nourishing claim is supported. **(b) framed.**

**European folk / phytotherapy (EF).** Post-Dioscoridean Europe read celandine through the doctrine of signatures: the vivid orange latex was taken as a sign of the yellow humour, and by that reasoning the plant was bound to the bile, the liver and jaundice. This is the reading systematised in the Renaissance signature-literature — della Porta's *Phytognomonica* (1588) and the wider Paracelsian doctrine that a plant's outward mark discloses its inward virtue — and it licensed the later European "liver-cleansing" tinctures and depurative preparations. The register records this as a doctrine historically held, not as a warrant: the signature reasoning is discredited, and modern pharmacovigilance shows the organ the latex was thought to purify is the organ its alkaloids can injure. European folk practice carries no formal subtle-anatomy or vital-substrate system into which a cultivation grade could be fixed, and the internal "cleansing" use it did generate is the hazardous one this entry proscribes. **(c) absent:** a historically-held symbolic doctrine and folk therapeutic use, no European vital-body and so no cultivation status to assign.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing internal with this plant — that is the whole lesson. Celandine is retained in the Purification phase as a teaching-stone: the vivid orange latex reads, by the doctrine of signatures, as a bile-and-liver cleanser, yet the clinical record is one of idiosyncratic liver injury, and the organ the sap was thought to purify is the organ its alkaloids harm. The only defensible practice is the topical dab of fresh latex on a wart or corn, kept off healthy skin and away from the eyes despite the ancient eye-remedy legend. Nothing about its signature licenses taking it inward, and the tradition's own toxicity note is the warning.

### Contraindications

**⚠️ HAZARD — DO NOT USE INTERNALLY.** Greater celandine is a documented cause of **idiosyncratic herb-induced liver injury**, presenting as acute hepatitis and cholestatic/hepatocellular liver damage, sometimes with jaundice, and reversible on withdrawal but occasionally severe. Onset is unpredictable and not dose-linear (idiosyncratic, metabolic-type), so no "safe" oral dose can be assured. Multiple European regulators have restricted or warned against oral celandine products on this basis.

- **Absolute:** Do not ingest tinctures, teas, capsules, or extracts. Avoid entirely in anyone with existing liver disease, and in pregnancy and lactation.
- **Topical:** The fresh latex is a **skin and eye irritant** and caustic. Despite the ancient eye-remedy legend, **never apply to the eyes** — it can cause serious ocular irritation and injury. Keep off healthy skin; discontinue on rash or burning.
- **Interactions/monitoring:** Anyone who has taken oral celandine and develops fatigue, dark urine, jaundice, or right-upper-quadrant pain should stop immediately and seek liver-function testing.
