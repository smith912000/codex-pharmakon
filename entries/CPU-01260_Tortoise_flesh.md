# Tortoise flesh — *Kurma* (कूर्म), "tortoise" (Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch.46, Bhishagratna's English)
## *The flesh of the tortoise, which Bhishagratna's translation prints as "tortoise" and "Kurma (tortoise)"; no binomial is given by the text or by the translator* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The only tradition attesting this material in the sources retrieved is the *Sushruta Samhita*, in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` and the neighbouring chapter files). The list row (`_sources/sushruta.csv` line 275) reads "Kurma (tortoise)", with the source "Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", and the last field marks the identification as not uncertain. The text lines below are what I rely on.

*Where the tortoise sits in the meat group.* Sutrasthana ch.46 is the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-vidhi*); the file's chapter opening reads "Now we shall discourse on the Chapter which treats of food and drink". Its meat group opens "Now I shall describe the properties of the different species of edible meats" (about line 386). The flesh is arranged by habitat and habit: the *Jangala* and *Anupa* divisions, the former with eight subdivisions. The tortoise is in a later part of the list. At lines 764-766: "The Pa'dina Species:— Animals such as, the tortoise, alligator, crab, black crab, porpoise, etc., belong to this species." The conchiferous group (*Koshastha*) immediately precedes it, at lines 758-762: "Animals such as, the Shankha, Shankhanakha (a species of small bivalve molluscs), Shukti, Shambuka and Bhalluka, etc."

*What the text says of it.* The properties follow for the two groups together (lines 775-785): "The flesh of animals of the Shankha and Kurma orders is sweet in taste and digestion, cooling in its potency, demulcent, and beneficial to stool and the Pittam. It destroys the deranged Vayu and produces Kapham." The next sentences concern the black and white crab and are not the tortoise's. Whether "Kurma order" in Bhishagratna's metrical text is the *Padina* group of line 764, which holds the tortoise, I take to be the case from position, and the translation does not say so explicitly. [USER: verify]

*Ranked best.* Near the end of the same chapter, in verses that say how the properties of cereals, flesh, fruits and pot-herbs are to be judged, Sushruta names the best of each kind and names the tortoise: "The flesh of the Lava, Tittiri, Saranga, Kuranga, Ena, Kapinjala, Mayura (peacock), Varmi and Kurma (tortoise) should be regarded as the best of those of all other animals respectively belonging to their own species" (lines 2161-2165). The tortoise is in the list as the best of its own species.

*Used in a diet.* In the *Uttaratantra* chapter on haemorrhage (*Rakta-pitta*, ch.45), the diet includes the flesh: "Soup of the meat of pigeons, S'anikha (conch) and tortoise as well as the gruels mentioned before mixed with the expressed juice of Dhatri and pomegranate and with a profuse quantity of clarified butter should be given to the patient as diet" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch45.txt` lines 131-136). The chapter's opening, at lines 1-4, names the subject as "Hemorrhage (Rakta-Pitta-Pratishedha)".

*Other parts, not folded in.* The tortoise provides other materials in Bhishagratna's text, each a different material from the flesh. The eggs: in the chapter on *vajikarana* (*Chikitsasthana* ch.26, headed in the translation "increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons (Kshina-Valiyam Vaji-Karana)", lines 4-7), "The eggs of a tortoise, of an alligator, or of a crab, or the semen of a male buffalo, of a he-ass, or of a he-goat should be similarly taken for the same purpose", that is, for aphrodisiac purposes (`SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` lines 133-136). The shell: "The ashes (Masi) of the skins of a peacock, cow, lizard (Godha) and snake and of tortoise shells should be dusted over an Apachi" (`SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt` line 320). The bile: soaked into a powdered collyrium, "in the bile of a tortoise or of a Rohita fish" (`SS_Uttara_ch17.txt` line 98). The tortoise also lends its shape to two skin conditions called *Kachchapika* (`SS_Nidana_ch06.txt` lines 203-212 and `SS_Nidana_ch13.txt` lines 57-60); these are diseases named for a likeness and not uses of the animal. I mention them so that the flesh is not confused with them.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, tortoise flesh is muscle: protein, water and a little fat, with minerals. The text gives qualities (sweet, cooling, demulcent) and not constituents. Nothing in the entry links any constituent to the uses given.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. In the sources the flesh is a food-and-diet article: sweet, cooling and demulcent, and it is a part of a soup for a haemorrhagic condition. The Codex does not recommend taking it. Tortoise and freshwater turtles are widely protected animals, many species are threatened, and wild reptile meat carries well-known infection risks. Haemorrhage is a medical emergency. No protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the sources for the flesh is the soup with pigeon and conch meat, mixed with the expressed juice of *Dhatri* and pomegranate and "a profuse quantity of clarified butter" (Uttara ch.45, lines 131-136), given as diet. The text gives no quantity of the soup and no length of use. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Reptiles, including tortoises and turtles, commonly carry *Salmonella*; the meat of wild or poorly kept animals can carry other bacteria and parasites. Sea turtle meat can also carry toxins in some regions. Bleeding that is heavy or comes from the lungs, stomach or bowel needs urgent medical care. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Printed in Sutrasthana ch.46 (lines 775-779) for the flesh of the *Shankha* and *Kurma* orders together: taste (*rasa*) sweet; post-digestive taste (*vipaka*) sweet ("sweet in taste and digestion"); potency (*virya*) cooling; demulcent; "beneficial to stool and the Pittam"; "destroys the deranged Vayu and produces Kapham". These are Bhishagratna's English renderings. The text states them for the order and not for the tortoise by itself.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status for the flesh.* The frame is the *Annapana-vidhi* chapter (Sutrasthana ch.46), a chapter on food and drink where the flesh is described by its taste, potency and effect on the *doshas*, and is named "best" of its own kind (lines 2161-2165). The flesh is used as a soup in the diet for *rakta-pitta* (Uttara ch.45), a therapeutic use. No passage read places the flesh in a *rasayana* chapter or in a *vajikarana* chapter. The tortoise's eggs are in the *vajikarana* chapter (Chikitsasthana ch.26, line 133), and that status belongs to the eggs and is not widened to the flesh. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for the flesh. The *sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here supports cultivation use of the flesh. It is a food in Sushruta's scheme and a diet article in a bleeding condition, and a cultivator has no claim to take from it. The one cultivation-adjacent record, the eggs in the *vajikarana* chapter, concerns a different material. The honest practice is abstention, which also spares an animal that is widely protected. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Infection and hygiene:** reptile meat carries *Salmonella* and other pathogens; do not eat wild tortoise or turtle meat or use it as medicine.
- **Law and conservation:** tortoises and freshwater and sea turtles are protected in most countries and many species are threatened; taking or trading them is unlawful and unethical.
- **Haemorrhage:** bleeding needs emergency medical care; do not use a diet article as a substitute.
- **Ayurvedic note:** the text itself says the flesh of this group "produces Kapham" (line 779) and is "cooling" in potency (line 777); the passages read draw no further instruction from this.
- **Identification:** the species is not given; the translation says only "tortoise".
