# Delphinium brunonianum — bya.rgod.spos, Jagoepoe (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Delphinium brunonianum Royle, the first of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for bya.rgod.spos (with Delphinium cashmirianum), and the first binomial in the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary's "J" run, set by position against "Jagoepoe"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. Both Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`, and both name the drug. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance", and here the line prints two. The row was added from the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, line 288), whose note reads: "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)". The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1011).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The entry stands in a very long OCR line that holds many entries; it follows *gang.ga.chhung* (*Gentiana urnula*) and precedes *yu.mo.srol.gong.wa*. It reads:

"bya.rgod.spos: Delphinium brunonianum Royle: Delphinium cashmirianum: [has six names. It grows in rocky meadows, has thick leaves and a pale blue, musk scented, furry flower shaped like an owl's head]. It dispels spirits, infectious fever with poisoning and heals insect bites."

Clark's preface (line 82) says: "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". Inside the brackets (the commentary layer) are the six names, the habitat, the thick leaves and the pale blue, musk-scented, furry flower. Outside the brackets (the root-text layer, as far as Clark's brackets mark it) are the Tibetan name, the two Latin names, which are Clark's identifications and not the root text's, and the clause "It dispels spirits, infectious fever with poisoning and heals insect bites". The line gives no taste, no part used and no preparation. Clark's index of Latin names also lists both binomials (line 1578: "Delphinium brunonianum Delphinium cashmirianum").

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text on "Jagoepoe".* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. The name is printed once in the text and once in the index. In chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225), within the run headed "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (line 3428), line 3432 reads: "Gangga chhung cures poison and dysentery. Jagoepoe cures disorders caused by evil spirits, poisoning and relieves contagious hot disorder." The two names stand in the same order as Clark's *gang.ga.chhung* and *bya.rgod.spos*, one after the other, and the actions agree in kind: poison, spirits, and infectious or contagious fever. The index (line 7396) lists "Jagoepoe 207". The text gives no Latin name in the passage.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary.* The Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, the "J" block (the letter at line 5335), prints its columns in separate runs. The phonetic names (lines 5337 to 5340) are "Jagoepoe", "Jakang", "Jeruk", "Ji tsher", "Jhapo Tsi tsi", "Jhew laphuk" and "Jitanga", seven in all. The transliterations (line 5342) are "bya rgod spos bya rkang byi rug byi tsher bya pho rtsi rtsi byi'u la phug byi tang ga", also seven. The scientific names are "Delphinium brunonianum Delphinium himalayi Munz. Mentha longifolia" (line 5344), "Xanthium strumarium" (5345), "Pisum sativum" (5346), "Torularia humilis" (5347) and "Embelia ribes" (5348), seven in all. The English names are "Larkspur Larkspur" (5350), "Horsemint", "Cocklebur", "Garden pea", "Alpencress" and "Marsh nut". Counting in order, "Jagoepoe" is the first phonetic name, "bya rgod spos" the first transliteration, and *Delphinium brunonianum* the first scientific name, against the first "Larkspur". The Codex therefore reads the glossary as pairing "Jagoepoe" with *Delphinium brunonianum*, by column position only. The transliteration "bya rgod spos" is the same as Clark's *bya.rgod.spos*, and the position holds at the fourth name ("Ji tsher", *byi tsher*, against *Xanthium strumarium* and "Cocklebur"), which agrees with Clark's *byi.tsher*. The two Tibetan texts therefore agree on the binomial for this name. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Delphinium brunonianum*, "Jagoepoe" and *bya.rgod.spos* found no entry on this plant. I_1065, I_1066, I_1067, I_560 and I_225 are, by their file names, *Delphinium* entries on other species (*elatum*, *oxysepalum*, *peregrinum*, *consolida*, *staphisagria*); they were not read. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Delphinium* is a genus of the buttercup family (Ranunculaceae) and that larkspurs are generally known to contain toxic diterpenoid alkaloids. Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a musk-scented drug that "dispels spirits, infectious fever with poisoning and heals insect bites" (Clark, line 1011) and that "cures disorders caused by evil spirits, poisoning and relieves contagious hot disorder" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3432). These are the texts' claims and not Codex protocols, and the genus is toxic. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no part used, no quantity, no frequency and no duration. Clark's commentary describes the leaves and flower and does not say which part is taken. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Tibetan terms (spirits, infectious fever with poisoning, contagious hot disorder) are not modern diagnoses or measured outcomes, so no modern finding can be set beside them here. It is a general, well-established fact that larkspurs are poisonous to grazing animals and to people who eat them.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** No taste, power or post-digestive taste is printed for this drug in the lines read. Clark's commentary gives its scent ("musk scented"), which is not a taste. The name does not appear in the Men-Tsee-Khang taste-group lists of chapter 19 that were read for this entry.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica classes this drug by what it does: it dispels spirits and infectious fever with poisoning, and it heals insect bites. The Men-Tsee-Khang text says the same in kind ("disorders caused by evil spirits, poisoning" and "contagious hot disorder"). The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this drug places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the passages cited above were read in full and none mentions one. The mention of spirits is a statement of what the drug is said to dispel and not a practice for the person. Its framing is therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a drug against spirits, poisoning and contagious fever in the Tibetan texts, the genus is toxic, and there is no preparation and no dose. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. *Delphinium* is generally known to be poisonous, the identity is not settled, and no preparation or dose is given, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Poisoning and fever need urgent medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
