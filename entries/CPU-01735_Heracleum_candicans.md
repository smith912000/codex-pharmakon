# Heracleum candicans — Tukar (spru dkar), "Hogweed" (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Heracleum candicans, as the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints it with the English name "Hogweed"; Clark 1995 names "Heracleum (candicans)" only in a class list of anthelmintics, and prints no materia medica entry for it under a Tibetan name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on a glossary line and one class-list line, and prints no action under a Tibetan name.** This entry rests on one tradition's texts, in two modern editions: the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary (the row's locus, line 5995) and Clark 1995 (one line). The Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. No other text read for this entry names this species: a search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Sushruta texts on disk (including the three Bhishagratna volumes) for the genus name *Heracleum* found only Dioscorides chapters on other species (the translator's glosses *Heracleum panaces*, *Heracleum sphondylium* and *Heracleum gummiferum*, for example at lines 7022, 7338 and 8613), which are not attributed to this row. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (row 387) the entry is recorded at the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary line 5995 with the note "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)". The row has one name only, so there is no first-or-later question for the key.

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (lines 5858 to 5998).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, the "T" block (page 314 printed at line 6000). The glossary prints its columns in separate runs. Twenty-four phonetic names are printed at lines 5860 to 5924, from "Tab seng" to "Tukar", with twenty-four transliterations at lines 5862 to 5926, the last being "spru dkar" (line 5926). The scientific names at lines 5928 to 5995 are twenty-four in all, ending "Meconopsis horridula Capsicum annum Rubia manijith" (line 5994) and "Heracleum candicans" (line 5995), and the English column ends "Blue poppy Red pepper Indian Madder" (line 5997) and "Hogweed" (line 5998). Counted in order, the last phonetic name, "Tukar", stands against the last scientific name, *Heracleum candicans*, and against "Hogweed". The count is consistent at the points where Clark's materia medica allows a check: "Tarbu" (seventh) against *Hippophae tibetana* and "Seabuckthom", "Thalka dorje" (eighth) against *Cassia tora*, "Tharam" (tenth) against *Plantago depressa*, "Tsher ngon" (twenty-first) against *Meconopsis horridula* and "Blue poppy" (Clark, *tsher.sngon*, line 1027), and "Tsoe" (twenty-third) against *Rubia manijith* and "Indian Madder" (Clark, *btsod*, line 1091, which prints *Rubia cordifolia* and *Rubia tinctorium*). The glossary does not print the pairing in one row, so the pairing of "Tukar" with this binomial is by position. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text.* A search of the Men-Tsee-Khang text for "Tukar", "Heracleum" and "hogweed" found only the glossary lines above (5924, 5995 and 5998). The index does not list the name. No action, taste or power is therefore printed for "Tukar" in the lines read. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, class list (line 1221).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, chapter 21, "The Principles of Medicines: Compounding of Medicines" (heading at line 1205). Line 1221 reads: "The category of anthelmintics contains Musk, Asafoetida, Garlic, Bengal Kino tree, Datura, Henbane, Embelia, seed of Iris ensata, Snail shell, Artemesia(nestita) ash, Daphne tangutica (tibetanum), Arisaema, Zanthoxylum and Heracleum (candicans)." The list mixes English names and Latin names, and gives no Tibetan name. The binomial also appears in Clark's index of Latin names (line 1595). Clark has no materia medica entry in chapter 20 that carries *Heracleum candicans* among its identifications. The Codex therefore cannot join the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary name "Tukar" to Clark's anthelmintic list by any shared Tibetan name; the only link is the binomial. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder found no entry on *Heracleum candicans*. I_1108 (*Iris ensata*), I_973 (*Zanthoxylum armatum*) and I_1233 (*Arisaema intermedium*) quote Clark's line 1221, in which this plant is the last item, for their own subjects. I_1131 (*Meconopsis horridula*) quotes the same glossary lines 5988 to 5995 and the English names at line 5997, because *Heracleum candicans* ends that run. I_981 (*Heracleum panaces*), I_1100 (*Heracleum gummiferum*) and I_1101 (*Heracleum sphondylium*) are entries on other species of the genus from other traditions. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Heracleum* is a genus of the carrot family (Apiaceae) and that members of the family commonly contain coumarins, including furanocoumarins. Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained or which part was used.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The one use the sources read give is Clark's placing of "Heracleum (candicans)" in the category of anthelmintics (line 1221). The Men-Tsee-Khang glossary prints a name and a scientific name and no use. These are the texts' claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The only frame the sources read give is therapeutic: Clark's chapter 21 lists *Heracleum candicans* in the category of anthelmintics. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this plant places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the lines cited above were read in full and none mentions one. The frame attaches to the binomial in Clark's list and not to a named Tibetan drug, so it is thin.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a name in a glossary and in a list of anthelmintics, with no preparation and no dose. The abstentive answer is the honest one.
