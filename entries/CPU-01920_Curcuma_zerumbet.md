# Curcuma zerumbet — Sathi (S'athi), Sushruta Samhita Appendix 3

## *Curcuma zerumbet Roxb. (shampoo ginger), a rhizomatous herb of the ginger family, as the translator's Latin gloss to the Sanskrit name Sathi; the Latin is Bhishagratna's, the pairing with the Sanskrit name is ours from the two OCR columns, and the identification is of medium confidence at the level of the Sanskrit name (the Latin gloss itself is unambiguous)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named in the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) only through the translator's Appendix No. 3, "Names of drugs with Latin synonyms".** This is a single-text entry. The appendix is printed in two columns, Sanskrit names on the left and Bhishagratna's Latin or English glosses on the right, and the e-text `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` splits the two columns into separate runs of lines. In that file the Sanskrit name stands at line 32798 as "Sathi" (the OCR garbles diacritics) and the Latin gloss "Curcuma  Zerumbet." at line 32871. The extraction file `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` lists the Latin line as unpaired ("OCR column split"). The pairing here is ours, made by counting entries in the two columns from points where the pairs are unambiguous: Sarala to Pinus Longifolia, Sarpagandha to Ophioxylon Serpentinum, Satavari to Asparagus Racemosus, Satapushpi to "See Satahva" and Satina to Pisum Sativum, all of which are standard identifications, so that Sathi falls on "Curcuma Zerumbet.". The count held at every checked anchor, but it remains a reconstruction from OCR and must be checked against the page scan. [USER: verify] 

**The name in the main text.** The same Sanskrit name, printed S'athi or Sathi, occurs in the Samhita itself, and the lines below were opened and read. In the Sutrasthana chapter on the properties of foods (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 1741) the potherb group says that "the species called the Rajakshavak-Shakam, as well as the one called Sathi-Shakam, is astringent in its property, cooling in its potency, easily digestible and is not hostile to (does not enrage or aggravate) the humours". Whether the Sathi-Shakam of the vegetable chapter is the plant glossed Curcuma zerumbet is not stated in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

In the Chikitsasthana the name is an ingredient in several compounds. In the ghee called Dhanvantara-Ghrita (`SS_Chikitsa_ch12.txt` line 64) ten pala of each of a long list of drugs, among them "S'athi, Snuhi, Varunaka, Pushkara, Danti and Haritaki", are boiled with barley, kola and kulattha in a Drona of water and the decoction is cooked with ghee; the lines read do not give its indication. [USER: verify] In an oil of the chapter on Vayu disorders (`SS_Chikitsa_ch37.txt` line 74) "Manjishtha, S'athi, Pushkara and Taskara (Choraka)" are pasted with other drugs and cooked in milk and oil, which the text calls "highly efficacious in the disorders of the deranged Vayu". S'athi also stands in a pasted compound (the "Tenth Sneha", line 209), in a decoction at `SS_Chikitsa_ch38.txt` line 329 where it is printed in brackets after the name "Nis'a-chchhada", in the pill Hingvadi-Vati of `SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt` line 682 ("One (such) pill should be taken every morning in all diseases of the deranged Vayu"), and in a fever decoction with Bhu-nimba, Guduchi, Draksha and Amalaki mixed with treacle (`SS_Uttara_ch39.txt` line 1134). In every one of these S'athi is one drug among many; none of the lines read treats it alone.

**No other text.** A search of the other source texts on disk for *Curcuma zerumbet* and its English and Sanskrit names found nothing.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Nothing is said of the plant alone. It is one drug among many in Vayu-disorder compounds and in a fever decoction, and the vegetable chapter calls the Sathi potherb astringent and cooling in potency.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose for the single drug, and no statement that the glossed species is the plant meant.

The Codex gives no protocol, makes no claim for this plant and does not recommend it.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Curcuma* species are rhizomatous herbs of the ginger family, and several are used as spice or dye in South and South-East Asia. The Sanskrit name Sathi has had more than one botanical identification in later literature, so the Latin gloss should not be taken as settled. ### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is used in the Samhita as one ingredient in therapeutic compounds for the deranged Vayu and for fever, and as a cooling potherb. No chapter of the Chikitsa on rasayana or vajikarana (chapters 26 to 31 of the Chikitsasthana on disk) contains this name in a search of `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/`, so no cultivation status is attested for it. The register is therefore (b): framed by the therapeutic formulas, with no *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status attested for it.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to cultivate here. The record is of a plant used as one ingredient in compounds for Vayu disorders and fever, with no cultivation status in the sources read. A cultivator has no sourced use for it, and the identification itself is open.
