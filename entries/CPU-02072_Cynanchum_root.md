# Cynanchum root — 白薇 (Bai Wei); also Bai Mu, Wei Cao, Chun Cao, Gu Mei

## **Cynanchum atratum* Bunge (Apocynaceae/Asclepiadaceae), the root; the usual modern identification of 白薇 *bai wei* (medium confidence at species level; modern Chinese practice also admits *C. versicolor*). The text gives the name, habitat and harvest but no plant description* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Bai wei* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The placeholder row it replaces was named "Cynanchum sp." and carried two loci in Yang: this drug (Yang line 2904) and *Bai Tu Huo*, "Herba Cynanchi Caudati" (line 2206, upper class), a different drug; only *bai wei* is covered here.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 白薇 in the middle classic (卷二 中经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2175, 味 paragraph; name line 2173). The Shennong text proper: "bitter, balanced" (味苦，平). It "mainly treats" "sudden wind stroke, body heat and full limbs, a dazed state of not knowing people, mania and confusion, evil qi, cold and heat with aching [one glyph is lost in the e-text], warm malaria with shivering that comes at set times" (主暴中风，身热肢满，忽忽不知人，狂惑，邪气，寒热酸[ ]，温疟洗洗，发作有时). "It grows in rivers and valleys" (生川谷). No 久服 clause, no alias in the text proper, and no toxicity statement.

*Wu Pu layer.* None for this entry.

*Mingyi bielu layer* (line 2177). Aliases 白幕, 薇草, 春草, 骨美. Habitat: plains. The root is gathered on the third day of the third month and dried in the shade.

*Sun's note.* None.

*Drug-relations table* (Sun's edition): 白薇 "dislikes (恶) 黄[one glyph lost], rhubarb, 大戟, dried ginger, dried lacquer, jujube, and 山茱萸" (line 4397); it is an envoy (使) of 贝母 (line 4373); two other entries name it: 天鼠屎 "dislikes" 白蔹 and bai wei (line 4573), and hemp (麻[one glyph lost]) and hemp seed "fear oyster shell and bai wei" (line 4645). 大戟 is itself a toxic lower-class drug (see its own entry).

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 2904) prints "Bai Wei (Radix Cynanchi Baiwei) is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats sudden wind stroke, generalized fever, limb fullness [i.e., distention], sudden inability to recognize people, manic and confusing evil qi, cold and heat, aching pain, and distressing warm malaria which attacks at regular intervals." The lost glyph in Sun's text is thus read as "aching pain." Yang's footnote 173 (line 2927) is his own later commentary (the drug "boosts the yin qi and precipitates water qi," treats "wind phlegm congestion causing generalized fever"); it is not Shennong text.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

This is a historical record of one text, not a recommended practice.

- **What the text says it is for.** Sudden wind stroke with fever, loss of recognition, mania, and malaria with set recurrences.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no 久服 promise, and no toxicity statement.

The indications are emergencies (sudden unrecognising states, mania, malaria) that need medical care today. No Kenetica protocol is derived from this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Mingyi layer gives the harvest only: the root, on the third day of the third month, dried in the shade (line 2177). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

*Cynanchum atratum* root (*baiwei*) is a standard Chinese crude drug. Whether it affects any condition named in the Shennong text has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Sun 1799).** Bitter, balanced (味苦，平; line 2175). No toxicity statement.
- **SBJ (Yang 1998).** Bitter and balanced (line 2904).
- No organ or channel assignment is given in the classic; Yang's footnote 173 supplies later vocabulary not attributed to the classic.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle classic (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1677: "ministers, mainly nourishing the nature to correspond with the human; toxic or nontoxic, to be judged as fitting," for "one who wishes to check disease and replenish the weak"). The entry has no 久服 clause; it lists acute complaints only. No cultivation status beyond the middle grade is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade drug listed only for sudden, acute conditions. A cultivator has nothing to do with it except read the record.

### Contraindications

- **Species.** The classic does not fix the species. [USER: verify]
- **Dose and form unknown.** The text gives none.
- **Combinations.** The classic's table lists drugs it "dislikes", including 大戟, a toxic drug that is not to be used.
- **Pregnancy and lactation.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Acute conditions.** Stroke, mania and fever with loss of recognition are emergencies.
