# Black alunite (vitriol) — nag.mtsur (Tibetan) / Nag tshur (Men-Tsee-Khang spelling)
## *A greenish-yellow mineral glossed "Black Alunite: Vitriol" by Clark (1995); its yellow type is ser.mtsur, glossed "fibroferrite"* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan Tradition (FMT).** The locus is Clark's 1995 translation of the Explanatory Tantra materia medica (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 867; name-index row `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` line 35). The entry reads: "nag.mtsur: Black Alunite: Vitriol: [has six names and is greenish-yellow. The bubbles of this mineral when boiled will turn copper red on contact, and, if sprinkled in a decoction of chebulic myrobalan, it will turn it black. [ser.mtsur (fibroferrite), the yellow type,] is used for dyeing wool and a solution of chebulic myrobalan will turn yellow when it is added. This and black alunite (nag.mtsur) halt necrosis, extract tumors [and are also beneficial for falling hair]."

Three things follow from the text as printed. (1) The action in the main sentence is that the two minerals, the yellow *ser.mtsur* and the black *nag.mtsur*, "halt necrosis, extract tumors"; the benefit for falling hair is in Clark's square brackets, which mark commentary he supplies. (2) The bracketing in the printed entry is unbalanced (an opening bracket is never closed inside the sentence), so the line between root text and commentary cannot be fixed with confidence from this file. (3) The identification tests (turning copper red on contact; blackening a myrobalan decoction) are a discipline of testing by reaction, which is how the entry distinguishes the black type from the yellow.

The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text gives the same pair a shorter treatment: "Nag tshur and Ser tshur heals gangrene and dissolves tumours" (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3373; index "Nag tshur 200", line 7449).

**Neighbouring entries.** The next entry, *big.pan* (chalcanthite, Skt. *Nilathota*), "has similar qualities to mTsur and is blue ... All types remove small abscesses, disperse tumors, cure conjunctivitis [and alleviate wind fevers]" (Clark line 868). Two salts are described by comparison with an alunite: lake salt (*rgya.tsha*) "has a hot taste similar to mtsbur (alunite)" (line 927) and white rock salt (*mdze.tsha*) "resembles clear, dried bubbles of mtshur (alunite) on rocks" (line 933). These help locate the mineral (it forms bubble-like crusts and has a hot taste); they are other drugs and are not transferred. The text also has a plain *mtsur* (alunite) entry in the index of Tibetan terms (line 1734), which was not found as a main entry in the lines read. [USER: verify]

**Position in the text.** The entry stands in Clark's section "(3) The Exposition of the Power of Earth Medicines" (heading at line 857), after the stone medicines (heading at line 816).

**Identity.** "Black alunite" and "vitriol" are the translator's glosses. Alunite is a hydrated potassium aluminium sulphate and vitriols are metal sulphates, so the two glosses name different minerals. The text does not reconcile them, and the identity is unsettled; as general chemistry, blackening with a tannin-rich decoction is typical of an iron salt, but the text does not say so and no identification is made here. ### Active Components

Stated generally. Alunite is a hydrated potassium aluminium sulphate; alums are astringent because the aluminium ion precipitates protein. Vitriols are hydrated metal sulphates (iron(II) sulphate is green, copper sulphate blue); an iron salt reacts with the tannins in a myrobalan decoction to give a black iron-tannate, which is the general chemistry behind the colour change the entry reports. Both kinds of compound are astringent and, in quantity, irritant and toxic. No figures were read in the sources retrieved.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved give *nag.mtsur* as a mineral that "halts necrosis" and "extracts tumors" (and, in commentary, helps falling hair) and as a dye-related mineral. They give no preparation, route or dose. Metal sulphates and alums are irritant to the gut and the skin, and the mineral's identity is unsettled, so the Codex proposes no use.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives tests, not preparations: boiling to see bubbles turn copper red; sprinkling into a myrobalan decoction to see it turn black (black type) or yellow (yellow type). No preparation for medicinal use is given in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Tibetan frame is that the three *nyes pa* (*rlung*, *mkhris pa*, *bad kan*) arise from the three poisons, and that medicine is a branch of treatment that supports practice and does not reach the root. Within it, *nag.mtsur* is a materia-medica mineral chosen by its action against necrosis and tumors (Clark line 867; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3373). No *bcud len* (essence extraction) use of alunite or vitriol was found in the lines read. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition, and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator to take. The sources describe a mineral by test and by an external-type therapeutic action, with no dose, and the material is an irritant. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity:** metal sulphates (iron, copper) and aluminium sulphates are irritant and, in quantity, toxic when swallowed; they should not be taken internally. Vitriol in particular is a corrosive historical name.
- **Skin and eyes:** astringent mineral salts can burn or irritate; the text's mention of conjunctivitis (for chalcanthite) is not an endorsement.
- **Identity:** the alunite/vitriol identity is unsettled, so the material cannot be named safely.
- **Dye mineral:** the text notes dyeing wool with the yellow type; such material is not clean for the body.
