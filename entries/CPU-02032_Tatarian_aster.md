# Tatarian aster — 紫菀 *zi wan*, "Purple Aster" (also 紫茜 *zi qian*, 青苑 *qing yuan*)

## *Aster tataricus* L.f., Tatarian aster, the purple-flowered daisy-family perennial whose root is the drug; the identification is the modern standard one for 紫菀 and is of high confidence, though the Shennong text itself is cut in the middle of its indications* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** A search of the other traditions' texts in `Sources/` for *Aster tataricus* found nothing; the Codex's other *Aster* entries (for example I_1021, I_1022, I_1238, I_1239) rest on other traditions and other species. The entry rests on one tradition, in Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction and Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 translation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified genus-level placeholder, *Aster* sp., from Yang's lines on 紫菀 and on 女菀; this entry replaces it for 紫菀 only.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), the Shennong text proper.** The entry sits in the middle classic (中经). **The paragraph is cut in Sun's file.** It reads: "味苦，温。主咳逆上气，胸中《吴普》曰：紫菀，一名青苑" ("Bitter, warm. It governs cough with counterflow and rising qi, [in the] chest…" and then the Wu Pu quotation begins in the same line, so the rest of the Shennong sentence is missing). (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 紫菀 *ziwan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2087)

**Second witness, Yang 1998 (attributed to Yang), which completes the paragraph.** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, lines 2874-2877) reads: "Zi Wan is bitter and warm. It mainly treats cough and counterflow qi ascent and cold and heat bound qi in the chest. It eliminates gu toxins and crippling wilt and quiets the five viscera. It grows in mountains and valleys." The first half matches Sun's surviving words; "eliminates gu toxins and crippling wilt, quiets the five viscera" and the habitat clause rest on Yang alone, because Sun's line is cut. Yang's translation does not mark whether those words came from a Chinese source or from his own reading. The Codex treats them as Yang's witness to a reconstruction that is not reproduced in the Sun file. [USER: verify]

**Wu Pu layer (3rd century, quoted by Sun).** The line preserves only an alias: "一名青苑" (Green Aster), the same alias the Mingyi layer repeats.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later additions, quoted by Sun).** Line 2089 reads: "一名紫茜，一名青苑。生房陵及真定邯郸。二月、三月采根，阴干" ("Another name: Purple Madder [紫茜]; another name: Green Aster. It grows at Fangling and at Zhending and Handan. The root is gathered in the second and third months and dried in the shade"). The part used is the root.

**Sun's own note (案).** Joined without a break to the Mingyi line: "《说文》云：菀，茈菀，出汉中房陵。陶弘景云：白者，名白菀。《唐本》注云：白菀，即女菀也。" ("The *Shuowen* says: *wan*, *zi wan* [茈菀], comes out of Fangling in Hanzhong. Tao Hongjing says: the white one is called *bai wan*. The Tang bencao note says: *bai wan* is *nü wan* [女菀]"). So two kinds are on record in the commentary: the purple (this entry) and a white aster identified with 女菀, which has its own Shennong entry in Sun's file (line 2293). That entry is a different drug and is not covered here; Yang's lines 2993-2998 (女菀 "acrid and warm", for "continual wind cold, sudden turmoil", diarrhea and others) are its witness. Yang's lines on 女菀 were part of the genus-level placeholder and need a separate entry. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The Chinese name, the purple colour of the root or flower implied by the name, and the habitat in the central and northern provinces agree with the modern assignment, *Aster tataricus*. The early text gives no botanical description beyond colour, so it cannot separate the species from other asters; confidence: high for the modern identification of the drug, moderate for the early text.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. As a general fact, *Aster tataricus* belongs to the daisy family (Asteraceae), and its root contains triterpenoid compounds, including shionone, characteristic of the species. This is a general statement and not drawn from the Codex's sources. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Shennong text, as far as it survives in Sun's file, lists a bitter, warm drug for cough with counterflow and rising qi; Yang adds "cold and heat bound qi in the chest", gu toxins, crippling wilt and quieting the five viscera. These are the text's terms; the Codex does not translate "counterflow qi" or "crippling wilt" into modern diagnoses and gives no protocol. Yang's note 110 (line 2890) calls it a "miraculous medicinal for vacuity heat and vacuity taxation", which is commentary of a later period. A persistent or blood-streaked cough needs medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text proper gives no preparation in the surviving lines. The Mingyi layer says the root is gathered in the second and third months and dried in the shade (line 2089). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

*Aster tataricus* root is a common cough remedy in current Chinese practice, which is a general fact and not a finding. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ.** The Shennong text prints "味苦，温" (bitter, warm). The Wu Pu and Mingyi layers add no flavour or temperature. No toxicity word is printed in the surviving text. Sun's appendix of drug relations (附：诸药制使, from line 4191) lists "紫菀 款冬为使；恶天雄、瞿麦、雷丸、远志；畏茵陈" (line 4393; "款冬 as its envoy; averse to 天雄, 瞿麦, 雷丸 and 远志; fears 茵陈"); the appendix is a later compilation (Sun cites Tang Shenwei's remark on it at line 4193) and is not the Shennong text proper.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle classic (中经), whose preface (line 1677) says middle drugs "nourish the nature to correspond with man; non-toxic or toxic, weigh what is fit; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the thin [羸], take the middle classic". No 久服 clause survives in the entry, and the paragraph is cut, so the Codex cannot say whether one was lost. Yang's rendering has none. The class is a frame; the drug carries no cultivation status of its own in the lines read. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 紫菀 *ziwan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2087)

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade cough drug, in the text's own frame. Nothing in the entry supports a long-term practice, and the surviving Chinese text is cut, so the Codex does not read more into it than Yang's translation gives. If anything is done, it is to read the entry for its indications and leave the root where it grows.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. Cough with counterflow can be a sign of serious illness and needs medical assessment. Roots sold as 紫菀 may be other asters or related plants; the Tang note's confusion of purple and white asters (above) is an example. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
