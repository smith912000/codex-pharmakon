# Job's Tears — Yi Yi Ren, Adlay, Coix Seed, Chinese Pearl Barley

## *Coix lacryma-jobi* L. | Family: Poaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SBJ, SS, FMT

**Energetic Classification:** Cool (or neutral-cool) and dry; sweet and bland — draining-damp in the Chinese reckoning, cooling-drying in the humoral

**Training Phase:** Foundation

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Coix lacryma-jobi* is a tall tropical grass native to South and East Asia, cultivated for millennia for its hard, tear-shaped grain — the "tears of Job." The wild form bears stony, bead-like husks (long strung as rosaries and jewellery), while the cultivated *ma-yuen* variety yields the soft, edible caryopsis used as food and medicine. Because it is simultaneously a staple grain and a drug, Job's tears occupies an unusually stable place across the great Asian medical traditions: a nourishing food that also drains, a gentle everyday remedy rather than a heroic one.

**Shennong (TCM):** In the Chinese canon, *yi yi ren* (薏苡仁) is one of the oldest documented grain-medicines, ranked among the mild, food-grade herbs suitable for prolonged use. The classical materia medica assigns it to the Spleen, Stomach, and Lung channels with functions of *draining dampness*, *strengthening the spleen*, *clearing heat*, *expelling pus*, and *relaxing sinews*. It is the archetypal "resolve damp without depleting" agent — used for oedema, damp painful obstruction (*bi*), diarrhoea from spleen weakness, and lung/intestinal abscess.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS):** The Ayurvedic tradition knew Coix (*gavedhuka*) as a cereal of cooling, drying, kapha-reducing character, taken as a gruel to lighten a damp, heavy, congested constitution. Its use as a nourishing yet drying grain parallels the Chinese reading closely — a food that reduces sluggish accumulation.

**Avicenna / Unani (CM):** Within the Galenic-Unani orbit, hard cereal grains of this cooling-drying type were used as demulcent yet desiccating gruels for the stomach and chest, thinning and moving thick phlegmatic matter while nourishing convalescents. The doctrine again converges: sustenance that dries damp.

**rGyud-bzhi / Tibetan (FMT):** The Tibetan *Four Tantras* pharmacy incorporated Coix-type grains among cooling, damp-clearing cereals used for joint and channel complaints and to settle disordered *bad-kan* (the phlegm/earth-water humour).

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** The bead-like grain — worn as rosaries, associated with tears and mourning — carries a symbolism of *release and drainage*. The pharmacology mirrors it: Coix is genuinely diuretic and anti-inflammatory, literally draining fluid and cooling swollen, damp, painful tissue. The "tears" it sheds are the water-damp the body no longer needs.

### Active Components

- **Coixenolide** — a characteristic glyceride ester long associated with the seed oil and its antitumour reputation; the principal active in the injectable derivative.
- **Coixol (6-methoxybenzoxazolinone)** — a benzoxazolinone with anti-inflammatory, antipyretic, and smooth-muscle effects; concentrates strongly in young adlay sprouts.
- **Coix seed oil (triglyceride fraction)** — the emulsified basis of the Kanglaite pharmaceutical.
- **Polysaccharides** — glucans and fructo-glucans with demonstrated analgesic and anti-inflammatory activity.
- **Lactams, sterols, and phenolics** — contributing antioxidant capacity.

**Kanglaite note:** *Kanglaite* (KLT) is a standardised, emulsified Coix seed oil preparation given intravenously (and orally) as an adjunctive cancer therapy, developed and licensed in China. It is the best-studied modern derivative of this grain and the reason Job's tears bridges folk food and formal oncology support.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Job's tears anchors the **Foundation** phase as a nourishing damp-drainer — a food-grade agent that lightens a heavy, waterlogged, congested system without depleting the practitioner's reserves. It is indicated for fluid retention, a damp-heavy digestion, sluggish and swollen joints, and skin that is puffy or slow to clear. Because it is simultaneously food and medicine, it is ideal early-phase groundwork: it drains and cools while it feeds, preparing a clean, dry substrate for later purification and tempering. In Kenetica terms it *removes standing water so the deeper work has dry ground to stand on*.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, Coix is among the better-evidenced grain-medicines. Sui & Xu (2022), *Natural Product Research*, isolated two polysaccharides from Coix seed and demonstrated **analgesic and anti-inflammatory** effects in mice — reducing writhing, raising pain threshold, and suppressing LPS-induced inflammation in RAW264.7 macrophages — a direct correlate of the traditional damp-painful-obstruction use. DOI: [10.1080/14786419.2022.2162896](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2022.2162896). Lee et al. (2023), *Plants*, quantified coixol and coixenolide in adlay sprouts and confirmed antioxidant (DPPH/ABTS) and COX-2-inhibiting anti-inflammatory activity that varied with harvest time. DOI: [10.3390/plants12162975](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants12162975).

On the Kanglaite derivative: Zhan et al. (2012), *Asian Pacific Journal of Cancer Prevention*, reported a 60-patient controlled study in advanced gastric cancer where Kanglaite injection added to chemotherapy improved response rate and quality-of-life (KPS) scores and reduced gastrointestinal and bone-marrow toxicity versus chemotherapy alone. DOI: [10.7314/apjcp.2012.13.10.5319](https://doi.org/10.7314/apjcp.2012.13.10.5319). An evidence-mapping overview by Lu et al. (2022), *Frontiers in Pharmacology*, examined 13 meta-analyses and found KLT as an adjunct appeared to improve efficacy and reduce adverse reactions in non-small-cell lung cancer, malignant pleural effusion, and digestive malignancies — but graded the **methodological quality of the underlying reviews as low to critically low**. DOI: [10.3389/fphar.2022.901875](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2022.901875).

**Evidence grade: Moderate (adjunctive) / preclinical for the whole grain.** Anti-inflammatory and analgesic mechanisms are real and reproducible. Kanglaite has genuine clinical trial data as a *supportive* oncology adjunct, but the evidence base is methodologically weak; it is not a standalone cancer cure and any such claim would be unsupported.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM (as attested):** Sweet, bland (*gan, dan*); cool/slightly cold; Spleen, Stomach, Lung channels. Drains dampness, strengthens spleen, clears heat, expels pus, relaxes sinews.
- **Ayurvedic (as attested, *gavedhuka*):** *rasa* sweet/astringent (madhura/kashaya); *virya* cooling (shita); *vipaka* pungent (katu); kapha-reducing, mildly grounding.
- **Galenic / Unani (as attested):** Cool and dry cereal; demulcent-yet-desiccating; thins and moves phlegmatic matter.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** Coix enters the Galenic-Unani orbit as a cooling-drying cereal — a demulcent yet desiccating gruel that thins and moves thick phlegmatic matter while nourishing convalescents. The attestation sits at the level of the cooling-drying grain type rather than a named monograph, and the Unani cultivation idea — conservation of innate heat and radical moisture across a life — is a frame of the whole regimen, not a status assigned to this grain. Its standing here is dietetic and therapeutic. **(b) framed:** used as a cooling-drying sustenance, no cultivation status recorded.

**Chinese — Shennong (SBJ).** In the *Shennong Bencao Jing*, the Han-era classic that is the root of the Chinese materia medica, *yi yi ren* 薏苡仁 is placed in the upper grade (上品) — the *shang pin* rank of substances taken long-term to nourish life rather than to attack disease — carrying the classic's characteristic upper-grade formula that prolonged use "lightens the body" (輕身, *qing shen*) and extends the years (the upper-class listing is rendered in Yang Shou-zhong's translation *The Divine Farmer's Materia Medica*; the grading is the classic's own). This is a genuine cultivation status: within *yang sheng* 養生 — conservational nourishing of life, not cure — the grain is a food-grade drug fit for prolonged, un-timed use. **(a) attested** — upper grade in the *Shennong Bencao Jing*.

**Ayurveda (SS).** Known as *gavedhuka*, Coix is a cereal of cooling, drying, *kapha*-reducing character, taken as a gruel to lighten a damp, heavy, congested constitution. No *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) assignment is recorded for it in the sources retrieved, so its standing is dietetic — a nourishing yet reducing grain — not a formal rejuvenative cultivation status. **(b) framed.**

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The *Four Tantras* pharmacy places Coix-type grains among cooling, damp-clearing cereals used for joint and channel complaints and to settle disordered *bad-kan*. Tibetan medicine is the third of the four treatment branches — it supports practice rather than reaching the root — and no formal category of "substances for subtle-body practice" exists to house this grain, nor is any *bcud len* (essence-extraction) status attested for it. Its role is therapeutic. **(b) framed:** used in the pharmacy, no cultivation status assigned.

**Practitioner's note.** Job's tears is the rare grain that is genuinely a cultivation substance in one tradition and plain medicine in the others. In the Chinese scheme it is an upper-grade food-drug — eaten as congee or decoction over long stretches to drain standing damp while it feeds, fit for un-timed use because it does not deplete. Elsewhere — Unani, Ayurveda, Tibetan — a cultivator uses it the same practical way but without any longevity status: a cooling, drying, damp-clearing gruel taken to lighten a waterlogged system, then eased off once the ground is dry. The Kanglaite injectable is a hospital oncology product and has nothing to do with self-directed cultivation.

### Contraindications

Contraindicated in pregnancy in classical Chinese usage — *yi yi ren* is traditionally avoided as it is held to be downward-draining and potentially destabilising to pregnancy; avoid medicinal doses if pregnant [USER: verify]. Its cooling-draining nature is unsuitable for those who are already dry, constipated, or deficient-cold without damp. The **Kanglaite injectable is a hospital-only prescription product** carrying infusion-related risks (including reported hypersensitivity/anaphylactoid reactions) and must never be self-administered or improvised from culinary seed oil. As a food, whole Job's tears grain is well tolerated.
