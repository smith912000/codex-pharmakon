# Euphorbia helioscopia — Dhurji, dur byid (rGyud-bzhi, Men-Tsee-Khang glossary, by column position)

## *Euphorbia helioscopia, as the binomial printed with the English "Sun spurge" in the scientific-name run of the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary's "D" block; the Tibetan name "Dhurji" is set against it by column position only, and Clark 1995 prints a different set of Euphorbia names for dur.byid* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Tibetan texts (FMT) under this binomial only in the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary; the same species is also named in two other texts on disk that this row does not tag.** The Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. Clark 1995 prints no line for *Euphorbia helioscopia* and none for the name Dhurji. Its entry for the same Tibetan name, *dur.byid*, prints other binomials (see below). The extraction note reads: "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, line 265). The spine row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and carries the tag FMT only. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain, and the row has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

*Other texts that name the species (not tagged on this row).* Yang 1998 prints "Ze Qi (Herba Euphorbiae Helioscopiae)" under "Herbs: Inferior Class" (`Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, heading at line 3221, entry at line 3266), and the entry reads: "Ze Qi (Herba Euphorbiae Helioscopiae)201 is bitter and slightly cold. It mainly treats skin fever, enlarged abdomen due to water qi, puffy swelling of the limbs, face, and eyes, and, in males, yin qi insufficiency." Dioscorides 4-165d prints "TITHUMALOS ELIOSKOPIOS" with the translators' line "SUGGESTED: Tithymalus helioscopius [Fuchs, Bauhin], Euphorbia helioscopia [Linnaeus] — Wartwort, Wartgrass, Sun Spurge, Cat's Milk" (`Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, lines 9540 to 9543), and says of it: "They have the same properties as those above but are not as forcible." Both lines were read for this entry. This entry writes no register paragraph for either text, because the tag line carries FMT only. The question of adding SBJ and DMM tags to the spine row is reported to the parent and is not decided here. The Yang entry gives a pharmaceutical Latin name and the Dioscorides binomial is the translators' suggestion, so neither is a certain identification of the Tibetan drug. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (lines 5179 to 5262).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, the "D" block (the letter at line 5179). The glossary prints its columns in separate runs. The phonetic names run from "Dali" (line 5181) to "Drita sazin" (line 5196); line 5188 reads "Dhong ga Dhugmo nyung Dhumtag Dhurji Doen shing". The transliterations (lines 5198 to 5213) include "dong ga dug mo nyung" (line 5204), "ldum stag" (line 5205) and "dur byid sgron shing" (line 5206). The scientific names begin at line 5215, and the line "Euphorbia helioscopia Sun spurge" stands at line 5235, directly after "Strychnos nux-vomica Nux vomica" (line 5233) and before "Pinus tabulaeformis" (line 5237). Counting in order, "Dhurji" is the twelfth phonetic name (counting Dhatig and Dharyaken as two) and *Euphorbia helioscopia* is the twelfth scientific name. The count can be checked at its neighbours: "Dhong ga" is ninth against "Cassia fistula" (line 5220, with the English "Golden shower tree" at line 5229), and the thirteenth, "Doen shing", stands against "Pinus tabulaeformis" with the English "Pine tree" (line 5239), and the transliteration "dur byid sgron shing" gives the two names in that order. The Codex therefore reads the glossary as pairing "Dhurji" with *Euphorbia helioscopia*, by column position only and not as a printed row. Several lines hold more than one name (lines 5183, 5188 and 5217), and the count rests on order. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text on "Dhurji".* The text gives no Latin name for the drug. In chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225), within the run headed "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (line 3428), line 3495 reads: "Dhurji and Tharnu purge all forms of hot and cold disorders." In chapter 21, line 3661: "Arura, Dhenda, Dhong ga, Shri khenda, Dhurji, Thamu, Chum tsa, Ngonbu, Tronbu, Tsi takmo, Rechagpa and Chhu tsa are the group of medicines that are purgatives." The index lists "Dhurji 211,223" (line 7337). The text gives the drug no action of its own beyond purging. That the "Dhurji" of these passages is the glossary's "Dhurji" rests on the identical spelling. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, the same Tibetan name.* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, line 1133, in the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997): "dur.byid: Jatropha glandulifera: Baliospermum montanum (Physic Nut): Iris dichotoma: Euphorbia adenochlora: Euphorbia fischeriana: [has ten names, for example tri.byi.ta. It has a single root, horizontally protruding petioles, a green leaf, reddish-brown four-petalled flower, and rounded, three-sided fruit. The inferior type is without fruit]." The line has no root-text sentence of its own. The next line, 1134, is *thar.nu* and ends "Jatropha and Euphorbia purge all hot and cold disorders", which is the nearest Clark counterpart to the Men-Tsee-Khang sentence at line 3495, and the order of the two names (*dur.byid*, *thar.nu*) is the order of "Dhurji and Tharnu". Clark's *dur.byid* line names *Euphorbia adenochlora* and *Euphorbia fischeriana*, and not *Euphorbia helioscopia*. Clark's chapter 21 list of purgatives names "Euphorbia sp." among others (line 1225). The two Tibetan texts therefore give different Euphorbia species for one Tibetan name, and the Codex does not choose. Clark's preface (line 83) says: "Therefore more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance". [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Euphorbia* is a very large genus of the spurge family (Euphorbiaceae), that its species carry a milky latex, and that the latex of many species is a skin and eye irritant. Diterpene esters are generally known to be responsible for much of that irritancy. Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Men-Tsee-Khang text records that "Dhurji and Tharnu purge all forms of hot and cold disorders" (line 3495) and lists Dhurji among the purgatives (line 3661). These are the text's claims, attached to this binomial only by a column position, and they are not Codex protocols. A purgative drug from a latex-bearing genus is a hazard and not a practice. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no part used, no quantity, no frequency and no duration for Dhurji. Clark's commentary for *dur.byid* describes the root, petioles, leaf, flower and fruit and does not say which part is taken. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Men-Tsee-Khang text places Dhurji in its list of the efficacy of medicinal substances and in its group of purgatives, and Clark's neighbouring lines say the same in kind. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this name places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the sentences cited above were read in full and none mentions one. Its framing is therapeutic only, and the link from this binomial to that framing rests on a column position.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a purgative in the Tibetan text, with no preparation and no dose, and its Latin name rests on a glossary column position. Other texts on disk name the same species (Yang's Inferior Class entry, Dioscorides 4-165d), and that material sits outside this entry's tag line and is reported to the parent. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. The latex of *Euphorbia* species is generally known to be an irritant, a purgative drug can cause dehydration, and the identity is not settled, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Latex that touches the eyes needs urgent medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
