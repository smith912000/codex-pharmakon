# Ocimum album — Arjaka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Ocimum album, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Ocimum  Album.", line 31328) against the Sanskrit name Arjaka (OCR "Axjaka", line 31269). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print the name Arjaka in a drug group, a spice list and several recipes, with no Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The chapters name Arjaka in seven places; they give no Latin and do not call it an Ocimum, so the Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed and the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 389 gives the name as "Axjaka", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31269; id line 31328)" and the Latin as "Ocimum  Album.", with the note that the pairing was proposed by an agent from the two OCR columns and checked by script. The OCR reads the j of this name as x; the Codex reads "Axjaka" as Arjaka, which is the spelling the chapters print.

**The pairing, checked by counting.** On the page headed "66" (line 31238) the Sanskrit names run from Amlika (line 31244) to Atma-gupta (31309): thirty-five entries, Ananta and Anantamula being bracketed as one. Arjaka is the fifteenth (Amlika, Amra, Amrataka, Amrita, Ananta/Anantamula, Ankotaka, Apamarga, Aparajita, Aragvadha, Aravinda, Arevata, Arimeda, Arishta, Arishta, **Arjaka**) and "Ocimum Album." is the fifteenth Latin entry (lines 31312 onward: "Tamarindus Indica", "Mangifera Indica", "Spondias Mangifera", "See Guduchi", "Hemidesmus Indicus", "Alangium hexapetalum", "Achyranthis Aspera", "Clitoria Ternatia", "Cassia Fistula", "See Ambhoja", "See Aragvadha", "Accacia Farnesiana", "Sapindus trifoliatus", "See Nimba", **"Ocimum Album"**). The next entries fit as well: Arjuna with "Terminalia Arjuna" (31329), Arka with "Calotropis gigantia" (31330), Asoka with "Saraca Indica" (31336) and Asva-gandha with "Witbania Somnifera ; Physalis" (31340). The same block is counted in the entry on Alangium hexapetalum (I_1387). [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Arjaka and its OCR forms and by fuzzy matching of every word. The name is printed in these places.

*A drug group (Sutra ch.38).* `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 119 to 126: "The Surasadi Group: The drugs known as Surasa, white Surasa, Fainjjhaka, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika form the group known as the Surasadi Gana." The metrical text (lines 127 to 130): "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough." The text speaks of the group, not of Arjaka singly. The same group list is quoted in the entry on Vitex negundo (I_1386).

*An errhine (Sutra ch.39).* `SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` lines 50 to 64, in the chapter on cleansing and soothing drugs: the list of errhines (*Shirovirechanam*) reads "Pippali, Vidanga, Apamarga, Shigru, Siddharthaka, Shirisha, Maricha, Karavira, Vimvi, Girikarnika, Kinihi, Vacha, Jyotishmati, Karanja, Arka, Alarka, Lashuna, Ativisha, Shringavera, Talisha, Tamala, Surasa, Arjaka, Ingudi, Meshashringi, ..." and the text adds that "the leaves of those commencing with Talisha and ending with the Arjaka therein" are the part to be used. So the leaf is the part used for Arjaka as an errhine.

*A herb among spices (Sutra ch.46).* `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1418 to 1425, in the chapter on food and drink: "The spices and herbs include the Pippali, Maricha, Shringavera, Ardraka, Hingu, Jiraka, Kustumvuru, Jamviraka, Sumukha, Surasa, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, ..." and, lines 1497 to 1503: "The herbs known as the Surasa, Arjaka and Bhustrina destroy the Kapham, are light of digestion, and tend to produce a state of parchedness in the organism. They are demulcent, generate the Pittam, and are heat-making in their potency and pungent in taste and digestion." This is the one place where the text gives qualities to Arjaka, and it gives them to the three herbs together.

*Recipes.* In the chapter on the great Vata disorders, `SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt` lines 493 to 496: "the juice of Mulaka, Eranda, Sphurja, Arjaka, Arka, Saptala and Shankhini should be used in washing (Parisheka), etc., the body of an Apatanaka-patient." In the fever chapter, `SS_Uttara_ch39.txt` lines 1509 to 1511, against shivering in fever of aggravated Kapha or Vayu: "Plasters of the leaves of Surasa, Arjaka and Shigru pasted together with water would prove beneficial." In the Gulma chapter, `SS_Uttara_ch42.txt` lines 617 to 619, for Kaphaja Shula: "... or the soup (of any Sula-subduing article) should be taken with an equal part of Arjaka." In the eye chapter (Abhishyanda due to Shleshma), `SS_Uttara_ch11.txt` lines 80 to 86, an eye-salve (*Anjana*) of the ashes of blue barley soaked in milk is mixed with "an equal part of burnt ashes of Arjaka, Ashphotaka, Kapittha, Vilva, Nirgundi and Jati flowers and an alkali".

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Arjaka found nothing.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Ocimum album* and Arjaka found no entry on this plant. ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Ocimum* is a genus of the mint family (Lamiaceae), and its members are aromatic herbs rich in volatile oils. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. In the Sushruta text the herb is a member of the Surasadi group (vermifuge, aseptic, Kapha-subduing), an errhine by its leaves, a spice-herb that is heat-making, and an ingredient in a washing juice, a fever plaster, a Gulma soup and an eye-salve. The Codex does not recommend any of these, gives no protocol and no dose, and notes that the plant behind the name rests on a translator's appendix pairing.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters give these preparations that include the name: the expressed juice for washing the body (Chikitsa 5); a plaster of the leaves pasted with water (Uttara 39); the soup taken "with an equal part of Arjaka" (Uttara 42); the burnt ashes mixed with those of blue barley and others for an eye-salve (Uttara 11); and the leaves as an errhine (Sutra 39). No quantity is given for Arjaka other than the "equal part" of the Gulma soup and the "equal part" of ashes in the eye-salve. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** for Surasa, Arjaka and Bhustrina together: Kapham-destroying, light of digestion, causing parchedness, "demulcent", Pitta-generating, "heat-making in their potency and pungent in taste and digestion" (Sutra 46, lines 1497 to 1503). For the Surasadi group: it subdues the deranged Kapham (Sutra 38, lines 127 to 130). The text prints no separate *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* for Arjaka alone.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Arjaka in a drug group (Surasadi), a list of errhines, a list of spices and herbs, and in recipes for washing, fever, Gulma and the eye. These are therapeutic and culinary contexts. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with this herb beyond its place in recipes for illnesses, and the Latin name behind Arjaka is a translator's appendix pairing. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of it.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this herb. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. An errhine is applied inside the nose, and an eye-salve inside the eye; neither is a home measure. Fever, eye disease and abdominal colic need medical care. No conservation information was read.
