# Shalyaka flesh — Shalyaka (also printed S'alyaka and S'allaka), flesh of a hole-dwelling (Vileshaya) animal, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the animal Bhishagratna prints as "Shalyaka" in Sutrasthana ch.46, "S'allaka (porcupine)" in Uttarasthana ch.50, and "A porcupine" in his Vol. 3 glossary; the spine list flags the identity as uncertain* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Shalyaka is named in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 218 lists "Shalyaka (porcupine)", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", flagged id_uncertain). The chapter says the flesh of high-ground animals is "generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394), and divides the high-ground (*Jangala*) animals into eight sub-groups, the seventh being the *Vileshaya* (lines 403-409). The group is defined: "Animals such as, the Shvavit, Shalyaka, Godha, Shasha, Vrishadansha, Lopaka, Lomasha-Karna, Kadali, Mriga-Priyaka, Ajagara, Sarpa, Mushika, Nakula and Maha-Vabhru belong to the group of Vileshaya (hole-dwellers)" (lines 574-578). The group text: "The general properties of the flesh of animals belonging to this species are to increase the consistency of stool and urine. They are heat-making in potency, sweet in digestion as those of the preceding group. They subdue the Vayu and generate the Pittam and Kapham. They are demulcent and beneficial in cough, dyspnoea and cachexia" (lines 580-587).

The Shalyaka is then taken singly: "The flesh of the Shalyaka is tasteful, light of digestion, cooling in its potency and anti-toxic, and subdues the deranged Pittam" (lines 597-599). Its listed property of "cooling in its potency" differs from the group's "heat-making in potency", and the lines read do not reconcile the two. The after-drink for the group: "Phalasara wine should be taken after the flesh of any of the hole-dwelling (Vileshaya) animals" (lines 2847-2849).

Other places. *Uttarasthana* ch.50, on hiccup (*Hikka*): "Meat as diet: The soup prepared with the meat of S'allaka (porcupine), S'vadamshtra, Godha, Vrisha-damsa (wild cat), Rishya (D.R. Riksha — a bear) and Mriga (different kinds of deer, as well as of Kapota and Paravata (different kinds of pigeon), Lava and other birds should be taken lukewarm with the expressed juice of acid fruits and with Saindhava and a Sneha (clarified butter, etc.)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch50.txt` lines 182-189; spellings as in the OCR). In the *Sutrasthana* chapter on foods that should not be taken together, "the flesh of Godha, Porcupine and hog" occurs in a sentence about foods that "should not be eaten together" (`SS_Sutra_ch20.txt` lines 160-162); the OCR runs several pairings together, and which items are paired was not resolved. Other porcupine materials occur elsewhere, the excreta, the hair and the skin (`SS_Uttara_ch54.txt` line 183; `SS_Uttara_ch51.txt` line 178; `SS_Uttara_ch60.txt` line 254), and are separate substances not treated here.

**Identity.** The Sutra ch.46 passage gives no gloss. Bhishagratna glosses the near spelling "S'allaka" as "porcupine" in Uttara ch.50 (line 183) and the Vol. 3 glossary reads "S'alyaka — A porcupine" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` lines 30913 and 30944; the glossary is an OCR table with entries and glosses in separate columns, and the pairing is by order). Whether the Shalyaka of ch.46 and the S'allaka of ch.50 are the same animal rests on the near spelling and that glossary entry. The group also names a Shvavit, which the spine list likewise glosses "porcupine" (`_sources/sushruta.csv` line 217). [USER: verify]

### Active Components

If the animal is a porcupine, its flesh is muscle meat: protein, with fat that varies with season and diet; the quills are keratin and are not part of the flesh. Wild rodents can carry parasites and bacteria. No analysis specific to this entry was read, and the identity is flagged uncertain. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text records the flesh as a food of the hole-dwelling group, with stated actions on *Pittam* and poison, and, in one of the soups for hiccup, as one of a list of meats. Nothing in the sources supports eating or sourcing it. Porcupines are protected or regulated in some countries, and the flesh of a wild rodent carries the usual food-safety risks. The Codex does not recommend the flesh for any complaint and gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives two preparations. The first is the hiccup soup in Uttara ch.50: meat of the S'allaka and others made into a soup, "taken lukewarm with the expressed juice of acid fruits and with Saindhava and a Sneha (clarified butter, etc.)" (lines 182-189). The second is the general after-drink for the *Vileshaya* group: Phalasara wine after the flesh of any hole-dwelling animal (ch.46 lines 2847-2849). No quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46. For the *Vileshaya* group (lines 580-587): increases the consistency of stool and urine; heat-making in potency; sweet in digestion; subdues *Vayu*; generates *Pittam* and *Kapham*; demulcent; beneficial in cough, dyspnoea and cachexia. For the Shalyaka singly (lines 597-599): tasteful; light of digestion; cooling in potency; anti-toxic; subdues deranged *Pittam*. No taste (*rasa*) in the sense of a named flavour is printed for it. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Shalyaka is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and the *ojas* principle (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food in general, not about this flesh. The by-name statement is a short list of digestive and humoral properties, and the hiccup soup is a regimen for a disease. Neither is a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* passage; no such chapter read names the Shalyaka. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta this is one of the hole-dwelling animals, with a few stated properties and a place in a soup for hiccup. A person who does not eat porcupine has nothing to adopt.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** the animal is unglossed in ch.46 and rests on a near spelling in ch.50 and a glossary; do not substitute a species on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Food safety:** wild rodent flesh can carry parasites and bacteria; cook through.
- **The text's own cautions:** the *Vileshaya* group generates *Pittam* and *Kapham* (ch.46, lines 584-585), and porcupine flesh is named in the Sutra ch.20 sentence on foods not to be eaten together (the pairing is unresolved).
- **Legal and conservation status:** porcupines are protected or regulated in some countries. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food is a moral question for many traditions, and meat is restricted in some; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
