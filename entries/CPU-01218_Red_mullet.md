# Red mullet — *Trigla*, "mullus" (Dioscorides, *De Materia Medica* 2-24)
## *Mullidae, Mullus barbatus — Red Mullet (the translator's suggested identification, not the text's)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The substance is one short chapter of Book Two, "Living Creatures", in the Osbaldeston and Wood English version (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`; "DMM text" below). The chapter is headed "2-24. TRIGLA" (line 5018), and below the heading comes the editors' line "SUGGESTED: Mullidae, Mullus barbatus — Red Mullet" (line 5019). The editors say in their preface that such lines are possibilities offered to the reader and are not a settled identification (line 43). The chapter in full (line 5020): "The mullus if often eaten is thought to cause dullness of sight, but cut apart whilst it is raw and applied it heals the hurt caused by the sea dragon [2-15], scorpion, and the spider." The bracketed "[2-15]" is the editors' cross-reference to the chapter on the sea dragon (line 4976, "2-15. DRAKON THALASSIOS", suggested as spiny-finned fishes of the weever family), where the text says the sea dragon "(opened and applied) is a cure for the hurt done by his prickles" (line 4978). The red mullet chapter sits between the cuttlefish (2-23, line 5015) and the hippopotamus's testicles (2-25, line 5032). The name index lists it on page 190 (line 16618).

That is the whole of the attestation. The chapter has two statements: a dietary caution, which Dioscorides hands on as a belief ("is thought to"), and an external remedy for three kinds of wound or sting. It gives no description of the fish, no place or season, no test for the genuine fish and no dose. The spine's locus, `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` (row 214, "TRIGLA, 2-24 (line 5018)"), gives the same Greek heading and identification.

### Active Components

Red mullet is a marine fish; its flesh is muscle protein with some fat, as with other marine fish. The chapter names no active constituent, and none is claimed. The remedy it describes is the raw flesh itself, cut open and applied, so the entry has no extract or compound to describe.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The chapter's external use, a freshly cut raw fish laid on a sting or bite, has no place in a modern protocol. Stings from weever fish and scorpions, and bites from spiders, are treated today by first aid and, where needed, medical care, and a raw fish adds infection risk to a wound. The dietary caution, that often eating the fish dulls the sight, is a belief Dioscorides reports and does not endorse. The Codex's application layer has nothing to add.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives one preparation: the raw fish "cut apart" and applied to the hurt (line 5020). It gives no quantity, no duration, no preparation of the fish beyond cutting it, and no dose for eating it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM).** The chapter prints no quality of any kind for the fish: no warming, cooling, drying, astringent or other term. Dioscorides' classification here is by use, and the use is stated above. No other tradition is tagged on this entry, so none is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is supplied here. What he offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention: provenance, season of gathering, assay by sense, detection of adulteration. This chapter gives little of it. It gives no provenance and no test of the fish. What it does show is a habit of marking the status of a claim, since the harmful effect of eating the fish is something that "is thought" and not something he asserts. The fish is a food as well as a remedy, and the one regimen-related statement in the chapter is a caution against eating it often. The status is therapeutic and dietary, not a cultivation one.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing to do with this entry for cultivation. The fish has no recorded role in nourishing life, and the only advice the chapter gives about eating it is to avoid doing so often. If the passage teaches anything, it is the discipline of separating what Dioscorides reports as belief from what he states as method.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** raw fish applied to broken skin can introduce bacteria; raw or undercooked marine fish can carry parasites, and an untreated sting needs proper care.
- **Stings and bites:** a weever sting, a scorpion sting or a spider bite can be serious. Apply recognised first aid and seek care; do not substitute a poultice. [USER: verify]
- **Fish allergy:** anyone with a fish allergy must avoid handling and eating it.
- **Legal and conservation status:** red mullet is a commercially fished species and legal as food in most places, but quotas and local regulations differ. [USER: verify]
