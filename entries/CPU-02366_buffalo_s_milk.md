# buffalo's milk — "milk of a she-buffalo" (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907); Sanskrit word not printed in the lines read

## *The milk of the female water buffalo, the domestic animal of the Indian subcontinent (*Bubalus bubalis*). Bhishagratna prints only "she-buffalo" and gives no binomial in the lines read; the species is our identification, from the English word and the setting among domestic milk animals. Confidence: medium* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this English phrase.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as a curated non-plant addition from the milk group of chapter 45 and is one of several milk rows added together (cow, goat, ewe, mare, camel, elephant and others); this entry replaces its placeholder. The first-pass guess was "*Bubalus bubalis* milk (buffalo milk)". That stands. The general entry on milk is `III_285_Milk.md` (CPU-00535), which is tagged Assyrian, Avicenna and Ebers, and does not rest on Sushruta; this entry records what Sushruta says of the buffalo's milk, and does not fold into that one.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The English is Bhishagratna's translation of the *Sushruta Samhita*. The text is *Sutrasthana* ch.45, "the Chapter, which deals with the rules to be observed in respect of liquid substances in general" (*Drava-dravya-vidhi*; `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`, lines 4 to 6).

1. **The milk group.** The group opens (lines 413 to 416): "The Milk Group: The milk of a cow, she-goat, she-camel, ewe, she-buffalo, mare, she-elephant, or of a woman, is what generally comes to the use of man." The buffalo is the fifth of eight. The text then speaks of milk in general (lines 419 to 462): "The milk is the white fluid essence of drugs and cereals, which enter into the food of the aforesaid milk-giving animals, and is therefore the best of all nutritive substances (literally life-giving). It is heavy, sweet, slimy, cold, glossy, emollient, laxative and mild", with a long list of diseases in which it is beneficial and an account of it as "a sacred, constructive, tonic, spermatopoietic, rejuvenating and aphrodisiac" that "increases the duration of life" and "augments the quality of bodily albumen (Ojah)". These sentences are about milk as a group, and the text does not say that each animal's milk shares each property alike.
2. **The buffalo's own line.** Lines 499 to 502: "The milk of a she-buffalo is sweet in taste, tends to impair digestion and increases the slimy secretion of the organs. It is heavy, soporific, cooling, and contains more fatty matter than cow's milk." **This is the text's only separate statement on the milk.** It names the taste (sweet), the effect on digestion (impairs it), on the slimy secretion (increases it, the *Kapham* quality), the heaviness, the soporific and cooling quality, and the comparison with cow's milk (more fatty matter).
3. **The milk's curd.** Lines 586 to 591: "Curd, prepared with the milk of a she-buffalo, is sweet in digestion, and spermatopoietic. It pacifies the deranged Vayu and Pittam, and serves to augment the normal quantity of bodily Kapham. It is specifically a demulcent substance."
4. **The milk's clarified butter.** Lines 810 to 814: "Clarified butter prepared with the milk of a she-buffalo is sweet, heavy in digestion, and proves remedial in haemoptysis. It is cooling and increases the quantity of bodily Kapham, and subdues the deranged Vayu and Pittam."
5. **Time and treatment of milk.** The chapter's general rules on milk apply to all milk: morning milk is "heavy, cold and takes a long time to be digested"; evening milk is "refrigerant and eye-invigorating"; "Cold or unboiled milk is extremely heavy, and serves to increase the slimy secretions of the organs, whereas by boiling it is freed from those injurious traits", except woman's milk; "freshly milched warm milk should be regarded as extremely wholesome"; "over-cooked milk is heavy and fat-making"; and spoiled milk, which "emits a fetid smell, or has become discoloured and insipid, or has acquired an acid taste and looks shreddy and curdled, or tastes saline, should be regarded as unwholesome and injurious" (lines 524 to 553). These sentences are about milk as a class.

**Other places where Sushruta names buffalo milk.** The chapter on clinical observations, *Sutrasthana* ch.35 (`SS_Sutra_ch35.txt` lines 458 to 464), says that in *Atyagni* (an abnormally keen digestive fire, voracious appetite) "a diet consisting of buffalo-milk, or its curd (Dadhi) and liquid buffalo-butter should be prescribed for the patient in addition", after the purgatives and sweet, cooling, fatty diet. In *Uttaratantra* ch.54 a footnote by Dallana says that "Milk of a cow or of a she-buffalo, etc., may be used here" (`SS_Uttara_ch54.txt` line 8); the passage it glosses was not read in full. ### Active Components

The text names no constituent beyond "fatty matter". In general terms, milk is an emulsion of fat in water that carries protein (casein and whey proteins), lactose, minerals such as calcium, and vitamins. Buffalo milk is generally richer in fat and in total solids than cow's milk, which agrees with the single comparison the text makes (more fatty matter than cow's milk); the figures are not given here because none was read from a source. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only; not a recommended practice.** Milk is an ordinary food in the regions where buffaloes are kept, and needs no protocol from this Codex.

- **What the text says.** Sweet, heavy, cooling, soporific, increasing *Kapham* and impairing digestion; richer in fat than cow's milk. Its curd and butter pacify *Vayu* and *Pittam*; the butter is remedial in haemoptysis. In one chapter (35) it forms the diet, with its curd and butter, for voracious appetite.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no quantity for the diet, no duration, and no property of the milk beyond the lines above.

The Codex gives no regimen built on buffalo milk, and does not translate the terms *Vayu*, *Pittam*, *Kapham* or *Atyagni* into modern diagnoses.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text's preparations of the milk are: boiling (which "frees" cold milk of its injurious traits, except woman's milk), curdling (to curd) and churning to clarified butter (lines 586 to 591 and 810 to 814). The *Atyagni* diet in ch.35 is milk, curd and liquid butter. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Buffalo milk is a mammalian milk, consumed widely in South Asia and the Mediterranean, and used for butter, ghee and some cheeses. Raw milk of any species can carry bacteria and other pathogens, and heat treatment (boiling or pasteurisation) reduces this risk; the text's rule to boil milk agrees in direction with that fact, though the text gives other reasons. People with lactose intolerance or milk-protein allergy react to buffalo milk as to other milks. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Ayurveda (SS), ch.45 line 499 ff.** Taste: sweet. Quality: heavy; cooling; soporific. Effect: impairs digestion and increases the slimy secretion (*Kapham*). The English prints no separate *virya* or *vipaka* for the buffalo's milk. For milk as a class the text says "heavy, sweet, slimy, cold, glossy, emollient, laxative and mild". The curd is "sweet in digestion"; the butter is "sweet, heavy in digestion" and "cooling" (lines 586 to 591, 810 to 814).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status of its own.* In *Sutrasthana* ch.45, which is a chapter on liquid substances and not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, milk as a class is called "constructive, tonic, spermatopoietic, rejuvenating and aphrodisiac", increases the duration of life, and augments bodily albumen (*Ojah*) (lines 452 to 462). The buffalo's milk is listed among the milks, but the sentence on it names no such status: it is "heavy, soporific, cooling", impairs digestion and increases the slimy secretion. The text does not say that the class description holds for the buffalo alike. This is therefore a framed claim: the milk group carries the language of *ojas* and rejuvenation as a group; the buffalo's own line contradicts, in part, a simple reading of it, and no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter naming the buffalo's milk was read. The popular *sattvic*/*rajasic*/*tamasic* food lists belong to *Samkhya*-era and later material, not to Sushruta, and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes buffalo milk a cultivation substance in its own right. The text puts it among the milks, calls it heavy and *Kapham*-raising, and prescribes it in one disorder of excess digestive fire. A cultivator uses it, if at all, as ordinary food, and takes the text's own cautions about heaviness and slow digestion.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene.** Raw or unpasteurised milk of any animal carries bacterial and parasitic risk (including brucellosis and tuberculosis in some regions). Boil or pasteurise.
- **Allergy and intolerance.** Milk-protein allergy and lactose intolerance apply.
- **Digestion and weight.** The text itself calls the milk heavy, digestion-impairing and *Kapham*-increasing; it is not suited to someone seeking a light diet, by the text's own account.
- **Spoiled milk.** The text calls milk that smells fetid, is discoloured, sour, curdled or salty "unwholesome and injurious".
- **Voracious appetite, haemoptysis.** These are medical conditions; use proper care. The text's uses are historical records.
- **Ethics.** Dairy buffalo are domestic animals; vegans and those with religious or cultural food rules will choose accordingly.
