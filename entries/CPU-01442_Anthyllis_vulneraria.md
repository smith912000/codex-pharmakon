# Anthyllis vulneraria — Anthullis (Dioscorides 3-153)

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. **Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading at line 7837.

*3-153. ANTHULLIS.* "There are two types of anthyllis. One has leaves similar to lens [lentils], and upright little branches the height of twenty centimetres, with the leaves soft, the root slender and little. It grows in sandy sunny places, somewhat salty to the taste. The other kind is similar in its leaves and small branches to chamepitys [3-175], but they are rougher, shorter, and sharper. The flower is a purple colour, smelling extremely strong, the root like chicory. Two teaspoonfuls of a decoction (taken as a drink) has considerable strength to help frequent painful urination and inflamed kidneys. Pounded into small pieces and given as a pessary (with rosaceum [1-53] and milk) they soften inflammations of the womb. They also heal wounds, and a decoction (taken as a drink with vinegar and honey) of that which is similar to chamepitys heals epilepsy (among other things). It is also called anthyllon, anthemis, eranthemis, leucanthemon, soranthis, or flos campestris; the Romans call it solaster." The index prints ANTHULLIS at p.526. Line 4477 of the same text, in an earlier chapter, prints "Anthyllis cretica — Cretan Silver Bush" as a translators' suggestion for a different Greek plant, and that is not this chapter.

### Active Components

*Anthyllis* is a genus of legumes (Fabaceae). The constituents of the plants Dioscorides describes were not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records a decoction for painful urination and inflamed kidneys, a pessary for womb inflammation, a wound-healing use, and for the chamepitys-like kind a drink in vinegar and honey for epilepsy. These are the text's claims, not a Codex protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

3-153 gives "two teaspoonfuls of a decoction (taken as a drink)" for painful urination and inflamed kidneys; a pessary of the pounded plant "with rosaceum and milk"; and a decoction "with vinegar and honey" for epilepsy. Beyond the two teaspoonfuls, no dose is given. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No claim is made for wound healing or kidney effects. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints "somewhat salty to the taste" for the first kind, and "rougher, shorter, and sharper" leaves and a "considerable strength" for the decoction. No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter offers a cultivator is the discipline of attention that the cultivation spec describes for this tradition: how to know the plant by its parts, where it grows, what it is called, and what is claimed for it. 3-153 separates two kinds by leaf, height, root, habitat and flower (sandy sunny places, and a purple, strong-smelling flower), and lists seven Greek and one Latin name. It assigns no status. The identification of either kind with *A. vulneraria* is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. Its lesson is Dioscorides' habit of telling two similar plants apart before using either.

### Contraindications

The identity of the two Greek kinds is uncertain, so no safety statement can be made for them. Kidney inflammation and epilepsy are conditions for a physician, and a vaginal pessary of an unidentified plant is not safe. None of the traditional uses above is advice. [USER: verify]
