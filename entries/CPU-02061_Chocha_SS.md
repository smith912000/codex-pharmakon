# Chocha — Chocha (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907); also printed Chochaka in one place

## *An aromatic drug of the Eladi group whose botanical identity is not established; the translator glosses it "cardamom" once (Chikitsa ch.17), but the same book lists Ela separately in the same recipes and lists, so the gloss is not safe. Identification: unresolved. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not identified. Nothing in this entry is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The names.csv row gives "no identification given here". A search of all the Sushruta text on disk (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`, the chapter files and the three Bhishagratna volumes) for "Chocha" found the places below.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

1. **Group list.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38, the Eladi group (`Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15509; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 168). The group list reads: "Ela, Tagara, Kushtha, Mansi, Dhyamaka, Tvaka, Patra, Naga-pushpa, Priyangu, Harenuka, Vyaghranakha, Shukti, Clianda, Sthauneyaka, Shriveshtaka, Chocha, Choraka, Valaka, Guggulu, Sarjarasa, Turushka, Kunduruka, Aguru, Sprikka, Ushira, Bhadradaru, Kumkuma, Punnaga and Keshara." The metrical text gives the virtue of the group as a whole: "subduing the action of Vayu and Kapham and in neutralising the effects of poison. It is a cosmetic and arrests the eruption of pimples and other vegetations on the skin such as rash, urticaria etc. and checks the itching sensation incidental thereto" (Vol. I lines 15512 to 15520). This is the group's virtue, not a statement about Chocha alone.
2. **A plaster.** *Chikitsasthana* ch.17 (`text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt` line 123; Vol. II line 18646). For the Kaphaja type of *Visarpa*, after a plaster of Aja-gandha, Ashva-gandha, Sarala, Kala, Ekaishika and Aja-shringi pasted in cow's urine, the text adds: "Drugs, such as Kalanusarya, Aguru, Chocha (cardamom), Gunja, Rasna, Vacha, Shita-shiva, Indra-parni, Palindi, Munjata and Mahi-Kadamba (applied similarly) are also efficacious in the present type." The gloss "(cardamom)" is Bhishagratna's and is the only identification offered anywhere in the text.
3. **A Painaka.** *Uttarasthana* ch.47 (`text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch47.txt` line 227; Vol. III line 12456). "A Painak prepared with Tvak, Patra, Chocha, Markka, Eld, Naga-pushpa and flowers of Shleshmataka ground together into a paste and mixed with treacle and Draksha, should be filtered and perfumed and given to a person suffering from an attack of Panatyaya". Here "Eld" (Ela) is named separately from Chocha. That makes the "cardamom" gloss doubtful, since the sources read do not equate Ela with Chocha. [USER: verify]
4. **Chochaka.** *Kalpasthana* ch.7 (`text/kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch07.txt` line 21) lists "Chochaka" among powders added to an alkaline solution. Whether this is the same drug is not stated. [USER: verify]

The same Eladi list holds Clianda and Choraka, which are the subjects of separate rows (CPU-02067 and CPU-02064).

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent, and the plant is not identified, so no general composition can be stated. If the translator's "cardamom" were right, the aromatic volatile oils of the ginger family would apply; that is not established. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unidentified.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Eladi group: calming Vata and Kapha, counteracting poison, and clearing rash and itch (group-level). As a named plaster ingredient: Kaphaja *Visarpa*. As a named ingredient: a Painaka drink for *Panatyaya*.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no taste or potency, no identification.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend any plant for these uses.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are a plaster (ch.17) and a Painaka paste mixed with treacle and Draksha, filtered and perfumed (ch.47). No quantity is given for Chocha in either. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for this drug in the lines found. The only qualities given are those of the Eladi group as a whole (calming Vata and Kapha; counteracting poison; cosmetic). No such classification is imported for Chocha itself.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in chapter 38's group list and in two therapeutic recipes. None of the places found is in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns it no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it. The popular sattvic, rajasic and tamasic food lists do not trace to Sushruta and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** An unidentified aromatic named in a group list and in two recipes. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without an identification nothing can be gathered or used. A cultivator reads it as a name in a record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified; do not substitute any herb or spice for it.
- **Visarpa and Panatyaya.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Companion ingredients.** The recipes contain other drugs (for example Aguru, Gunja) whose safety is not addressed here.
