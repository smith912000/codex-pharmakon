### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is Sutrasthana chapter 38, "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam), in Bhishagratna's translation: `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15592, and `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 251.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS).** The chapter opens by saying that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)". The Ka'kolya'di group is introduced: "The drugs known as Kakoli-Kshira-Kakoli, Jivaka, Rishabhaka, Mudgaparni, Mashaparni, Meda, Mahameda, Chhinna-ruha, Karkata-Shringi, Tugakshiri, Padmaka, Prapaundarika, Riddhi, Vriddhi, Mridvika, Jivanti and Madhuka, combinedly form the group known as the Kakolyadi Gana" (Vol. I lines 15591-15604). The Metrical Text reads: "The group of medicinal drugs under discussion subdues the action of the deranged Pittam, blood and Vayu. It increases the quantity of milk in the breast (galactagogue) and favours the accumulation of phlegm (Kapham) in the body. It is a restorative and an elixir and is endued with the therapeutic virtue of augmenting the virile potency of a man" (ch.38 file lines 265-273). That statement is made of the group as a whole, and the text does not say which member does what.

The printing "Kakoli-Kshira-Kakoli" has no comma between the two halves, while every other item in the list is separated by commas. The reading as two drugs, Kakoli and Kshira-Kakoli ("milk Kakoli"), is ours. No Sanskrit text was read here to confirm it. [USER: verify against the printed page and the Sanskrit]. For the two drugs themselves, and for their identifications, see the existing entries named above; no identification is repeated here.

The chapter closes with a rule of use (ch.38 file line 558): the physician should prepare plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, ghritas or potions "according to the exigencies of each individual case", and "only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group ... or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety" are to be used as the physician decides.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The group's Metrical Text calls the group "a restorative and an elixir" and says it augments virile potency, which is the vocabulary of *rasayana* and *vajikarana*. That is said of the group of seventeen items, in a chapter on the classification of drugs, and not in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and nothing in the lines read assigns the status to this item singly. The register is therefore (b): a group-level frame, with the cultivation status of the item unattested. The text does not use the word *ojas* for the group.

**Practitioner's note.** The group is described in restorative terms, but the text gives no dose, no course and no instruction for any single member, and the plants behind the names are scarce. A cultivator has nothing sourced to follow here.
