# Biota orientalis — Bai Shi 柏實 (wood), "Semen Biotae Orientalis"; current name Bai Zi Ren or Bai Ren per Yang's note (Shennong Bencao Jing)

## *Biota orientalis, as the pharmaceutical Latin "Semen Biotae Orientalis" that Yang 1998 prints for the entry "Bai Shi"; only the opening of the entry is legible in the text file* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong text (SBJ).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The entry is in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`). The Latin name is the translator's pharmaceutical Latin and was matched to the binomial by stem in the extraction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv`, row 205). The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The characters 柏實 come from the Codex's own list of Shennong names (`_sources/shennong.csv`, line 78), which prints them alone, and not from Yang's English text. The list's next *Bai* entry, 卷柏 (line 134), is a different drug. [USER: verify]

**Chinese — Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ).** *Bai Shi (Semen Biotae Orientalis), PDF page 104.* The page marker `[[p104]]` is at line 3842. The entry begins at line 3856 and is the third on the page, after the end of Fu Ling and the whole of Song Zhi (*Resina Pini*, lines 3850 to 3855). **Only its first line is legible in the text file.** The line reads (line 3856):

"Bai Shi (Semen Biotae Orientalis)246 is sweet and balanced. It mainly"

The text file then runs on into the translator's footnotes: the end of note 243 (lines 3857 to 3862, on Poria), notes 244 and 245 (lines 3863 to 3867), and the start of note 246 (lines 3868 to 3870). The rest of the file, from `[[p105]]` (line 3872) to `[[p225]]` (line 4112, the last), is page-marker lines with no text. The rest of the entry (what it treats, any sentence on protracted taking, any other name, where it grows) is therefore **not available** in the sources on disk, and the Codex does not supply it from memory. Note 246 reads, so far as it is legible: "The current name of this medicinal is Bai Zi Ren or Bai Ren. In an annotation on this passage, Ye Gui said: Biota Seeds enter" and breaks off there. The note is the translator's and not the classic's. [USER: verify the entry's full text against Yang 1998, p. 104 onward in the printed book]

*The class heading.* Yang's table of contents prints "Woods: Superior Class" (line 753). The heading is printed at line 3809, immediately under the page marker `[[p103]]` (line 3808), at the head of the first page of the woods. Page 104 has no heading under its marker: it opens (line 3843) with the running title "The Divine Farmer's Materia Medica Classic". The entry therefore stands under the superior-class heading by position (forty-seven lines and one page break below it), and not directly beneath it. No further heading is printed between line 3809 and the entry, and the file has no text after the entry, so the text file cannot show whether a "Woods: Middle Class" heading follows later. The preface (lines 352 to 355 and 383 to 384) says of the superior class: "There are 120 superior class medicinals which are used as sovereigns. They mainly nourish life and correspond to heaven. They are nontoxic and taking them in large amounts and for a long time will not harm people." Yang's commentary (lines 356 to 358) says that superior-class medicinals "are able to nurture life and therefore bestow longevity".

*Neighbours on the same page.* Two entries on this page carry sentences on protracted taking: Fu Ling (lines 3846 to 3848: "Protracted taking may quiet the ethereal soul, nourish the spirit, make one free from hunger, and prolong life") and Song Zhi (lines 3852 to 3853: "Protracted taking may make the body light and prolong life"). These belong to those entries. The Codex does not assume that Bai Shi's lost lines say the same.

*Identity.* The name "Bai Shi" is also printed elsewhere in Yang for a mineral. Line 1004 gives "Bai Shi Ying (Quartz Crystal)", and lines 1022 and 1147 give a "Bai Shi" ("White Stone") in a list of stones. They are different drugs and the Codex keeps them apart. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Biota orientalis* is a name used for a conifer of the cypress family (Cupressaceae) that botanists now commonly place in the genus *Platycladus*, and that conifer seeds generally contain fixed oils. The new binomial is general knowledge and not from the sources read. Nothing here says what the Shennong drug contained. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Chinese — Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ).** *(a) Attested for the class only: the superior class (上品), by position after the page-top heading of the woods.* The class is assigned by the printed heading "Woods: Superior Class" (line 3809, `[[p103]]`), above the entry by one page break and not directly beneath it. This is a printed class. In the preface (lines 352 to 355 and 383 to 384) the superior class mainly "nourish[es] life", is nontoxic, and is the class on which one bases the effort to "make one's body light, boost the qi, prevent aging, and prolong life". The entry's own text is not legible beyond "sweet and balanced. It mainly", so what the entry says of protracted taking, of making the body light or of prolonging life is **unknown** to the Codex and nothing is asserted about it. The phrase *qing shen* 輕身 ("make the body light") has no pharmacological equivalent and is not translated into one. No *jing*, *shen* or *neidan* action is asserted. The grade (a) is for the printed class by position, and for nothing else.

**Practitioner's note.** A superior-class entry in the Chinese scheme, with an entry text that survives only in its first line. The class is the classic's grade for substances taken long-term, but the Codex cannot read what the entry itself says, has no preparation and no dose, and holds no clinical record to set beside it. Nothing here supports taking this seed as a cultivation practice. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The legible lines print no warning, and the lines that would carry one are missing. The preface says of the superior class that it is nontoxic, but that is a statement about the class. The identity rests on a translator's Latin name and on a mostly illegible entry, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No safety information for pregnancy was read.
