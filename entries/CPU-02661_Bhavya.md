# Bhavya — Bhavya (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation; printed "Bhavya" and "Bhavyam")

## *Dillenia indica L., a large evergreen tree of the family Dilleniaceae whose acid fruit is the likely meaning of Bhavya; the identification is ours, taken from Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3, which prints "Dilenia Indica" against the name by position in the OCR'd columns, and is of medium-to-high confidence for the name, since the body text of the translation gives no Latin name and describes no plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, in four places.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 8 November 2026 as an unverified residual, "Dilenia Indica [SS]", read from the translation's Appendix; this entry replaces it. Nothing was found for the drug in the other texts on disk. The search covered the Sushruta chapter files and the three Bhishagratna volumes under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS), the index.** In Bhishagratna's Vol. III, Appendix No. 3, the Sanskrit name is printed as "Bhavya" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 31412). The Latin names sit in a separate column further down the page (id line 31465). Pairing them by position, the name that stands against Bhavya is "Dilenia Indica", i.e. *Dillenia indica*. The neighbouring pairs fit the same count: the name after Bhavya, Bhringa-raja, carries "Wedelia Calendulacea; Eclipta Erecta" (line 31466), and the one after that, Bhuchampaka, carries "Kemferia Rotunda" (line 31467). The Latin name is Bhishagratna's, not Sushruta's.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS), the acid group.** Sutrasthana ch. 42 lists the acid-tasting substances and names Bhavya among them: "Koshamra, Bhavya, Paravata, Vetraphala, Lakucha, Amla-Vetash, Dantashatha and curd, whey, Sura, Shukta, Sauvira, Tushodaka and Dhanyamla, etc. are generally included within the acid group" (`Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 17009; chapter file `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` line 313).

**Sushruta Samhita (SS), the fruit group.** Sutrasthana ch. 46 opens the Fruit Group (Phala-varga) with a list of fruits, among them "Amra, Amrataka, Karamarda, Piyala, Lakucha, Bhavya, Paravata, Vetraphala, Prachinamalaka, Tintidika, Nipa" (`Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 20650; `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 990). The same chapter then gives its qualities: "The Bhavyam is pleasant, tasteful, astringent, and acid in taste. It cleanses the mouth and subdues the Pittam and Kapham. It is astringent, heavy, cooling and is long retained in the stomach" (Vol. I line 20745 onward; `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1085 to 1089). The translation does not say which part of the tree is meant beyond calling it a fruit; no preparation, no dose and no indication for disease is given.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** It is a fruit of the acid group and of the fruit group. Its listed qualities are pleasant, tasteful, astringent, acid, cooling, heavy, long retained in the stomach; it cleanses the mouth and subdues Pittam and Kapham.
- **What the text does not say.** No disease indication, no preparation, no dose, no duration, no part of the plant other than the fruit.

The Codex does not translate these qualities into modern effects and gives no protocol. The identification is ours, and a reader should not assume the fruit sold under a Sanskrit name today is the one Sushruta meant. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Dillenia indica* is a tree of tropical Asia in the family Dilleniaceae, grown for its large flowers and its sour fruit, which is eaten in several regional cuisines. The text's "acid" agrees in kind with the sour taste of the fruit. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Bhavya stands in the dietetic and dravyaguna frame of Sutrasthana: the acid group (ch. 42) and the fruit group (ch. 46), described by taste and by its action on the humours. The passages read do not place it in a rasayana or vajikarana chapter, and no passage read assigns it a status for *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The sattva, rajas and tamas food lists are a later Samkhya-derived layer and not Sushruta's. Its status here is therefore therapeutic and dietetic only.

**Practitioner's note.** A sour, cooling fruit named in two taste lists, with no disease indication and no dose. A cultivator has nothing in the sources to act on. If the fruit is eaten, it is eaten as a fruit.
