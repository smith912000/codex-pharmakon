# Gavaya flesh — Gavaya (गवय), "Gavaya" (Bhishagratna 1907); the translator's glossary prints "Gavaya (Bos gavaeus [OCR: 'Bas gavivus']) wild ox" and "A kind of ox, the gayal"
## *The flesh of the gavaya, a wild bovine of the shore-dwelling group in Sushruta's food chapter; the project list row reads "gaur", and the translator's glossary reads "a kind of ox, the gayal"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Gavaya* is named twice in the *Sutrasthana* chapter on food and drink, ch.46 (*Annapana-vidhi*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`). The chapter opens by saying that food is "the chief source of their bodily strength and complexion, as well as of the albuminous (Ojas) principle in their organisms" (lines 8-12), and goes on to list the flesh of animals by group. The first mention is in the *Anupa* group, "animals which are generally found to frequent marshy places", divided into five groups, of which the first is the *Kulacharas* (lines 659-662). The text names the members of the *Kulachara* group (page 488, lines 671-680): "Of these, the elephant, the Gavaya, buffalo, Ruru (an animal of the deer species which casts its antlers and wanders roaming about in the forests in autumn) Chamara, Srimara (an animal of the Zebra species with green and red stripes), Rohita (red deer), boar, rhinoceros, Gokarna, Kalapuchchaka, together with the Nynku (a species of antlered deer) and the wild cow, etc., frequent the cool shores of swamps and lakes, and are accordingly included within the group of Kulacharas (shore-dwellers)." The *gavaya* is the second name in that list, after the elephant. The list ends with "the wild cow", which is a separate name from the *gavaya* in the translation.

The second mention is the sentence about the flesh, on page 488 (lines 693-695): "The flesh of the Gavaya is demulcent and sweet in taste, and proves beneficial in cough and is sweet of digestion. It tends to increase sexual capacity." The sentence stands in the run of flesh sentences that follows the group's general properties, immediately after the elephant (lines 688-692) and before the buffalo (line 695). The list row is `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 239, "Gavaya (gaur)", citing "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1".

*Identity.* The ch.46 text prints no gloss for the name. Bhishagratna's glossary in volume 3 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`) prints in its name column, at line 30541, "Gavaya (Bas gavivus) wild on" (the OCR for "*Bos gavaeus*, wild ox", as best I can read it), and in the meaning column, by list order, "A kind of ox, the gayal." (the meanings follow the names in order here: "A kind of sea-fish" for *Gargara*, then the gayal, then "A kind of mountain-bird" for *Girisa*, then "A cow" for *Go*). The glossary also has a separate name, which the OCR prints "ArnayaGavaya" (line 30368), whose meaning by list order is "A wild cow". So the glossary reads the *gavaya* as the gayal, while the project list row reads "gaur". These are different bovines in current usage, and the Sanskrit name was not analysed in the sources read. *The group.* Immediately after the list the chapter gives the properties of the group (lines 682-686): "The flesh of an animal of this group is spermatopoietic and destroys the deranged Vayu and Kapham. It is sweet in taste and digestion, cooling, tonic, demulcent and diuretic, and increases the quantity of Kapham." The sentence on the *gavaya* is its own: it gives "demulcent", "sweet in taste", "sweet of digestion", "beneficial in cough", and "tends to increase sexual capacity".

A search of the whole Sushruta text on disk (`text/sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`, and the three Bhishagratna volumes) for *gavaya* found only ch.46 (lines 671 and 693) and the glossary.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, the flesh of a large grazing bovine is mostly water and muscle protein, with fat, iron and B vitamins. Nothing links any constituent to the actions the text gives.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced use is dietary: the flesh of the *gavaya* is demulcent, sweet, beneficial in cough, and "tends to increase sexual capacity" (ch.46, lines 693-695), as a food in a chapter that lists the flesh of many animals by their effect on the humours. The Codex does not recommend eating the flesh of a wild bovine, and the sentence on sexual capacity is not an instruction. The gaur is a threatened, protected species; the gayal is a semi-domesticated form kept in parts of north-east India. A persistent cough needs a clinician. The entry exists to record the text.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives no preparation of this flesh and no amount. The sentence describes the flesh as food and says nothing of how it is cooked or how often it is taken. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Cough has many causes and is assessed by clinicians, and no common meat is established to treat it or to raise sexual function. Sexual function depends on circulation, hormones, nerves and psychological state, and problems with it are assessed medically. Wild-animal meat can carry bacteria and parasites. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** For the animal itself the text prints: flesh demulcent; sweet in taste (*rasa*) and in digestion (*vipaka*); beneficial in cough; tends to increase sexual capacity (ch.46, lines 693-695). For the *Kulachara* group of which it is a member (lines 682-686) the text prints: spermatopoietic; destroys deranged *Vayu* and *Kapham*; sweet in taste and digestion; cooling; tonic; demulcent; diuretic; increases *Kapham*. The group qualities apply to the *gavaya* by group membership, which is an inference from the classification and not a sentence about the animal; the sentence about the animal gives no potency (*virya*) of its own.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The ch.46 frame is food as the source of strength, complexion and *ojas* (lines 8-12), but the chapter assigns *ojas* to food in general and not to this flesh. The group sentence calls the flesh of the *Kulachara* group "spermatopoietic" and "tonic" (lines 683-685), and the sentence about the *gavaya* says it "tends to increase sexual capacity". That is a property in a food list. It is not a placement of the *gavaya* in a *vajikarana* chapter, which is the formal Sushrutan discipline of generative vitality, and the whole Sushruta text on disk, searched for the name, finds it only in ch.46. So it is not read as a *vajikarana* status. The substance has only a dietary and therapeutic status. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. The sattvic and rajasic food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The sourced use is a dietary one for cough, with a remark on sexual capacity, in a food chapter, and the animal's identity is uncertain between two bovines. The Codex does not recommend taking the flesh of a protected wild animal. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** wild-animal meat can carry bacteria and parasites; it should be fully cooked, and wild-caught meat is not recommended.
- **Cough and sexual difficulties:** a cough that lasts, or sexual difficulty, needs a clinician; the text's statements are historical and not treatments.
- **Conservation and law:** the gaur is a threatened species protected by law in its range, and trade in it is restricted; check local law before any hunting or trade.
- **Identity:** the Sanskrit text names no species; the project list says gaur, and the translator's glossary says gayal.
- **Ethics:** the Codex does not recommend taking an animal for these uses.
