# Hyena — ضبع (ḍabʿ)

## *The hyena, as the Canon names it; the species is not given (the striped hyena,* Hyaena hyaena*, is the modern default for the region)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Avicenna (CM).** The hyena is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. This is a deliberately short entry, because the Book II entry itself is a cross-reference. It is read in the Arabic (`Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`, Book II, entry *ḍabʿ*, heading at Arabic text line 19481).

The whole entry reads: "*Al-khawāṣṣ*: we have mentioned in the third book the measure of the benefit from it for gout and joint pain, and we have no need to repeat it, so let the aim be sought from there" (قد ذكرنا في الكتاب الثالث مبلغ الانتفاع به من النقرس ووجع المفاصل ولا حاجة بنا أن نكرر ذلك فليطلب الغرض من هناك). The letter *ḍād* then closes with "seven items of medicines".

I did not locate or read the Book III chapter that Avicenna points to by name. What I did read are three places in the same text that use the hyena for joints, none of them labelled as that chapter, and each is therefore a side-evidence only: a bath in which a fox or hyena has been boiled "is the best treatment for those with joint pains and gout" (line 4257); a recipe that boils a live or slaughtered hyena and a fox "in their blood" until they fall apart, strains the water, and has the patient sit in it, or boils them down in oil (lines 45239 to 45243); and hyena fat among the external fats rubbed on (line 39766). The fox entry (III_335) is the sibling of this one.

**Identity.** Arabic *ḍabʿ* is the hyena; the *Canon* gives no species. Striped hyena is the usual species in the Arabic-speaking lands; this is modern knowledge, not a statement of the text. [USER: verify if a species is wanted.]

### Active Components

None is stated in the text. The fat is animal fat; nothing more specific is asserted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The single use is for gout and joint pain, as a bath or external fat. This is not a recommended practice and involves killing and boiling a wild carnivore.

### Preparation & Dosing

None in the Book II entry. The side-evidence preparations above are a boiled-animal bath and a rub with fat. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Historical record only. Wild hyenas are protected or declining in many countries and are carrion feeders that can carry disease; handling and boiling carcasses is a hygiene risk. Do not treat any use here as medical advice.
