# Rishavaka — Rishavaka (Sushruta Samhita); the text also prints "Rishabhaka"

## *A plant named Rishavaka in the Vidarigandhadi gana, and in several recipes; no Latin name and no description is printed in any passage read, so the identification is unresolved, at low confidence. The text prints a second spelling, Rishabhaka, in the Kakolyadi gana and in recipes; the two are probably one name, but that is our reading and not stated.* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry; the text is Bhishagratna's English translation (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`). The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the group list (Vol. I line 15357). The names file (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, line 12) records "group list (ch.38); no identification given here", and no Latin name was found for it in the passages read.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The Vidarigandhadi gana (Sutra ch. 38, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 10-22; Vol. I line 15357).* "The drugs known as Vidarigandha, Vidari, Sahadeva, Vishvadeva, Shvadanstra, Prithakparni, Shatavari, Sariva, black Sariva, Jivaka, Rishavaka, Mahasaha, Kshudra-Saha, Vrihati, Kantakari, Punarnava, Eranda, Hansapadi, Vrishchikah, and Rishavi, form the group known as the Vidari-gandhadi." The metrical text: "The present group of drugs subdues the action of the deranged Vayu and Pittam and proves beneficial in phthisis (Shosha), Gulma, aching of the limbs, Urdha Shvasa and cough." The action is the group's. Rishavaka stands next to Jivaka in this list, and Rishavi stands at the end of it (see `I_1753_Rishavi_SS.md`).

*Rishabhaka in the Kakolyadi gana (Sutra ch. 38, line 251).* "The Ka'kolya'di Group:— The drugs known as Kakoli-Kshira-Kakoli, Jivaka, Rishabhaka, Mudga-parni, Mashaparni, Meda, Mahameda, Chhinna-ruha, ..." The OCR prints the name with *b-h* here, and with *v* in the Vidarigandhadi list. Jivaka stands before it in both lists. The text does not say the two spellings are one drug; the likeness of the spelling and the neighbour make it likely, and [USER: verify].

*In recipes.* Chikitsa ch. 2 (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch02.txt` lines 219-221): a medicated ghee for use as an errhine is made of "Clarified butter prepared from goat's milk, Madhuka, Utpala, Jivaka and Rishavaka taken in equal parts ... pasted together, and cooked with sixteen seers of cow's milk and four seers of clarified butter." The quantities are for that recipe and are not a dose of Rishavaka. In Chikitsa ch. 37 (lines 72 and 111) Rishavaka is in the lists of two compound recipes (with Jivaka, Kakoli, Meda, Deva-daru and others). In Chikitsa ch. 5 (line 140) and ch. 38 (line 409), "Rishabhaka" is in a paste with Kakoli, Kshira-kakoli and Jivaka. A search of the Sutra, Kalpa and Uttara files found no further description. The "Rishabha'gada" (Kalpa ch. 5, lines 407-421) is a named antidote compound for snake poison; it is named after a Rishabha and is not shown here to contain Rishavaka.
