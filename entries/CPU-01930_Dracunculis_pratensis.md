# Dracunculis pratensis — Ptarmike (PTARMIKE, De Materia Medica 2-192), Sneezewort

## *Achillea ptarmica L. (sneezewort), a perennial of the daisy family, as the first modern name in the translator's list for Dioscorides' Ptarmike; "Dracunculis pratensis serrato folio" is a pre-Linnaean phrase name by Bauhin that the translator offers as a synonym, so the row name is not a Dioscoridean word; identification of medium confidence, since the translator also lists Achillea macrocephala and Ptarmika vulgaris and Dioscorides' description is short* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named in Dioscorides (DMM) only, as one of the translator's suggested names for chapter 2-192. This is a single-text entry.** The chapter is headed "2-192. PTARMIKE" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 6201). The translator's line below it reads: "SUGGESTED: Ptarmice, Pyrethrum sylvestre [Fuchs], Dracunculis pratensis serrato folio [Bauhin], Achillea ptarmica, Achillea macrocephala, Ptarmika vulgaris — Sneezewort, Bastard Pellitory" (lines 6202 and 6203). The row "Dracunculis pratensis" is the head of Bauhin's phrase name in that list. It also appears in the book's index (line 15587, "Dracunculis pratensis serrato folio 320"). The name must not be confused with the Dracunculus of chapters 2-196a and 2-196b (lines 6244 to 6254), which are the dragon arums, a different drug.

**Dioscorides' text (DMM).** The chapter is split by a plate across the page break (lines 6203 to 6216 of the file). It reads: "Ptarmica is a little shrub with many small round branches similar to southernwood, around which are many leaves — somewhat long, similar to those of the olive tree. On the top is a little head like anthemis arvensis [3-156], small, round, sharp according to the smell, causing sneezing, from which it is named. The leaves smeared on (with the flowers) are able to take away bruises below the eyes. The flowers induce sneezing most effectively. It grows in mountainous and rocky places." The chapter gives two uses: leaves with flowers smeared on to remove bruises below the eyes, and the flowers as an inducer of sneezing. The preceding chapter, 2-191 (Hydropiper, water pepper), is marked "SLIGHTLY POISONOUS" in the translator's heading; Ptarmike carries no such mark.

**Other source rows.** The name index `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` (row 382) and the project list `_sources/dioscorides.csv` (row 365: "Ptarmike, Pyrethrum sylvestre, plant, DMM II") both record the chapter. No other text on disk, among the Sushruta, Shennong, rGyud-bzhi, Ebers and Assyrian sources, was found to name the plant.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The flowers to cause sneezing and the leaves with flowers, smeared on, to take away bruises below the eyes.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation beyond smearing, no timing and no season.

The Codex gives no protocol. Sneezing powders are an irritant use and the Codex does not recommend one.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation Dioscorides gives is to smear on the leaves together with the flowers (line 6216). The flowers are said to cause sneezing, with no stated manner of use. **No dose is given in the sources retrieved.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Achillea ptarmica* is a European perennial whose common names (sneezewort, bastard pellitory) refer to the old use of the dried flower head as a sternutatory. Daisy-family plants may provoke contact dermatitis. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** Dioscorides prints no warming, cooling, drying or moistening quality for Ptarmike. The chapter calls the head "sharp according to the smell", and the book's running head over the chapter reads "HERBS WITH A SHARP QUALITY". These are sensory descriptions, not a four-quality classification.
- No other tradition is tagged, and none is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and the chapter assigns no cultivation status. What the chapter does offer is a discipline of attention: a morphological description by comparison (southernwood, olive leaf, the anthemis head), a habitat ("mountainous and rocky places") and a sensory assay (the sharp smell that causes sneezing, from which the drug takes its name). Recording a use is not endorsing it.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to cultivate here. The record is of a sneeze-inducing plant and a poultice for bruising. A cultivator can take from Dioscorides the habit of identifying a plant by its form, place and smell, and nothing more.

### Contraindications

- **Irritant.** Sneezing powders irritate the nose and eyes. Avoid any such use.
- **Identity.** The row name is a Renaissance phrase name; the translator lists three possible species. [USER: verify]
