# Isopyrum fumaroides — Isopuron (Dioscorides 4-121, "Isopyron"); "Fumitory-leaved Isopyron"

## *Isopyrum fumaroides [Bedevian], the first of two binomials on the translators' SUGGESTED line for chapter 4-121 (the second is Isopyrum thalictroides, "Meadow Rue-leaved Isopyron"), glossed "Fumitory-leaved Isopyron"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk names this plant under this name. The entry rests on one chapter of one text. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed under the chapter heading with the attribution [Bedevian], and not Dioscorides' own identification. The SUGGESTED line gives two *Isopyrum* species, and the entry takes the first. The Codex does not state the identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-121. ISOPURON* at line 9113, SUGGESTED lines at 9115 and 9116, the cross-reference "see 2-170, 2-176" at line 9117, text at line 9118. A page break (a plate caption for *Isopyrum fumaroides* after Faguet, 1888, and the page number 671) falls inside the last sentence, which resumes at line 9124. The chapter stands in Book Four, "Other Herbs & Roots", after 4-120 *ASTER ASTIKOS* and before 4-122 *ION* (violet), both different chapters. The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 701.

The whole chapter reads: "Isopyron bears a tendril towards the upper leaf. On the top of the stalk are thin little heads full of small seeds, similar to melanthium [3-93] according to the taste, but the leaf tastes like anise [3-65]. A decoction of the seed of this (taken as a drink with honey and water) helps disorders of the chest and coughs, and is good for bloodspitters, and liver disorders. Some call it phasiolum because it is similar to phasiolus." The square brackets are the translators'.

So the chapter gives a description for recognition (a tendril towards the upper leaf; thin little heads full of small seeds at the top of the stalk), two taste comparisons (the seeds like *melanthium*, chapter 3-93 *MELANTHION*; the leaf like anise, chapter 3-65 *ANISON*), one preparation (a seed decoction in honey and water) and its uses (disorders of the chest, coughs, bloodspitters, liver disorders), and one other name. The other name, *phasiolum*, is explained by a likeness to *phasiolus*, which is the name of chapter 2-130 *PHASIOLOS*, a different plant and not read for this entry. The chapter gives no habitat and no season.

**Overlap.** The chapter is already cited by the existing entry I_821 (Rue-leaved Isopyrum, *Isopyrum thalictroides*, tags DMM, CM), whose Dioscorides paragraph cites "De materia medica 4.121", summarises the same chapter and notes that the translators' gloss lists *I. fumaroides* first and *I. thalictroides* second, so that the identification is genus-level. This entry takes the first binomial on the same chapter, so two entries stand on one chapter by different binomials. The two are not merged, and the discrepancy is for the maintainer. The cross-references "see 2-170, 2-176" on the SUGGESTED line (chapters *EUZOMON* and *SMILAX*) are to other chapters and are not read here. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The text prints no constituent and no analysis of the plant Dioscorides describes was read. It is a general fact that *Isopyrum* is a genus of small perennial herbs of the buttercup family (Ranunculaceae), many members of which are generally known to be irritant or toxic when fresh. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the translators' *Isopyrum fumaroides*. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records a seed decoction in honey and water for chest disorders, coughs, spitting of blood and liver disorders. These are Dioscorides' claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol for spitting of blood, which needs urgent medical assessment, and the identity is a suggestion between two species.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives one preparation: "A decoction of the seed of this (taken as a drink with honey and water)". It prints no quantity, frequency or duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The identity is uncertain between two species, so a modern study of one would not bear directly on the Dioscorides drug. It is a general, well-established fact that coughing blood is a serious sign that needs medical assessment.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** chapter 4-121 prints no hot, cold, moist or dry quality. The only qualities printed are by taste comparison: the seeds "similar to melanthium according to the taste", and the leaf tastes "like anise".

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a sensory assay: the plant is to be told by tasting the seed against one known drug and the leaf against another, together with a field mark (the tendril towards the upper leaf). The chapter assigns the plant no cultivation status. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The transferable point is the taste test, which asks the reader to know melanthium and anise first. Tasting an unknown wild plant is not advised, however, and the text offers nothing more. The abstentive answer is the right one.

### Contraindications

Chapter 4-121 prints no warning. The identification is only the translators' suggestion between two species of a genus in a family that includes irritant and toxic plants, so no plant should be gathered, tasted or used on the strength of this entry. Coughs, spitting of blood and liver disorders need medical care. The text gives no dose, and no safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
