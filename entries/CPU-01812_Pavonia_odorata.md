### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The chapters name the drug only by Sanskrit name. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Tibetan text folders for *Pavonia* found no hit. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. Quotations keep the translator's words; the OCR's garbled diacritics are normalised. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** The row has two source rows in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, each keyed by a Sanskrit name as the OCR prints it: line 371, "Ambu" (appendix line 31178; Latin line 31231, "Pavonia odorata.", "paired by position, block of 31"), and line 472, "Hrivera" (appendix line 31759; Latin line 31777, "Pavonia odorata."). A third row, line 419, keys the Latin to an OCR line "Bskla" (appendix line 31387; Latin line 31446, "Pavonia Odorata."), which the extraction classes as "other"; it is not a row of this entry, but the Codex notes that the damaged name falls alphabetically between "Bala" and "Balga". The extraction's note says each pairing was proposed by an agent from the two OCR columns and checked by a script.

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The names in the chapters.* "Hrivera" is in the chapters in eight places, all in lists of drugs for a stated disease. "Ambu" is a word that also means water, and the chapters use it in more than one sense, so the two names are read separately.

*Hrivera in the Pitta-soothing group (Sutra 39, lines 99 to 110).* "Pitta-Samshamana-Varga — The drugs known as Chandana, Kuchandana, Hrivera, Ushira, Manjishtha, Payasya, Vidari, Shatavari, Gundra, Shaivala, Kahlara, Kumuda, Utpala, Kadali, Kandali, Durva, Murva, etc. and the drugs forming the groups of Kakolyadi, Sarivadi, Anjanadi, Utpaladi, Nyagrodhadi, and Trina-Panchamula groups generally prove soothing to the deranged Pittam."

*Hrivera in recipes.* In Chikitsa 4 (nervous disorders, *Vata-vyadhi*, lines 275 to 292), the *Kalyanaka-Lavana* is made from a long list of drugs "with their roots, leaves and twigs" including "Kadali, Varshabhu, Hrivera, Kshuraka, Indra-varuni, Svetamokshaka and Asoka", gathered green, mixed with rock-salt, thrashed in an *Udukhala*, burnt in a sealed pitcher, filtered and boiled in the manner of alkaline preparations. In Chikitsa 17, under "Treatment of Pittaja Visarpa" (lines 59 to 70), "a paste composed of Hrivera, Lamajjaka (Us'ira-mula), Chandana, Srotoja (Rasanjana), Mukta, Mani and Gairika, pasted together with milk and mixed with clarified butter should be applied thin and cool to the affected part to alleviate the pain." In Chikitsa 9 (skin diseases), a case of *Svitra* "would prove amenable to the application of peacock's bile, or of burnt Hrivera mixed with the said bile" (lines 354 to 356). In Uttara 30 (the chapter on an attack by Sakuni, lines 10 to 17) plasters "composed of Madhuka, Us'ira, Hrivera, Sariva, Utpala, Padmaka, Rodhra, Priyangu, Manjishtha and Gairika" are applied. In Uttara 40 (diarrhoea, lines 344 to 349), a decoction "of Yashti-madhu, Utpala, Vilva, Antra, Hrivera, Us'ira and Nagara mixed with honey proves curative" in the Pittaja type. In Uttara 39 (fever, lines 1640 to 1645), "Vomiting would yield to the use of a compound consisting of Madhuka flower, Hrivera, Utpala and Madhulika mixed with honey and clarified butter and used to be licked up with the tongue," and the text adds that it is "equally efficacious in water-brash, hiccough, Rakta-pitta (hemoptisis) and asthma." In Kalpa 8 (insect bites, lines 654 to 657), an *Agada* "composed of Priyangu, Hrivera, Kushtha, Lamajja, Vanjula, Shala-pushpa and the sprouts of the Pippala and the Vata trees" is the remedy for a bite by a spider of the *Ala-visha* class.

*Ambu.* The appendix prints "Ambu". The chapters print the word in five places, and in only one of them does Bhishagratna say it is a drug: Uttara 52 (cough, lines 182 to 185) reads "Milk duly boiled and cooked with the admixture of Draksha, Ambu (Valaka), Manjishtha and Puraf should be taken with honey," with the gloss "(Valaka)". A footnote at the head of the Uttara 11 file (lines 1 to 3) adds that in the first two compounds "some take 'Toya' and 'Ambu' for water. But Dallana having explained 'Toya' as 'Valaka' in a preceding compound we adopt that meaning of the word here also." Two more recipes carry the word without a gloss: Chikitsa 12, in the passage on the abscesses after Prameha (line 62, the *Dhanvantara-Ghrita*, "Bhallataka, Vilva, Ambu, roots of Pippali ..."), and Chikitsa 17 (line 83, the *Gauryadi Ghrita*, "Gauri, Yashti-madhu, Aravinda, Rodhra, Ambu, Rajadana ..."). In Sharira 2 (line 316) "(Ambu)" is "nutrient liquid i.e. chyle of digested food", a different sense. The drug *Valaka* ("Vdlaka") also occurs in Uttara 11 (lines 36 and 64), Uttara 40 (line 346), Uttara 47 and in the Eladi group of Sutra 38 (line 169, where the chapter gives no identification). A search of the appendix for a legible "Valaka" found none; the only candidate is the damaged line "Bskla" noted above. The link from Ambu, through Valaka, to *Pavonia odorata* therefore rests on the appendix's pairing of the word Ambu and on Bhishagratna's gloss in one recipe, and the Codex does not treat Valaka as attested for this plant. ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Pavonia* is a genus of the mallow family (Malvaceae). Nothing is stated here about the composition of the Sushruta drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Hrivera as a member of the Pitta-soothing group and as an ingredient of plasters, pastes, a decoction, an *Agada* (antidote), a lambative and a burnt-and-mixed application, and Ambu (Valaka) in a cough recipe. These are the text's statements about Sanskrit names, and the Latin name that the appendix attaches to them is the translator's, by position. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives these preparations, each with its locus. Chikitsa 4, lines 275 to 292: the green drug burnt with rock-salt in a sealed pitcher and filtered as an alkali. Chikitsa 17, lines 66 to 70: pasted with milk and ghee, applied thin and cool. Chikitsa 9, lines 354 to 356: burnt, mixed with peacock's bile. Uttara 30, lines 14 to 17: plasters. Uttara 40, lines 347 to 349: decoction with honey. Uttara 39, lines 1640 to 1645: licked with honey and ghee. Kalpa 8, lines 655 to 657: an *Agada*. Uttara 52, lines 182 to 185: milk boiled and cooked with it, taken with honey. No dose, part or quantity is given for the drug itself. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Sushruta terms (Pittam, *Visarpa*, *Svitra*, *Atisara*, *Ala-visha*) are not modern diagnoses or measured outcomes, so no modern finding can be set beside them. ### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text names Hrivera in the Pitta-soothing group and in recipes, and Ambu in a few recipes. A search of the chapter files for "Hrivera", "Hriv" and "Ambu" found them in none of the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) or the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30), so the text assigns the drug no rejuvenative or generative status in the lines retrieved. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The Latin identification is the appendix's. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read print no warning for the drug itself. Several uses are for conditions that need medical care (fever with vomiting, *Rakta-pitta* or bleeding from the lungs, a spider bite, diarrhoea in the weak, a child said to be possessed). The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
