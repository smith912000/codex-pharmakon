# Ceterach (scale fern) — شيطرج (shīṭaraj, as printed in the Arabic *Canon*)

## *Plain English label "Ceterach (scale fern)" is the spine's name; the Canon's description does not support a fern (see identity note), so the material is here described only as the text describes it: a pungent Indian drug of thin wood and bark pieces, and a wall-growing plant with cress-like leaves* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Avicenna (CM).** This substance is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. It is read here in the Arabic (`Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`, Book II, entry *shīṭaraj*, heading at Arabic text line 18094).

Under *al-māhiyya* (what it is) Avicenna says: "the Indian kind is cut pieces of wood, small and thin, and bark like the bark of cinnamon; broken, it is toward red and black" (الهندي منه قطاع خشب صغار دقاق وقشور كقشور الدارصيني والمكسر إلى الحمرة والسواد). It "grows on old walls and where it does not snow", and "has leaves like the leaves of *ḥirf*" (garden cress); "in summer it has many leaves, then it grows smaller and smaller until it can hardly be seen". The same passage then says "there is no smell in it" and that "its taste and smell resemble *qardamānā* and its power is like it" (طعمه ورائحته تشبه القردمانا وقوته مثله). The text is not self-consistent on smell (none, and then like *qardamānā*); no reconciliation is attempted here.

**Identity doubt.** The spine row calls the entry "Ceterach (scale fern)". That label is ours and modern. Nothing in Avicenna's description fits a fern: he gives wood and bark pieces, leaves like cress, and a pungent, blistering taste. The identification of *shīṭaraj* with the fern genus *Ceterach* is therefore not supported by the text; the entry is kept under the spine's name and the doubt is listed. A pungent cress-like drug is the more natural reading of the description, but no identification beyond the text is asserted here. ### Active Components

The identity of the plant is uncertain, so no composition can be stated for it. The text itself describes only taste and action: a taste and strength like *qardamānā* (a pungent, cress-type drug) and a cleansing, blistering action. Pungent, blistering plant drugs generally owe that action to irritant compounds, but which compounds apply here cannot be said without an identification. No chemistry is asserted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The uses below are what Avicenna reports; none is offered as a recommended practice, and the identity of the drug is uncertain.

- Skin (*al-jirāḥ wa-l-qurūḥ*, wounds and sores): applied with vinegar on scaling (*taqashshur*) and scab or mange (*jarab*), "it removes it" (فيقلعه).
- Joints (*ālāt al-mafāṣil*): "it is drunk for pain of the joints and benefits greatly" (يشرب لوجع المفاصل فينفع نفعا بليغا).
- Spleen (*aʿḍāʾ al-ghidhāʾ*): smeared on the spleen, "it shrinks it" (فيضمره).
- Bladder (*aʿḍāʾ al-nafḍ*): "if its root is hung on the ear of one with bladder pain it calms it, as is said" (فيما يقال). Avicenna passes this on as hearsay, not as his own finding.

### Preparation & Dosing

Preparations given: a topical paste in vinegar for scales and scab; a drink for joint pain (no vehicle or amount stated); a smear for the spleen; the root hung on the ear (an amuletic use reported with "as is said"). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Avicenna (CM).** *Al-ṭabʿ* (temperament): "hot, dry at the end of the second degree" (حار يابس في آخر الثانية). *Al-khawāṣṣ* (actions): "cleansing, blistering" (جال مقرح). *Al-abdāl* (substitute): "its substitute is its like of *fuwwa*" (madder), i.e. madder in equal measure (بدله مثله فوة).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The *Canon* entry is organised by temperament and by the complaint treated (scab, joint pain, spleen, bladder pain). It assigns the drug no place in the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture, and no strengthening of the *ruḥ*. Nothing in the entry can be developed into a cultivation claim without inventing one. The tradition's strong cultivation idea, longevity as the conservation of a finite endowment, is not attached to this substance in the text read.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this entry. It is a drying, cleansing, blistering drug recorded for skin, joint and spleen complaints, its identity is uncertain, and it is not a drug to be taken for the sake of vitality.

### Contraindications

Historical record only. The drug is described as hot and dry to the end of the second degree and as blistering (*muqarriḥ*), so applications to the skin may irritate or blister it. The identity of the plant is uncertain, so safety cannot be judged from the name; no traditional safety statement is given in the entry read. Do not treat any use here as medical advice.
