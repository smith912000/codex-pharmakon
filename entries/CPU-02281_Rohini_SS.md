# Rohini — Rohini (Sushruta Samhita)

## *A plant, or perhaps several, named Rohini in the Sushruta Samhita: a member of the Nyagrodhadi gana, a bitter-group drug, a bark and a fruit in recipes, and a pot-herb. The translator's footnote gives two readings (a kind of Haritaki, or Katu-tumbi); no Latin name is printed. The identification is unresolved, at low confidence. Rohini is not Katurohini, which has its own entry.* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry; the text is Bhishagratna's English translation (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`). The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the group list (Vol. I line 15689). A first-pass check said "unresolved (unknown)". The names file (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, line 271) records "no identification given here". The name Rohini is also the text's name for a disease and for other things, and the search below separates them.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The Nyagrodhadi gana (Sutra ch. 38, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 345-352; Vol. I line 15689).* "The Nya'grodha'di Group:— The drugs known as Nyagrodha, Audumvara, Ashvattha, Plaksha, Madhuka, Kapitana, Kakubha, Amra, Koshamra, Chorakapatra, the two sorts of Jamvu, Piyala, Madhuka (Maula), Rohini, Vanjula, Kadamva, Vadari, Tinduki, Sallaki, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Bhallataka, Palasha, and Nandi-Vriksha, combinedly form the group known as the Nyagrodhadi Gana." The metrical text: "This group proves beneficial in cases of ulcer, cures all disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones and all sorts of secretions of the body in addition to its astringent properties (Sangrahi) and proves curative in a case of haemoptysis. It is an anti-fat and assuages the burning sensation of the body." The action is the group's.

*The bitter group (Sutra ch. 42, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` lines 339-345).* Among the drugs "collectively" forming the bitter group: "... Karavira, Sumanah, Sankha-pushpi, Apamarga, Trayamana, Ashoka, Rohini, Vaijayanti, Suvarchala, Punarnava, Vrishikali and Jyotishmati, etc." Here Rohini is placed among bitter drugs, while the Nyagrodhadi gana is listed in the same chapter's astringent group (line 350). The text does not reconcile the two placements, and they may be different plants or the same one. [USER: verify]

*Bark (Chikitsa ch. 1, `chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch01.txt` lines 723-730).* In the hardening measure (Daruna-karma) for soft ulcers: "Barks of Dhava, Priyangu, Ashoka, Rohini, Triphala, Dhataki flowers, Lodhra and Sarjarasa, taken in equal parts and pounded into fine powders, should be strewn over the ulcer".

*Fruit, and the footnote (Chikitsa ch. 1, lines 786-814).* For the Pandu-karana (restoring the natural skin colour to a black scar): "The fruit of the Rohini should be immersed in goat's milk for seven nights and, afterwards finely pasted with the same milk, should be applied to the skin." Bhishagratna's footnote to this line reads: "Rohini, according to some commentators, means a kind of Haritaki; according to others, it means Katu-tumbi." That footnote attaches to the fruit in this recipe; it is not said to apply to the Rohini of the gana or of the bitter group.

*Pot-herb (Sutra ch. 20, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch20.txt` lines 141-143).* In the list of incompatible foods: "The pot-herbs, known as the Rohini and the Jatu-shaka, should not be partaken of in combination with milk and honey".

*Not this entry.* "Rohini" is also the name of a disease of the throat and mouth (Sutra ch. 11 line 64 and ch. 25 line 40; Nidana ch. 16, lines 294-305, glossed "Diphtheria" by Bhishagratna), the sixth layer of the skin (Sharira ch. 4, line 56), and in the forms "Agni-Rohini", "Katu-rohini" and "Tikta-rohini" other things. Katurohini is the drug of `I_408_Katurohini_Picrorhiza_kurroa.md`. These are not treated here.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Nyagrodhadi gana: ulcers, disorders of the uterus and vagina, fractures, secretions, haemoptysis, fat and burning of the body (group action). The bark is a dusting powder for soft ulcers; the fruit is pasted on a scar to restore colour. Among the foods, a Rohini pot-herb is not to be combined with milk and honey.
- **What the text does not say.** What the plant is, where it grows, or whether the gana, bitter-group, bark, fruit and pot-herb Rohinis are one plant.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend any plant under this name.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the text gives are the bark powder strewn on ulcers (Chikitsa ch. 1, "in equal parts") and the fruit soaked seven nights in goat's milk and pasted (Chikitsa ch. 1). No dose is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Rohini itself. The Nyagrodhadi gana is astringent (Sangrahi) and reduces fat and burning (Sutra ch. 38); the bitter group lists Rohini under bitter drugs (Sutra ch. 42). Both are group statements.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda (SS).** *(c) Absent.* Rohini appears in a gana for ulcers, fractures and haemoptysis, in the bitter group, as bark and fruit in wound recipes, and as a pot-herb with a food-combination warning. No rasayana or vajikarana chapter names it among the passages read, and no ojas, tejas or prana status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A name that the text uses for several things and a plant it does not identify. For a cultivator the only point of practice in the record is the dietary one, that a Rohini pot-herb is not to be combined with milk and honey, and even that cannot be applied while the plant is unknown.
