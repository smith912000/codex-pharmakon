### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Vanjula [SS]", taken from an extraction of Bhishagratna's translation; this entry replaces it. The locus given was "SS Sutra 38, Nya'grodha'di group (Vol. I line 15689)".

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Vanjula is printed in the Sutrasthana, chapter 38 (locus: Nya'grodha'di group, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 348; the same text is in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, line 15689). Chapter 38 is "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3-6), and says that "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (lines 8-10). The name is given by the translator without a gloss, a botanical name or a description.

In the Nya'grodha'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 346-351 (the list), lines 353-361 (the metrical text)), Vanjula is one of the drugs listed. The group's metrical text reads: "This group proves beneficial in cases of ulcer, cures all disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones and all sorts of secretions of the body in addition to its astringent properties (Sangrahi) and proves curative in a case of haemoptysis. It is an anti-fat and assuages the burning sensation of the body." That is a property of the group as a whole, not of Vanjula alone.

**Chikitsasthana ch.5 (line 205).** In a plaster (Pradeha) recipe the drugs are printed "S'dli, Shashtika, Nala, Vanjula, Tdlis'a, S'rigdtaka, Galodya, Haridrd, Gairika, S'aivala, Padma-kashtha, leaves of padma (lotus), pasted with Dhdnydmla and mixed with clarified butter", to be applied to the affected part, and the text adds that this plaster "may be applied lukewarm even in cases of Vdta-Rakta, marked by a preponderance of the aggravated Vayu" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt` lines 205-210).

**Kalpasthana ch.8 (lines 632 and 656).** The chapter gives antidotes (Agadas) for spider bites. Vanjula is in the Agada for the bite of the Svetaka spider ("Chandana, Rdsnd, Eld, Harenu, Nala, Vanjula, Kushtha, ...", lines 632-633; the bite is described at lines 628-631) and in the Agada for the bite of the Ala-visha spider ("Priyangu, Hrivera, Kushtha, Lamajja, Vanjula, S'ala-pushpd and the sprouts of the Pippala and the Vata trees", lines 655-657) (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch08.txt`). The e-text of the Svetaka list is cut by a page break and the list is continued on the next page. [USER: verify the full ingredient list against the printed page]

The chapter closes (lines 554-566) by saying that the groups "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", and that plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, Ghritas or potions are to be prepared "according to the exigencies of each individual case". That is a general rule of the chapter and is not a recipe for this drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The text uses it in the Nya'grodha'di group (said to help ulcers, uterine and vaginal disorders, the union of fractured bones, haemoptysis and burning of the body), in a plaster that may be used even in Vata-Rakta, and in two Agadas against spider bites.
- What the text does not say: what Vanjula is, which part is used, its taste or potency, or any dose.

The Codex does not translate the text's terms (Vayu, Pitta, Kapham, Gulma, Shosha, Asmari and the rest) into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend a plant it cannot identify.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Vanjula stands in the dravya-group classification of Sutrasthana ch.38, a classification by therapeutic action. The Nya'grodha'di group is a group of astringent, ulcer-healing and bone-uniting drugs, not a rasayana or vajikarana group. A search of the chapter files on disk for the spellings printed here found no mention of Vanjula in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters, and no text read calls it an elixir or a drug of vitality. The register is therefore (b): framed by its therapeutic group, with no cultivation status attested. The Codex does not assign *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* effects to it, because no source read does.

**Practitioner's note.** Vanjula is a name in a list. The tradition's text gives the drug a place among therapeutic groups and little more, and nothing in the record supports a cultivation use. A cultivator can learn from it only how Sushruta organises drugs into groups by action, and that some names in that classification are no longer certainly identified. Nobody should try to find a plant to match the name.
