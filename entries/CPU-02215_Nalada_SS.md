# Nalada — Nalada (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *A drug of the Anjanadi group whose botanical identity is not settled in the text read: the translator's glossary refers it to Usira, while bracketed glosses elsewhere in his translation give "fatamansi", "Mansi" and "Khus-khus". Identification: unresolved. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not identified with confidence. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The first-pass guess was "possibly *Nardostachys jatamansi*"; the reading below shows why that guess is one of three the text leaves open.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

1. **Group list.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38, the Anjanadi group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15641 to 15645; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 300 to 303). Bhishagratna prints: "The group known as the Anjanadi Gana consists of Anjana, Rasanjana, Nagapushpa, Priyangu, Nilotpala, Nalada, Nalina, Keshara and Madhuka." The chapter opens by saying that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (ch.38, lines 3 to 9).
2. **The group's virtue.** The metrical text gives it for the group as a whole: "An attack of haemoptysis readily yields to the curative virtue of the group under discussion. It is anti-toxic in its character and allays the internal burning sensation of the body" (Vol. I lines 15646 to 15650). This is the group's virtue, not a statement about Nalada alone.
3. **The translator's glossary.** Bhishagratna's glossary in Vol. III (Appendix 3, line 32374; the identification column at line 32434) prints, against "Nalada", the cross-reference *See "Us'ira"* and no botanical name of its own. Usira is a separate name in the text, and the Codex has its own entry for it (Canonical ID CPU-00365, *Ushira*).
4. **Bracketed glosses elsewhere in the translation, which disagree.** *Chikitsasthana* ch.2 (`text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch02.txt` lines 489 to 492) lists "Nalada (fatamansi)" and, a few words later, "Usira" as two separate ingredients of one medicated oil. *Uttarasthana* ch.39 (`text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt` line 1635) prints "Nalada (Mansi)" in a plaster. A footnote in *Uttarasthana* ch.31 (`text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch31.txt` line 37) records an addition found in the *Bhavaprakasha* that names "Nalada (Khus-khus)". "Mansi" and "fatamansi" are the translator's rendering of the name of spikenard (jatamansi); "khus-khus" is the common name of the vetiver root. The translator therefore glosses the one word both ways, and the text on disk does not say which is right. [USER: verify against the printed pages]

Other places the name stands, listed without any further reading of them: *Kalpasthana* ch.1 line 190 (a preparation of Kushtha, asafoetida, Nalada and honey, used as an eye-salve and a snuff against the vapours of poisoned food), *Kalpasthana* ch.8 line 637 (an ingredient in a compound said to be "efficacious in such a case", in a chapter on bites; the case itself was not read), and *Uttarasthana* ch.39 line 1314 ("Nalada and Padma" among the drugs of a ghrita named Maha-Kalyanaka).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unresolved identity.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Anjanadi group: haemoptysis, poisoning and internal burning (group level). In the recipes above it appears as an eye-salve and snuff against poisoned-food vapours, in a compound in the bites chapter, in a plaster and in medicated oils and ghee.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no taste or potency for this drug alone, and no single identification.

The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no plant for any of the conditions named.

### Preparation & Dosing

The recipes named above are the only preparations found, and each is a compound; none is a dose for Nalada. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any recipe from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Nalada in the lines found. The Anjanadi group's virtue (anti-toxic; allaying burning; curative in haemoptysis) belongs to the group and is not imported for this drug.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in a chapter 38 group list, which classifies drugs by the use of the whole group (*Dravya-Sangrahaniya*). Chapter 38 is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns this drug no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only, and only at the level of its group. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it.

**Practitioner's note.** A name whose identity the translator himself gives two ways. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without an identification nothing can be gathered or used.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is unresolved. Do not substitute spikenard, vetiver or any other herb for it.
- **The conditions named.** Haemoptysis, poisoning and burning sensations are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Group membership.** The drug is one of nine in its group, and the group's virtues are not safety statements about any one member.
