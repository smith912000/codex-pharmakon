**Avicenna, Canon (CM), quoting Dioscorides.** The entry is *sirīsh* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text line 16398, to line 16416). It opens: قال ديسقوريدوس: يسميه بعض الناس سريش إذ هو نبات يتخذ منه السريش معروف (16399-16402): "Dioscorides said: some people call it *sirīsh*, since it is a plant from which the *sirīsh* [glue] is made, [and it is] known." Leaf like the leaf of the Syrian leek (*kurrāth*), smooth stem, at its tip a flower called *anbārīqūn*; long round roots like the shape of large acorns (*ballūṭ*), "and their power is hot" (16402-16403). Temperament: حار في الأولى, "hot in the first degree" (16404); property: مسخن, "warming" (16404).

**Uses (16404-16416).** Swellings: mixed with barley meal (*sawīq*) it helps hot swellings at their start. Wounds: as a poultice for dirty malignant ulcers, for ulcerating wounds and boils, and for burns from fire. Cosmetic: its ash, as a poultice, grows hair in alopecia (*dāʾ al-thaʿlab*) after the place is rubbed with a wool cloth; for white vitiligo (*bahaq*) rubbed with a cloth in the sun, the root with vinegar is smeared on and "removes it". Head: alone, or mixed with frankincense, honey, wine, myrrh and a further ingredient, and dripped into the ear on the side opposite the aching tooth, it calms the pain; the water of its root with old sweet wine and myrrh, and the same compound, is "an excellent remedy for painting on various eye pains". Chest: *mithqālān* (two mithqals) with *ṭilāʾ* (boiled-down wine) helps pain of the two sides, cough and weakness of the muscle; the root boiled in wine lees, as a poultice, is "very" helpful for breast swellings. Excretion: one mithqal with *ṭilāʾ* promotes urine and menses. Poisons: three mithqals drunk help bites of vermin; the leaf as a poultice also; the fruit and flower drunk in wine are "of great benefit" against scorpion sting. The line closes the letter Sīn with a count of 52 drugs (16416-16417).

**Not the other glue rows.** The spine has Lithocolla (CPU-02681) and the Dioscorides glue rows KOLLA and ICHTHUOKOLLA (CPU-02167, CPU-02133). They are a mineral glue and animal glues, not this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

Doses printed (16412-16415), from the Canon quoting Dioscorides: two *mithqāl* drunk with *ṭilāʾ* for pain of the sides, cough and muscle weakness; one *mithqāl* with *ṭilāʾ* to promote urine and menses; three *mithqāl* for bites of vermin. The weight of a *mithqāl* is not stated in the passage; the plant part for the internal doses is the root ("water of its root" is the eye preparation). Do not use these doses. [USER: verify]

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Sirish, quoted from Dioscorides by the Canon, is hot in the first degree and warming. Its root and leaf are used externally for hot swellings at their start, for dirty malignant ulcers, ulcerating wounds, boils and fire burns. An ash poultice is used for hair loss (alopecia), and for white vitiligo (bahaq) the root with vinegar is smeared on. For the head, a compound dripped into the ear on the side opposite an aching tooth is said to calm pain, and the root water with old wine and myrrh is for eye pains. For the chest, it is said to help pain of the two sides, cough and weakness of the muscle, and the root in wine lees helps breast swellings. Internally it promotes urine and menses. The leaf is used for vermin bites and the fruit and flower in wine for scorpion sting. The plant is unidentified, so no constituent can be assigned.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy.** The passage says it promotes urine and the menses; avoid in pregnancy. [USER: verify]
- **Skin.** The root is a skin-applied irritant in the passage; do not apply to broken skin on the strength of this entry.
- **Antidote use.** No evidence; do not rely on a plant as an antidote for stings or bites.
- **Identity.** Unknown; do not substitute.
