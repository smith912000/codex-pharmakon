# Paulownia leaf and flower — 桐叶 *tongye* (Shennong bencao jing)

## *The leaf, and in a second clause the flower, of a tree called tong; our identification (high confidence) is Paulownia tomentosa from the standard modern identification, but Sun's own note glosses tong as "wutong", which in modern usage names a different tree, so the identification is a point of doubt* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Shennong bencao jing (SBJ).** The *tong* leaf is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The identification as *Paulownia tomentosa* is ours. The source is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`); the entry is in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 桐叶 *tongye*, same file, line 3689).

**The Shennong text proper** (line 3689): "Flavour bitter, cold. It governs malignant eroding sores and sores that cling to the skin of the yin parts; it governs the five haemorrhoids and kills the three worms. The flower: it governs [applying to] pig sores; fed to pigs, it makes them three times fatter and larger. It grows in mountain valleys." (味苦，寒。主恶蚀、创着阴皮，主五痔，杀三虫。花：主传猪创，饲猪，肥大三倍。生山谷。) The file prints 传 in "主传猪创"; "apply to" is the sense the context requires (the next entry, 梓白皮, has the same clause), and the character may be a copying variant of 傅. [USER: verify] The paragraph therefore has two uses for the leaf (human sores and haemorrhoids, worms) and a separate veterinary use for the flower.

**The Mingyi bielu layer** (line 3691): "Grows at Mount Tongbai" (生桐柏山). That is all it adds.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 3693).** Sun quotes the *Shuowen* ("*tong* is *rong*"; "*wu*, the *wutong* tree, also called *zi*"), the *Erya* with Guo Pu: "*chenwu*, now the *wutong*", and "the *rong tong* tree, Guo Pu: that is *wutong*", and the *Mao shi* with its commentary: "the *wutong* is a soft tree" (梧桐，柔木也). So Sun's note leads from *tong* to *wutong*. In modern usage *wutong* usually names *Firmiana simplex* (the Chinese parasol tree), a different genus from *Paulownia*, while the tree sold in Chinese pharmacy as *tong* leaf is taken to be *Paulownia*. I did not open a botanical source to settle this. The identification here follows the standard modern reading of the drug name, and the doubt is recorded. [USER: verify]

**Preface.** The lower class is "the left-hand envoy, mainly treating disease to correspond to earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken for long" (line 2935).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, the leaves and flowers of *Paulownia* trees, like most flowering plants, contain flavonoids, glycosides and phenolic acids, and the wood is light. No analytical source for the leaf was retrieved, so no compound is named. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records the leaf for malignant eroding sores and sores on the skin of the yin parts, for the "five haemorrhoids", and for killing the three worms. The flower is recorded for pig sores and for fattening pigs. The pig-fattening clause is a farming remark and is not a human use. The Codex does not turn the human uses into modern indications. Sores that erode, and haemorrhoids, need diagnosis.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph gives no preparation and no dose (leaf: none; flower: applied to pig sores, or fed to pigs, in an amount not stated). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

*Paulownia tomentosa* is a fast-growing ornamental and timber tree with large leaves and spring flowers, and it is a pest in some regions outside Asia. Whether the leaf acts on sores or haemorrhoids has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong).** One flavour and one nature are printed: bitter (苦), cold (寒). Lower class by placement. No further quality is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry is in the lower classic, which the preface (line 2935) describes as mainly for treating disease, mostly toxic and not for long use. The paragraph has no *jiu fu* ("taken for long") clause and no life-nourishing wording, and one of its clauses is about fattening pigs. Nothing in it bears on the person's constitution or life. The status is the lower class's: a remedy for a defined complaint, not for long use.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing for a cultivator here. The leaf is a lower-class remedy for sores and haemorrhoids, with no dose, and the flower clause concerns pigs. Do nothing with this entry beyond reading it.

### Contraindications

- **Identification.** The tree called *tong* is not settled by the text, and Sun's note points to *wutong*. Do not use a field-collected leaf on the basis of a name.
- **Toxicity.** Lower-class drugs are "mostly toxic" and not for long use (line 2935). No safety data were retrieved for the leaf. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, children, medicines.** The text says nothing; no use is supported. [USER: verify]
- **Wounds.** Sores that erode the skin may be infected or malignant and need medical assessment before any herb is applied.
- **Livestock.** The pig clause is historical record only and is not a feeding recommendation.
