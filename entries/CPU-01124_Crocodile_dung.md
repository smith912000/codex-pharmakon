# Crocodile dung — not printed in Bryan's English; the source-list row stands alone, and the nearest printed item is "Crocodile-earth"
## *Crocodile dung as a remedy attributed to the Papyrus Ebers by a source-list row. The row `_sources/ebers.csv` line 79 gives the binomial "Crocodylus niloticus" and the note "Ebers (contraceptive)"; neither the binomial nor the contraceptive use appears in Bryan's 1930 English, which is the Ebers text on disk* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The only Ebers text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 *The Papyrus Ebers*, a translation with summaries (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). The Egyptian words are not printed. The attestation for this entry rests on one list row, `_sources/ebers.csv` line 79: "Crocodile dung, Crocodylus niloticus, animal, Ebers (contraceptive); Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus". The row's own source column names Wikipedia, which the Codex does not rely on (CULTIVATION_REGISTER_SPEC.md, trap 4), so the row is a pointer and not a locus. The same list has a separate row for the animal itself, "Crocodile" (line 107, from Ghalioungui's glossary), and one for "Crocodile fat" (line 71), neither of which is the dung.

What Bryan's English says, which I searched and read:

- **No "Crocodile dung" is printed.** A search of the Bryan text for "crocodile" returns fourteen lines, and none of them reads "Crocodile dung" or "Dung-of-the-Crocodile". A search for "contracept", "conception" and "become pregnant" returns nothing. I could not find the contraceptive use in Bryan's text. [USER: verify]
- **Bryan's list of animal parts includes "Excrement".** In Chapter VII, "Organic Remedies", after his list of animals (which includes "Crocodile", line 1392), Bryan lists the substances the Egyptians "extracted from them": "Hair Feathers Horns Hoof Hide Eyes Ears Teeth Brain Blood Bile Bones Marrow Liver Spleen Guts Uterus Vulva Phallus Testicles Semen Fat Tallow Flesh Shell Wings Excrement Urine Eggs Milk Toes Tail" (lines 1408-1415). The list is general and is not tied to any one animal. Bryan then says the Ass "sacrificed his blood, dung, ears, fat..." (lines 1428-1429), the Cow gave "her bile, brain, blood, fat, flesh, horns, liver, milk, spleen, and tallow", and the Scribe notes that "the gods contributed their excrement in the sacred cause". The crocodile is not given a parts list.
- **"Crocodile-earth" is printed, four times.** It is an ingredient in a headache poultice, "Crocodile-earth beaten up with Egeg-of-the-Ostrich" (line 2112), in a cataract remedy with "Lapis lazuli, Milk, Incense, and Crocodile-earth" (line 2824), in a poultice for pterygium, "Black Knife-stone and Crocodile-earth, mixed up with Honey and flavoured with Incense" (line 2844), and in a remedy for grey eyebrows, "Crocodile-earth-mixed-with-Honey-which-has-been-dissolved-in-Onion-water" (line 4058). Bryan does not say what it is. Whether "Crocodile-earth" is crocodile dung, a mineral, or an earth named after the animal is not stated in any line read. I do not equate it with the list row's "Crocodile dung". [USER: verify]
- **Other crocodile lines.** Crocodile oil in a limb liniment (line 2181), "Fat-of-the-Crocodile" in a hair-restorer (line 4027), raw meat on a crocodile bite (lines 2211-2213), and a recitation that says "THE CROCODILE IS WEAK AND POWERLESS" (line 2978). These concern other crocodile materials or the animal itself. The entry III_65 Crocodile records them in full.

So the honest statement is: in the Ebers text on disk, the dung of the crocodile is not printed as such. The list row is the only reason the entry exists, and its basis is a secondary web page that was not opened.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, the droppings of any carnivorous reptile are a mixture of undigested remains (bone, keratin, hair), uric acid and urate salts, bacteria and water. Nothing more specific can be said without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice, and in this case the record itself is thin. The Ebers text on disk does not print crocodile dung, so the Codex cannot say what the Egyptian physicians did with it. The Codex does not recommend any use of crocodilian droppings, as a contraceptive or otherwise: faeces carry infection risk, contraception by an unverified animal product is unreliable and can lead to unintended pregnancy, and the animal is protected in many places.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is printed for crocodile dung. For "Crocodile-earth", whose identity is unsettled, Bryan reports mixtures with ostrich egg, with lapis lazuli, milk and incense, with black knife-stone, honey and incense, and with honey in onion water (lines 2112, 2824, 2844, 4058), and the lines read give no quantity. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. There is no established evidence that crocodile faeces, or any animal faeces, act as a contraceptive when applied or taken; the contraceptive effect of a method has to be shown by trial. Reptile faeces can carry *Salmonella* and other pathogens. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Infection and hygiene:** faeces of a wild reptile carry *Salmonella* and other bacteria; do not ingest, insert or apply any.
- **Contraception:** do not rely on any animal product to prevent pregnancy; use a method of established effectiveness.
- **Identity:** the use is unverified against the text, and "Crocodile-earth" is not identified, so no safe use of either can be stated.
- **Law and conservation:** crocodilians are protected or trade-controlled in most jurisdictions (the Nile crocodile among them), and the Codex does not recommend sourcing any part.
- **Ethics:** the Codex does not recommend harming wild animals for these uses.
