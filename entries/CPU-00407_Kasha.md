## *Saccharum Spontaneum* (as printed, in OCR form, against the garbled name "Esbs'a" in Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 glossary; the spine row carries *Saccharum spontaneum* L.) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** The spine names this grass *Saccharum spontaneum*, and the Latin printed in the glossary agrees. The weak point is the Sanskrit side. The glossary's name line is damaged ("Esbs'a", line 31940), and it is read as *Kasha* only from its position between *Karpura* and *Kasa-marda* in an alphabetical list, which is an inference. [USER: verify] The word is also a lookalike in English: *Kasa* (cough, as in the chapter title at Uttara ch.52, `SS_Uttara_ch52.txt`, line 6), *Kasa-marda* (a drug that the glossary pairs with "Cassia Sophera.", line 32023) and *Kashaya* (a decoction, Chikitsa ch.31, line 140) are different things and are not treated here. The finished entry `I_472_Sugarcane_Saccharum_officinarum.md` covers a different species of the genus and does not cover this grass. Two other grasses of the same group have entries (`I_914_Kusha.md`, `I_916_Nala.md`). A search of Volume I found no earlier entry for this grass.

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

*The ganas (Sutra ch.38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`).* Kasha stands in two groups.

- The Viratarvadi group (lines 57 to 64): "The drugs known as Virataru, the two kinds of Sahachara, Darbha, Vrikshadani, Gundra, I\'ala [OCR, read as Nala], Kusha, Kasha, Ashmabhedaka, Agnimantha, Morata Vasuka, Vasira, Bhalluka, Kuruntaka, Indivara, Kapotavanka, and Shvadanstra enter into the composition of the group known as the Viratarvadi." The metrical text (lines 66 to 73) says the group "subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles."
- The Pancha-Trina group (lines 531 to 539): "The group consisting of the five medicinal herbs (of the grass species) and known as Kusha, Kasha, Nala, Darbha, Kandekshuka, is called the Pancha-Trina." The metrical text reads "Cases of Haemoptysis, renal defects or of uninary diseases are found to speedily yield to the curative efficacy of the compound internally administered through the medium of cow's milk." The summary at the end of the chapter (lines 545 to 552) says that the Trina-Panchamula, "standing in the bottom of the list", "is endued with the property of killing the deranged Pittam".

Membership of these groups is a grouping by therapeutic action, grade (b) at most.

*Appendix 3 glossary (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`).* The page of K-names lists, in order, "Karpasi" (line 31936, OCR garbled), "Karpura" (line 31938), "Esbs'a" (line 31940), "Kasa-marda" (line 31942) and "Ka'smari" (line 31944). The page's Latin block, counted down from its first line (line 31992, "Bauhinia Variegata." for *Kanchana*), reaches "Gossypium Harbeceum." (line 32017), "Cinamomum Camphora." (line 32019), "Soccharum Spontaneum." (line 32021, OCR) and "Cossia Saphora." (line 32023). The seventeenth name on the page is therefore the damaged one, and its Latin is "Soccharum Spontaneum." The Latin entries around it fit the neighbouring names (cotton, camphor, *Cassia sophera*), and the count was checked from both ends of the block and agrees. The pairing is by OCR column position, not by a printed link. The glossary prints "Saccharum Munja" at line 32327 against another name and "Saccharum ofHcinarum. Sugarcane." against *Ikshu* (line 31781), so it separates three species of the genus.

*Passages read, in the order of the book.*

- **Sutra ch.6** (`SS_Sutra_ch06.txt`, lines 366 to 376), the description of the seasons, in autumn: "The level plains are covered with shrubs and undergrowths, and plants and trees such as, Vana, Saptahva, Vandhuka, Kasha and Asana, flower in abundance." This is a seasonal marker for the flowering of the grass and gives it no use.
- **Sutra ch.39** (`SS_Sutra_ch39.txt`, lines 20 to 36), the purgative drugs: "... Chitraka, Kinihi, Kusha, Kasha, Tilvaka, Kampillaka, Ranwaka, Patala, Puga, Haritaki ... are possessed of purgative properties. Of these the roots of plants, which precede Tilvaka in the above list, should be used for purgative purposes." Kasha precedes Tilvaka, so its root is the part named. The Pitta-soothing list of the same chapter closes with the Trina-Panchamula group (lines 108 to 110).
- **Chikitsa ch.7** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch07.txt`, lines 38 to 56), the treatment of Pittaja *Asmari*: a ghee "cooked with the decoction of Kus'a, Kas'a, S'ara, Gundra, Itkata, Morata ..." together with a paste of Shilajatu, Madhuka and the seeds of Indivara, Trapusha and Ervaruka "would speedily bring about the disintegration of Pittaja As'mari (calculi, etc.)". A footnote at line 298 says "The urine-purifying drugs are the Trina-Panchamulas, Gokshura, Kasamarda, Pashanabheda, etc."
- **Chikitsa ch.32** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch32.txt`, lines 88 to 92 and 109 to 113), on fomentation: a steam pipe made of "the materials (such as Kus'a, Kas'a, etc.) used in the making of a Kilinji (basket)", and a patient laid "on a mattress (made of Kus'a, Kas'a, etc.)" over the fumes of boiled paddy. The translator's brackets name the materials, and the lines read do not give the Sanskrit original.
- **Uttara ch.42** (`SS_Uttara_ch42.txt`, lines 236 to 241) and **Uttara ch.59** (`SS_Uttara_ch59.txt`, lines 125 to 131), on *Gulma* (abdominal swelling) and on *Mutrakrichchhra* (painful urination): ghees cooked "with the decoction of the component members of the Trina-pancha-mula" for the Pittaja and Raktaja types of Gulma, and "with the drugs of the Trina-pancha-mula, Utpaladi, Kakolyadi and Nyagrodhadi groups" for Pittaja Mutra-krichchhra. These name the grass group, of which Kasha is one member, and do not name Kasha alone.

No rasayana or vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) was found to name Kasha, so no grade (a) status is claimed. Chikitsa ch.29 names the neighbouring grass Kusha as bedding in the Soma rite, and that line does not name Kasha.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Sushruta records Kasha as one of the grasses of the Trina-Panchamula group, a root in purgatives, a decoction ingredient in a ghee for calculi, a mattress or pipe material for steam, and a flowering marker of autumn. These are the text's own claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol for this grass.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted above: a decoction of the grass roots cooked into ghee (Chikitsa ch.7, with the group in Uttara ch.42 and ch.59), and the root as a purgative (Sutra ch.39). No quantity is given for Kasha in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** The text prints no *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* for Kasha in the lines read. Sutra ch.38 (lines 545 to 552) says the Pancha-Trina group, to which it belongs, "kills the deranged Pittam", and the Viratarvadi group (lines 66 to 73) "subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata".

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Kasha is a named grass in the Viratarvadi and Pancha-Trina groups, a root in purgatives and a decoction in a urinary-stone ghee. The Sushruta frame for it is therapeutic. The ch.38 gana is a grouping by action, not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. Nothing read ties Kasha to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*, and none of the rasayana or vajikarana chapters searched (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) names it. The status is unattested, and the Sanskrit name is only read from a damaged line of an OCR-paired appendix. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this grass. In Sushruta it is a grass of a Pitta-quieting group and an ingredient of a physician's ghee. The abstentive answer is the correct one: any use is a matter for a qualified practitioner, and the text supplies no long-term regimen.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. The urinary-stone, painful-urination and purgative uses above are conditions needing medical care, and none of them is advice. A root purgative must not be tried at home, and anyone with pain on passing urine, blood in the urine or loin pain needs ordinary medical care. Tall wild grasses can harbour ticks and cut the skin, and wild harvesting may be restricted. Do not use in pregnancy.
