**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *ṭīn maʾkūl* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 13619-13623). Under the digestive organs (أعضاء الغذاء): مسدد مفسد للمزاج إلا أنه يقوي فم المعدة ويذهب بوخامة الطعام ومع ذلك فلا أحب أن يستعمل (13620-13622): "obstructing, spoiling to the temperament, except that it strengthens the mouth of the stomach and removes the heaviness (*wakhāma*) of food; and with all that, I do not like it to be used". The passage names no constituent. In general terms edible earths are clays (hydrated aluminium silicates) with variable mineral and organic contamination. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice, and the Canon itself discourages it.** The text credits clay with strengthening the stomach mouth, removing food's heaviness and stopping vomiting, and then says its author does not like it used, since it obstructs and spoils the temperament. The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend eating clay.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Eating earth (geophagy) is recorded in many cultures. Clays can bind substances in the gut but may also carry soil pathogens, parasites eggs and heavy metals, and can cause constipation. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]
