# White Henbane — Yellow Henbane · Bang · Altercum · Apollinaris

## *Hyoscyamus albus* L. | Family: Solanaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, CM, DMM, EP, SS
**Energetic Classification:** Cold and dry in the third degree (Galenic) — a narcotic-soporific of the deadly class
**Training Phase:** Phase V — The Deadly Threshold (soporifics and anaesthetics; masters only)

### Origin & Tradition

☠️ **LETHAL PLANT — READ THE CONTRAINDICATIONS FIRST.** White henbane is the second of this volume's great tropane Solanaceous drugs, standing beside mandrake (I_672) as a deliriant-narcotic with a deadly overdose margin. It is documented here for study, not for use.

If mandrake is the plant of *legend*, henbane is the plant of *practical medieval anaesthesia and toothache*. The Mediterranean white henbane was, across the classical and medieval world, the workaday soporific — the drug of the sleeping-sponge, the toothache smoke, and the poppy's frequent partner.

**Assyro-Babylonian (AT).** Mesopotamian medicine used henbane-type Solanaceous narcotics for pain and sleep; the drug sits in the same *asû/āšipu* class of strong soporifics as mandrake and poppy. The specific cuneiform binomial is philologically uncertain — henbane is one candidate for several "sleep" and "tooth-worm" plant entries — and should be flagged [USER: verify]. The functional role as an analgesic-soporific is secure within the Near-Eastern stream.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM) and Apollo/the Pythia (DMM).** Dioscorides distinguishes the henbanes and *prefers the white-flowered / white-seeded kind (leukos)* — *Hyoscyamus albus* — as the safest for internal medicine, the black being the most dangerous and delirious. He records it for pain of all kinds, for sleep, for coughs and catarrh, for toothache, and as a topical anodyne. The plant's Greek name *hyoskyamos* ("hog's bean") and its later title *herba Apollinaris* tie it to Apollo and to prophetic-oracular use; henbane is among the plants associated (in later tradition, and to be read cautiously) with the intoxicated frenzy of the Pythia and with witchcraft's flying and vision.

**Egyptian (EP).** Egyptian medicine possessed strong Solanaceous analgesic-soporifics; henbane fits the Ebers-era class of pain-dulling, sleep-bringing plant drugs, though specific papyrus attributions for *H. albus* are weaker than the Greek record and should be treated as [USER: verify].

**Hebrew / Levantine (SBJ).** *Hyoscyamus* (the *bang / shikran* complex of Semitic drug vocabulary — a name-cluster that also attaches to other narcotics, so identification is not exclusive) is known in the Levantine medical tradition as a potent soporific and analgesic; the name *bang* travels widely across the Near East as a general term for such intoxicant-narcotics [USER: verify exact referent].

**Ayurveda (SS).** White henbane enters the Indo-Persian/Ayurvedic and Unani streams as *khurāsānī ajwāʾin* / *pārasīka yavānī* — "Persian/Khorasani ajwain" — a potent narcotic-antispasmodic and analgesic, explicitly flagged in those texts as toxic and dose-critical. This is a rare instance of a tradition naming the danger in the doctrine itself.

**The uses, itemised.** Henbane's signature classical-to-medieval applications: (1) **toothache** — the seeds burned and the smoke directed at the aching tooth to kill the "tooth-worm" believed to cause caries; (2) **sleep and anaesthesia** — a key component of the medieval *spongia somnifera* ("soporific sponge"), soaked in a decoction of henbane, opium, mandrake and hemlock, then inhaled to render a patient insensible for surgery; (3) **topical analgesia** for gout, earache and painful swellings; (4) **sorcery/vision** — a witch's and oracle's herb, again a face of its deliriant chemistry.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** Henbane's active principles are the same **tropane alkaloids as mandrake — hyoscyamine (the main alkaloid of *H. albus*) and scopolamine (hyoscine)** — competitive muscarinic antagonists. The doctrines map onto that chemistry with unusual tidiness:

- **The soporific / anaesthetic doctrine (spongia somnifera) → real CNS depression.** Scopolamine is a genuine sedative-amnesic and central anticholinergic; combined with opium's morphine it produced a workable surgical narcosis. The sleeping-sponge is not superstition but rational, if perilous, poly-pharmacy. This is the **strongest** henbane doctrine pharmacologically.
- **The analgesic / antispasmodic doctrine → real peripheral antimuscarinic action.** Tropanes relax visceral smooth muscle and blunt secretions and colic; henbane's use for gut, biliary and urinary spasm, and its topical anodyne role, correspond to genuine antispasmodic pharmacology (hyoscine/hyoscyamine derivatives remain in clinical use as antispasmodics).
- **The "tooth-worm smoke" doctrine → a real analgesic effect wrapped in a false aetiology.** There is no tooth-worm; the mythological explanation is wrong. *But the intervention worked well enough to persist for millennia* because the burning seeds deliver volatilised tropane alkaloids locally to the inflamed, exposed pulp, producing genuine anticholinergic/analgesic relief of the pain. Here the Codex must hold two things at once: the **doctrine (worm) is false**, the **effect (pain relief) is pharmacologically real.** A perfect specimen of right practice justified by wrong theory.
- **The oracular/witchcraft "vision and flight" doctrine → central anticholinergic delirium.** As with mandrake, muscarinic blockade in the CNS produces hallucination, disorientation, and the sensation of flight — the material engine of the flying-ointment and the frenzied-prophecy traditions. Real deliriant chemistry; but toxic, dysphoric, and uncontrollable, not a reliable visionary tool.

Henbane thus resolves into an almost entirely **pharmacologically real** doctrinal set — soporific, analgesic, antispasmodic, deliriant — with the single conceptual error being the *tooth-worm* aetiology, not the treatment.

### Active Components

- **Hyoscyamine (l-hyoscyamine)** — the principal tropane alkaloid of *Hyoscyamus albus* roots and aerial parts; racemises to **atropine**. Muscarinic antagonist.
- **Scopolamine (hyoscine)** — the CNS-penetrant tropane responsible for sedation, amnesia, antiemesis and delirium; biosynthesised from hyoscyamine by the iron-dependent enzyme **hyoscyamine 6β-hydroxylase (H6H)** in the roots.
- **Minor tropanes and biosynthetic intermediates** — tropinone, tropine, 6β-hydroxyhyoscyamine, littorine, cuscohygrine.
- Alkaloid content is **variable** by tissue, season and iron/nutrient status — again the reason the toxic margin is unpredictable.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

White henbane sits in Phase V as a *studied* soporific-anaesthetic archetype, not a self-administration protocol. Its historical Kenetica functions: (1) the **soporific/anaesthetic** of the spongia somnifera lineage — the practical ancestor of inhalational anaesthesia; (2) the **local analgesic** (toothache smoke, topical anodyne) — a lesson in a real effect surviving under a false theory; (3) the **antispasmodic** for visceral colic. As with mandrake, no self-dosing regimen is provided; the responsible modern engagement is intellectual and, at most, correct botanical identification to avoid poisoning.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the phytochemistry and pharmacology confirm that *Hyoscyamus albus* is a genuine hyoscyamine/scopolamine source, and that the drug's real actions are the CNS and peripheral anticholinergic effects its doctrines describe.

- **The plant is a bona fide tropane-alkaloid drug.** *H. albus* is characterised as "a well-known source of the tropane alkaloids, hyoscyamine and scopolamine, which are biosynthesised in the roots," with scopolamine produced from hyoscyamine by the iron-dependent enzyme H6H (Khandakar et al., *Front Plant Sci* 2013; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2013.00331)). Earlier enzymology confirms hyoscyamine as the main alkaloid of cultured *H. albus* roots and maps the putrescine→tropane pathway (Hibi et al., *Plant Physiol* 1992; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.100.2.826)). Analytical work confirms hyoscyamine and scopolamine in *H. albus* hairy-root extracts alongside atropine (Mateus et al., *J Pharm Biomed Anal* 1998; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0731-7085(98)00223-4)). The chemistry underwriting every henbane doctrine is verified.
- **The soporific/antiemetic action of the scopolamine limb is clinically real.** Scopolamine significantly reduces nausea versus placebo in controlled trials (RR 0.35, 95% CI 0.24–0.52; systematic review and meta-analysis, Zhang et al., *Acta Otolaryngol* 2024; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00016489.2024.2397556)) — direct modern confirmation that the plant's chief CNS alkaloid is a genuine sedative/antiemetic, the basis of the soporific doctrine.
- **The tooth-worm aetiology is false; the analgesic effect is real.** No retrieved evidence supports a dental "worm"; the mechanism of relief is the local and systemic anticholinergic/analgesic action of the volatilised tropanes, consistent with the poisoning literature on the same alkaloids (see mandrake case series, I_672: Piccillo et al. 2006 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1368-5031.2006.00864.x); 2002 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/00063110-200212000-00010)). Graded: **effect real, doctrine (worm) false.**

Honest evidence-grade summary: the **soporific, analgesic, antispasmodic and deliriant** doctrines are **well-corroborated** by the confirmed hyoscyamine/scopolamine chemistry and clinical anticholinergic pharmacology; the **tooth-worm** rationale is a **debunked aetiology** attached to a genuinely effective treatment; there is no safe therapeutic self-use.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Greek/Galenic–Unani:** cold and dry in the third degree; a *narcotic-soporific*; Dioscorides grades the white kind as the least dangerous of the three henbanes but still a poison. In Unani, *khurāsānī ajwāʾin* is cold-dry, *muḫaddir* (narcotic/anaesthetic), explicitly *sammī* (toxic).
- **Ayurveda (SS):** as *pārasīka yavānī* — *vīrya* uṣṇa in some sources, *madakārī* (intoxicant) and *nidrājanana* (sleep-inducing), flagged *viṣa* (toxic); precise rasa/guṇa/vipāka grading varies by text and is **not invented here** [USER: verify].
- **TCM:** *Hyoscyamus* (*tian xian zi*, henbane seed) exists in the Chinese materia medica but is not part of the traditions attested for this *H. albus* entry (BM/SBJ/DMM/EP/SS); **not tagged CM here.**
- **Assyro-Babylonian / Egyptian:** grouped with the strong soporific-analgesic drugs; specific humoral grading not securely preserved.

### Contraindications

☠️ **SEVERELY TOXIC — POTENTIALLY DEADLY. DO NOT INGEST.**

- **Deliriant anticholinergic poisoning.** All parts — seeds and roots especially — contain hyoscyamine and scopolamine capable of producing life-threatening anticholinergic syndrome: hyperthermia, mydriasis and blurred vision, dry mouth and skin, flushing, urinary retention, tachyarrhythmia, agitated delirium and hallucination, seizures, respiratory depression, coma and death. The margin between soporific and lethal dose is narrow and unpredictable because alkaloid content varies widely.
- **Foraging and preparation hazards.** Henbane has caused poisonings when its parts (or seeds) are mistaken for edible plants/spices, and its smoke and topical preparations can absorb enough alkaloid to poison systemically. Do not ingest, burn-and-inhale, or apply to skin.
- **No safe recreational/"entheogenic" use.** The deliriant state is toxic, amnesic and dysphoric; historical oracular/witchcraft use reflects poisoning, not a controllable visionary tool.
- **Contraindicated absolutely** in children, pregnancy, the elderly, and anyone with glaucoma, cardiac disease, prostatic obstruction, or on other anticholinergic/QT-affecting drugs.
- **First aid / treatment** is a medical emergency: supportive care, decontamination, and, in severe anticholinergic toxicity, **physostigmine** under clinical supervision. Seek emergency services immediately for any suspected ingestion.
