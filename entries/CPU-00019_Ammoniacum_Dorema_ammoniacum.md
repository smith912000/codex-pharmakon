### Origin & Tradition

Gum ammoniac is the milky oleo-gum-resin that exudes from the stems of *Dorema ammoniacum*, a tall Persian giant fennel of the dry Iranian and Central Asian steppe. The name is traditionally derived from the **Temple of Ammon** (the oracle of Amun in the Libyan desert) near which the resin was said to be collected or traded — an etymology repeated since antiquity, though the principal source was always the Persian plateau rather than Libya. The resin appears in commerce as "tears" and as lumps, pale to amber, hardening on exposure.

Dioscorides describes ammoniacum among the gum-resins, valuing it as a warming softener and resolvent: it ripens and disperses hard, cold swellings, clears the chest of thick phlegm, eases the spleen and "hard" abdominal masses, and serves in plasters. It belongs squarely to the umbelliferous gum-resin family — galbanum, sagapenum, opopanax, asafoetida — and shares their discutient, antispasmodic and expectorant signature, distinguished by being especially associated with **resolving hard glandular and abdominal swellings** and with **chronic bronchial congestion**.

In the Unani and Persian traditions ammoniacum (**oshaq**) is a standard deobstruent and resolvent: warming, drying, used internally for cold phlegmatic chest conditions, asthma-type breathlessness with thick mucus, and amenorrhoea, and externally in resolvent plasters for indurated swellings, enlarged spleen, and chronic joint conditions. Its emulsifiability in water (forming a milky "lac ammoniaci") made it a convenient base for liquid expectorant medicines well into the era of Western pharmacy, where Mistura Ammoniaci and similar preparations persisted in the pharmacopoeias.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Expectorant for chronic cold/phlegm chest.** The defining internal use: warming and loosening thick, cold bronchial mucus — the basis of the old "milk of ammoniac" expectorant mixtures.
2. **Resolvent for hard swellings (external).** Warming plasters to soften and disperse indurated glandular swellings, splenic enlargement and chronic joint indurations.
3. **Antispasmodic / deobstruent.** Used for "wind," colic and obstructive abdominal complaints in the umbelliferous-resin manner.
4. **Emmenagogue (traditional).** Recorded to promote menstruation. [USER: verify before any practical statement.]
5. **Plaster base.** Its emulsifying gum content made it a practical vehicle for compound plasters and liquid medicines.

### Preparation & Dosing

- **Resin tears/lumps:** softened by warmth and worked into pills or plasters.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy — caution/avoid medicinal doses.** Emmenagogue reputation; internal medicinal use traditionally avoided in pregnancy. [USER: verify.]
- **Hot/acute inflammatory conditions.** A warming resolvent; less suited to frankly hot, acute states.
- **Resin allergy / hypersensitivity.** Possible with all aromatic gum-resins.
- **GI irritation.** Larger internal doses can irritate the gut.
