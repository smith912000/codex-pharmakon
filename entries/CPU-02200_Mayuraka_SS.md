# Mayuraka — Mayuraka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907; printed "Mayuraka")

## *An unidentified drug named in the Arka'di group, in an ulcer-cleansing oil and in a wind-disease oil; the translation gives no Latin name and no description. The identification is ours and unresolved, at low confidence: in the oil passage of Sutrasthana ch.36 the translator prints "(Apang)" in brackets straight after the name, which may be a synonym or gloss, but it is not explained [USER: verify against the printed page and Dallana]* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** The group and the oil that carry this name contain strongly acting or poisonous drugs by the text's own listing (Arka, the two Karanjas, Haritala and Manahshila, and others), and the text says the group acts against poison. The plant itself is unidentified. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take this item or either preparation.

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, in three places.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from an extraction of the translation's group lists; the 30 October check pass kept it as a separate row with the identification open. The Sutra 38 passage on the Arka'di group is the one the row cites.

**Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.38, the Arka'di group.** The chapter classifies drugs "usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 9). The group is printed across a page break at lines 102 to 111 (Vol. I lines 15447 to 15452): "The drugs known as the Arka, Alarka, the two kinds of Karanja, Nagadanti, Mayuraka, Bhargi, Rasna, Indrapushpi, Kshudrashveta, Mahashveta, Vrishchikali, Alavana and Tapasha-Vriksha, enter into the composition of the group known as the Arkadi Gana." The metrical texts (lines 113 to 117) read: "The group known as the Arkadi destroys Kaphani, fat, and the effects of poison. It acts as a vermifuge and a specific aseptic agent in the case of an ulcer and proves curative in diseases of the skin." These are group claims, not claims for Mayuraka alone.

**Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.36, an oil for the inside of an ulcer.** In the chapter on miscellaneous remedies, "a medicated oil prepared with Mayuraka, (Apang), Rajabriksha, Ximva, Koshataki, Tila, Vrihati, Kantakari, Haritala, Manahshila, and the aforesaid plug-drugs (purgative drugs according to others), should be used for the purpose of purifying the interior of an ulcer" (`SS_Sutra_ch36.txt` lines 134 to 138; Vol. I line 15046). The bracketed "(Apang)" follows the name directly. Whether it glosses Mayuraka, names a separate drug, or is an alternative reading is not stated. In the usual Sanskrit lexica *apanga* and *apamarga* name the same plant, and *mayuraka* is one of the names listed for that plant, but this is from outside the text and is not checked here. [USER: verify]

**Sushruta (SS), Chikitsitasthana ch.5, wind disease.** In the chapter on the treatment of *Maha-Vata-vyadhi* (the great diseases of wind), the text prescribes, as an alternative, "the oil cooked with the decoction of Shatavari, Mayuraka, Madhuka, Kshira-Vidari, Vala, Ati-vala and Trina-pancha-mula, with the paste of the drugs belonging to the Kakolyadi group, or the oil cooked with the decoction and a Kalka of Vala for a hundred times" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt` lines 115 to 120; Vol. II line 13750). This is a different kind of oil from the ulcer oil: it is one of several alternatives for wind disease and is built on the Kakolyadi group paste and a decoction of roots including Shatavari, Madhuka and Bala. The way in which Mayuraka suits both an ulcer cleanser and a wind oil is not explained, and the two uses may well refer to two different plants sharing one name. [USER: verify]

The text on disk does not give a taste, a part used, or a habitat for Mayuraka. The only other lexical hint is the Appendix No. 3 index, which does not list the name (the M page of the animal appendix in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, around line 30787, has a different name, *Mayura*, among the animal names; it is not this item).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unidentified. Companion drugs are toxic.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Arka'di group: Kapham, fat, the effects of poison, worms, ulcers and skin disease. In the ch.36 oil: purifying the inside of an ulcer. In the Chikitsa ch.5 oil: part of a wind-disease oil.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no amount in either oil.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant or either oil.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources give two oils: one cooked with Mayuraka and others for application to an ulcer (ch.36), and one cooked with a decoction of Shatavari, Mayuraka, Madhuka and others (Chikitsa ch.5). The preparation of an oil from a decoction and a paste ("Kalka") is described in a footnote to ch.36 as mixing drugs with water "eight or sixteen times their combined weight" and boiling down (a note in the translation at `SS_Sutra_ch36.txt` lines 173 to 175, attached to a different passage). No quantity of Mayuraka itself is printed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Bhishagratna 1907).** No taste (*rasa*), potency (*virya*) or post-digestive effect (*vipaka*) is printed for Mayuraka. The Arka'di group's metrical text gives only the group's actions (lines 113 to 117).

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* Mayuraka appears in a therapeutic group and in two oils, one for ulcers and one for wind disease. It was not found in a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) chapter, and no *ojas* effect is assigned to it.

**Practitioner's note.** A name without a plant behind it, in a group of strong and partly toxic drugs and in two medicated oils. There is nothing to source or take. The honest outcome is to keep the name and its three contexts on record and leave it.

### Contraindications

- **Identity unresolved; companions toxic.** Do not substitute any product for it. The group includes *Arka* and *Karanja*, and the ch.36 oil includes mineral poisons.
- **Open wounds.** The ch.36 oil is for purifying an ulcer's interior; do not apply unidentified oils to wounds.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Wind disease, ulcers, skin disease, poisoning.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Uncertain text.** The bracketed "(Apang)" is unexplained in the translation.
