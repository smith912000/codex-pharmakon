# Sirāj al-quṭrub — "lamp of the quṭrub" (سراج القطرب), Dioscorides

## *A plant "near hyssop" with a purple hellebore-like flower, whose seed is used; Avicenna quotes Dioscorides; the plant is not identified and no species is offered; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. Book II, entry *sirāj al-quṭrub*, Arabic text from line 15841 of `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk was not run beyond the spine check. 

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** What it is: "a plant near hyssop (*zūfā*). Dioscorides said: it is a plant whose flower is like the hellebore (*kharbaq*) and in its colour purple (*furfūriyya*); medicated sticks (*ashyāf*) are made from it; its flower is like a lamp on the head of a green plant; and it has another kind" (the text breaks here). Choice (*al-ikhtiyār*): "the part used is its seed" (المستعمل منه بزره). Temperament: "hot in the first, dry in the second, and it is at the end of the second". Properties: "it opens, but astringency predominates in it; it cuts bleeding however it be". Ulcers: "very healing". Head: applied as a poultice it cuts nosebleed. Chest: it stops spitting of blood. Excretory organs: "benefits ulcers of the intestines as an enema; some people claimed that two drachms of the seed of the wild kind loosened the belly". Poisons: "its seed drunk with wine benefits scorpion sting and bite; some people claimed that the seed of the wild kind, laid on scorpions, numbs them, nullifies their action and makes them as if dead."

**Identity doubt.** "Near hyssop" and a purple lamp-like flower do not fix a plant. The name is Arabic ("lamp of the quṭrub"). [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None is given in the text. The seed is called astringent; in general this suggests tannins, which is an inference and not in the text.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** Bleeding, ulcers, spitting of blood, scorpion sting are all recorded; a sting and any bleeding need medical care. The seed is also said to purge in a stated amount; nothing recommends this.

### Preparation & Dosing

Seed drunk in wine for scorpion bite; an enema for intestinal ulcers; a poultice for nosebleed (text above). One dose is printed: two drachms (*dirhamayn*, درهمين) of the wild seed said to loosen the belly, reported as what "some people claimed". Not advice. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Avicenna: hot in the first degree, dry in the second, "at the end of the second". Astringency predominates.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No cultivation framing is attested. **(c) Absent.** The *Canon* entry is organised by organ and complaint and says nothing here about innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruh*. 

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to practise. It is a styptic seed of uncertain identity.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

The entry's organ targets are spread across several systems. Excretory organs: the seed given as an enema benefits ulcers of the intestines, and the text reports, as a claim, that two drachms of the wild seed loosened the belly. Head and nose: a poultice of the seed cuts nosebleed. Chest: it stops spitting of blood. Blood: it cuts bleeding however it be. Ulcers, with no site given: very healing. Poisons: seed drunk with wine benefits scorpion sting and bite, and the wild seed laid on scorpions is said to numb them. Energetically, Avicenna gives hot in the first degree, dry in the second and at the end of the second, with astringency predominating and an opening action. The entry names no nerve or joint. The plant is not identified, so no modern pharmacology can be tied to these claims. Bleeding, scorpion stings and intestinal disease need medical care, and the purging seed is not recommended.

### Contraindications

- Bleeding, bloody spit and scorpion stings need medical care.
- Enemas and purging seeds can harm; none is recommended.
- The plant is not identified.
