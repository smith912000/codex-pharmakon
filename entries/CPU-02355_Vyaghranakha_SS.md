# Vyaghranakha — Vyaghranakha (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907; printed "Vydghra-nakha" in one OCR line)

## *A drug named in the Elardi (Eladi) group of Sushruta chapter 38 and once in a head plaster for fever in the Uttaratantra. The text gives no description, no part used and no Latin name. Identification: unresolved. Basis: none in the sources read; the compound's literal sense, "tiger's claw", is the reader's observation and does not tell whether a plant or an animal part is meant. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The name is a bare entry in a drug list. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should gather or take anything on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38 and was filed with the class "plant" by the extraction, not by the text; this entry replaces it. The first-pass guess was "unresolved (Vyaghranakha)". That stands.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

1. **Group list, Elardi.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15506 to 15516; `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 164 to 171). Bhishagratna prints: "The Elardi Group: The group of medicinal drugs known as the Eladi-Gana consists of Ela, Tagara, Kushtha, Mansi, Dhyamaka, Tvaka, Patra, Nagapushpa, Priyangu, Harenuka, Vyaghranakha, Shukti, Chanda, Sthauneyaka, Shriveshtaka, Chocha, Choraka, Valaka, Guggulu, Sarjarasa, Turushka, Kunduruka, Aguru, Sprikka, Ushira, Bhadradaru, Kumkuma, Punnaga and Keshara." (Some names are damaged in the OCR, for example "Clianda" for *Chanda*.)
2. **The group's virtue.** The metrical text that follows (ch.38 lines 172 to 181) says: "The therapeutic virtue of the group consists in subduing the action of Vayu and Kapham and in neutralising the effects of poison. It is a cosmetic and arrests the eruption of pimples and other vegetations on the skin such as rash, urticaria etc. and checks the itching sensation incidental thereto." This is a statement about the group as a whole. It is not a statement about *Vyaghranakha* alone, and the sources read give the drug no property of its own.
3. **A fever plaster.** In the *Uttaratantra* chapter on fevers (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt` lines 1630 to 1640; same passage as `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 8962) the translator prints "Specific treatment of the complications: Now hear me tell you other specific remedies for the complications. A plaster composed of Yashtimadhu, Rajani, Musta, Dadima, Amla-vetasa, Rasanjana, Tintidika (tamarind), Nalada (Mansi), Patra, Utpala, Tvak (cinnamon), Vyaghra-nakha [OCR: "VydgJira-nakha"], the expressed juice of Matulunga, honey and Madhu-shukta, if applied to the head, would alleviate heat in the head, delirium, vomiting, hic-cough, and shivering — concomitants in cases of fever." The plaster is a compound; no effect is given to the drug alone. Its neighbours in the list (*Patra*, *Tvak*, *Utpala*, *Mansi*) are also names in the *Elardi* group, but that overlap is the reader's observation.
4. **No gloss found.** Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 (Vol. III, "Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms") was searched for *Vyaghranakha*, *Vyaghra-nakha* and the OCR variants; no row was found. The machine-made name index `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` (row 155) records the name as "group list (ch.38); no identification given here". No other text on disk was found to name it.

**What the name could be.** The compound is *vyaghra* ("tiger") plus *nakha* ("nail, claw") in Sanskrit; that is the reader's etymology, not a statement of the text. A tiger-claw name could in principle stand for a plant with claw-like thorns or pods, or for a hard claw-like animal or shell part used as a perfume; the translator prints neither. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unresolved identity.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Elardi group: subduing deranged *Vayu* and *Kapham*, neutralising poison, as a cosmetic and against rash, urticaria and itching (group level). As one ingredient of a compound head plaster for the heat, delirium, vomiting, hiccough and shivering of fever (*Uttaratantra* ch.39).
- **What the text does not say.** No part used, no habitat, no preparation by itself, no dose.

The Codex does not translate *Vayu*, *Kapham* or the other terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend any material under this name.

### Contraindications
