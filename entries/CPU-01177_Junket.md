# Junket (curdled milk) — Gala schiston, "split milk" (as the heading is printed in the English edition of Dioscorides)
## *Milk curdled and separated into curd and whey; the English edition's suggested identification is "Junket"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek Tradition (DMM).** The chapter is Dioscorides, *De Materia Medica*, Book 2, chapter 77, headed "2-77. GALA SCHISTON" with the editor's "SUGGESTED: Junket" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 5244; row in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 267). The preceding chapter, with "SUGGESTED: Whey", opens: "All milk has whey contained within it. Separated out, this is fitter for purging and is given to those whom we would purge without distress, as well as for depression, epilepsy, leprosy, elephantiasis, and pustules that break out over the whole body" (line 5243). The 2-77 chapter itself belongs with it.

What 2-77 prints, in order:

1. The method: "All milk is separated by boiling it in a new ceramic jar and stirring it with a freshly cut down fig tree branch. After it has boiled two or three times a wine-cup of vinegar and honey for every half-pint of milk is poured into it: thus the whey is parted from the cheesy matter." Against boil-over, "you must continually rub the brim of the jar with a sponge dipped in cold water during boiling, and let down a silver pint jar full of cold water into it" (line 5246).
2. The whey is "given to drink (at intervals) in amounts of from one half-pint to five, and let those who drink it walk around during that time."
3. Uses of milk: "New milk is good for ulcers, and inflammation caused by deadly medicines" and the chapter names cantharis, pine caterpillar, salamander, buprestis, hyoscyamus, dorycnium, aconitum and ephemerum; "For this cow's milk helps best." It is also gargled for ulcers of the mouth and tonsils, and "especially asses' milk gargled in the mouth strengthens the gums and the teeth."
4. "If you place hot burning flints into the milk of sheep, cows, or goats to boil it, this stops discharges accompanied with ulceration." It is "given as a suppository or enema (either by itself or with barley water or cream of halica) to relieve considerable gnawing of the intestines" and "squirted into an ulcerated vulva" (lines 5246-5251).

The text on disk uses "junket" as the editor's suggestion, and the chapter describes an acid-and-heat separation of curd and whey rather than a modern junket set with rennet. Dioscorides treats rennet in its own chapter ("SUGGESTED: Rennet", from line 5290), which is a different material. In this entry "junket" therefore means what the chapter describes. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Milk is an emulsion of fat in a water solution of lactose, proteins and minerals. When milk is acidified or heated with acid, as in this chapter's vinegar, the main protein (casein) coagulates into curd and leaves whey, which carries lactose, whey proteins and minerals. These are general facts. No figures are stated.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

This is a food material, but the Greek text's uses are medical claims from antiquity and are historical record only. The Codex does not recommend curdled milk or whey as a treatment for epilepsy, leprosy, poisoning or ulcers, and it provides no protocol. The text's claim that milk helps in poisoning by named toxins is not safe advice. As a food, curd or whey is an ordinary dairy product for those who tolerate milk; that statement is general knowledge and makes no claim about the text.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives the method above (new ceramic jar, fig-branch stir, two or three boils, a wine-cup of vinegar and honey per half-pint of milk). The only quantity for use is of the whey: "from one half-pint to five" taken at intervals, with walking (line 5246). Those units are Dioscorides' own, in the translation. No dose is given for the other uses (gargle, enema, suppository). [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General facts only: whey is a by-product of cheese-making, and milk can be an allergen and a source of lactose intolerance. Milk does not neutralise the poisons named in the chapter.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Greek: the chapter prints no warming, cooling, drying or moistening quality for junket. It gives actions only, and the whey chapter gives "fitter for purging" (line 5243). No other tradition is tagged.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is manufactured here. This chapter is a recipe and a list of uses. The nearest thing to a discipline of attention in it is practical: new ceramic jar, fresh-cut fig branch, a sponge on the rim to stop boil-over, and walking while the whey is drunk. These are procedural cautions, not cultivation steps. No cultivation status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing specific. Junket and whey are, in this text, ordinary preparations. A cultivator may treat dairy as food if it suits them; the text does not support anything beyond that.

### Contraindications

Milk and its derivatives are unsuitable for people with milk allergy or lactose intolerance. Raw milk can carry pathogens and should be heated properly. Do not use milk as an antidote to poisoning; call emergency services or a poison-control centre. The chapter's own listing of toxic agents (cantharis, aconitum, hyoscyamus and others) records danger, not a safe practice. Enemas and vaginal application of home-made preparations risk infection and injury.
