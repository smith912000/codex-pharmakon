# Cyperus-from-the-North — as printed by Bryan 1930 (Ebers papyrus); forms Cyperus-from-the-Fields, -the-Meadow, -the-Marshes, Knots-, Thorns-of-the-Cyperus, Cyperus-grass

## *A sedge or rush called "Cyperus-from-the-North" in Bryan's English, one of a group of seven cyperus forms in his Chapter VI; Bryan prints no Egyptian word and no botanical name in the lines read. Identification: a Cyperus species, species not known (the spine's other Ebers cyperus rows suggest C. rotundus, C. esculentus or C. papyrus, none of which Bryan's English decides). Confidence: low as to species, medium that a sedge of the genus is meant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Ebers papyrus among the texts on disk, in Bryan's 1930 English.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it.

**Overlap with the spine.** CPU-00368 (Musta, *Cyperus rotundus*, which absorbed the Ebers row "Cyperus / sedge (northern variety)", CPU-00421, in the 30 October duplicate merge), CPU-00422 (Tigernut, *Cyperus esculentus*) and CPU-00706 (Papyrus, *Cyperus papyrus*) already carry Ebers tags. This row is Bryan's printed name "Cyperus-from-the-North" and is likely to belong with one of them. The text read here cannot choose between them. The maintainer should decide. [USER: verify]

**Egyptian Papyrus Ebers (EP).** Bryan's Chapter VI, "Plant Remedies", lists, as one alphabetical group, "Cyperus-from-the-North, Cyperus-from-the-Fields, Cyperus-from-the-Meadow, Cyperus-from-the-Marshes, Knots-of-the-Cyperus, Thorns-of-the-Cyperus, Cyperus-grass" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1273; names.csv row). Cyperus is a head word of its own in the recipes; the lines found by searching the text are these.

1. **Cyperus-from-the-North, in an eye remedy.** "For Bleary Eyes one beat up Myrrh, Onions, Verdigris, and Cyperus-from-the-North, with Antilope-dung, Clear Oil, and Entrails-of-the-*qadit*-animal. This could be painted on with a Vulture's feather" (line 2735).
2. **Cyperus-from-the-Meadow, to allay itching.** "Another to allay itching: Cyperus-from-the-Meadow, Onion-meal, Incense, Wild Date-juice. Make into one and apply to the scurvy place" (lines 2622 to 2626).
3. **Cyperus-from-the-Marshes, -from-the-Fields and Cyperus-knots, in a skin poultice.** For "Scabs in every Limb", a poultice in which these were crushed in fresh oil, goose-oil and semen with red corn (line 2644, Bryan's paraphrase).
4. **Plain Cyperus** appears in a poultice for diseased toes (line 2149), in a spell-accompanied eye remedy ("Add Cyperus thereto and carefully apply to the Eye", line 2840), in a plaster for blisters in the teeth with incense, crocus, onions and aloes-wood (line 3145), in a face application "to drive away wrinkles" ("Cyperus. Crush, grind, put in Fresh Milk, and apply to the face for six days", lines 4089 to 4092), and in a list of "substances to use in order to make pleasant the smell of the moves or of the clothes" (line 4198). Whether Bryan's plain "Cyperus" is the same plant as any of the place-qualified forms is not stated. [USER: verify]
5. **Thorns-of-the-Cyperus** stand in the large poultice of Bryan's "largest prescription in the Papyrus" (line 3186).

No description of the plant, and no part used beyond the "knots" and "thorns" named in the list, was found in the lines read. "From-the-North" is Bryan's rendering of a qualifier; the Egyptian original is not in the English text on disk. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, sedges of the genus *Cyperus* have starchy or fibrous rhizomes and tubers, and some species (for example *C. rotundus*) are aromatic, with essential oils in the tuber. Which species the Ebers recipes used is not known, so no constituent is asserted for this row. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Bleary eyes (with myrrh, onions and verdigris), itching skin, scabs, diseased toes, teeth, wrinkles and perfuming clothes, in each case inside a mixture.
- **What the text does not say.** No quantity of the cyperus in any recipe and no description of the preparation beyond crushing, beating up and applying.

The Codex gives no protocol. Several companion ingredients (verdigris, antelope dung, semen, red corn) are unsuitable or hazardous, and none of these recipes is recommended. Verdigris on the eye is dangerous.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations found are painted-on or applied mixtures, a poultice, a plaster, and a face application kept on for six days. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any recipe from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Sedges are widely used as fibre and fragrance plants, and aromatic *Cyperus* tubers have long been used in traditional perfumery. The Ebers terms (bleary eyes, scurvy place) are not translated here into modern diagnoses. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Do not use the recipes.** Verdigris (copper acetate) is irritant and poisonous, and must not be put in the eye. The other companion ingredients carry their own risks.
- **Eye disease, scabs, itching.** These are medical conditions needing proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** "Cyperus-from-the-North" is Bryan's label; the species is not shown. Cyperus material sold today comes from several species. [USER: verify]
