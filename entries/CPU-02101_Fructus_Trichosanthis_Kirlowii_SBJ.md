# Fructus Trichosanthis Kirlowii — Gua Lou (Yang 1998); in the classic 栝蒌根 *gua lou gen*, alias 地楼 *Di Lou* "Earth Building"

## *Trichosanthes kirilowii Maxim., the climbing gourd of northern China; the identification is Yang 1998's own ("Fructus Trichosanthis Kirlowii") and agrees with the Codex's identification of the same monograph at I_1536, but it is of medium confidence as to the part used, because Yang's Latin names the fruit while the Sun Xingyan text of the same monograph is headed "root" (栝蒌根)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry: named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The row was filed from Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 English translation, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, "Herbs: Middle Class, PDF p.72 (line 2697)". The principal text for the Chinese is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; Yang is read as a second witness and is attributed to Yang.

**Probable duplicate.** The Codex already holds an entry for the Sun monograph 栝蒌根, "Trichosanthes root", at `Volume_I_Plant_Based/entries/I_1536_Trichosanthes_root.md`. Yang's "Gua Lou" paragraph (line 2697) translates the same monograph. The only difference found is in the Latin part-name that Yang prints (fruit, against the root of Sun's heading). This entry records Yang's reading and should be merged with I_1536 or kept as a variant by the editor. [USER: verify whether to merge.]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yang 1998 (line 2697, p.72): "Gua Lou (Fructus Trichosanthis Kirlowii) is bitter and cold. It mainly treats wasting thirst, generalized fever, vexatious fullness, and great heat. It supplements vacuity, quiets the center, and mends expiry and damage. Its other name is Di Lou (Earth Building). It grows in rivers and valleys as well as shady places in the mountains."

Sun's text (line 1857, entry 栝蒌根, the middle classic) reads 味苦，寒。主消渴，身热烦满，大热，补虚安中，续绝伤。一名地楼。生川谷及山阴 ("bitter, cold; governs wasting thirst, generalized heat with vexation and fullness, great heat; supplements vacuity and quiets the center; joins broken injuries. One name 地楼. Grows in river valleys and the shady side of mountains"). The two agree. The Wu Pu layer (line 1861) gives the aliases 泽巨 and 泽姑. The Mingyi bielu layer (line 1863) adds 果裸 and 天瓜 and 泽姑, then a line that names the fruit: 实，名黄瓜 ("the fruit is called 黄瓜"), and a harvest, 二月、八月采根，曝干，三十日成 ("the root is gathered in the second and eighth months and dried in the sun; ready in thirty days"), with the habitat 生宏农. That is the only line in the Sun text that names the fruit separately. The classic's own paragraph, so far as read, does not specify which part is meant.

Yang's footnote 158 (line 2702) is modern commentary and is not part of the classic: "Trichosanthes clears upper burner fire, moistens the lungs, flushes phlegm, and disinhibits the throat... it is an ideal medicinal for cough and a miraculous one for thirst... In the text, it is said to be bitter, but actually it is bitter and sweet."

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Wasting thirst, generalized fever, vexation and fullness, and great heat; it "supplements vacuity" and "quiets the center".
- **What the text does not say.** No preparation, no dose, no duration, and no 久服 clause.

The Codex does not translate these terms into modern diagnoses and gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

Neither Yang nor Sun gives a preparation or a dose in the monograph. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest of the root (line 1863). The preface's general rule of method for toxic drugs (Sun line 4125, begin as small as a millet grain) is not a dose for this plant and this monograph is not marked toxic. General, well-established facts only. *Trichosanthes kirilowii* is a perennial climbing gourd of East Asia, and the genus is classified in the gourd family. ### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* In Sun's reconstruction the monograph stands in the middle classic, whose preface (line 1677) reads 中药一百二十种为臣，主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经: the middle drugs are "ministers, mainly nourishing the nature to correspond with humanity; non-toxic or toxic, to be weighed as suits; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the emaciated". Yang also prints it among the middle-class herbs. The paragraph says "supplements vacuity" but has no 久服 clause and no promise of lightening the body, of not ageing or of prolonging life. The register is therefore (b): framed by the middle class, with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade drug in the Chinese scheme, recorded for thirst, heat and vexation. Nothing in the text supports a long-term or life-nourishing use. A cultivator has nothing to do with this entry beyond reading it.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The part used (root or fruit) is not fixed by the classic's paragraph; do not assume. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Thirst, fever.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Incompatibilities (Sun, line 4375, the drug-relations table).** 栝蒌 has 枸杞 as its envoy; it dislikes 干姜, fears 牛膝 and 干漆, and stands in the 反 ("counter") relation with 乌头. The table names the drug 栝蒌 without saying root or fruit.
