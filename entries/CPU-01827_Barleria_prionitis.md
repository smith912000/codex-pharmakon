# Barleria prionitis — Koranta (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3)

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The chapters retrieved do not print the Sanskrit name Koranta, so the entry holds an appendix pairing and no chapter statement about this plant.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` (row 516) gives the name as "Eoranta", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31978; id line 32048)" and the Latin as "Barleria  Prionitis.", with the note that the pairing was proposed by an agent from the two OCR columns and checked by script. The OCR reads the initial K of this run as E throughout ("Ehadira" for Khadira at line 31962, "Eodrava" for Kodrava at line 31972, "Eramuka" for Kramuka at line 31982), so the Codex reads "Eoranta" as Koranta.

**The pairing, checked by counting.** The page (page number "71" at line 31901) prints its Sanskrit names at lines 31904 to 31986, from Kanchana to Krishna-jiraka, and its Latin names at lines 31992 to 32052. Counting from the end of both columns: Krishna-jiraka stands against "Nigella Sativa" (line 32052), Krishna against "See Pippali" (32051), Kramuka against "Areca Catechu" (32050), Koshataki against "Luffa Amara ; L. Acutangula" (32049) and **Koranta against "Barleria Prionitis"** (32048). The next names up also fit: Kovidara with "Bauhinia Variegata" (32047), Kokilaksha with "Hygrophila Spinosa" (32046), Kodrava with "Paspalum Scrobiculatum" (32045), Kirata-tikta with "Swertia Chirata" (32043), Kimsuka with "Butea frondosa" (32041), Kharjura with "Phoenix sylvestris" (32040) and Khadira with "Accacia Catechu" (32038). (The OCR prints a bracket glyph after Kirata and Kirata-tikta at lines 31968 and 31970, and the Swertia line stands once, so those two names share one Latin line.) The pairing therefore rests on the count from the end and on eleven neighbours that agree with it. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* A search of all chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) for Koranta and its OCR variants (Koranta, Koranda, Korantaka, Kuranta, Kuranda) found no hit for that name. Three nearby spellings do occur, and they are set out here so that no reader takes them for this plant's attestation.

*Kurantika and Kurantakam (Sutra ch.46).* `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1730 to 1732 read: "The leaves of the Lonika, Jatuka, Triparnika, Pattura, Jivaka, Suvarchala, Kurmaka, Kathinjara, Kuntalika, Kurantika etc., are sweet in taste and digestion and cooling in their potency." Line 1739 adds: "The species Kurantika has an astringent taste." These are potherb leaves in the chapter on food and drink. Lines 1810 to 1818, in the passage on flowers, end: "The Kinshukam, like the Kurantakam, subdues the Kapham and Pittam." The chapter gives no Latin name for either spelling, and nothing in it links Kurantika or Kurantakam to Koranta or to *Barleria*.

*Kuruntaka, Dasi-kuruntaka and Sahachara (Sutra ch.38).* In the Aragvadhadi group (`SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 26 to 40) the drugs include "Kuruntaka, Dasi-kuruntaka"; the text says of the group that it "destroys the deranged Kapham and the effects of poison and proves beneficial in cases of Meha ..., Kushtha, fever, vomiting and itching of the body and acts as a purifying (aseptic) agent in the case of an ulcer". In the Viratarvadi group (lines 57 to 64) the drugs are "Virataru, the two kinds of Sahachara, Darbha, Vrikshadani, Gundra, Nala, Kusha, Kasha, Ashmabhedaka, Agnimantha, Morata Vasuka, Vasira, Bhalluka, Kuruntaka, Indivara, Kapotavanka, and Shadanstra". Of the group the text says only that it "subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata" and "proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles". The same appendix pairs Sahachara, by the same kind of count, with "Barlaria Cristata" (line 32722, against Sahachara at line 32673; Rodhra stands against "See Lodhra" before it and Sahadeva against "See Bala" after it), and pairs Kuruvaka with "Barleria Cristata." (names.csv row 531, Latin line 32113). The appendix therefore prints *Barleria prionitis* against Koranta alone, and the Sutra 38 names Sahachara and Kuruntaka are, on the appendix evidence read here, not tied to it. The appendix line for Dasi-kuruntaka was not read for this entry.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Koranta, Kurantika, Kurantakam and Kuruntaka found nothing.
