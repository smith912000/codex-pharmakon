# Kiln earth (burnt red earth) — Ge Kaminon (GE KAMINON), Dioscorides 5-179

## *Red earth that has been well burnt in furnaces, which Dioscorides says has the same qualities as furnace tiles; the translator's gloss is "Burnt Red Earth". The identification is ours and is of medium confidence: a fired red clay or earth, since the chapter names no deposit, no colour test and no mineral* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the non-plant master list; this entry replaces it. A search of `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` for "kaminon", "furnaces" and "burnt" found the chapter below and its neighbour (5-178, *Ostraka*) and nothing further under this name.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-179, *Ge Kaminon* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10762; name-index row in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` at row 952, translator gloss "Burnt Red Earth", class guess mineral; list row in `_sources/dioscorides.csv` at line 904, "Ge Kaminon", mineral; the book index lists "GE KAMINON 829", text line 12151). The whole chapter was read. It is one sentence (line 10764):

"Well-burnt red earth from furnaces has the same qualities as the tiles [above]."

The chapter therefore gives no properties of its own. It is defined by reference to the chapter directly before it, 5-178, *Ostraka* ("Clay Tiles", line 10754), which reads: "The tiles in furnaces that are very burnt are crustforming: as a result (rubbed on with vinegar) they heal itching and pustules breaking out, and are good for gout. Used with wax ointments they dissolve scrofulous tumours [glandular swelling] and goitres." (line 10757). Read together, the two chapters make the following record: a burnt red earth taken from furnaces, which "forms a crust" (the translator's "crustforming"), is rubbed on with vinegar for itching and pustules and for gout, and is mixed into wax ointments for scrofulous tumours and goitres. The second and third uses belong to the tiles' chapter and reach the earth only through the phrase "the same qualities"; the chapter does not say that the earth is used with vinegar or wax in the same way. **Joined in the source line.** In the text file the heading of the next chapter, "5-180. GE MELIA Earth from Melia", is printed on the same line as the end of this chapter (line 10764). That is a layout fault of the e-text, not an overlap of content; the Melian earth has its own entry (CPU-02429).

**What the chapter does not give.** No place of origin, no colour test beyond "red", no method of burning (the earth is already "well-burnt" when it comes from the furnace), no washing, no storage, no adulterant and no dose.

**Identity.** The translator offers only "Burnt Red Earth". The Greek *Ge Kaminon* is printed as the heading and the text does not explain it. Our reading, "kiln earth", comes from the chapter's own phrase "from furnaces". A fired clay of that kind is a common by-product of furnaces, but the chapter does not tell us which furnaces, so the material cannot be tied to a deposit or a mineral. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated from the chapter. The material is described only as red earth that has been burnt in furnaces. In general terms, fired clay is mainly aluminium silicate with iron oxide, which gives clays their red colour after heating in air; this is a general fact about fired clays and is not drawn from Dioscorides, and it is not asserted that this particular material has that composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** Dioscorides records a burnt red furnace earth as having "the same qualities" as furnace tiles, which are used on the skin for itching, pustules, gout, scrofulous swellings and goitres. The material is unspecific, the uses are ones for which proper medical assessment is needed (gout, glandular swelling, goitre), and furnace residue may carry whatever was smelted or fired in the furnace. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given in 5-179. For the tiles whose qualities it shares, 5-178 gives two vehicles: "rubbed on with vinegar" and "used with wax ointments" (line 10757). The earth's own chapter gives neither. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] As general facts: fired clay is inert and insoluble, and it has been used as a mild abrasive and drying powder; a furnace earth may be contaminated with metals or other residues from the furnace, and a powder applied to broken skin can carry infection. Gout and goitre are conditions with causes unrelated to any topical earth. These points are not drawn from the chapter.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

### Contraindications

- **Broken skin:** a non-sterile earth applied to broken skin can introduce infection.
- **Unknown furnace:** the chapter does not say which furnace the earth came from, and furnace residues may contain metals; do not use any furnace residue on the body.
- **Gout, swellings, goitre:** these need medical assessment; they are not conditions to treat with a rubbed-on earth.
- **Identity:** the material is a general description ("burnt red earth"), not a tied identification.
