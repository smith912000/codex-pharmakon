# Sison amomum — Sison (Dioscorides 3-64); Petroselinon (3-77)

## *Sison amomum* (the Dioscorides translators' suggested identification; hedge sison, bastard stone parsley) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** This plant is named only by Dioscorides (DMM). The binomial is the suggestion printed on the SUGGESTED lines of the translation by Osbaldeston and Wood (2000), not Dioscorides' own. In 3-64 *sison* is glossed *Sison amomum*, *Sium amomum*, *Sium aromaticum* ("Hedge Sison, Bastard Stone Parsley"). In 3-77 *petroselinon* the line reads "Petroselinum, Amomum officinarum, Petroselinum macedonicum [Fuchs], Sison amomum [Linnaeus]" with "[other usage] Petroselinum oreoselinum, Athamanta oreoselinum — Mountain Parsley". The two chapters are distinct plants in Dioscorides (a Syrian seed; Macedonian parsley), so the single binomial may merge them. **Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).**

*3-64 SISON* (heading at line 7104). The whole chapter: "Sison is a little seed similar to apium [3-77] that grows in Syria — somewhat long, black, with an acrid taste. It is taken in a drink for the spleen, painful urination, and retention of the menstrual flow. The inhabitants use it for a sauce, eating it with cucurbita [2-164] boiled with vinegar. It has (as it were) many little grains on the tops."

*3-77 PETROSELINON* (heading at line 7205). "Apium (also called petroselinum) grows in steep places in Macedonia. It has seed similar to ammi visagna but with a sweeter, sharp, aromatic scent. It is diuretic and expels the menstrual flow. It is good (taken in a drink) for gaseousness, griping of the stomach, and colus [colic], as well as pain in the sides, kidneys, and bladder. It is also mixed with urinary antidotes."

### Active Components

Both chapters describe aromatic seeds of the carrot family (Apiaceae), whose seeds generally contain essential oil. No constituent information was read for the plants Dioscorides describes. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides records an acrid seed drunk for spleen, urinary and menstrual complaints and used as a sauce (3-64), and an aromatic diuretic seed (3-77), as his claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

*3-64:* the seed "taken in a drink"; as a sauce, eaten with boiled *cucurbita* and vinegar. *3-77:* the seed taken in a drink, or mixed into urinary antidotes. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** 3-64 prints the seed as "acrid" in taste. 3-77 prints the seed as having "a sweeter, sharp, aromatic scent" and as diuretic. No warming or cooling quality or degree is printed in either chapter.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. The chapters offer a short discipline of attention: where the seed grows (Syria; Macedonian steep places), its shape, colour, taste and smell, and what it resembles (apium, *ammi visagna*). No status is assigned. The binomial is the translators' gloss and may merge two plants. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the source read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. This is a short record of an uncertain identity. Nothing is to be done with it.

### Contraindications

The identity is uncertain and no safe preparation can be stated. Both seeds are recorded as expelling the menstrual flow; do not use in pregnancy. The traditional uses are not advice.
