# Larkspur — Forking Larkspur, Field Larkspur, Knight's-Spur, Lousewort

## *Consolida regalis* (syn. *Delphinium consolida*) | Family: Ranunculaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT (Assyrian), DMM (Dioscorides), CM (Avicenna)

**Energetic Classification:** Hot and Dry in the 3rd degree (Galenic) — a sharp, biting, "vermin-killing" acrid whose heat is corrosive rather than nourishing.

**Training Phase:** Purification — and strictly as an **external, parasite-clearing** agent. Larkspur belongs to the phase of clearing the surface of the body, never to internal work.

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Assyrian (AT).** Mesopotamian medical practice made heavy use of acrid, insect- and vermin-repelling plants applied to the body and living quarters. Larkspur-type seeds sit within this Assyrian class of *pest-clearing* simples — sharp seeds pounded into washes to rid the head and skin of lice and their nits.

**Dioscorides (DMM).** In *De Materia Medica* the larkspurs (the *delphinion* / *consolida* group) are recorded chiefly for their seed, applied externally. The classical tradition's dominant use is unmistakable: a **pediculicide** — the pounded seed, in wine or wash, kills lice. Its ornamental spur-flowered form (the "dolphin" bud, *delphis*, giving the genus its name) made it a recognisable garden and field plant, but its medicine was surface-acting and antiparasitic, not internal.

**Avicenna (CM).** The *Qānūn* carries larkspur seed forward in the same register — a hot, sharp, drying external application against lice and skin vermin, and a topical for certain wounds and swellings. The Unani framing as Hot-and-Dry to a high degree matches its acrid, corrosive character and warns against internal use.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Larkspur is the plant of the *biting edge*. Its very folk-names — lousewort, knight's-spur — braid its two faces: the martial, spurred flower and the louse-killing seed. In the doctrine of signatures the sharp backward spur of the bloom mirrors the sharp, penetrating action of the seed on the skin. This is a plant whose entire traditional identity is *cleansing the body's exterior of parasites* — the symbolism points outward, away from ingestion, exactly as the toxicology demands.

### Active Components

- **Norditerpenoid (diterpenoid) alkaloids** — the defining toxic principals, including **delphinine, delcosine, delsoline, methyllycaconitine (MLA)** and related esters.
- These are potent antagonists at **nicotinic acetylcholine receptors** (MLA is a classic α7-nAChR probe), producing **neuromuscular blockade** and cardiac disturbance.
- Alkaloid profile and concentration vary by species, part, and growth stage — seeds are notably alkaloid-rich.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica, Larkspur is a **Purification-phase external antiparasitic only**. Its sanctioned use is topical: a seed preparation applied to the scalp or skin to clear lice and nits, then thoroughly rinsed — echoing its unbroken Assyrian–Dioscoridean–Unani record as a pediculicide. It is never taken internally, never used on broken skin over large areas, and never applied to children or in pregnancy. Its place in the curriculum is to teach the principle that some purifying agents act *at the boundary* and must be kept there — a poison harnessed against a smaller pest, under strict containment.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed: Wang et al. (2025), *Toxins*, reviewed the **insecticidal and antifeedant activity of diterpenoid alkaloids from *Delphinium* and *Aconitum***, confirming that C-diterpenoid alkaloids act as potent, selective insect-active agents — the pharmacological basis of larkspur's ancient use as a lice-killer and insecticide. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins17050254). Yin et al. (2021), *RSC Advances*, catalogued 157 hetisine-type diterpenoid alkaloids largely from Ranunculaceae (including *Delphinium*), documenting antimicrobial, insecticidal, and cardioactive (antiarrhythmic) properties tightly linked to alkaloid structure. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/d1ra07173d). Welch et al. (2017), *Toxicon*, quantified the serum toxicokinetics of the larkspur alkaloids methyllycaconitine and deltaline, establishing their absorption/elimination profile in a mammalian model and framing larkspur as a serious livestock poison. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.toxicon.2017.05.008).

**Evidence grade: MODERATE for toxicity and insecticidal pharmacology (well characterised); TRADITIONAL for the external pediculicide use.** There is no clinical evidence supporting any internal therapeutic use, and the toxicology firmly contraindicates it.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM analogue:** hot, acrid, toxic (*du*) — surface-clearing, insect-killing; used externally.
- **Ayurveda (by analogy):** *rasa* — katu (pungent), tikta (bitter); *guna* — tikshna (sharp), ruksha (dry); *virya* — ushna (heating); *vipaka* — katu. A *visha* (poison) class agent, external use only.
- **Galenic/Unani:** Hot and Dry, 3rd degree; sharp, biting, vermin-killing — external application, contraindicated internally.

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian (AT).** Larkspur-type acrid seeds fall within the Mesopotamian class of vermin- and pest-clearing simples pounded into washes for the head and skin. Illness in the Assyrian frame is classified by causal agency — divine hand, ghost, oath — and a purifying agent is understood partly by what agency it drives off; but the *asû*/*āšipu* record here is a surface therapeutic and apotropaic one, not the assignment of a vital status to the person's constitution. Under the standing blocking warning that governs every Assyrian substance identification and tablet reference — and given that the attestation rests on a seed-type class rather than a secured identification of *Consolida regalis* itself — no cultivation status is claimed. **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The larkspurs (*delphinion* / *consolida* group) are recorded in *De Materia Medica* chiefly for the seed, applied externally as a pediculicide. Dioscorides carries no subtle-body system into which such a substance could be graded, so there is no vital-cultivation status to state. What the tradition offers a cultivator is instead his discipline of attention: recognising the spurred "dolphin" bud, gathering and assaying the alkaloid-rich seed, and — the governing point for this plant — knowing that its action is surface-acting and antiparasitic and belongs on the skin, never taken inward. That is a real practice of provenance and sensory judgement, not a doctrine of the vital body. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The *Qānūn* carries larkspur seed as a Hot-and-Dry-to-a-high-degree acrid — a sharp, drying external application against lice, skin vermin, and certain swellings. The strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus is the conservation of innate heat (*ḥarārat gharīziyya*) and radical moisture as a finite vital endowment whose depletion is ageing; but that is a frame belonging to the whole regimen of life, and this corrosive, heating surface-drug is assigned no such conservational role, no *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* standing. It is attested therapeutically, with no cultivation status recorded. **(b) framed.**

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a substance a cultivator takes to build or conserve vitality. Across all three traditions larkspur seed is a poison harnessed at the boundary of the body — pounded into a wash to kill lice and nits, rinsed off, and never ingested. Its whole traditional identity points outward, away from the person's interior; the norditerpenoid alkaloids that make it an effective pediculicide are the same ones that make internal use lethal. The correct cultivation posture toward it is abstention from all internal work and strict external containment.

### Contraindications

**⚠️ TOXIC — NEUROMUSCULAR AND CARDIAC POISON. EXTERNAL, LIMITED USE ONLY.**

- **Never ingest larkspur in any form.** Norditerpenoid alkaloids (delphinine, methyllycaconitine and relatives) block nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, causing **muscle weakness and paralysis, respiratory failure, bradycardia and cardiac arrhythmia, collapse, and death.** Larkspur is a leading cause of livestock poisoning deaths.
- **Do not apply to broken skin, mucous membranes, the eyes, or over large body areas** — transdermal absorption of the alkaloids is a genuine poisoning route.
- **Contraindicated in pregnancy, in children, and in anyone with cardiac or neuromuscular disease.**
- Keep seeds away from children and animals; accidental ingestion is a medical emergency.
- Historical use is **insecticidal/pediculicidal only**. This entry endorses **no** internal use whatsoever. If poisoning is suspected, **seek emergency medical care immediately.**
