# Deer testicle — AIDOION ARRENOS ELAPHOU, Dioscorides De Materia Medica II.43, "Testes of Deer"
## *The genitals of a male deer (a "hart") as a remedy in Dioscorides. The 2000 English edition on disk prints the heading "2-43. AIDOION ARRENOS ELAPHOU" and the title "Testes of Deer"; it gives no binomial for this chapter. For the hart's horn at 2-63 the editor glosses "adult male Red Deer"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The text on disk is the English edition of *De Materia Medica* in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The testes of the deer stand in Book II, in the section headed "LIVING CREATURES" (line 4910), among the parts and products of land animals. The name list `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` has the row "DMM, AIDOION ARRENOS ELAPHOU, 2-43 (line 5111), Testes of Deer, animal". In the file the heading runs on from the end of the preceding chapter's last sentence, on the same line (5111). The chapter reads in full (lines 5111-5113):

"2-43. AIDOION ARRENOS ELAPHOU. Testes of Deer. The genitals of a male hart (pounded into small pieces and taken in a drink with wine) help those bitten by vipers."

So the chapter gives one part, the genitals of a male hart, one preparation (pounded and taken in a drink with wine), and one use, for those bitten by vipers. It is not described as an aphrodisiac or for any complaint of the generative organs, and I do not supply one. The word "hart" is the translator's English for the male deer, and the edition's own gloss at 2-63 reads "Burnt Horn of a Hart — adult male Red Deer" (line 5185). The edition's index lists "Testes of Deer 198".

Neighbouring chapters show how Dioscorides treats the animal's other parts, which I read and list so that they are not lost:

- **2-63, horn of a hart** (lines 5184-5188). The horn "(burnt, washed and two spoonfuls taken in a drink) is good with gum tragacanth for bloodspitters, dysentery, coeliac [intestinal complaints], jaundice and disorders of the bladder", and for women with "excessive [menstrual] discharge". Burnt until white and washed, it is "good for discharges and ulcers in the eyes" and cleans the teeth; "If burnt raw it drives away snakes with the smell." This chapter gives a measure ("two spoonfuls"), and the testes chapter does not.
- **2-94, fats** (line 5344). "Fat from elephants and deer (rubbed on) drives away snakes."
- **2-95, marrow** (line 5351). "Deer marrow is the best, then that from a calf, after, that from a bull, then a goat and a sheep"; "The bone marrow of a deer (rubbed on) also drives away venomous creatures."
- **2-88, suet** (line 5308). Goat, sheep "and furthermore deer suet" are prepared by washing, beating with water and melting.

In the lines read, Dioscorides pairs the deer with snakes repeatedly: the testes help those bitten by vipers, the burnt horn and the fat and marrow drive snakes away. That is a pattern in the text and I record it as one, without explaining it.

No provenance, season of gathering, adulteration or assay is given for the testes.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, testicular tissue is glandular tissue made of protein and lipid, and contains sex-steroid hormones (such as testosterone) and their precursors in small amounts that vary with the animal, its age and the season. Nothing more specific can be said without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records the testes of a male deer, pounded and taken in wine, for those bitten by vipers. The Codex does not recommend this. A snakebite is a medical emergency with a specific treatment, wine and animal tissue are not an antidote, and deer products are subject to hunting and trade controls in many places.

### Preparation & Dosing

The one preparation given (line 5113): the genitals of a male hart, "pounded into small pieces and taken in a drink with wine". The text gives no quantity, no number of doses and no duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. No animal tissue neutralises snake venom when drunk; venom is treated with antivenom, and a bite needs emergency care. Taking animal glandular tissue by mouth also carries an infection risk. Testicular tissue contains steroid hormones, but nothing in the chapter relies on that. ### Contraindications

- **Snakebite:** a bite from a venomous snake is a medical emergency; go to emergency care. Do not rely on any animal product or on wine.
- **Infection and hygiene:** raw or poorly prepared animal tissue is an infection risk, and wild game can carry zoonotic disease.
- **Hormones:** glandular tissue can contain active hormones; do not take it as a supplement.
- **Law and conservation:** deer are hunted under licence or protected in many jurisdictions, and trade in wild-animal products is restricted; the Codex does not recommend sourcing any.
- **Ethics:** the Codex does not recommend killing or harming wild animals for these uses.
