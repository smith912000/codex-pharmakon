# Tragion allo — ṭarāghiyūn ākhar (طراغيون آخر), also called saqūlūqandriyūn (سقولوقندريون)

## *A small leafless seashore plant with red berry-like grains, filed under the Canon's second heading "tragion, another"; the Canon gives the alternative name saqūlūqandriyūn, which is not resolved here; the cheap first-pass guess of an Artemisia (via the Dioscorides tradition) does not fit the description read, so the plant is left unidentified; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. Book II, entry *ṭarāghiyūn ākhar*, Arabic text from line 13744 of `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk was not run beyond the spine check. 

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** What it is (*al-māhiya*): "Some people call it *saqūlūqandriyūn*. It is a small plant on the face of the earth, a span long or a little more, and it mostly grows on the sea coasts. It has no leaf. On its twigs is something like grapes, small and red, the size of a grain of wheat, sharp-pointed, very many, astringent. Some people pound this grain and make tablets of it and store it for the time of need". Excretory organs: "if about ten grains are mixed with wine it benefits long-lasting diarrhoea and long-lasting flow of moistures from the womb, according to what Dioscorides claimed" (فيما زعم ديسقوريدوس). The entry therefore names Dioscorides as the source of the use, with the Canon's usual "claimed".

**Overlap.** The spine has the preceding heading as CPU-02748 *Tragion (Cretan gum plant)* and CPU-02723 *Tariqulyun*; the Canon calls this one "another". It is not merged here.

**Identity doubt.** The text describes a plant with no leaves, red astringent grains and a seashore habitat. It does not give a Greek chapter number. The spine's note says the Dioscorides tradition on disk maps to *Artemisia*; the description does not fit that, and no identification is offered. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None is given. The text calls the grains astringent (*qābiḍ*), which in general points to tannins; this is a general inference, not in the text. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The text records ten grains in wine for chronic diarrhoea and chronic discharge from the womb. Both are medical conditions; the Codex offers no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

Ten grains or so mixed with wine. Tablets of the pounded grain were stored (13747 on). No dose in modern units. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Astringent plant matter is generally rich in tannins. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

The grains are "astringent" (قابض). No degree of heat or dryness is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No cultivation framing is attested. **(c) Absent.** The *Canon* entry is organised by organ and complaint and says nothing here about innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruh*. 

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to practise. It is a coastal astringent grain of unknown species, quoted from Dioscorides.

### Contraindications

- Chronic diarrhoea and uterine discharge need medical care.
- The plant is not identified; do not gather seashore plants on the strength of this entry.
