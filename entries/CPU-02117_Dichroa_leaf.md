# Dichroa leaf — Shu Qi 蜀漆 (Shennong bencao jing); "Shu lacquer", the leaf of Heng Shan 恒山

## *Dichroa febrifuga Lour. (Hydrangeaceae), a shrub of south-west and southern China whose leaf and young shoot are the drug shu qi and whose root is the drug chang shan or heng shan; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence at species level, since the classic describes no plant, but the Wu Pu layer and the Guangya, quoted by Sun, equate shu qi with the leaf of heng shan, and Yang 1998 prints "Herba Dichroae Febrifugae"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** This is a lower-class drug that the Wu Pu layer marks "toxic" (有毒) and that Yang's translation prints as "toxic". The plant is strongly emetic and potentially dangerous. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take this plant.

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; the other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), is read as a second witness and attributed to Yang. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Herba Dichroae Febrifugae [SBJ]", taken from Yang's English; this entry replaces it. A search of the other texts on disk for *Dichroa* and for the Chinese and pinyin names found nothing.

**Overlap.** The same plant supplies a second Shennong entry, 恒山 *heng shan* (the root), which is I_1525 (Dichroa, tag SBJ). Sun's edition has two separate monographs (恒山 at line 3241 and 蜀漆 here). The Wu Pu layer of this entry says 蜀漆叶，一名恒山 ("*shu qi*, the leaf; one name *heng shan*"). The two entries are not merged. This one covers the monograph named 蜀漆.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 蜀漆 in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 蜀漆 *shu qi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3251, 味 paragraph; name line 3249). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味辛，平 ("acrid, balanced"); 主疟及咳逆寒热 ("governs malaria and cough and counterflow with cold and heat"), 腹中症坚 ("hard concretions in the abdomen"), 痞结 ("glomus and binding"), 积聚 ("accumulations and gatherings"), 邪气 ("evil qi"), 蛊毒 ("*gu* toxins"), 鬼注 ("demonic influx"; a note says the old text wrote 疰 and the *Yulan* 蛀 for the last word); and 生川谷 ("grows in rivers and valleys"). The paragraph does not call the drug toxic, and it has no 久服 clause.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 3253) gives: "*Shu qi* leaf, one name *Heng shan*. Shennong, Qi Bo, Lei Gong: acrid, toxic (有毒); Huangdi: acrid; another classic: sour." It describes the plant: 如漆叶蓝青相似 ("like the lacquer leaf, resembling indigo-green"), gathered in the fifth month. The Mingyi bielu layer (line 3255) gives the habitat 生江陵山及蜀汉中常山 ("Mount Jiangling and, in Shu and Hanzhong, Changshan") and says 苗也，五月采叶，阴干 ("it is the seedling; the leaf is gathered in the fifth month and dried in the shade"). This is the only place that names the part used, the leaf.

Sun's note (案, line 3257) cites the *Guangya*: 恒山，蜀漆也 ("*Heng shan* is *shu qi*") and the *Fanzi jiran*: 蜀漆，出蜀郡 ("*shu qi* comes from Shu commandery"). These are the commentator's citations and are not Shennong text.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 3512): "Shu Qi (Herba Dichroae Febrifugae) is acrid, balanced, and toxic. It mainly treats malaria, cough and counterflow, cold and heat, hard concretions and binding glomus in the abdomen, accumulation and gathering evil qi, gu toxins, and demonic influx. It grows in rivers and valleys." This agrees with Sun's indications. Yang's "toxic" is not in Sun's Shennong paragraph; it agrees with the Wu Pu layer and is Yang's addition to the Shennong text. Yang's footnote 217 (line 3553) on the root entry (Heng Shan) says the drug "is more often prescribed to provoke vomiting" than to stop it (see I_1525).

**Appendix.** The drug-relations table reads 蜀漆 栝楼为使；恶贯众 ("*shu qi*: *gua lou* is its envoy; it dislikes *guan zhong*", line 4449), 虎掌 蜀漆为使；畏莽草 ("*hu zhang*: *shu qi* is its envoy; it fears *mang cao*", line 4451), and, at lines 4587 and 4609, two animal drugs (海蛤 and 鱼甲) for which *shu qi* is the envoy. The preface (line 4117) says to use pairs that "need" or "serve" one another and not those that "dislike" (恶) or oppose one another; the Codex draws no further conclusion.

### Active Components

*Dichroa febrifuga* is the source of the quinazolinone alkaloids febrifugine and isofebrifugine, which have antimalarial activity and are strongly emetic and toxic; a synthetic derivative, halofuginone, has been developed from febrifugine. These are well-established facts about the plant, which occur in both the root and the leaf. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Toxic.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Malaria, cough and counterflow with cold and heat, hard concretions and binding in the abdomen, accumulations, *gu* toxins and demonic influx.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation beyond drying the leaf, no duration.

The emetic action is the practical danger: the plant causes severe vomiting. No Kenetica protocol is derived from this entry, and none should be.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest: the leaf, gathered in the fifth month and dried in the shade (line 3255). The preface contains a general rule for poisonous drugs (line 4125: 若用毒药疗病，先起如黍粟，病去，即止。不去，倍之；不去，十之。取去为度, "if a poisonous drug is used to treat disease, begin at the size of a millet grain; when the illness goes, stop; if not, double it; if not, tenfold; take going [of the illness] as the measure"). This is the classic's own rule of method, written for toxic drugs in general; it is not a dose for this plant and it is not offered as one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

*Dichroa febrifuga* is the plant from which the antimalarial and emetic alkaloid febrifugine was isolated, and the toxicity in the Wu Pu layer agrees with the plant's strongly emetic action. The classic's malaria indication agrees in kind with the plant's antimalarial alkaloids, but the same alkaloids are what make the plant emetic and toxic. The other conditions named (concretions, glomus, *gu* toxins, demonic influx) have no modern equivalents and are not translated into any. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Sun 1799).** Flavour and nature 味辛，平, "acrid, balanced" (line 3251). Wu Pu: "Shennong, Qi Bo, Lei Gong: acrid, toxic; Huangdi: acrid; another classic: sour" (line 3253).

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic, whose preface (line 2935) reads 下药，一百二十五种，为左使。主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经: the lower drugs are "the left envoys, mainly treating disease to correspond with earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to remove cold and heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and heal illness". The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no promise of lightening the body or of not ageing. The register is therefore (b): framed by the lower class, whose own preface is the warning, and with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade, toxic emetic leaf for malaria and abdominal masses. The tradition's own grading is the warning, and nothing in the record supports any cultivation use. A cultivator should not use it.

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC AND POTENTIALLY DANGEROUS.** The Wu Pu layer marks it toxic, and the plant is strongly emetic. Do not ingest it. Do not gather it.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Given the toxicity, avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Malaria and fevers.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Identity.** The leaf (*shu qi*) and the root (*chang shan*, *heng shan*) are two Shennong monographs of one plant, and the early plant is our identification, of medium confidence. [USER: verify]
