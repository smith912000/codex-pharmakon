# Camel thorn — Duralabha (printed "Dursilabliab" in the OCR of the Sushruta Appendix; "Duralabha" in the translation's text), Alhagi maurorum

## *Alhagi maurorum Medik., the camel thorn, a spiny desert shrub whose twigs exude a sugary manna. The identification rests on the translator's Appendix 3, where the name printed "Dursilabliab" (OCR for Dursilabha/Duralabha) is paired by position with "Alhagi Maurorun" (OCR); the pairing was checked in column order, and the text's own sugar passage calls the sugar "Yavasa Sharkara (Duralabha)". Confidence medium.* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta Samhita (Bhishagratna) among the texts on disk, under this Sanskrit name.** This is a single-text entry as tagged. The Dioscorides spine row "Aspalathus" (CPU-00010) carries the botanical "Convolvulus dorycnium L. OR Alhagi maurorum Medik." as an uncertain identification; it is a different text and name and is not merged here.

**Sushruta (SS), the Appendix index.** The Appendix 3 index of Bhishagratna's Vol. III (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`) is a two-column list of Sanskrit names with their botanical identifications, which the OCR separates into a name block and an identification block. The names block (page 69) runs: Dravantika / Dravanti (printed together), Drona-pushpi, Dugdhika, "Dursilabliab" (line 31638), Durva. The identification block runs: "Anthericum Tuberosum", "Leucas Linifolia", "Oxystelma esculentum", "Alhagi Maurorun" (line 31646), "Cynodon dactylon". Five names and five identifications; "Dursilabliab" is fourth in the names and "Alhagi Maurorun" fourth in the identifications. Drona-pushpi is paired with *Leucas*, Dugdhika with *Oxystelma* and Durva with *Cynodon*. The names and identifications are in the same order throughout the page, which supports the column pairing.

**Sushruta (SS), the chapters.**

- **Sutrasthana ch. 45** (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` line 1351) in the sugars passage: "Sugar prepared from a decoction of Yavasa Sharkara (Duralabha) has a sweet and astringent taste, leaves a bitter after-taste, and is possessed of laxative properties, and subdues the deranged Kapham." The translator's bracket equates *yavasa sharkara* with Duralabha.
- **Sutrasthana ch. 44** (`SS_Sutra_ch44.txt` line 221) in the purgatives: "A purgative compound consisting of Shyama (Vriddhadaraka), Trivrit, Xili [sic, OCR], Katvi, Musta, Duralabha, Chavya, Indrayava and Triphala, administered through the vehicle of clarified butter, essence of meat, or water, is commended to persons of dry temperament."
- **Uttarasthana ch. 39** (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt` line 1166; also Vol. III line 8492), in the fever treatment: "Two-Tola-measure of powdered Ananta (Duralabha), Valaka, Musta, Shunthi and Katuka should be given with (one Pala of) tepid water." Here the translator's bracket places Duralabha beside "Ananta". Whether Ananta is a second name for the same plant, or a different drug that the translator equates with it, is not said in the lines read. [USER: verify.]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Alhagi* is a spiny legume of dry regions, and the sugary exudate known as camel-thorn manna contains sugars; the plant is grazed by camels. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says.** The sugar from its decoction is sweet and astringent, bitter in after-taste, laxative, and subdues deranged Kapham (ch. 45). The plant is an ingredient in a purgative compound for persons of dry temperament (ch. 44) and in a powder given with tepid water in fever (Uttara 39).
- **What the text does not say.** It gives no separate description of the plant.

The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

Three forms are given: sugar from a decoction (ch. 45); a purgative compound with ghee, meat essence or water as vehicle (ch. 44); and a powder in tepid water (Uttara 39). The one quantity read is "Two-Tola-measure" of powdered Ananta (Duralabha), with the other four drugs, given in "one Pala of" tepid water (Uttara 39, line 1166), with the proviso above about Ananta. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Alhagi maurorum* is a legume of dry regions in the Middle East and Central and South Asia, and its manna is a sugar-rich exudate. Laxative sugars and plant exudates exist in general, but this entry draws no conclusion for this plant from outside the text. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (ch. 45).** The sugar from Yavasa (Duralabha) is *madhura* (sweet) and *kashaya* (astringent) in taste, with a bitter after-taste, laxative, and "subdues the deranged Kapham." No *virya* or *vipaka* is given in the lines read for the plant itself.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Duralabha appears in the purgative and fever passages and, as a sugar, in the dietetic section. None of the lines read places it in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no statement about *ojas* is attached to it. Its status is therapeutic and dietetic only.

**Practitioner's note.** A purgative and laxative drug in the Sushruta lists. Nothing in the record supports a cultivation use; the sources attach no *rasayana* status to it.

### Contraindications

- **Laxative effect.** The text calls the sugar laxative; avoid in diarrhoea and dehydration.
- **Identity.** The identification rests on an OCR column pairing and a translator's bracket; the Ananta equation is unresolved. [USER: verify.]
