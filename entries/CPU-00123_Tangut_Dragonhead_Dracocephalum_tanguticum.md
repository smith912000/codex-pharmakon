### Origin & Tradition

Tangut dragonhead is an aromatic mint-family herb (Lamiaceae) of the Tibetan plateau and high western China, one of the *Dracocephalum* ("dragonhead") species used in Tibetan and regional Chinese medicine. Strongly fragrant, with the family's volatile-oil richness, it is gathered in flower and dried for use, and is documented in the *rGyud-bzhi* and Tibetan materia medica among the aromatic herbs for liver, stomach and "heat" disorders.

In the Tibetan tradition Tangut dragonhead is used as a **cooling, aromatic herb for the liver and stomach** — for liver "heat" and inflammatory conditions (including hepatitis-type and biliary disorders in the Tibetan framework), for stomach complaints and indigestion, and for cough and respiratory conditions; it is also used for wounds and as a general aromatic. Related *Dracocephalum* species (e.g. *D. moldavica*, Moldavian dragonhead/Moldavian balm) are used across Central Asia and the Caucasus as calming, carminative, cooling aromatic teas, and the genus as a whole carries this fragrant, cooling, digestive-and-calming character.

The activity rests on the **essential oil** (rich in the Lamiaceae monoterpenes — citral, geraniol, geranial and relatives in several *Dracocephalum*) and on **flavonoids and rosmarinic acid**, the antioxidant-anti-inflammatory constituents typical of the mint family.

Botanically, the Flora of China (Vol. 17, via efloras.org) describes *D. tanguticum* as a perennial herb, noted there as fetid, with erect obtusely four-angled stems to 55 cm. The leaf blades are pinnatisect with two or three pairs of linear segments, the calyx is purplish and golden-glandular, and the corolla is purple-blue to dark purple, 2-2.7 cm long. It flowers from June to September. The Flora records it from riverbanks, fields, grassy beaches, dry lake beds, sunny hillsides and pine-forest margins at 3200-4700 m in Gansu, Qinghai, Sichuan and Xizang.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan medicine (FMT).** The rGyud-bzhi materia medica gives the drug pri.yang.ku with two alternative modern identifications, Callicarpa macrophylla or Dracocephalum tanguticum. It is described as bitter-sweet, haemostatic, wound-healing, drying lymph accumulations and curing liver and stomach fevers (rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch. 20, Clark tr. 1995). The Men-Tsee-Khang translation of the same chapter has "Tiyangku cures hot disorder of the stomach and liver" (rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch. 20, Men-Tsee-Khang tr. 2011). This is a therapeutic attestation within the tradition's drug classification, grade (b): no bcud-len or rejuvenation assignment is recorded for it, and the species identification is modern and uncertain.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing cultivation-specific. In the tradition it is a remedy for hot liver and stomach disorders, prescribed by a physician; the sources give no long-term or rejuvenative use.
