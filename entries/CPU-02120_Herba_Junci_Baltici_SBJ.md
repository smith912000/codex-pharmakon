# Herba Junci Baltici — Shi Long Chu (Yang 1998); in the classic 石龙刍 *shi long chu*, aliases 龙须 *Long Xu* "Dragon's Beard", 草续断 *Cao Xu Duan*, 龙珠 *Long Zhu*

## *A rush-like herb that the classic's commentary says resembles 莞 but is finer and grows in rock crevices; Yang 1998 prints "Herba Junci Baltici" (Juncus balticus Willd.), which is his identification and is not confirmed here, so the species is of low confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry: named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The row was filed from Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 English translation, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, "Herbs: Superior Class, PDF p.52 (line 2016)". The principal text for the Chinese is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; Yang is read as a second witness and is attributed to Yang. A search of the other traditions' texts on disk was not made beyond the Chinese name. [USER: verify no other tradition names it.]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun's entry is 石龙刍 in the upper classic (line 1015, closing 卷一 上经 at line 1025; also in the upper-class list at line 45). Line 1017 reads 味苦，微寒。主心腹邪气，小便不利，淋闭，风湿，鬼注，恶毒。久服，补虚羸，轻身。耳目聪明，延年。一名龙须，一名草续断，一名龙珠。生山谷 ("bitter, slightly cold; governs evil qi in the heart and abdomen, inhibited urination, dribbling block, wind-damp, 鬼注, malign toxin. Taken long, it supplements vacuity and emaciation, lightens the body, sharpens ears and eyes, and extends years. Names 龙须, 草续断, 龙珠. Grows in mountain valleys").

Yang (line 2016): "Shi Long Chu (Herba Junci Baltici) is bitter and slightly cold. It mainly treats evil qi in the heart and abdomen, inhibited urination, dribbling block, wind dampness, demonic influx, and [worm] malign toxins. Protracted taking may supplement vacuity with languor, make the body light, sharpen the ears and eyes, and prolong life. Its other name is Long Xu (Dragon's Beard). Yet another name is Cao Xu Duan (Herbaceous Dipsacus). It grows in mountains and valleys." Yang omits the alias 龙珠.

Layers in Sun (line 1019 onward, as printed): the Wu Pu layer lists further names (龙刍, 龙多, 续断, 龙本, 草毒, 龙华, 悬莞) and gives differing flavour judgements by earlier authorities ("Shennong, Li Shi: slightly cold; Lei Gong, Huang Di: bitter, non-toxic; Bian Que: acrid, non-toxic"), with the habitat 梁州 and harvest on the seventh day of the seventh month. The Mingyi layer gives 生梁州湿地，五月、七月采茎，曝干 ("grows in damp ground in Liangzhou; the stem is gathered in the fifth and seventh months and dried in the sun"), so the part named is the stem. Sun's note (line 1023) says the plant "resembles 莞 but is finer; it grows in cavities in mountain rock; the stems hang in rows and can be made into mats", and cites Zheng Qiao that it is the 鼠莞 of the *Erya*. These are the only descriptions of the plant retrieved.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Evil qi in the heart and abdomen, inhibited urination and dribbling block, wind-damp, "demonic influx" (鬼注) and malign toxins; and, on long taking, the four promises quoted above.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation beyond drying the stem, and no duration.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend using this plant. Identification is unresolved, so even a historical use cannot be assigned to a modern species. [USER: verify]

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given in the classic. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest: the stem, gathered in the fifth and seventh months and dried in the sun (Sun, line 1021). Sun's note says the stems can be woven into mats (line 1023). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Rushes of the genus *Juncus* are widespread wetland plants, and some are used as lamp wicks and for weaving. The classic's "inhibited urination" and "dribbling block" have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* Sun's reconstruction places 石龙刍 in the upper classic (line 45 list; entry under 卷一 上经, line 1025), whose preface (line 43) reads 上药一百二十种，为君，主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经: the upper drugs are "lords, mainly nourishing life to correspond with heaven, non-toxic; taken in quantity or long, they do not harm; for those who wish to lighten the body, benefit qi, not age, and extend years". The drug's own paragraph carries the promises 久服…轻身…延年 ("taken long: lightens the body… extends years"), which meet the project's rule for (a) (Spec, SBJ grading rule of 2026-10-30). Yang prints the same promises ("make the body light… prolong life", line 2016) and also places it in the Superior class (p.52), so there is no difference of grade between the two editions. The grade is a text-internal one, and 轻身 is not translated into any modern effect. Neidan practice is not a bencao category and is not claimed here.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade drug in the Chinese scheme, which is the grade of substances taken long-term to nourish life. The plant cannot be named with confidence, and the classic gives no dose or preparation. A cultivator should treat this as a textual record and not as something to take.
