# Magnolia flower bud — 辛夷 *xinyi*, also 辛矧, 侯桃, 房木

## *Magnolia biondii and related species (M. denudata and M. liliflora are also given in the modern literature), the magnolia flower bud of Chinese materia medica; the identification is ours, made from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence. The classic's own harvest word is "fruit", not "bud", as set out below* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on one text in one edition, Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). A search of the other texts on disk for *Magnolia biondii*, *M. denudata* and *M. liliflora* found nothing.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The name line is 辛夷 (line 1259). The classic's own paragraph, the only layer that is "Shennong", is at line 1261: 味辛，温。主五脏身体寒风，头脑痛，面[a character is missing in the transcription]。久服下气、轻身、明目、增年、耐老。一名辛矧（《御览》作引），一名侯桃，一名房木。生川谷。 In English: "Flavour acrid, warm. Governs cold-wind (寒风) in the five zang and the body, pain in head and brain (头脑痛), [a facial complaint, the character lost]. Taken long: brings down qi (下气), lightens the body, brightens the eyes, adds years, resists ageing (久服下气、轻身、明目、增年、耐老). Also called 辛矧 (the *Yulan* has 辛引), 侯桃, 房木. Grows in river valleys." The facial complaint is cut off by the lost character, and the Codex does not supply it.

The classic places the entry in the upper class (上经; heading 卷一 上经 at line 1267). The preface (line 43) says the upper drugs nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic, and may be taken long and much without harm.

**Mingyi bielu (《名医》, later additions) layer.** Line 1263: "gathered in the ninth month as the fruit, dried in the sun" (九月采实，曝干). The word is 实, "fruit" or "seed". The modern drug is the flower bud, and the Chinese name of the modern drug is the same name. The classic's own paragraph names no part. The Codex therefore says only that the ancient text's drug is called 辛夷 and that the Mingyi layer gathers its "fruit" in the ninth month. Whether the drug of this text was the bud is not shown by the lines read. [USER: verify]

**Sun Xingyan's note (案).** Line 1265 collects glosses. The *Han shu*, in Yang Xiong's *fu*, says "arrayed the 新雉 in the woods and thickets" (列新雉于林薄), and Yan Shigu comments that 新雉 is 辛夷, "a very large tree, wood, branches and leaves all fragrant, also called 新矧" (为树甚大，其木枝叶皆芳，一名新矧). The *Shiji* biography of Sima Xiangru has "mixed with 流夷", glossed in the *Han shu yinyi* as 新夷. Tao Hongjing says: "when small, the qi is acrid and fragrant, which is the 辛夷 that the *Lisao* calls" (小时气辛香，即《离骚》所呼辛夷者). A last citation, whose author's name is garbled in the transcription (printed 陈藏笔器), says "at first it comes out like a brush" (初发如笔) and "northerners call it..." and then breaks off. The brush-like shape is the only trace of the bud in this edition, and it comes from a later commentary, not the classic.

### Active Components

None is stated in the text. As a general fact, *Magnolia* flower buds contain volatile oils (the buds are aromatic, and the 案 quotes "acrid and fragrant") and a range of lignans and alkaloids typical of the genus. The Codex retrieved no analytical source for this entry and names no compound. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic uses the drug for cold-wind in the body and head and brain pain, and promises a series of long-use effects. The Codex gives no protocol and does not turn "cold-wind" or "pain in head and brain" into modern diagnoses. The magnolia bud remains a standard drug in modern Chinese materia medica, a general fact, and the Codex does not describe its modern use further.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic gives no preparation, no part and no quantity. The Mingyi layer gives the harvest (the ninth month, fruit) and the drying (in the sun). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

The identification is ours; the Mingyi layer's "fruit" and the modern flower bud do not match on the face of the text, and the 案 quotes a description of an emerging "brush". No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the upper class (上经), and its 味 paragraph carries a 久服 clause with life-nourishing effects: 久服下气、轻身、明目、增年、耐老, "taken long: bring down qi, lighten the body, brighten the eyes, add years, resist ageing" (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 辛夷 *xinyi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1261). "Lighten the body" (轻身) is the classic's effect category and not a weight claim, and 下气 is rendered literally ("bring down qi") and is not given a modern meaning. The grade and the clause are the classic's. The Mingyi and 案 layers add no cultivation clause.

**Practitioner's note.** The text promises long-use benefits and gives no way to realise them: no part, no preparation, no dose, and a fragmentary indication. A cultivator can read it as the record of an upper-class drug and no more. Any use of a magnolia bud today should rest on a modern source for the actual plant and part. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

The classic does not warn. No safety information for pregnancy, children, allergy or other medicines was read, and none is asserted. The identification is ours and several *Magnolia* species are sold under this name. Head pain and any facial complaint need medical assessment. No conservation information was read. [USER: verify]
