**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Two chapters in Book 5 (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`). 5-109 KINNABARI, "red crystalline Mercuric Sulphide — Cinnabar" (line 10394), which the text distinguishes from "ammium", made in Spain "from a certain stone mixed with Arguritidic sand"; the ammium "has among metals a choking smell, and therefore the workmen there put bladders around their faces that they may see but not inhale the vapour". The cinnabar he describes is "brought from Africa" and is so scarce that painters have difficulty getting it, and "as a result some thought it was the blood of dragons"; it "has the same properties as haematite, good for eye medicines and even more effectively, for it is more astringent and blood staunching", and "Taken with wax ointments it heals burns and pustules that break out." 5-110 UDRAGUROS, "Hydrargyrum — Argentum vivum — Mercury — Quicksilver", headed TOXIC (line 10398): "Hydrargyrum is made from ammion, which is also incorrectly called cinnabar", by heating it in a covered pot with an iron spoon and scraping off the soot that sticks to the pot, which "becomes hydrargyrum"; it is also found "where silver is smelted, gathered together in drops on the roofs"; it "is kept in glass, lead, tin or silver jars for it eats through all other matter and runs out. It is destructive. Taken as a drink it eats through the internal organs by its weight." The remedy is "a lot of milk", or wine with wormwood, or decoctions of smallage, *Salvia horminum*, origanum or hyssop with wine, and "(Gold dust, that is, the smallest scraping, is a miraculous help for hydrargyrum poisoning)". No therapeutic use of mercury itself is given in 5-110.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** `_sources/avicenna.csv` has a single list row, "Mercury, Hydrargyrum Hg, mineral, Canon II" (id_uncertain: no). That row is the whole attestation. No Avicenna text is on disk, no *Canon* chapter reference exists to give, and nothing is said of what the *Canon* does with mercury. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), "Jades and Stones: Superior Class". Shui Yin (Mercurius), line 878 (`extract/names.csv`): "Shui Yin (Mercurius) is acrid and cold. It mainly treats scabs, itching sores, and bald white scalp sores, kills worms and lice on the skin, induces abortion, and eliminates fever. It kills the toxins of gold, silver, copper, and tin. When melted, it reduces to Cinnabar. Protracted taking may make one an ever-living immortal. It is produced from the earth in the plains" (lines 878-883). Dan Sha (Cinnabar), line 869, is "sweet and slightly cold", treats "hundreds of diseases of the five viscera and the body", and "is capable of transforming into mercury" (lines 869-877); Yang's note 34 says it "is now mainly used to treat confused spirit, fright palpitations, fearful throbbing, and insomnia".

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Parada* is rare. In *Chikitsasthana* ch.25, "Misraka-Chikitsita" (miscellaneous treatments), the recipe for *Vyanga* and similar blemishes of the face lists "Lakshá, Rodhra ... Nága (lead), Gairika ... Mercury and the drugs of the Kakolyadi group" pasted with milk and cooked with lard, marrow, wax, ghee and milk, giving "the best of all the unguents that may be applied to the face" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch25.txt` lines 232-246). In *Kalpa* ch.3, drums and instruments smeared with anti-poisonous compounds are beaten round poisoned animals, with "equal parts of silver, mercury and Indra-Gopa insects with Kuru-Vinda ... pasted with the bile of a Kapila (brown) cow" (`kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch03.txt` lines 131-141). ### Active Components

Mercury is the element Hg, a dense metal that is liquid at room temperature and gives off vapour. Its principal ore is cinnabar, mercuric sulphide (HgS), red, which is the "ammion" and "kinnabari" of the Greek text and is the source of the metal when heated, as Dioscorides describes in 5-110. Cinnabar is poorly soluble; the metal, its vapour, its soluble salts and organic mercury compounds are the hazards. The traditional preparations are unstandardised and their composition is not given in the lines read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Mercury is a cumulative poison, and nothing in these sources supports taking it. The Chinese source puts it in the grade of long-term "nourishing life" drugs while the Greek source headings it TOXIC; the Codex records both and recommends neither. It offers no protocol, dose or preparation. Ritual uses (the Sanskrit offering) are recorded as ritual.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only preparations the texts give. DMM 5-110: quicksilver made by heating *ammion* in a clay-sealed pot with an iron spoon and a fire of coals underneath, the soot scraped off and cooled; kept in vessels of glass, lead, tin or silver. SBJ: "When melted, it reduces to Cinnabar" (Shui Yin, line 881); Dan Sha "is capable of transforming into mercury". SS: pasted with milk with other drugs into a face unguent (ch.25), as an offering (Uttara ch.35), in a drum plaster (Kalpa ch.3); no preparation of mercury itself is given in these lines. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Mercury and its compounds are toxic to the nervous system and the kidneys, and inorganic mercury salts were used in European medicine for centuries (for example against syphilis) until abandoned for toxicity. Heating cinnabar to obtain the metal, as Dioscorides describes, releases mercury vapour, which is hazardous; his note about workmen covering their faces is consistent with that. No clinical studies were retrieved for the traditional uses. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No Egyptian locus for mercury was found. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*.

**Assyrian (AT).** *(c) Absent, no locus.* Nothing was read. The blocking warning is in force. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, thinly.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. The chapters offer the discipline of attention in its most protective form: tell ammion from cinnabar, know that the ore's vapour is dangerous (the workmen's bladders), know that the metal eats through every vessel but glass, lead, tin and silver, and know the antidotes. Nothing in them assigns mercury a place in the cultivation of the person.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent, and the attestation rests on a list row.* The only attestation is the `_sources/avicenna.csv` row. Innate heat and radical moisture cannot be tied to mercury without a source and are not tied to it here. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: Superior Class.* Shui Yin stands under the heading "Jades and Stones: Superior Class". The preface (lines 352-355 and 383-384) says of that class that its members "mainly nourish life and correspond to heaven. They are nontoxic and taking them in large amounts and for a long time will not harm people", and "If one intends to make one's body light, boost the qi, prevent aging, and prolong life, one should base [one's efforts] on the superior class". The entry itself says "Protracted taking may make one an ever-living immortal". Yang's note to the preface says "it was principally the Daoists who created the early materia medica literature", concerned with "the achievement of 'immortality'" through "elixirs" (lines 375-378). The "nontoxic" wording is the text's claim for the class, and it conflicts with Dioscorides' TOXIC heading; the Codex records the class assignment as the tradition's and does not endorse the claim. The text's own entry for Shui Yin also lists "induces abortion". The translator renders the substance "Mercurius"; the jing, qi and shen language is not printed for Shui Yin and none is asserted. Neidan is an overlay and not a *bencao* category.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, thinly.* *Parada* is a rare, late-appearing ingredient: a face unguent, a ritual offering, an anti-poison drum plaster. It is not named in the *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapters searched (*Chikitsasthana* ch.26-30), and the editor's note says ch.25 is its first mention. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim for it is printed. The mercurial *rasashastra* literature is later than the text on disk and is not read here.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing safe to take from this entry, and nothing that supports taking mercury. The Chinese grading is a historical claim about a toxic metal, recorded as such. The practical lesson of the sources is Dioscorides': know the vapour hazard, the vessel, and the antidotes, and leave the material alone. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Mercury, cinnabar and every mercurial "ash" or "elixir" preparation are poisonous: they injure the nervous system, kidneys and gut, and heating them releases toxic vapour. The risk is cumulative, and the Chinese source's own entry lists abortion. Mercury is banned or restricted as a medicine in many countries, and its handling and disposal are regulated. Do not use. Ritual and offering uses are ritual, not therapy.
