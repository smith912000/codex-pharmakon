### Origin & Tradition

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000). **Named only by Dioscorides in this row.** The binomial is the translators' suggestion, not Dioscorides' own identification. The text gives almost nothing, and this is a SHORT entry.

*4-56 CHRUSOGONON* (heading at line 8492). The SUGGESTED line (line 8493) reads "Bongardia chrysogonum — Golden Rod". That is the only name the translators give, and the common name is printed as it stands.

The whole chapter (line 8494): "Chrysogonum has leaves similar to the oak, but the shrub is thick, and has flowers similar to verbascum coronarium [4-104]; a root similar to rape [coleseed], strongly red within but with the exterior black. Pounded finely with vinegar and applied, it helps the bites of the shrewmouse."

That is a short description (oak-like leaves, a thick shrub, flowers like *verbascum coronarium*, a rape-like root red inside and black outside) and one use (the root pounded with vinegar and applied to shrew bites). The chapter prints no alternative names, no habitat and no dose. The preceding chapter, 4-55 CHRUSOKOME (heading at line 8484), is a different plant ("Chrysocoma ... Goldylocks") and is not read into this row, and the following 4-57 ELICHRUSON (line 8495) is likewise different.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The one use is an external application of the pounded root with vinegar to the bite of a shrew. This is the text's own claim and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol for animal bites, which need medical care.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives one preparation: the root "pounded finely with vinegar and applied" (line 8494). No quantity or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and the chapter assigns the plant no status. What the chapter offers is a short discipline of attention: the oak-like leaf, the thick shrub, the flower likened to a mullein, and the root that is black outside and strongly red within, which is the character a gatherer would test. The identification with *Bongardia chrysogonum* is the translators' suggestion, and the text's description is too thin to confirm it. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in this chapter gives a cultivator a practice. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The chapter prints no warning. The plant's identity is uncertain, and its safety is unknown from the sources retrieved, so no plant should be gathered or applied on the strength of this entry. An animal bite needs medical assessment, including for infection and for rabies risk where relevant.
