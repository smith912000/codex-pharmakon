### Origin & Tradition

Gypsum is soft, fibrous, translucent calcium sulfate — the same mineral that, calcined, becomes plaster of Paris and the surgeon's setting-cast (a use its Chinese and Western names both echo). In its raw, uncalcined form it is one of the most important **cooling minerals** of the Chinese materia medica: **Shí Gāo** ("stone paste"), the reference-standard drug for high, blazing, "Yangming" fever. Where the bitter-cold herbs (goldthread I_302, gypsum's frequent partner) drain fire by their bitterness, gypsum drains it by a different, acrid-sweet, intensely cold and "heavy-sinking" action — the mineral that quenches the body's furnace.

**Chinese Tradition (SBJ — Shén Nóng Běncǎo Jīng):**
Shí Gāo is recorded in the *Shén Nóng Běncǎo Jīng* and became the cornerstone of the heat-clearing method. Its actions: it **clears heat and drains fire** at the Qì level — the chief mineral for the "four bigs" of blazing Yangming/Stomach heat (high fever, profuse sweating, great thirst, a flooding pulse), as the monarch herb of the classic formula **Bái Hǔ Tāng** ("White Tiger Decoction"); it **clears Lung heat** to calm wheezing (with ephedra and apricot kernel in Má Xìng Shí Gān Tāng); it **clears Stomach fire** (headache, toothache, bleeding gums); and, **calcined**, it is applied topically to drain damp-heat, heal sores, and stop bleeding. The apparent paradox — a "heavy" heat-clearing mineral that also promotes the resolution of surface heat — made it uniquely versatile.

**Tibetan Medicine (FMT — rGyud bzhi):**
Gypsum and the calcium-mineral "stone medicines" (*do sman*) are used among the cooling agents for hot disorders, bone and joint conditions, and — reflecting the calcined-plaster property — in bone-setting and fracture contexts.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the cold stone that quenches the furnace:**
Shí Gāo is the Codex's emblem of **cooling by mineral weight and substance** rather than by bitterness. Its doctrine is that of the *heavy, sinking, cold stone*: a dense, cold mineral that "sits on" and extinguishes the blazing fire of high fever, draining excess heat downward and outward. The name "White Tiger Decoction" encodes the symbolism — the White Tiger is the celestial animal of the West, of Autumn, of Metal, and of the cooling, clearing, descending direction that answers the Summer-fire of Yangming heat; gypsum *is* the autumn-metal cold that quells the fever's fire. That the same mineral, transformed by fire (calcination), becomes the rigid cast that *sets and holds* the broken bone is a fitting secondary signature — cold consolidation in both senses.

### Active Components

- **Calcium sulfate dihydrate (CaSO₄·2H₂O)** — the mineral itself; the raw (uncalcined) hydrated form is the internal antipyretic drug, while **calcined gypsum** (partly dehydrated, CaSO₄·½H₂O — plaster) is the topical/setting form. The water of crystallisation is pharmacologically relevant to the traditional distinction between raw (cooling, internal) and calcined (drying/astringent, external) use.
- **Calcium ions and trace minerals** — soluble calcium (modest, given gypsum's low solubility) and variable trace elements; the precise mechanism of gypsum's antipyretic action is not fully established and is the subject of ongoing (limited) pharmacological study.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Primary domain: RESET — acute clearance of intense, "blazing" heat (high fever, Lung/Stomach heat). A short-course, formula-based mineral, not a supplement.**

Gypsum's Kenetica role is narrow and acute: the mineral for **intense heat states** — high fever with sweating and thirst, hot-type wheezing, Stomach-fire toothache/gum inflammation — used **within formula** and short-course. Calcined gypsum has topical use for weeping, damp-heat skin lesions and minor bleeding. It has no tonic, building, or daily role; its whole action is to drain acute excess heat.

### Scientific Correlation

*Attribution: gypsum has very limited controlled clinical literature as an isolated agent; this is stated honestly. Its identity is established mineral chemistry (hydrated calcium sulfate).*

Gypsum's chemistry is unambiguous (hydrated calcium sulfate; calcination drives off crystal water to give plaster of Paris — the basis of both the surgical cast and the traditional raw-vs-calcined distinction). Its **antipyretic action is documented clinically only in the context of the compound formulas** in which it is the monarch ingredient (notably Bái Hǔ Tāng), rather than as an isolated mineral, and the precise mechanism by which so poorly-soluble a mineral produces a systemic febrifuge effect **remains incompletely explained** — an honest open question rather than a settled pharmacology. This entry therefore grades gypsum as **chemically well-defined and traditionally central to heat-clearing practice, but mechanistically under-explained and lacking single-agent clinical trials.** No specific efficacy statistics are asserted, because none are verifiable for the isolated mineral.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested, with a naming limit.* In Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) Shí Gāo (Gypsum) stands under "Jades and Stones: Inferior Class" (p.32, lines 1241-1247): acrid and slightly cold, mainly treating wind stroke cold and heat, counterflow qi below the heart, panting, dry mouth and abdominal hardness and pain; it also "eliminates evil ghosts, promotes lactation, [heals] incised wounds". So by grade this is a drug for a defined course against a defined complaint, not one that nourishes life. The limit is Yang's note 64 (lines 1255-1259): in ancient times "Gypsum Fibrosum", now called Shí Gāo, was called Lì Shí, while the ancient Shí Gāo is the present Yìng Shí Gāo ("Hard Gypsum"). The fibrous gypsum this entry describes would therefore correspond to Lì Shí, which Yang places under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (p.30, lines 1181-1186) with the actions "disinhibits the stomach, resolves vexation, boosts the essence, brightens the eyes". Both gradings are read; which applies to the entry's material depends on that note, and the "boosts the essence" wording is Yang's English, with no claim here about a *jing* action. The "clear heat, drain fire" action in the Origin section belongs to later materia medica and carries no grade here.

### Contraindications

- **Cold / deficiency patterns; Stomach cold; no genuine heat:** contraindicated — gypsum is very cold and only for true excess-heat; misuse injures Stomach/Spleen yang.
- **Spleen/Stomach deficiency:** the heavy-cold nature is hard on weak digestion; used cautiously and in formula (often with rice/licorice to protect the Stomach, as in Bai Hu Tang).
- **Pregnancy / debility:** caution with strong cold-draining minerals; use only on clear indication and supervision.
- **Internal vs external form:** do not confuse raw (internal) with calcined (external-only) gypsum.
- **Evidence honesty:** single-agent clinical data are lacking and the mechanism is under-explained; use rests on pharmacopoeial tradition within formula.
