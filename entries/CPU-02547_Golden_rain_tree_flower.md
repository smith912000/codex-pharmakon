# Golden rain tree flower — Luan Hua 栾花 (Shennong bencao jing)

## *Koelreuteria paniculata Laxm., the golden rain tree, here its flowers; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence, with the caveat that Sun Xingyan's note quotes Shen Kuo on a bead-bearing "mu luan" whose relation to the golden rain tree the Codex does not settle* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The only text read for this entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk for *Koelreuteria* found nothing.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 栾花, "luan flower", in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 栾花 *luan hua*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3763). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，寒 ("bitter, cold"); 主目痛、泪出、伤 [a character is missing] ("governs eye pain, running tears, injury of [the missing part]") and 消目肿 ("disperses swelling of the eye"); habitat 生川谷 ("grows in river valleys"). The paragraph is short and every named use concerns the eye. There is no 久服 clause and no alias.

The Mingyi bielu layer adds: 生汉中。五月采, "grows in Hanzhong; gathered in the fifth month" (line 3765). The flower is the gathered part by the entry's name.

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 3767) quotes the Shuowen (栾木，似栏, "luan wood resembles lan"), the Shan hai jing ("on Mount Yunyu there is a tree called luan, yellow wood, red branches, green leaves; the many emperors take medicine from it", 群帝焉取药), the Baihutong (feudal lords plant cypress at tombs, high officials luan, scholars huai), and Shen Kuo's Bu bitan: there is a kind of luan, growing as a tree, "whose fruit can make prayer beads, called mu luan, which is the luan hua of the bencao" (栾有一种，树生，其实可作数珠者，谓之木栾，即本草栾花是也). These are the commentators' and not the Shennong text's. The Shan hai jing colours (yellow wood, red branches, green leaves) and the Shen Kuo gloss about a bead-bearing fruit are descriptive pointers, and the Codex does not push them further than the text does.

An appendix of drug pairings at the end of the volume gives: 栾花 决明为使, "luan hua: jue ming is its envoy" (line 4529). It is a later appendix and gives no further statement.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, cold flower for eye pain, tearing and swelling of the eye. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose, and does not recommend applying any preparation to the eye.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Mingyi layer names the harvest month (the fifth); the Shennong paragraph names no part and no preparation. The preface (line 4121) says medicines differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The golden rain tree is widely planted in Asia, and its flowers have a place in the modern Chinese materia medica for the eye, which agrees in kind with the classic. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph for 栾花 reads 味苦，寒 and files the flower in the lower grade (下品). The classic's preface (line 2935) says the lower medicines "are for treating disease, to correspond to earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to clear cold-and-heat evil qi, break accumulations and heal illness, base it on the lower classic" (主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经). The paragraph has no 久服 clause and is a short list of eye complaints. The register is therefore (b): a lower-grade drug whose grade is the tradition's own warning against long use. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 栾花 *luan hua*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3763)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The flower is a lower-grade drug for eye complaints, taken, if at all, for a defined complaint and then stopped, and the tradition's own grading is the warning. The text is short and the Codex adds nothing to it.

### Contraindications

- **Eye disease:** pain, redness, swelling or loss of vision needs medical assessment; do not put plant preparations in the eye.
- **Lower-grade drug:** the classic's preface calls the lower medicines mostly toxic and not for long use (line 2935); the paragraph does not say this flower is toxic, and no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy and nursing:** no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given.
