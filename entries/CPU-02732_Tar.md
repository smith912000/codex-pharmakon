# Tar — قطران (*qaṭirān*)

## *Tar: the sap of a tree called *shurbīn* (الشربين), a conifer; the species is not fixed here, since the Canon gives only the tree's name (confidence medium for 'a conifer tar', low for the species)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** The Canon itself says this drug kills the fetus and is hot and dry in the fourth degree. This entry is historical record only; nobody should swallow, instil or inject tar.

**Single-text entry.** Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk, so far as searched. Source file: `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`.

**Locus (CM).** *Canon* Book II, entry *qaṭirān* (قطران), heading at Arabic text line 17443; the entry runs to the next heading, *qusṭ* (line 17461). The Codex has pitch and resin entries (for example Pinus sylvestris, I_185, and Pinus nigra, I_041) but no tar, so this is a separate substance.

**What it is (CM).** 'It is the sap of a tree called *al-shurbīn*; the power of its smoke is like the smoke of pitch (*zift*), and an oil is made from it, separated with wool as it is separated from pitch' (lines 17444-17445, approximate). Temperament: 'hot and dry in the fourth degree' (within lines 17443-17460).

**What the text says (CM).** Cosmetic: it kills lice and nits, even in cattle (within lines 17443-17460). Wounds: it strengthens loose flesh and helps scab, even animal scab, its oil especially for quadrupeds, dogs and camels. Joints: it helps bruised muscle and gathered blood and pus in it, and is a remedy for elephantiasis and varicose veins, as a linctus and a smear. Head: 'the greatest thing in quieting cold headache is smearing the head with tar'; dropped in the ear it kills ear worms; with hyssop water it is dropped for ringing and roaring in the ears and for a painful tooth, and it helps eroded teeth. Eye: it sharpens sight and clears ulcer marks. Chest: smeared on the throat for the tonsils, and 'licking an ounce and a half (*ūqiya wa-niṣf*) of it' is said to heal lung ulcers and chronic cough. Digestive: the fruit of its tree is bad for the stomach. Excretory: it kills worms in the intestines, especially as an enema; promotes the menses; kills the fetus; spoils semen; smeared on the penis before intercourse it prevents conception. Poisons: a poultice for the bite of the horned viper; given as a smear and a drink for poisoning by the sea hare; and dissolved in camel fat and rubbed on the limbs so that vermin do not approach (all within lines 17443-17460).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, wood and conifer tars are complex mixtures of phenols and resin-derived compounds from the destructive distillation of wood; they are irritant. That is general chemistry, not from the Canon.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The entry is a parasiticide, a skin and ear remedy, and, for internal use, a licked, drunk and enema remedy for worms and lung ulcers, together with antifertility and abortifacient statements. The Codex does not recommend swallowing, instilling or injecting tar.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only quantity is 'an ounce and a half' licked for lung ulcers (within lines 17443-17460; the *ūqiya* is not converted). Forms named: a smear on the head, throat, penis and skin; ear and tooth drops with hyssop water; a linctus; an enema; an oil separated with wool; a solution in camel fat. No other dose is given. **Do not use any amount from this entry.** [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Hot and dry in the fourth degree (*Canon* Book II, entry *qaṭirān*, line 17443-17460). No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(b) Framed.* The entry is a drug-by-drug list of qualities and uses inside the *Canon*'s materia medica. It assigns no cultivation status and says nothing of innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruh*. Its fourth-degree heat is the opposite of a conserving drug in this tradition's own scheme, and the text promotes no long-term use.

**Practitioner's note.** A pitch-like sap used by the text for parasites, skin disease, ears and worms, with antifertility statements. Nothing supports a cultivation use, and it is not something to swallow or instil.

### Contraindications

- **Toxic and irritant.** Tar is an irritant and the tar itself is not a food. The text's own statements that it kills the fetus and spoils semen, and that its tree's fruit harms the stomach, mark it as a drug to avoid.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** Avoid. The Canon says it kills the fetus (within lines 17443-17460).
- **Ears and eyes.** Do not instil. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** *Shurbīn* is not fixed to one species here. [USER: verify]
