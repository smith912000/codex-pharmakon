# Basella rubra — As'va-vala (the Sanskrit name is OCR-garbled in Bhishagratna's Appendix 3: "Asva-6al£C")

## *Basella rubra (Indian or Malabar spinach, a climbing pot-herb), as Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 prints it opposite a Sanskrit name that the OCR reads "Asva-6al£C" and the text of the treatise gives as As'va-vala or Ashvavala, a pot-herb (shaka) in the Sutrasthana lists. The Latin is printed "Basella Bubra" (OCR for rubra). Medium-to-high confidence for the Latin name as Bhishagratna's equivalent; medium for the Sanskrit reading* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

This is a single-text entry. The row was entered on 8 November 2026 from the residual key "Basella Bubra [SS]" with the locus "SS Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31291; id line 31339)".

**Sushruta (SS), Appendix 3.** In Bhishagratna's Volume III, Appendix No. 3, "Names of drugs (with Latin synonyms)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 31119), the Sanskrit column has the sequence Asoka (31285), Asphota (31287), Asthi-sambakraka (31289), then the garbled "Asva-6al£C" (line 31291), then "Asva-gandha" (31293), "As'va-karna" (31295), "As'va-maraka" (31297) and "Asvattha" (31299). The Latin column (lines 31330 onward) runs in the same order: "Saraca Indica" (31336), "Salvodoia Persica", "Vitis quadrangularis" (31338), "Basella Bubra" (line 31339), "Withania Somnifera; Physalis Flexuosa" (Asva-gandha), "See S'iila" (As'va-karna), "Nerium Odorum" (As'va-maraka) and "Ficus Religiosa" (Asvattha). Three anchors agree with the order (Asoka with Saraca indica; Asva-gandha with Withania somnifera; Asvattha with the peepul tree, Ficus religiosa), so the Latin line at 31339 belongs to the fourth Sanskrit name, whose OCR is "Asva-6al£C". The letters "Asva-" and the final "-6al£C" fit As'va-vala/bala; the reading is the Codex's, not the book's. [USER: verify]

**Sushruta (SS), the chapters.** In the Sutrasthana pot-herb passage (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 21273 to 21280) the English reads: "The potherbs, such as the Tanduliyaka, Upodika, Ashvavala, Chilli, Palankya, and Vastuka etc., are laxative and diuretic, and have a sweet and alkaline taste. They slightly generate the Vayu and Kaphani, and cure haemoptysis." The verses that follow give separate qualities for named herbs, and the Upodika species is described as "sweet in taste and digestion. It is spermatopoietic, anti-narcotic, cooling, laxative, demulcent and tonic" (line 21289). That verse is for Upodika; no separate verse for Ashvavala was read, and the Codex does not carry Upodika's qualities across. In the Chikitsa, a meal for a patient is to include "Vastuka, Tanduliyaka, Jivanti, Upodika, As'va-vala, tender Mulaka, Palanka, Asana, Chilli, Chuchchu, Kalaya, Valli, or any other Shakas (pot-herbs), according to the nature of the Doshas involved" (Vol. II, line 14590).

Whether the Sushruta pot-herb As'va-vala is the plant Bhishagratna calls Basella rubra in the appendix is, as far as the appendix goes, his identification; the Codex has no second source. Upodika, which is named beside it, is not a synonym on the page. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms, *Basella* leaves and shoots are a mucilaginous leafy vegetable and the berries contain a red-purple pigment. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. A food plant in the text; no practice is recommended.**

- **What the text says.** As a pot-herb: laxative and diuretic, sweet and alkaline, slightly generating Vayu and Kapha, and curing haemoptysis (Vol. I, lines 21273 to 21280); allowed in the patient's meal in a regimen (Vol. II, line 14590).
- **What the text does not say.** No amount, no preparation beyond its being a pot-herb, no dose.

The Codex does not translate the Sushruta terms into modern diagnoses. "Haemoptysis" is Bhishagratna's English.

### Preparation & Dosing

None is given beyond serving it as a pot-herb in a meal. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Basella alba* and *B. rubra* are eaten as leaf vegetables in South and South-East Asia and are rich in water and mucilage. The classic's "laxative" is consistent with a mucilaginous vegetable in kind but is not tested here. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Bhishagratna, group statement).** For the pot-herb group including Ashvavala: "sweet" and "alkaline" in taste, laxative and diuretic, slightly generating Vayu and Kapha (Vol. I, lines 21273 to 21280). No separate rasa, virya or vipaka for Ashvavala alone is printed in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The herb is placed in the dietetic classification of pot-herbs and in a regimen meal, with a therapeutic frame (Vayu, Kapha, haemoptysis). The one tonic and "spermatopoietic" verse in the passage belongs to Upodika, not to Ashvavala, so no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status is attested for it. The register is (b): framed, with no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** An ordinary leafy vegetable of the diet lists. Nothing here supports any cultivation use beyond its place as food, and nothing in the lines read is a dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The Sanskrit name is OCR-garbled and its link to *Basella rubra* is Bhishagratna's appendix only. [USER: verify]
- **Food hygiene.** Leafy vegetables should be washed and cooked in the usual way.
- **Haemoptysis and other conditions.** These are medical conditions that need proper care; this entry is not a treatment.
