### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, and only in one list.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from an extraction of the translation's group lists. A search of the Sushruta chapter files on disk finds the name only in this chapter 38 line (and its copy in `Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, line 15495).

**Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.38, the Pippalya'di group.** The chapter classifies drugs "usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 9). The group is printed at lines 148 to 155: "The group of medicinal drugs known as the Pippalyadi consists of Pippali, Pippali mulam, Chavya, Chitraka, Shringavera, Maricha, Hasti-Pippali, Harenuka, Ela, Ajamoda, Indrayava, Patha, Jiraka, Sarshapa, Maha-Nimva-Phala, Hingu, Bhargi, Madhurasa, Ativisha, Vacha, Vidanga and Katurohini." Madhurasa is the eighteenth item by the printed order, between Bhargi and Ativisha (line 154). The translator offers no gloss for it.

The metrical text (lines 157 to 162) reads: "The present group acts as a good appetiser and is an absorbant of intestinal mucous and unassimilated lymph chyle. The range of its therapeutical application includes catarrh, deranged Kapham and Vatam, non-relish for food, abdominal glands, colic and gastralgia." These are claims for the group as a whole. The text attributes nothing to Madhurasa separately.

**What the neighbours suggest, and what they do not.** The group is made of spices and bitter-pungent roots and seeds, which fits the stated actions as an appetiser and a Kapham-reducing group. That is a statement about the company the item keeps, not about the item. The name "sweet taste" sits oddly in such a list, and the translator does not comment. A different, similar-looking name, Madhurika, appears in the translator's Appendix No. 3 (the M page of `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, around line 32161, paired by position with *Pimpinella anisum*, aniseed) but is a different name and is not equated here. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Nothing specific to Madhurasa. Its group is an appetiser and an absorbent of intestinal mucus and undigested chyle, used in catarrh, Kapham and Vata disorders, loss of appetite, abdominal glands, colic and gastralgia.
- **What the text does not say.** No part, no preparation, no dose, and no named formula.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this item.

### Preparation & Dosing

The group entry gives no preparation. Chapter 38 ends with a general instruction for all its groups: they "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", using "two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group", or a whole group, as the physician decides (lines 561 to 568). That is a rule for choosing among members, not a dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Bhishagratna 1907).** No taste (*rasa*), potency (*virya*) or post-digestive effect (*vipaka*) is printed for Madhurasa. The Pippalya'di group's metrical text gives only the group's actions (lines 157 to 162). ### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* Madhurasa is a name in a gana list, and the gana is therapeutic (appetiser, Kapham and Vata disorders). It was not found in a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) chapter, and no *ojas* effect is assigned to it.

**Practitioner's note.** A name without a plant behind it, in a list of spices for the appetite and the gut. There is nothing to cultivate, source or take. The honest outcome is to keep the name on record and leave it.
