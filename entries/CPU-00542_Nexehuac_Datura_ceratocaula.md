# Nexehuac / Aquatic Datura / Torna-loco / Sister of Ololiuhqui / Atlinan

## *Datura ceratocaula* Ortega | Family: Solanaceae

**Tradition Tags:** BM, SA

**Energetic Classification:** Cold, wet, heavy, stupefying. In post-conquest Mexican folk-humoral terms a **cold** drug (*fría*) — consistent with its swamp habitat and its narcotic action. Sedating-then-dissociating; downward and inward; sleep-bearing, then sleep-breaking.

**Training Phase:** ☠️ **NONE — Toxicological and ethnobotanical reference only. This plant has no training phase because it has no safe use.**

---

### ☠️ DEADLY — READ THIS BEFORE READING ANYTHING ELSE

***Datura ceratocaula* is a tropane-alkaloid plant. Its principal alkaloid is hyoscyamine, and its alkaloid profile places it squarely in the same lethal class as jimsonweed, henbane, belladonna and mandrake.**

Berkov's GC/MS survey of the species identified **thirty-six alkaloids** across the plant's organs — thirty-three of them previously unreported for *D. ceratocaula* — with **hyoscyamine as the main alkaloid in every organ examined** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/znc-2003-7-801), PMID 12939026). That is not an ambiguous finding. It is a direct, species-specific chemical identification of the plant as a tropane deliriant.

**No dose is given in this entry. No dose will be given in this entry.** The reason is not squeamishness; it is arithmetic. In the *Datura* genus the concentration at which delirium begins and the concentration at which the patient dies are **not meaningfully separated**. Marc et al., reporting the first *Datura* intoxications correlated with LC-MS/MS confirmation in an emergency setting, state the problem plainly: severe and fatal complications "are not rare since the lethal concentration of the drug's toxic substances (i.e., atropine and scopolamine) is close to the level at which delirium occurs," with coma, respiratory distress or death in **more than 5% of cases** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lpm.2007.04.017), PMID 17560071). One of their patients showed a blood atropine of 1.7 ng/mL — which the authors describe as close to the lethal level — in a person who was awake, agitated, and hallucinating being assaulted by animals.

**There is no dose that produces the vision without approaching the dose that stops the breathing.** Suspected ingestion is a medical emergency. Contact emergency services / poisons information immediately. Do not induce vomiting. Tell the clinicians it is an **anticholinergic plant poisoning** — that single phrase redirects management to the correct pathway.

**This is the fourth Datura in the codex. Do not read across.**

The taxonomic isolation is not a footnote. McLean's crossing experiments found that hybrids involving *D. ceratocaula* could be obtained **only by embryo dissection** — the crosses abort without surgical rescue ([PMID 20280152](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20280152/); *Am J Bot* 33:630–638, 1946; no DOI assigned), and Cummings documented distinctive chromosome-end arrangement in the species ([PMID 18875249](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18875249/); *Am J Bot* 35:444–454, 1948; no DOI assigned). *D. ceratocaula* is the odd sibling of the genus in habitat, in stem morphology, and in genome. **It is not odd in its toxicity.** Whatever else the swamp made of it, it did not make it safe.

### Origin & Tradition

**Badianus Manuscript (BM) — *nexehuac*, *atlinan*, *tlápatl* complex [USER: verify species-level attestation]**

The *Libellus de Medicinalibus Indorum Herbis* (Martín de la Cruz, translated to Latin by Juan Badiano, 1552) is the earliest surviving materia medica written by an indigenous Mexican physician, and it carries a family of Solanaceous narcotics under Nahuatl names — *tlápatl* and its relatives — used for pain, for "cold" swellings, and applied as poultices.

**The honest position on this tag: the Badianus attestation is at the genus level at best, not the species level.** The *Libellus* is illustrated in a stylised, pre-Columbian pictorial idiom, its Nahuatl plant names are polysemous, and generations of botanists have disagreed over what its figures depict. *Tlápatl* is confidently a *Datura*; **which** *Datura* is a different question, and the codex should not pretend otherwise. What can be said is that the aquatic datura grew in exactly the lacustrine landscape of the Valley of Mexico — the chinampa canals, the marsh margins of Texcoco — that the *Libellus*'s authors inhabited, which makes availability certain and identification merely probable. **[USER: verify against a facsimile / the Gates or Emmart editions before the BM tag is treated as species-secure.]**

**South American / Mesoamerican colonial record (SA) — *torna-loco*, "hermana de ololiuhqui" [USER: verify]**

The plant's most famous label — **the "sister of ololiuhqui"** — reaches modern readers through the sixteenth-century Spanish natural-historical corpus on New Spain and its later ethnobotanical retellings. In that record the aquatic datura is described as a **ritual divinatory narcotic and analgesic**: given to induce a state in which the practitioner could speak with, or be spoken through by, something not present; and given to dull the pain of setting bones and of surgery.

The name is a doctrinal claim, and it should be read as one. **Ololiuhqui** — the seeds of *Turbina corymbosa*, an ergoline-bearing morning glory (codex I_195) — was the pre-eminent divinatory drug of central Mexican practice. To call the swamp datura its *sister* is to file it in a category: **plants through which the invisible speaks**. It is a taxonomy of function, not of botany, and the two plants are not remotely related — Convolvulaceae and Solanaceae, ergolines and tropanes, a partial 5-HT₂A agonist and a muscarinic antagonist. **The tradition grouped them by what they did to the practitioner, and by that criterion the grouping is defensible. By any pharmacological criterion it is a category error — and an unusually consequential one, because the two drugs have wildly different lethality.**

The Spanish colonial epithet ***torna-loco*** — "it turns you mad" — belongs to the same period and carries the coloniser's reading: not a door, a derangement. **Both readings are partly right, and the pharmacology below adjudicates between them.** [USER: verify the specific application of *torna-loco* and of "hermana de ololiuhqui" to *D. ceratocaula* rather than to *Datura* generally, against Hernández's *Historia* and the Schultes/Hofmann secondary literature.]

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

Three doctrines, mapped onto real chemistry. One survives, one inverts, one collapses entirely.

**Doctrine 1: the water-sibling — the plant that lives where the world is thinnest.** Every other datura of the Mexican pharmacy is a plant of dry ground, roadside and waste. This one grows in standing water, hollow-stemmed, half-floating. In a cosmology where the lake, the cave, the spring and the cenote are apertures — the places where *Tlalocan* and the surface world interpenetrate — a narcotic that grows **in the aperture itself** is not an arbitrary drug. It is a drug whose habitat is its credential. **This doctrine is symbolically coherent, ecologically accurate, and pharmacologically null.** The swamp did not put anything in this plant that the roadside did not put in *D. stramonium*. The habitat is real, the reasoning from habitat to potency is decoration, and the codex should say so. The plant's most distinctive feature — the one that gave it its section, its name (*ceratocaula*, "horn-stemmed"), and its mystique — **is the one feature with no bearing on what it does to a human nervous system.**

**Doctrine 2: the analgesic.** This one holds up, and it is the most defensible thing the tradition believed about this plant. Tropane-rich solanaceous preparations were the ancient world's general-purpose surgical stupefacients from Mesopotamia to Mesoamerica, and they were selected independently, on four continents, by people who could not have been copying one another. Hyoscyamine and scopolamine are genuine sedatives, genuine amnestics, and genuinely reduce the misery of a procedure — scopolamine remained in obstetric practice into the twentieth century for exactly this reason. **The doctrine identified a real drug class for a real indication.** It also had no way to bound the dose, which is why it is in this entry's prohibition table rather than its protocol section.

**Doctrine 3: the divination — and this is where the entry earns its keep.** The doctrine says: the sister of ololiuhqui *opens* something. The vision is a **revelation** — information arriving from outside, granted by an agency that was already there and is now permitted to speak. The practitioner's altered state is a *receptive* state.

**The pharmacology says the exact opposite, and the inversion is total.**

Tropanes are **competitive muscarinic acetylcholine receptor antagonists**. They do not add a signal. They **subtract** one — they occupy the receptors that carry the cholinergic traffic underwriting attention, sensory integration, reality-monitoring and the encoding of new memory, and they hold those receptors shut. The resulting state is a **deliriant** state, and deliriant is a technical word, not a rude one. Its defining features are: **true hallucinations indistinguishable from perception** (the patient does not know), **confabulation**, **loss of insight**, and **dense amnesia for the episode**. The classical clinical picture is a person conversing at length with someone who is not in the room, and remembering none of it afterwards.

This is categorically unlike the serotonergic state that ololiuhqui produces, in which insight is characteristically **retained** — the user typically knows the visions are visions. The sisterhood breaks precisely here. **Ololiuhqui alters the picture and leaves the viewer. Datura removes the viewer.**

And the deflation has an unusually sharp modern edge. **Scopolamine is not merely known to impair cognition — it is the standard laboratory tool for *simulating dementia in healthy people*.** Sambeth et al. open their paper with the field's own sentence: "Traditionally, the non-selective muscarinic antagonist scopolamine has been used to induce episodic memory impairments as found in Alzheimer's disease" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-014-3822-9), PMID 25466702). Broocks et al. confirmed it experimentally in ten healthy volunteers: scopolamine "induced significant impairments in episodic memory and processing speed" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0006-3223(97)00388-0), PMID 9532345).

So: the tradition's instrument for acquiring knowledge that could not otherwise be had is, in the modern laboratory, **the instrument used to manufacture the inability to acquire knowledge at all.** The drug does not open a channel. It **degrades the machinery that distinguishes a channel from noise**, and the practitioner — sincerely, and with no way to know better — reads the degradation as content.

**This is not a claim that the practitioners were fools.** It is a claim about what a deliriant can and cannot deliver. A drug that reliably produces vivid, unquestioned, forgotten experience will produce, in any culture that expects revelation, a great deal of testimony about revelation — and there is no experiment available from inside the state that could distinguish a revelation from a confabulation, because the faculty that would run the experiment is the faculty the drug has removed. **The doctrine is not falsified by the pharmacology. It is rendered unfalsifiable by it — which, for a claim about acquiring true information, is the worse outcome.**

### Active Components

**Tropane alkaloids — the lethal fraction, and species-confirmed.** Berkov identified thirty-six alkaloids in *D. ceratocaula* organs by GC/MS, **thirty-three new to the species**, with **hyoscyamine the principal alkaloid throughout**, plus a novel tropane ester tentatively assigned as 3-(3′-formyloxytropoyloxy)tropane on mass-spectral fragmentation ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/znc-2003-7-801), PMID 12939026). Hyoscyamine is the *(S)*-enantiomer of atropine; racemisation during extraction, drying or heating converts it toward atropine. Scopolamine (hyoscine) is the standard companion alkaloid of the genus and is the more CNS-penetrant of the pair. **Note precisely what is and is not established:** the *identity* of the alkaloids is directly established for this species; the *quantities*, and their variation by organ, season, provenance and water depth, are not, and the tropane content of Solanaceae is notoriously variable — which is another reason no dose appears below.

**Withanolides — species-confirmed, and pharmacologically ironic.** González et al. isolated withanolides from *D. ceratocaula*, including the new **datucerolide A**, and tested the series for **acetylcholinesterase inhibition**, finding IC₅₀ values from **2.8 to 21.5 µM** for the most active compounds ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fitote.2023.105655), PMID 37595646).

Sit with that. The same plant contains (a) tropanes that **block** muscarinic receptors and (b) withanolides that **inhibit the enzyme clearing acetylcholine** — that is, compounds from the same class as **the antidote**. Anticholinergic delirium is reversed clinically by cholinesterase inhibitors; the Cluj team ran a neostigmine infusion on their comatose *Datura* patient ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.21454/rjaic.7518.241.szb), PMID 28913501).

**This does not make the plant self-antidoting, and the entry will not imply that it does.** The tropanes act at nanomolar-to-low-micromolar potency at the receptor; the withanolides inhibit an enzyme at single-to-double-digit micromolar in a cuvette. Their concentration in the plant is unquantified, their blood-brain barrier penetration is unknown, their in-vivo activity against this poisoning is **entirely untested**, and a competitive receptor blockade is not reliably outrun by raising the ligand anyway. The finding is a genuine curiosity of the species' chemistry and **contributes nothing whatsoever to its safety.**

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**None. There is no Kenetica use case for this plant, in any preparation, at any dose, for any purpose.**

It is retained in the codex for four documentary reasons:

1. **The deliriant/entheogen distinction.** *D. ceratocaula* is the cleanest teaching case the codex has for the difference between a drug that **alters** consciousness and a drug that **dismantles** it, because the tradition itself filed it alongside a true entheogen and the pharmacology refuses the filing.
2. **Identification.** An aquatic, hollow-stemmed, white-to-purplish trumpet-flowered solanaceous plant in a Mexican ditch is unlikely to be mistaken for anything else — but it is also unlikely to be *recognised as a Datura* by anyone whose mental image of the genus is a dry-ground weed. The habitat is a disguise.
3. **The data-sparsity case study.** Four PubMed-indexed papers exist for this species. Two are from the 1940s and are about chromosomes. **The chemical literature on this plant is two papers deep**, and both are Mexican-institute isolation chemistry, not toxicology. This entry is what an honest codex entry looks like when the evidence is thin.
4. **Cultivator's caution.** *D. ceratocaula* is occasionally grown as a botanical curiosity. Growers should know what is in the stem.

### Scientific Correlation

**Provenance statement, first, because it governs everything below.** The clinical toxicology of *Datura* is almost entirely **congener data** — overwhelmingly *D. stramonium*, plus *Brugmansia*. **This entry does not launder congener clinical data into *D. ceratocaula*.** What licenses the cross-read at all is the one thing that *is* species-specific: Berkov's alkaloid identification. Because the species is confirmed to be hyoscyamine-dominant, its **class membership** is established, and class-level anticholinergic pharmacology applies. Its **potency, dose–response and case-fatality rate are unknown** and are not asserted.

**1. *D. ceratocaula* is a hyoscyamine-dominant tropane plant — species-specific. Grade: STRONG.**
Berkov, GC/MS, thirty-six alkaloids, hyoscyamine principal in the plant's organs ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/znc-2003-7-801), PMID 12939026). Single study, single laboratory, no independent replication located — but it is direct, species-specific, and analytically unambiguous. This is the load-bearing citation of the entry and it holds.

**2. Tropane deliriant pharmacology in humans — congener, but unusually well documented. Grade: STRONG for the class, NOT SPECIES-SPECIFIC.**
Marc et al.: two adolescents, LC-MS/MS-confirmed; agitation, persecutory delirium, frightening hallucinations (one patient saw green turtles walking on her), atropine 1.7 ng/mL near lethal level, resolution at 36–40 hours ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lpm.2007.04.017), PMID 17560071). Trancă et al.: a 22-year-old presenting **comatose** two hours post-ingestion, requiring rapid-sequence intubation and mechanical ventilation, with fever, right bundle branch block, urinary retention and **rhabdomyolysis**; extubated at 36 hours ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.21454/rjaic.7518.241.szb), PMID 28913501). Shebani et al.: intentional seed ingestion, severe confusion and delirium, two-day ICU admission ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.20152), PMID 35003983). Jayawickreme et al., *Brugmansia suaveolens* leaves in a herbal drink: delirium, mydriasis, urinary retention, sinus tachycardia, resolution at 15 hours ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13256-019-2250-1), PMID 31665073). **The toxidrome is consistent, reproducible across species and continents, and includes coma and death.**

**3. The deliriant-not-entheogen claim — the doctrinal deflation. Grade: STRONG, from human experimental data (scopolamine, not this plant).**
Broocks et al., ten healthy volunteers, scopolamine 0.4 mg: "significant impairments in episodic memory and processing speed" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0006-3223(97)00388-0), PMID 9532345). Sambeth et al. document the field's use of scopolamine as the **translational model for Alzheimer-type episodic memory deficit**, and find the M₁-selective antagonist biperiden impairs verbal, recognition and spatial memory in a placebo-controlled crossover ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-014-3822-9), PMID 25466702) — establishing that the amnestic effect is **muscarinic and mechanism-specific**, not a nonspecific consequence of sedation. **The claim "muscarinic antagonism degrades the encoding and reality-monitoring machinery" is not speculation. It is the assumption on which an entire experimental literature is built.**

**4. Withanolide AChE inhibition — species-specific, real, and irrelevant to safety. Grade: MODERATE in vitro, NULL in vivo.**
González et al., IC₅₀ 2.8–21.5 µM ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fitote.2023.105655), PMID 37595646). Enzyme-assay data only. **No in-vivo, no CNS exposure, no relevance to intoxication demonstrated.**

**5. NULLS — stated explicitly, because their absence would be a lie by omission.**
- **No clinical toxicology of *D. ceratocaula* exists.** No case reports, no case series, no fatality data, no analytically confirmed intoxication for this species was retrieved this run. **Grade: NULL.** This absence is a **reporting gap, not a safety signal** — the species is regionally restricted, rarely used, and never reached a toxicovigilance system.
- **No quantitative alkaloid assay** (mg/g by organ) for this species was retrieved. Berkov identified; he did not, in the retrieved abstract, quantify. **Grade: NULL.**
- **No pharmacological, psychometric or phenomenological study of the divinatory use** — of any datura, in any tradition — was retrieved. Every claim in this entry about what the vision *is* rests on class pharmacology plus the clinical picture of anticholinergic delirium. **The doctrine has never been tested on its own terms, and cannot be, for the reasons given above.** **Grade: NULL / UNTESTABLE.**
- **No evidence of analgesic efficacy for this species** in any controlled setting. **Grade: NULL.** The analgesic doctrine is class-plausible and species-unevidenced.

**Honest summary.** One good chemistry paper establishes what the plant is. One good chemistry paper establishes an irony. A large congener literature establishes that plants of this class hospitalise and kill people. **Nothing retrieved supports any use of *Datura ceratocaula* by anyone for anything.**

### Contraindications

☠️ **ABSOLUTE — USE IS PROHIBITED IN ALL PERSONS, BY ALL ROUTES, AT ALL DOSES, IN ALL PREPARATIONS. THERE ARE NO EXCEPTIONS AND NO QUALIFYING CONDITIONS.**

- **Everyone.** There is no population for whom this plant is appropriate and no condition it should be used to treat.
- **Children and adolescents.** The congener case literature is dominated by teenagers seeking hallucinogenic effect, and it is dominated by intensive care admissions.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding.** Absolute. Tropanes cross the placenta and enter milk.
- **The elderly, and anyone with cognitive impairment or dementia.** Anticholinergic burden is a recognised driver of delirium and cognitive decline; this plant is that burden in concentrated botanical form.
- **Concurrent anticholinergic load** — antihistamines, tricyclics, antipsychotics, antispasmodics, some antiparkinsonian drugs. **Additive, and the additivity is how people die.**
- **Glaucoma (angle-closure), prostatic hypertrophy / urinary retention, gastrointestinal obstruction or ileus, tachyarrhythmia, cardiac disease, hyperthermia risk, febrile illness.** Every one of these is a standard anticholinergic contraindication and every one is directly worsened.
- **Handling.** Gloves; wash hands; keep fingers away from eyes. Do not burn the plant (irritant, alkaloid-bearing smoke). Keep away from children and livestock.

**If ingestion is suspected — emergency medical care immediately.** The anticholinergic toxidrome: **mydriasis, dry flushed skin, hyperthermia, tachycardia, urinary retention, absent bowel sounds, agitated delirium with true hallucinations, progressing to seizure, coma, rhabdomyolysis and respiratory failure.** Say the words *anticholinergic plant poisoning* to the clinicians. Management is supportive (airway, cooling, benzodiazepines for agitation, activated charcoal where indicated) with **physostigmine considered by clinicians in selected cases** — a decision for a physician with cardiac monitoring in place, never a home intervention, and never something to attempt with a plant.

**A note on the codex's own risk surface.** The specific hazard of this entry is not that a reader will find aquatic datura in a Mexican ditch. It is the phrase **"sister of ololiuhqui"** — which is romantic, which is genuinely ancient, and which functions as an implied safety claim by association with a plant of very different lethality. **The sisterhood is a claim about what the practitioner experienced, not about what the molecule does.** Ololiuhqui and *Datura* are not siblings in any sense that constrains a dose. Treating them as such is the exact error this entry exists to prevent.
