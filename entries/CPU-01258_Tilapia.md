### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The only tradition attesting this material in the sources retrieved is the Egyptian *Papyrus Ebers*, and the attestation rests on a list row, not on a passage I read. The row is `_sources/ebers.csv` line 159: "Tilapia Nilotica,,animal,"Ebers Papyrus; Ghalioungui 1987 trans., Glossary I (Medicaments)",yes". The columns are name, botanical, class, source reference and an identification-uncertain flag, and the last column reads "yes": the list itself marks the identification as uncertain. The source named is Ghalioungui's 1987 translation and its glossary of medicaments. That book is not among the sources on disk, so I could not see the passage, the Egyptian word it renders, or the use.

The only Ebers text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). I searched it for "tilapia", "bulti" (a common Arabic name of the fish, which I used only as a search term), "perch" and "Nile" among fish words, and found no tilapia. In his chapter VII, "Organic Remedies", Bryan lists the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (about lines 1385-1404); the water creatures in it are "Frog Tadpole ... Eel Electric Eel Crab Swordfish" (line 1404). Tilapia is not in that list. The name-extract (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`) has no row for it either, since its animal rows come from Bryan's list. So the Bryan list, which is the only text of the papyrus I could read, does not name the fish.

Fish do occur in Bryan's pages, under other names. He prints a remedy for pain in the head with "Bone-of-the-Sword-fish, cooked Redfish, cooked Skull-of-the-Crayfish, cooked Honey" and an *abra*-ointment (about line 2102); an eczema course in which the head is anointed on the second day with "Fish-oil" (line 2717); "Fish-glue in Beer" for swollen glands of the arm (line 3416); and, for leucoma of the eye, "a 'paste' made from Collyrium and the Bile-of-the-abdu-fish" (line 2806). A "Stickleback-fish" occurs in a simile for the matter that flows from a tumour (line 3855). Bryan does not say that any of these fish is a tilapia, and the *abdu*-fish is an untranslated Egyptian word in his text; I do not identify it. A reader who wishes to test the list row should look at Ghalioungui's glossary for the Egyptian word, which this entry cannot do. [USER: verify]

`Sources/EP_Ebers/EP_Overview.md` and `EP_Original_Hieroglyphic.md` were searched for "tilapia" and "Ghalioungui" and returned nothing. No other tradition's text on disk was found to name it.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, fish flesh is a source of protein, with fat that varies by species, and bile is a digestive secretion. The Nile tilapia is a freshwater fish now classed in the genus *Oreochromis* in current fish taxonomy; the older name *Tilapia nilotica* is the one the list uses. Nothing in the entry links any constituent to a use in the papyrus.

### Contraindications

- **Parasites and infection:** raw or undercooked freshwater fish and fish bile are not safe to take; do not use any fish part as a medicine.
- **Fish allergy:** fish are a common cause of allergy; avoid in anyone with a known allergy.
- **Contaminated waters:** wild freshwater fish from polluted waters can carry toxins and heavy metals.
- **Identification:** the material is a list row with an uncertain identification; no use is stated, so nothing here is advice.
