# Flixweed seed — 亭历 (Ting Li; later written 葶苈); also Da Shi, Da Shi (大适), Xia Li

## **Descurainia sophia* (L.) Webb ex Prantl (Brassicaceae), the seed, with *Lepidium apetalum* Willd. as the other source of the later Chinese crude drug 葶苈子 *tinglizi*; the identification is of medium confidence and the Shennong text does not choose between them* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Ting li* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The placeholder row it replaces was named "Lepidium sp." (from Yang's "Semen Lepidii Seu Descurainiae").

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 亭历 in the lower classic (卷三 下经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3129, 味 paragraph; line 3127 is his note that the old text wrote 葶苈 and the *Yulan* writes 亭历). The Shennong text proper: "acrid, cold" (味辛，寒). It "mainly treats" "concretions, conglomerations, accumulations and gatherings, bound qi; food and drink, cold and heat; it breaks what is hard" (主症瘕、积聚、结气，饮食、寒热，破坚). "One name: Da Shi" 大室; "one name: Da Shi" 大适 (two different characters, both read *shi*). "It grows in level marshes and in fields" (生平泽及田野). No 久服 clause and no toxicity statement.

*Wu Pu layer.* None for this entry.

*Mingyi bielu layer* (line 3131). Aliases 下历 and one with a lost glyph (...蒿). Habitat: a city name partly lost in the e-text. "The fruit (实) is gathered after the beginning of summer and dried in the shade. It does well with wine" (得酒，良).

*Sun's note* (案, line 3133). *Shuowen*: [lost glyph] is *ting li*. *Guangya*: "*gou ji* (dog shepherd's-purse), *da shi* are *ting li*." *Erya* with Guo Pu: "the fruit and the leaf both resemble mustard (芥)." Also *Huainanzi*, "Miu cheng xun": "*ting li* grows ever more spread" (亭历愈张); and *Xijing zaji*: "*ting li* in midsummer." So the only description is "like mustard" in the fruit and leaf, and the "dog shepherd's-purse" gloss, both pointing at a crucifer.

*Drug-relations table* (line 4427): 葶苈 "elm bark is the envoy (使); does well (得) with wine; dislikes (恶) 僵蚕 and 石龙芮."

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 3236) prints "Ting Li (Semen Lepidii Seu Descurainiae) is acrid and cold. It mainly treats concretions, conglomerations, gatherings, and accumulation bound qi and [abiding] food and drink with cold and heat. It breaks hardness, expels evils, and disinhibits and frees the flow of the water passageways. Its other name is Da Shi (Great Room). Another name is Da Shi (Great Comfort)." Yang adds "expels evils" and "disinhibits and frees the flow of the water passageways," which Sun's text does not have. His footnote 199 (line 3248) says: "There are two kinds of Lepidium, the sweet and bitter. Here, the author appears to only be referring to the bitter," and goes on with later commentary on downbearing lung qi (his own gloss).

Neither witness names a species. The two-source reading (*Descurainia* and *Lepidium apetalum*) is that of later Chinese practice.

### Active Components

The seeds of *Descurainia* and *Lepidium* belong to the mustard family, and seeds of this family commonly contain glucosinolates and, in *Descurainia*, cardiac-glycoside-type compounds have been reported in the seed. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

This is a historical record of one text, not a recommended practice. It is a lower-class drug, which the preface describes as mostly toxic and not to be taken long.

- **What the text says it is for.** Hard masses and accumulations ("concretions, conglomerations"), bound qi, and "cold and heat" with food and drink.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no 久服 promise; the toxicity of this entry is not stated, and the class heading is the only caution in the text.

No Kenetica protocol is derived from this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Mingyi layer gives the harvest and one pairing: the fruit is gathered after the start of summer and dried in the shade; "it does well with wine" (line 3131; repeated in the relations table, line 4427). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

*Descurainia sophia* (flixweed) is a weedy crucifer; its seed and that of *Lepidium apetalum* are the crude drug *tinglizi* in Chinese practice. The seed of *Descurainia* has been reported to contain cardiac-glycoside-type compounds, which is a reason for caution. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic, which the preface (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2935) defines as "the left envoys, mainly treating disease to correspond with earth; mostly toxic and not to be taken long," for one who "wishes to remove cold and heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and heal illness." The entry has no 久服 clause. No cultivation status is attested; the grade is itself the caution.

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug: the classic itself says such drugs are for a defined disease and are not for long taking. A cultivator has nothing to do with it.

### Contraindications

- **Species and source.** The classic does not fix the species, and the seeds of different crucifers have been sold under this name. [USER: verify]
- **Lower-class drug.** The classic says mostly toxic and not for long use.
- **Dose and form unknown.** The text gives none.
- **Pregnancy and lactation.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Masses and swellings.** The indications (hard masses, accumulations) need medical diagnosis.
