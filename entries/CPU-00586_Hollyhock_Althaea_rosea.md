# Hollyhock — Arabic *Khatmī kabīr / Ward khatmī* · Greek (mallow group) *Althaia* · Sanskrit (adopted) *Gul-khairū* · Chinese *Shǔ kuí (蜀葵)*

## *Alcea rosea* L. (syn. *Althaea rosea* (L.) Cav.) | Family: Malvaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT · CM · DMM · SBJ · SS

**Energetic Classification:** Cooling, Moist (moistening demulcent with a mild cooling astringency, Galenic reading)

**Training Phase:** Foundation

---

### Origin & Tradition

Hollyhock is marshmallow's ornamental sister — a tall, showy Malvaceae long grown for its flowers but carrying the same defining virtue: mucilage. Its taxonomy has shifted (formerly *Althaea rosea*, now generally *Alcea rosea*), but its pharmacology sits squarely in the mallow demulcent family. Unlike true marshmallow, hollyhock is also a genuinely East Asian medicinal — *shǔ kuí* — which is why it carries a Shennong/Chinese tag that marshmallow does not.

**Dioscorides / Greek (mallow group):** The cultivated and wild mallows are treated together in the Greek tradition as emollient, soothing plants; hollyhock belongs to this *malaktika* (softener) family, its flowers and roots used for the same softening and soothing purposes as marshmallow. The attestation is at the mallow-group level for the specific ornamental.

**Avicenna (Qanun):** In Unani medicine hollyhock (a *khatmī* type) is used as a demulcent and emollient for cough, chest roughness, urinary irritation, and as a soothing poultice — the same category as marshmallow, with the flowers particularly valued. This is a genuine continuing Unani use.

**Shennong Bencao / Chinese:** Hollyhock (*shǔ kuí*, 蜀葵) is a recognised plant of the Chinese materia medica; the flowers, seeds, and root are used as cooling, moistening, and diuretic agents — to clear heat, promote urination, soothe the throat and intestines, and for menstrual and urinary complaints. This is a real and distinct tradition, and modern Chinese pharmacology has specifically studied hollyhock flowers for metabolic (antidiabetic) effects.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda framing:** As with marshmallow, hollyhock enters Indian practice through the Unani-influenced *gul-khairū* demulcent category — *snigdha* (unctuous), *picchila* (slimy), *shita* (cooling) — soothing irritated mucous membranes and pacifying *pitta*.

**Assyrian / Mesopotamian:** The mallow group is named among the soothing plants of the cuneiform medical tablets; hollyhock, as a cultivated Malvaceae of the region, belongs to this attested mallow-poultice tradition at the group level.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Hollyhock carries the same demulcent doctrine as marshmallow — protection by interposition of a moist, clinging film — but it adds a **flower-forward** character. Where marshmallow's power is in the root, hollyhock concentrates striking pigmented flowers rich in anthocyanins and flavonoids, and the tradition reads the flower as the seat of its cooling, heat-clearing, and "blood-moving" (menstrual/urinary) virtues. Here the symbolism of the conspicuous flower — visible, colourful, superficial — maps onto a real pharmacological division of labour: the mucilage (soothing barrier) sits alongside anthocyanin and flavonoid pigments (antioxidant, mildly vasoactive, metabolically active). The plant teaches that surface (flower, colour, display) and depth (root, mucilage, substance) can carry complementary medicines in one organism.

### Active Components

- **Mucilage polysaccharides** — as in marshmallow, acidic rhamnogalacturonan/arabinogalactan gels giving the demulcent action; present in root, leaf, and flower.
- **Anthocyanins** — including malvidin/delphinidin-type pigments (notably in the dark "black" hollyhock cultivars), responsible for colour and antioxidant activity; used historically as a natural colourant and pH indicator.
- **Flavonoids and dihydroflavonols** — kaempferol and quercetin glycosides, apigenin, dihydrokaempferol derivatives, and novel dihydroflavonol glycosides (roseaflavanonolosides) characterised from the flowers.
- **Phenolic acids and tannins** — astringent and antioxidant fraction.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Hollyhock is a **Foundation-phase demulcent with a metabolic edge**. It shares marshmallow's role as a mucosal protective primer — soothing throat, gut, and urinary irritation and calming dry cough — but is selected in the Kenetica curriculum when a *cooling, mildly astringent, and metabolically supportive* demulcent is wanted rather than pure emollience. Its documented effects on glucose handling make it the demulcent of choice for practitioners whose Foundation work overlaps with metabolic regulation. The flowers are used as a cooling infusion (and as a natural indicator/colourant in the practical craft of preparation), while root and leaf supply the demulcent gel. Its teaching function is the same protection-before-action principle as marshmallow, with the added lesson that a single plant's showy surface and quiet depths can be harvested for different ends.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- **Zhang et al. (2015), *Fitoterapia*** — an ethanolic extract of hollyhock (*Althaea rosea*) flower significantly reduced serum triglyceride and glucose in KK-Aʸ diabetic mice over 8 weeks and up-regulated hepatic AMPK, IRS2, PI3K, AKT and GLUT4; three new dihydroflavonol glycosides (roseaflavanonolosides A–C) were isolated, and these dihydroflavonols increased glucose uptake ~30–40% in HepG2 cells, linking the antidiabetic effect to the flower flavonoids. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fitote.2015.02.001
- **Abdel-Salam et al. (2017), *Natural Product Research*** — six flavonoids were isolated from *Alcea rosea* flowers; a coumaroyl-kaempferol glucoside showed potent, selective cytotoxicity against HepG-2 hepatocellular carcinoma cells (IC50 ≈ 3.8 µg/mL), while other isolates showed antioxidant and immunostimulant activity, documenting the flower's flavonoid pharmacology. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2017.1332602

Evidence grade: **preclinical.** The demulcent use is mechanistically shared with marshmallow (mucilage), and the flower's antidiabetic, antioxidant, and cytotoxic activities are demonstrated in animal and cell-based models with characterised constituents — but there are no controlled human trials. The traditional soothing/cooling/diuretic uses are long-attested across Unani and Chinese practice.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Cool (liang), taste sweet-slightly bitter (gan-wei ku); clears heat, moistens dryness, promotes urination, soothes throat and intestine; flowers and seeds used for urinary and menstrual complaints.
- **Ayurveda (adopted framing):** Rasa *madhura-kashaya* (sweet-astringent), guna *snigdha-picchila* (unctuous, slimy), virya *shita* (cooling), vipaka *madhura*; pacifies *pitta* and *vata*, may increase *kapha*.
- **Galenic/Unani:** Moist and cooling (first–second degree); emollient/demulcent (*mulaiyin*), soothing to chest and urinary passages.

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian (AT).** In the cuneiform medical tablets the mallow group is named among the soothing, poulticing plants, and hollyhock — a cultivated Malvaceae of the region — sits within that group-level attestation rather than as a securely identified single drug. Illness in this corpus is classified by causal agency (the hand of a god, a ghost, a broken oath), so a plant is known partly by what agency its application is set against; but the standing blocking warning on Assyrian substance identifications, deity attributions and tablet references means no such reading is asserted here. The attestation is therapeutic and at the group level only. Cultivation status: **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides treats the cultivated and wild mallows together as *malaktika*, softening emollient plants, and hollyhock belongs to that mallow family at the group level rather than as his own separately named drug. He carries no subtle-body system into which a cultivation status could be placed — the Galenic three spirits are later and post-Dioscoridean. What the tradition offers a cultivator is instead his discipline of attention: the distinction of cultivated from wild mallow, the season and the part gathered, the sensory assay of the mucilage. That is a real practice of provenance and judgement, not a vital-body doctrine. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** Hollyhock, a *khatmī* type, is used in Unani practice as a demulcent and emollient (*mulaiyin*) for cough, chest roughness and urinary irritation, classed cooling and moist. The strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus — the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture, ageing being the depletion of that finite endowment, so that longevity practice is conservation and not acquisition — is a frame of the whole regimen, not a status this cooling demulcent is individually assigned; no *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed for it, and no *Canon* chapter is cited. Attested therapeutically, with no cultivation status sourced: **(b) framed.**

**Chinese — Shennong / Bencao (SBJ).** Hollyhock (*shǔ kuí* 蜀葵) is a recognised plant of the Chinese materia medica, its flowers, seeds and root worked to clear heat, promote urination and soothe the throat and intestines. No *san pin* 三品 grade is recorded for *shǔ kuí* in the sources retrieved, so within *yang sheng* 養生 — the conservational, not curative, project — it carries a therapeutic rather than a cultivation status; and a *qing shen* 輕身 or life-nourishing standing must not be silently borrowed from other, differently named mallow-family drugs of the Chinese corpus. **(b) framed.**

**Ayurveda (SS).** Hollyhock enters Indian practice through the adopted, Unani-influenced *gul-khairū* demulcent category, given the *dravyaguna* reading *madhura-kashaya* rasa, *snigdha-picchila* guna and *shita* virya, pacifying *pitta*. No *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) assignment — the formal Sushrutan cultivation divisions — is recorded for it; its standing is therapeutic and dietetic, an adopted demulcent rather than a native rejuvenative *dravya*. **(b) framed.**

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a longevity substance. Across all five traditions hollyhock is worked as a gentle demulcent — a cooling flower infusion, a cold-macerated root or leaf gel, a soothing poultice — taken for a defined irritation of throat, gut or urinary passage and then set aside. Its one distinctive edge, the flower's documented effect on glucose handling, is a therapeutic and metabolic use, not a vital-constitution practice. A cultivator uses it to soothe and protect a surface, then stops; it builds nothing that must be conserved.

### Contraindications

- **Absorption interference:** as with all mucilaginous demulcents, hollyhock can slow or reduce absorption of oral drugs taken at the same time — separate by 1–2 hours.
- **Hypoglycaemia caution:** given the documented glucose-lowering activity of the flower, monitor when used alongside antidiabetic medication — additive hypoglycaemia is plausible.
- **Hypersensitivity:** rare Malvaceae allergy; hollyhock pollen is a recognised contact/airborne allergen for sensitive individuals.
- **Pregnancy/lactation:** traditional cooling/diuretic and menstrual uses mean concentrated preparations should be avoided in pregnancy without advice; food-level flower infusions are traditionally regarded as mild.
- **Cytotoxic constituents:** isolated flower flavonoids show cytotoxicity in vitro — relevant to concentrated extracts/isolates, not to gentle traditional infusions, but a reason not to self-administer high-dose extracts.

Overall toxicity of the whole-plant traditional preparations is low; the practical cautions are the drug-absorption interaction and the hypoglycaemic potential.
