# Fox — ثعلب (thaʿlab)

## *The fox, whole animal, fat, fur and dried lung, as the Canon uses them; usually* Vulpes vulpes *(modern identification of Arabic* thaʿlab*)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Avicenna (CM).** The fox as a whole animal is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. It is read in the Arabic (`Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`, Book II, entry *thaʿlab*, heading at Arabic text line 18793). The fox lung is a separate entry already on the spine, **III_20 Fox lung** (Dioscorides and the Tibetan text); this entry adds the whole animal and its other parts from the *Canon*, and repeats the *Canon*'s lung line only as part of the entry.

*Al-khawāṣṣ* (actions): "there is dissolving in it (*taḥlīl*); its fur is the warmest of furs, and the moist benefit from it because it dissolves" (فيه تحليل. وفراؤه أسخن الفراء ينتفع بها المرطوبون لتحليلها).

*Ālāt al-mafāṣil* (joints): "if the fox is boiled in water and the painful joints are anointed with it, it benefits strongly; likewise the oil in which it is boiled alive, and this is much stronger; and he must sit long in it. The best is that this be after evacuation and purification, so that, by its strength of drawing and dissolving, it does not draw a humour to the joints; and if the body is evacuated after that too, nothing will pour to the joints; if it returns, it is slight. Likewise fox fat may draw something more than it dissolves. It may be boiled in oil alive and boiled in it slaughtered; whichever is used dissolves what is in the joints." Here Avicenna states a risk in his own terms: the drug that draws and dissolves may draw a humour into the joints if the body has not first been purged.

*Aʿḍāʾ al-raʾs* (head): "its fat calms ear pain when dropped into it."

*Aʿḍāʾ al-ṣadr* (chest): "its dried lung is very beneficial for the asthmatic (*ṣāḥib al-rabw*); the draught is the weight of a dirham" (والشربة وزن درهم).

Elsewhere in the text, outside this entry, the fox and the hyena are named together for the same joint complaint: a bath in which a fox or hyena is boiled is "the best treatment for those with joint pains and gout" (line 4257), the recipe boils a live or slaughtered hyena and fox "in their blood" until they fall apart, strains the water, and has the patient sit in it, or boils them in oil (lines 45239 to 45243), and fox and hyena fat are among the external fats for rubbing (line 39766). I read those lines only to see how the entry is used; the main text is the Book II entry above.

**Identity.** *Thaʿlab* is the fox; the species is not named in the text. *Vulpes vulpes* (red fox) is the modern default for the Middle East; other fox species also occur there. [USER: verify if a species is wanted.]

### Active Components

The fox as a source: its fat is animal fat (triglycerides) and its lung is soft tissue; nothing more specific is known to apply to the uses described. No composition is asserted from the text. A general caution applies: wild canids can carry rabies and other zoonoses.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Avicenna's uses: hot broth or oil of the fox as an external bath or rub for joint pain; fox fat in the ear for ear pain; the dried lung as a drink for asthma; the fur as a warm garment for those with moist temperaments. This is not a recommended practice: it involves killing and cooking a wild predator, the risk of zoonotic infection, and, for the lung, a drug with no tested effect (see Scientific Correlation).

### Preparation & Dosing

Preparations given: the fox boiled whole in water (a wash); the fox boiled in oil, alive or slaughtered (a bath or rub); the fat dropped into the ear; the dried lung drunk by weight of a dirham. Only the lung has a dose: "the weight of a dirham" (line 18793 ff.). No other dose is given. The dirham is not converted to a modern weight here. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Warm baths and fat-based rubs soothe aching joints by warmth, which is a general fact. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] No evidence is claimed for fox lung in asthma.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Avicenna (CM).** No temperament degree is printed for the fox in this entry. Action: "dissolving" (*taḥlīl*); the fur is "the warmest of furs".

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The fox entry is a list of external and internal remedies by organ. Its one general theme, the dissolving and drawing of humours, is a therapeutic one. Avicenna's warning that drawing may bring a humour into the joints of an unpurged body is a caution about the humours, not a statement about the *ruḥ*, innate heat or radical moisture. No cultivation status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to cultivate here; the fox is a joint and chest remedy of a hunting culture, and the cultivator's task would be restraint.

### Contraindications

Historical record only. Avicenna's own caution: use after purging, otherwise the drawing action may bring a humour into the joints. General cautions not in the entry: wild foxes can carry rabies, mange and other infections, handling and boiling carcasses is a hygiene risk, and the killing of wild animals is regulated in most countries. Do not treat any use here as medical advice.
