# Hermodactyl — aṣābiʿ ṣufr (أصابع صفر), "fingers of *ṣufr*" (the word can mean yellow or brass; the entry does not say which)

## *Hermodactyl, the finger-shaped corm of a* Colchicum*-type plant (in the standard trade identification); the identification is the modern one, made from the Arabic name and the shape, and is of low-to-moderate confidence, because Avicenna's entry names no plant, only the shape and colour of the dried pieces* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

Hermodactyl is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry, and the text is very short. The spine's *Colchicum* entries (for example I_074 and I_486) come from other traditions and are not merged here: this entry stands on the Arabic name *aṣābiʿ ṣufr*, "fingers of *ṣufr*", and I did not search the other texts on disk for it. The equation with *Colchicum* is the modern one.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The *Canon* (Book II, entry *aṣābiʿ ṣufr*, Arabic text line 10369) says what it is in one sentence: "the shape of the fingers of *ṣufr*, like the palm, mottled with yellow and white, hard, with a little sweetness; and of it a yellow kind with dustiness and no white" (شكل أصابع الصفر كالكف أبلق من صفرة وبياض صلب فيه قليل حلاوة ومنه أصفر مع غبرة بلا بياض). Nothing is said of where or how the plant grows, of the leaf or the flower. Nothing is said of gout. There is no quotation of Dioscorides or Galen in the entry.

### Active Components

Corms of *Colchicum* species contain colchicine and related alkaloids. This is general, well-established chemistry. Colchicine is a potent toxin: it interferes with cell division and its therapeutic dose is close to a toxic one. If the Arabic *aṣābiʿ ṣufr* is a *Colchicum* corm, as the trade identification holds, this applies to it. Avicenna's entry does not mention any toxicity, and the identification is not certain, so the chemistry is offered as background and not as a statement about what Avicenna handled. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The *Canon* gives three actions: it "dissolves very thick superfluities"; it "has a property of benefiting the nervous (*ʿaṣabiyya*) organs and their afflictions"; and for the head it "benefits madness (*junūn*) especially" (lines 10372-10374). It gives no condition by name for the joints. A modern reader must not infer that this is a gout drug from the entry; the text does not say so.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation, dose or duration is given in the entry. The only direction is a substitute: "its substitute in its benefit for madness is its like and half of it of *hazārjashān*, with a third of *saʿd*" (الأبدال: بدله في منفعته من الجنون مثله ومثل نصفه هزارجشان مع ثلثه سعدا, lines 10376-10377). I have not identified *hazārjashān* or *saʿd*, and they are left unidentified here. [USER: verify] No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Colchicine, from *Colchicum* corms, is used in modern medicine for gout and familial Mediterranean fever, and overdose is dangerous. That is general knowledge, and it concerns the alkaloid, not Avicenna's drug. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry on the uses the *Canon* names (madness, nervous afflictions). ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **CM (Avicenna), line 10371-10372.** "It is hot and dry in the second [degree], approximately" (حار يابس في الثانية تقريبا). Its action is "dissolving of very thick superfluities."
- No other tradition is tagged.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The entry is six lines long, and it speaks only of dissolving thick superfluities, the nervous organs, and madness. It has nothing on the innate heat, the radical moisture or the *ruh*, and it ascribes no standing in the care of life to the drug.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing is asked of a cultivator. If the identification is right, this is a potent-alkaloid corm, and the tradition's own record here is of a short, almost silent entry. Treat it as a record of a name and a shape, and not as a usable remedy.

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity.** If the drug is a *Colchicum* corm, it can poison. Poisoning with colchicine causes severe gastrointestinal illness and can be fatal. Do not take unidentified or home-prepared material. This is general knowledge and not from the Arabic text.
- **Identification.** "Hermodactyl" in the trade has covered more than one plant; the identification of Avicenna's drug is not secure.
- **Madness.** The mental-illness use is historical record only; it is not a treatment.
