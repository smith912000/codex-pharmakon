### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk (Bhishagratna's 1907 English translation).** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from a name list (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, which records "group list (ch.38); no identification given here"). This entry replaces it. The text was opened at the line given and the neighbouring lines read. Searches of the other texts on disk (Dioscorides, Shennong, rGyud-bzhi, Ebers) were not made under a Sanskrit spelling, so absence elsewhere is not claimed.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Kandekshuka is printed in the Pancha-Trina group, Sutrasthana chapter 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15874 (line 15873 gives the first part of the sentence). The translator's sentence reads: "The group consisting of the five medicinal herbs (of the grass species) and known as Kusha, Kasha, Nala, Darbha, Kandekshuka, is called the Pancha-Trina." The metrical text that follows (lines 15877 to 15880) reads: "Cases of Haemoptysis, renal defects or of uninary diseases are found to speedily yield to the curative efficacy of the compound internally administered through the medium of cow's milk." The OCR spelling "uninary" is the e-text's. Near the end of the chapter (line 15890) the translation calls the same group "Trina-Panchamula" and says it "is endued with the property of killing the deranged Pittam".

**What the text gives about the plant.** Only that it is one of five "medicinal herbs (of the grass species)", the parenthesis being the translator's. No description, habitat, part used or synonym is printed. It may be a compound of *kanda* and *ikshu* (a joint or root-stock and sugar-cane), which would point to a sugar-cane-like grass; this is our guess from the word and is not in the text. ### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is named inside a therapeutic drug group (*gana*) of Sutrasthana chapter 38, the Pancha-Trina group. The group's metrical text speaks of curing or subduing disorders.
