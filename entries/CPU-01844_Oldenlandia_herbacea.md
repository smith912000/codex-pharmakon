# Oldenlandia herbacea — Kshetra-parpata; Parpata, Parpataka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

(line 32096) against the Sanskrit name the OCR gives as Kshetra-parpata (line 32063), and, in the OCR spelling "Oldenlandia  herbocea." **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Kshetra-parpata (and Kshetra, Ksetra) and by fuzzy matching of every word: nothing was found (the word Kshetra in Sharira ch.1 and 2 means the field or the soil in a physiological sense, not a plant). Parpataka and Parpata were searched in the same way and are printed in these places.

*A bitter herb (Sutra ch.46).* `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1653 to 1659, in the chapter on food and drink, lists Parpataka among the potherbs that "are light and palatable, and prove curative in haemoptysis, Kushtha, Meha, fever, dyspnoea, cough and create a relish for food". Lines 1698 to 1705 add: "The herbs and creepers such as the Atarushaka, Kiratatikta, Parpataka and Guduchi together with the tender shoots of the Vetra and the Nimva, have a bitter taste and subdue the Pittam and Kapham."

*Medicated ghee in the chapter on Kushtha (Chikitsa ch.9).* Line 113: for the Pittaja type "clarified butter prepared with (a decoction and Kalka of) Dhava, Ashvakarna, Kakubha, Palasha, Pichumarda, Parpataka, Madhuka, Rodhra and Samanga". Line 140: Parpataka is one of the drugs of the *Maha-tikta Ghrita*, "which proves curative in Kushtha, chronic fevers, haemorrhage, heart-disease, insanity, Apasmara, Gulma, ..." Lines 155 to 166: the *Tikta-Sarpih*: "Two Pala weight of each of the following drugs, viz., Triphala, Patola, Pichumarda, Atanishaka, Katurohini, Duralabha, Trayamana and Parpataka should be taken and boiled together in a Drona measure of water" down to a quarter, with a Kalka of further drugs and a Prastha of clarified butter. The text says the ghrita is for Kushtha, chronic fever, Gulma, haemorrhoids, Grahani, oedema, jaundice, erysipelas and impotency (the list continues on the next page).

*Fever (Uttara ch.39).* Parpataka is in at least seven recipes of the fever chapter. Lines 1096 to 1104: a decoction of "Nagara, Dhanyaka, Bhargi, Abhaya, Devadaru, Vacha, Parpataka, Musta, Bhutika and Katphala mixed with honey and Hingu" for fever "accompanied with bronchitis, cough, asthma, constriction of the throat, hic-cough". Lines 1114 to 1120, in the Pitta-Shleshmaja fever: a decoction of "Katuka, Vijaya (Haritaki), Draksha, Musta and Parpataka", or of "Bhargi, Vacha, Parpataka, Dhanyaka, Hingu, Abhaya, Ghana, Nagara and Kasmarya mixed with honey". Lines 1262 to 1268, a medicated ghee with a decoction of Kalasi, Vrihati, Draksha, Tryanti, Nimba, Gokshura, Bala, Parpataka, Musta, Shala-parni and Yavasaka. Lines 1272 to 1280, the *Patoladi Ghrita*, with the Kalka of Patola, "Parpata", Arishta, Guduchi and others; lines 1353 to 1358, the *Triphaladi Ghrita*; lines 1374 to 1388, a ghrita from a decoction of one Pala each of Patola, Katuka, Darvi, Nimba, Vasa, Triphala, Duralabha, Parpataka and Trayamana with a Prastha of Amalaka, for "Raktapitta, diseases due to Kapha, ... fever, chlorosis, erysipelas and Ganda-mala (scrofula)"; and lines 1398 to 1403, a medicated oil with "Parpata" among its Kalka drugs. In Uttara ch.40 (the diarrhoea chapter) line 222 names it in a list of drugs, "Musta, Parpataka, Shunthi, Vacha, Ativisha and Abhaya", for Atisara.

*Enemas and oils (Chikitsa ch.37 and 38).* In the chapter on Anuvasana and Uttara-vasti, lines 157 to 166, a paste of Utpala, Saluka, the two Sariva, Nagakesara, the two Chandana, Bhunimba, Padmavija, Kaseruka, Patola, Katuka, Manjishtha, Gundra, "Parpataka and Vasaka (weighing one seer in all)" is cooked with a decoction of Trina-mula, with oil and with milk twice as much as the oil, for "a variety of Pittaja diseases". In the chapter on the Nirudha-vasti, lines 396 to 416, a paste including "Renu (Parpataka)" among Jivaka, Rishabhaka, Kakoli, Medd, Parushaka and other drugs is mixed into a decoction with milk, honey and ghee; the translator's note says it is cold and non-acid "as the injection is to be applied in cases of diseases due to the derangement of Pitta". The bracket "(Parpataka)" after Renu is the translator's.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Parpata, Parpataka and Kshetra-parpata found nothing.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Oldenlandia* is a genus of the madder family (Rubiaceae), and *O. herbacea* is a small herb. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. In the Sushruta text, under the name Parpataka, the herb is a bitter drug of Pitta and Kapha disorders: a member of many medicated ghees and decoctions for Kushtha and fever, a bitter potherb, and an ingredient in Pitta enemas and oils. The Codex does not recommend any of these, gives no protocol and no dose, and notes that the plant behind the name rests on a translator's appendix pairing.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters give these preparations that include the name: decoctions with honey (Uttara 39); medicated ghee (Chikitsa 9; Uttara 39); medicated oil (Uttara 39; Chikitsa 37); the enema paste (Chikitsa 38). Quantities for the drug itself are given in two recipes: "Two Pala weight" of Parpataka among the Tikta-Sarpih drugs (Chikitsa 9); "one Pala weight each" with the Patola group, with a Prastha of Amalaka, in the ghrita of Uttara 39 (lines 1374 to 1388); and "one seer in all" for the pasted drugs in Chikitsa 37. These are quantities for the making of the preparation, not a patient dose. No dose for a patient is given in the sources retrieved for Parpataka. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** Parpataka is bitter and subdues Pitta and Kapha (Sutra 46, lines 1698 to 1705), and is counted among light, palatable potherbs that create a relish for food (lines 1653 to 1659). The text prints no *virya* or *vipaka* for it singly. For the name Kshetra-parpata none is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Parpataka in therapeutic contexts: bitter ghees and decoctions for Kushtha and fever, enemas and oils for Pitta disorders, and a potherb list. The row's own key name, Kshetra-parpata, is not in any chapter. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name either name, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The grade rests on the Codex's second pairing, not on the row's key name. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with this herb; it is a bitter ingredient in treatments of illness, and the Latin behind the name is a translator's appendix pairing.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this herb. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Fever, Kushtha (skin disease), bleeding and Gulma need medical care; an enema is a clinical measure and not a home one. No conservation information was read.
