# Nagakeshara (Cobra-Saffron / Ironwood) — Nāgakeśara, Nāgapuṣpa, Kāñcana, Kesara, Nagkesar

## *Mesua ferrea* L. | Family: Calophyllaceae (formerly Clusiaceae/Guttiferae)

**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā) · Ayurvedic classical materia medica · the **fourth member** completing the **Caturjāta** (Trijāta + Nāgakeśara) aromatic set

**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa (taste): Kaṣāya (astringent), Tikta (bitter), Kaṭu (pungent) · Vīrya (potency): Uṣṇa (warming) — *classically also described as light/slightly cooling by some authorities; see note* · Vipāka (post-digestive): Kaṭu (pungent) · Guṇa (qualities): Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry), Tīkṣṇa (sharp) · Doṣa action: Kapha-Pitta-śāmaka (pacifies Kapha and Pitta by its astringency).

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** Nāgakeśara — classically the dried **stamens (and flower buds)** of the ironwood tree — is an Ayurvedic astringent, haemostatic, and aromatic drug. In the classical corpus it is prescribed for *raktapitta* (bleeding disorders / haemorrhage), *atisāra* and *pravāhikā* (diarrhoea and dysentery), *arśa* (haemorrhoids, especially bleeding), excessive menstrual and other fluxes, and skin and Pitta-heat conditions. Its dominant astringency (*kaṣāya rasa*) makes it the classical instrument for **binding and staunching** — arresting bleeding, checking discharge, and toning lax tissue.

**The fourth of the Caturjāta:** When the aromatic triad **Tvak–Elā–Patra (Trijāta)** is expanded to four, the added member is **Nāgakeśara**, forming the **Caturjāta** ("four-born"). Nāgakeśara brings the *astringent-aromatic* note to this warming set, contributing a binding, toning quality that the three warming-aromatics lack. This membership places it at the heart of a great many classical compound formulae.

**Etymology & the "cobra-saffron":** *Nāga* (serpent/cobra) + *keśara* (stamen/filament, as of saffron). The name evokes the mass of fine golden stamens of the flower, likened to saffron threads — hence the English "cobra's saffron." The species epithet *ferrea* ("iron") and the common name **ironwood** record the extraordinary heaviness and hardness of its timber. The tree is native to the Indomalayan tropics (Indian subcontinent, Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia), cultivated for its form, glossy foliage, and fragrant flowers, and holds cultural and religious significance across the region.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Iron-Wooded Astringent that Binds and Seals:**
Nāgakeśara's symbolic role is that of the *sealer* — the substance of closure, binding, and containment. Astringency, in the Ayurvedic reading, is the taste of *contraction*: it draws tissue together, checks flow, and holds form; it is the flavour that stops leakage, whether of blood, of fluid, or, metaphorically, of vital resource. Everything about the plant reinforces this: its wood is so dense it is named for iron, an emblem of solidity and the arrest of motion; its action staunches the most alarming of outflows, haemorrhage. Where the warming aromatics of the Caturjāta *open* and *activate*, Nāgakeśara *closes* and *consolidates* — which is precisely why it completes the set, supplying the binding counter-note to their diffusive heat. In Kenetica it is filed as the **sealing agent of the consolidation phase**: the substance taken to bind and finish, to staunch loss and tone what has been opened, drawing a cycle of work to a contained, secured end. Its symbol is the golden serpent-saffron stamen set in iron-hard wood — fineness and fragrance held within absolute solidity; the beautiful surface that seals a hard, unyielding core.

### Active Components

*Mesua ferrea* is characterised by **coumarin-type constituents** — notably **mesuol** (a 4-alkyl/aryl coumarin isolated from the seed oil) and related compounds — together with **xanthones**, **triterpenoids**, **flavonoids/polyphenols**, and an aromatic **volatile oil** in the flowers/stamens. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Nāgakeśara is the **astringent haemostatic and sealing aromatic** — reached for where binding, staunching, and toning are called for: bleeding, flux, lax or leaking tissue, and the finishing/consolidating end of a protocol. Traditional and preliminary experimental uses:

- **Haemostatic / anti-haemorrhagic** — classical *raktapitta*, bleeding piles, menorrhagia.
- **Antidiarrhoeal / antidysenteric** (*grāhī*, binding) — checking flux.
- **Anti-inflammatory / antiarthritic** — classical pain and rheumatic use (see Scientific Correlation).
- **Immunomodulatory interest** — ingredient of classical rasāyana formulae (Brahma Rasāyana, Chyavanaprāśa).
- **Astringent completing note** in the Caturjāta.

Use-case framing: a **sealing/consolidation agent** — the binding, finishing counterpart to the opening warming aromatics.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- Various extracts of ***Mesua ferrea* seed** showed significant **antiarthritic / anti-inflammatory activity** against formaldehyde- and Complete-Freund's-Adjuvant-induced arthritis in rats, validating the classical ethnomedicinal use for pain, inflammation, and rheumatic conditions (Jalalpure et al., *J Ethnopharmacol*, 2011) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2011.09.042).
- **Mesuol**, isolated from *M. ferrea* seed oil, demonstrated **in-vivo antioxidant and immunomodulatory activity** in rodents — restoring haematological profiles in cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression and potentiating humoral/cellular immune responses, phagocytosis, and neutrophil adhesion (Chahar et al., *Int Immunopharmacol*, 2012) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intimp.2012.05.006).

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical (in vivo animal + isolate-level). The anti-inflammatory/immunomodulatory signals are experimentally supported and consonant with tradition, but derive largely from the **seed/seed-oil** (not the classical stamen drug) and are **not established in human trials**. Note also that the **haemostatic/astringent action** — the drug's signature classical use — is *not* directly evidenced by the sources retrieved this run; treat that specific claim as **traditional, pending clinical/pharmacological confirmation**.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṣāya (astringent) — dominant — with Tikta (bitter) and Kaṭu (pungent).
- **Vīrya (potency):** Classically most often **Uṣṇa (warming)**; some authorities describe it as light/slightly cooling. `[USER: verify preferred vīrya against chosen classical authority — nighaṇṭus differ.]`
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** Kaṭu (pungent).
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry), Tīkṣṇa (sharp) — drying, binding, toning.
- **Doṣa action:** *Kapha-Pitta-śāmaka* — its astringency and bitterness pacify Kapha and Pitta (notably the bleeding/heat of aggravated Pitta); excess dryness may aggravate Vāta.
- **Karma (actions):** *Grāhī / stambhana* (binding, checking flux), *raktastambhaka* (haemostatic), *śothahara* (anti-inflammatory), *dīpana* (mild digestive kindling), *varṇya* (complexion-enhancing), *rasāyana*-adjunct (rejuvenative in compound context).

### Contraindications

- **Astringent and drying — may aggravate Vāta**, constipation, and dryness; caution in Vāta-dominant or depleted constitutions.
- Its binding/*grāhī* action means caution where retention rather than flux is the problem.
- **Drug-part confusion:** the classical drug is the **stamen/flower**; seed and seed-oil have a different (coumarin-rich) profile — source and use deliberately.
