### Origin & Tradition

Great burnet is one of the rare drugs where three literate traditions — Chinese, Greek and Arabic-Persian — independently arrived at the *same* indication, and where the plant's **Latin name is itself the clinical claim**: *sanguis* (blood) + *sorbere* (to absorb) — the **blood-absorber**.

**Shennong / Chinese (SS).** The root — **Di Yu (地榆)**, "earth elm" — is a foundational drug of the Chinese haemostatic materia medica, entering the tradition from the *Shennong Bencao Jing* stratum and standardised in the later *bencao* through to the modern Pharmacopoeia. Its doctrinal category is precise and not interchangeable with other styptics: **凉血止血 (liáng xuè zhǐ xuè)** — *cool the blood and stop bleeding* — plus **解毒斂瘡 (jiě dú liǎn chuāng)** — *resolve toxin and astringe sores*. The classical indication set is therefore narrow and specific: bleeding **from the lower burner and from heat** — bloody stool, haemorrhoidal bleeding, bloody dysentery, excessive uterine bleeding — and, in the external register, **burns and scalds**, for which charred/powdered Di Yu applied to the burn surface is one of the tradition's signature applications. Di Yu is *cold*: it is for **hot** bleeding, not cold-deficiency bleeding, and the tradition is emphatic about that distinction. A carbonised form (**Di Yu Tan**, 地榆炭) is prepared specifically to intensify the haemostatic action.

**Dioscorides (DMM).** The Greek pharmacological corpus places the burnet-type Rosaceae among the **stypticals** (στυπτικά) — drugs that draw together, close, and staunch. The action is described in terms of *contraction*: they check fluxes, close wounds, stop haemorrhage and the loose bowel. The Greek reading is mechanical rather than humoral-thermal: the drug *tightens*.

**Avicenna / Unani (SBJ).** In the Arabic-Persian nosology burnet falls under the *qābiḍ* (astringent, contracting) drugs, cold and dry, indicated as *ḥābis* — a stancher — for haemorrhage, for *isḥāl* (diarrhoea and dysentery), and applied to wounds and ulcers to close and dry them. The Unani framework adds the thermal reading the Greeks left implicit and that the Chinese made central: a cold, dry, contracting drug is the natural antagonist of hot, wet, escaping blood.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.**

The doctrinal signature here is unusually clean, because the *name is the doctrine*: **the blood-absorber**. Three claims are being made, and all three land on real chemistry.

- **"Astringes / contracts / absorbs" = hydrolysable tannin protein precipitation.** *Sanguisorba* root is one of the most tannin-dense drugs in the Rosaceae — dominated by **hydrolysable tannins** (gallotannins and ellagitannins), **gallic acid**, and **catechins**. Tannins are polyphenols with many phenolic hydroxyls; they cross-link proteins by hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interaction, precipitating them. Applied to a bleeding or weeping surface they **denature and coagulate surface proteins into a sealing layer**, constrict exposed capillary beds, and reduce exudation. "Astringency" is not a metaphor for a sensation; it is protein precipitation, and the puckering mouthfeel is the same reaction happening to salivary proteins. The doctrine of *contraction* is a direct, correct description of tannin–protein chemistry.
- **"止血 — it stops bleeding" = a genuine haemostatic mechanism, plus a second one nobody in the tradition could have seen.** The tannin layer explains *topical* haemostasis. But modern work has found a second, deeper mechanism that maps onto the *systemic* half of the Chinese claim: constituents of Di Yu promote **megakaryocyte differentiation and platelet production**. The tradition said "cool and tonify the blood" — a dual claim that looked like doctrinal excess. It is now the best-supported thing about the plant.
- **"斂瘡 — it astringes sores; the burn drug."** Burn injury is exactly the lesion for which a protein-precipitating, exudate-reducing, antimicrobial polyphenol layer is mechanistically apt: tannins reduce plasma leakage from the burn surface and gallic acid is antibacterial. The choice of *charred* Di Yu for burns adds a carbon adsorbent to the tannin — a physically coherent burn dressing built centuries before the chemistry existed.
- **"Cool the blood" (凉血) = the thermal frame.** In TCM, blood that is *hot* moves recklessly and escapes its vessels. Cooling drugs settle it. Modern language would call the same clinical picture inflammation-associated bleeding; the doctrinal instruction "use for hot bleeding, not cold-deficiency bleeding" is, functionally, an instruction to reserve an astringent-anti-inflammatory for an inflammatory-haemorrhagic presentation rather than a hypofunctional one.
- **The triterpene layer.** Beyond tannins, the root carries **ziyuglycosides I and II** — the plant's characteristic ursane-type triterpene saponins (*ziyu* 地榆 = the drug's own Chinese name embedded in the compound name). These carry the anti-tumour and immunomodulatory pharmacology, which the traditional record only gestures at through *jiě dú* — "resolve toxin."

### Active Components

- **Hydrolysable tannins:** gallotannins and **ellagitannins** (sanguiin-type); the dominant mass fraction of the root and the basis of the astringent/haemostatic action.
- **Gallic acid** and **ellagic acid** (tannin hydrolysis products; antimicrobial, astringent).
- **Catechins**, including **epigallocatechin (EGC)** — the compound now linked to platelet production.
- **Ziyuglycoside I and ziyuglycoside II** — ursane-type triterpene saponins; the principal anticancer/immunomodulatory constituents.
- **Polysaccharides** — glucose/galacturonic-acid/arabinose/galactose-based, including highly branched arabinogalactans, some with triple-helix conformation; associated with the haemostatic, wound-healing, anti-inflammatory and immune-regulatory activities.
- Flavonoids, sterols, organic acids.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Di Yu is Kenetica's **type-specimen astringent-haemostatic**: the drug of *closure*. It is deployed where the system is **losing** — losing blood, losing fluid through a raw surface, losing integrity of the mucosal or cutaneous boundary — and where that loss carries a *hot*, inflammatory character. Its canonical applications are (a) **topical**: burns, scalds, weeping ulcers, bleeding surfaces, haemorrhoids; and (b) **internal, in the classical Chinese indication set**: haemorrhagic dysentery, bloody stool, uterine bleeding of heat type. Its modern extension — support of platelet counts in therapy-induced myelosuppression — is treated as a **clinical-adjacent, not self-administered** application.

It is a *containment* agent, not a builder. It closes; it does not nourish.

### Scientific Correlation

Retrieved from PubMed this run:

- **Huang et al. (2026), *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* 366:121620** — opening explicitly from the classical description of *S. officinalis* as a herb of "cooling and tonifying the blood," this study identified **epigallocatechin (EGC)** as an active monomer that promotes **megakaryocyte differentiation and platelet production** *in vitro*, in zebrafish, and in mouse models of radiation-induced thrombocytopenia, without detectable toxicity; mechanism was **ERα-mediated SRC/PI3K/AKT signalling** with downstream regulation of haematopoietic transcription factors. This is a direct, mechanistic modern correlate of the traditional haemostatic claim. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2026.121620)
- **Qi et al. (2026), *Drug Design, Development and Therapy* 20:591949** — review of **ziyuglycosides** from Di Yu: in vitro inhibition of tumour-cell proliferation, cell-cycle arrest, mitochondrial apoptosis, suppression of invasion/metastasis via Wnt/β-catenin, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, MAPK and EGFR/NF-κB; in vivo tumour-growth inhibition and immune modulation (Th17/Treg balance, CD8⁺ T-cell activation). Notably, the authors record that **Diyu Shengbai Pian**, a Di Yu–derived preparation, is used clinically to alleviate **leukopenia caused by chemotherapy or radiotherapy** — and state plainly that large-scale standardised clinical trials are still lacking. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2147/DDDT.S591949)
- **Meng et al. (2026), *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* 371:122102** — review of *S. officinalis* **polysaccharides**: the authors state directly that the anti-inflammatory, wound-healing, haemostatic and immune-regulatory activities "align precisely" with the traditional efficacy of cooling blood, promoting haemostasis, detoxifying and astringing sores. They also state the honest limitation: existing studies are **predominantly in vitro**, structure–activity relationships lack quantification, and **in vivo efficacy and safety data remain insufficient**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2026.122102)
- **Tang et al. (2025), *ACS Applied Bio Materials* 8(7):6215–6230** — oxidised *S. officinalis* extract was used to modify collagen into a haemostatic composite (OC-1.3), which achieved a blood-clotting index of **5.44 ± 0.34%** and clotting time of **5.56 ± 0.82 s** in vitro, and in rats reduced liver-bleeding time to **30.99 ± 2.47 s** and tail-amputation bleeding to **18.05 ± 0.63 s** with blood loss of **127.03 ± 42.28 mg**, outperforming commercial gauze. A *Sanguisorba*-derived material outperforming standard gauze at stopping bleeding is about as literal a vindication of "blood-absorber" as pharmacognosy offers. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsabm.5c00718)

**Evidence grade — honest:** **Emerging / Preclinical, converging strongly on the traditional indication.** The haemostatic and wound/burn indications have **coherent mechanism** (tannin protein precipitation), **strong animal and biomaterial evidence** (Tang 2025), and a **plausible systemic mechanism** for the platelet claim (Huang 2026). The oncological and immunomodulatory claims are **preclinical only**. The one clinically-used derivative (Diyu Shengbai Pian, for chemo/radio-induced leukopenia) is reported in the review literature but, as those authors themselves state, **lacks large-scale standardised trials**. No randomised controlled human trial of *S. officinalis* for haemorrhage or burns was retrieved this run. Grade: **traditional indication + strong preclinical support; not clinically proven in humans.**

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM (attested, Shennong lineage):** **Taste** bitter (苦), sour/astringent (酸, 澀). **Nature** slightly cold (微寒). **Channels entered** Liver, Large Intestine, Stomach. **Actions** 凉血止血 (cool blood, stop bleeding); 解毒斂瘡 (resolve toxin, astringe sores). **Indications** bloody stool, haemorrhoidal bleeding, bloody dysentery, uterine bleeding of heat type; burns and scalds (external). Charred form (地榆炭) for intensified haemostasis.
- **Galenic / Unani (attested):** *Bārid* (cold) 1°–2°, *yābis* (dry) 2°. *Qābiḍ* (astringent), *ḥābis li'l-dam* (stancher of blood), *mujaffif* (drying), wound-closing. Antagonist of hot, fluxive humour.
- **Greek (Dioscoridean):** στυπτικόν — styptic, contracting; checks fluxes, closes wounds, staunches haemorrhage, binds the bowel.
- **Ayurveda:** not attested for this species in the classical Sanskrit corpus. No rasa/guna/virya/vipaka is assigned here. (The functional analogue class is *rakta-stambhana* / *kashaya rasa* astringents — noted as a structural parallel only, **not** as an attestation.)

### Contraindications

- **Contraindicated in cold-type / deficiency-type bleeding (TCM).** Di Yu is a *cold* drug for *hot* bleeding. In spleen-yang deficiency, cold-pattern diarrhoea, or bleeding from deficiency-cold, the tradition explicitly warns against it — and that warning is doctrinally load-bearing, not decorative.
- **Extensive burns — do not self-treat.** Large-surface, deep, or infected burns require medical management. Applying powders to a major burn risks infection and impedes assessment. The traditional burn indication is for *minor* superficial injury only.
- **High tannin load.** May cause nausea, gastric irritation and constipation. **Impairs iron absorption** — avoid concurrent use with iron supplements and in iron-deficiency anaemia. Caution in chronic use.
- **Drug interactions:** tannins precipitate alkaloids and protein-based drugs; separate dosing from oral medication by ≥2 hours. Theoretical interaction with **anticoagulants/antiplatelets** given the demonstrated pro-platelet and pro-coagulant activity — **do not combine without clinical supervision**, and avoid in patients with thrombotic risk or on warfarin/DOACs.
- **Anticancer/immunomodulatory claims are preclinical.** Di Yu is **not** a cancer treatment and must never be substituted for one. The leukopenia preparation is a clinical product used under supervision, not a self-administered herb.
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** insufficient data. Avoid. [USER: verify.]
- Not for use as a hepatotoxicity-risk-free "safe herb" by default — high-dose chronic tannin exposure is not benign.
