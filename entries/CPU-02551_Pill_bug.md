# Pill bug — Shu Fu 鼠妇 (Shennong bencao jing); aliases Fu Fan 负蟠, Wei 威

## *Armadillidium vulgare (Latreille), the common pill bug or woodlouse, a small terrestrial crustacean that rolls into a ball; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence, since the Sun Xingyan note describes only a small creature living at wall bases and in the bottoms of jars, which fits woodlice in general (Porcellio scaber is a plausible alternative)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The only text read for this entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Dioscorides' *koubarides* (DMM 2-37, "Oniscus asellus — Common Woodlouse", spine entry III_219, tag DMM) is a woodlouse of a different species and tradition; it is not this entry and is not merged with it.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 鼠妇, "mouse's wife", in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 鼠妇 *shu fu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3999). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味酸，温 ("sour, warm"); 主气癃不得小便 ("governs qi-blockage with inability to pass urine"), 女人月闭 ("women's blocked menses"), 血症 ("blood lumps", the character as printed), 痫症 and 寒热 ("convulsive seizures", the characters as printed, "and chills and fever") and 利水道 ("frees the water passages"); aliases 一名负蟠 and 一名威 ("also called fu fan; also called wei"); habitat 生平谷 ("grows in level valleys"). There is no 久服 clause.

The Mingyi bielu layer (line 4001) adds an alias, whose character is missing in the transcription, and: 生魏郡及人家地上。五月五日取, "grows in Weijun and on the ground of people's houses; taken on the fifth day of the fifth month".

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 4003) is philological and describes the animal. It cites the Shuowen (a character that is missing in the transcription, "wei", "wei shu"; "wei shu is shu fu"; "pan is shu fu"), the Erya ("pan, shu fu"), Guo Pu ("an insect at the bottom of pottery jars"; and, for wei and wei shu, "the old saying is that these are other names of shu fu"), the Mao Shi ("the yi wei is in the room", 伊威在室) with its commentary ("wei shu"), and Lu Ji: 在壁根下，瓮底中生，似白鱼, "it is at the base of walls, born at the bottom of jars, like a white fish". These are the commentators' and not the Shennong text's. They place the creature in damp, shaded places in houses and give its shape only by a comparison with a small pale animal.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the animal's body is an arthropod exoskeleton of chitin with calcium salts; woodlice can concentrate metals from the soil and litter they feed on.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a sour, warm small creature for blocked urine, blocked menses, blood lumps, convulsive seizures and chills-and-fever, and for freeing the water passages. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose, and does not recommend using any animal body.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Mingyi layer gives the day of taking: the fifth day of the fifth month (line 4001). The Shennong paragraph names no part, route or preparation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Woodlice are crustaceans, not insects, and live on decaying plant matter in damp places. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The classic's categories (blocked urine, blood lumps, convulsive seizures) are not modern diagnoses, so no modern finding can be set beside them.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味酸，温, "sour, warm" (line 3999). No other quality is printed in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph for 鼠妇 reads 味酸，温 and files the creature in the lower grade (下品). The classic's preface (line 2935) says the lower medicines "are for treating disease, to correspond to earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to clear cold-and-heat evil qi, break accumulations and heal illness, base it on the lower classic" (主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经). The paragraph has no 久服 clause and is a list of urinary, menstrual and febrile complaints. The register is therefore (b): a lower-grade drug whose grade is the tradition's own warning against long use. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 鼠妇 *shu fu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3999)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The creature is a lower-grade drug for urinary and menstrual complaints, and the Codex does not recommend taking animal bodies. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

- **Urinary retention:** an inability to pass urine is an emergency and needs medical care, not this drug.
- **Pregnancy:** the paragraph uses it for blocked menses and "blood lumps"; no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Hygiene:** animals from damp ground and house floors carry bacteria and parasites; do not eat them.
- **Ethics:** the Codex does not recommend harming any animal for these uses.
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given.
