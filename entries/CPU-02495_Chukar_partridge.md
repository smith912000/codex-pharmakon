# Chukar partridge — لحم الحجل (*ḥajal*, TBN) / *qabaj* (قبج, as printed in the Canon body)

## *Partridge: the flesh of the partridge, a game bird; "chukar" (Alectoris chukar) is the spine's species-level label, which neither text supports* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM, TBN

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Two-text entry, with an identity doubt.** Named by Avicenna's *Canon* and by Ibn Qayyim's *al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī* among the texts on disk, and by no others here.

**Avicenna (CM).** Locus: *Canon* Book II, an entry whose heading line is empty in the OpenITI text (line 17761 reads only the bare heading mark in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`); the body runs to line 17767. The body speaks of "the flesh of the *qabaj*" (لحم القبج; line 17765). Description: "known; and the *ṭahyūj* shares it in its qualities" (معروف والطهيوج يشاركه في صفاته; line 17762). Action: "its flesh is the most subtle of the meats" (لحمه ألطف اللحمان). Cosmetic: "its flesh fattens" (لحمه يسمن; line 17763). Breath organs: "its flesh polishes the heart (*fuʾād*)" (لحمه يجلو الفؤاد; line 17765). Digestive: the *qabaj* flesh "benefits dropsy and benefits the stomach" (ينفع لحم القبج من الاستسقاء وينفع المعدة; lines 17765-17766). Excretory: "their flesh is light; they bind (the bowel) and increase coitus" (لحمهما خفيف يعقلان ويزيدان في الباه; lines 17766-17767). No temperament degree and no dose are given. The "both" (*-humā*) in the last sentence shows the entry covers the *qabaj* and the *ṭahyūj* together.

**Ibn Qayyim (TBN).** Locus: *al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī*, entry *laḥm al-ḥajal* (لحم الحجل), Arabic text line 6658 in `Sources/TBN_Prophetic/text/IbnQayyim_TibbNabawi_OpenITI_Shamela0023649.txt`, one line, within the discussion of the flesh of birds: "The flesh of the partridge: it generates good blood and is quick to digest" (لحم الحجل: يولد الدم الجيد، سريع الانهضام). No hadith is cited and none is graded.

**Identity doubt.** Ibn Qayyim's word is *ḥajal*; the Canon's body says *qabaj* and *ṭahyūj*, and its heading is lost in the text on disk. The two names may both be partridges, but that they are the same bird is our equation, not either text's (the added-note row joins them by the Canon's missing heading and Ibn Qayyim's *ḥajal*). The species "chukar" is not supported by either text. Confidence: low to moderate for "partridge", low for the species. [USER: verify the Canon heading in a printed edition]

### Active Components

The texts give no composition. Partridge flesh is lean game-bird meat; nothing more is stated without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only: a game bird eaten as food, called quick to digest and good in making blood by Ibn Qayyim and the most subtle of meats, fattening and binding by Avicenna. Not a recommended practice.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No temperament degree is printed in this entry; "light", "most subtle", binding. **Prophetic medicine (TBN).** No temperament is printed; "generates good blood", quick to digest (*al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī*, line 6658).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The *Canon* entry gives food-medicine uses (fattening, dropsy, stomach, coitus). No cultivation status is attested; the "polishes the heart" line is a humoral remark and is not converted into one.

**Prophetic medicine (TBN).** *(c) Absent.* Ibn Qayyim's line is a dietetic note with no hadith, no grading and no *sunna* reference. It falls under food and drink in the six necessary things as ordinary regimen only.

**Practitioner's note.** A light, easily digested bird in both texts. For a cultivator it is plain food. Nothing supports it as a practice.

### Contraindications

Ordinary food-hygiene cautions for game birds. Wild partridges are hunted under seasons and licences in many places; legal status varies. [USER: verify] The Canon notes it binds the bowel.
