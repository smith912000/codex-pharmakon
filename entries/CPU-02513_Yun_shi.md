# Yun shi (Caesalpinia decapetala) — 云实 *yunshi*, also 员实, 天豆, 云英

## *Caesalpinia decapetala, a thorny climbing leguminous shrub of China; the identification is ours, made from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence at species level. The text read describes the plant only in a few phrases, and does not by itself fix the species* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on one text in one edition, Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). That edition keeps four layers apart, and the Codex does the same.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The name line is 云实 (line 1037). The paragraph of the classic itself, the only layer that is "Shennong", is at line 1039 and reads: 味辛，温。主泄利（旧作痢，《御览》作泄利）肠[a character is missing in the transcription]，杀虫蛊毒，去邪恶结气，止痛、除热，平，主见鬼精物；多食，令人狂走。久服，轻身、通神明。生川谷。 In English: "Flavour acrid, warm. Governs diarrhoea and dysentery, [the gut], kills worms and gu-poison (杀虫蛊毒), removes evil bound qi (去邪恶结气), stops pain, removes heat... governs seeing ghost-spirit things (主见鬼精物). Eaten in quantity it makes a person run mad (多食，令人狂走). Taken long: lightens the body, opens through to the spirit-light (久服，轻身、通神明). Grows in river valleys." Three details of the transcription should be noted. The Chinese text at "肠" has a gap. The clause "平" stands alone after "除热", where a flavour or nature word would not normally fall, and the Codex does not interpret it. And the clause 主见鬼精物 is rendered literally here; "gu-poison" (蛊毒) and the "ghost-spirit" clause are classical categories that the Codex keeps in the text's words and does not turn into modern diagnoses. The classic grades the entry in the upper class (上经; the entry sits under the heading 卷一 上经, line 1047). The Classic's preface (line 43) says that the upper drugs are the "lords", "nourish life to correspond to heaven", are non-toxic, and "taken much or taken long do not harm a person" (多服、久服不伤人), and that whoever wants to lighten the body, benefit qi, and not age and extend years should take from the upper classic (欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经). The plant's own 味 paragraph nonetheless carries a warning, 多食，令人狂走, which is a statement about eating a lot and which sits oddly beside the preface's description of the upper class. The Codex records both and does not reconcile them.

**Wu Pu (《吴普》, third century) layer.** Line 1041: "云实, also called 员实 and 天豆. Shennong: acrid, slightly warm; Huangdi: salty; Leigong: bitter." Wu Pu then describes the plant: "leaf like hemp, in pairs facing each other, four or five *chi* high, the great stalk hollow, flowers in the sixth month, fruit in the eighth and ninth months, gathered in the tenth month" (叶如麻，两两相值，高四、五尺，大茎空中，六月花，八月、九月实，十月采), quoted from the *Yulan*. This is the earliest description printed in this edition, and the earliest record of a disagreement over flavour, since three named authorities give three flavours.

**Mingyi bielu (《名医》, later additions) layer.** Line 1043: other names 员实, 云英, 天豆; "grows in Hejian; gathered in the tenth month and dried in the sun" (生河间。十月采，曝干).

**Sun Xingyan's own note (案).** Line 1045: "The *Guangya* says: 天豆 is 云实." This is all that Sun adds.

### Active Components

None is stated in the text. As a general fact, *Caesalpinia* is a genus of the legume family (Fabaceae), and the genus is known to produce a wide range of secondary metabolites, among them diterpenoids and phenolic compounds. The Codex has retrieved no analytical source for this species, and names no compound. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. In the classic's own words the drug is acrid and warm, is for diarrhoea and dysentery, worms and gu-poison, bound qi, pain and heat, and, when eaten in quantity, drives the person mad. The Codex does not translate these into modern syndromes, gives no protocol, and does not endorse the 久服 promise. A classic that promises "opening through to the spirit-light" for long use and in the same line warns of madness for large quantities is a reason to treat any use of the plant as unsafe unless a competent source settles it. The seeds of a legume that the classic itself says can drive a person to run mad are not a food.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic names no preparation, no part and no quantity. The layers add only the gathering time (the tenth month, Wu Pu and Mingyi bielu). The only quantity word in the text is a warning (多食, "eat much"). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

The identification to *Caesalpinia decapetala* is ours and rests on the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, not on a trial. The Wu Pu description is too brief to prove the species by itself (leaf "like hemp", paired, hollow stalk), and the Codex does not test it against the plant. A search of the other texts on disk found no *Caesalpinia decapetala*. Clark 1995 prints *Caesalpinia sepiaria* Roxb. as the third name of the Tibetan *karany.ja* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, line 917). As the Codex understands it that name is a synonym of *C. decapetala*, but that is not checked against a flora, and the Tibetan drug is a different record (a seed said to increase digestive heat). The two are not equated here. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Divine Farmer's Classic).** 味辛，温, "acrid, warm" (line 1039). Wu Pu records other authorities' flavours (Shennong: acrid, slightly warm; Huangdi: salty; Leigong: bitter; line 1041). No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry is placed in the upper class (上经), the class the preface says is taken to nourish life, without poison, and for a long time. The 味 paragraph itself carries a 久服 clause that promises a life-nourishing status: 久服，轻身、通神明, "taken long: lighten the body, and open through to the spirit-light" (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 云实 *yunshi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1039). "Lighten the body" (轻身) is the classic's own effect category and is not a weight claim. The same paragraph adds 多食，令人狂走, "eaten much, makes a person run mad". Only the classic's paragraph is Shennong. The Wu Pu and Mingyi layers add no cultivation clause.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator should read this as a text and not as a practice. The classic gives an upper-class promise and a madness warning for the same plant, with no part, no preparation and no dose. Nothing here supports taking the plant. The honest use of the entry is as a record of how the Shennong tradition graded it.

### Contraindications

The text itself warns that eating much of it makes a person run mad (多食，令人狂走). The Codex has no source that tells how much that is, and no safety information for pregnancy, children or other medicines was read. The identification is ours: plant material sold under the Chinese name may be a different *Caesalpinia* or another legume. Diarrhoea, dysentery, worms and pain need medical assessment.
