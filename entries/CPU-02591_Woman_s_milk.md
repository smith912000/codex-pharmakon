# Woman's milk — Gala gunaikos (γάλα γυναικός, "woman's milk"), Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 2-78

## *Human breast milk as Dioscorides treats it, in the chapter headed "SUGGESTED: Woman's Milk". Human milk is the material; no species question arises beyond that.* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 5251, chapter 2-78 "GALA GUNAIKOS". The row is separate from the general milk entry (III_285, CPU-00535), whose sources are Assyrian, Avicenna and Ebers, with Ebers human breast milk folded in as a variety; Dioscorides is not among that entry's tags, so this chapter is not already covered there.

**Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter reads: "Woman's milk is the sweetest and most nourishing. Sucked, it is good for pangs of hunger in the stomach and for consumption. It is also an antidote in a drink for someone that has taken sea hare [2-20]. Mixed with frankincense that has been pounded into small pieces, it is dropped into eyes that are bloodshot from a blow. It is good for gout rubbed on with meconium [4-65] and ceratium [wax ointments]. All milk is worthless for the splenetic and hepatic, vertigo, epilepsy, and those troubled in their tendons, those who have fevers or whose heads ache, unless at any time one gives them whey for purgation as was formerly shown. Some say that the milk of a bitch when she first whelps removes hair when rubbed on. Taken as a drink it is an antidote against poisonous medicines, and casts out dead embryos."

The sentences from "All milk is worthless" onward concern milk in general and the milk of a bitch, not only woman's milk; they close a run of dairy chapters that includes whey (2-76, line 5241) and junket (2-77) just before it. The chapter's last sentence, "Taken as a drink it is an antidote against poisonous medicines, and casts out dead embryos", has no stated subject; it may refer to the dog's milk or to milk in general. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** "Sweetest and most nourishing"; sucked, for hunger pangs in the stomach and for consumption; as an antidote in a drink for sea hare; with frankincense dropped in the eyes for a blow; rubbed on for gout with meconium and wax ointments.
- **What the text says against all milk.** Worthless for the splenetic and hepatic, vertigo, epilepsy, tendon trouble, fevers and headache, unless whey is given for purgation.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, volume or frequency.

The Codex gives no protocol. Human milk is a body fluid and the historical uses are recorded only.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Human milk is the natural food of infants. Body fluids can carry infectious agents and drugs taken by the donor, and putting unprocessed fluid in the eye carries a risk of infection. The classical indications (consumption, gout, sea hare poisoning) are not translated into modern equivalents. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify.]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and no cultivation status is attested for woman's milk. His chapter ranks it for sweetness and nourishment and then lists its drug uses; nothing in it is a statement about vital constitution.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. A cultivator has no use for this chapter beyond reading what the author says about milk; the one caution it records, that milk is "worthless" in several conditions, is the one to keep.

### Contraindications

- **Infection.** Human milk from an unscreened donor can transmit infections; do not put it in the eyes or give it to others.
- **Drugs and illness in the donor.** Medicines and infections of the donor pass into milk.
- **Ethics and consent.** This entry does not endorse the use of another person's milk.
- **Conditions the text excludes.** The text names the splenetic, hepatic, vertigo, epilepsy, fever and headache as cases where milk is worthless; these are medical conditions and call for proper care.
