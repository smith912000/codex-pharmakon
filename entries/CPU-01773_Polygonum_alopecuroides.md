# Polygonum alopecuroides — na.ram, Naram (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Polygonum alopecuroides, the first of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for na.ram (before "Polygonum viviparum"); the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints no Polygonum for "Naram" and sets Plantago lanceolata ("Ribwort plantain") against it, so the two editions disagree on the plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi, read in two modern editions, and under this binomial on Clark's edition alone, where the line prints a description and no action.** The editions are Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, both in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. They are two translations of one tradition's text and are not two independent traditions. A search of the Dioscorides, Ebers, Assyrian, Sushruta and Shennong text on disk for *Polygonum* found only other species: Dioscorides prints *Polygonum* names in translators' lines for other chapters (for example *Polygonum hydropiper* at line 6197 and *Polygonum aviculare* at line 8038), and the Shennong text (Yang 1998) has *Bian Xu* (*Herba Polygoni Avicularis*, line 3731) and a note naming *Radix Polygoni Tenuicaulis* (line 3214). None is *Polygonum alopecuroides* and none is read as this drug. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (line 206) the row is keyed by the Tibetan name *na.ram*, with the identification "Polygonum alopecuroides; Polygonum viviparum", the locus "Clark 1995 (line 1124)" and the note "more than one Latin name given; key uses the first". The spine row (CPU-01773) took the **first** of the two binomials. *Polygonum viviparum* has no spine row as far as the files read show.

**Identity doubt.** This is the weakest identification in the batch, and the reasons are in the texts. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance" and (line 68) that "the data given in some of these is occasionally suspect". Here Clark gives two species of one genus. The Men-Tsee-Khang glossary gives a different plant for the same name: its "N" block prints the phonetic name "Naram" (line 5504), the transliteration "na rams" (line 5529) and then "Plantago lanceolata" (line 5531) with "Ribwort plantain" (line 5533), in a block laid out in consecutive units (Naga gesar, Naga pukhpa, Nalaesham, Nampar, Naram, Ngoga against their transliterations, binomials and English names in turn, lines 5501 to 5537). In Clark's own chapter 21 the class of anti-diarrhoeals names "Plantain (Plantago depressa), Plantago lanceolata" (line 1222), and the Men-Tsee-Khang group of anti-diarrhoeals names "Tharam, Naram" together (line 3644). The Codex reads the two lists as parallel in kind from the order of the names; neither text says so. So the glossary's plant and Clark's chapter 21 point to a plantain, while Clark's materia medica line prints a knotweed. The Codex does not choose. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1124).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 ("The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines", line 792), within "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997), which runs to "(8) Medicines of Animal Origin" (line 1147). The entry follows *tha.ram* (*Plantago depressa*, line 1123) and precedes *bya.rkang* (larkspur, line 1125). It reads, in full:

"na.ram: Polygonum alopecuroides: Polygonum viviparum: [has three names, for example ram.bu and skam.rtsi. It grows on shady hills and plains and has an elongated, green leaf, greyish flower similar to a dog's tail, small red seed and a glossy red root.]"

Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries. **The whole of the line after the binomials is inside the brackets and ends with the closing bracket, and no action follows it.** So, as Clark's marks stand, the root-text layer gives this drug only the name and the identifications, and Clark's line prints no action at all, from either layer. Clark's name index lists *na.ram* (line 1710) and his Latin index lists *Polygonum alopecuroides* (line 1629). The brackets give two further names for the drug ("for example ram.bu and skam.rtsi") and say nothing more about them.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (read through the name, not the binomial).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, chapter 20, within the run headed "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (line 3428), line 3493: "Tharam, Naram and Jhakang stops diarrhea." Chapter 21 ("The Group and the Method of Compounding Medicine", contents, line 229), lines 3644 and 3645: "Kapae, Bilwa, Dhatig, Moen chhara, Mak, Tharam, Naram, Gya kyeg, antelope's blood and Jhakang are the group of medicines that cure diarrhea." The index lists "Naram 211, 222" (line 7449). So the only action either edition prints for the drug is in the Men-Tsee-Khang text, and the Codex attaches it to Clark's *na.ram* through the identical-sounding name only. That the "Naram" of these passages is Clark's *na.ram* rests on the spelling. The name is not in the taste groups of chapter 19 and not in the rejuvenation chapter (chapter 23).

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Polygonum alopecuroides*, *na.ram* and *Naram* found no entry on this plant. I_1163 (*Plantago depressa*, FMT) quotes the same Men-Tsee-Khang lines (3493 and 3644) for *Tharam*, and its overlap note names I_676 (*Plantago lanceolata*, tags CM, AT, DMM, EP, SS), the entry of the plant the glossary sets against "Naram", which carries no FMT tag. I_1265 (*Delphinium grandiflorum*), I_1266 (*Delphinium himalayi*) and I_1268 (*Dracocephalum heterophyllum*) print neighbouring lines of Clark's herbal run or of the Men-Tsee-Khang "N" block. The spine has a separate row for the other Clark *Polygonum* line (*Polygonum convolvulus*, *hbra.go*, line 954, CPU-01868, not yet written). Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Polygonum* (with its relatives) belongs to the knotweed family (Polygonaceae), and that members of the family commonly contain tannins and phenolic compounds; this is a statement about the family and not about this plant. Which compounds the Tibetan drug holds, and which of the candidate plants it is, is not shown by the lines read, so no statement about its composition is made.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Clark's line prints no action. The Men-Tsee-Khang text records that "Tharam, Naram and Jhakang stops diarrhea" (line 3493) and lists *Naram* among the medicines that cure diarrhoea (line 3644). That claim is attached to this binomial only through a spelling, and the glossary points to a different plant. It is a record of the texts' claims in their own terms and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose. Diarrhoea can cause dehydration and needs medical care in children, the elderly and when prolonged, and no plant should be used for it on the strength of this entry.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Clark's line 1124 prints no taste, power or post-digestive taste; its only qualities are the colours and shapes of the plant (inside the brackets). The Men-Tsee-Khang lines read (3493, 3644, 5504 to 5533) print none. The only class the drug stands in is the class of anti-diarrhoeals (Men-Tsee-Khang, lines 3644 and 3645), a class of action and not of taste or power.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The materia medica gives this drug a name and a description in Clark, and the Men-Tsee-Khang text classes it by what it does: it stops diarrhoea. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. A search of both Tibetan texts for the drug's names (*na.ram*, *Naram*, *Polygonum alopecuroides*) found it only in the lines cited above, the glossary and the indexes. It is not in the rejuvenation chapter (Men-Tsee-Khang chapter 23, lines 3900 to 3950; Clark's chapter 23 is headed "Normal health", lines 1262 to 1276), and Clark's materia medica names other drugs as essence-extraction ingredients (for example lines 853, 971 and 1144), none of them this one. Its framing is therapeutic only, and it attaches to the Tibetan name and not securely to this binomial.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a diarrhoea drug in one short Men-Tsee-Khang sentence, with no action printed in Clark, an identity the two editions dispute, and no preparation or dose. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. Diarrhoea needs medical care when severe, prolonged, bloody or in the young and old; no preparation or dose is given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Because the plant behind the Tibetan name is disputed between the two editions, a plant could not be matched to the name from this entry in any case. No conservation information was read.
