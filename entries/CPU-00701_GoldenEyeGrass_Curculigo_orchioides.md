# Golden Eye Grass — Xian Mao (仙茅, "immortal grass"), Kali Musli, Black Musli

## *Curculigo orchioides Gaertn.* | Family: Hypoxidaceae (formerly Amaryllidaceae s.l.)

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SBJ, SS

**Energetic Classification:** Hot and dry in the third degree (a strong warming, drying "yang-fire" tonic in the Galenic-Unani reading).

**Training Phase:** Tempering

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Curculigo orchioides* is a small stemless herb of monsoon Asia — India, southern China, Southeast Asia — grown from a slender, dark, aromatic rhizome that is the drug part. The narrow pleated leaves and low golden-yellow star-flowers give it the English name "golden eye-grass"; the black rootstock gives it the Ayurvedic name kali musli ("black musli"). It is one of the more genuinely trans-traditional aphrodisiac/tonic roots of Asia, appearing under near-parallel indications in both Chinese and Indian systems.

**Shennong / TCM (SBJ):** As **Xian Mao** the rhizome is a canonical **kidney-yang tonic** — pungent, hot, and (importantly) classed as slightly toxic in the standard materia medica. It is used to "warm the kidneys and strengthen yang," for impotence, cold-type infertility, weak aching low back and knees, and cold uterus. Its very name — "immortal grass" — signals its status as a warming longevity/vitality drug.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS):** Kali musli is a classical **vajikarana (aphrodisiac/rejuvenative)** and rasayana rhizome, used for male reproductive weakness, seminal debility, and general strengthening; it is warming, unctuous, and vata-pacifying.

**Avicenna (CM):** The plant is not native to the Mediterranean and enters the Unani stream through the Indo-Persian tibb tradition, where "musli" roots (both this black musli and the white musli, *Chlorophytum*) are used as hot, strengthening, muqawwi-e-bah (aphrodisiac) tonics — a transfer into the Galenic hot/dry frame rather than a citation from the original *Qanun*.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Xian Mao is fire stored in a black root — a cold, dark, unremarkable rhizome that pharmacologically behaves as a warming, mobilising stimulant of reproductive and skeletal vitality. In Kenetica it belongs to **Tempering**: the phase of controlled application of heat and stress to strengthen. The plant literally models tempering — it drives yang, bone-formation and drive, but it is "slightly toxic" and must be dosed with restraint, exactly as tempering heat must be applied without scorching.

### Active Components

The signature marker is **curculigoside**, a benzyl-benzoate **phenolic glycoside** that crosses the blood-brain barrier, alongside related curculigosides (J, K and others) and further phenolic/norlignan glycosides such as **orcinol glucoside** and **orcin**; plus cycloartane triterpenoid saponins (curculigosaponins), and a dihydrobenzofuran (curcorchidihydrobenzofuran A) recently characterised. Curculigoside is the accepted chemical marker and the compound behind most of the pharmacology.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Golden eye-grass is the codex's principal **Tempering-phase yang/vitality tonic**: a warming rhizome deployed for reproductive vigour, bone and joint resilience, and as a broadly restorative "drive" tonic. Modern work reframes curculigoside as a multi-target agent — bone-building, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant and neuroprotective — which maps onto the traditional kidney-tonic remit (in TCM the kidney governs bones, marrow and the brain). It is used in short, restrained courses, formula-combined, never as an open-ended solo tonic given its toxicity flag.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed: Zheng et al. (2025), *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* — a comprehensive review positions curculigoside, the chemical marker of the kidney-tonifying herb *Curculigo orchioides*, as a blood-brain-barrier-penetrant, multi-target **neuroprotective** candidate across Alzheimer's, depression, ischaemic stroke and vascular dementia models, while explicitly flagging that its toxicological profile and clinical efficacy still require large-scale study. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2025.121048). You & Xu (2021), *Journal of Orthopaedic Surgery and Research* — curculigoside promoted osteogenic differentiation of stem cells and prevented bone loss in an ovariectomised osteoporosis mouse model via PI3K/Akt signalling, corroborating the traditional "strengthens bones" claim. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-021-02389-3). Liu et al. (2023), *Journal of Orthopaedic Surgery and Research* — network pharmacology plus in-vivo validation identified curculigoside and orcinol glucoside as active anti-inflammatory constituents acting on MMP9/JUN/PTGS2 in rheumatoid arthritis. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13018-023-04352-w). Guo et al. (2022), *Biological & Pharmaceutical Bulletin* — curculigoside suppressed osteosarcoma proliferation and metastasis via JAK/STAT and NF-κB pathways in vitro and in vivo. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1248/bpb.b22-00311).

Evidence grade: **preclinical–moderate.** There is a substantial and growing mechanistic literature (bone, anti-inflammatory, neuroprotective, anti-tumour), consistent with the traditional kidney/bone/vitality remit, but it is overwhelmingly cell-culture and rodent work. Robust human clinical trials — including for the headline aphrodisiac indication — are lacking, and the reviews themselves call for toxicology and clinical validation.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**TCM:** Xian Mao — pungent (xin), hot (re), **slightly toxic**; enters Kidney, Liver, Spleen; warms kidney-yang, strengthens sinew and bone, dispels cold-damp. **Ayurveda:** rasa madhura–tikta; guna guru–snigdha (heavy, unctuous); virya ushna (hot); vipaka madhura; balya/vajikarana/rasayana, vata-pacifying, may aggravate pitta. **Galenic/Unani:** hot and dry, muqawwi-e-bah and muqawwi-e-badan (aphrodisiac and general strengthener).

### Contraindications

**Reported hepatotoxicity:** *Curculigo orchioides* / Xian Mao is documented in the toxicology literature and Chinese materia medica as slightly toxic with **hepatotoxic potential**, and it appears in herb-induced liver injury discussions; avoid high doses, prolonged use, and combination with other hepatotoxic agents, and monitor in anyone with liver disease. **Pregnancy and reproductive caution:** as a strongly warming, yang-driving, reproductive-active rhizome it is **contraindicated in pregnancy** and should be avoided during lactation and in those trying to conceive without professional supervision. Its pitta-aggravating, hot nature makes it unsuitable in yin-deficiency heat, active inflammation with heat signs, or febrile states. Not for children. Use only in short, formula-combined, correctly-processed courses; discontinue if signs of liver stress (fatigue, jaundice, dark urine, right-upper-quadrant discomfort) appear and seek medical care.
