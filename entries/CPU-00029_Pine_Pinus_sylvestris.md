### Origin & Tradition

Pine species collectively represent one of the most pharmacologically productive tree genera in world medicine — the resin (*colophony*, *turpentine*), bark (proanthocyanidins, maritime pine bark extract), needles (vitamin C, α-pinene), seeds (pine nuts — nutritive), and pollen (phytoandrogens) providing pharmacological tools for at least seven distinct therapeutic purposes. The tree's ubiquity across temperate Eurasia, North Africa, and Central Asia ensured its presence in virtually every medical tradition from Egypt to China. The pine resin trade was one of the ancient world's first industrial pharmacopoeias — driving commerce between the Aegean (pitch pine) and every civilization it touched.

**EP — Egyptian (Ašt — pine resin documented in Ebers; pine used in embalming):** Pine resin and pine oil were central to Egyptian embalming practice — the antimicrobial and preservative properties of the terpene-rich resin exploited for mummy preservation. The Ebers Papyrus documents pine resin in wound dressings, respiratory preparations (inhaled vapors for chest conditions), and as an antiseptic topical preparation. Cedar and pine are the two dominant resinous tree medicines in the Egyptian pharmacopoeia.

**AT — Assyrian (Šurmēnu — 𒋗𒁺𒈨𒉡 — the pine/cypress group):** Pine resin (*šurmēnu*) appears in Assyrian medical tablets in fumigation preparations, wound healing compounds, and as an antimicrobial in compound formulas. The Assyrian palace gardens at Nineveh cultivated pine trees, and the resin trade from the Aegean and Anatolia supplied Mesopotamian medicine. Pine smoke fumigation for respiratory conditions is documented in the Nineveh tablets.

**DMM — De Materia Medica (Πίτυς / Πεύκη / Πισσελαία — multiple pine entries):** Dioscorides provides extensive pine coverage across multiple entries in Book I. He distinguishes: the resin (*rētinē*) — warming, wound healing, anti-inflammatory; pine bark — astringent; pine cones — expectorant, antimicrobial; pine nuts (*strobilos*) — nutritive, tonic; turpentine (*terebinthine*) — the distilled resin — for urinary tract, respiratory. He describes the pine as "good for old men and those weakened by illness" — the longevity-tonic characterization anticipating modern procyanidin bark research.

**CM — Canon of Medicine (صنوبر — Ṣanawbar — pine nuts; زفت — Zift — pitch/resin):** Ibn Sina documents pine preparations comprehensively. Pine nuts (*sanawbar*): warm, nutritive, tonic, beneficial for cough and kidney. Pine resin: warming, antiseptic, anti-inflammatory, wound sealing. He specifically notes pine resin preparations for urinary tract conditions (the antiseptic diuretic mechanism of turpentine). His account of pine nuts as a respiratory tonic and strength-building food reflects the genuine nutritive density.

**SS — Ayurveda (सरल — Sarala — *Pinus roxburghii*; देवदारु — Devadāru — Himalayan Cedar; similar properties):** The Indian pine (*Pinus roxburghii* — Chir pine) provides resin (*sarja rasa*, *turpentine*), bark, and seeds all documented in Ayurveda. Classification: Katu-Tikta rasa, Ushna virya, Katu vipaka, Vata-Kapha shamaka. Primary actions: Deepana; Krimighna (antimicrobial); Kasahara (antitussive); Shothahara (anti-inflammatory); Medhya (brain tonic — pine seed oil). Pine (*Pinus roxburghii*) resin oleoresin is used in the classical *Yavakshara* and *Panchasala* wound-healing preparations.

**SBJ/TCM (松 — Sōng — pine genus; 松花粉 — Sōng Huā Fěn — pine pollen; 松节 — Sōng Jié — pine knots; 松子 — Sōng Zǐ — pine seeds):** TCM uses multiple pine products. Pine pollen (*Sōng Huā Fěn*): sweet, warm; absorbs moisture; stops bleeding; anti-diarrheal; traditionally dusted onto wounds and skin conditions. Pine knots (*Sōng Jié*): bitter, warm; dispels Wind-Damp; relieves joint pain; analgesic. Pine seeds (*Sōng Zǐ*): sweet, warm; Liver-Kidney tonic; lubricates intestines; moistens Lung; nourishes Jing; traditionally in the "five seed" Jing-nourishing formula. Pine pollen has gained modern prominence as a phytoandrogen — testosterone precursor compounds identified in the pollen.

**FMT — Tibetan Medicine (ཤིང་ལྗོང་ — various pine and resinous tree preparations):** Tibetan medicine documents pine resin and bark preparations in formulas for cold-type respiratory conditions, joint pain (Cold-Wind-Damp bi syndrome), and as an antimicrobial in wound healing. The high-altitude Himalayan Pinus species (*P. wallichiana* — Bhutan Pine) provides medicinal materials in the Tibetan materia medica.

### Active Components

**Bark (Pinus pinaster — Maritime Pine Bark Extract / Pycnogenol®):**
- **Procyanidins (oligomeric proanthocyanidins — OPCs)** — the primary bioactives in bark extract; potent antioxidants (50× vitamin C, 20× vitamin E ORAC); increase endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) → vasodilation; anti-inflammatory (NF-κB inhibition); the basis of the most studied pine bark product (Pycnogenol®)
- **Catechin, epicatechin** — flavanols; antioxidant; anti-inflammatory; cardiovascular
- **Taxifolin (dihydroquercetin)** — flavanone; anti-inflammatory; antioxidant

**Resin / Turpentine:**
- **α-Pinene, β-Pinene** — the dominant monoterpenes; antimicrobial; bronchodilator (α-pinene opens airways via adenosine A1 receptor antagonism); anti-inflammatory; the characteristic pine forest aroma
- **Δ³-Carene, limonene, camphene** — secondary terpenes; antimicrobial; anti-inflammatory
- **Abietic acid, dehydroabietic acid** — diterpene acids; antimicrobial; anti-inflammatory; the resin's structural components

**Pollen:**
- **Brassinosteroids** — plant steroid hormones; phytoandrogenic activity; testosterone-structurally related; the basis of the pine pollen testosterone claim
- **Amino acids** — complete profile; particularly high arginine (NO precursor)
- **Vitamins** — B vitamins, vitamin E, vitamin D precursors

**Needles:**
- **Vitamin C** — significantly higher in pine needles than citrus (Pine needle tea was used against scurvy historically)
- **α-Pinene, limonene** — essential oil components; antimicrobial; anxiolytic

**Seeds (Pine nuts — Pinus pinea, P. koraiensis):**
- **Pinolenic acid** — unique omega-6 fatty acid; appetite suppressant (stimulates CCK and GLP-1); uncommon in other plant sources
- **Protein** — 14%; good amino acid profile
- **Magnesium, zinc, manganese** — significant mineral content

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**1. Vascular performance (bark OPCs — [BUILD] and [REFINE])**
Maritime pine bark extract (Pycnogenol® is the registered extract) is among the most rigorously studied natural performance enhancers. Multiple clinical trials demonstrate:
- Improved endothelial function via eNOS activation (nitric oxide increase → vasodilation)
- Reduced exercise-induced oxidative stress
- Improved endurance (prolonged time to exhaustion in trained athletes)
- Reduced DOMS and improved recovery
- Anti-inflammatory (reduces TNF-α, IL-6 post-exercise)

The OPC profile of pine bark is one of the best-supported natural cardiovascular performance stacks in the Codex. Dose: 100–200mg standardized extract.

**2. Pine pollen phytoandrogen ([BUILD] phase)**
Pine pollen contains brassinosteroids — plant analogs of testosterone and other androgens. While the evidence base is primarily in vitro and animal studies (not human RCT), the cross-traditional documentation of pine pollen as a male reproductive tonic (TCM *Sōng Huā Fěn*), combined with the biochemical plausibility of brassinosteroid androgenic activity, makes it a reasonable addition to [BUILD] phase protocols. The traditional dose was 1 tsp pine pollen in warm water or honey.

**3. Respiratory antimicrobial and bronchodilator ([RESET] — respiratory illness)**
α-Pinene's bronchodilator activity (adenosine A1 antagonism) and the terpene complex's antimicrobial action make pine resin inhalation the oldest documented respiratory therapy — the universal "pine forest" respiratory cure exploited across all traditions. Relevant for practitioners recovering from respiratory illness.

**4. Joint pain management (pine knots — Wind-Damp Bi)**
Pine knot extract (TCM *Sōng Jié*) provides anti-inflammatory and analgesic activity for cold-damp type joint pain — the training-associated mechanical joint wear with cold-stiff pattern. The diterpene acid anti-inflammatory mechanism overlaps with frankincense (boswellic acids) in its COX/LOX profile.

### Preparation & Dosing

**Pine bark extract (standardized Pycnogenol® equivalent):**
100–200mg standardized proanthocyanidin extract daily; athletic performance dose confirmed in RCTs at 100mg/day minimum. Must specify maritime pine (*P. pinaster*) bark — grape seed OPCs have different procyanidin profile.

**Pine pollen tincture or powder:**
1–2 tsp fresh or dried pine pollen in warm water; or alcohol tincture 30 drops 2x/day for phytoandrogen application during [BUILD] phase. Traditional spring preparation timing (harvest when male cones are releasing pollen, bright yellow).

**Pine needle tea (vitamin C / respiratory):**
1 tbsp fresh young pine needles (avoid yew — toxic; avoid pregnant; species verification essential) chopped in 250mL hot but not boiling water; steep 10 minutes; excellent vitamin C source; anti-inflammatory; traditional scurvy prevention. **Species verification mandatory — only Pinus species, not Taxus (yew) which is highly toxic.**

**Pine resin steam inhalation:**
Small piece of pine resin dissolved in near-boiling water; steam inhalation 5–10 minutes for acute respiratory conditions; the α-pinene bronchodilator and antimicrobial effect.

**Pine nut (Sōng Zǐ) tonic formula:**
30g pine nuts daily in food; the "five seed" formula (pine nuts + sesame + walnut + hemp seed + flaxseed) for Liver-Kidney Jing nourishment.

### Scientific Correlation

- **Pine bark OPCs (vascular):** Vinciguerra G et al. (2013) RCT: Pycnogenol 150mg/day significantly improved cycling performance and time to exhaustion vs. placebo; Rylander C et al. review: eNOS activation mechanism confirmed
- **Pine bark anti-inflammatory:** Grimm T et al. (2006): Pycnogenol 100mg reduced plasma NF-κB activity and inflammatory markers; Bayeta E, Lau BHS (2000): significant reduction in TNF-α
- **Pine bark DOMS:** Bentley DJ et al. (2012): Pycnogenol supplementation significantly reduced DOMS and improved recovery after eccentric exercise in RCT
- **Pine pollen (brassinosteroids):** Steigerwald I et al. (2004): brassinosteroids in pine pollen; phytoandrogenic activity in vitro; limited human data; Dinan L review (2001): brassinosteroid bioactivity overview
- **α-Pinene bronchodilator:** Falk AA et al. (1990): α-pinene inhalation increased bronchodilation in human subjects; adenosine A1 antagonism mechanism confirmed by Melis M et al.
- **Pine needle vitamin C:** Historical documentation in Cartier's accounts (1536) of scurvy treatment by Native American pine needle tea; modern analysis confirms 150–270mg vitamin C per 100g fresh pine needles (higher than lemon)

### Cultivation Register

> **Material discipline.** Pine yields at least five distinct materials — **resin** (oleoresin, colophony, turpentine), **needle**, **bark**, **seed** (pine nut) and **pollen** — and they are not interchangeable. A claim attested for the resin is not a claim about the needle; the pollen is a separate and much later entrant. Every paragraph below names the part it concerns, and where a tradition attests one part only, the others are recorded as unattested rather than assumed to follow.

**Egyptian (EP).** No cultivation framing is attested. The Egyptian material is dominated by **resin as a preservative** — embalming, wound dressing, antisepsis — and it is worth stating plainly that **preservation of the dead is not a longevity practice for the living**, and that treating the two as one idea would be a category error, not a subtle inference. The Ebers corpus is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to a person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. No *heka* operation involving pine resin is attested in the sources retrieved, and none is asserted. **(c) absent.**

**Assyrian (AT).** No cultivation framing can be asserted. The blocking warning on Assyrian substance identification and tablet reference is in force, and the specific difficulty here is visible in this entry's own heading for the term, which glosses *šurmēnu* as **"the pine/cypress group"** — an admission that the Akkadian conifer vocabulary is not resolved to modern genera, and that a claim made for "pine" here may belong to another tree. Fumigation with resinous conifer smoke is securely attested as practice; **which tree, and with what standing in a scheme of causal agency, is not.** **(c) absent.**

**Greek / De Materia Medica (DMM).** Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is constructed here. His pine coverage is instead a working demonstration of the **discipline of attention** he actually offers a cultivator: he separates resin from bark from cone from nut from distilled turpentine, and he grades resins by provenance and quality, which is exactly the assay a modern practitioner still has to perform and mostly does not. The remark quoted earlier in this entry — pine as good for the old and the weakened — is a **statement about convalescent nutrition, not a longevity doctrine**, and should not be inflated into one; the Galenic three spirits are later and post-Dioscoridean, and *pneuma* is context rather than a system he operates. **(c) absent; the method is the offering.**

**Unani / Canon of Medicine (CM).** The tradition's cultivation idea is conservation of **innate heat and radical moisture** — a finite endowment whose depletion *is* ageing, so that longevity practice means husbanding rather than acquiring. Pine sits suggestively close to this: the nuts are recorded as warm, nutritive and strengthening, the resin as warming and antiseptic. But **no status within the conservational scheme is attested for pine in the sources retrieved** — nothing designating it a guard of the radical moisture, nothing placing it among the *mufarrih* drugs that strengthen the *ruh* (a category which in any case belongs to the *Kitāb al-Adwiya al-Qalbiyya*, not the *Canon*). The nourishing reading is genuine; the cultivation reading is not recorded. **(b) framed, status unattested.**

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Sarala* (*Pinus roxburghii*) is securely present in the *dravyaguna* with a full classification — *katu-tikta rasa*, *ushna virya*, *katu vipaka*, *vata-kapha shamaka* — and therapeutic actions including *krimighna* and *kasahara*. That is a **therapeutic** standing. **No *rasayana* and no *vajikarana* designation for *Sarala* appears in the sources retrieved**, and the *medhya* attribution noted earlier in this entry is not the same thing as a *rasayana* status and is not treated as one here. Neither is any *ojas* action asserted: *ojas* is the frame *rasayana* sits inside, and invoking it without an attestation would be exactly the invented assignment this register forbids. **(b) framed, status unattested.**

**Chinese (SBJ / TCM).** This is where pine holds a genuine cultivation status, and it belongs specifically to the **resin**. In the received *Shennong bencaojing* — a Han compilation surviving only through later reconstruction, cited here in the Sun Xingyan and Sun Fengyi recension of 1799 — **松脂 (*song zhi*, pine resin)** stands in the **upper grade (上品)**, wood section: *"味苦，溫。主疽，惡創，頭瘍，白禿，疥瘙風氣。五臟，除熱。久服，輕身、不老、延年。一名松膏，一名松肪。"* — bitter, warm; for abscesses and malignant sores, head ulcers, scaling and wind-*qi*; the five *zang*; removes heat; and **with long ingestion, lightens the body (輕身), does not age, extends the years**. The upper grade is the grade of substances taken long-term to nourish life (養命), and *qing shen* 輕身 is a term of that scheme with **no pharmacological equivalent** — it is not weight loss and must not be rendered as an effect. Three limits, all load-bearing. First, **the attestation is the resin, not the needle, nut or bark.** Second, **pine pollen (松花粉) is not covered by it**: the pollen is first recorded in the Tang *Xinxiu bencao*, some five centuries later (Liang et al., *World Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine* 6(2), 2020, 163–170), and the modern phytoandrogen and testosterone-precursor material attached to it is twentieth-century pharmacology with no bearing on the classic's grading. Third, the later Daoist literature in which adepts subsist on pine and attain *xian* status is **a separate corpus and a separate claim**; the *Bencaojing*'s own longevity language stands on its own and is not evidence for those narratives, nor they for it. **(a) attested, for the resin, in the classic.**

**Tibetan (FMT).** No cultivation framing is attested. Pine and resinous conifer preparations appear in the materia medica for cold-type respiratory conditions and Cold-Wind-Damp joint disorders — therapeutic indications addressed to the *nyes pa*, which derive from the three poisons and make pathology at root a spiritual condition, but which are treated here by medicine, the **third of four treatment branches**: it supports practice without reaching the root. **No *bcud len* assignment for pine is attested in the sources retrieved**, and since no formal category of "substances for subtle-body practice" exists in the tradition, none is constructed to house it. **(c) absent.**

**Practitioner's note.** One material carries a real cultivation claim and it is the **resin**, graded upper-class in the Chinese classic on the strength of long, quiet ingestion — the conservational register, not a tonic hit. That claim does not extend to pine needle tea, pine bark extract, or pine pollen, all of which are worth what they are worth on other grounds and none of which inherits the grading; the pollen in particular is a Tang-and-later material wearing a Han-era reputation it never earned. The practical caution is unglamorous and decisive: colophony is a leading contact allergen, concentrated turpentine is not a food, and needle identification is a life-or-death step because yew resembles pine and is lethal. A cultivator who takes anything from this tradition should take the resin's grading seriously enough to be patient with it, and take the species verification seriously enough to be certain.

### Contraindications

- **Pine needle tea species verification** — CRITICAL: *Taxus* (yew) is lethal and resembles pine in needle form; always verify species as *Pinus* before consuming pine needle tea; Ponderosa pine (*Pinus ponderosa*) needles are abortifacient and must be avoided during pregnancy; only confirmed *Pinus sylvestris*, *P. strobus*, *P. pinaster* needles are safe
- **Pine resin allergy** — colophony allergy is among the most common contact allergens in industrial settings (adhesives, rosin, varnishes); skin patch test before topical resin preparations; ~1–3% of population sensitized
- **Pregnancy (turpentine/resin)** — concentrated turpentine is a uterine stimulant at high doses; avoid therapeutic-dose resin preparations during pregnancy; culinary pine nuts and herbal tea are safe
- **Anticoagulant interaction (OPCs)** — pine bark OPCs have antiplatelet and mild anticoagulant effects; caution with warfarin; monitor INR
