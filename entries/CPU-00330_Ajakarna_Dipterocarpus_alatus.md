### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda (Suśruta):** Ajakarṇa/Aśvakarṇa ("goat's-ear / horse's-ear," from the shape of its large winged leaves and fruit-wings) is one of the sāra-wood trees named in Suśruta's **Sālasārādi gaṇa** — the cluster of hard-heartwood, resin-bearing trees prescribed together against kuṣṭha (skin disease), medas/obesity, prameha (urinary-diabetic disorders), kṛmi and kapha disorders. The heartwood contributes the gaṇa's lekhana (scraping) and kaphamedohara character. Its most distinctive product, however, is the abundant **oleoresin (gurjun balsam / garjan-tel)** tapped from the trunk — a clear-to-amber balsam used as a wound-dressing, a topical for skin disease and (in regional practice) a genito-urinary and mucous-membrane remedy analogous to copaiba. Nomenclature is genuinely tangled in the classical literature: *Aśvakarṇa* and *Sarja* are applied variously to *Shorea robusta*, *Vateria indica* and *Dipterocarpus* species, all Dipterocarps — so Ajakarṇa is best read as the Dipterocarp-resin position in the Sālasārādi gaṇa, botanically fixed here to *D. alatus*.

**Southeast Asian ethnomedicine:** Across Thailand, Cambodia, Laos and Vietnam *D. alatus* (Yang-na / Dầu) is a keystone tapped tree; its oleoresin is traditionally a wound and skin medicine, a torch/lamp oil, a wood preservative and a caulking balsam, and is now studied as a biodiesel feedstock — the route by which much of the modern chemistry literature reaches it.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Winged Wood and the Weeping Balsam:** Ajakarṇa doubles the Sal-symbolism of durable heartwood, but adds the image of the **winged seed** (the samara that spins to earth) and the **weeping trunk** (the tapped balsam). Where Sal is the still core and the rising smoke, Ajakarṇa is the tree that *gives its own sap as medicine* — a self-yielding, closing balsam. Doctrinally it belongs to the same Sālasārādi scraping-and-restoring class: pare back kapha-meda excess, then seal and heal the surface with the resin. In Kenetica it reads as a `[RESET]` repair-and-seal agent, the balsam counterpart to Sal's dammar.

### Active Components

The oleoresin is a **sesquiterpene–triterpene** system: the marker triterpenoid **dipterocarpol** (dammarenolic-type dammarane) plus sesquiterpenes (caryophyllene-type) giving the balsam its aroma and long-chain hydrocarbons in the crude resin. The heartwood carries tannins and triterpenoids conferring the astringent, drying action.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Ajakarṇa sits in `[RESET]` as a **wound-sealing and tissue-restoration** agent — the gurjun-balsam function of dressing and closing wounds and skin lesions, and (via the Sālasārādi heartwood) clearing kapha-meda excess. In the Kenetica frame it is a topical/external restorative resin rather than a systemic tonic, paired conceptually with Salasara as the two Dipterocarp resins of the recovery phase.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the modern evidence is **preclinical (in-vitro)** and centres on the oleoresin's triterpenoid chemistry and cytotoxicity.

- Puthongking et al. (2022), *Molecules*, identified **dipterocarpol** as the marker triterpene of *D. alatus* oleoresin and its resinous byproducts, and showed the oleoresin (and byproducts) exerted cytotoxicity against Jurkat, HeLa and HepG2 cancer cell lines — with selectivity for Jurkat over non-cancer Vero cells — and induced apoptosis, suggesting synergy among the resin's constituents. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27103187)
- Pocasap et al. (2024), *Molecules*, formulated the oleoresin into stable oil-in-water Pickering emulsions (confirming dipterocarpol as biomarker) and demonstrated antiproliferative, apoptosis-inducing activity in colon (HCT116) and liver (HepG2) cancer cells, addressing the resin's poor water solubility for pharmaceutical development. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules29112695)

**Evidence grade:** preclinical / traditional. The cytotoxic/anti-proliferative and formulation work is congruent with a bioactive wound-and-skin resin but is not human-validated; classical vraṇaropana use remains traditional in grade.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṣāya (astringent), Tikta (bitter)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu–Rūkṣa (heartwood); Snigdha–Guru (oleoresin)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating) — oleoresin
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** Vraṇaropana balsam; Sālasārādi gaṇa member (kaphamedohara heartwood)
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha śāmaka; heartwood kapha-medohara
- **Karma (actions):** Vraṇaropana-śodhana (wound cleansing/healing), kuṣṭhaghna (anti-dermatosis), grāhī/stambhana (astringent), medohara/lekhana (heartwood), regional mūtra/mucous-membrane use of the balsam

### Contraindications

Balsam dressings: patch-test; discontinue on irritation. Internal (copaiba-style) use of the oleoresin only under qualified supervision — can irritate the GI and urinary tract in quantity. Pregnancy/lactation: internal use best avoided without guidance. Botanical-identity caution: "Aśvakarṇa/Sarja" is applied to several Dipterocarps — confirm the species before sourcing.
