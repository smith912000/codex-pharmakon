# Euphorbia lathyris — Lathuros (Dioscorides 4-167)

## *Euphorbia lathyris [Linnaeus], the third form on the translators' SUGGESTED line ("Lathyris [Fuchs], Lathyris major [Bauhin], Euphorbia lathyris [Linnaeus], Euphorbia lathyrus, Euphorbia spongiosa — Wild Caper, Myrtle Spurge, Caper Spurge")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant under this name. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) printed under the chapter heading, not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED line gives pre-Linnaean forms credited to Fuchs and Bauhin, the Linnaean *Euphorbia lathyris*, a variant spelling and a synonym, and three English names. The row takes the Linnaean binomial. The plate caption printed under the heading, "Euphorbia lathyris after FAGUET — 1878", is an illustration credit and not part of Dioscorides' text. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-167. LATHUROS* at line 9565, caption at 9567, SUGGESTED lines at 9569 and 9570, text at line 9571. The chapter stands in Book Four, "Other Herbs & Roots", after 4-166 *PITUOUSA* (pityusa, one of the spurge group) and before 4-168 *PEPLOS*. The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 745.

The whole chapter reads: "Lathyris sends out an empty stalk the height of a foot, the thickness of a finger, and on the top of it are wings. The leaves on the stalk are somewhat long like those of the almond tree, but broader and smoother. Those on the tops of the little branches are smaller, like those of aristolochia [3-4, 3-5, 3-6] or of cissus [2-210]. It has seed on the tops of the branches in three heads, round like capparis [2-204], in which are three little seeds separated by loose skins from one another, round, bigger than ervum [2-129, 2-131]. Peeled, they are white and sweet to the taste. The root is thin and white, of no use. The whole shrub is full of juice like tithymal. As many as seven or eight of the seeds are able to purge the bowels taken in a pill or eaten and swallowed down with dry figs or dates, and cold water sipped with it. It expels phlegm, bile, and water. The juice (taken like tithymal) does the same. The leaves are boiled together with a hen or vegetables for the same effect. Some also call this tithymal and regard it among the tithymals."

So the chapter has a description (a hollow stalk of about a foot with wings, almond-like leaves, seeds in three-lobed heads, a thin white useless root, a juicy shrub), one action (it purges the bowels, expelling phlegm, bile and water) in three forms (the seeds, the juice, the boiled leaves), and a classification remark: some count it among the tithymals.

*The comparisons it uses.* The leaf comparison to *aristolochia* points to the three aristolochia chapters (3-4, 3-5, 3-6, from line 6548); the seed comparison to *capparis* points to 2-204 (line 6342); *cissus* is 2-210 (line 6426). These are used only to describe this plant's leaves and seeds. *Tithymal* is the group at 4-165 (line 9515), where the same actions (expelling phlegm and bile, purging through the belly) are given for the juice. The plant is not among the seven kinds listed there, and the group's own list is not read into this chapter.

*A name collision.* The name *lathyris* is also printed on the SUGGESTED line of the *ervum* chapter (line 5633: "Lathyris sativus [Bauhin, Linnaeus] ... Indian Pea, Riga Pea, Dogtooth Pea") and in the SUGGESTED line of another chapter, whose binomial is *Lathyrus tuberosus* (line 9636). Those are other chapters and other plants, and are not read into this entry. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

No analysis of the plant Dioscorides describes was read. It is well established that members of the genus *Euphorbia* have a milky latex that is irritant, and that the seeds of *Euphorbia lathyris* in particular have a long record as a purge. Nothing further is stated here about the composition of this plant. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The one use Dioscorides records is as a violent purge of the bowels. The Codex gives no protocol. The text itself puts the effective amount at seven or eight seeds, which shows how strong the drug is, and the seeds are described as "sweet to the taste" when peeled. A sweet-tasting seed that purges strongly is a hazard, and nothing here supports taking it.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives these preparations. "As many as seven or eight of the seeds" are "taken in a pill or eaten and swallowed down with dry figs or dates, and cold water sipped with it". The "juice" is "taken like tithymal". The leaves "are boiled together with a hen or vegetables". The seed count is the only quantity printed. The juice and leaf doses and any frequency or duration are not given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved for the juice or leaves. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints one taste, that the peeled seeds are "white and sweet to the taste". It prints the action that the seeds purge the bowels and "expel phlegm, bile, and water". No hot, cold, moist or dry quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a description for recognition, built on comparison with plants he has already described (almond, aristolochia, cissus, capparis, ervum), with the part to use (the seed, peeled) and the part to leave (the root, "of no use"), and a count of seeds instead of a vague measure. That is the kind of attention to the plant's parts and to measure that the spec names, and it is not a cultivation status. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. A cultivator who reads it can take away the habit of counting what is given instead of guessing, and the lesson that a pleasant-tasting seed can still be a drastic drug. The text offers nothing more, and the abstentive answer is the right one.

### Contraindications

The chapter prints no warning, but its own statement that seven or eight seeds purge the bowels shows the drug is drastic. Spurge seeds and latex are irritant and can cause severe vomiting, diarrhoea and dehydration, so no part of this plant should be taken. Spurge latex also irritates skin and eyes. The identification is only the translators' suggestion, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No safety information for pregnancy was read. None of the traditional uses above is advice. [USER: verify]
