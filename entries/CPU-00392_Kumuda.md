# Kumuda — Kumuda (Sushruta, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *Nymphaea Esculenta* (as printed against "Kumuda" in Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 glossary; the spine row carries *Nymphaea pubescens* Willd.) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** The spine names this water-lily *Nymphaea pubescens*. The Latin printed against the Sanskrit word in the text on disk is *Nymphaea esculenta*. Neither source read says that the two binomials are one plant, so the link is partly an inference. [USER: verify] Sushruta lists *Kumuda* as one water-lily among several that have their own names (*Padma*, *Utpala*, *Nalina*, *Kuvalaya*, *Pundarika*, *Saugandhika*), and the Appendix 3 glossary gives them different Latin names, for example "Nymphoea Lotus." against *Prapaundarika* (line 32584) and "Nymphea Stellata." counted by position against *Nilotpala* (line 32448). The finished entries on other water-lilies (`I_425_Prapaundarika_Nymphaea_lotus.md`, `I_479_Utpala_Nymphaea_stellata.md`, `I_462_WhiteWaterLily_Nymphaea_alba.md`, `I_494_BlueLotus_Nymphaea_caerulea.md`, `I_152_Lotus_Nelumbo_nucifera.md`) do not cover this one. `I_425` names Kumuda only in its heading and its closing cross-reference. A different word, *Kumudaghni*, stands in a list of milky exudations at Kalpa ch.2 (`SS_Kalpa_ch02.txt`, line 46), and is not treated here.

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

*The gana (Sutra ch.38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`).* Lines 375 to 378: "The Utpala'di Group:— The drugs known as Utpala, Raktotpala, Kumuda Saugondhika, Kuvalaya, Pundarika and Madhuka constitute the group known as the Utpaladi Gana." The text prints no comma between Kumuda and Saugondhika, so whether they are two drugs or one is not clear. [USER: verify] The metrical text (lines 380 to 386) says: "This group is possessed of the therapeutic virtue of allaying thirst and corrects the deranged Pittam and the vitiated blood. It assuages the burning sensation of the body and proves curative in cases of vomiting, in Hridroga (Angina pectoris) in syncope, in haemoptysis and in cases of poisoning as well." Membership of the group is a grouping by therapeutic action, grade (b) at most.

*Appendix 3 glossary (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`).* "Kumuda" is printed as "Eumuda" (OCR, line 32077) on the page of K-names. The names on that page, counted down by position from "Krita-vedhana", are sixteen, and the page's Latin block starts at line 32094 with "See Koshataki." Counted down in step, "Kumari" pairs with "Aloe Barbadensis." (line 32104), "Kunkuma" with "Crocus Sativus. Saffron." (line 32107), "Kumuda" with "Nymphaea Esculenta." (line 32109) and "Kunda" with "Jasminum Multiflorum," (line 32111). The count was checked from both ends of the block and agrees. The pairing is by OCR column position, not by a printed link.

*Passages read, in the order of the book.*

- **Sutra ch.13** (`SS_Sutra_ch13.txt`, lines 124 to 134), on leeches: the origin of the non-venomous leeches "is ascribed to such decomposed vegetable matter, as the petrified stems of the several aquatic plants known as Padma, Utpalam, Nalina, Kumuda, Pundarika, and the common zoophytes which live in clear waters." Here Kumuda is a plant of the water and nothing is said of its use.
- **Sutra ch.39** (`SS_Sutra_ch39.txt`, lines 102 to 109), the *Pitta-Samshamana-Varga*: "... Chandana, Kuchandana, Hrivera, Ushira, Manjishtha, Payasya, Vidari, Shatavari, Gundra, Shaivala, Kahlara, Kumuda, Utpala, Kadali, Kandali, Durva, Murva, etc. and the drugs forming the groups of Kakolyadi, Sarivadi, Anjanadi, Utpaladi, Nyagrodhadi, and Trina-Panchamula groups generally prove soothing to the deranged Pittam."
- **Sutra ch.46** (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`), on flowers and bulbs: at lines 1799 to 1803, "The Padma has a bitter and sweet taste, is cooling in its potency, and subdues the deranged Pittam and Kapham. The Kumuda has a sweet taste, and is slimy, demulcent, pleasing and cooling in its potency. The two varieties of the same species known as the Kuvalayam and the Utpalam, slightly differ from the preceding varieties in their properties." At lines 1996 to 1999, "The bulbs of such aquatic plants as the Kumuda, Utpala and Padma have an astringent taste and are sweet in digestion. They are cooling in their potency and tend to enrage the Vayu and ..." (the sentence runs on past the lines read).
- **Chikitsa ch.17** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt`, lines 80 to 90), after a plaster for Pittaja erysipelas: the *Gauryadi Ghrita*, "A Prastha measure of clarified butter should be cooked with the Kalka of Gauri, Yashti-madhu, Aravinda, Rodhra, Ambu, Rajadana, Gairika, Rishabhaka, Padmaka, Sariva, Kakoli, Meda, Kumuda, Utpala, Chandana, Madhu-Sharkara, Draksha, Sthira, Prishni-parni, and Shatahva taken in equal parts (and weighing one seer in all)". The measure is for the whole compound.
- **Kalpa ch.7** (`SS_Kalpa_ch07.txt`, lines 100 to 124 and 125), the *Maha-sugandhi Agada*, an antivenom compound of "eighty-five ingredients": "the five kinds of officinal salts, Kumuda, Utpala, Padma, flower of Arka, flowers and fruits of Champaka ..." are among the drugs that "should be carefully collected and pasted with honey, clarified butter and the bile of a cow and should be kept inside a horn". Kumuda is one ingredient among many, and no separate role is stated for it.
- **Uttara ch.18** (`SS_Uttara_ch18.txt`, lines 241 to 244), in a cooking method: a ball of meat and drugs is "well covered with the leaves of Kadali, Kasmari, Eranda, Kumuda or of Padma plant" and coated with clay before scorching.
- **Nidana ch.13** (`SS_Nidana_ch13.txt`, lines 83 to 85) uses the bulb as a shape comparison: small swellings "Kumuda bulb in shape, are called Panasikas."
- **Sharira ch.3** (`SS_Sharira_ch03.txt`, lines 364 to 369), in the signs of the sex of a child: a woman "who dreams of having received lotus flowers (red and white), Utpala, Kumuda, Amrataka, or flowers of such masculine denomination in her sleep ... may be expected to give birth to a male child". This is a sign and not a treatment, and the Codex does not endorse it.

No rasayana or vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) was found to name Kumuda, so no grade (a) status is claimed.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Sushruta records Kumuda as a cooling, sweet and slimy flower, a cooling bulb, a member of two groups that soothe Pitta, a flower in a medicated ghee and in an antivenom compound, and a leaf that wraps food. These are the text's own claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol for this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted above: a medicated ghee (*Gauryadi Ghrita*, Chikitsa ch.17) and a flower pasted into the *Maha-sugandhi Agada* with honey, ghee and cow's bile (Kalpa ch.7). Chikitsa ch.17 gives a measure for the compound as a whole ("one seer in all"), not for Kumuda alone. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** Sutra ch.46 (lines 1799 to 1803) prints "a sweet taste", "slimy, demulcent, pleasing and cooling in its potency" for the Kumuda flower, and (lines 1996 to 1999) "an astringent taste", "sweet in digestion" and "cooling in their potency", with a tendency "to enrage the Vayu", for the bulbs of Kumuda, Utpala and Padma. The sweet taste, the cooling potency and the sweet digestion are the nearest the text comes to *rasa*, *virya* and *vipaka*. Sutra ch.38 (the Utpaladi group) and ch.39 (the Pitta-soothing list) place it among drugs that quiet Pitta.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Kumuda is a named water-lily in the Utpaladi group and in the Pitta-soothing list, a sweet, cooling flower and bulb in the food and flower chapters, and a flower in a ghee and an antivenom compound. The Sushruta frame for it is therapeutic. The ch.38 gana is a grouping by action, not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. Nothing read ties Kumuda to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*, and none of the rasayana or vajikarana chapters searched (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) names it. The status is unattested, and the identification with *Nymphaea pubescens* rests on an OCR-paired appendix that prints a different binomial. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this water-lily. In Sushruta it is a cooling flower and bulb and an ingredient in compounds made by a physician. The abstentive answer is the correct one: any use is a matter for a qualified practitioner, and the text supplies no long-term regimen.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Sushruta itself says the bulbs "tend to enrage the Vayu" (Sutra ch.46). Water-lilies grow in open water, and wild material may carry water-borne contamination, so wild flowers or bulbs should not be eaten or applied. Some water-lilies are protected plants in some places, so check local rules before collecting. Do not use in pregnancy. Seek a qualified practitioner, and ordinary medical care for fever, bleeding, poisoning or chest pain, all of which the traditional group verse names.
