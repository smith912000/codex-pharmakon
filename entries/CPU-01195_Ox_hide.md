# Ox hide — as printed in Clark's translation of the rGyud-bzhi ("Rhino hide and Ox hide")
## *The hide of the ox, cattle skin; named in the Tibetan materia medica of animal origin* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan Tradition (FMT).** The text is Barry Clark's 1995 translation of the rGyud-bzhi, file `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`. The locus is the Explanatory Tantra, chapter 20, "The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines" (heading at line 792), section "(8) Medicines of Animal Origin" (line 1147), sub-group "Skin" (line 1183). The group reads: "Snake skin: cures leucoderma, glang.shu (a skin disease) [and is beneficial against bhutaspirits]. Rhino hide and Ox hide: cure smallpox. Rat and Mouse skin: drains all types of pus accumulations." (lines 1184-1185). The ox hide is thus named together with the rhinoceros hide in one sentence, and the sentence assigns a single action to both. No Tibetan name is printed for the ox hide in this line, and no species or binomial is given beyond "Ox". The project list gave no `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` row that I found for this item; the only locus is line 1185 of the Clark file. [USER: verify]

The brackets in the printed text mark added matter: Clark says (line 80) that he "interpolated liberally from the Blue Aquamarine commentary". The ox-hide sentence carries no bracket, so it is not marked as interpolated. That reading of the convention is an inference. [USER: verify]

The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 edition (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, line 3554) has the parallel sentence: "The hide of the rhinoceros and the ox cure small pox." Both translations agree on the indication. Clark's meat classification (line 672) lists "bull" in section "(g) Domestic animals"; the Men-Tsee-Khang list (line 2917) has "cow" among the domesticated animals. Which animal "ox" renders in the hide line is not stated. [USER: verify]

Beef as food is described separately: "Beef is cool and oily and dispels wind fever" (Clark line 674). That sentence is about the meat and is not carried over to the hide.

### Active Components

Hide is mostly collagen, with some elastin, keratin in the epidermis, and lipids; tanning chemically cross-links it. Untreated hide from a slaughtered animal may carry bacterial spores. The text names no active compound.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The Tibetan text says ox hide, with rhinoceros hide, "cure smallpox". Smallpox was declared eradicated worldwide in 1980, so there is no case to treat, and the Codex gives no protocol. The line is a record of how the text described a disease that no longer circulates. Using hide from a slaughtered animal for any infectious disease would be unsafe in itself (see Contraindications).

### Preparation & Dosing

The line gives no preparation (ash, boiled, worn, applied, taken) and no dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General facts: smallpox is caused by the variola virus and has been eradicated through vaccination; collagen from hide, as gelatin, is a protein food and not an antiviral.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Tibetan: the line prints an action only ("cure smallpox"), with no taste, power or post-digestive taste. None is given for the hide in the sources retrieved. No other tradition is tagged.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan (FMT).** *(c) Absent.* The ox hide appears once in the animal-origin materia medica, in the "Skin" group (line 1185), with a therapeutic action against one disease and no more. No cultivation status, no essence-extraction (*bcud len*) role and no rejuvenation role is printed for it in the lines read. The group's first item carries a spirit-related note (snake skin against *bhuta* spirits, line 1184); the ox hide has none.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. A cultivator does not use ox hide as a remedy. The text records it against smallpox, a disease that no longer circulates, and the Codex keeps the record without carrying it into practice.

### Contraindications

Raw or poorly treated hide can carry anthrax spores and other pathogens, and handling hide from an animal that died of disease is hazardous. Do not use any animal product to treat a suspected infectious disease; seek medical care. Cattle products have religious and cultural restrictions in some communities; respect them. This entry gives no medical advice.
