# Colostrum — Piyusha (Sushruta Samhita)

## *The first milk of a cow after calving. The translator's footnote defines Piyusha as the milk of a cow recently delivered of a calf, up to the seventh day after the birth; the identification as cow's colostrum is the translator's, the animal is ours (Bos taurus) and is of medium to high confidence; the Sanskrit is not printed in the English, and the OCR of the footnote reads "I'iyusha" (damaged)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta Samhita only, among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The English text is Bhishagratna's translation, Sutra ch.45, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`. The word occurs twice. In the body (line 729) the text says of two dairy products that "Morathat and Piyusha? are sweet to the taste and restorative and aphrodisiac in their properties" (the OCR has turned the footnote marks into "t" and "?"). The translator's footnote on that line (line 750) reads: "The milk of a cow recently delivered of a calf is called I'iyusha till the seventh day after its birth, while, it is subsequently called JNIoratha till it is perfectly purified and becomes fit for the use of man." The two names are therefore two stages of the milk of a cow after calving, and the translator treats the second as milk that is not yet fit for use.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The sentence sits in the part of ch.45 that lists the products of milk (curd, whey, inspissated milk, the sediment called *Manda*, *Kilata*, and so on) and ends with fresh butter (*Navanita*). *Kilata* is glossed by the translator in the same footnote as boiled milk curdled and then heated and made into a paste (line 748). In the text before Piyusha, *Kilata* is "heavy, hypnotic, spermatopoietic and subdues Vayu" (line 727). Piyusha and Moratha are then described together in one clause: sweet in taste, "restorative and aphrodisiac" (lines 729-730). The text gives no separate property for Piyusha alone. The word "colostrum" is ours and does not appear in the source.

### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms, the first milk after calving is known to be richer in protein, especially immunoglobulins, than later milk, and to be lower in lactose and fat. These are well-established general facts, not read in the source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Nothing specific. It names Piyusha with Moratha as sweet and "restorative and aphrodisiac".
- **What the text does not say.** No indication, no dose, no preparation. The footnote's remark that the milk is not fit for use until it is "perfectly purified" is the translator's, and is the only caution.

The Codex gives no protocol for this material.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation and no dose for Piyusha. The only definition is the footnote at line 750 (the milk of the first seven days after calving). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Colostrum from cows is a common modern food and feed product; its immunoglobulin content is the best-known fact about it. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** Taste: "sweet"; properties: "restorative and aphrodisiac" (lines 729-730), stated for Piyusha and Moratha together. No *virya* or *vipaka* is given for it.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Piyusha is mentioned in ch.45 (the dietetic chapter) as "restorative and aphrodisiac", terms that sit near the vocabulary of the formal Sushrutan disciplines of *rasayana* and *vajikarana*. The sentence is a property line in a list of milk products and does not place the substance in a rasayana or vajikarana chapter, and the sources retrieved give no *ojas* claim for it. The group statement on milk (lines 453-461) names the milk of the animals listed as rejuvenating; this entry does not extend that to a substance it does not name. The status is framed, not attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A neonatal cow's milk named by the translator as the first stage of the cow's lactation, recorded in one clause. A cultivator has nothing in this text to act on, and the modern product is a food supplement for which the Codex retrieved no evidence.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene.** Raw milk of any animal can carry bacteria that cause infection; the translator's footnote itself says the early milk is not yet fit for use.
- **Allergy.** Cow's milk protein allergy is a common general fact.
- **Ethics and supply.** Taking early milk deprives the calf of it. The Codex records this plainly and does not weigh it further.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The word is the translator's gloss of a Sanskrit term not printed in the English; the match to modern colostrum is ours.
