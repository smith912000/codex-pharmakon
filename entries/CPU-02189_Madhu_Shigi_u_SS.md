# Madhu-Shigru — printed "Madhu-Shigi-u" in the Varuna'di group list (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907); "Madhu-Shigru" in ch.46

## *A pungent-bitter potherb or spice plant that the Sushruta text sets beside Shigru as a "variety" of it; the translation gives no Latin name for it. The identification is ours and of low confidence: Shigru is glossed Moringa pterygosperma in the translator's index (the species now called Moringa oleifera), so Madhu-Shigru ("honey-Shigru") is probably another kind of drumstick tree, but nothing read says which* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, in three chapters.** This is a single-text entry. The spine row carries the name as the extraction printed it from the group list, "Madhu-Shigi-u"; the OCR of Bhishagratna's page reads *Madhu-Shigi-u* in chapter 38 (probably a misprint or OCR slip for *Shigru*) and *Madhu-Shigru* or "Madhii- Shigru" in chapter 46. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it. The name is a Sanskrit compound and is not expected in the other texts on disk; no search of them was made. **Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.38, the Varuna'di group.** The chapter classifies drugs "usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 9). The Varuna'di group is printed at lines 44 to 50 (Vol. I lines 15385 to 15391): "The drugs known as Varuna, Artagala, Shigru, Madhu-Shigi-u, Tarkari, Mesha-Shringi, Putika, Xaktamala, Morata, Agnimantha, the two kinds of Sairiyaka, Vimvi, Vasuka, Vasira, Chitraka, Shatavari, Mlva, Ajashringi, Darbha, and the two kinds of Vrihati form the group known as the Varunadi." The metrical text (lines 52 to 55) says the group "is possessed of the efficacy of reducing the deranged Kapham and fat and proves efficacious in cases of cephalaegia, Gulma and internal abscesses." Shigru and Madhu-Shigru stand as two separate members of the same list, which is the reason the Codex keeps them as separate rows.

**Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.46, foods and drugs by property.** Four further passages name it.

- *Among the spices and herbs* (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 1422): "Pippali, Maricha, Shringavera, Ardraka, Hingu, Jiraka, Kustumvuru, Jamviraka, Sumukha, Surasa, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Kasamaraddka, Kalamala, Kutheraka, Kshavaka, Ksharapushpa, Shigru, Madhu-Shigru, Phanijhyaka, Sarshapa, Rajika ..." The metrical text that follows (lines 1427 to 1431) speaks of the potherbs as a whole: they "have a pungent taste, are relishing, and heat-making in their potency, and subdue the Vayu and Kapham. They are variously used for seasoning food." Nothing in it is specific to Madhu-Shigru.
- *In the potherb passage* (lines 1506 to 1509): "The Shigru has a pungent, alkaline, sweet and bitter taste and generates the Pittam, while the Madhii-Shigru variety is laxative, has a bitter and pungent taste; is appetising and removes oedematous swelling of the body." This is the one place the text separates the two and calls Madhu-Shigru a "variety".
- *In the flower group* (line 1780): "The flowers of the Madhu-shigru and Karira are pungent in digestion. They destroy the Vayu and increase the discharge of stool and urine" (lines 1779 to 1788; the sentence runs across a page break).
- *In the shoot passage* (line 1827): "The (tender stem) of the Madhu-Shigru is pungent in taste and subdues the deranged Kapham."

The parts named are therefore the herb (leaf or shoot, in the potherb passage), the flower, and the tender stem. No root, bark or seed use is printed for it. No source on disk says what colour its flower is or why it is called "honey".

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the drumstick tree genus (*Moringa*) is known for pungent glucosinolate-derived compounds in its leaves, seeds and roots, which give the characteristic mustard-like bite, and for a range of other plant compounds. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Varuna'di group: reducing Kapham and fat, headache, Gulma (abdominal lump) and internal abscesses. In ch.46: a laxative, appetising, bitter-pungent herb that removes swelling, whose flowers destroy Vayu and raise stool and urine, and whose tender stem subdues Kapham.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation of its own, no named formula in which it is the lead drug.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The group entry gives no preparation. Chapter 38 ends with a general instruction for all its groups: they "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", using "two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group", or a whole group, as the physician decides (lines 561 to 568). Chapter 46 treats the plant as a food-class herb, flower and stem and gives no cooking or compounding method. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The drumstick tree (*Moringa oleifera*) is a cultivated food plant of South Asia whose leaves, pods and flowers are eaten. The classic's "oedematous swelling", "Kapham" and "Gulma" have no modern equivalents and are not translated into any. The identification of Madhu-Shigru with a species is open, so nothing about *Moringa* in the literature can be carried over to it. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Bhishagratna 1907).** Madhu-Shigru: taste "bitter and pungent" and "laxative" (ch.46 lines 1507 to 1508); the flowers "pungent in digestion" and destroying *Vayu* (line 1780); the tender stem "pungent in taste" and subduing *Kapham* (line 1827). Shigru, by contrast, is "pungent, alkaline, sweet and bitter" and generates *Pittam* (line 1506). No potency (*virya*) is printed for Madhu-Shigru.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* Madhu-Shigru appears in a therapeutic group (Varuna'di) and in the chapter on the properties of foods and drugs. It was not found in a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) chapter, and the text assigns it no *ojas* effect. The word *madhu* ("honey") in the name is not a cultivation claim.

**Practitioner's note.** A pungent, bitter potherb, flower and stem that the Sushruta text sets apart from ordinary Shigru, for swellings, Kapham and the bowel. Nothing here supports long-term use or any vital-substrate claim. A cultivator can only note that the tradition distinguished two kinds of drumstick-like herb by taste and use, and that this entry cannot say which plant the second one was.

### Contraindications

- **Identity unresolved.** The species is unknown; do not substitute a *Moringa* product for it.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Laxative action.** The text itself calls the herb laxative; loose bowels and dehydration are the obvious risks.
- **Swelling, abscess, abdominal lumps.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Uncertain text.** The chapter 38 spelling is garbled in the OCR of the translation, and the flower sentence in ch.46 runs across a page break. [USER: verify against the printed page]
