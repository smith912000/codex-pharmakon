### Botanical Identity
Small woody perennial herb 15–40cm; tiny grey-green aromatic leaves; pink to lilac flowers; native to western Mediterranean; extensively cultivated globally; six distinct chemotypes based on dominant volatile: thymol-type (most medicinal), carvacrol-type, linalool-type, sabinene-type, geraniol-type, thuyanol-type (gentlest, safest). Thymus was among the most important incense and purification herbs of the ancient Mediterranean — smoke fumigation for surgical tools and sickrooms.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 De Materia Medica (DMM)

Dioscorides (*Book III, Chapter 36*):
> *"Thymon: warming and drying in the third degree. Promotes urine and menstruation, expels worms, benefits those with coughs and shortness of breath. The juice mixed with honey clears the chest of phlegm. Applied externally it relieves stings of venomous creatures."*

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā on *za'tar*:
> *"Za'tar: hot and dry in the third degree. Expels and dissolves phlegm in the lungs and chest. Relieves cough, promotes expectoration, kills intestinal worms, promotes menstruation. The strongest expectorant among the aromatic herbs."*

### 2.3 Ebers Papyrus (EP)

Thyme identified in Egyptian embalming preparations (anti-putrefactive, antimicrobial) and in medical preparations for respiratory conditions.

### 2.4 Assyrian Medical Tablets (AT)

Thyme-type herbs in Babylonian medical texts for respiratory and anti-infective conditions.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Expectorant / Mucolytic

**Thymol**: Stimulates cilia in bronchial mucosa → increased mucus transport → expectorant. Reduces mucus viscosity → mucolytic. β-Adrenergic receptor stimulation → mild bronchodilation.

**Bronchiolitis/croup application**: Thymol reduces bronchiolar smooth muscle spasm → relieves acute bronchospasm.

### 5.2 Antimicrobial

**Thymol + carvacrol**: Membrane disruption mechanism (same as carvacrol in oregano) → broad-spectrum bactericidal. Thymol additionally inhibits biofilm formation and disrupts quorum sensing. Active against respiratory pathogens: *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (in vitro).

### 5.3 Antispasmodic (Bronchial)

**Thymol + luteolin**: Calcium channel antagonism → bronchial smooth muscle relaxation → antispasmodic. Explains the "relieves shortness of breath" classical application.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Acute bronchitis**: German Commission E and ESCOP monographs: Thyme preparations licensed as expectorants in multiple European countries. Kemmerich et al. (2006, *Arzneimittelforschung*): Thyme-ivy combination significantly reduces acute bronchitis symptoms vs placebo.

**Cough**: Multiple RCTs confirm significant reduction in cough frequency and intensity with thyme-based preparations.

**Oral antimicrobial**: Thymol is a component of Listerine® mouthwash — the primary active antimicrobial constituent.

---

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The ancient disinfectant**: The use of burning thyme to purify air and disinfect spaces — a practice attested in both Greek temples and medieval plague preparations — is antimicrobial pharmacology expressed as ritual and public health. Burning thyme releases thymol vapour into the air; thymol is active against airborne pathogens. Before germ theory, the empirical observation that burning aromatic thyme reduced infection in closed spaces translated into ritual purification practice. The priests burning thyme incense were unwitting antiseptic fumigators.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Thyme tea | 2–3g dried herb per cup | Acute respiratory; expectorant; cough |
| Syrup (honey + thyme decoction) | 15ml 3–4× daily | Traditional cough syrup |
| Steam inhalation | 5–10 drops EO in hot water | Acute bronchitis; sinusitis |
| Tincture | 3–5ml 3× daily | Antimicrobial; respiratory |
| Commercial (Bronchipret®, Tussamag®) | Per product | Licensed European phytomedicines |

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Acrid, slightly warm. Channels: Lung (肺), Spleen (脾). Disperses wind-cold, warms the lungs, transforms phlegm.

**Ayurvedic**: Pungent; hot. Reduces Kapha ↓↓, reduces Vata ↓. *Krimighna, Kāsahara* (cough-relieving), *Śwāsahara* (anti-dyspnoea).

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Pregnancy**: Emmenagogue at high doses — therapeutic doses avoid; culinary amounts safe
- **Thyroid conditions**: Thymol may affect thyroid function — monitor
- **Topical EO**: Always dilute; undiluted thymol caustic
- Generally very safe for culinary and tea preparations
