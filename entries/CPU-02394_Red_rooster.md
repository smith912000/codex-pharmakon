# Red rooster — Dan Xiong Ji 丹雄鸡 (Shennong bencao jing), with its parts and the black hen 黑雌鸡

## *The red (cinnabar-coloured) male domestic fowl, Gallus gallus domesticus, as listed in the Shennong bencao jing with its head, fat, intestine, gizzard-skin (?), droppings, feathers and egg, and with the black hen; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence for the animal but says nothing about which breed or colour the classic meant beyond the word "red"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** The text read is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. The other edition on disk, Yang 1998, has its animal pages unreadable (the scan is not legible there), so it is not used for this entry. The same animal, as the domestic fowl, is treated from other traditions in III_34 (Domestic fowl; tags DMM, CM, FMT), which does not cite the Shennong text; III_164 (Rooster's comb blood) and III_91 (Fowl brain) are other chicken entries. This row began as an unverified placeholder ("丹雄鸡 (SBJ list, unglossed)") and is now written from the text.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 丹雄鸡 in the upper classic, in the bird section (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 丹雄鸡 *dan xiong ji*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1413). The contents (line 45) lists it as one of two upper-class birds, with 雁肪 (goose fat). The paragraph is long and is a list of parts. The main text reads: 味甘，微温 ("sweet, slightly warm"); 主女人崩中漏下，赤白沃 ("governs women's flooding and spotting, red and white discharge"), 补虚温中 ("supplements depletion, warms the centre"), 止血 ("stops bleeding"), 通神 ("communicates with the spirit", a phrase the text does not explain), 杀毒 ("kills poison"), 辟不祥 ("wards off the inauspicious").

It then runs through the parts, each with its own indication:

- **Head** (头): 主杀鬼 ("governs killing ghosts"); 东门上者尤良 ("the one on the east gate is especially good", a phrase I take to mean a head hung there; the text does not say how).
- **Fat** (肪): 主耳聋 ("deafness").
- **Intestine** (肠): 主遗溺 ("involuntary urination").
- **Yellow skin** (裹黄皮): 主泄利 ("diarrhoea"). The character before 裹 is lost in the digitised text. Sun's note glosses a bird's stomach (鸟胃) in this place, so I read the item as the yellow lining that wraps the gizzard; that is a reading and not a certainty. [USER: verify]
- **White of the droppings** (尿白): 主消渴 ("wasting thirst"), 伤寒寒热 ("cold damage with chills and fever"). The Sun note treats 尿 as a loan for a character meaning dung (粪也), which is lost in the digitisation; I read "white of the droppings". [USER: verify]
- **Black hen** (黑雌鸡): 主风寒湿痹 ("wind, cold, damp impediment"), 五缓六急 ("the five slackenings and six tensions", not explained), 安胎 ("quiets the foetus").
- **Flight feathers** (翮羽): 主下血闭 ("brings down blocked blood").
- **Egg** (鸡子): 主除热 ("removes heat"), 火疮痫痉 ("fire sores, epilepsy, spasm"), and 可作虎魄神物, "can be made into a 'tiger-soul' divine thing". 虎魄 is a variant spelling of 琥珀, amber, and the Wu Pu layer writes it that way (below). How an egg is made into this is not said.
- **鸡白蠹**: 肥脂 ("rich fat"). The meaning of this item is not clear from the line.

The habitat line is 生平泽, "grows in level marshes", which is odd for a domestic animal and is recorded as printed.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, line 1415) is a single line: 丹鸡卵，可作琥珀 ("the egg of the red chicken can be made into amber"). The Mingyi bielu layer (line 1417) says only 生朝鲜, "born in Chaoxian" (Korea).

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 1419) is a word-gloss from the *Shuowen* dictionary: the chicken is 知时畜 ("the domestic animal that knows the time"); 肪 is fat (肥也); 肠 is the large and small intestines; the gizzard is a bird's stomach; 粪 is dung; 翮羽 is a bird's long feathers (鸟长毛也). These are the commentator's explanations of the parts, not the classic's claims.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, chicken flesh is mainly water and protein with some fat; the egg holds protein, fat (including cholesterol) and vitamins; feathers are keratin; droppings carry the bird's waste and the bacteria of its gut. Nothing more specific can be said without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records one bird and its parts for women's bleeding and discharge, for depletion, for deafness, for involuntary urination, for diarrhoea, for wasting thirst and fevers, and for epilepsy, and ascribes to the head and the whole bird actions against "ghosts" and the "inauspicious". The Codex does not recommend any of them. Most of the parts named (head, droppings, feathers, a hung head) are not foods, and none of these uses has a safe, general modern form. Heavy bleeding in women, diarrhoea, thirst with weight loss, fits and deafness each need medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text names parts, not preparations. The one transformation it mentions is that the egg "can be made into" a divine thing, resembling amber (lines 1413 and 1415), without a method. The head is described as best when on the east gate (line 1413). No quantity, count, frequency or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The chicken is *Gallus gallus domesticus*, descended from the red junglefowl, and is an important food animal; its eggs and meat are ordinary foods when cooked. Raw poultry and eggs and poultry droppings can carry *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*, and poultry can carry avian influenza viruses, so handling raw poultry material and droppings carries infection risk. Nothing in modern pharmacology supports a use of droppings or heads for the conditions listed. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味甘，微温, "sweet, slightly warm" (line 1413). The Wu Pu layer does not record a flavour for this drug; its only line concerns the egg (line 1415).

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The paragraph for 丹雄鸡 stands in the upper classic, whose preface (line 43) says the upper medicines "nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic; taken much or long they do not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body and benefit qi, not age and extend the years, base it on the upper classic" (主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经). On the grading rule used for this series, placement in the upper classic is itself the ground for (a). The paragraph has no 久服 clause ("taken long"), so no promise of lightening the body or extending the years attaches to it; the next entry, 雁肪, has one (line 1425). The phrase 通神 ("communicates with the spirit") appears in the list of indications for the whole bird and is not explained; the Codex records it but does not rest the grade on it or read it as a life-nourishing status. The grade is (a) by placement alone. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 丹雄鸡 *dan xiong ji*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1413)

**Practitioner's note.** The classic's list is a catalogue of parts of one bird, with remedies for bleeding, deafness, diarrhoea and fever, and a ritual use of the head. A cultivator has nothing to do with it beyond the record. It is no licence to eat or apply any of the parts named, and the grade is a frame, not an instruction.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** raw poultry, eggs, droppings and heads can carry *Salmonella*, *Campylobacter* and other pathogens; do not apply or eat them raw. Do not handle sick or dead poultry without protection, since avian influenza viruses exist.
- **Bleeding, discharge, diarrhoea, thirst, fits, deafness:** each needs medical assessment; do not substitute any part named here.
- **The "ghost" and "inauspicious" uses:** the Codex records these as the text's claims and endorses no ritual use of the head.
- **Animal welfare:** the Codex does not recommend harming an animal to obtain a part named here.
- **Quantities:** no dose, count or duration is given.
