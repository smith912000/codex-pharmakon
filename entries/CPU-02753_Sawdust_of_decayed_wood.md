# Sawdust of decayed wood — نشارة (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *Nushāra: the dust or sawdust of wood that has been eaten away (al-mutaʾakkil), best from astringent trees. No species is named; the identification to decayed-wood dust is the plain reading of the Arabic (confidence medium)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *nushāra* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 15586-15590).

*Actions (الخواص).* نشارة المتأكل منقية ولها وجفيف إن كان في شجرها (15587): "the dust of the eaten-away [wood] is cleansing, and it has a *wajīf* (a word that is unclear here; it can mean a throbbing or a drying) if it is in its tree". The sense of the last clause is uncertain in the e-text. [USER: verify against another witness.]

*Wounds and ulcers (الجراح والقروح).* نشارة الخشب المتأكل تدمل وخاصة التي تكون عن أشجار قابضة مثل بعض أجناس الشوك (15587-15589): "the dust of eaten-away wood heals over (*tudmil*), especially that which comes from astringent trees, such as some kinds of thorn". Then a compound: ثم تجمع مع مثلها أنيسون بشراب وتحرق ثم تسحق (15589): "then it is gathered with an equal amount of anise, with wine, and burned, then ground", and when sprinkled فإذا ذرت على القروح النملية نفعتها (15589-15590): "on *namlī* ulcers (spreading, creeping ulcers) it benefits them". No temperament, no source author and no dose are printed.

### Active Components

The passage names no constituent. In general terms the dust of rotted wood is cellulose and lignin altered by fungi, and wood from astringent trees can carry tannins. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Canon records rotted-wood dust as a wound dusting and, burned with anise and wine, as a powder for creeping ulcers. The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend putting decayed wood on wounds, which is an infection risk.

### Preparation & Dosing

Preparation given: equal parts of the dust and anise, with wine, burned, then ground and dusted on the ulcer (15589-15590). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Rotted wood carries fungal spores and bacteria; burned plant matter is largely ash and charcoal, which are absorbent. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **CM.** "Cleansing" (منقية, 15587) and, for the wound use, healing over (*tudmil*), best from astringent trees (15588). No degree of heat, cold, dryness or moisture is printed.

### Contraindications

- **Infection.** Decayed wood is not sterile; tetanus and fungal risk on open wounds. Do not apply. [USER: verify]
- **Wounds and spreading ulcers.** Medical conditions; use proper care.
