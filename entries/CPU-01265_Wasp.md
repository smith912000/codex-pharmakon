# Wasp — "Wasp", "Wasp's dung", "Wasp's Blood" (Bryan 1930, Ebers Papyrus; no Egyptian word printed in the list)
## *The wasp, as Bryan's English identification names it; the list gives no Egyptian word and no binomial* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The only text on disk for this tradition is C. P. Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). In his chapter VII, "Organic Remedies", Bryan says that a complete list of the animal materia medica "would not be possible", because "the identity of a considerable number of the animals mentioned is still beyond us", and goes on to list those "whose identity is not in doubt, and who were called upon to sacrifice themselves in the cause of the Healing Art". The wasp is in that list: "... Tarantula Beetle Bat Wasp Fly Tapeworm" (line 1404). The extract row (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 130, "Bryan 1930, Ch. VII Organic Remedies (animals) (line 1404)") records "Wasp" with the note "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list". There is no wasp row in `_sources/ebers.csv`, so the attestation rests on Bryan's chapter alone. In this corpus the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis, since the papyrus is organised by complaint and not by substance; the Egyptian word and the kind of wasp are not stated in anything on disk.

Bryan prints the wasp in three forms. I searched his text for "wasp" in any case and read every hit.

1. *The wasp as the complaint.* Describing the contents he says the conditions "range from the Sting of a Wasp to the Bite of a Crocodile" (line 1036), and his table of contents lists "Stings of Wasps and Tarantulae" (line 1074). For prevention he writes that "we are told what to do to prevent Wasps stinging: we simply take the Fat-of-the-bird-coraciasgarrula and smear ourselves with it" (line 4224). In these places the wasp is the hazard, not the remedy; the bird fat is Bryan's rendering and I do not identify it.
2. *Wasp's dung.* This is the commonest form, in eight places. Among the recipes "to draw out splinters in the flesh": "Incense Dough Sea-salt Wasp's dung Fat" (line 2346). For wounds: "For those who had any objection to Cows or Goats there remained the Hippopotamus and the Wasp" (line 2371), followed by "Dough Incense Tooth-grain Wasp's dung Red-lead Salt-from-the-North Wax. Crush and put thereon." (line 2375). In the diseases of women: "Fennel Incense Garlic sert-juice Fresh Salt Wasp's dung. Make into a ball and put in the Vagina." (lines 2536-2539), and "to remedy a diseased Breast one could plaster it for four days with a compound of Calamine, Cow's Brain, and Wasp's dung" (line 2544). In the skin chapter, among remedies for ulcers: "Wasp's dung in Milk-of-the-Sycamore. Put on the Ulcer until it falls off." (line 2658), and, in the list of what to put on after the scabs have fallen, "Wasp's dung Fresh Milk. Put thereon." (line 2672). In the eye section there is a recipe with Red-lead and Urine "to put in the places where the Hair has been pulled out", which begins "Wasp's dung" (lines 2938-2948). For baldness Bryan reports "Flax-plant-mixed-in-Oil-with-Wasp's-dung" (line 4061).
3. *Wasp's blood.* Three of the growth recipes use it: for a growth in the neck "with an irritation of the *atut*", "Saltpetre, teun-plant, Wasp's Blood, Ox Bile, Sea-salt, Bean-meal. Crush and poultice therewith for four days" (lines 3800-3812); for a fatty growth in the neck that is to be treated "with the Knife", a poultice of "teun-plant Xehui-plant Berry-of-the-sames-plant Blood-of-the-nehur-bird Wasp's Blood sasa-chips amamu-plant Lead-vitriol Sea-salt" (line 3841); and for a growth of the *uxedu* "which has developed pus", "teun-plant Xehui-berries Wasp's Blood Sea-salt Watermelon hemit-grains" with other ingredients (lines 3918-3926). The Egyptian plant names are as Bryan prints them and are not identified here.

Bryan's text is a summary with selected quotations, not a full translation, so the absence of further wasp recipes in it proves little about the papyrus. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent, and Bryan does not say what "Wasp's dung" or "Wasp's Blood" is in the Egyptian. In general terms, an insect's excreta are digestive waste and nitrogenous waste, and insect blood (haemolymph) is a watery fluid of proteins and salts. Wasp venom is a separate matter, a mixture of peptides and proteins that causes the pain and allergic reactions of stings; the papyrus passages read do not use it, and nothing in this entry links any constituent to the uses given.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Ebers material, as Bryan prints it, uses the wasp's dung in poultices and pastes for splinters, wounds, ulcers, the breast, the scalp and the hair-line of the eye, and in a ball placed in the vagina, and its blood in poultices for neck growths. The Codex does not recommend any of them. Insect excreta on a wound or ulcer invite infection, a ball of mixed material placed in the vagina is a source of infection, a neck growth needs a clinician's examination, and wasps sting; collecting dung or blood from a wild nest has no value beyond the record. The entry is a record of what Bryan reports.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted: poultices and pastes of the dung (crushed with incense, dough, sea-salt, fat, red-lead, wax or milk); a ball of fennel, incense, garlic, *sert*-juice, fresh salt and the dung placed in the vagina; a four-day plaster for the breast; the dung in sycamore milk on an ulcer "until it falls off"; and poultices of the blood with the *teun*-plant and others "for four days" or without a stated duration. Bryan gives no quantity for any ingredient. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Wasps are insects of the order Hymenoptera, and their stings are venomous and can cause serious allergic reactions in some people. Animal excreta and body fluids on a wound or in the vagina can introduce bacteria. I have retrieved no evidence that wasp dung or blood treats any of the conditions named. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Egyptian (EP):** None. Bryan's list and the recipes quoted give no quality (hot, cold, moist, dry) for the wasp or its dung or blood. The Ebers corpus is organised by complaint and records what was done for a condition, not a classification of the substance.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. For the wasp, the recipes read are for splinters, wounds, ulcers, a breast, hair and eye-margin complaints, a vaginal ball and neck growths. In none of them is the wasp's dung or blood given any status beyond being an ingredient. The Egyptian practice of treating spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*) is relevant in general: Bryan writes that "nowhere ... is the Magic Formula advocated as a cure per se, but it is an essential part of the treatment" (line 1489). Against the wasp, Bryan prints no recitation. At the breast recipe he offers a magic formula as an alternative, "or one could invoke the goddess Isis by means of a magic formula" (line 2544), the formula itself (about lines 2550-2556) being said over the *aat*-plant, "Health-corn" and reeds and not over the wasp's dung. Since Bryan summarises, that is no proof the papyrus gives none for the other recipes. No deity is attributed to the wasp itself, and none is supplied here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The wasp enters the Ebers material as a source of dung and blood for external poultices and as a hazard to be smeared against, and the Codex does not recommend the medical uses. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Wounds, ulcers and the eye margin:** do not apply insect excreta, blood or any unsterile animal material; wounds and ulcers need cleaning and examination.
- **Vagina and breast:** do not place mixed animal or plant material in the vagina or plaster it on a breast; a diseased breast needs a clinician.
- **Neck growths:** these need examination, not poultices.
- **Stings and allergy:** wasp stings can be dangerous, and a person with a history of allergic reaction to stings must avoid nests and insects.
- **Conservation and ethics:** wasps are useful predators and pollinators in many places; the Codex does not recommend raiding nests for these uses.
