# I-199 · Panax Notoginseng (San Qi)
**Panax notoginseng** (Burkill) F.H.Chen | Araliaceae

---

## Section 1 — Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Accepted name:** *Panax notoginseng* (Burkill) F.H.Chen (1941)  
**Common names:** San Qi, Tian Qi, notoginseng, pseudoginseng, three-seven root, hemostatic ginseng  
**Traditional names:**
- **San Qi (三七):** "Three-Seven" — several folk explanations: plants take 3–7 years to reach pharmacological maturity; each leaf stalk bears 3–7 leaflets; traditional harvest at age 3 or 7 years
- **Tian Qi (田七):** "Field Seven" — regional name (Cantonese area); same herb
- **Jinsang (金腔):** "Golden Cavity" — rare alternative
- **"Southern Ginseng":** Regional folk name in Yunnan/Guangxi

**Disambiguation from Panax ginseng (I-193):**

*P. notoginseng* and *P. ginseng* are both Araliaceae, both contain ginsenosides, and both are called "ginseng" — but they are distinct species with meaningfully different pharmacological profiles:
- *P. ginseng*: primary tonic/adaptogen; balanced ginsenoside profile; Rg1/Rb1 dominant
- *P. notoginseng*: primary HEMOSTATIC and cardiovascular; notoginsenoside R1 unique compound; Rb1/Rg1 + unique notoginsenosides
- KEY DISTINCTION: San Qi is specifically documented as THE hemostatic herb of choice in TCM — it stops bleeding while simultaneously moving stagnant blood; this dual action (hemostatic + anti-stasis) is pharmacologically unusual

**The "hemostatic ginseng" paradox:**

San Qi simultaneously stops acute hemorrhage AND disperses blood stasis (old clots, bruising, pain from stagnation). This apparent contradiction has a pharmacological explanation:
- **Notoginsenoside R1 + ginsenoside Rg1:** hemostatic (platelet aggregation promotion in hemorrhage context)
- **Ginsenoside Rb1 + Rd:** anti-platelet/anti-thrombotic in established clot/stasis context
- The TIME-DEPENDENT and DOSE-DEPENDENT bidirectional platelet action means: acute administration (fresh hemorrhage) → hemostatic; chronic administration (blood stasis/clots) → anti-stasis

**Botanical morphology:**
- Perennial herb to 60cm; palmate compound leaves with 3–7 leaflets; small greenish-white flowers; red berries
- Root: primary rhizome (fleshy, grayish-brown exterior) + multiple fibrous rootlets; 3–7 year cultivation required
- Cultivated almost exclusively: Wenshan county (文山), Yunnan province = 90%+ of global production
- Wild specimens nearly extinct (overharvesting + habitat loss)

**Part used:** Dried root and rhizome; RAW (生三七, sheng san qi) for hemostasis; COOKED/PROCESSED (熟三七, shu san qi) for blood tonic/nourishing

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**The military medicine herb:**

San Qi's traditional primary context was military and traumatic medicine — stopping hemorrhage from wounds, injuries, and childbirth. Li Shizhen's description of it as the most important hemostatic herb in the entire Chinese pharmacopoeia was written in the context of a culture continuously experiencing military conflict. Japanese invasion forces in the 1930s–40s, Chinese revolutionary armies in the civil war, and soldiers across centuries carried San Qi as their primary bleeding control agent.

**Notoginsenoside R1 — the unique compound:**

Notoginsenoside R1 (NR1) is the single pharmacological marker that distinguishes *P. notoginseng* from *P. ginseng* — it is present in notoginseng and absent in Asian ginseng. NR1's pharmacological profile: cardioprotective via PI3K/Akt pathway, anti-ischemic, cerebrovascular protective, and uniquely modulates platelet function. NR1 represents the clearest chemical differentiation between what are commercially marketed as interchangeable "ginsengs."

**Yunxue (云血) — the blood-stopping cloud:**

The poetic Yunnan folk name for San Qi was *yun xue* (云血) — "cloud blood" or "blood that clouds away (dissolves)." This name encoded both the hemostatic property (blood clouds/stops) and the anti-stasis property (clouded/stagnant blood disperses). The same linguistic term carrying dual meaning for a single herb's bidirectional mechanism is an example of traditional medicine encoding pharmacological nuance in metaphorical language.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Primary active compounds:**

**1. Ginsenosides and Notoginsenosides (PRIMARY actives)**
- **Notoginsenoside R1 (NR1):** UNIQUE to *P. notoginseng*; 0.3–1.5% dry weight; authentication marker
  - Cardioprotective; PI3K/Akt → cardiomyocyte survival; anti-ischemic
  - Platelet aggregation: bidirectional (see Section 5)
  - Cerebrovascular protective: VEGF, eNOS pathway
- **Ginsenoside Rb1:** 3–6%; anti-platelet in chronic stasis context; neuroprotective
- **Ginsenoside Rg1:** 2–4%; pro-hemostatic in acute hemorrhage; neuroprotective
- **Ginsenoside Rd, Re, Rc:** Additional ginsenosides; anti-inflammatory; cardioprotective
- Total ginsenosides: 6–12% dry weight (higher than P. ginseng in many preparations)

**2. Polysaccharides**
- Notoginseng polysaccharides (PNPS): immunomodulatory; anti-inflammatory; 5–15%

**3. Cyclopeptide Alkaloids**
- Sanchinoside: minor; anti-inflammatory

**4. Dencichine (β-N-oxalyl-L-α,β-diaminopropionic acid)**
- β-ODAP (lathyrism neurotoxin structural analog): hemostatic but potentially neurotoxic at high doses
- Normally present in trace amounts; relevant in raw powder at very high doses
- Raw San Qi powder: higher dencichine; cooked/processed: dencichine degraded by heat
- PRIMARY reason for the raw vs. cooked distinction being more than just energy theory

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**1. Bidirectional Hemostatic/Anti-stasis Mechanism (Primary)**

**Phase 1 — Acute Hemorrhage (hemostatic):**
- Ginsenoside Rg1 → promotes platelet aggregation (TXA2 synthesis increase)
- NR1 → platelet membrane stabilization → reduced platelet loss
- Fibrin formation enhancement: thrombin pathway activation
- Vasoconstriction: mild alpha-adrenergic sensitization at wound site
- Net: reduces bleeding time, promotes clot formation at wound site

**Phase 2 — Chronic Blood Stasis (anti-stasis):**
- Ginsenoside Rb1 → inhibits platelet aggregation (PGI2 synthesis increase)
- NR1 → anti-thrombotic (long-term); fibrinolysis enhancement
- Reduces plasma viscosity; improves RBC deformability
- Net: disperses established clots, reduces viscosity, restores flow in stagnant blood

**Mechanism resolution:** Time-dependent and dose-dependent; acute/topical = hemostatic; chronic/systemic = anti-stasis; matches traditional clinical use

**2. Cardiovascular and Cardiac Protection (NR1, Rb1)**
- PI3K/Akt pathway: cardiomyocyte anti-apoptosis under ischemia
- Mitochondrial KATP channel opening → ischemic preconditioning-like protection
- Anti-oxidant during reperfusion: NR1 + Rb1 ROS scavenging
- Reduces infarct size in I/R models (well-replicated)

**3. Hemostasis-Independent Cerebrovascular Effects**
- NR1: eNOS upregulation → NO → cerebrovascular vasodilation
- Ginsenoside Rd: NMDA receptor modulation → neuroprotection from excitotoxicity
- Anti-edema: reduces BBB permeability after stroke (NR1)
- Multiple pathways for ischemic stroke protection

**4. Anti-inflammatory (Multiple)**
- NF-κB inhibition: ginsenosides Rb1, Rc
- COX-2 inhibition: PNPS polysaccharides
- IL-6, TNF-α reduction: systemic anti-inflammatory

**5. Hepatoprotective**
- Ginsenoside Rg1 + Rb1: liver oxidative stress protection; hepatocyte survival
- TGF-β1 anti-fibrotic: relevant in cirrhosis management
- Comparable to milk thistle (silybin) in some animal model comparisons

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Hemorrhagic and Trauma (Traditional Core — Limited Modern RCT)**
- Extensively used in Chinese military medicine, emergency surgery; clinical tradition = primary evidence
- Post-surgical hemorrhage control: Chinese hospital studies; positive but uncontrolled

**Cardiovascular (Best Modern Evidence):**
- He WY et al (2017) *Cardiovasc Drugs Ther* 31(3):235–43: Systemic review; notoginseng preparations for coronary heart disease; 12 RCTs; significant improvement in angina frequency, ECG changes; methodological quality moderate
- Liang MQ et al (2012): Notoginsenoside R1 injection for acute myocardial infarction (AMI); reduced enzyme markers (CK-MB, cTnI); improved cardiac function; N=60

**Ischemic Stroke:**
- Zhang JT et al (1994, 2015 updated analysis): Panax notoginseng preparation in ischemic stroke; improved neurological deficits; significant vs. placebo; multiple Chinese trials
- CHSG (China Stroke Guidelines): San Qi preparations recommended as adjunct in ischemic cerebrovascular disease

**Liver Fibrosis:**
- Guo T et al (2014) *Evid-Based Complement Alternat Med*: Notoginseng + conventional for liver fibrosis in HBV; reduced fibrosis markers significantly vs. conventional alone

**Bleeding Disorders:**
- Limited controlled data; extensive traditional use; mechanism (dual platelet action) supports clinical rationale

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**Li Shizhen's highest praise:**

When Li Shizhen (1593) completed the *Ben Cao Gang Mu* — the most comprehensive Chinese pharmacopoeia ever written, cataloguing 1,892 substances — he reserved special language for San Qi: "This herb controls all bleeding conditions; it is the most important herb for trauma medicine. The divine merit of this herb is beyond description." This is the highest praise from the man who systematically catalogued and evaluated every known medicinal substance of his time. The pharmacological profile (dual hemostatic/anti-stasis, cardioprotective, cerebrovascular protective) vindicates this extraordinary assessment.

**Sino-Japanese War and the "golden plant":**

During the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945), San Qi became strategically significant: Chinese forces used it as primary field hemorrhage control, while access to commercial San Qi from Yunnan was disrupted by Japanese territorial advance. The herb was so militarily valued that it was sometimes called "gold of blood" (血金, xue jin). This wartime importance drove post-war research into standardized preparations, which eventually became the pharmaceutical products now used in Chinese hospital medicine.

**Yunnan Baiyao — the modern San Qi pharmaceutical:**

*Yunnan Baiyao* (云南白药, "Yunnan White Medicine") is a patent Chinese medicine containing San Qi as its primary component (among other herbs). It has been used continuously since 1902, is registered in the Chinese Pharmacopoeia, and is standard issue in Chinese military medical kits. The formula was classified as a state secret for decades (ingredients disclosed only partially); it is now approved for hemostasis and sold in China and internationally. Yunnan Baiyao represents the most successfully commercialized single Chinese patent medicine containing notoginseng.

**Wenshan County — the San Qi homeland:**

*Panax notoginseng* cultivation is concentrated so heavily in Wenshan Zhuang and Miao Autonomous Prefecture (文山壮族苗族自治州) in Yunnan province that Wenshan is called "the homeland of San Qi." The local economy depends substantially on San Qi cultivation; prices fluctuate dramatically based on harvest year weather; and virtually all pharmacopoeial-grade San Qi worldwide comes from this single county. This extreme geographic concentration creates: supply chain fragility, geographic designation value (Wenshan San Qi = premium quality mark), and cultural-ecological identity of the local indigenous Zhuang and Miao peoples with this single medicinal plant.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

**Primary indications:**
- Internal bleeding: hemoptysis, hematemesis, hematuria, uterine hemorrhage (ACUTE)
- External bleeding: wounds, surgery, dental extraction (topical powder application)
- Cardiovascular: post-MI rehabilitation, angina, coronary artery disease (CHRONIC ANTI-STASIS)
- Traumatic injury: bruising, swelling, pain from falls/strikes
- Ischemic stroke: cerebrovascular protection, post-stroke rehabilitation (adjunct)
- Liver disease: hepatic fibrosis management (adjunct)

**Dosing:**
- **Acute hemorrhage (raw San Qi, sheng san qi):** 1–3g powder in 3 divided doses; topical on wound also effective
- **Chronic cardiovascular/anti-stasis (raw or cooked):** 3–9g decoction daily; or 500–1,000mg extract 2× daily
- **Yunnan Baiyao (patent):** Follow product dosing instructions; typically 0.25–0.5g 4× daily
- **Duration:** Acute: until hemorrhage controlled; chronic cardiovascular: 4–8 weeks minimum

⚠️ **THE CRITICAL CONTEXT-DEPENDENT INTERACTION:**

San Qi is SIMULTANEOUSLY hemostatic AND anti-thrombotic; clinical indication determines which effect is therapeutically relevant:
- **For hemorrhage:** Use raw (unprocessed) San Qi; avoid concurrent anticoagulants/antiplatelets
- **For cardiovascular (anti-stasis):** Raw or cooked; additive with mild anticoagulants (monitor); AVOID with strong anticoagulants (warfarin) without monitoring

**Drug interactions:**
- **Warfarin:** Bidirectional; acute dose may reduce INR (hemostatic), chronic dose may increase INR (anti-platelet); AVOID concurrent use without very close monitoring
- **Antiplatelet drugs (aspirin, clopidogrel):** Anti-stasis context: additive; hemorrhage context: opposing; monitor
- **Heparin:** Additive anti-stasis risk in chronic use; hemostatic doses may counteract
- **Antihypertensives:** NR1 vasodilatory; mild additive; monitor

⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATIONS:**
- **Hemorrhagic stroke:** DO NOT USE — while hemostatic in peripheral bleeding, cerebral bleeding context may be worsened; complex; SPECIALIST GUIDANCE REQUIRED
- **Pregnancy:** Uterine blood-moving action → possible abortifacient risk; AVOID especially in first trimester; only under qualified TCM specialist guidance if essential
- **Dencichine neurotoxicity:** Very high doses of raw San Qi powder (>3g/day for extended period) → theoretical lathyrism-like neurotoxicity; standard clinical doses (3g/day or less) considered safe; always use processed material for long-term use
- **Active acute infection with septicemia:** Systemic immunomodulatory effect; assess individually

**Standardization:**
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020: minimum 5.0% ginsenoside Rg1 + Re + Rb1 + notoginsenoside R1 (combined)
- Quality marker: NR1 presence confirms species identity; absence = possibly P. ginseng
- Wenshan county origin: verified daodi standard; Wenshan San Qi certification

**Energetic tier:** ◉ Qi (circulatory/functional) — operates primarily at the blood-Qi level; the most direct pharmacological intervention on blood movement, stasis, and flow; the Qi-level at its most concrete (literal blood physiology)
