# Aristolochia pistolochia (long birthwort) — Aristolochia makra (Dioscorides 3-5)

## *Aristolochia pistolochia (the Dioscorides translator's suggestion for chapter 3-5; the chapter's own subject is the "long" aristolochia)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000). The binomials below are the translator's SUGGESTED line, not Dioscorides' own identification. **Named only by Dioscorides in this row.**

*3-5 ARISTOLOCHIA MAKRA* (heading at line 6551). The translator's line reads "Aristolochia pistolochia — Birthwort", marked POISONOUS. The whole chapter (lines 6551-6554) reads: "The long aristolochia is called male and dactylitis, with leaves somewhat longer than the round aristolochia, slender branches of about twenty centimetres length, and purple flowers with a bad scent. These, withering, become similar to a pear. The root of the round aristolochia is like a turnip, but the root of the long kind is the thickness of a finger, being twenty centimetres long or more. Both of them are mostly of wood colour, bitter to taste, and poisonous. It is also called melocarpum, or teuxinon, and the Romans call it herba aristolochia."

The chapter gives no use. The medicinal text for the group is printed under 3-6 (see I_1011) and says of it that "the long one is given for poisons of snakes and deadly poisons" (line 6570). That sentence is the only place read where the long kind is set apart by use.

**Identity.** The binomial *A. pistolochia* is the translator's. In the neighbouring chapter 3-4 (the round kind) the translator's line prints "Pistolochia [Fuchs]" as an old herbal name together with *Fumaria bulbosa* and *Corydalis cava*, so the translator's glosses for 3-4 and 3-5 are not in agreement about which plant the herbal name *pistolochia* belongs to. Dioscorides' own words describe the plant only by leaf, branch, flower and root. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** I_035 Birthwort and I_1011 *Aristolochia clematitis* (3-6) treat the same Book III group. This row holds only the "long" kind.

### Active Components

*Aristolochia* species are generally known to contain aristolochic acids, which are nephrotoxic and carcinogenic. That is a statement about the genus. What this plant contained was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Chapter 3-5 gives a description and the word "poisonous", and no use. Its sole attributed use, a drink for snakebite and deadly poisons, is printed in 3-6. The Codex does not recommend the plant, and no protocol is given.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given in 3-5. The group text in 3-6 gives "One teaspoonful of the round one (taken in a drink with wine, and also applied)" and says "the long one is given for poisons of snakes and deadly poisons". No separate dose for the long kind is printed. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Aristolochic acid nephropathy and urothelial cancer after consumption of *Aristolochia* products are well documented, a general statement about the genus. No study of *A. pistolochia* was opened. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** "bitter to taste" and "poisonous" are printed for both the round and the long kind. No warming or cooling quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is manufactured here. What the chapter offers is the discipline of attention the cultivation spec describes: how to tell the long kind from the round (longer leaves, twenty-centimetre branches, purple ill-scented flowers that wither to a pear shape, a finger-thick root twenty centimetres or more long), its names (male, dactylitis, melocarpum, teuxinon, herba aristolochia), and its bitter taste and poison. It records no practice for the person and assigns no status. The identification is the translator's. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice. It is a plant to recognise and to leave alone.

### Contraindications

*Aristolochia* plants (aristolochic acid) are nephrotoxic and carcinogenic and are banned or restricted in many countries. Dioscorides' own chapter says "poisonous". Do not use in pregnancy. No traditional use above is advice.
