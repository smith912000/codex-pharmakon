### Origin & Tradition

Amaranth is unusual among the champions of this Codex because it converges on *two* uses at once — a nourishing leafy pot-herb of the Old World and a sacred pseudo-grain of the New — and the genus *Amaranthus* straddles both hemispheres. Old-world species (the *blitum*/*blite* group and *A. hybridus* itself, now cosmopolitan) were eaten as greens and used medicinally around the Mediterranean, the Near East, India and China for millennia; the great grain amaranths (*A. cruentus*, *A. hypochondriacus*, and the *A. hybridus* complex from which they were domesticated) were staple sacred crops of Mesoamerica. The name itself carries the doctrine: Greek *amarantos* = "unfading, unwilting" — the everlasting flower, emblem of immortality.

**Mesoamerican (Badianus Codex):** This is amaranth's most charged tradition. To the Aztecs, *huauhtli* was a staple grain rivalling maize, and — crucially — a **sacred substance**: amaranth dough (often mixed with honey or, in ritual accounts, blood) was moulded into images of deities such as Huitzilopochtli and consumed ceremonially, a practice the Spanish suppressed precisely because of its sacramental power. The *Libellus de Medicinalibus Indorum Herbis* (the **Badianus Codex**, 1552 — the Aztec herbal of Martín de la Cruz and Juan Badiano) documents indigenous medicinal plant use of the period, situating amaranth-group plants within the living Nahua materia medica alongside its role as food and offering. `[USER: verify the exact Badianus folio if quoting a specific remedy]`

**Greco-Roman (Dioscorides):** Old-world amaranths enter the classical record as pot-herbs. Dioscorides describes *bliton* (blite, *Amaranthus blitum* / *Blitum*) in *De Materia Medica* as an edible cooling green, and the "amaranton" everlasting-flower group was known to the Greeks as an emblem of permanence used in wreaths. The eaten forms were treated as mild, cooling, moistening nourishment — the humoral reading that follows the plant everywhere.

**Islamic / Unani (Avicenna):** Amaranth/blite greens (the *baqla*/*yarbūz* group) appear in the Arabic dietetic-medical tradition and in Avicenna's *Canon* as cooling, moistening, laxative pot-herbs — soothing to a heated stomach and bowel, emollient, and gently nourishing. The classification as a cool-moist leafy food-medicine is stable across the Unani corpus.

**Egyptian (Ebers tradition):** Leafy amaranth-type greens belong to the broad Egyptian repertoire of cooling, nourishing pot-herbs and poultice plants. As with other leaf vegetables, precise species identification in the Egyptian sources is uncertain, so the safe claim is that the *Amaranthus/Blitum* leafy group sits within the Egyptian food-medicine tradition rather than resting on a single securely identified passage. `[USER: verify before asserting a specific Ebers reference]`

**Assyrian (cuneiform):** Edible and medicinal leafy plants of this family belong to the Mesopotamian repertoire of cooling greens and poultices; specific cuneiform-to-*Amaranthus* identifications are philologically open, so the attribution is at the family/food-medicine level.

**Chinese (Shennong lineage):** Amaranth greens (莧 *xiàn*) are a long-standing Chinese leaf vegetable and folk medicine, classed as sweet, cool, and clearing — used to clear heat, resolve damp-heat in the intestines, cool the blood, and support the eyes and bowels. This is a genuine, continuous East-Asian food-medicine use of the leafy amaranths.

**Ayurvedic (Sushruta lineage):** As *rajgira* / *tanduliya*, amaranth (leaf and grain) is embedded in Indian dietetics as a *śāka* (pot-herb) and later a fasting-grain (*rajgira*, the "royal grain" eaten on fast days). It is read as cooling, nourishing, blood-supporting and *tridoṣa*-balancing in moderation, and the leafy *tanduliya* appears in the classical materia medica as a mild, wholesome green.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Unfading Grain of Immortality:**
Amaranth's symbolism is one of the most coherent in the entire Codex: the "unfading" (*amarantos*) flower, the grain of the gods, the food of ceremony and permanence. That symbolism sits directly on top of real nutrition. Amaranth grain is a **complete, gluten-free protein source unusually rich in lysine** — the amino acid limiting in most true cereals — so a diet built on maize (lysine-poor) plus amaranth (lysine-rich) is nutritionally *completed*, exactly the pairing Mesoamerican agriculture arrived at. The "immortality/everlasting" doctrine is the culture's honest reading of a food that genuinely sustains and rebuilds the body: high-quality protein, minerals (iron, magnesium, calcium), squalene-rich oil, and bioactive peptides. In Kenetica amaranth is therefore a **`[BUILD]` food-medicine**: a foundation grain-and-green for tissue repair, recovery, and daily nourishment rather than an acute-acting drug.

### Active Components
- **Complete protein rich in lysine** — seed storage proteins with a favourable amino-acid profile for a plant food.
- **Bioactive peptides / protein hydrolysates** — with reported antioxidant and antihypertensive (ACE-inhibitory) activity.
- **Squalene** — amaranth oil is one of the richest plant sources; antioxidant lipid precursor.
- **Fibre, minerals (iron, magnesium, calcium, manganese), tocopherols** — dense micronutrient load; **gluten-free** starch.
- **Polyphenols, betalains (in pigmented leaf/inflorescence), rutin & other flavonoids** — the antioxidant fraction of leaf and grain.
- **Note:** raw leaves contain **oxalates and nitrates** (as many leafy greens do) — cooking reduces both; grain is anti-nutrient-lower after cooking.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica
A **`[BUILD]` foundational food-medicine.** Use cases: cooked grain as a gluten-free, lysine-complete protein and mineral base for recovery and tissue-building diets; cooked leaf as a cooling, iron-and-antioxidant leafy green; amaranth oil as a squalene-rich culinary/skin lipid. Its cool-moist, nourishing signature makes it a *building and clearing* staple — sustained nourishment and gentle heat-clearing, not stimulation or sedation.

### Scientific Correlation
According to PubMed, the strongest modern evidence for amaranth is nutritional and biochemical. A review by **Zhu (2022), *Food Research International*** summarises that amaranth grains are **gluten-free**, protein-significant, and that their proteins and enzymatic hydrolysates yield **peptides with antihypertensive (ACE-inhibitory) and antioxidant activities**, with applications in improving the nutritional quality of breads and pastas. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2022.112405)

Consistent with this, **Orona-Tamayo, Valverde & Paredes-López (2018), *Critical Reviews in Food Science and Nutrition*** review bioactive peptides from Latin-American ancient grains including amaranth, documenting **antihypertensive, anti-cholesterolemic, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anticancer, antimicrobial and immunomodulatory** activities encrypted in the seed-storage proteins — the molecular basis of amaranth's "superfood" reputation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10408398.2018.1434480)

**Evidence grade:** *Strong for nutritional composition (complete/lysine-rich, gluten-free, mineral-dense); moderate/preclinical for the peptide-derived antihypertensive and antioxidant bioactivities (mostly in-vitro and animal).* The nourishment claim is solid; the "functional-food" therapeutic claims remain largely preclinical.

### Traditional Energetic Classification
- **TCM:** sweet, bland, cool; enters Large Intestine, Liver. Actions: clear heat, resolve damp-heat, cool the blood, benefit the bowels and eyes (leaf); nourishing grain.
- **Ayurveda:** rasa — *madhura* (sweet), *kaṣāya* (astringent); guṇa — light, dry-to-neutral; vīrya — *śīta* (cooling); vipāka — *madhura*; dosha — balances *pitta* and *kapha*, broadly *tridoṣa*-suitable in moderation.
- **Galenic / Unani:** cool and moist; emollient, mildly laxative, nourishing pot-herb.
- **Dantian mapping (Kenetica):** lower/middle dantian — a building, rooting nourishment that also gently clears heat.

### Contraindications
- **Oxalate load (raw leaf):** relevant in oxalate-type kidney-stone susceptibility — favour cooked leaf and moderate intake.
- **Nitrate accumulation (leaf):** avoid large quantities of raw, over-mature, or long-stored greens.
- **Rare cross-reactive sensitivity** to Amaranthaceae/pollen.
- Grain is very well tolerated and gluten-free; principal cautions attach to the leaf, not the grain.
