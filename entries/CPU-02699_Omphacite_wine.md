# Omphacite wine — OMPHAKITES OINOS (Dioscorides 5-12)

## *A wine pressed from sour, partly ripe grapes that have been sun-dried for three or four days, said by the translator's SUGGESTED line to be Lesbian wine; the grape link is the chapter's; medium confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Attested only in Dioscorides, *De Materia Medica*, book 5, chapter 12, OMPHAKITES OINOS (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 9833). The grape row is CPU-00120; this is a particular wine and is kept as its own entry. The two neighbouring chapters, 5-13 (*deuterias oinos*, secondary wine, made by watering the pressed husks) and 5-14 (wine of wild grapes), are separate wines and are not folded in.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter reads: "The wine called omphacites is made properly in Lesbos. The grapes (not yet ripe in every part but tasting sour) are dried in the sun for three or four days until the clusters are wrinkled, and after pressing out the wine is placed in the sun in ceramic jars. It is astringent, good for the stomach, for lustful women [anaphrodisiac], those with pains in the small intestine, those with difficult digestion, and a squeamish stomach. It is sipped up to help plague symptoms. Such wines as these are used after many years, for otherwise they are not drinkable." The bracketed gloss and the SUGGESTED line ("Lesbian Wine [from Lesbos]") are the translator's.

**Context in book 5.** The wine-listing text just before 5-11 says that "the Lesbian wine [from Lesbos] is easily digested, lighter than the Chian wine [from Scios in the Aegean sea] and good for the intestines" (line 9825), and chapter 5-11 says that unmixed simple wine is warming and good for the stomach, and that "wines of a middle age are best for drinking, such as those of seven years" (line 9826 onward). The last point sets the many years that omphacite wine needs against Dioscorides' general rule of middle age for drinking wine. Those statements concern Lesbian wine and wine in general. They are not transferred to omphacite wine, except as showing that Dioscorides knew Lesbos as a wine place.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Astringent; stomach; pains in the small intestine; difficult digestion and a squeamish stomach; reduction of desire in women (the translator's gloss "anaphrodisiac"); sipped for plague symptoms.
- **What the Codex does.** It gives no protocol. The "plague symptoms" use is a historical report, and there is no basis for acting on it. The Codex also does not take the sexual-desire claim as practice.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Alcohol is a central nervous system depressant and a liver toxin in excess, and it interacts with many medicines. Fermentation, not the added materials, produces the alcohol. Tannins are astringent in the mouth, which is a sensory property. Sun-drying of grape clusters concentrates sugars, as in raisin wines. The name *omphacites* is built on the Greek word for unripe grape, but a search of the chapter headings on disk found no other chapter with that stem, so the unripe-grape juice (verjuice) that the name might suggest is not described separately in this text. ### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter shows the discipline of attention Dioscorides does offer: a named place of making (Lesbos), a stated state of the fruit (sour, not ripe in every part), a timed sun-drying with a visual endpoint (wrinkled clusters) and a warning that the wine needs many years. That is provenance and assay, not a cultivation frame. No status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to practise. A sour, aged, astringent wine from an indication-organised chapter; historical only.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Per the entry's own Dioscorides (DMM) claims, Omphacite wine is directed at the stomach, the small intestine, digestion and the appetite. The chapter calls it astringent and good for the stomach, says it helps those with pains in the small intestine, difficult digestion and a squeamish stomach, and says it is sipped to help plague symptoms. The translator's gloss reads it as reducing sexual desire in women, which the entry records as a gloss and not as a claim it accepts. In modern terms the mapped systems are the gastric tract, the small bowel and the digestive process, including digestion and nausea. The chapter prints no warm or cool quality for this wine. The general warming statement at 5-11 concerns wine as a class and is not assigned here. The text says the wine is not drinkable until it has aged for many years, and the entry records the whole wine as historical only.

### Contraindications

- **Alcohol.** Avoid in pregnancy, breastfeeding, liver disease, alcohol-use disorder, and with sedating or interacting medicines.
- **Plague and gut disease.** Medical conditions that need proper care.
- **Children.** Not for children.
- **Stomach conditions.** Very acidic wines can worsen reflux and ulcers. [USER: verify]
