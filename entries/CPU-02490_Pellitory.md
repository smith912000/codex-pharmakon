# Pellitory — عاقر قرحا (*ʿāqir qarḥā*, as printed)

## *Pellitory: the pungent root of Anacyclus pyrethrum (L.) Lag., "pellitory of Spain" (identification modern, good confidence)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the Arabic texts on disk. The locus is *Canon* Book II, entry *ʿāqir qarḥā* (عاقر قرحا), Arabic text line 16469 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; it runs to line 16483. (Dioscorides has a *pyrethrum* chapter in the English text on disk, line 7298; this entry does not cite it, and a DMM tag would be an editor's addition. See report.)

**Dioscorides quoted (CM).** Avicenna opens with him: "Dioscorides said: it is a plant with a stem like the stem of *māzariyūn* and a crown like the crown of dill, resembling hair, and a root of the thickness of the fingers, but it burns the tongue very strongly when tasted" (قال ديسقوريدوس: هو نبات له ساق مثل ساق المازريون وإكليل مثل إكليل الشبث ...; lines 16471-16474). "Most used is its root" (أكثر ما يستعمل من هذا النبات أصله; line 16470).

**Choice, temperament (CM).** "Its best is the hot, the one that burns the tongue, its size that of a finger" (lines 16474-16475). Avicenna rebukes "some of no account" who say it is cold and subtle: "it is hot and dry in the third degree" (حار يابس في الثالثة; lines 16475-16476).

**Actions (CM).** Chewed it draws phlegm; its power is burning; rubbed on with oil it brings sweat (lines 16476-16477). Joints: rubbing with it, its decoction and its oil helps chronic relaxation of the nerve and its numbness and "prevents the arising of tetanus (*kazāz*) in one in whom it is arising" (lines 16478-16479). Head: strongly opens the obstructions of the nasal passages (lines 16479-16480); the decoction helps toothache, "especially the cold kind", and the root boiled in vinegar and held in the mouth "firms loose teeth" (lines 16480-16481). Fevers: rubbed over the body with oil before the fit of rigor it helps rigor with fever and without, "as some claim" (فيما زعم قوم; lines 16481-16483). Avicenna himself hedges that last claim.

**Identification.** *ʿāqir qarḥā* is the standard Arabic name of *Anacyclus pyrethrum*, and the root's tongue-burning pungency in the text fits it. The English Dioscorides on disk glosses its *pyrethrum* as the same species (line 7298).

### Active Components

The root is known to contain pungent alkamides (pellitorine and related compounds) that cause tingling and salivation. This is general phytochemistry and the Canon names no constituent.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only: a rubefacient, sialagogue and tooth remedy. Not a recommended practice. No modern protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

Forms in the text: chewed root; rubbed on with oil or as its oil; decoction (for teeth); the root boiled in vinegar and held in the mouth. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General fact: the root is a strong local irritant that increases salivation.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Hot and dry in the third degree; burning; drawing phlegm; opening obstructions (*Canon* Book II, entry *ʿāqir qarḥā*, lines 16475-16480).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* Pellitory in the Canon is a drug for the nerve, head, teeth and fevers. It carries no cultivation status, and the text says nothing about innate heat, *ruh* or the vital faculty.

**Practitioner's note.** A strong, burning root for tooth and nerve complaints in the text. Not a cultivation substance.

### Contraindications

The root is a strong irritant of mouth and skin (the text says it burns the tongue). Third-degree heat in the text's own terms means caution in hot temperaments. Do not use on broken skin or mucosa. [USER: verify]
