# Guarana / Guaraná

## *Paullinia cupana* Kunth | Family: Sapindaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SA
**Energetic Classification:** South American ethnobotanical framing — a "warming, quickening tonic" in Amazonian practice (a paste that drives away hunger, fatigue and sleep); the Galenic humoral hot/cold axis is *not native* here and is marked inferred.
**Training Phase:** Foundation — Signature Study

---

### Origin & Tradition

Guaraná is a woody climbing vine of the Sapindaceae (the soapberry and lychee family), native to the central Amazon basin of Brazil, especially the Maués region of Amazonas state. Its fruit is a small red capsule that splits to reveal a dark seed set in white aril — an "eye"-like appearance woven into the origin narratives of the peoples who cultivate it. The prepared drug is the **roasted, ground seed**, traditionally worked into a hard paste or bread, then grated (classically against a bony fish tongue) into water.

The **Sateré-Mawé** people are the recognised traditional cultivators and stewards of guaraná, and other Amazonian peoples likewise used it as a **stimulant and tonic** — taken to suppress hunger and fatigue, sustain effort on long journeys or hunts, and ward off sleep. From this indigenous base guaraná became a Brazilian national beverage and, in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries, a global ingredient in soft drinks, "energy" drinks, shots, and dietary supplements. Its commercial identity now rests almost entirely on one property: it is among the most **caffeine-rich** plant materials known.

### Active Components

- **Caffeine** — the dominant and defining bioactive. Guaraná seed is markedly caffeine-rich: values commonly reported in the phytochemical literature place it at roughly **2–6% caffeine by dry weight**, appreciably higher than roasted coffee bean (roughly 1–2%). The frequently repeated "2–4× coffee" figure is a reasonable *order-of-magnitude* summary, but exact content varies with cultivar, roasting and preparation — it should be treated as approximate, not a fixed constant. In guaraná, caffeine is sometimes termed "guaranine," which is chemically **identical to caffeine**, not a distinct compound.
- **Theobromine** and **theophylline** — minor xanthine congeners, also present in cocoa and tea respectively; contribute to the overall stimulant/xanthine profile.
- **Tannins and catechins (proanthocyanidins)** — abundant polyphenols that give guaraná its astringency and antioxidant chemistry, and which are proposed (but not proven in humans) to slow caffeine release.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica guaraná is filed as the **Amazonian caffeine tonic** — the New World counterpart to coffee, tea and cocoa, whose pharmacology is *essentially caffeine* acting as an adenosine-receptor antagonist in the central nervous system, producing increased alertness and reduced perception of fatigue.

The honest Kenetica position separates two claim tiers:

1. **Caffeine-attributable effects — supported.** Caffeine modestly but consistently improves alertness and reduces fatigue, with cognitive benefits largely confined to **attention/concentration** rather than working memory, executive function or long-term memory (Kennedy & Wightman, 2022). Guaraná delivers these because it delivers caffeine. There is also modest clinical signal in **cancer-related fatigue**: a randomised crossover trial in breast-cancer patients on chemotherapy found low-dose guaraná (50 mg twice daily) improved fatigue scores versus placebo without significant toxicity (de Oliveira Campos et al., 2011), and guaraná appears among the more promising agents in CRF reviews (De Waele & Van Belle, 2010).

2. **"Beyond caffeine" synergy — deflated.** Marketing routinely claims guaraná (and multi-ingredient energy formulas) do more than their caffeine content would predict, via tannins, theobromine or "slow-release" kinetics. The best available synthesis notes that while some low-caffeine plant extracts and multi-ingredient products *may* exert effects exceeding caffeine alone, the research is generally **not designed to disentangle** the component contributions (Kennedy & Wightman, 2022). The Codex therefore does **not** endorse a proven "beyond-caffeine" advantage; the load-bearing molecule is caffeine.

The Kenetica reading keeps the Sateré-Mawé "wakefulness tonic" and the modern stimulant aligned — both are caffeine delivery — while refusing the over-claims layered on by the supplement trade.

### Preparation & Dosing

Guaraná is a **caffeine source**, and it must be dosed as caffeine. The central hazard is *dose stacking* — guaraná is frequently a hidden caffeine contributor in "energy" blends, so total intake is easily underestimated.

| Form | Typical context | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional grated paste in water | Amazonian tonic | Highly variable caffeine per serving; no standardisation |
| Guaraná extract (supplement) | Fatigue, alertness | Trial dose for cancer-related fatigue was **50 mg extract twice daily** (de Oliveira Campos et al., 2011) — low dose |
| Energy drinks / shots | Consumer stimulant | Guaraná adds to, and is often not clearly labelled within, the total caffeine load — read for stacking |
| Guaraná soft drinks | Beverage | Lower caffeine; still counts toward daily total |

Safety caveats: count guaraná toward the **whole-day caffeine budget**. General adult guidance places habitual intake around ≤400 mg/day of caffeine, and pregnancy limits are lower (commonly cited at ~200 mg/day) — but these are general population figures; individuals vary and stimulant-sensitive, cardiac, pregnant and adolescent users should use less or avoid. No fabricated per-product doses are given here because label content varies widely.

### Scientific Correlation

- **Guaraná's stimulant action is caffeine-mediated** — *Established (mechanism).*
- **Caffeine improves alertness/attention and reduces fatigue** — *Established*, but cognitive benefit is narrow (attention, not memory/executive function).
- **Guaraná reduces cancer-related fatigue (short term)** — *Preliminary clinical* — one positive phase-II RCT in breast-cancer patients plus supportive review; needs confirmation and does not generalise to all cancers or chronic CRF.
- **"Beyond-caffeine" synergy from tannins/xanthines** — *Unproven / NULL* — studies rarely isolate the components; not endorsed.
- **High-caffeine energy products cause cardiovascular/neurological adverse events** — *Established (case series / review level)* — documented arrhythmia and cardiac-arrest cases in heavy consumers.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

In Amazonian practice guaraná is a **warming, quickening tonic** — the medicine of wakefulness, endurance and appetite suppression, carried for sustained exertion. Mapped onto the Codex grid it reads as a **hot, stimulating, drying** agent. This placement is analogical and inferred; the Sateré-Mawé and neighbouring peoples did not use Galenic humoral categories.

### Contraindications

Guaraná's risk profile is the risk profile of **caffeine, concentrated and easily hidden**. Real safety flags:

- **Caffeine toxicity / dose stacking** — because guaraná is a covert caffeine source in energy blends, total intake is easily pushed into the toxic range: tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmia, tremor, agitation, insomnia, anxiety, GI upset; severe overdose can cause seizures and cardiac arrest.
- **Cardiovascular disease / arrhythmia** — documented cardiac events (including fatal arrest) in heavy energy-drink consumers; caution or avoid with known arrhythmia, hypertension or structural heart disease.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — caffeine crosses the placenta and enters milk; keep total caffeine low (commonly cited ~200 mg/day ceiling in pregnancy) — guaraná counts toward this.
- **Anxiety disorders and insomnia** — caffeine worsens both.
- **Adolescents and children** — energy products are implicated in adverse events in young people; not appropriate.
- **Drug interactions** — caffeine is metabolised via CYP1A2; interacts with, e.g., fluvoxamine, ciprofloxacin, oral contraceptives (raising caffeine levels) and can compound other stimulants and some bronchodilators. Additive risk with other xanthines and sympathomimetics.
- **Not a "clean energy" or detox agent** — the stimulation is pharmacological caffeine, with caffeine's whole liability.
