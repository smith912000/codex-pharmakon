# hug.pa — owl, feather (rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20); Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 "Owl feather"

Clark prints only the English "Owl" with no Latin name and says the Tibetan name "has fifteen names"; the identification as "an owl, unspecified species" is the translators' own, of high confidence at the level of the bird group and nil at species level. ### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the rGyud-bzhi among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry in the sense that one tradition is attested, through two English translations of the same chapter. The locus is the Explanatory Tantra, chapter 20, the chapter on the efficacy of medicinal substances, in Clark 1995 (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). It sits in section "(8) Medicines of Animal Origin" (line 1147), whose heading says that "the thirteen medicines of animal origin consist of horn, bone, meat, blood, bile, fat, brain, skin, nails, hair, urine, droppings and whole body", and the feather is listed under "Hair" (line 1192).

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Clark line 1195 reads: "hug.pa: Owl: [has fifteen names]. Its feather cures oedema." The bracket is the translators' note that the Tibetan text lists fifteen names for it; the names are not printed in the pages read. The second translation, Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`), renders the same item at line 3558 as "Owl feather cures ascites." The two translations therefore agree on the bird and the part, and differ in the disorder: Clark gives "oedema", Men-Tsee-Khang gives "ascites" (fluid in the abdomen). Which Tibetan term lies behind the two words is not shown in either text. [USER: verify against the Tibetan]. Men-Tsee-Khang also lists the owl, without a medicinal use, in a group of birds "that survive by their hunting skills" (line 2914) together with the vulture, crow, eagle, kite, raven and hawk. That line is a classification of birds and is not an indication.

A search of the other texts on disk for the Tibetan name found only the Clark index of Tibetan terms (line 1747), which lists the name and gives no further information. The row began as an unverified placeholder; its gloss, "Unknown Tibetan drug", is replaced by this entry.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The feather "cures oedema" (Clark) or "cures ascites" (Men-Tsee-Khang).
- **What the text does not say.** No preparation, no dose, no species, no instruction on how the feather is obtained.

Owls are protected wild birds in many countries, and collecting or trading their feathers can be illegal. The Codex gives no protocol, and does not recommend this material. Swelling of the body or the abdomen is a medical sign that needs proper assessment.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan medicine (rGyud-bzhi) (FMT).** *(c) Absent.* The line records an indication for a bodily disorder and nothing more. The tradition's own frame is that medicine is the third of four treatment branches and supports practice without reaching the root of illness; that frame does not assign any status to this item, and no cultivation use of owl feather is attested in the lines read. The translations' bracketed additions in Clark (for other items in this passage, such as subduing spirits) do not appear for this item.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here supports a cultivation use. The line is a one-clause indication for swelling, set among thirteen classes of animal medicine, and a cultivator has no reason to seek the material, which in many places is also legally protected.
