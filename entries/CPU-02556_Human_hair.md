# Human hair — 发 *fa* (Shennong bencao jing); the later drug name 发髲 *fabi* is the form the Codex title uses

## *Human hair (Homo sapiens), our identification (high confidence); the entry head in Sun's text is the single character 发, "hair", and Sun's own note says the classic's drug was not freshly cut hair but trimmings or the hair of boys* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Shennong bencao jing (SBJ).** Human hair is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The identification is ours. The file prints the head as 发 alone. The Codex title "Fa Bi" (发髲) is the later drug name; in the file the character *bi* is dropped from Sun's note, so I cannot show it from the text. The source is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`); the entry stands in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 发 *fa*, same file, line 1339). The upper class is followed by the heading "above, human, one kind; same as the old" (上人，一种，旧同; line 1343).

**The Shennong text proper** (line 1339): "Flavour bitter, warm. It governs the five *long* (urinary difficulties) and *guan-ge* (blocked and closed passages); it makes the urine and water passages flow; it treats epilepsy in children and spasm in adults; and it itself returns to spirit-transformation." (味苦，温。主五癃，关格不通，利小便水道，疗小儿痫、大人痉，仍自还神化。) The last clause, *reng zi huan shen hua* (仍自还神化), is literally "and still of itself returns to spirit transformation". The text does not explain it and I do not resolve its meaning. [USER: verify] There is no Wu Pu or Mingyi layer for this entry.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 1341).** Sun quotes the *Shuowen* and the *Mao shi* (characters dropped in the file), and the *Yili* ("the mistress wears a *bei xi*") with the gloss that in antiquity the hair of the lowly and of punished persons was sometimes cut off and used to make a woman's hair ornament (古者或剔贱者、刑者之发，以被妇人之 为饰). He quotes Li Dangzhi: "it is the hair of boys" (是童男发). Then he gives his own view: according to the Han writers, the drug was probably hair shaved from [punished persons] or hair of boys; "the *Ben jing* could not bear to take human hair for use, and so used what remained from shaving" (《本经》不忍取人发用之，故用剃余也). He adds a remark against a later practice: "Prescribers have gone so far as to use the skull (*tianlinggai*), harming even dry bones, and in the end they cannot cure disease; the ancients had none of it" (方家至用天灵盖害及枯骨，卒不能治病。古人所无矣). All of this is Sun's own layer, Li Dangzhi's and Han-writers' opinion, and not the Shennong text. One character in "shaved hair of ... persons" is printed 荆 in the file, where 刑 ("punished") is the sense; I give it as 刑 on the strength of the *Yili* sentence in the same note. [USER: verify]

**Preface.** The upper class "mainly nourishes life, to correspond to heaven; non-toxic; taken much or long does not harm people" (line 43).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, hair is made of keratin, a protein rich in sulphur-containing amino acids, together with small amounts of lipid, water and trace elements. Hair also takes up some metals and drugs from the body, which is why it is used in forensic testing. Nothing more is stated in the texts. No analytical source for the drug was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records hair for urinary difficulty, blocked passages, children's epilepsy and adult spasm. The Codex does not recommend taking human hair for any of these. Urinary retention, seizures and spasm need medical care. Sun's own note counts the use of human remains as something the classic avoided, and the Codex follows that caution: the use is recorded as history only. The legal and ethical position is set out in Contraindications.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph gives no preparation and no dose; the note says only that the trimmings (剃余) or boys' hair were the material. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Hair is keratin, which is poorly digested and is not a nutrient. There is no established evidence that swallowed hair acts on urinary flow, epilepsy or spasm. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong).** One flavour and one nature are printed: bitter (苦), warm (温). Upper class by placement. No further quality is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the upper classic (the heading "above, human, one kind", line 1343), the class the preface (line 43) describes as nourishing life, corresponding to heaven, non-toxic and fit to be taken long (主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人). On the grading rule used for this series, placement in the upper classic is itself the ground for (a). The paragraph has no *jiu fu* ("taken for long") clause and no *qing shen*-type wording. Its last clause, "returns to spirit transformation" (仍自还神化), has the flavour of a change of state but is unexplained, and it does not state a status for the person who takes it; I do not read a cultivation claim into it. The attestation is of the upper-class placement.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing here for a cultivator to do. The classic's own commentator says the drug was taken from trimmings out of reluctance to use human hair, and criticises the later use of human bone. The practice the entry supports is abstention.

### Contraindications

- **Infection and contamination.** Hair carries lice, fungi and bacteria, and may carry metals or drugs absorbed from the body. Do not swallow it.
- **Law and ethics.** Collecting and using human tissue is regulated in many places, and Sun's note itself voices an ethical reluctance. The Codex does not recommend any use.
- **Seizures and urinary retention.** The conditions named need medical care.
- **Pregnancy, children, medicines.** The text says nothing; no use is supported. [USER: verify]
