# Chorakapatra — Chorakapatra (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *A drug of the Nyagrodhadi group, named in the translation as "Chorakapatra", which reads as Choraka plus patra, "leaf"; the botanical identity is not established in the sources read. Identification: unresolved; whether it is the leaf of the drug Choraka is not stated. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not identified. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry, and it has exactly one attestation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The names.csv row gives "no identification given here". A search of all the Sushruta text on disk (the chapter files and the three Bhishagratna volumes) for "Chorakapatra" found one place only.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *Sutrasthana* ch.38, the Nyagrodhadi group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15688; the names.csv row gives 15687; the same text is in `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 347 to 350). The passage reads:

> "The Nyagrodhadi Group: The drugs known as Nyagrodha, Audumvara, Ashvattha, Plaksha, Madhuka, Kapitana, Kakubha, Amra, Koshamra, Chorakapatra, the two sorts of Jamvu, Piyala, Madhuka (Maula), Rohini, Vanjula, Kadamva, Vadari, Tinduki, Sallaki, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Bhallataka, Palasha, and Nandi-Vriksha, combinedly form the group known as the Nyagrodhadi Gana."

The metrical text gives the virtue of the whole group (Vol. I lines 15695 to 15701): "This group proves beneficial in cases of ulcer, cures all disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones and all sorts of secretions of the body in addition to its astringent properties (Sangrahi) and proves curative in a case of haemoptysis. It is an anti-fat and assuages the burning sensation of the body." This is the group's virtue, not a statement about this member alone.

The sources read do not gloss the other members of the list, and do not say whether Chorakapatra is a tree or a herb.

**Relation to Choraka.** The group list does not tell us. The existing row Choraka (CPU-02064) is a separate printed name in the Eladi group, and the form *Chorakapatra* may mean "leaf of Choraka"; this is a reading of the word and is not stated by the text. [USER: verify]
