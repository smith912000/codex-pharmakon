# Goat liver — Hepar Aigos (Goat's Liver) / Goat liver (rGyud-bzhi)
## *The liver of the goat (in Dioscorides, especially the he-goat or "buck goat"), roasted or boiled* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM, FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 2-47, *Hepar Aigos*, "Goat's Liver" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 5125; the translation prints this heading inline, in the middle of the line, after the chapter on goats' hooves). The whole chapter: "The watery fluid that drips from the liver of a goat whilst it is a roasting is good rubbed on for those troubled with night blindness. If anyone receives the smoke of it with open eyes whilst it is boiling he receives benefit from this. Eaten roasted it is good for the same purpose. They say that epilepsy may be discerned by eating the liver (especially) of the buck goat." Three forms are given for night blindness: the fluid that drips from the roasting liver, rubbed on; the smoke of the liver as it boils, received with open eyes; and the liver itself, eaten roasted. The last sentence reports ("They say") a way of recognising epilepsy by eating the liver of a buck goat. The translation does not say what the sign is. The same run of chapters has 2-42 ass's liver ("eaten roasted is good for epilepsy but it should be taken while fasting"), 2-48 boar's liver and 2-49 the liver of a mad dog.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** In Clark's translation of the Explanatory Tantra ch.20, in the "Meat" paragraph (the third of the thirteen animal medicines): "Goat liver: [has nine names]. It is supremely beneficial in eye diseases [and as an anthelmintic]. The lungs, heart, liver, spleen, kidneys and flesh (of various animals) cure diseases of those respective organs." (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 1170). Just before it in the same paragraph: otter liver "cures urinary retention" and marmot liver "unites cracks [and fractures] in bones". Men-Tsee-Khang 2011: "Goat liver is excellent for eye problems" (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3534), with the organ-to-organ rule in the next sentence. The brackets in Clark's version mark words from the commentary, so the anthelmintic use rests on the commentary and not on the root verse as Clark prints it. The spine's rGyud-bzhi row is `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 349, "Goat liver", id uncertain: yes. The same Tantra lists goat brain ("unites severed ligaments and tendons"), goat blood ("cures venereal diseases, smallpox") and the pubic hair of the billy goat among the other goat products (Clark lines 1171-1196).

### Active Components

Liver is an organ meat. In general it is rich in vitamin A (retinol), iron, vitamin B12, folate and other B vitamins, copper and protein, and it holds stored glycogen and some fat. Night blindness is, in general, a symptom of vitamin A deficiency in many parts of the world. The texts do not name vitamin A or any other component, and no quantities are given here because none were read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The two traditions record goat liver for the eyes: night blindness in the Greek text, "eye diseases" in the Tibetan. Liver as food is an ordinary nutrient source in many cuisines, and the Codex's glandulars entry (III_11) covers the modern category; this entry records the historical sources and gives no protocol. The Dioscorides method of exposing the open eye to the smoke of boiling liver, and rubbing the dripping fluid on the eye, is not safe. The Codex does not offer a dose or schedule for eating goat liver, none being given in the sources.

### Preparation & Dosing

Dioscorides gives methods and no quantity: roast the liver and rub the fluid that drips from it on the eyes; or receive the smoke of the boiling liver with the eyes open; or eat the liver roasted (2-47). The Tibetan texts give the organ and its action with no method in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Liver is a concentrated source of preformed vitamin A. Vitamin A deficiency is a leading cause of night blindness and xerophthalmia worldwide, and eating liver has long been recognised as a way to prevent it; this is a general nutritional fact and does not confirm the Greek or Tibetan methods, which do not state the mechanism. The same richness is a hazard: excessive vitamin A (hypervitaminosis A) is toxic, and pregnant women are advised to avoid large amounts of liver. Wild and domestic ruminants can carry liver flukes (*Fasciola*) and other parasites in the liver. No clinical studies of the traditional uses were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** None is printed for goat's liver in 2-47; the chapter gives forms and an action only.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The translated lines print an action only ("supremely beneficial in eye diseases", Clark; "excellent for eye problems", Men-Tsee-Khang), with no taste, potency or humour assignment.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. His report that eating a buck goat's liver reveals epilepsy ("They say") is the sort of lore he sometimes records without endorsing it; recording is not endorsing. The chapter's discipline of attention is in how the liver is handled: the fluid, the smoke and the roasted flesh are separate preparations. It assigns no status beyond the therapeutic.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Goat liver is among the meats of the Explanatory Tantra's animal medicines, singled out with "supremely" for the eyes, and within the text's rule that the organ eaten treats the matching organ. The lines read name no essence-extraction (*bcud len*) use, and no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition; none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do for cultivation. Both traditions name the liver for the eyes. The ordinary use of liver as a food is for the cook, not the cultivator, and the sources read give no basis for a tonic or longevity use. The one transferable habit is Dioscorides's care to separate what drips, what smokes and what is eaten.

### Contraindications

- **Vitamin A toxicity.** Large or frequent amounts of liver can cause hypervitaminosis A; it is a particular risk in pregnancy. [USER: verify specifics]
- **Parasites and infection.** Raw or undercooked liver can carry liver flukes, hepatitis E and bacteria (*Campylobacter*, *Salmonella*); cook it through.
- **Eyes.** Putting the drip or smoke of boiling liver on or into the eye risks burns and infection; do not follow Dioscorides's method for night blindness. Night blindness needs medical assessment.
- **Epilepsy.** The "diagnosis" by eating a buck goat's liver has no basis; see a physician.
- **Dose unknown.** None is given in the sources read.
- **Ethics and religion.** Using the liver means slaughter; dietary rules in several religions restrict some meats.
