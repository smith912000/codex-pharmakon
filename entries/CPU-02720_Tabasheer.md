# Tabasheer — طباشير (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *Ṭabāshīr, described by the Canon as burnt roots of the cane (qanā) in the land of India; the modern identification, from the name, is tabasheer, the siliceous deposit of bamboo, of medium confidence, and the Canon's wording (burnt roots) does not match that deposit* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *ṭabāshīr* (*Canon* Book II, Letter Ṭāʾ, Arabic text line 13476, to line 13485). The description reads: هو أصول القنا المحرقة يقال أنها تحرق لاحتكاك أطرافها عند عصوف الرياح بها وهذا يكون في بلاد الهند (13477-13478): "it is the burnt roots of the cane (*qanā*); it is said that they burn through the rubbing of their tips together when the winds blow on them, and this happens in the land of India." No source author is named.

Powers (13478-13480): فيه قبض ودفع وقليل تحليل, "in it is astringency, expulsion and a little dissolving"; "its cooling is greater"; its dissolving is from "a slight bitterness"; "from its dissolving and astringency its drying becomes strong, and it is compound in powers like the rose" (وهو مركب القوى كالورد). Head: it helps thrush (*qulāʿ*) and "wildness" (*tawaḥḥush*, 13480-13481; sense uncertain, [USER: verify]). Eye: it helps hot swellings of the eye (13481). Chest: it strengthens the heart and helps hot palpitation and the fainting that comes from bile pouring into the stomach, "drunk and applied" (سقيا وطلاء, 13481-13483). Digestive organs: it helps thirst, vomiting, inflammation and weakness of the stomach and "prevents bile from pouring into it" (13483-13484). Excretion: it prevents the bilious humour (13484). Fevers: it prevents acute fevers (13485).

**Other places in the Canon.** The word occurs in many other passages of the Canon, for example in lists of cooling drugs and in compound recipes (lines 21082, 27095, 28079, 30686, 30825 by search). Those passages were not read for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The passage names no constituent. In general terms, tabasheer as known to modern materia medica is a hard siliceous concretion found in bamboo joints. The Canon, however, calls the material burnt cane roots, so the modern composition is not asserted for the Canon's drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this material. The terms of the *Canon* (hot, cold, dry, moist, the humours) are the tradition's own and are not translated into modern diagnoses. The Canon uses it as a cooling, astringent drug against heat, thirst and bilious fevers.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established fact: silica is chemically inert and is not absorbed in the gut in any meaningful way. Whether this holds for the Canon's drug is not established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** Our identification (bamboo silica) rests on the name and the setting in India; the Canon's own description (burnt roots of cane) is different. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, children, medication.** No statement in the passage. [USER: verify]
