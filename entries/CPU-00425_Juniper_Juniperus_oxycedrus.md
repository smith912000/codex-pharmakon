# Prickly Juniper / Cade — Oil of Cade, Juniper Tar, *oxykedros* (Greek), Sharp Cedar

## *Juniperus oxycedrus* L. (and *J. drupacea* Labill.) | Family: Cupressaceae

**Tradition Tags:** EP, AT, DMM, CM
**Energetic Classification:** Hot and dry in the third degree; the tar strongly heating, drying, and resolving (Galenic — a sharp, penetrating, escharotic resin)
**Training Phase:** Purification

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ebers (Egyptian):** Juniper and its resinous relatives among the Cupressaceae were part of the Egyptian aromatic and embalming stock — resins and berries valued for their drying, preserving, and disinfecting virtue. The Egyptian pharmacopoeia drew on the region's pungent conifer resins for fumigation and for salves that dried weeping surfaces.

**Assyrian:** Cuneiform Mesopotamian medicine used aromatic conifer resins and tars extensively in fumigations and topical dressings — heating, drying agents applied to skin complaints, parasites, and foul wounds. The wood-tar principle — destructive distillation of resinous wood to yield a drawing, antiseptic pitch — belongs to this ancient technological lineage.

**Dioscorides (DMM):** Dioscorides describes the *kedros* / *oxykedros* junipers and the pitch (*pissa*/*kedria*) obtained from resinous cedar-junipers, prescribing the tarry oil for skin eruptions, ulcers, mange in animals, lice and parasites, and as a drawing dressing. This is the direct classical ancestor of the modern "oil of cade."

**Avicenna (CM):** The *Qanun* places juniper and its pitch among the hot, dry, resolving simples — used topically for chronic skin disease, itch, and indurations, and by fumigation as a disinfectant. The tar is understood as an intensely heating *muhallil* (resolvent) and cleanser.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Cade is a fire-born medicine — literally the product of wood cooked in heat until it yields a black, penetrating pitch. Its doctrine is transformation-by-fire: the plant that has passed through combustion becomes an agent that dries, resolves, and drives out what festers. The symbolism (purification through fire, the driving-out of parasites and corruption) maps onto a real pharmacology of keratolytic, antiseptic, antiparasitic tar. This is why it sits in the **Purification** phase — it is a corrosive, cleansing agent, not a nourishing one, and its power and its danger are the same property.

### Active Components

- **Guaiacol, cresols, and phenolic tar constituents** — the keratolytic, antiseptic, and irritant fraction of cade oil (juniper tar).
- **Sesquiterpenes and diterpenes** — including cadinene-type compounds and, in the volatile oil, manoyl oxide, abietatriene, cubebol, α-cadinol, and α-pinene (constituents vary strongly by population and plant part).
- **Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)** — trace pyrolysis products of concern in crude tar, the basis for modern restrictions on unrefined cade oil.
- **α-Pinene-rich volatile oil** in the foliage/berry — antimicrobial, penetrating.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Cade is a **Purification-phase** topical: a keratolytic, antiseptic, antiparasitic tar for chronic, scaling, or infested skin. In the training arc it is used sparingly and externally to strip and disinfect a congested surface — the classical use against itch, mange, scale, and parasites — never as an internal or nourishing agent. It is a tool of driving-out, applied to a defined area and then withdrawn. The refined foliage/berry essential oil is the gentler face, used for its antimicrobial and wound-supporting action; the crude wood-tar is the sharp, restricted face.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- Battah et al. (2023/2024), *Pharmaceuticals (Basel)* — juniper essential-oil-loaded microneedles showed significant antimicrobial activity against clinically isolated bacterial and fungal strains and accelerated healing of infected wounds in rats, with complete healing by day 12, supporting the traditional dermatological reputation of juniper preparations. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph17010040)
- Fadel et al. (2019), *Natural Product Research* — GC-MS/GC-FID characterisation of *Juniperus oxycedrus* essential oil from Algeria identified manoyl oxide, abietatriene, cubebol and α-cadinol among the major constituents, documenting the strong population-dependent chemical variability of the species. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2019.1687473)

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical and traditional. Antimicrobial/wound-healing data are in-vitro and rodent-level; the dermatological uses are strongly attested traditionally and in clinical dermatology practice for tar preparations generally, but rigorous modern trials specific to *J. oxycedrus* cade oil are limited. Crude cade oil carries recognised toxicological concerns (see Contraindications).

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM (by analogy):** strongly warming, drying, pungent; surface-releasing and toxin-dispersing — a topical "resolve dampness/kill parasites" agent. (Mapped by property, not a Bencao entry.)
- **Ayurveda (by analogy):** *rasa* katu-tikta (pungent-bitter); *guna* sharp, dry, penetrating; *virya* heating (ushna); *vipaka* pungent. *Kapha*-reducing, strongly *pitta*-aggravating; topical parasiticide/keratolytic.
- **Galenic/Unani (as attested):** hot and dry, third degree; the tar an intense *muhallil* and cleanser, escharotic in excess.

### Contraindications

- **Crude/unrefined cade oil contains polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons** and is restricted in cosmetics in several jurisdictions; use only refined, pharmaceutical-grade material and only as directed.
- **Skin and mucosal irritant** — the tar and undiluted volatile oil can cause burning, contact dermatitis, and, in excess, chemical burn (escharotic). Always dilute; spot-test; keep off eyes and mucosa.
- **Not for internal use.** No safe oral dose; ingestion is toxic.
- Avoid on **large or broken skin areas**, and in **pregnancy, lactation, infants, and children** absent clear medical direction.
- Photosensitivity and staining possible with tar preparations. Discontinue on reaction.
