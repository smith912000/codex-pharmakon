# Nyagrodha (Banyan) — Nyagrodha, Vaṭa, Bahupāda, Vṛkṣarāja, Bar / Bargad

## *Ficus benghalensis* L. | Family: Moraceae

**Tradition Tags:** Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS) — head of the **Nyagrodhādi gaṇa**; one of the **Panchavalkala** (five astringent barks) and the **Kṣīrī-vṛkṣa** (milk-exuding trees); Caraka Saṃhitā; classical nighaṇṭus
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Kaṣāya (astringent) rasa · Śīta (cooling) vīrya · Kaṭu (pungent) vipāka · pacifies Pitta and Kapha (Kapha-Pitta-hara)
**Training Phase:** Containment · Stabilisation · Wound-consolidation (astringent / *saṃgrāhī* phase)

### Origin & Tradition

The banyan is the great *containing* tree of the Indian imagination and pharmacopoeia alike. A single specimen, dropping aerial roots that thicken into secondary trunks, can spread into a living grove — a self-perpetuating architecture the tradition read as a symbol of endurance, shelter, and the downward-flowing generative force. Its Sanskrit name **Nyagrodha** — literally "growing downward" — encodes exactly this habit: the tree that grows toward the earth through its aerial roots.

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (surgical-Ayurvedic tradition):** Nyagrodha is the *head-drug* and namesake of the **Nyagrodhādi gaṇa**, a group of astringent, cooling, Kapha-Pitta-pacifying dravyas that Suśruta deploys for wound-cleansing, arrest of discharge, consolidation of ulcers, and control of the "sweet-urine" and obesity-type disorders (medo/prameha). Nyagrodha bark and its aerial-root and latex are core *vraṇaśodhana/vraṇaropaṇa* (wound-cleansing and wound-healing) materials — a natural fit for a surgical school preoccupied with getting wounds to close cleanly. It also anchors the **Panchavalkala** — the "five barks" (Nyagrodha, Udumbara, Ashvattha, Plaksha, and Pārīśa/Vetasa depending on tradition) used as a compound astringent wash and decoction for cleansing, staunching, and healing wounds and mucosal surfaces.

**Caraka Saṃhitā and the Kṣīrī-vṛkṣa group:** Nyagrodha is a principal **Kṣīrī-vṛkṣa** ("milk-tree") — the latex-bearing figs whose white sap is prized as *saṃgrāhī* (astringent/absorbent), *stambhana* (arresting), and *ropaṇa* (healing). The group is used for diarrhoea and dysentery (*atisāra*, *pravāhikā*), excessive menstrual and other bleeding (*raktapitta*, *pradara*), and for cooling the burning of Pitta.

**Folk and pan-Indian use:** Bark decoction for diabetes and diarrhoea; aerial-root and latex for skin, teeth/gums, and wounds; the tree itself as village shelter and assembly-ground. According to PubMed, its bark is documented as traditionally "used to manage diabetes" and "indicated as an insulin sensitizer" (Madiwalar et al., 2022, *J Diabetes Metab Disord*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40200-022-00989-2).

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The World-Tree and the Downward Root:**
Nyagrodha is among the most sacred trees of the subcontinent. It is *Vṛkṣarāja*, "king of trees," and *Bahupāda*, "the many-footed," for its forest of prop-roots. In the *Bhagavad Gītā* and Upaniṣadic imagery the cosmic banyan is the *aśvattha/nyagrodha* inverted world-tree — roots above, branches below — an emblem of the manifest cosmos rooted in the transcendent. Its near-immortal, ever-spreading, shade-giving form made it the tree of village councils, of Yama and the ancestors, and of feminine longevity vows (the *Vaṭa-sāvitrī* fast, in which married women circle the banyan praying for their husbands' long life). The tree that "grows downward" and holds a whole grove together is the perfect botanical sign for the Kenetica containment principle: it *stabilises, shelters, and consolidates*. Where Jīvantī (the paired sweet rasāyana) pours life in, the banyan holds it in place and stops it leaking out — the astringent, staunching pole of the healing polarity. Its milky latex — the *kṣīra* that names its group — is read as a downward, cooling, gathering current: milk that binds rather than nourishes, the astringent inverse of Jīvantī's lactogenic sweetness.

### Active Components

- **Triterpenes:** lupeol, β-amyrin (also α-amyrin) — abundant in the latex and bark
- **Sterols:** β-sitosterol (and stigmasterol)
- **Flavonoids / phenolics:** including reported flavonol glycosides; tetrahydroxy/methoxy-flavones; kaempferol- and apigenin-type compounds; the antidiabetic principle historically described from banyan bark
- **Tannins:** high condensed-tannin content — the material basis of its astringency
- **Others:** ursolic acid; 4-methoxybenzoic acid; lupeol acetate (reported bark constituents)

Per the retrieved literature, network- and experimental-pharmacology work identified banyan-bark bioactives including apigenin, a tetrahydroxy-methoxyflavone, kaempferol, and ursolic acid as putative antidiabetic constituents (Khanal & Patil, 2021, *3 Biotech*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13205-021-02788-7); and a separate GC/MS analysis of a banyan-containing herbal tea reported "the presence of lupeol, β-Amyrin and β-sitosterol ... higher levels of lupeol and β-Amyrin in Nuga Ficus bengalensis" (Mahmoud et al., 2016, *J Ethnopharmacol*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2016.10.073).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Nyagrodha is filed as the **primary containment / consolidation agent** of the Ficus astringent tetrad — the tree-drug for *closing, staunching, and stabilising*. Its astringent (*kaṣāya*), cooling, absorbent (*saṃgrāhī*) profile makes it the reach-for when the system is *leaking, discharging, or failing to consolidate*.

Primary Kenetica use cases:
- **Wound-consolidation:** Suśruta's *vraṇaropaṇa* action — cleansing and closing ulcers and lesions; core to the **Panchavalkala** compound wash.
- **Arrest of discharge / bleeding:** diarrhoea, dysentery, excessive menstrual or mucosal loss (*stambhana* / astringent staunching).
- **Metabolic containment:** the traditional and preclinically-supported antidiabetic / glucose-uptake action for prameha-type disorders.
- **Mucosal and dermal astringent:** teeth, gums, skin, and inflamed surfaces.
- **Head of the Nyagrodhādi gaṇa:** the anchor astringent-cooling group prescribed as a unit for Kapha-Pitta and medas (fat/metabolic) disorders.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, banyan's traditional antidiabetic reputation has attracted the most modern study. A network-and-experimental pharmacology paper reported that a hydroalcoholic bark extract lowered the glucose area-under-curve and raised insulin AUC in an oral glucose tolerance test, reversed altered hepatic enzymes over 28 days, and enhanced antioxidant enzymes (catalase, SOD), proposing action via the PI3K/Akt pathway and PTP1B inhibition (Khanal & Patil, 2021, *3 Biotech* 11(5):238) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13205-021-02788-7). A companion *in silico* + yeast-cell study reported extract-driven glucose uptake and flagged lupeol acetate and 4-methoxybenzoic acid as lead constituents (Madiwalar et al., 2022, *J Diabetes Metab Disord* 21(1):429–438) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40200-022-00989-2). A clinical herbal-tea study containing banyan reported hypoglycaemic and anti-inflammatory effects attributed partly to banyan's triterpene/phytosterol content (Mahmoud et al., 2016, *J Ethnopharmacol* 194:1069–1077) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2016.10.073).

**Evidence grade — MODERATE / PRECLINICAL, with limited clinical signal.** Antidiabetic and antioxidant actions are supported by animal, *in vitro*, and network-pharmacology data plus one small multi-herb clinical study; monotherapy human trials isolating *F. benghalensis* are not established here. Present metabolic benefits as **traditionally-grounded and preclinically-supported, not proven as standalone therapy.**

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṣāya (astringent), with Tikta (bitter) note
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Guru (heavy), Rūkṣa (dry) — astringent-drying
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha-Pitta-hara (pacifies Kapha and Pitta); may aggravate Vāta if used dry/excessively owing to its rough, cooling nature
- **Karma (actions):** Saṃgrāhī (astringent/absorbent), Stambhana (arresting of flow), Vraṇaśodhana & Vraṇaropaṇa (wound-cleansing and healing), Grāhī (bowel-binding), Raktastambhaka (haemostatic), Varṇya (complexion-improving), Medohara / prameha-hara (anti-obesity/antidiabetic tendency)
- **Gaṇa membership:** **Head of the Nyagrodhādi gaṇa**; member of **Panchavalkala** (five astringent barks) and the **Kṣīrī-vṛkṣa** (milk-tree) group
- **Prabhāva:** Its pre-eminent wound-consolidating and astringent-metabolic action

### Contraindications

- **Vāta aggravation / constipation:** its dry, astringent, binding nature can worsen constipation, bloating, and Vāta-dryness — combine with unctuous adjuvants and avoid in Vāta-predominant dryness.
- **Not a substitute for glycaemic medical management:** antidiabetic use is traditional/preclinical — do not present as a proven monotherapy or delay standard diabetes care.
- **Pregnancy, paediatric, and drug-interaction data are not established** in the retrieved literature — `[USER: verify]` and defer to a qualified practitioner.
- **Astringent excess** can impair mucosal moisture and, taken long-term dry, dryness-related complaints.
