# Harenuka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's rendering of the Sanskrit name)

## *An unidentified drug named in two Sutrasthana ch. 38 groups (Pippalyadi and Eladi), in the ch. 42 pungent group, and in one Chikitsasthana oil; the translation prints "Harenuka" with no gloss, so the identification is unresolved and of low confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry: named only in the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, in lists.** The row's loci are Bhishagratna Vol. I line 15493 (Pippalyadi group) and line 15508 (Elardi group) (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`); they are lines 152 and 167 of `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`. A search of the Sushruta chapter files for "Harenuka" found three further places, listed below. A search of the other traditions' texts on disk was not made. [USER: verify.]

**Sushruta (SS).**

- *Pippalyadi group* (Sutra 38, line 15493): "Pippali, Pippali mulam, Chavya, Chitraka, Shringavera, Maricha, Hasti-Pippali, Harenuka, Ela, Ajamoda, Indrayava, Patha, Jiraka, Sarshapa, Maha-Nimva-Phala, Hingu, Bhargi, Madhurasa, Ativisha, Vacha, Vidanga and Katurohini." The metrical text reads: "The present group acts as a good appetiser and is an absorbant of intestinal mucous and unassimilated lymph chyle. The range of its therapeutical application includes catarrh, deranged Kapham and Vatam, non-relish for food, abdominal glands, colic and gastralgia."
- *Elardi group* (Sutra 38, line 15508): "Ela, Tagara, Kushtha, Mansi, Dhyamaka, Tvaka, Patra, Nagapushpa, Priyangu, Harenuka, Vyaghranakha, Shukti, Chanda, Sthauneyaka, Shriveshtaka, Chocha, Choraka, Valaka, Guggulu, Sarjarasa, Turushka, Kunduruka, Aguru, Sprikka, Ushira, Bhadradaru, Kumkuma, Punnaga and Keshara." The metrical text, read across a page break, says the group's therapeutic virtue is "subduing the action of Vayu and Kapham and in neutralising the effects of poison", and that it is a cosmetic that arrests pimples and other skin eruptions, "rash, urticaria etc.", and checks the itching incidental to them.
- *Pungent group* (Sutra 42, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` line 328; Vol. I line 17024): Harenuka appears in the list of drugs which, with the Pippalyadi and Surasadi groups and the Salasaradi group, "collectively form the pungent group".
- *Chikitsasthana ch. 2* (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch02.txt` line 489): Harenuka is one of the ingredients of an oil (with Vidanga, Lodhra, Yashti-madhuka, Manjishtha, Usira, Laksha and others) that the text calls "a good healing remedy in respect of all non-malignant traumatic sores or ulcers".

The text prints no description of the plant, no part used, no habitat and no property for Harenuka on its own. No translator's gloss is printed beside the name in the lines read, so the identification rests on the name alone.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The text uses Harenuka as an ingredient of groups said to act on appetite, catarrh and the doshas, on poison and skin eruptions, and in a wound oil. It says nothing of Harenuka alone. The Codex does not translate these terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and recommends no use.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** The Pippalyadi and Eladi groups are classed by therapeutic action. The Sutra 42 list places Harenuka among the pungent (*katu*) group of drugs, which is a rasa-group classification of the group as a whole. No virya or vipaka is printed for Harenuka in the lines read.
