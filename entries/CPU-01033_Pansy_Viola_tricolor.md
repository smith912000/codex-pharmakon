# Wild Pansy — Heartsease / Johnny-Jump-Up / Herba Violae tricoloris

## *Viola tricolor* L. | Family: Violaceae

**Tradition Tags:** DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Sweet–mucilaginous with a bitter edge; cool and moist; demulcent-relaxant

**Training Phase:** Restoration — Integumentary & Respiratory

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Viola tricolor* is the wild pansy of European meadows, waste ground and arable margins — a small, sprawling, purple-yellow-white annual that self-seeds everywhere and is nobody's rarity. It carries two reputations that could not be further apart, and the distance between them is the point of this entry.

One is **love magic**. The other is **cradle cap**.

The English name **Heartsease** belongs to the first. So do the rest of the folk names, and there are dozens: **Love-in-Idleness**, **Love-Lies-Bleeding**, **Kiss-Me-at-the-Garden-Gate**, **Three-Faces-under-a-Hood**, **Johnny-Jump-Up**, **Herb Trinity**. The flower had a European reputation as a love-charm and a cordial "for the heart," and it reached its most famous statement in Shakespeare, where Oberon's flower — "before, milk-white; now purple with love's wound" — is squeezed onto sleeping eyelids to make the sleeper dote on the next creature seen (*A Midsummer Night's Dream*, II.i). Shakespeare names it **love-in-idleness**. It is *Viola tricolor*.

The second reputation is pharmacopoeial and unglamorous. **Herba Violae tricoloris** is a monographed drug in European pharmacopoeial tradition, indicated for **seborrhoeic skin conditions — including cradle cap in infants — mild eczema, and cough**. It is still on the shelf in Europe for exactly this. Its documented application is as an anti-inflammatory herb, and it is regarded as a traditional remedy for scabs, itching, ulcers, eczema and psoriasis, as well as for inflammation of the lungs and chest such as bronchitis or asthma.

**Western Herbal / Doctrine of the Materia Medica (DMM).** The pharmacopoeial thread. Heartsease is classed as an alterative and demulcent: a mild, safe, mucilaginous herb given internally and topically for chronic scaling and weeping skin, and internally as a soothing expectorant. Its safety profile — mild enough for infants — is central to its standing. It is also documented in the Pharmacopoeia of Europe, and in anthroposophical practice it appears in compounded skin preparations. Contemporary ethnobotany confirms it has not left use: a survey of 326 mothers in Southern Romania recording ethnopediatric practice found *Viola tricolor* among 25 plants currently used for children's diseases, principally for skin disease ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2024.1377341)).

**Classical / Renaissance European (CM).** The love-charm and cordial thread. "Heartsease" reads, in the humoral idiom, as a herb that eases the heart — both the organ and the passion, which the period did not sharply distinguish. It appears in the cordial category: things that comfort and gladden the heart. The doctrine bleeds into signature-reading — a flower with three colours in one face (*Herb Trinity*), a face that seems to look at you, a bloom that changes colour — and into sympathetic magic, where a flower "wounded by love's bolt" transmits love.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** This plant demands that its two doctrines be graded **separately and unequally**, because one is folklore and one is chemistry.

*The heart/love doctrine — deflate it, plainly.* There is **no cardiac pharmacology in *Viola tricolor*, no cardiotonic glycoside, no aphrodisiac principle, and no mechanism by which the herb could ease a heart in either the anatomical or the amorous sense.** "Heartsease" is not a pharmacological observation that later chemistry failed to confirm — it is a name from a system of signatures and sympathies, reinforced by rhyme, by the flower's expressive face, and finally by a playwright who put it in a fairy king's mouth and fixed it in the language forever. Shakespeare is the reason this name survives; he is not evidence for it. The love-charm is **folklore, full stop**, and the Codex records it as cultural history rather than as a claim with a defeated hypothesis. It is worth naming the mechanism of the error precisely: an anthropomorphic flower plus a euphonious name plus a canonical literary citation produced four centuries of a therapeutic reputation with nothing under it at all. The most durable claims are not the best-evidenced ones; they are the best-phrased ones.

*The skin/demulcent doctrine — this one maps.* Here the traditional reading and the chemistry line up on three independent axes:

1. **Mucilage → demulcency.** A mucilaginous herb forms a hydrated polysaccharide film. On inflamed skin and on irritated respiratory mucosa this is a genuine physical barrier and emollient effect. The doctrine ("soothing, moistening") describes exactly what a polysaccharide gel does. No metaphor required.
2. **Salicylates → keratolysis and anti-inflammation.** *Viola* carries salicylate derivatives (methyl salicylate is the classic Violaceae marker, familiar from *V. odorata*). Salicylic acid is the **actual, current, first-line keratolytic** for seborrhoeic scale and cradle cap. A herb with salicylates, applied to scale, is doing the same chemistry as the modern preparation — at lower and less controlled concentration. This is the tightest doctrine-to-drug mapping in the entry: the tradition found the right chemical class for the right indication by trial and error, centuries before the class was named.
3. **Flavonoids → anti-inflammatory tone.** *Viola tricolor* bears flavonoid C-glycosides — violanthin and iso-violanthin among them — and the biosynthetic machinery has been characterised: **VtCGTc**, the first selective C-rhamnosyltransferase reported to prefer UDP-rhamnose as sugar donor, was isolated from this plant, with Val144 identified as the residue governing sugar-donor recognition ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apsb.2023.05.011)). Real, specific, modern chemistry from the pansy.

*And then the thing nobody's doctrine predicted: the cyclotides.* This is where *Viola* stops being a mild folk herb and becomes genuinely remarkable. **Cyclotides** are plant mini-proteins with a head-to-tail cyclic peptide backbone and three interlocking disulfide bonds — a **cyclic cystine knot**. The topology makes them extraordinarily stable: resistant to heat, to proteolysis, to gut enzymes. They are the reason a *Viola* preparation can be boiled and still contain intact bioactive peptide, and the reason cyclotides are pursued as oral peptide scaffolds — a hard problem in drug design, solved by a meadow flower.

*Viola tricolor* is one of the richest known sources. Transcriptome and proteome mining of this single species identified **164 individual cyclotides**, leading the authors to estimate that violaceous plants globally may express on the order of **150,000** distinct cyclotides ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jproteome.5b00681)). One flower, a combinatorial peptide library.

The symbolic irony is worth stating: the tradition prized this plant for the flower and the face — the parts that carry the folklore and none of the pharmacology. The real discovery was in the herb, invisible, in a molecular architecture (a knotted circle) that no doctrine of signatures could have read, and that turns out to be the most pharmaceutically interesting thing about the entire family. The signature was there. It just wasn't legible to signature-reading.

### Active Components

- **Cyclotides** — cyclic cystine-knot mini-proteins; 164 characterised in *V. tricolor*. Named members include **vitri A** (cyclo-GESCVWIPCITSAIGCSCKSKVCYRNGIPC), vitri B–F, **varv A** (cyclo-GETCVGGTCNTPGCSCSWPVCTRNGLPVC), varv D, E, F, H, varv Hm, He, **cycloviolacin O2**, and **kalata S**.
- **Salicylates** — including methyl salicylate; the Violaceae marker and the keratolytic principle.
- **Mucilage** — polysaccharide; the demulcent principle.
- **Flavonoid C-glycosides** — violanthin, iso-violanthin; biosynthesised via VtCGTc.
- **Anthocyanins** — the flower pigments (delphinidin-type/copigmented complexes characterised spectroscopically in blue-violet flowers).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

*Viola tricolor* is filed as an **integumentary and respiratory restoration agent** — a mild, low-risk demulcent-keratolytic for scaling, seborrhoeic and mildly eczematous skin, and a soothing expectorant.

It is also filed as the Codex's **primary worked example of doctrinal bifurcation**: one plant, two traditions, one of which (skin) maps cleanly onto real chemistry across three independent mechanisms, and one of which (heart/love) maps onto nothing whatsoever and survives purely on the strength of its name and its literature. Kenetica does not treat "traditional use" as a single credential to be granted or withheld per plant. Heartsease demonstrates why: **the same plant's two traditions differ in evidential status by the entire available range.**

Its third role is as a **peptide-chemistry landmark**. The cyclotides are a live, unexploited pharmacological frontier attached to a common weed.

The honest position: a safe, mild, mechanistically sensible topical whose **one retrieved human RCT was negative**, and whose most interesting chemistry has never been tested in a human for anything.

### Scientific Correlation

*Evidence below was retrieved from PubMed for this entry; DOIs link to the original reports.*

**Human clinical evidence for the skin indication. Grade: NEGATIVE. This is the most important result in the entry, and it must not be softened.**

A randomised, double-blind, **vehicle-controlled**, half-side comparison enrolled 88 adults with mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis, treating for 4 weeks with an ointment containing *Mahonia aquifolium*, ***Viola tricolor*** and *Centella asiatica*. The primary endpoint was a composite score (erythema, oedema/papulation, oozing/crust, excoriation, lichenification).

**The study ointment reduced the primary and secondary endpoints only slightly more than the base cream, and the differences were not statistically significant.** The authors' conclusion is unambiguous: the ointment "could not be proven to be superior to a base cream" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.5414/cpp45583)).

The paper reports a *post hoc* subanalysis of 64 patients treated at mean outside temperatures ≤10 °C, in which the primary endpoint reached "high statistical significance." **This must be discounted.** It is a post-hoc subgroup analysis, defined after the data were seen, on a weather variable, in a trial that failed its pre-specified primary endpoint. It is hypothesis-generating at absolute best and is exactly the manoeuvre that turns null trials into marketing copy. The trial was negative.

Two honest caveats in the other direction: this was a **three-herb combination**, so it does not isolate *Viola*; and the comparator was a base cream, which in eczema is itself an active emollient treatment — a hard comparator to beat. Neither caveat rescues the result. **The one retrieved vehicle-controlled RCT touching *Viola tricolor* in inflammatory skin disease was negative on its primary endpoint.**

A COPD trial deserves mention as an adjacent and much weaker signal: a 6-month randomised double-blind placebo-controlled study in 60 patients with moderate COPD tested "Inflaminat," combining black elder berries, **violet herb** and calendula flowers, reporting BCSS symptom score falling from 3.0±0.6 to 1.9±0.7 (p=0.002) and FEV1 rising from 66±18% to 73±17% (p=0.042), with no benefit in placebo ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biology9040083)). **Weight this low:** n=60, three-herb combination, the "violet herb" is not clearly specified as *V. tricolor*, and the effect sizes are modest. It is not evidence for this plant.

**Immunomodulation — the mechanistic core. Grade: MODERATE in vitro, cleanly done, human-untested.**

This is where the work is genuinely good. An aqueous *Viola* extract (C18 solid-phase prepared) inhibited proliferation of activated human lymphocytes by **reducing IL-2 secretion without affecting IL-2 receptor expression**, and reduced IFN-γ and TNF-α production while leaving degranulation capacity intact. Bioassay-guided fractionation and phytochemical analysis identified **cyclotides as the bioactive components** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2013.10.044)). The authors — properly — state that "further studies to evaluate its clinical potency and potential risks have to be performed."

This matters for the doctrine: an IL-2-dependent brake on activated T-lymphocyte proliferation is a *mechanistically coherent* rationale for an anti-inflammatory skin herb, since T-cells drive eczema and psoriasis pathology. The tradition's indication and the cell-level mechanism agree. **The clinical trial still failed.** Both facts are true, and holding them together is the correct epistemic position: mechanism is not efficacy.

Follow-up work compared preparation types on primary human T lymphocytes and macrophages. **Hot water herbal decoctions had stronger immunosuppressive activity** (on proliferation, IFN-γ and IL-21 secretion) than a DCM/MeOH cyclotide-enriched extract, with the isolated cyclotide **kalata S** identified as one active component — and the effect **increased with longer boiling**. Conversely, the DCM/MeOH extract was more effective at reducing macrophage IL-6, IL-12, IL-23, TNF-α and CXCL10 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2173-8627)). This is a genuinely useful result: **the traditional preparation method (boiling) is not merely acceptable, it outperforms the fancy extract on T-cells** — a rare case of a folk technique being vindicated on its own terms, explained by cyclotide thermostability.

**Cyclotide chemistry. Grade: STRONG — this is established, high-quality natural-product science.**

- Peptidomics/transcriptomics identified **164 cyclotides** in *V. tricolor*, characterising precursor architecture and processing sites, and projecting ~150,000 cyclotides across violaceous plants globally ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jproteome.5b00681)).
- Fourteen cyclotides isolated including seven novel (vitri B–F, varv Hm, He) alongside varv A, D, E, F, H, vitri A and cycloviolacin O2; several cytotoxic against U251, MDA-MB-231, A549, DU145 and BEL-7402. **Cytotoxicity did not correlate with haemolytic activity**, indicating distinct membrane interactions ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.peptides.2010.05.004)).
- The first report of cyclotides from this species sequenced vitri A (IC₅₀ 0.6 µM in U-937 lymphoma, 1 µM in RPMI-8226/s myeloma), varv A (6 µM, 3 µM) and varv E (4 µM, both) by Edman degradation, amino acid analysis and MS ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/np030101l)). **Sub-micromolar to low-micromolar potency for a natural product is a real number** — unlike the crude-extract IC₅₀s in the tens of µg/mL that pad much of this literature.
- Cyclotide-enriched *V. tricolor* subfractions inhibited HIV-1 infection with IC₅₀ 0.6–11.2 µg/mL and selectivity indices up to 8.1 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2022.888961)). **A selectivity index of 8.1 is poor** — the margin between antiviral effect and cell toxicity is narrow. Honest reading: interesting scaffold, not a drug lead.
- *Viola tricolor* cyclotide-containing extracts activated the cholecystokinin-2 receptor; the representative cyclotide caripe 11 (from *Carapichea ipecacuanha*) proved a partial agonist (E=71%) with **moderate potency of 8.5 µM against CCK-8's EC₅₀ of 11.5 nM** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-13142-z)). That is roughly a 700-fold potency gap. Reported as proof that cyclotides can engage GPCRs, **not** as a pharmacologically meaningful ligand.

**Lineage note.** The cyclotide story did not begin with *Viola*. It began with **kalata B1**, the uterotonic peptide from *Oldenlandia affinis* (Rubiaceae), whose use to facilitate childbirth was recorded in Congo in 1962 — the observation that a *boiled* African herbal tea retained uterine activity was the anomaly that eventually exposed the cyclic cystine knot. Similar peptides were subsequently found in *Viola tricolor* and *Viola arvensis* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0378-8741(99)00175-0)). **A traditional preparation's implausible survival of boiling was the clue that opened a peptide class.**

**Ecological aside, no medicinal bearing.** *V. tricolor* subsp. *macedonica* is a metallophyte surviving at the Allchar mine site amid up to 5,750 mg/kg thallium and 12,800 mg/kg arsenic in soil, apparently limiting shoot translocation and possibly excreting thallium via stomata ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcaf166)); metalliferous populations show impaired photosynthetic electron transport ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jplph.2020.153185)). **This is a real contamination warning for wild-harvested material from mine spoil and industrial ground** — the plant colonises exactly such sites.

**Discount entirely:** two veterinary trials of an "ultra-diluted complex" nominally containing *Viola tricolor* at homeopathic dilution ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/vetsci12020128); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3168/jds.2021-20170)). At the stated dilutions no material remains; these are not studies of this plant. The first, notably, was **null on every endpoint**.

**Summary grade: The skin doctrine is mechanistically supported on three independent axes and was not confirmed by the one vehicle-controlled human RCT retrieved. The heart/love doctrine has no chemistry at all. The cyclotides are excellent science with no human evidence for any indication.**

### Contraindications

- **Salicylate sensitivity — the principal caution.** The herb bears salicylate derivatives. Individuals with aspirin/NSAID hypersensitivity, salicylate intolerance, or aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (Samter's triad) should avoid it, topically and internally. Cross-reactivity within the salicylate class is the mechanism.
- **Infants and cradle cap — the classic use needs the classic caution.** Cradle cap is *the* named indication and infants are *the* population in which salicylates warrant most care: high surface-area-to-mass ratio, immature clearance, and — for oral salicylates in febrile viral illness — the Reye's syndrome association. No case of Reye's from topical *Viola* was retrieved and none is being asserted. But a salicylate-bearing herb on infant skin is not a null-risk act, and the burden of proof runs against reassurance. Dilute preparations, limited area, medical advice first.
- **Anticoagulant / antiplatelet therapy.** Theoretical additive effect via salicylate content. No clinical interaction data retrieved.
- **Pregnancy — caution, with a specific reason.** The cyclotide class was discovered *through a uterotonic*: kalata B1, from a plant used in Central Africa to facilitate childbirth. *V. tricolor* is cyclotide-rich. **No uterotonic activity has been demonstrated for *V. tricolor* itself**, and this is a class-based inference, not a finding — but "cyclotide-rich plant, class defined by a uterine-active peptide, pregnancy" is a combination that warrants avoidance rather than reassurance.
- **Immunosuppression.** Aqueous extracts demonstrably suppress activated T-lymphocyte proliferation and cytokine secretion in vitro. Caution with immunosuppressive therapy, and in the immunocompromised.
- **Heavy-metal contamination — a real, specific hazard.** *V. tricolor* is a pseudometallophyte that colonises mine spoil and industrial waste ground, with documented populations accumulating thallium and arsenic. **Do not wild-harvest from mine sites, spoil heaps, industrial land or roadsides.** This is the plant's most concrete and most overlooked risk, and it is a consequence of exactly where the plant likes to grow.
- **Cytotoxic constituents.** Several *V. tricolor* cyclotides are cytotoxic at low micromolar concentrations against human cell lines and some are haemolytic. Traditional aqueous preparations have a long safety record; **concentrated or solvent-based extracts do not, and should not be assumed to inherit it.**
- **The love-charm is not a use.** Recorded as cultural history. There is no basis for any cardiac, aphrodisiac or mood indication.
- **Efficacy not established.** The one retrieved vehicle-controlled RCT was negative on its primary endpoint. Pharmacopoeial registration reflects traditional use and safety, not proven efficacy.
