# Peplis portula — Peplion, the maritime peplis (Dioscorides 4-169)

## *Peplis portula, the single binomial on the translators' SUGGESTED line for 4-169 ("Common Water Purslane"); a sea-shore shrub with white juice, used like peplus* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** The row was added because one text on disk, Dioscorides, names the plant under this chapter, and no other tradition on disk is tagged for it. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The row has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is *4-169. PEPLION*, heading at line 9580, the SUGGESTED line at line 9581 and the text at line 9582, in Book Four ("Other Herbs & Roots"), in the run of spurges (tithymals) and their relatives. The whole chapter reads:

"Peplis grows (particularly) in maritime places. It is a shrub spread around, full of white juice, with leaves like garden portulaca [4-168] but round, with the parts beneath a reddish colour; and round seed under the leaves like peplus [4-168], hot to the taste. The single root is thin and useless. It is gathered, stored, and given like peplus preserved in brine, and it has the same properties. It is also called portulaca sylvestris, while Hippocrates calls it peplion."

As with the pancratium and paronychia chapters, this one is defined by comparison. Its own description has a habitat (maritime places), a habit ("a shrub spread around"), a white juice, round leaves with a reddish underside, round seed under the leaves that is "hot to the taste", and a "single root" that is "thin and useless". Everything about its use is by reference: it is "gathered, stored, and given like peplus preserved in brine, and it has the same properties". The Codex therefore reads the chapter it points to, *4-168. PEPLOS* (heading at line 9576, text at line 9579): peplos is "a little shrub full of white juice", "gathered at harvest time, dried in the shade, and turned often", its seed "pounded, made clean, and stored"; "an acetabulum [vinegar cruet] of a decoction (taken as a drink with a half pint of honey water) expels phlegm and bile"; "mixed in meats it disturbs the digestion"; and "it is preserved in brine". The properties 4-169 claims are therefore, by its own reference, those of 4-168, which the chapter itself does not repeat. The last sentence gives two names, *portulaca sylvestris* and Hippocrates' *peplion*, so the text attributes the name *peplion* to Hippocrates. The chapter gives no deity and no amuletic use.

**A cross-reference worth noting.** The text says its leaves are "like garden portulaca [4-168]". In the same book the chapter printed as 4-168 is *PEPLOS*, not purslane. The garden purslane of this book is *2-150. ANDRACHNE* (heading at line 5797), whose SUGGESTED line prints *Portulaca oleracea* and "Garden Purslane"; the next chapter, *2-151. ANDRACHNE AGRIA* (line 5813), prints *Portulaca sylvestris* on its SUGGESTED line (line 5815), the same words 4-169 gives as its own synonym. Whether the bracketed [4-168] is the translators' error or points to the right sense is not settled by the text, and the Codex does not decide it. It also means that the name *portulaca sylvestris* is used for two chapters. [USER: verify]

**The chapters around it.** 4-170 *CHAMAISUKE* (line 9583, "Crenated Annual Spurge" in the translators' suggestion, with a "see 3-96") follows and is a separate chapter, treated in I_1084.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for the chapter id 4-169 and for the names *peplis*, *peplion* and "water purslane" found no existing entry on the same chapter. I_1086 (*Euphorbia peplus*, 4-168) describes it as "a different, maritime plant (suggested *Peplis portula*, 'Common Water Purslane') whose seed is 'hot to the taste'" and so treats 4-169 as a neighbour, not a duplicate; I_1084 (*Euphorbia chamaesyce*) says its chapter stands after 4-169; I_1087 (*Euphorbia resinifera*) matched only on the spurge-group heading's names ("Tithymalus peplis, Euphorbia peplis"). None is a duplicate. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The composition of the shrub Dioscorides describes was not read. As a general fact, *Peplis* (now generally placed in *Lythrum*, family Lythraceae) is a small creeping marsh and shore plant that is not a spurge; the "white juice" Dioscorides gives it is the feature that ties it, in his chapters, to the spurges. No constituent is named in the chapter. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records no use of its own: it is gathered, stored and given like *peplus* and has its properties, and the properties of peplos are a drink that expels phlegm and bile and a food additive that disturbs digestion. The Codex gives no protocol. A white-juiced shore shrub placed in the same class as a purgative spurge is not something to take.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives no preparation of its own, with locus (line 9582): it is "gathered, stored, and given like peplus preserved in brine". The only measure in the related chapter is "an acetabulum [vinegar cruet] of a decoction (taken as a drink with a half pint of honey water)" for peplos (4-168, line 9579). Whether that is the intended dose for peplion the chapter does not say. No dose is given in the sources retrieved for peplion. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] It is a general, well-established fact that herbal preparations which "expel phlegm and bile" in the old sense are purgatives, and that purgatives can cause dehydration. The identification is a translators' suggestion, so the chapter does not show that the plant it describes is *Peplis portula*.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints a taste for the seed ("hot to the taste") and says the plant "has the same properties" as peplus. It prints no warming, cooling, drying or moistening quality of its own for 4-169.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The cultivation spec says that what he offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention: provenance, season of gathering, sensory assay, adulteration detection. This chapter gives a habitat, a sensory assay of the seed (tasted, and hot) and a judgement that the root is "useless", and then hands the preparation to a neighbouring chapter. It names no season of its own. The plant has no status in the chapter beyond that of a medicinal shrub, and the text records no amuletic use. No cultivation framing is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The small lesson is that Dioscorides tastes the seed and rejects the root, and that a short chapter can refer the reader to a better-described neighbour. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The chapter ties the plant to a drug that expels phlegm and bile and disturbs digestion; purgatives are unsafe in pregnancy, in dehydration and in bowel disease. A white-juiced shrub of unsure identity should not be eaten or drunk. The identification rests on a translators' suggestion, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry.
