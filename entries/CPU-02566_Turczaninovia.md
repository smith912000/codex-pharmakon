# Turczaninovia — 女菀 *Nü Wan* ("Radix Asteris Fastigiati", Yang 1998; also 白菀 *bai wan*, 织女菀 *zhi nü wan*, 女肠 *nü chang*)

## *Turczaninovia fastigiata (Fisch.) DC. (syn. Aster fastigiatus), a daisy-family perennial of northern Asia, the identification we give to 女菀; of medium confidence: the name, the aliases "white wan" and "weaving-girl wan" and the Guangya gloss "女肠" fix a plant of the 菀 (aster) group, and Yang names it Aster fastigiatus, but the Shennong text gives no description of the plant, and its paragraph is cut off in Sun's text before the habitat* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** *Nü wan* is a monograph of the Divine Farmer's Classic. It has no chapter in Sushruta, Dioscorides, Ebers or the Assyrian texts. The Codex already has the entry I_1512 (Tatarian aster, 紫菀 *zi wan*), a close relative; this entry is the separate monograph of 女菀 from Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction. It replaces the genus-level placeholder "Aster sp." (one of whose two loci is this drug) for the Nü Wan locus.

**The Shennong text proper.** Sun's name line is followed by a bracket, 《御览》作苑 ("the *Taiping yulan* writes the second character 苑"). The 味 paragraph, in the middle (中) classic, reads 味辛，温 ("acrid, warm"). It governs 风洗洗 ("wind [chills] *xi xi*", a shivering-with-cold phrase), 霍乱泄利 ("cholera-like vomiting and diarrhoea"), 肠鸣 ("rumbling of the intestines"), 上下无常处 ("moving up and down, with no fixed place"), 惊痫 ("fright epilepsy") and 寒… ("cold…"). **Sun's text breaks off at 寒:** the paragraph runs without a stop into the Wu Pu layer, so any clause that followed is missing in this transcription, and no 久服 clause is visible. Yang 1998 completes it as "hundreds of diseases with cold and heat" and ends with "It grows in rivers and valleys", a reading that matches the usual form of the classic's paragraphs but is Yang's, not Sun's. The Codex records the cut-off paragraph as it is. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 女菀 *nü wan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2297; Yang: `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` lines 2993-2996)

**Wu Pu layer (3rd century).** 《吴普》曰 gives the names 女菀，一名白菀，一名识女苑 ("*Nü wan*, also called *bai wan*, also *shi nü yuan*"; the *yuan* character is the Yulan's spelling) (line 2297).

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 gives the aliases 白菀, 织女菀 ("weaving-girl *wan*") and 茆 (*mao*), the place 生汉中 ("Hanzhong"), and the harvest 正月、二月采，阴干 ("gather in the first and second months; dry in the shade") (line 2299).

**Sun's note (案).** The only note is that the *Guangya* says 女肠，女菀也 ("*nü chang* is *nü wan*") (line 2301). Sun gives no further description.

**Yang 1998 (second witness).** Yang prints "Nu Wan (Radix Asteris Fastigiati)" in "Herbs: Middle Class" (line 2993). His note 180 says "Nu Wan (Aster Fastigiatus) and Zi Wan (Aster Tataris) belong to the same species. Nowadays, only Aster Tataris is used in Chinese medicine" (lines 3015-3016). The first sentence is Yang's own judgement and is more than the Shennong text says; the Codex notes it and does not adopt it, since the two are separate monographs in the classic. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The shared character 菀 and the aliases place *nü wan* in the aster group. The plant now called *Turczaninovia fastigiata* (formerly *Aster fastigiatus*) is the standard modern identification, and the Codex follows it with medium confidence. The classic does not describe the plant, and the earlier first-pass identification as a pearl-everlasting (*Anaphalis*) was rejected on the same ground.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records the root for chills, cholera-like vomiting and diarrhoea, rumbling bowels, pain that moves from place to place, and fright epilepsy; its further indications are lost in Sun's text. The Codex records these as the classic's claims. Vomiting and diarrhoea with dehydration, and seizures, are emergencies and need medical care. The plant is little used in modern Chinese medicine, as Yang notes.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph gives no preparation, dose or course. The Mingyi layer gives the harvest (first and second months) and "drying in the shade" (阴干). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] The later appendix 附：诸药制使 (line 4193 ff.) records one compatibility line in which the name appears: nitre (消石) "fears *nü wan*" (畏女菀, line 4213). It is a later layer and gives no weights.

### Scientific Correlation

The aster family includes many medicinal and food plants, and the *Aster* group is used in East Asian medicine for cough; this is a general statement about the genus and does not come from the texts read. Whether *Turczaninovia fastigiata* has any effect on the complaints named has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong text proper).** 味辛，温: acrid; warm. No organ or channel affinity is printed. The paragraph prints no toxicity word in the part that survives; the middle-class placement carries the preface's general statement that middle drugs may be non-toxic or toxic and are to be weighed accordingly.
- No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph stands in the middle classic. The preface says middle drugs are "ministers" that "nourish the nature (养性) to correspond with humans", may be non-toxic or toxic and are to be weighed as suits, and are for those who want to "check illness and restore the lean" (line 1677; the drug is named in that list). In the text as it survives, the paragraph is a flavour, a nature and a list of complaints and shows **no 久服 clause**; because the end is cut off, whether the lost words included one cannot be shown from Sun's text, and Yang's completion has none. It therefore carries only the middle class's frame and no cultivation status. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 女菀 *nü wan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 1677 and 2297)

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: a remedy for a defined set of complaints, taken for a course and then stopped, and nothing here supports long-term use. The entry is a record, and its main caution is that the paragraph is damaged. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Gut illness.** Vomiting and diarrhoea with dehydration need medical care; do not self-treat with this root.
- **Seizures.** Fright epilepsy in the classic is a historical term; seizures need diagnosis and care.
- **Identity.** Plants of the aster group are easily confused; roots sold as *nü wan* or *zi wan* may be other species. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy and children.** No source read addresses them; avoid. This is a general precaution, not a statement of the text.
