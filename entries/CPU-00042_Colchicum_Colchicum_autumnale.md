### Origin & Tradition

The autumn crocus is the leafless lilac flower that rises from bare ground in autumn — "naked ladies" — while its leaves and the capsule of seed come up in spring. It is not a true crocus and yields no culinary saffron; the resemblance has caused fatal confusions. Dioscorides places *kolchikon* among the **poisons** in Book IV and warns plainly of its lethality, classing it with the deadly bulbs — a remarkable instance of the ancient pharmacy correctly identifying a poison whose medical value it had not yet learned to harness. The plant's name points to **Colchis**, the Black Sea land of Medea and her poisons, fixing its toxic reputation in myth.

Yet the same plant carried, through the Byzantine and Arabic traditions, a second identity as **hermodactyl** ("Hermes' fingers") — a remedy for **gout and joint disease**. Where the Greek mainstream saw mainly poison, Alexandrian and later Arabic physicians (and the tradition that flowed through them into medieval Europe) used colchicum corm cautiously for the agonising acute attack of gout, an indication it has never lost. Colchicine — the alkaloid isolated from the plant in the 19th century — remains to this day a **standard, scientifically validated treatment for acute gout** and for familial Mediterranean fever, one of the oldest plant drugs in continuous rational use.

Colchicum's third, and scientifically most far-reaching, identity is as a **mitotic poison**. Colchicine binds tubulin and arrests cell division at metaphase by preventing spindle formation — a property that made it a foundational tool of cell biology and cytogenetics (it is used to arrest dividing cells for chromosome analysis) and in plant breeding (to double chromosome number and produce polyploids). One plant thus spans ancient poison, enduring gout medicine, and a cornerstone reagent of modern biology — united by a single, beautiful, dangerous molecule.

### Active Components

- **Colchicine** — the principal alkaloid (a tropolone alkaloid). It **binds tubulin and inhibits microtubule polymerisation**, arresting mitosis at metaphase. In gout, it disrupts neutrophil microtubule-dependent functions (motility, the inflammasome response to urate crystals), damping the acute inflammatory attack. Narrow therapeutic index; toxic and potentially lethal in overdose, with no specific antidote.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Recorded as **historical and specialist material.** Colchicum is never a self-administered herb; only purified, accurately dosed colchicine under medical supervision has a legitimate therapeutic role.

1. **Acute gout (validated, specialist/medical).** The enduring use: colchicine arrests the acute gouty attack by damping urate-crystal–driven neutrophil inflammation — a rational, evidence-based indication descended directly from the hermodactyl tradition.
2. **Familial Mediterranean fever and other autoinflammatory conditions (medical).** Colchicine is standard prophylaxis — a modern extension of its anti-inflammatory action.
3. **Mitotic arrest reagent (research).** Tubulin binding makes colchicine a foundational laboratory tool for arresting cell division and for chromosome (karyotype) analysis.
4. **Polyploidy induction (plant breeding).** Used to double chromosome number and generate polyploid crops/ornamentals.
5. **Historical poison (documented).** The Book IV identity — a lethal toxin, source of accidental and deliberate poisoning.

### Preparation & Dosing

**No therapeutic home dose is given. The toxic dose is close to the therapeutic dose, overdose is frequently fatal, and there is no antidote.**

- **Purified colchicine (pharmaceutical):** the only legitimate therapeutic form, dosed precisely under medical supervision for gout/FMF. [USER: do not reproduce dosing numbers; this is prescription-only specialist medicine.]
- **Corm/seed (raw plant):** a poison; never used as a home preparation. Historical "hermodactyl" corm preparations were the specialist physician's province and are obsolete.
- **No safe culinary or casual use.** Fatal confusion with wild garlic/saffron is a recurrent cause of poisoning.

Safety framing: colchicum/colchicine poisoning has a characteristically **delayed** onset (GI symptoms hours later, then multi-organ failure) which lulls victims and delays care; it is a medical emergency. Any ingestion of the plant requires urgent medical attention.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM / Galenic humoral degree:** classed essentially as a **poison** (Book IV) — frankly toxic, beyond the routine humoral degrees; the Greek mainstream did not treat it as an ordinary medicine.
- **Byzantine/Arabic mapping:** *hermodactyl* — the gout remedy; the strand that preserved colchicum's therapeutic use through the medieval period.
- **Kenetica Qi/Shen tier:** ◉ **Qi** for its action on inflammatory "heat-and-swelling" stagnation (the gouty joint), with a ☿ **Shen** ascription reflecting its systemic, cell-level reach (mitotic arrest) and life-or-death margin — a substance that acts at the deepest regulatory level of the dividing cell.

### Contraindications

- **Narrow therapeutic index — absolute.** Toxic dose near therapeutic dose; overdose frequently fatal; no antidote.
- **Renal and hepatic impairment.** Reduced clearance markedly increases toxicity; major contraindication/dose-limiting factor for medical colchicine.
- **Pregnancy.** Colchicine crosses the placenta and is an antimitotic; use only under specialist judgement. [USER: verify current obstetric guidance.]
- **Drug interactions.** Serious interactions with CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein inhibitors (e.g. certain macrolides, statins, ciclosporin) can precipitate fatal toxicity. [USER: verify the interaction list.]
