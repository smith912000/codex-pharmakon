# Sedum acre — Aeizoon eteron (Dioscorides 4-91)

## *Sedum acre [Linnaeus], "Wall Pepper, Stonecrop", the last of the names on the translators' SUGGESTED line for 4-91 ("Sedi tertium genus [Fuchs], Sempervivum minus vermiculatum acre [Bauhin], Sedum acre [Linnaeus]")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED line (lines 8855 and 8856) prints three names, the last two with the namers Bauhin and Linnaeus; the row takes the last. It was added from that line (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is *4-91. AEIZOON ETERON* ("the other aizoon"), heading at line 8853, SUGGESTED line at 8855, text at line 8857. It stands in Book Four ("Other Herbs & Roots"), after *4-90. AEIZOON TO MIXRON* and before *4-92. KOTULEDON* (heading at line 8858). The translators' index gives *Aeizoon eteron* at page 643 (line 11975).

The whole chapter reads: "There seems to be a third kind of sempervivum that has little leaves, thicker, similar to those of portulaca [4-168], and rough. It grows among rocks. It is warming, sharp, and ulcerating, dissolving tumours [possibly goitre] applied with goose grease. It is also called portulaca agrestis, or telephium, and the Romans call it illecebra."

The chapter is a short one. It says it "seems" that there is "a third kind of sempervivum", which places it after the two chapters of the same family (4-89 and 4-90), and describes it by its leaves: little, thicker than the portulaca's and rough. It grows "among rocks". The one use is that, "applied with goose grease", it dissolves tumours (the translators add "possibly goitre"). Three further names are printed: *portulaca agrestis*, *telephium* and the Roman *illecebra*. The plant is called "warming, sharp, and ulcerating", which differ from the "cooling and astringent" that 4-89 prints for the great kind; the text gives no other account of the "ulcerating" quality.

**The three sempervivum chapters.** 4-89 (*AEIZOON TO MEGA*, heading at line 8827; existing row for *Sempervivum tectorum*, CPU-01624, entry I_1188 in this batch) and 4-90 (*AEIZOON TO MIXRON*, heading at line 8836; row CPU-01623, entry I_1187 in this batch) are separate chapters read for their own entries. The chapter 4-90 says its leaves "have the same strength as the previous one", which is a reference to 4-89, not to 4-91. 4-91 makes no such reference, and gives its own qualities. The name *telephium* is also printed in the earlier chapter *2-151. ANDRACHNE AGRIA* (heading at line 5813, names at line 5818: "also called aizoon agrion i.e. sempervivum sylvestre, or telephium"), which names the same alternatives *portulaca* and *illecebra*; that chapter is not read into this entry. The name overlap is the text's and its reason is not given. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** The existing entry I_1058 (*Cotyledon orbiculata*) discusses the three sempervivum chapters in connection with the cotyledon chapters 4-92 and 4-93 and records that 4-91 is "warming, sharp, and ulcerating". It does not treat 4-91 as an entry of its own. A plate captioned "Sedum acre after FAGUET — 1874" is printed at line 8462, which falls in the pages of chapter 4-52, not beside 4-91; the text does not say why. No entry is duplicated.

The constituents of the plant Dioscorides describes were not read, and the chapter prints none. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the translators' *Sedum acre*. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides reports a small rock-growing plant that is warming, sharp and ulcerating and that dissolves tumours when applied with goose grease. These are the text's reports. The Codex gives no protocol for tumours or goitre, and the identification is the translators'.

### Preparation & Dosing

4-91 gives one preparation: the plant "applied with goose grease". It gives no quantity, frequency or duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints the plant as "warming, sharp, and ulcerating". It prints no taste other than "sharp" and no cooling or drying quality.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter records no amuletic use and prints *illecebra* and *telephium* as names only. The part of the discipline of attention that the cultivation spec describes for this tradition that is present here is the description of the plant (little, thick, rough leaves, growing among rocks) and the printed qualities. The plant is given no status beyond that of a warming, sharp and ulcerating herb. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a short record of a sharp, ulcerating herb applied in fat to a tumour, without amounts, so it is history and not a practice. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The chapter itself warns of an effect: the plant is "ulcerating", so applied to the skin it is by the text's own account an irritant. A tumour or goitre needs medical assessment, and a home application must not replace it. The identification rests on a translators' suggestion, so no wild plant should be gathered or applied on the strength of this entry. [USER: verify] None of the traditional uses above is advice.
