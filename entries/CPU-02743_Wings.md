# Wings (poultry; dove feathers) — جناح (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *The wing of a bird: chicken and goose wings as food, and the wing feathers of the waršān (a pigeon-type bird) in a remedy. The identification of the bird is ours, from the Arabic names, and is of medium confidence; the text names the birds but gives no species* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *janāḥ* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 11746-11753). It has two parts.

*Choice (food).* خيرها أجنحة الدجاج وأجنحة الأوز صالحة الهضم والغذاء (11748): "the best of them are the wings of the hen and the wings of the goose, good for digestion and for nourishment". The text gives two reasons: they are light (خفت) "because of much movement and exercise", and their nourishment is abundant "because of the much flesh in them and their nearness to the heart" (11748-11749). The e-text reads وإما where وإنما is expected; the sense is clear. This is the passage's own reasoning and is quoted, not extended.

*Swellings and pustules (feathers).* يقال فيما يقال: إن ريش جناح الورشان إذا خلط مع مثله بنجا وأحرق (11751-11752): "it is said, among what is said, that the feathers of the wing of the *waršān*, when mixed with an equal amount of *banj* and burned..." Under the heading of the excretory organs the text goes on: قيل إن الخبز المعمول بما ذكر يطلق البطن ويسهل جدا (11752-11753): "it is said that bread made with what has been mentioned loosens the belly and purges very much". The grammar of this passage is compressed and the exact process (what is burned, what is added to the bread) is not fully clear in the e-text. [USER: verify against another witness of the Arabic.] *Waršān* is commonly glossed as a wood pigeon or ring-dove, and *banj* as henbane; both glosses are ours. [USER: verify] Both clauses are introduced by "it is said" (يقال / قيل), and no source author is named. A page marker (`PageV01P426`) falls inside the entry and is not part of the sense.

### Active Components

The passage names no constituent. In general terms poultry wing is skin, connective tissue and a little muscle, which is why it is fatty and gelatinous when cooked. Burned feather is largely ash. If *banj* is henbane, that plant carries tropane alkaloids and is poisonous. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Canon treats the wing as an easily digested, nourishing food and records, at one remove ("it is said"), a purging bread made with burned dove feathers and *banj*. The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend the feather remedy, which may involve a poisonous plant. The terms of the *Canon* are the tradition's own and are not translated into modern diagnoses.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation given is the one quoted: wing feathers mixed with an equal amount of *banj*, burned, and used in bread. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] Do not use any amount from this entry.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Poultry wings are a common food rich in fat and protein. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Avicenna / Unani (CM).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested in the passage read. It says that the wing is nourishing and easily digested; it says nothing of the *ruh*, of innate heat or radical moisture, or of any life-lengthening status. Nothing is imported from outside the passage. The *Canon* is read here from the Arabic text on disk, not from the English adaptation.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do beyond ordinary food. The Canon's own reading is that hen and goose wings are good food. The feather-and-*banj* bread is reported by hearsay and is not for use.

### Contraindications

- **Feather and *banj* remedy.** Possibly poisonous (henbane) and strongly purging by the text's own account. Do not use.
- **Poultry hygiene.** As for any poultry: cook through. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The bird behind *waršān* is a gloss, not established by the text.
