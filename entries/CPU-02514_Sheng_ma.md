# Sheng ma — 升麻 *Shengma* (Shennong bencao jing), also 周升麻 *Zhou shengma*
## *Cimicifuga foetida L. (now often placed in Actaea), the usual source of the drug 升麻; our identification, of medium confidence: Cimicifuga dahurica and C. heracleifolia are also candidates, and the text gives no description to choose between them* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on one text, Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The plant behind the name is ours: the text prints no description, only a flavour, indications, an alias, a habitat and the month and part gathered, so it can neither confirm nor exclude a species.

**The Shennong text proper.** In Sun's edition the 味 paragraph reads 味甘，辛 ("sweet, acrid"). It "governs" 解百毒 ("resolves the hundred poisons"), 杀百老物殃鬼 (kills the "hundred old things" and calamity-ghosts), 辟温疾 ("wards off warm/epidemic illness") and 障邪毒蛊 ("screens off perverse poison and *gu*"). Its long-use clause reads 久服，不夭: "taken long: does not die young". It gives the alias 一名周升麻 and the habitat 生山谷, "grows in mountain valleys". (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 升麻 *shengma*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1061)

**Where Sun's text differs from the Dadguan edition.** Sun says in a bracketed note that the Dadguan edition reads the flavour as 甘，平 ("sweet, balanced"), gives the alias as 周麻, and words the indications differently (it has 杀百精老物殃鬼, 辟瘟疫瘴气、邪气虫毒: "kills the hundred essences, old things and calamity-ghosts; wards off epidemic disease, miasmic qi, perverse qi and worm-poison"). He says he used the wording quoted in the Taiping yulan (《御览》) instead. He also says that the old edition printed 生山谷 in black ink (the Mingyi layer), and that because Wu Pu quotes "Shennong: sweet" (神农：甘), the classic must have had that line, so he put the flavour and habitat back into the Shennong layer. The edition therefore rests on an editorial decision by Sun, not on an unbroken text.

**Wu Pu (3rd century, 《吴普》).** The only Wu Pu quotation is 神农：甘 ("Shennong: sweet"), from the Yulan.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 adds the origin 生益州 (Yizhou) and the harvest: 二月、八月采根，晒干, "root gathered in the second and eighth months, sun-dried". The part used is therefore stated only in this later layer; the Shennong paragraph names no part.

**Sun's note (案).** Sun cites the Guangya: 周麻，升麻也, "Zhou ma is Sheng ma" (taken from the Yulan). He adds no further gloss.

**Identity.** The alias 周升麻 and the Yizhou habitat are all the text gives. The modern drug 升麻 is the root of several *Cimicifuga* species; which one the classic meant is not known from these lines. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records a sweet, acrid drug against "poisons" and against "old things", calamity-ghosts, epidemic illness and *gu* poison, with a promise of "not dying young" for long use. These are the classic's own terms, not modern diagnoses, and the Codex does not turn them into protocols. The Codex gives no recommendation to take it.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives the part (the root, in the Mingyi layer only), the harvest months (second and eighth) and drying in the sun. It gives no decoction, powder, pill, quantity or course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

In general knowledge the root of *Cimicifuga* species is a long-standing article of Chinese pharmacy, and related *Actaea* species (black cohosh) are used in modern herbal products; the Shennong claims about "poisons" and "ghosts" are not modern outcomes and cannot be set beside any measured result. ### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The classic files 升麻 in the upper grade (上品); its preface says upper-grade drugs nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic, and may be taken for a long time. Its 久服 clause reads 久服，不夭 ("taken long: does not die young"), a promise about the span of life, which is what makes the register (a). The wording of the clause comes from the Yulan quotation that Sun preferred; the Dadguan wording Sun sets in brackets does not show a 久服 clause, so the (a) grade rests on Sun's chosen text. The Mingyi bielu, not the Shennong text, gives Yizhou as the origin and the months of harvest. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 升麻 *shengma*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1061)

**Practitioner's note.** A record, not a practice. The classic's long-use promise ("not dying young") is attached to the upper-grade drug, but the plant's identity within the genus is open, no preparation or dose is given, and the text's own concern is "poisons" and ill influences. Nothing here supports a cultivator taking the root.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** Several *Cimicifuga* species are candidates; do not use a plant on the strength of this entry.
- **Safety.** The text prints no warning, and no safety or pregnancy information was read. Related species are used in modern herbal products and have been the subject of safety reports; the Codex has not read any of them. [USER: verify]
- **Long use.** The classic's upper-grade promise is not a safety record.
