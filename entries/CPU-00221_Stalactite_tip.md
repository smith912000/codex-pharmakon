# Stalactite tip (Shi Zhong Ru) — Shi Zhong Ru, "Stalactitum" (Yang 1998); list row 石鐘乳
## *The stalactite in the Divine Farmer's Classic; Yang's footnote 53 says this medicinal "is obtained from the root of a stalactite" and, after Li Shi-zhen, that the nipple is Shi Zhong Ru, the body Kong Gong Nie and the root Yin Nie* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Shi Zhong Ru is printed in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) on PDF page 28, directly under the heading "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (line 1097). The text (lines 1107-1110): "Shi Zhong Ru (Stalactitum)53 is sweet and warm. It mainly treats cough and counterflow qi ascent. It brightens the eyes, boosts the essence, quiets the five viscera, frees the hundreds of joints, disinhibits the nine orifices, and promotes lactation. It is produced in mountains and valleys."

Yang's footnote 53 (lines 1120-1126, printed after the page's entries; the OCR is damaged): "This medicinal is obtained from the root of a stalactite. In differentiating Shi Zhong Ru and the next two medicinals, Li Shi-zhen, the great Ming dynasty pharmacologist, explains that a stalactite is like a breast. The nipple is Shi Zhong [Ru], the body is Kong Gong Nie, and the root is Yin Nie." The footnote is not self-consistent as printed: its first sentence says this medicinal comes from "the root", while Li Shizhen's image, which it then reports, puts Shi Zhong Ru at the nipple and the root at Yin Nie. The image is Li Shizhen's 16th-century explanation as Yang reports it, not the Classic's own text. The two further stalactite entries follow directly: Yin Nie (lines 1111-1114, "acrid and warm", for frostbite, blood stasis, dysentery, cold and heat and others; other name Jiang Shi, Ginger Stone) and Kong Gong Nie (from line 1115, "acrid and warm", for food damage, malign sores, flat abscesses, hemorrhoids and fistulas; it promotes lactation). ### Active Components

Stalactites are cave formations of calcium carbonate, CaCO3, usually the mineral calcite, deposited from water dripping from the cave roof; they grow slowly and are often layered. Traces of magnesium, iron and other elements may colour or flaw them. The sources read give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records the stone for cough and rising qi and says that it brightens the eyes, "boosts the essence", quiets the five viscera, frees the joints, opens the nine orifices and promotes lactation. The Codex does not endorse these claims and offers no protocol. Taking stalactites also means breaking a cave formation that cannot regrow on a human timescale, which is an argument against any use of them.

### Scientific Correlation

Calcium carbonate is an antacid and a dietary calcium source in general; it is the main ingredient of chalk, limestone and shell. A ground stalactite would be calcium carbonate with trace contaminants, and no property specific to the stalactite is established. No medical benefit of swallowing a stalactite for the other conditions the Classic names is established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- SBJ: "sweet and warm" (Yang line 1107). Class: Middle (heading line 1097). No other quality is printed. Yang's footnote 32 (lines 849-855) explains that his rendering omits the word "flavor" and gives "balanced" for neither cold nor hot; this entry is "warm".

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: a middle-grade drug (中品).* Shi Zhong Ru stands under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (Yang line 1097, the top of its page). Yang's preface says the medium class medicinals "mainly nurture personality and correspond to humanity. They may or may not be toxic, and [therefore,] one should weigh and ponder before putting them to their appropriate use. If one intends to control disease, supplement vacuity, and replenish exhaustion, one should base [one's efforts] on the middle class" (lines 385-390). Yang's own note on the class (lines 356-366) says middle class medicinals "are able to cultivate personality or modify temperament" and that "Since this class of medicinals may be toxic, their prescription requires care". The monograph prints "boosts the essence" and "quiets the five viscera" (line 1108); "essence" is Yang's English and no *jing* claim is made here beyond that printed word. Unlike the superior class, the middle class carries no promise that taking the drug is safe for long use, and the monograph prints no phrase about protracted taking, lightness or longevity. The grade is a statement of the Classic as translated by Yang in 1998 and is not carried forward from later materia medica works.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: not a "nourish life" drug for long use, but a drug for controlling disease and supplementing vacuity under care. Nothing in the sources supports taking a stalactite, and removing one damages a cave. For a cultivator the entry is a record of how the Classic ranked a stalactite's tip. ### Contraindications

Powdered stone sold as a remedy may be a different mineral or may be contaminated. Mineral medicines are unstandardised and their sale is regulated in many places. Cave formations are protected in many countries and collecting them is restricted or prohibited. [USER: verify] the legal status in any given place; the sources retrieved do not address it. A large dose of calcium carbonate can cause constipation and, over time, excess calcium.
