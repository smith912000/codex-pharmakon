### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese (Shennong / SBJ):** Bitter apricot kernel (*kǔ xìng rén*, 苦杏仁) is one of the oldest continuously used Chinese cough medicines, entered in the early materia medica tradition attributed to Shennong and formalised across later bencao. Its classical role is to **descend rebellious Lung qi** — the mechanism TCM assigns to cough and wheezing — and to **moisten the intestines** in dryness-type constipation, exploiting the seed's high fixed-oil content. It is a component of canonical formulas such as *Ma Huang Tang* (with ephedra, for wind-cold wheezing) and *Sang Ju Yin*. Two grades are distinguished: bitter kernels (*kǔ xìng rén*, medicinal, higher amygdalin) and sweet kernels (*tián xìng rén*, food-grade, low amygdalin). Classical practice **always processes** bitter kernels — blanching, de-tipping, decoction — which volatilises and hydrolyses much of the cyanogenic load.

**Assyrian / Mesopotamian (AT):** Rosaceous stone-fruit kernels of the apricot/almond type appear in the Mesopotamian medical corpus among aromatic and fixed-oil drug materials. Attribution at the exact-species level is uncertain for antiquity — *Prunus armeniaca* and bitter almond (*Prunus dulcis* var. *amara*) were not sharply distinguished — so treat the Assyrian tag as **genus/kernel-tradition level**, not a modern binomial identification. `[USER: verify]` the specific cuneiform drug-list entry if a precise citation is needed.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Kernel that Descends:** The apricot kernel sits at a doctrinal knife-edge that the Codex treats honestly: it is simultaneously *medicine and poison*, the same molecule (amygdalin) that "moves qi downward" also releasing hydrogen cyanide, the most downward, silencing agent in metabolism — it halts the electron-transport chain, the body's own respiratory fire. The classical insistence on **processing before use** is not superstition but an encoded pharmacovigilance rule: the tradition knew the raw kernel could kill and built preparation into doctrine. Read symbolically, *xìng rén* is the discipline of the descending breath — useful only when the descent is controlled.

### Active Components

- **Amygdalin** (mandelonitrile-β-gentiobioside) — the cyanogenic diglucoside; on hydrolysis by β-glucosidase (emulsin, present in the kernel and gut flora) it yields glucose, benzaldehyde, and **hydrogen cyanide (HCN)**. Concentrated in *bitter* kernels.
- **Prunasin** — the monoglucoside intermediate in the cyanogenic pathway.
- **Fixed oil (~40–50%)** — oleic and linoleic acid triglycerides; the basis of the intestine-moistening action.
- **Benzaldehyde** — the almond-scent aglycone fragment.
- **Emulsin (β-glucosidase)** — endogenous enzyme that liberates cyanide once the kernel is crushed/wetted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Positioned as a `[RESET]` respiratory-descent agent: traditional use is antitussive and mild bowel-moistening in dry, downward-stuck patterns. **Kenetica does not endorse amygdalin/"vitamin B17" as an anti-cancer agent** — the evidence is negative and the toxicity real (see Scientific Correlation). ### Scientific Correlation

Attribution: findings from PubMed.

- **Anti-cancer claims — negative.** A Cochrane systematic review found **no** RCT evidence that laetrile/amygdalin benefits cancer patients and flagged a "considerable risk of serious adverse effects from cyanide poisoning," concluding the risk–benefit balance is "unambiguously negative" (Milazzo & Horneber, 2015, *Cochrane Database Syst Rev*) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005476.pub4).
- **In-vitro anti-proliferative signal only.** Apricot and peach kernel extracts showed dose- and time-dependent, biphasic effects on HT-29 colon cancer cells in culture, with a possible S-phase block — a **preclinical, cell-line** observation, not a clinical result (Cassiem & de Kock, 2019, *BMC Complement Altern Med*) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-019-2437-4).
- **Documented toxicity.** A 4-year-old given oral and IV amygdalin plus apricot kernels developed severe cyanide poisoning (encephalopathy, lactic acidosis, markedly elevated serum cyanide), reversed by sodium thiosulfate (Sauer et al., 2015, *Wien Med Wochenschr*) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10354-014-0340-7).

**Evidence grade:** Anti-cancer efficacy — **none / evidence against.** Antitussive traditional use — traditional, not RCT-supported here. Toxicity — well documented.

### Contraindications

- **Cyanide toxicity — primary hazard.** Raw bitter kernels can be lethal; poisoning is documented (Sauer 2015, above). Never self-dose raw kernels; never treat "vitamin B17" as safe.
- **Never use as a cancer therapy** — no efficacy evidence, real harm (Milazzo & Horneber 2015).
- **Children** — especially vulnerable; case above was a 4-year-old.
- **Pregnancy/lactation** — avoid medicinal bitter kernel; cyanogenic risk.
- **Interactions** — additive risk with other cyanogenic sources; antidote pathway (thiosulfate/hydroxocobalamin) is emergency medicine, not routine.
- **Regulatory** — bitter apricot kernel and amygdalin are restricted/scheduled in several countries.
