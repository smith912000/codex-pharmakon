# Kadamba — Kadamba, Nīpa, Priyaka, Halipriya, Vṛttapuṣpa (Sanskrit); Kadam, Burflower tree

## *Neolamarckia cadamba* (Roxb.) Bosser | Family: Rubiaceae
**(syn. *Anthocephalus cadamba*, *Anthocephalus chinensis*)**
**Tradition Tags:** CM, SS
**Tags (previous line, retained for reference):** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā), CS (Caraka Saṃhitā); Nighaṇṭu literature; Purāṇic/devotional corpus
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa — Kaṣāya (astringent), Tikta (bitter), Madhura (sweet, secondary); Vīrya — Śīta (cooling); Vipāka — Kaṭu (pungent); Guṇa — Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry). Pacifies Pitta and Kapha; the astringent-dry character can aggravate Vāta.
**Training Phase:** Stabilisation / Astringent-restorative — a cooling astringent for flux states (diarrhoea, discharge, metabolic excess).

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** Kadamba (also *Nīpa*) appears in the classical materia medica as a cooling astringent tree, its **bark and fruit** used for their kaṣāya-śīta action. The tradition directs it at *atisāra* (diarrhoea/flux), *pravāhikā* (dysentery-type states), and as a *stambhaka* (binding/holding) and *śothahara* (anti-oedema) agent. Its astringency lends it to conditions of excess discharge and loose, hot flux; its cooling potency suits the associated Pitta component. External and decoction uses for wounds and inflamed tissue follow from the same astringent-cooling logic.

**Caraka Saṃhitā (CS) & Nighaṇṭus:** The wider corpus and the Nighaṇṭus additionally attribute *mūtrala* (diuretic) and metabolic actions, and the plant is folk-associated with *madhumeha* / *prameha* (the diabetic-type polyuric disorders) — an antidiabetic reputation that persists in ethnomedicine across the subcontinent. Bhāvaprakāśa and allied texts preserve the astringent-cooling identity.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Kṛṣṇa Tree:** No account of Kadamba is complete without its devotional dimension, which in this tradition **is** part of its identity, not decoration. The Kadamba is **Kṛṣṇa's tree**: the *rāsa-līlā*, Kṛṣṇa's divine play with the gopīs on the banks of the Yamunā, is set beneath and within the Kadamba groves of Vṛndāvana; the iconography of Kṛṣṇa playing the flute in a Kadamba tree, or leaping from it into the river to subdue the serpent Kāliya, is one of the most reproduced images in the Vaiṣṇava corpus. The tree's spherical, orange, densely-many-flowered head — a globe of fused fragrant florets bursting simultaneously — became a symbol of **fullness, monsoon abundance, and erotic-devotional longing** (its flowering coincides with the rains, the season of union). The name *Halipriya* ("dear to Balarāma/the plough-bearer") and *Priyaka* ("the beloved") carry the same charge. For Kenetica this matters doctrinally: Kadamba is the plant of **fullness that is nonetheless bound** — a cooling astringent whose symbolic register is monsoon plenitude and divine union, and whose physiological register is the *holding and cooling of overflow* (flux, discharge, polyuria). The sphere that contains many is the visual emblem of a drug that gathers and binds what would otherwise pour out.

### Active Components
Phytochemical screening of *N. cadamba* reports **phenolics, flavonoids, tannins and saponins** in leaf and fruit extracts; identified phenolics in the ripe fruit include **caffeic acid, tannic acid, syringic acid and quercetin** (see Scientific Correlation). The genus is characterised by **indole alkaloids** of the cadambine type (e.g. cadambine, cadamine, isodihydrocadambine) reported in bark and other parts. The tannin fraction is the material basis of the astringent (stambhaka) action; the phenolic/flavonoid fraction underlies antioxidant and antibacterial behaviour.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica
Kadamba is positioned as a **cooling astringent for flux and discharge states** — the reference agent for hot, loose diarrhoea/dysentery patterns and, in the ethnomedical register, for the polyuric metabolic (prameha/madhumeha) cluster. It gathers and binds (stambhana) while cooling associated heat, and its antioxidant/antibacterial fraction supports the anti-flux use. It is a stabilisation-phase agent — used to hold and settle overflow rather than to nourish or stimulate. The antidiabetic reputation is real in tradition but scientifically underpowered (see grade).

### Scientific Correlation
According to PubMed:

- Aqueous **fruit extracts of *N. cadamba*** (ripe fruit) showed the highest phenolic content (caffeic, tannic and syringic acids, quercetin) with **DPPH/ABTS antioxidant activity** and **antibacterial activity** (membrane-permeabilising mechanism, leakage of genetic material) — supporting the antioxidant/antimicrobial basis of traditional use and a role as a natural preservative. Pandey & Negi, 2017, *Natural Product Research* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2017.1323209).
- A **methanolic leaf extract** demonstrated **antiproliferative / pro-apoptotic activity** against human glioblastoma (U-87 MG) cells (dose- and time-dependent cytotoxicity, AO/PI-confirmed apoptosis, relative cytoselectivity vs. Vero cells), with phytochemistry showing phenolics, flavonoids, tannins and saponins — the authors, however, characterise the therapeutic potential as *low*. Muhamad et al., 2025, *Med. J. Malaysia* (PMID 41447003).

**Evidence grade — Low (preclinical, in vitro).** Both citations are laboratory studies (antioxidant/antibacterial assays; a cancer-cell cytotoxicity screen the authors themselves rate as low-potential). **No study retrieved here validates the antidiabetic (prameha/madhumeha) claim** — that indication remains traditionally/ethnomedically grounded only and should not be presented as scientifically established. No human clinical evidence is cited.

### Traditional Energetic Classification
- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṣāya (astringent), Tikta (bitter), Madhura (sweet, secondary)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry)
- **Doṣa action:** Pitta-Kapha-śāmaka (pacifies Pitta and Kapha); may aggravate Vāta via dryness/astringency
- **Dhātu (tissue) focus:** Rasa, Rakta; acts on the *purīṣavaha* (faecal) and *mūtravaha* (urinary) channels
- **Karma (actions):** Stambhaka / Grāhī (binding, anti-diarrhoeal), Śothahara (anti-oedema), Mūtrala (diuretic, per tradition), Prameha-hara (per tradition), Vraṇaropaṇa (wound-healing, external), Dāha-praśamana (relieves burning)
- **Srotas:** Purīṣavaha, Mūtravaha; Raktavaha (external)

### Contraindications
- Astringent-dry character can **aggravate Vāta**, constipation, and dryness — combine with appropriate anupāna in Vāta constitutions.
- The **antidiabetic use is not scientifically validated** here; anyone using it for glycaemic control must not substitute it for prescribed treatment and must monitor with a clinician — `[USER: verify with clinician]`.
- **Pregnancy/lactation:** safety not established from the evidence retrieved; avoid self-directed use.
- Persistent or severe diarrhoea/dysentery requires medical evaluation (dehydration, infection risk), not self-treatment.
