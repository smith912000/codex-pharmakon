# Aparajita — Clitoria ternatea (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna, Appendix 3)

## *Clitoria ternatea L., butterfly pea. The identification is the translator's Appendix 3, which pairs the name printed "Aparajit£i" with "Clitoria Ternatia" by position; the pairing was checked in column order and holds. In the chapter text the name Aparajita is also the name of an insect (Kalpa ch. 8), so the plant is identified by the index and by two translator's glosses, not by the bare word. Confidence medium.* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta Samhita (Bhishagratna) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry.

**Sushruta (SS), the Appendix index.** The Appendix 3 index in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` names block (line 31255) runs: Amlika, Amra, Amrataka, Amrita, Ananta / Anantamula (printed together), Ankotaka, Apamarga, Aparajita (printed "Aparajit£i"), Aragvadha, Aravinda. The identification block runs: Tamarind, Mango, Spondias mangifera, "See Guduchi", Hemidesmus indicus, Alangium hexapetalum, Achyranthes aspera, "Clitoria Ternatia" (line 31321), Cassia fistula, "See Ambhoja". Every pair agrees in order (Amrita is "See Guduchi", Aragvadha is Cassia fistula, Aravinda cross-refers to Ambhoja), so Aparajita is paired with Clitoria.

**Sushruta (SS), the chapters.** The bare word *Aparajita* in the chapter text is not the plant in every place. At `kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch08.txt` line 135 it names an insect: "The Trikantaka, Kuni, Hasti-kaksha and the Aparajita are the four kinds of insects that belong to the Kanabha group", which is not a plant use. The plant appears in the translator's glosses:

- **Kalpa ch. 2** (`kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch02.txt` line 325), in a gruel for poisoning, between the stages of poisoning: decoctions of Koshataki, Agnika, Patha, Suryavalli, Amrita, Abhaya, Shirisha, "Kinihi, Shelu, Giryahva (OCR 'Girydhvd'), (white Aparajita)", the two kinds of Rajani, and others, "mixed with honey and clarified butter. This is beneficial in both the cases of (animal and vegetable) poisoning."
- **Uttara ch. 61** (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch61.txt` line 155), in the Siddharthaka ghrita: a ghee cooked with, among others, "Giri-karpa (white Aparajita)" (the OCR's spelling; the intended Sanskrit name is not restored here) and the seeds of Karanja and Shirisha, with cow's urine, "which proves curative in cases of poisoning, worms (in the intestines), Kushtha, asthma, derangements of Kapha, Vishama-Jvara, Bhuta-graha, insanity and Apasmara" (lines 150-158).
- **Chikitsa ch. 1** footnote (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch01.txt` line 7): the word "Sveta" in the text "may either be adjective to 'Durva' and mean 'white' or it may mean white Vacha or white Aparajita or white Durva." The footnote is a translator's alternative reading, not a statement of the text.

Where the text says "white Aparajita", the gloss names the plant by its colour, and whether the white form is the same as the species indexed is not stated. [USER: verify.]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Clitoria ternatea* is a climbing legume known for deep blue flowers coloured by anthocyanin pigments. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says.** The (white) Aparajita is one ingredient in a poisoning gruel (Kalpa 2) and in a medicated ghee (Uttara 61); the ghee's stated actions apply to the whole compound.
- **What the text does not say.** It gives no property of the plant alone, and no dose.

The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

Two forms are given: a decoction with others, taken as a gruel with honey and ghee (Kalpa 2); and a ghee cooked with the others and cow's urine (Uttara 61). The Uttara footnote notes that Dallana has the ghee cooked in a decoction of Krishna and Vacha with the Aragvadhadi drugs as paste. No quantity or dose is given in the lines read. ### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The plant appears (as "white Aparajita" in the translator's gloss) in a poisoning gruel and in a medicated ghee. No line read places it in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no statement about *ojas* is attached to it.

**Practitioner's note.** A minor ingredient in compound formulae for poisoning and skin disease, identified only through the translator. Nothing in the record supports a cultivation use.

### Contraindications

- **Poisoning, Kushtha, insanity.** Medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Cow's urine ghee.** Compound ghrita preparations are not for self-preparation.
- **Homonym.** The name also denotes an insect in Kalpa ch. 8; do not read those lines as the plant.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]
