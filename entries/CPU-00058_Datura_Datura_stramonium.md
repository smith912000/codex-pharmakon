# Datura — thornapple, jimsonweed, devil's trumpet, the deliriant nightshade

## *Datura stramonium* L. — the tropane-bearing weed of delirium, antispasmodic and poison

### Origin & Tradition

Datura is a rank, fast-growing annual of waste ground with large trumpet flowers and a spiny seed capsule (the "thornapple"). It is one of the great **tropane-alkaloid nightshades**, kin to henbane and mandrake (CPU-00043, CPU-00044) and belladonna (I_031), and like them it has a double history as medicine and as poison/intoxicant. The genus is pantropical/temperate and the precise classical referents are tangled with the other Solanaceae, but datura-type plants appear across the world's pharmacopoeias wherever the genus grows. In **Ayurveda** *Dhattura* is a recognised (and carefully handled) toxic medicine for respiratory and painful conditions and is associated with Shiva in ritual use; in folk traditions worldwide datura served as a deliriant in shamanic, divinatory and — notoriously — criminal contexts (stupefaction and robbery).

The plant's most respectable and enduring medical use is as a **bronchodilating antispasmodic for asthma.** The dried leaves of datura (and the related *Datura/Brugmansia* and stramonium species) were smoked or burned and the fumes inhaled to relieve asthmatic bronchospasm — the "asthma cigarettes" and "asthma powders" that were a mainstay of respiratory medicine into the twentieth century. The atropine-type alkaloids relax bronchial smooth muscle and dry secretions, a genuine and rational effect that anticipated modern anticholinergic bronchodilators. Datura preparations were also used (cautiously) as antispasmodics, analgesics and in the soporific/anaesthetic traditions alongside henbane, mandrake and opium.

But datura is **dangerously toxic and a frequent cause of serious and fatal poisoning**, especially among young people seeking its hallucinogenic effects. The intoxication is a true **anticholinergic delirium** — the classic toxidrome: "hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter" — and it is unpleasant, dangerous and unpredictable, with a narrow margin between effect and lethality. The plant therefore stands firmly as specialist/archival material.

### Active Components

- **Atropine (DL-hyoscyamine) and L-hyoscyamine** — the principal tropane alkaloids; **competitive antagonists at muscarinic acetylcholine receptors** (anticholinergic). They relax smooth muscle (bronchodilation, antispasmodic), dry secretions, dilate the pupil, raise heart rate and, centrally, produce delirium and hallucination in toxic doses.
- **Scopolamine (hyoscine)** — tropane alkaloid with stronger central (CNS-depressant, amnestic, deliriant) effects; contributes to the stupefying/"zombie" reputation and to the antiemetic/sedative actions of the tropanes.
- **Other minor tropanes** — contributing to the alkaloid profile, which **varies enormously by plant part, age and growing conditions** — a key reason for the unpredictability and danger. [USER: do not quote concentration figures without verification.]
- **Distribution:** alkaloids occur throughout the plant; the **seeds** are a common vehicle of poisoning.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Recorded as **specialist and archival material.** Datura is not a self-administered herb; recreational use is a frequent cause of severe poisoning and death.

1. **Antispasmodic bronchodilator for asthma (historical/specialist).** The defining medical use — inhaled fumes relaxing bronchospasm; the antecedent of modern anticholinergic bronchodilators.
2. **Antispasmodic / analgesic (historical).** Cautious use for spasmodic and painful conditions; part of the soporific/anaesthetic tradition with henbane, mandrake, opium.
3. **Anticholinergic pharmacology (research/medical lineage).** The plant is a natural source of atropine and scopolamine, themselves essential modern drugs (bradycardia, organophosphate poisoning, motion sickness, premedication) — used as purified agents, not crude plant.
4. **Ritual/divinatory deliriant (ethnographic).** Documented traditional psychoactive use; dangerous; recorded, not endorsed.
5. **Criminal stupefaction (documented).** The "robbery/zombie" misuse — a forensic, not therapeutic, note.

### Preparation & Dosing

**No therapeutic home dose is given. Datura has a narrow margin, wildly variable alkaloid content, and causes frequent fatal poisoning.**

- **Purified alkaloids (pharmaceutical):** atropine and scopolamine are precisely-dosed modern drugs — the only legitimate therapeutic form of datura's chemistry. [USER: do not reproduce dosing; prescription-only.]
- **Crude plant (leaf/seed):** a poison; the historical "asthma cigarettes/powders" are obsolete and were themselves hazardous. No figures reproduced.
- **No recreational or casual use.** The variable potency makes any self-dosing a gamble with delirium and death.

Safety framing: anticholinergic poisoning (hyperthermia, mydriasis, dry skin/mouth, flushing, urinary retention, tachycardia, delirium, seizures, coma) is a medical emergency; physostigmine is an antidote used only in controlled settings. Any significant ingestion requires urgent care.

### Cultivation Register

**Ancient Egyptian (EP).** No cultivation framing is attested, and the tag itself is doubtful: *Datura stramonium* is a New-World species with no secure presence in the pharaonic record, so any Egyptian identification is a modern back-projection rather than an attested assignment. The Ebers-type corpus is indication-organised in any case — it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the *ka*/*ba*/*ib*. Absent.

**Assyrian (AT).** Absent, and under the standing blocking warning for all Mesopotamian substance identifications. The Old-World *banj* narcotic complex of the Nineveh tablets covers henbane and mandrake; extending it to a New-World *Datura* is a species transfer, not an attestation. No causal-agency classification survives that can be tied to this plant.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides has no subtle-body system to draw on, and his *strykhnos*-group nightshades are debated Old-World Solanaceae rather than New-World *D. stramonium*. What the tradition genuinely offers a cultivator here is his discipline of attention — provenance, assay, and the emphatic recording of lethal danger. No cultivation status; the honest register is his warning.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Attested only through the Old-World *banj* category, not for this species; no *mufarrih* or innate-heat framing is recorded for it. Because the Avicennan cultivation idea is conservation of a finite innate heat, a deliriant poison sits outside it entirely — it depletes, it does not conserve. Framed, status absent.

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Dhattūra* is a recognised toxic drug (*visha*), handled only after *shodhana* purification — and *shodhana* here is measurable, not merely ritual. But the Indian *dhattūra* is chiefly *Datura metel*/*innoxia*, not *stramonium*, and the classification is therapeutic-toxic: it carries **no** *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* status. Framed as a purified *visha*; no cultivation status attested.

**Tibetan (FMT).** Absent. No formal category of "substances for subtle-body practice" exists to place it in, and medicine is in any case the third of four treatment branches — supporting practice, not reaching its root. Any use would be therapeutic and tightly controlled.

**Nahua / Badianus (BM).** This is the species' own home ground and the one attested ritual assignment: *toloatzin* / *tlapatl* (*Datura* spp.) is documented in Nahua ethnohistory as a divinatory and insight-seeking agent, and as a plant of dangerous, "evil" association (López Austin, *The Human Body and Ideology*, 1988; and *Tamoanchan, Tlalocan*, 1993). The caution the spec requires: the Badianus manuscript is itself naturalistic and does **not** contain the *tonalli*/*teyolia*/*ihiyotl* model — that model is reconstructed from later ethnohistory, so the *ritual* use is attested while the vital-substrate assignment is a scholarly reconstruction, not a period claim.

**South American (SA).** Attestation is ethnographic, not textual, and it mostly attaches to *Brugmansia* (*toé*) rather than *Datura stramonium*; within Amazonian *vegetalismo* such plants are *plantas maestras* — teacher-agents met through the *dieta* of restriction. Stated as a generalisation across named practices, not a pan-continental system, and with the species caveat flagged. Framed; not securely this species.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this plant. Across every tradition that genuinely attests it, *Datura* is a poison or a specialist divinatory agent handled only by initiated specialists — never a nourish-life or constitution-building substance. The one attested ritual register (Nahua) belongs to a closed specialist role, and this entry's own toxicology is the reason: a deliriant with a lethal margin. The register's answer is abstention.

### Contraindications

- **Recreational/self-administered use — absolute prohibition.** Narrow margin, variable potency, frequent fatal/severe poisoning.
- **Glaucoma (angle-closure).** Anticholinergic mydriasis can precipitate acute angle-closure — absolute contraindication.
- **Prostatic enlargement / urinary retention; paralytic ileus; tachyarrhythmia.** Anticholinergic effects aggravate all of these.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding — avoid.**
- **Heat / dehydration.** Anticholinergic anhidrosis causes dangerous hyperthermia.
- **Drug interactions.** Additive with other anticholinergics (antihistamines, tricyclics, some antipsychotics).
- **No crude-plant or casual use whatsoever.** Specialist/archival framing throughout.
