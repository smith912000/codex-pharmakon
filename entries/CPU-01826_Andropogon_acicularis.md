# Andropogon acicularis — Amarapushpi, Choraka, Chora-pushpi (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3)

## *Andropogon acicularis, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Andropogon acicularis.", line 31225) against the Sanskrit name Amarapushpi (line 31173) and, on a later page, a second time (OCR "Andropogon Aciculaiis.", line 31558) against Choraka and Chora-pushpi (lines 31530 and 31532). The pairing of each Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The one Sanskrit name among these that the chapters print is Choraka, and the identification of Choraka with this grass is the appendix's alone.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. A search of the name lists of the Dioscorides, Shennong, Ebers, Assyrian and Tibetan source folders for *Andropogon* found nothing. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. Lines 461 and 462 read "Choraka" and "Chora-pushpij", appendix line 31530, Latin line 31558, "Andropogon Aciculaiis.", as rows of class "other" (the OCR spelling of the Latin did not match the spine binomial). (31222), **"Andropogon acicularis."** (31225), "Nelumbium speciosum." (31227), "Stephania hernandifolia." (31229), "Pavonia odorata." (31231), "See Changeri." (31233), "Rumex vesicarius." (31235). **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The names in the chapters.* A search of the chapter files, allowing for the OCR's accents, found no occurrence of Amarapushpi or Chora-pushpi. Choraka is printed on three lines, and the compound Chorakapatra on one. The passages were read.

*A group (Sutra 38, lines 164 to 171; Vol. I raw text lines 15505 to 15520).* Choraka is named in the Eladi group: "Ela, Tagara, Kushtha, Mamsi, Dhyamaka, Tvaka, Patra, Naga-pushpa, Priyangu, Harenuka, Vyaghranakha, Shukti, Chanda, Sthauneyaka, Shriveshtaka, Chocha, Choraka, Valaka, Guggulu, Sarjarasa, Turushka, Kunduruka, Aguru, Sprikka, Ushira, Bhadradaru, Kumkuma, Punnaga and Keshara". Of the group: "The therapeutic virtue of the group consists in subduing the action of Vayu and Kapham and in neutralising the effects of poison. It is a cosmetic and arrests the eruption of pimples and other vegetations on the skin such as rash, urticaria etc. and checks the itching sensation incidental thereto." The chapter file `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` splices this sentence at a page break (lines 173 to 179) and continues it with the text of another group, so the continuation is read from the Vol. I text.

*An oil (Chikitsa 37, lines 68 to 78).* In the chapter on the *Anuvasana* and *Uttara* injections, the "Third Taila" lists some thirty drugs, among them "Manjishtha, Sathi, Pushkara and Taskara (Choraka)" (the bracket is the translator's), "pasted together and cooked with the proper quantity of milk and oil. The oil thus prepared is highly efficacious in the disorders of the deranged Vayu."

*An antivenom (Kalpa 5, lines 505 to 512).* In the chapter on snake-bite the *Ekasara Agada* is made of "Somaraji seeds and Somaraji flowers, Katabhi, Sindhuvara, Choraka, Varuna, Kushtha, Sarpagandha, Saptala, Punarnava, flowers of Shirisha, Aragvadha and of Arka, Shyama, Ambashtha, Vidanga, Amra, Ashmantaka, black earth and Kuravaka"; "These should be applied singly or in combination of two or three to destroy the effects of poison."

*A different name.* "Chorakapatra" is named in the Nyagrodhadi group (Sutra 38, line 347). The compound is not the same Sanskrit name as Choraka or Amarapushpi, and the appendix lines read do not pair it with this Latin, so it is not used.

*What the chapters do not say.* A search of the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) found none of the names. No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Choraka in the passages read. The text does not say which part of Choraka is used. The identification of Choraka with a grass is the appendix's alone. **Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Andropogon acicularis*, *Amarapushpi*, *Choraka* and *Chora-pushpi* found no entry on this grass. The name Choraka is, however, used in other entries as a reading of a different plant. I_010 and I_011 (*Angelica sinensis*) name *Choraka* in their Sushruta paragraphs as a possible reading of *Angelica glauca*; I_1231 (*Angelica glauca*) records that the Sushruta files print *Choraka* (for example `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 168) and print no Latin name for it, and does not make that reading. The Appendix No. 3 pairing used here prints *Andropogon acicularis* against the same Sanskrit name. The chapter passages above are therefore the passages that those entries' Choraka reading would rest on, under a different Latin reading, and the choice between them is left to the parent. I_899 (Indian Snakeroot, Sarpagandha) quotes the same *Ekasara Agada* of Kalpa 5; I_908, I_909, I_910 and I_1370 quote the Nyagrodhadi group with the different name Chorakapatra. Nothing is merged. ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Andropogon* is a genus of grasses (Poaceae). Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Choraka as a member of the Eladi group, as an ingredient of a Vayu-quieting oil used for injection, and as one of the drugs of the *Ekasara Agada*. These are the text's statements about a Sanskrit name, and the Latin name the appendix attaches to it is the translator's, by position. Nothing is recorded for Amarapushpi. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives these preparations, each with its locus. The Third Taila, a milk-and-oil preparation used as an injection (Chikitsa 37, lines 68 to 78). The *Ekasara Agada*, applied "singly or in combination of two or three" (Kalpa 5, lines 505 to 512). No quantity of Choraka is given in these passages. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** the passages read print for Choraka its place in the Eladi group, whose stated virtue is "subduing the action of Vayu and Kapham", neutralising poison, and being "a cosmetic" that checks pimples, rash and itching (Sutra 38, Vol. I raw lines 15513 to 15520). No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Choraka or Amarapushpi in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text names Choraka as a group member and as an ingredient in an injection oil and an antivenom; it names Amarapushpi nowhere. A search of the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) found none of the names, so the text assigns the plant no rejuvenative or generative status in the lines retrieved. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The Latin identification is the appendix's. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. In the Sushruta scheme Choraka is a group member and a component of a physician's oil and antivenom, and the identity of the plant is not settled. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of a Latin name that the chapters do not give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read print no warning for the drug itself. The antivenom use describes an emergency that needs medical care, and the injection use is a physician's procedure. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
