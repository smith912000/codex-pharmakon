# Diviner's Sage / Salvia divinorum

## *Salvia divinorum* Epling & Játiva | Family: Lamiaceae

**Tradition Tags:** BM, SA
**Energetic Classification:** Not applicable — a Mesoamerican ritual plant standing outside the classical humoral systems; any hot/cold or moist/dry assignment here is inferred and non-canonical (NA).
**Training Phase:** Reference — Entheogen / Restricted Study

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Salvia divinorum* is a New World plant of the Sierra Mazateca in Oaxaca, southern **Mexico**. Its only securely documented traditional use is among the **Mazatec** people, who know it as *Ska María Pastora* — the "leaves of the Shepherdess," or Herb of the Virgin Mary — and employ it in divinatory and healing ritual conducted by a curandero, typically to diagnose illness or seek guidance. The plant entered the Western record through the ethnomycologist R. Gordon Wasson in the mid-twentieth century, and its principal active compound was later isolated and characterised largely through the work of Daniel Siebert and others. It is a member of the mint family (Lamiaceae) and, notably, rarely sets viable seed; traditional stands are propagated vegetatively.

**⚠️ Attestation correction (required).** The Kenetica spine currently tags this entry **BM** (Badianus manuscript) and **SA** (South America). **Both tags are disputed and should not be read as established fact.**

- **BM (Badianus):** *Salvia divinorum* is **not securely identified** in the Badianus manuscript (the 1552 Aztec *Libellus de Medicinalibus Indorum Herbis*). Proposals linking the plant to Aztec herbals such as the Nahuatl *pipiltzintzintli* remain contested and unproven. Treat any pre-Columbian textual attestation as **unconfirmed**.
- **SA (South America):** This is **geographically incorrect**. *Salvia divinorum* is a **Mesoamerican** plant native to **Mexico**, not South America. Its documented tradition is Mazatec (Oaxaca), not Andean or Amazonian.

Both tags are retained in the header only because the index carries them, and are flagged here as **[USER: verify]**. The accurate provenance is: **Mesoamerican / Mazatec / Mexican**, with no confirmed ancient documentary attestation.

### Active Components

The single compound responsible for the plant's psychoactivity is **salvinorin A**, a **neoclerodane diterpene**. It is pharmacologically remarkable for two reasons. First, it is described in the literature as the most potent naturally occurring hallucinogen yet identified. Second — and unusually for a psychoactive of this class — it is **non-nitrogenous**: it contains no nitrogen atom and is therefore **not an alkaloid**, unlike almost every other well-known plant hallucinogen. According to PubMed, salvinorin A is a neoclerodane (clerodane) diterpene and the major active constituent of the leaf ([Grundmann et al., 2007, *Planta Medica*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2007-981566); [Listos et al., 2011, *Pharmacological Reports*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1734-1140(11)70694-6)). Its rigid diterpene scaffold is chemically distinct from all classical opioid ligands, which has made it a new starting point ("scaffold") for medicinal-chemistry work ([Butelman & Kreek, 2015, *Frontiers in Pharmacology*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2015.00190)).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Within Kenetica this entry exists as an **ethnobotanical and pharmacological reference only**. It documents what the plant is, where its tradition sits, and what the peer-reviewed science says about its mechanism. It does **not** describe how to prepare, take, or dose the plant or its extracts, and it assigns the plant **no practical role** in any Kenetica protocol.

The reported acute experience is one of intense, abrupt, and **strongly dissociative** alteration of consciousness — loss of contact with ordinary reality, distorted perception of body and space, and in traditional Mazatec use a state sought under the guidance of an experienced practitioner within a structured ritual container. Kenetica records this ethnographically; it does not endorse or facilitate self-experimentation. There is **no demonstrated medical or therapeutic indication** for the plant in humans.

### Preparation & Dosing

**No preparation method, dose, route, or administration guidance is given for this plant — deliberately.** Two reasons: (1) legal status is restrictive and highly variable across jurisdictions, and (2) the acute effects are powerfully disorienting and carry real risk of injury and psychiatric harm. This section is intentionally left as a statement of omission rather than a dosing table. Anyone with a scholarly, clinical, or harm-reduction need should consult qualified professionals and the primary literature directly.

### Scientific Correlation

The scientific story here is genuinely distinctive and well characterised. Salvinorin A does **not** act like classical psychedelics: it is **not** a serotonin 5-HT2A agonist. Instead it is a highly selective, high-efficacy **agonist at the kappa-opioid receptor (KOR / KOPr)** — the principal receptor for the endogenous dynorphin peptides. This places it in a completely separate pharmacological class from LSD, psilocybin, or mescaline.

According to PubMed:

- Salvinorin A is a **highly selective, non-nitrogenous kappa-opioid receptor agonist** — the first such natural product identified — and this KOR selectivity is the accepted mechanism of its hallucinogenic action ([Yan & Roth, 2004, *Life Sciences*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lfs.2004.07.008); [Grundmann et al., 2007, *Planta Medica*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2007-981566)).
- It is a **selective, high-efficacy KOPr agonist** and, being a non-nitrogenous neoclerodane, provides a **novel medicinal-chemistry scaffold** for exploring KOR-mediated effects distinct from all prior opioid ligands ([Butelman & Kreek, 2015, *Frontiers in Pharmacology*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2015.00190)).
- Structure-activity work on salvinorin analogues continues to probe KOR affinity, potency, and selectivity, framing the receptor as **an emerging target for next-generation analgesics** ([Roach & Shenvi, 2018, *Bioorganic & Medicinal Chemistry Letters*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bmcl.2018.03.029)).
- Preclinical (animal) studies report a **rapid onset and short distribution/elimination half-lives**, consistent with the very brief duration of effect and rapid metabolism reported for the compound ([Grundmann et al., 2007, *Planta Medica*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2007-981566); [Listos et al., 2011, *Pharmacological Reports*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1734-1140(11)70694-6)).
- KOR/dynorphin signalling is a powerful counter-modulator of dopaminergic reward pathways; in preclinical models KOR activation (including by salvinorin A) tends to produce **aversion and anhedonia** rather than reward, which is why the compound is studied as a template for possible mood, pain, and addiction research — as an **early, preclinical scientific interest, not an established therapy** ([Butelman & Kreek, 2015, *Frontiers in Pharmacology*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2015.00190); [Listos et al., 2011, *Pharmacological Reports*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1734-1140(11)70694-6)).

On the **risk** side, the clinical literature is cautionary. According to PubMed, reviews note that although salvinorin A has often been assumed to have low addictive potential and low toxicity, recent findings **suggest it can precipitate psychiatric symptoms and negatively affect cognition** ([Mahendran et al., 2015, *Asia-Pacific Psychiatry*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/appy.12225)). (That same review repeats the erroneous "South America" provenance — a further reason the SA tag should be corrected rather than propagated.) A cross-sectional hospital study of heavy drug users documented a high prevalence of *Salvia* use with rapid onset and dissipation of hallucinogenic and perceptual effects ([Karam et al., 2018, *Community Mental Health Journal*, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10597-018-0347-4)).

**No therapeutic efficacy in humans has been demonstrated.** All medical interest to date is preclinical and mechanistic.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

*Not classifiable within the Galenic/humoral or Ayurvedic dosha frameworks.* As a Mesoamerican ritual plant, *Salvia divinorum* was never assigned a hot/cold or wet/dry quality in the Old World humoral traditions, and imposing one would be a fabrication. Its indigenous framing is **oracular and divinatory** — an intermediary consulted for insight and diagnosis under a curandero's guidance — rather than an energetic-temperature agent. Classification here is therefore recorded as **NA (inferred / non-canonical)**.

### Contraindications

- **Legal status — restrictive and patchwork.** The plant and/or salvinorin A are **scheduled, banned, or otherwise controlled in many U.S. states, in Australia, and in several European countries**, while remaining unscheduled or legal elsewhere. Status changes and varies sharply by jurisdiction; possession or supply may be a criminal offence where you are. Verify current local law before any handling. (No jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction "where it's legal" list is provided by design.)
- **Acute dissociation and injury risk.** Effects can be **intense, abrupt, and profoundly disorienting**, with loss of contact with reality, impaired awareness of surroundings, and consequent **risk of falls and injury**. Effects are short but can be overwhelming and cannot be titrated once begun.
- **Psychiatric precipitation.** May **precipitate or worsen psychiatric illness** and has been associated in the literature with precipitation of psychiatric symptoms and adverse effects on cognition. Anyone with a personal or family history of psychosis, bipolar disorder, or other serious mental illness is at heightened risk.
- **No medical indication.** There is **no demonstrated therapeutic use in humans**; KOR-related findings are preclinical only. This entry is **not for self-experimentation** and provides no dosing or preparation guidance.
- **Vulnerable populations.** Not appropriate for minors, during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or for anyone operating vehicles or machinery.
