# Lonfurulus — لونفرولس (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *A soft Egyptian stone that fullers use to whiten cloth, which dissolves quickly in water; the Arabic transliterates a name (lūnfurūlus) that is not identified here. The earlier suggestion that it is Cimolian earth is not supported by the Canon text and is not adopted (confidence low)* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk, so far as searched.** This is a single-text entry. The spine row attests Avicenna alone (Canon Book II, heading *lūnfurūlus*, Arabic text line 14641 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`). The Cimolian earth entry on disk (II_173, CPU-02409, from Dioscorides 5-176) was read: it describes a white or purplish, fatty, cool earth and does not mention fullers or Egypt, and the Canon passage does not cite Dioscorides. The two are therefore not merged. [USER: verify]

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *lūnfurūlus* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 14641-14647).

*What it is.* حجر مصري يستعمله القصارون في تبييض الثياب رخو مذاب في الماء سريعا (14642-14643): "an Egyptian stone which the fullers use in whitening clothes, soft, dissolved in water quickly".

*Actions.* مغر يجفف بلا لذع قابض مانع لسيلان المادة إلى العضو (14643): "ochre-like (*maghr*), it dries without stinging, astringent, preventing the flow of matter to the organ". No temperament degree is printed in the lines read.

*Wounds (القروح).* It is useful for ulcers and abscesses (*khurājāt*), "especially those in the soft organs" (14644-14645).

*Eye.* It benefits *gharab* (the lacrimal swelling or fistula) and enters the medicines for ulcers of the eye (14645-14646).

*Excretory organs.* The text, which gives this heading twice, says it is "good for spitting blood", is useful against chronic diarrhoea and pain of the bladder, and is borne as a suppository to cut off bleeding (14646-14647: يحتمل لقطع النزف). No source author is named. *Maghr* is read here as "ochre-like" from the root; the exact sense is ours. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The passage names no constituent. The description (soft, quickly dissolving in water, used to whiten cloth) is consistent with a fuller's-earth type clay or a soft chalky mineral, but nothing in the passage fixes the identity. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Canon records a drying, astringent stone for ulcers, abscesses, eye ulcers, spitting of blood, diarrhoea and bleeding. The Codex gives no protocol, cannot identify the material, and does not recommend it.

### Preparation & Dosing

Forms given: applied to ulcers; in eye medicines; as a suppository (14644-14647). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only: absorbent clays and chalky minerals dry wounds and bind fluid, and fuller's earth type clays are used for cleaning cloth. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **CM.** "Drying without stinging" (مجفف بلا لذع), "astringent" (قابض), "preventing the flow of matter to the organ" (14643). No degree of heat or cold is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Avicenna / Unani (CM).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested in the passage read. The entry gives qualities and organ uses and says nothing of the *ruh*, of innate heat or radical moisture, or of any life-lengthening status. Nothing is imported from outside the passage. The *Canon* is read here from the Arabic text on disk, not from the English adaptation.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do. An unidentified drying stone, recorded for the record only.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** Not established; do not substitute Cimolian earth or any other clay. [USER: verify]
- **Wounds, eye, bleeding, bladder pain.** Medical conditions; use proper care, not an unidentified mineral.
- **Suppository use.** Contaminated earth is an infection risk.
