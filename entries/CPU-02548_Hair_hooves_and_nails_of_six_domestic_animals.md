# Hair, hooves and nails of six domestic animals — 六畜毛蹄甲 *liu chu mao ti jia* (Shennong bencao jing)

## *A composite entry, not a single species: the hair, hooves and nails (claws) of the "six domestic animals", which Tao Hongjing in Sun's note lists as horse, ox, sheep, pig, dog and chicken; the text adds that camel hair is "especially good", although the camel is not among the six* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Shennong bencao jing (SBJ).** The hair, hooves and nails of the six domestic animals are named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The identification is ours (medium confidence) and is a composite: the name is a class of animal parts, not a species. The source is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`); the entry stands in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 六畜毛蹄甲 *liu chu mao ti jia*, same file, line 3819).

**The Shennong text proper** (line 3819): "Flavour salty, balanced. It governs *guizhu* (鬼注, 'ghost infusion'), *gu* poison (蛊毒), cold and heat, fright-epilepsy, [a character is missing in the file], and mad running. Camel hair is especially good." (味咸，平。主鬼注、蛊毒，寒热、惊痫， 、狂走。骆驼毛，尤良。) The missing character sits in a list of afflictions, so one of the conditions named cannot be read. *Guizhu* and *gu* poison are classical terms for afflictions attributed to spirits and to poison; the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. There is no Wu Pu layer and no Mingyi layer for this entry.

The hair, hooves and nails are set out as three parts, and there is no statement of which animal each part comes from, apart from the camel hair remark.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 3821).** Sun quotes Tao Hongjing: "The six domestic animals are the horse, ox, sheep, pig, dog and chicken; and *ti* (hoof) is the abbreviated form of *zhi*" (六畜，谓马、牛、羊、猪、狗、鸡也；蹄，即踯省文). That is Tao's reading (about 500 CE), a later layer than the classic. It leaves the camel outside the six, so the camel-hair clause is an addition the text makes to the six, and it leaves the chicken without a hoof, which Tao's abbreviation remark may be meant to address. I do not resolve it. [USER: verify]

**Class heading** (line 3823): "Above, beasts, lower grade, four kinds; same as the old" (上兽，下品四种。旧同). The preface's list of lower-class beasts names it (line 2937).

**Preface.** The lower class is "the left-hand envoy, mainly treating disease to correspond to earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken for long" (line 2935).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, hair, hooves and nails are made of keratin, a tough structural protein with a high sulphur content, and are largely inert when swallowed. Hair and hooves also collect whatever the animal has been exposed to (see Contraindications). Nothing more specific is stated in the texts. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records the hair, hooves and nails for *guizhu*, *gu* poison, cold and heat, fright-epilepsy and mad running, with camel hair named best. The Codex does not turn these into modern indications. Fright-epilepsy and mad running describe seizures and psychosis, which need medical care. Animal hair is also a recognised carrier of infection (see Contraindications).

### Scientific Correlation

Keratin is poorly digested by people and is not a nutrient in the ordinary sense. There is no established evidence that swallowed hair or hoof acts on seizures or on the other conditions the text names. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong).** One flavour and one nature are printed: salty (咸), balanced (平). Lower class by placement. No further quality is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry is in the lower classic, which the preface (line 2935) describes as mainly for treating disease, mostly toxic and not for long use. The paragraph has no *jiu fu* ("taken for long") clause and no life-nourishing wording; every claim is therapeutic. The tradition's own grading places it among drugs for a short course.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing for a cultivator here, and no reason to take hair or hoof. The classic is a record of an old therapeutic custom. The lower-class label is the warning.

### Contraindications

- **Infection.** Animal hair, hide and hooves can carry spores and organisms; anthrax spores in animal hair and hides are a well-established example. Do not handle or swallow untreated animal hair, hoof or claw as a remedy.
- **Seizures and psychosis.** The conditions named need medical care.
- **Camel hair.** The text's preference for camel hair is a historical remark, not a recommendation.
- **Related entries.** The spine has separate entries for horse hoof (III_112), goat hoof (III_100), donkey hoof (III_19) and camel flesh (III_56); they come from other traditions and nothing from them is transferred here.
- **Pregnancy, children, medicines.** The text says nothing; no use is supported. [USER: verify]
