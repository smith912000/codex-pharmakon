# Babalus — بابلس (*bābalus*, as printed)

## *A 'wild' and 'foamy' poppy that purges like the spurges (the printed adjectives are the Canon's; the first adjective is spelt الوبري, probably OCR for البري 'wild'); no modern species is assigned here (confidence low)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk, so far as searched. Source file: `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`.

**Locus (CM).** *Canon* Book II, entry *bābalus* (بابلس), heading at Arabic text line 11424; the entry is three lines (11425-11427) and runs straight into the next heading, urine (*bawl*), at line 11427.

**What it is (CM).** 'It is the one that is called the wild and foamy poppy (*al-khashkhāsh al-wabrī wa-l-zabadī*), and it acts in purging as the spurges (*yatūʿāt*) act' (lines 11425-11426). **Identity doubt:** the Canon says only 'poppy'. Dioscorides's *mēkōn aphrōdēs* (foamy poppy) was not searched for on disk, so no Dioscorides link is claimed. This Codex's poppy entries (for example I_192 opium poppy, I_695 corn poppy) are different plants. [USER: verify]

**What the text says (CM).** Temperament: hot in the extreme (حار جدا; line 11426). Excretory: it purges like the spurges (line 11426). Nothing else is given.

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms spurges carry irritant milky latex; whether this poppy does is not known.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The entry is a short note that this poppy is a drastic purgative. It is not a recommended practice.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(b) Framed.* The entry is a drug-by-drug list of qualities and uses inside the *Canon*'s materia medica. It assigns no cultivation status and says nothing of innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruh*. A drug the text equates with the drastic spurges has no conserving role in its scheme.

**Practitioner's note.** A purgative wild poppy in a three-line entry. The identity is open and nothing supports a cultivation use.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Little to map: the entry states that this wild, foamy poppy purges in the way the spurges purge, and that its temperament is hot in the extreme. Those are the only actions named. Purging maps to the intestinal tract and bowel, which the Canon groups under the excretory organs, but the entry names no organ, tissue or system beyond that. No dryness is printed, and no degree beyond 'hot' is given. The passage has no preparation, no dose and no contraindication. The Canon's comparison with the spurges is the only other pointer, and the text gives no further detail about them. The species is not identified, so no modern pharmacology is attached to the plant. The entry is three lines long and runs directly into the next heading.

### Contraindications

A drastic purgative by the text's own comparison to the spurges. Do not use an unidentified plant. No contraindication is printed. [USER: verify]
