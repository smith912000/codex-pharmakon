**Egyptian Papyrus Ebers (EP).** Bryan's Chapter VI, "Plant Remedies", lists "Cedar-tree" with three forms: Fat-of-the-Cedar-tree, Splinters-of-the-Cedar-tree and Tops-of-the-Cedar-tree (`PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` lines 1255 to 1257; names.csv row, Ch. VI, line 1255). The bare name "Cedar-tree" is not used alone in a recipe in the lines found; each recipe uses one of the three forms.

1. **Fat-of-the-Cedar-tree**, in a poultice "to heal the diseased toes". The ingredients run: Fennel, Wax, Incense, Cyperus, Wormwood, Dried Myrrh, Poppy-plant, Poppy-grain, Elderberries, Berries-of-the-*uan*-tree, Resin-of-Acanthus, Dough-of-Acanthus, Resin-of-the-*mafet*-tree, Grain-of-Aloes, Fat-of-the-Cedar-tree, Fat-of-the-*uan*-tree, Fresh Olive Oil and Water-from-the-Rain-of-the-Heavens; the instruction is "Make into one and Poultice for four days" (lines 2146 to 2153).
2. **Tops-of-the-Cedar-tree**, in the section Bryan calls "Diseases of the Skin". After a remedy to make an ulcer's scab fall off ("Put on the Ulcer until it falls off", lines 2653 to 2657), the lesions are treated with "Scribe's excrement" in fresh milk "as a Poultice", and then: "Another. Tops-of-the-Cedar-tree. Mix with Milk-of-a-Woman-who-has-borne-a-Son, and apply as a Poultice." (lines 2660 to 2670).
3. **Splinters-of-the-Cedar-tree**, one of the thirty-seven ingredients that Bryan calls "the largest prescription in the Papyrus", a poultice "to make the *met* supple" (his printed term, not interpreted here). The splinters stand in a run of woods and resins, with splinters of mulberry, willow, *Zizyphus Lotus*, sycamore and the *uan*-tree, and the resins of acanthus and other trees; the instruction ends "Make into one and poultice therewith" (lines 3181 to 3195 and Bryan's introduction at lines 3172 to 3180).

No fourth use of the tree, and no description of the tree itself, was found in the lines read. The Ebers text on disk is Bryan's English; Egyptian terms for the cedar and the Egyptian originals of the recipes are not in it. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** A fat of the cedar in a poultice for diseased toes; the tops with a woman's milk as a poultice after a skin ulcer's scab has fallen; splinters in a large poultice for a complaint Bryan calls the *met*.
- **What the text does not say.** No amount of cedar in any recipe, no way of making the fat, no description of what "tops" are.

The Codex gives no protocol for this material. Several companion ingredients in these recipes (poppy, red lead, natron, excrement) are hazardous, and none of these recipes is recommended.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Cedar and other conifer resins and oils contain terpenes with mild antiseptic and aromatic properties, and cedar essential oil can irritate the skin. The Ebers terms (diseased toes, the *met*) are not translated here into modern diagnoses. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Do not use the recipes.** Cedar oil and resin can irritate or sensitise the skin. Several companion ingredients (poppy, red lead, excrement, a woman's milk) carry their own risks.
- **Ulcers and diseased toes.** These are medical conditions needing proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** "Cedar-tree" is Bryan's label; the species is not shown. Cedar wood and oils sold today are of several unrelated trees. [USER: verify]
