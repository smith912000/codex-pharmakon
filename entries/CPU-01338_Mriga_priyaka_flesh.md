# Mriga-priyaka flesh — Mriga-priyaka (also printed Mriga-Priyaka), flesh of a hole-dwelling (Vileshaya) animal, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the animal Bhishagratna prints as "Mriga-Priyaka" in the Vileshaya group of Sutrasthana ch.46; his Vol. 3 glossary reads "A boa (lit. one fond of a deer)"; the spine list flags the identity as uncertain* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Mriga-priyaka is named in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 224 lists "Mriga-Priyaka", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", flagged id_uncertain). The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The high-ground (*Jangala*) animals fall into eight sub-groups; the seventh is the *Vileshaya* (lines 403-409). The group is defined: "Animals such as, the Shvavit, Shalyaka, Godha, Shasha, Vrishadansha, Lopaka, Lomasha-Karna, Kadali, Mriga-Priyaka, Ajagara, Sarpa, Mushika, Nakula and Maha-Vabhru belong to the group of Vileshaya (hole-dwellers)" (lines 574-578).

The group text: "The general properties of the flesh of animals belonging to this species are to increase the consistency of stool and urine. They are heat-making in potency, sweet in digestion as those of the preceding group. They subdue the Vayu and generate the Pittam and Kapham. They are demulcent and beneficial in cough, dyspnoea and cachexia" (lines 580-587). The text then takes some members singly. For this one it says only: "The flesh of the Mriga-Priyaka proves a wholesome diet in diseases due to the action of the deranged Vayu (Vayu-Roga), while that of the Ajagara is beneficial in piles" (lines 599-602). The name occurs nowhere else in the chapter files of the Sushruta text on disk (a name search of the Sutra, Chikitsa, Uttara, Kalpa, Nidana and Sharira folders found it only in ch.46 under the spellings searched; OCR variants such as "Mrigii-Priyaka" at line 600 mean the search is not exhaustive).

Other places. In the after-drinks passage of ch.46, "Phalasara wine should be taken after the flesh of any of the hole-dwelling (Vileshaya) animals" (lines 2847-2849). The Vol. 3 index of the translation has "Mriga-Priyaka ("Animals")" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, line 22445). No Dioscorides, Tibetan or other text is attached to this entry; the tag is SS only.

**Identity.** The translator's Vol. 3 glossary reads "Mriga-priyaka A boa (lit. one fond of a deer)" (line 30803). The glossary's reading is a snake, which would sit with the Sarpa, Ajagara and other burrowing animals of the group, but the group list is a list of names and the chapter does not itself say what the Mriga-priyaka is. The spine list gives no gloss and flags the identity as uncertain. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

If the animal is a large snake, its flesh is muscle protein and fat; snake and reptile flesh can carry *Salmonella* and parasites. No analysis specific to this entry was read, and the identity is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The animal's identity is not settled and the text gives one indication, a "wholesome diet" in Vayu disorders. Nothing in the sources supports eating or sourcing it, and several animals plausibly meant (large snakes, burrowing mammals) are protected or regulated. The Codex does not recommend the flesh as a therapy for any complaint and gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation or dose is given for the Mriga-priyaka. The only related instruction is the general after-drink for the *Vileshaya* group: Phalasara wine after the flesh of any hole-dwelling animal (ch.46, lines 2847-2849). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Reptile and wild-animal flesh must be cooked through and handled hygienically; reptiles are recognised carriers of *Salmonella*. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 580-602). For the *Vileshaya* group: increases the consistency of stool and urine; heat-making in potency; sweet in digestion; subdues *Vayu*; generates *Pittam* and *Kapham*; demulcent; beneficial in cough, dyspnoea and cachexia. For the Mriga-priyaka alone: a wholesome diet in *Vayu* diseases. No taste (*rasa*) is printed for it by name. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Mriga-priyaka is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food in general, not about this flesh. The text's whole statement on the animal is one clause, a wholesome diet in *Vayu* disease, and no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter read names it. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta this is one of the hole-dwelling animals, with a single stated use in *Vayu* disease, and it cannot be identified with confidence. A person who eats meat may read the classification as the tradition's own description of the food; a person who does not has nothing to adopt.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** the animal is not settled (a boa in the glossary, unglossed in ch.46); do not substitute a species on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Food safety:** reptile and burrowing-animal flesh can carry *Salmonella* and parasites; cook through.
- **The text's own cautions:** the *Vileshaya* group generates *Pittam* and *Kapham* (ch.46, lines 584-585).
- **Legal and conservation status:** large constricting snakes and many burrowing species are protected or regulated in many countries. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food is a moral question for many traditions, and meat is restricted in some; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
