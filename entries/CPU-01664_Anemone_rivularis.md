# Anemone rivularis — srub.ka (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Anemone rivularis Buch-Ham, the first of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for srub.ka (with Pulsatilla patens), and a third word "Ranunculus" printed after them; the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text was searched and prints no matching name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** **Named only by the Tibetan text in this row (Clark 1995).** No other text read for this entry names the plant under this name; the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text, searched for "Anemone", "Srubka" and "Srub", prints "Anemone" only in the name of a different plant (see I_1229). The locus is in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance", and the row's binomial is the first of two (and a bare genus name) on Clark's line. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1118).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The entry reads: "srub.ka: Anemone rivularis Buch-Ham: Pulsatilla patens: Ranunculus: [has five names, for example ra.srub, rgyal.po.tso.ra, etc. It has a flexible petiole, rounded, smooth tri-segmented leaf, a dull pale blue or white flower and a stalk which splits into four parts. Anemone has hot and bitter tastes], halts necrosis, increases digestive heat, drains lymph [accumulations and removes cold tumors]."

Clark's preface (line 82) says "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". So the five names, the description of petiole, leaf, flower and stalk, and the statement of "hot and bitter tastes" are the commentary layer, as is the clause "accumulations and removes cold tumors". What stands outside the brackets is three actions: the drug "halts necrosis, increases digestive heat, drains lymph". The root-text layer, as Clark gives it, names three actions and no taste or power.

The two entries below it refer back to it. The next entry, Ranunculus (lche.tsha, line 1119), "removes cold tumors, diphtheria and quinsy, etc., and dries oedema fluid". Then the Clematis entry (dbyi.mong, line 1120) ends outside the brackets: "Ranunculus and Clematis both have the same use as Anemone."

Two further Clark lines name the drug. Line 769, in the exposition of tastes, reads: "The group of medicines having hot taste includes black pepper, wild ginger, longpeper, fresh (moist) ginger, Asafoetida, a type of Anemone rivularis (srub.ka), che.tsha, Arisaema intermedium, onion, garlic, etc." In chapter 21, "The Principles of Medicines: Compounding of Medicines" (heading at line 1204), line 1217 reads: "The category of medicines which cure cold phlegm conditions includes Pomegranate, Black Pepper, Longpeper, medicinal Ginger, white-flowered Leadwort, Greater Cardamon, Smaller Cardamon, Cinnamon, Indian Beech, Embelia (Bay breng), Rhododendron affcephoelanthum Franch, Black Cumin seed, Trachyspermum amni, (black) Clematis, Anemone (rivularis), Ranunculus pulchellus, white mineral salt, lake salt, rock salt, horn salt and ash salt." Clark's name index prints "Anemone rivularis" (line 1554) and "srub.ka" (line 1768).

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Anemone rivularis*, *srub.ka* and *Pulsatilla patens* found no entry on this drug. I_1053 (*Clematis montana*, tag FMT) quotes this entry's neighbours (lines 1118 and 1120) because the Clematis line refers to it; it is the entry for the next drug. I_779 (Pasque Flower, *Pulsatilla vulgaris*, tag EF) is on the genus of Clark's second binomial and carries no FMT tag; the Codex does not state that the Tibetan drug is that plant. I_1003 (*Anemone alba*, Dioscorides) is an entry on a different *Anemone*. I_632 (*Ranunculus acris*) is on the species Clark prints for the neighbouring lche.tsha (line 1119) and carries no FMT tag. None is an entry on srub.ka.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan text records a plant of hot and bitter taste (commentary layer) that halts necrosis, increases digestive heat and drains lymph, and, in the commentary layer, removes cold tumors (Clark line 1118). It is listed among the hot-tasting medicines (line 769) and the cold-phlegm medicines (line 1217). These are the text's own claims and not Codex protocols. The botanical identity is uncertain between two binomials and a bare genus name, and the Codex gives no protocol for necrosis, tumors or digestion.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica classes the plant by its tastes and by what it does: hot and bitter, halting necrosis, increasing digestive heat, draining lymph (line 1118), and it stands in the hot-taste group (line 769) and the cold-phlegm class (line 1217). The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this entry places srub.ka in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice. In the lines read, the essence-extraction mentions in the herb section are for other plants (for example at lines 971 and 1121), and srub.ka is not named there. Its framing is therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a hot, bitter drug for necrosis, digestive heat, lymph and cold phlegm in the Tibetan text, and nothing more can be said from it. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. The botanical identity is uncertain (two binomials and a bare genus name in Clark), so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Plants of the buttercup family are generally known to irritate the skin and mouth. Necrosis and tumors need medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
