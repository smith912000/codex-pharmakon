### Origin & Tradition

Maca grows exclusively in the Peruvian Andes at altitudes of 4000–4500 meters — one of the few crops that survives and thrives in the extreme cold, intense UV radiation, and thin air of the high puna grasslands. This extremophile adaptation, identical to what we observe in Rhodiola, is the pharmacological signature: its chemistry is shaped by the same forces it will help the human body resist.

The *Badianus Manuscript* (Aztec herbal codex, 1552 CE) and pre-Columbian Andean oral records document Maca's use as a primary warrior and agricultural laborer tonic: it was distributed to Incan warriors before campaigns to increase strength and stamina. Conquistadors documented that Spanish horses grazed on Maca root became extraordinarily vital compared to those that did not.

Within the integrated pharmacological context of this Codex, Maca represents the Andean branch of the universal "altitude Jing tonic" archetype — an herb that concentrates the elemental force of high-altitude survival into a form the body can assimilate. It is one of the most complete single-food sources of the Jing-Qi spectrum, containing the full range of essential amino acids, fatty acids, and endocrine-active compounds.

### Active Components

- **Macamides** (N-benzyl fatty acid amides) — unique to Maca; endocannabinoid-system adjacent; energy and mood modulating; adaptogenic
- **Macaridine, lepidiline A & B** (alkaloids) — androgenic and energetic
- **Glucosinolates** — sulfur compounds; hormone-modulating (indole glucosinolates primarily)
- **Sterols (β-sitosterol, campesterol, ergosterol)** — testosterone precursor phytosterols
- **Meyenii polysaccharides** — immune and gut support
- **Complete amino acid profile** (including BCAA: leucine, isoleucine, valine) — anabolic substrate
- **High mineral content:** iron, zinc, copper, manganese, iodine — altitude bioavailability optimized
- **Vitamin C** — high concentration; altitude oxidative stress protection

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Maca operates as a food-grade Jing tonic — its safety profile allows doses that deliver genuine nutritional replenishment alongside pharmacological action. In Kenetica, it provides the broad-spectrum endocrine and energetic support that is neither as targeted as single compound herbs nor as generic as multivitamins.

- **Endocrine stabilization** — adapts to both estrogen and testosterone systems; neither purely anabolic nor purely estrogenic — a true hormonal adaptogen
- **Altitude and hypoxic stress** — improves oxygen utilization; reduces altitude sickness symptoms
- **Sustained energy without stimulant effect** — not a caffeine-type stimulant; builds energy through nutritional completeness
- **Libido and reproductive vitality** — most well-documented effect in clinical literature
- **Bone density** — glucosinolates support bone mineral metabolism
- **Mood and mental endurance** — macamides support sustained mental effort without the crash of stimulants

### Preparation & Dosing

**Gelatinized Maca powder (preferred — removes raw starch which is hard to digest):** 5–15g/day in smoothies, oatmeal, or warm milk.
**Raw powder:** 3–9g/day (start lower — raw starch can cause digestive upset in some).
**Extract (4:1):** 1–3g/day.
**Color note:** Black Maca is preferred for male athletic use (stronger androgenic effect); Red Maca is preferred for female use (bone density and hormonal balance); Yellow Maca is general purpose.

**Cycle:** Food-level use can continue without cycling. Therapeutic extract cycling: 8–12 weeks on, 2 weeks off.

**Stacking partners:**
- + Ashwagandha — Andean + Ayurvedic endocrine tonic combination
- + Pine Pollen — androgenic amplification stack
- + Cistanche — deep Jing + endocrine restoration protocol

### Scientific Correlation

- Libido: 2 RCTs demonstrate significant improvement in sexual function scores in healthy men (Gonzales 2002) and women on SSRI (Dording 2008)
- Testosterone: does not directly increase testosterone in men with normal levels; however boosts fertility markers (sperm count, motility)
- Glucosinolates show aromatase-inhibiting activity — may shift estrogen/androgen ratio favorably in estrogen-dominant states
- Macamides: proposed to inhibit FAAH (fatty acid amide hydrolase) — the enzyme that breaks down endocannabinoids; increases endocannabinoid tone (similar mechanism to CBD and beta-caryophyllene)
- Bone density: RCT in post-menopausal women shows Maca consumption associated with increased bone mineral density
- Energy: clinical trial shows reduced fatigue and improved psychomotor performance in trained cyclists (Stone 2009)

### Contraindications

- **Thyroid disorders** — glucosinolates are goitrogenic at very high doses; limit to moderate amounts with hypothyroidism
- **Hormone-sensitive conditions** (estrogen-dependent cancers) — glucosinolate estrogenic activity requires caution
- **Pregnancy** — insufficient safety data for therapeutic doses
- **Raw powder and digestive sensitivity** — raw starch may cause bloating; use gelatinized form
