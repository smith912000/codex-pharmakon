# Dasha-Mulam — Dasha-Mulam, "the ten roots" (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *Not a single plant but a named group of ten roots, formed by joining the Svalpa-Pancha-Mula (minor five roots) and the Mahat-Pancha-Mula (major five roots) of Sushruta's chapter 38; the text names the member plants but Bhishagratna's English gives no identification of them. Identification: a compound drug group, whose named members are listed below; no species is asserted. Confidence: high that it is a group, none as to species* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** This row is a group of ten roots, not one plant, and the members are not identified here. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a root on the strength of the names alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The first-pass check called the row a misreading; the audit disagreed, because Dasha-mulam is a real named drug group in Sushruta, and the reading below bears that out. A search of all the Sushruta text on disk (the chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and Bhishagratna's three volumes) for the name found the group definition in ch.38 and a use of "the drugs which collectively go by the name of Dashamulam" in ch.44.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Chapter 38 of the *Sutrasthana* is the chapter "which deals with the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam)" and says that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (Vol. I lines 15343 to 15350; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3 to 9). The drug is placed there by group, and a group's virtue is stated for the group as a whole.

1. **The definition.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38, "The Dashamula Group" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15835 to 15850; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 494 to 500): "The two preceding groups in combination form the one technically known as the Dasha-Mulam (the ten roots), which is possessed of the virtue of destroying the deranged Vata, Pittam and Kapham. It proves beneficial in cases of asthma and difficult respiration. It acts as a good digestant in respect of undigested lymph chyle, etc and is used with satisfactory results in all types of fever" (the sentence runs across a page break).
2. **The two five-root groups.** The "two preceding groups" are the *Svalpa Panchamulam* and the *Mahat Panchamula* (lines 15806 to 15834). The minor group "consists of the roots of medicinal plants known as the Trikantaka, the two species of Vrihati, Prithakparni, and Vidarigandha"; its metrical text reads "The compound possesses a taste blended of astringent, bitter and sweet. It is a tonic and aphrodisiac, subdues the deranged Vayu and proves soothing to the deranged Pittam" (lines 15818 to 15822). The major group "consists of the roots of such trees as Vilva, Agnimantha, Tuntuka, Patala and Kashmari"; its metrical text reads "It is bitter in taste and subdues the deranged Kapham and Vatam. It is light (easily digestible) and appetising, and acquires a subsequent sweet taste in its reaction (Anurasa)" (lines 15830 to 15834). The words "tonic and aphrodisiac" are said of the minor five-root group alone and are Bhishagratna's English.
3. **A use in the purgative barks.** *Sutrasthana* ch.44 (`text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch44.txt` line 438; Vol. I lines 17700 to 17720). In preparing a purgative from Lodhra bark, the powder is divided in three, two parts being soaked in a decoction of the bark and filtered twenty-one times, and the remaining third being "soaked in the aforesaid filtered decoction and subsequently dried in the sun, and again soaked in a decoction of the drugs, which collectively go by the name of Dashamulam". The medicine "should be prescribed in forms (wines, electuaries, etc.) previously described in connection with the Trivrit compounds."

The list of ten is therefore the five from the minor group and the five from the major group: Trikantaka, the two species of Vrihati, Prithakparni and Vidarigandha make the minor group, and Vilva, Agnimantha, Tuntuka, Patala and Kashmari make the major. None of these ten names is identified in the lines read. Each may have its own entry or row in the Codex; this entry records the group only. [USER: verify against the printed page]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Group of roots, members unidentified here.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a group: destroying deranged Vata, Pitta and Kapha, helping asthma and difficult breathing, digesting undigested chyle, and treating all types of fever (ch.38). As a decoction: used to soak and treat Lodhra bark powder in a purgative preparation (ch.44).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation of the decoction beyond the Lodhra-bark use, no identification of the members, and no taste or potency for the ten as a whole.

The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no plant for any of the conditions named.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation found is the decoction "of the drugs which collectively go by the name of Dashamulam" used to soak the dried Lodhra-bark powder (ch.44), after which the medicine is given in the forms described for the Trivrit compounds. The quantities of the roots and of the decoction are not given in the lines read. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** The tastes printed are those of the two five-root groups, not of the ten as one: the minor group "astringent, bitter and sweet" (line 15819); the major group "bitter", "light", with a subsequent sweet taste (*Anurasa*) (lines 15830 to 15834). No *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for the ten.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The group is defined in a chapter 38 drug classification and used in a purgative preparation in ch.44; neither is a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns the group no cultivation status. The word "tonic and aphrodisiac" belongs to the minor five-root group alone, in Bhishagratna's English, and is a remedy-level description and not a statement of *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for the group.

**Practitioner's note.** A named group of ten roots, set down in a classification and used to prepare a purgative. It is not a plant to be gathered, and the record gives no basis for any cultivation use. A cultivator reads it as the name of a classical formula element.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The ten member roots are not identified here. Do not gather or substitute any root on the strength of the group name.
- **Fever, asthma, difficult breathing.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Purgative use.** The ch.44 preparation is a purgative of Lodhra bark; purgatives can be harmful, and none is recommended here.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
