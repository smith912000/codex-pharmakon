# Shrimp — روبيان (*rūbiyān*, as printed)

## *Shrimp: the shrimp or prawn (rubiyan); the Canon compares it to the crab and gives no description* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the Arabic texts on disk. The locus is *Canon* Book II, entry *rūbiyān* (روبيان), Arabic text line 18021 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; the entry runs to line 18027 and quotes Galen twice.

**What the text says (CM).** Galen is quoted first: "Galen said: the state in it is as the state in the crab (*saraṭān*)" (قال جالينوس: إن الحال فيه كالحال في السرطان; line 18022), so the reader is sent to the crab entry. Action: "if salted and aged it generates black bile (*sawdāʾ*) and a bad itch" (إذا ملح وعتق يولد سوداء وحكة رديئة; lines 18022-18023). Swellings: "Galen said: it dissolves hard swellings" (قال جالينوس: إنه يحلل الأورام الصلبة; line 18023). Digestive: "it gives good nourishment" (يغذو غذاء صالحا; line 18024). Excretory: "it increases semen and increases coitus, softens the belly, and evacuates *ḥabb al-qarʿ*" (يزيد في المني ويزيد في الباه ويلين البطن ويستفرغ حب القرع; lines 18026-18027). *Ḥabb al-qarʿ* ("gourd seeds") is the Arabic name for the segments of tapeworm; that gloss is my reading. [USER: verify]

**Identification.** *rūbiyān* is the common name of the shrimp or prawn in Arabic; the text gives no description, so the species is open and the identification is general (good confidence for "shrimp", none for a species). The spine's "Shrimp" is the English word only.

### Active Components

Shrimp are crustaceans; their flesh is a protein food, and the shell contains chitin. The text names no constituent. General fact only.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only: a food from the sea with a note that, salted and aged, it breeds black bile and itch, plus claims for hard swellings, semen, coitus and tapeworm. Not a recommended practice; no protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation named is salting and ageing, which the text warns against (line 18023). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General fact: shellfish are a common cause of food allergy.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No temperament degree is printed; the entry sends the reader to the crab by Galen (*Canon* Book II, entry *rūbiyān*, line 18022).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The entry is a food-medicine note quoting Galen. The remark that it "increases semen" and coitus is a generative claim in humoral terms and is not a cultivation status; none is attested, and no further vital doctrine is brought to bear.

**Practitioner's note.** Shrimp is food with a warning in the text about salted and aged seafood. A cultivator has nothing here beyond that warning.

### Contraindications

Shellfish allergy can be severe. Raw or poorly stored shellfish carry bacterial and parasite risks. The text itself warns that salted, aged shrimp "generate black bile and a bad itch" (line 18023). Fisheries are subject to sustainability and farming-practice concerns; these are general, not from the text.
