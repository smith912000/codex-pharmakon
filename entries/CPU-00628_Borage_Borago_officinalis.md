# Borage — Starflower, Bee Bread, Bugloss, Lisan al-Thawr ("ox-tongue")
## *Borago officinalis* L. | Family: Boraginaceae
**Tradition Tags:** AT (Assyrian), CM (Avicenna), DMM (Dioscorides)
**Energetic Classification:** Hot and moist (Galenic 1st degree) — a sanguine, gladdening herb
**Training Phase:** Integration (mood-lifting and restorative), with the **seed oil** as a Tempering-phase anti-inflammatory adjunct

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Assyrian (cuneiform materia medica):** The Boraginaceae group — rough, tongue-shaped, blue-flowered herbs of the *bugloss* type — appears among the plant drugs of the Mesopotamian tradition catalogued on the medical tablets. Borage and its close relative bugloss were used interchangeably in this lineage as cooling-yet-strengthening pot-herbs and infusions; the "ox-tongue" naming (*lisan al-thawr*, carried into Arabic) descends directly from this ancient recognition of the plant's rough, tongue-like leaf.

**Dioscorides (De Materia Medica):** Described under *buglosson*, the plant "put into wine, increases the joy and delight of drinkers" — the classic euphoric/cordial reputation. Dioscorides used the leaves and flowers as a cooling demulcent for the throat and chest and as a gladdening cordial, establishing the antidepressant folk-signature that follows borage across two millennia.

**Avicenna (Qanun fi'l-Tibb):** *Lisan al-thawr* is a canonical Unani cardiac and cerebral tonic — *mufarrih* (exhilarant, gladdener of the heart), prescribed for melancholy, palpitation, and low spirits, and as a blood-purifier and diaphoretic in fevers. Avicenna classed it hot and moist, sanguine in nature, correcting the black bile of melancholic states. This is the most developed doctrinal use of the herb.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Borage's emblem in the herbals was courage — *"Ego Borago gaudia semper ago"* ("I, Borage, always bring courage/joys"). The symbolism of a warming, blood-gladdening, heart-lifting herb maps onto a genuine pharmacology: the aerial parts are mucilaginous and mineral-rich (a demulcent, mildly diaphoretic tonic), while the **seed** carries gamma-linolenic acid (GLA), the one component with real modern anti-inflammatory evidence. The tradition intuited a "courage of the blood"; the chemistry delivers an eicosanoid-modulating fatty acid.

### Active Components

- **Gamma-linolenic acid (GLA, ~20–24% of seed oil)** — omega-6 precursor of the anti-inflammatory eicosanoid series (via DGLA); the primary evidence-based constituent, found in the **seed oil only**.
- **Mucilage and tannins** (leaf/flower) — demulcent, mildly astringent.
- **Potassium, calcium, and organic acids** — mineral content behind the diuretic/diaphoretic reputation.
- **Rosmarinic acid and other phenolics** — antioxidant.
- **⚠️ Pyrrolizidine alkaloids (PAs)** — hepatotoxic, in the leaf, flower and aerial parts (see Contraindications). This is the defining safety fact of the plant.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Two distinct materials with two distinct roles:

**Seed oil (Tempering phase):** a standardised GLA source used as an anti-inflammatory adjunct — the fraction with clinical support, relevant where the training goal is dampening inflammatory tone (skin, joints, inflammatory signalling). Must be PA-certified/PA-free.

**Aerial parts (Integration phase, cautiously):** the traditional gladdening cordial — a mood-lifting, restorative demulcent used in the Integration phase where the objective is recovery of spirit and equilibrium. Because of PA hepatotoxicity, aerial-part use is restricted to short-term, low-dose, and never for pregnant/lactating users or those with liver concerns. Kenetica's default is to prefer the seed oil and treat the leaf as a traditional, short-course cordial only.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- Jung et al. (2014), *Acta Dermato-Venereologica* — a 10-week randomised, double-blind controlled trial (n=45) in which borage-oil GLA (400 mg/day) significantly reduced both inflammatory and non-inflammatory acne lesions, with reduced tissue interleukin-8 staining and no severe adverse effects. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2340/00015555-1802)
- Yue et al. (2021), *Cellular and Molecular Biology* — borage oil (30 mg/day, 7 days) in type-II diabetic patients with ARDS significantly lowered NLRP3, TLR4 and inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-18) in mild-to-moderate disease, but showed no benefit — and raised blood pressure — in severe ARDS, a genuine "double-edged" caution. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.14715/cmb/2021.67.3.28)
- Calder (2010), *Proceedings of the Nutrition Society* — reviews enteral formulas combining fish oil, borage oil and antioxidants, reporting benefits on gas exchange, ventilation requirement and outcomes in acute lung injury / ARDS / severe sepsis. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0029665110000157)

**Evidence grade:** Moderate for the **seed-oil GLA** in inflammatory conditions (small RCTs and clinical reviews); traditional-only for the aerial parts as a mood cordial. The gladdening/antidepressant reputation is historically genuine but not clinically validated.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Not a classical Chinese herb; by analogy, sweet-cool, moistening, calming to the *shen* (spirit).
- **Ayurveda (by analogy):** *rasa* madhura (sweet); *guna* snigdha (unctuous, esp. seed); *virya* — leaf shita (cooling), Unani reads the whole herb as warming; *vipaka* madhura; pitta- and vata-pacifying, sattvic/mood-lifting.
- **Galenic / Unani:** Hot and moist, 1st degree; sanguine; *mufarrih* (exhilarant of the heart), blood-purifier, diaphoretic (Avicenna).

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian / Mesopotamian (AT).** Borage reaches this entry as a member of the rough, tongue-leaved *bugloss* group of the Boraginaceae rather than through a secured cuneiform identification of *Borago officinalis* itself — borage and bugloss were used interchangeably in the lineage, and the "ox-tongue" naming descends from that generic recognition. The Mesopotamian corpus classifies a remedy partly by the causal agency it opposes (divine hand, ghost, oath), but under the standing blocking warning on Assyrian plant identifications and their etiological framing, no such agency-role and no cultivation status can be assigned to this species from a named tablet. The attestation is generic and therapeutic. **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides gives *buglosson* a real account — the herb "put into wine, increases the joy and delight of drinkers," used as a cooling demulcent for throat and chest and as a gladdening cordial. That euphoric wine-cordial reputation is genuinely his, but Dioscorides *records* the effect within a therapeutic-and-convivial frame; he carries no subtle-body doctrine into which a cultivation status could be placed. What the tradition offers a cultivator is therefore his discipline of attention — recognising the rough tongue-shaped leaf and blue star-flower, distinguishing borage from its bugloss kin, judging the fresh herb for the cordial — not a vital-body grade. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** This is the herb's most developed doctrinal home: *lisan al-thawr* is classed hot and moist, sanguine in nature, and worked as a gladdener of the heart and cerebral tonic for melancholy, palpitation and low spirits, correcting the black bile of melancholic states. The gladdening-cardiac (*mufarrih*) doctrine, however, belongs to the *Kitāb al-Adwiya al-Qalbiyya* and not to the *Canon*, so no *Canon* chapter reference and no *mufarrih* constituent list is published here; and the strongest cultivation idea in the corpus — conservation of innate heat and radical moisture — is a regimen-level longevity frame rather than a status assigned to this single sanguine herb. With no verified named source fixing a cultivation status for this species, its standing is recorded as therapeutic — a gladdening cordial and blood-corrective — not as a sourced cultivation grade. **(b) framed:** richly claimed in the materia medica, cultivation status not sourced.

**Practitioner's note.** Borage is two materials with two honest uses, neither of them rejuvenative. The **seed oil** is a PA-free GLA source taken as a short-run anti-inflammatory adjunct — a supplement with modest clinical support, not a vitality-builder. The **aerial parts** are the traditional gladdening cordial, and here the tradition's own strongest idea (the Unani exhilaration of the heart and lifting of melancholic spirits) is a real frame but not a graded cultivation status — and it is capped hard by the defining safety fact of the plant: the leaf and flower carry hepatotoxic pyrrolizidine alkaloids, so any sustained or repeated aerial-part use is ruled out regardless of how gladdening it is said to be. A cultivator takes the certified seed oil for a defined inflammatory job, treats the leaf cordial as a brief, occasional lift only, and does not use this plant for the long term.

### Contraindications

- **⚠️ Pyrrolizidine-alkaloid hepatotoxicity (defining hazard):** the leaf, flower and aerial parts contain PAs that are hepatotoxic and potentially genotoxic/carcinogenic with cumulative exposure. **Do not use aerial parts long-term.** Use only **PA-certified (PA-free) seed oil** for any sustained supplementation.
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** avoid — PA risk to liver and fetus; seed oil also carries a theoretical prostaglandin/uterine caution.
- **Liver disease:** avoid aerial parts entirely.
- **Anticoagulants / antiplatelets:** GLA may modestly affect bleeding/platelet function — caution with warfarin, aspirin, etc.
- **Seizure threshold / phenothiazines:** GLA oils have a theoretical seizure-lowering caution in susceptible individuals.
- **Severe acute respiratory illness:** per Yue et al. (2021), borage oil showed no benefit and raised blood pressure in severe ARDS — not a self-treatment for serious lung disease.
