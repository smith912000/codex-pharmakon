# Crabapple — Agriomela (Dioscorides 1-163), "Crab Apples, Wild Apples" (translator's gloss)

## *Malus pumila* (spine botanical; the translator's list for Dioscorides 1-162 reads "Pyrus pumila, Pyrus praecox — Apple", and for 1-163 "Pyrus malus var sylvestris — Crab Apples, Wild Apples") | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity warning.** The spine's *Malus pumila* is a modern name, and the Dioscorides locus reaches it by two routes: the translator's synonym "Pyrus pumila" under 1-162, which he glosses as the ordinary "Apple" (not the crab), and the "crab apples" of 1-163, which he glosses under a different name, *Pyrus malus* var. *sylvestris*. The row's English name "Crabapple" follows 1-163 and its botanical follows 1-162, so the row blends two chapters. A finished entry already exists for the cultivated apple, I_459 "Apple" (*Malus domestica*, CPU-00825, tagged EP, DMM, CM, TCM, SS), and the two overlap. The Avicenna and Shennong tags have no locus. **Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** Three chapters in Book 1 were read (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`).

*1-163 AGRIOMELA* (line 4724; gloss "Pyrus malus var sylvestris — Crab Apples, Wild Apples") is one sentence: "Wild apples are similar to spring apples and are astringent, but for those things which need an astringent you must use those which are least ripe." This is the only chapter for the crab.

*1-162 MELA EPEIROTIKA* (line 4719; gloss "Pyrus pumila, Pyrus praecox — Apple", with the note "epirot — one who dwells inland"): "The fruit of epirotica (which the Latins call orbiculata) are good for the stomach and astringent in the intestines, encouraging urine [diuretic], yet less effective than quinces."

*1-159 MELEA* (line 4687; gloss includes "Malus communis, Malus sylvestris, Pomus, Pyrus malus — Apple" together with the quince): "The leaves, blossoms and sprigs of all sorts of melea trees are astringent, especially those of the quince tree. The unripe fruit is astringent, but if ripe it is not so. Those apples which are ripe in the springtime encourage bile, are hurtful to all that is sinewy, and are flatulent." The neighbouring 1-161 MELIMELA (line 4715, "Honey Apples, Must Apples, Cider Apples") is a sweet kind and is not this row.

### Active Components

Apples are generally known to contain sugars, organic acids, pectin and polyphenols, and wild crab apples are generally more sour and astringent than cultivated ones. The composition of the fruit in Dioscorides' chapters was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides' statements are brief: wild apples are astringent, to be used unripe where an astringent is wanted, and apples in general act on the stomach, bowel and urine. The fruit is an ordinary food in its cultivated forms and a sour one in its wild forms, and the Codex gives no protocol for it.

### Preparation & Dosing

Dioscorides gives no preparation for the crab beyond choosing the least ripe fruit (1-163, line 4724). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent* as to cultivation status. Dioscorides carries no subtle-body system. What he offers a cultivator is attention to the state of the material: he tells the reader to choose the least ripe fruit when an astringent is wanted and says that ripe fruit loses the astringency (1-159). Recording is not endorsing.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The tag rests on a MASTER_INDEX row, not on a list row or a text read. No cultivation framing, and no attestation of any kind, was found.

**Chinese — Shennong (SBJ).** *(c) Absent.* No entry for this plant was found in the readable pages of Yang 1998, and the pages for fruits are blank on disk, so no *san pin* grade can be stated. A grade belongs to an entry that sits under a printed class heading, and none was found.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing to cultivate here in the Codex's sense. The one real account is Dioscorides' one-line note that wild apples are astringent, which is a note on choosing fruit and not a practice. The Avicenna and Shennong tags are unsupported.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Apple seeds are generally not eaten in quantity, and the Codex does not recommend any therapeutic use of the fruit from the passages read. Dioscorides himself says that spring-ripe apples "are hurtful to all that is sinewy, and are flatulent" (1-159). Seek ordinary medical care for stomach, bowel or urinary complaints.
