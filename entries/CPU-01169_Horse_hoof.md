# Horse hoof — "Horse's hoof" (rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20, "Nails"; Tibetan name not printed in Clark's line)
## *The hoof of the horse, a "Nails" item of the animal medicines in the Tibetan materia medica* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The locus is Clark's 1995 translation, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, "the powers of medicines" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). Section (8) is "Medicines of Animal Origin", the "thirteen medicines of animal origin" being "horn, bone, meat, blood, bile, fat, brain, skin, nails, hair, urine, droppings and whole body" (lines 1147-1148). Under "Nails" (line 1186) the text lists the crocodile claw, then "the (right) hoof of (a one-year-old black) Donkey: cures urinary retention [and infectious fevers]", and then, at line 1189:

"Horse's hoof: removes tumors [and is beneficial against nagas]. Horse's ankle bone: cures anterior transposition of the calf muscle [and subdues nagas]."

For the hoof the text gives one plain action (it "removes tumors") and one bracketed action (it "is beneficial against nagas"). Clark's chapter notes define *nagas* as "Serpent spirits" (Notes, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, note 5, line 1541). The hoof and the ankle bone share one line, one group and the *naga* clause.

The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3556) has: "Donkey hooves cure obstruction of urine, whereas horse hooves cure tumors and the fetlock cures cramps of the calf muscle." The tumour action agrees with Clark's. The Men-Tsee-Khang line does not carry the *naga* clause, and it states no colour, age or side for either hoof, where Clark's donkey line specifies the right hoof of a one-year-old black donkey. It belongs to the plant sections of the same chapter and is not covered here. Clark's preface states that "nowadays only very few animal products are used, although many are documented in the traditional texts" (line 57). It also says he "interpolated liberally from the Blue Aquamarine commentary" (line 80); the square brackets in the lines above are not explained in the lines read, so which words belong to the root text and which to the commentary is not established here. ### Active Components

A hoof is made of keratin, the tough structural protein also found in hair, nails and horn, laid down in tubules and layers, with a softer, living tissue beneath. It is a protein, not a mineral. The texts do not say whether the hoof was used raw, burnt or ground, and no analysis of the material was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The text gives the hoof one therapeutic action (against tumours) and one spirit-related action (against *nagas*). The Codex's application layer adds nothing, and the entry is not a protocol.

It is not a recommended practice. Hoof is raw animal material with hygiene risks; the sources give no preparation, dose or route; and a "tumor" in the translation covers swellings and growths of unspecified kind that need proper medical assessment. The entry stands as a record of one line of a materia medica that the translator says is little used today.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given for the horse's hoof in either translation; the line says only what the hoof "removes" and is "beneficial against". No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] The contrast with the donkey hoof, for which Clark gives a colour, an age and a side, shows how little is specified for the horse.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT): the chapter 20 line gives actions only (removes, is beneficial against). It prints no taste, potency or post-digestive taste for the horse's hoof, and none is assigned here.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The horse's hoof is attested in the Explanatory Tantra ch.20 animal section as a therapeutic substance: one plain action and one bracketed action against *nagas*. The frame it sits in is read from Clark's preface and root text: the three humors (wind, bile, phlegm) "are rooted in attachment, hatred and obscuration respectively" (line 51), and treatment relies on "the fourfold food, behavioral pattern, medicine and accessory therapy" (line 1299). Nothing in the lines read gives the hoof a place in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or in any practice of the subtle body, and no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition. The *naga* clause is a statement about spirit-caused illness, not about cultivation.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this. The line offers no cultivation claim, no dose and no method, and the translator says animal products of this kind are little used now. The honest use is as a record.

### Contraindications

- **Not a recommended practice.** This records a Tibetan materia medica entry. Nothing here is advice to use horse hoof.
- **Hygiene and infection:** raw hoof and hoof dust can carry bacteria, fungi and spores; do not eat or apply it.
- **Tumours and lumps:** any new or growing lump needs medical examination; no traditional animal preparation is a substitute.
- **Unknown preparation and dose:** neither is given in the sources.
- **Spirit claims:** the *naga* clause is recorded as the text states it and is not a basis for treatment.
