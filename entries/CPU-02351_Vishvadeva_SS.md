### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Vishvadeva [SS]", taken from an extraction of Bhishagratna's translation; this entry replaces it. The locus given was "SS Sutra 38, Vidarigandha'cli group (Vol. I line 15355)".

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Vishvadeva is printed in the Sutrasthana, chapter 38 (locus: Vidarigandha'di group, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 14; the same text is in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, line 15355). Chapter 38 is "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3-6), and says that "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (lines 8-10). The name is given by the translator without a gloss, a botanical name or a description.

In the Vidarigandha'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 12-20 (the list), lines 21-24 (the metrical text)), Vishvadeva is one of the drugs listed. The group's metrical text reads: "The present group of drugs subdues the action of the deranged Vayu and Pittam and proves beneficial in phthisis (Shosha), Gulma, aching of the limbs, Urdha Shvasa and cough." That is a property of the group as a whole, not of Vishvadeva alone.

**Other places the name appears.** None. A search of the chapter files on disk for Vishvadeva and for "Vishva-deva" found only this line. The ch.39 Vata-soothing list refers to the Vidarigandhadi group as a whole (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt`, lines 92-93), which includes this drug, but does not name it.

The chapter closes (lines 554-566) by saying that the groups "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", and that plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, Ghritas or potions are to be prepared "according to the exigencies of each individual case". That is a general rule of the chapter and is not a recipe for this drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The text uses it only as a member of the Vidarigandha'di group, said to subdue deranged Vayu and Pitta and to help phthisis (Shosha), Gulma, aching limbs, Urdha Shvasa and cough. Through the group it is also covered by the ch.39 statement that the Vidarigandhadi group soothes deranged Vata.
- What the text does not say: what Vishvadeva is, which part is used, or any dose.

The Codex does not translate the text's terms (Vayu, Pitta, Kapham, Gulma, Shosha, Asmari and the rest) into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend a plant it cannot identify.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Vishvadeva stands in the dravya-group classification of Sutrasthana ch.38, a classification by therapeutic action. The Vidarigandha'di group is therapeutic, not rasayana or vajikarana. A search of the chapter files on disk for the spellings printed here found no mention of Vishvadeva in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters, and no text read calls it an elixir or a drug of vitality. The register is therefore (b): framed by its therapeutic group, with no cultivation status attested. The Codex does not assign *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* effects to it, because no source read does.

**Practitioner's note.** Vishvadeva is a name in a list. The tradition's text gives the drug a place among therapeutic groups and little more, and nothing in the record supports a cultivation use. A cultivator can learn from it only how Sushruta organises drugs into groups by action, and that some names in that classification are no longer certainly identified. Nobody should try to find a plant to match the name.
