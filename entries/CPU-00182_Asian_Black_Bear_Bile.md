# Asian Black Bear Bile — Bear Bile, dom.mkhris (Clark 1995) / "Asian Black Bear Bile" (project rGyud-bzhi list)
## *The bile of a bear, a resin-class entry in Explanatory Tantra ch.20; the text prints "Bear Bile" and the species Selenarctos thibetanus appears in Clark's meat classification and in the project's list, not at the bile entry itself* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The entry is in Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Clark 1995, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` lines 890-891, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` and the list `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 100). It is printed in the English under the heading "(5) Resins" (line 879), between musk (gla.rtsi) and blue lotus (ut.pa.la), and not in the animal section: "dom.mkhris: Bear Bile: [has seven names. The summer bile is golden whilst the winter bile is turquoise-colored. Good quality bear bile forms a hanging thread in water.] It constricts the mouths of the channels, halts necrosis, grows new flesh [and is beneficial in eye disorders]." Clark's introduction (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets are taken from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries, so the seven names, the colours, the water test and the eye-disorder line are commentary, and the three unbracketed actions are the root text's. The neighbouring entry, "gi.wam: Animal Bile" (line 880), says the superior type "is said to be obtained from the liver of an elephant, but the bile duct of a bull, pig, etc. is also a common source" and that the "brown type is greatly superior to the yellow type", and "cures infectious fever, poisoning, liver fever and vessel organ fever". The section on medicines of animal origin ("(8) Medicines of Animal Origin", line 1147) gives the general rule for all bile at line 1178: "All the various types of bile constrict the mouths of the channels, halt necrosis, stimulate flesh growth, neutralize poison and are beneficial in eye disorders." The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3545) gives the same rule in its animal section: "All kinds of bile stops bleeding. They also heal necrosis and muscle tissues, act as an antidote against poisons, and are beneficial to eye problems." and prints no separate bear-bile entry (a search for "bear" returned no bile line). On identity: in Clark's classification of meats the carnivorous animals "include the tiger, leopard, bear (Selenarctos thibetanus), snow bear (Ursus arctos), snow leopard ..." (line 672), and Men-Tsee-Khang's glossary lists "Tibetan Brown bear Ursus arctos" and "Black Bear Ursus thibetanus" (lines 7017-7021; Tibetan names not paired in the OCR). The bile entry itself does not say which bear, so the "Asian black bear" of the spine is the list's and the translator's identification and not the bile entry's. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Bear bile is a digestive secretion stored in the gallbladder. Like other bile it contains bile acids and bilirubin; in bears the main bile acid is ursodeoxycholic acid, which is the compound that became a modern drug (taken in some liver and gallstone conditions). The sources read give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Tibetan text gives bear bile a short list of actions, most of them on wounds and the eye, plus a test of quality. Bear bile is obtained from a threatened wild animal, and farmed bile extraction from living bears is an animal-welfare problem. The Codex does not recommend taking bear bile. Where a modern drug of the bile acid is wanted, that is a matter for a physician, and a synthetic form exists. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given in the entry. The only practical instruction is the quality test: "Good quality bear bile forms a hanging thread in water" (Clark line 890-891), and the colours: summer bile golden and winter bile turquoise. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Ursodeoxycholic acid is an established bile acid drug used for certain gallstones and some chronic liver diseases, under medical supervision, and it is made synthetically. That does not make raw bear bile a medicine, and nothing here tests the Tibetan actions (closing the channels' mouths, halting necrosis, growing flesh). No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- FMT: no taste, potency or post-digestive taste is printed for bear bile in the entry. The text gives colour by season and the water test only (Clark lines 890-891). Its general statement for the class of bile (Clark line 1178) lists actions, not qualities.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed.* The medicine is the third of the four treatment branches: it supports practice and does not reach the root, since the three *nyes pa* are, at root, a spiritual condition derived from the three poisons. Bear bile is claimed by the materia medica in the resin section of Explanatory Tantra ch.20 with wound, channel and eye actions; no longevity, rejuvenation or *bcud len* action is attested in the lines read, and the Tibetan text places it among the medicines that treat the body. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator to take. The only transferable lesson in the entry is its attention to quality: season of collection, colour, and a simple test in water. A threatened wild animal and a living-animal harvest are reasons to leave it out. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Raw bear bile is an unsterile animal secretion of unknown strength and purity. It should not be used on a wound or taken by mouth on the strength of these sources. Asian bears are threatened wild animals and trade in their parts is restricted by wildlife law in many countries; the exact present status has not been checked here. [USER: verify] Farming of bile from live bears is a well documented cruelty problem, and the Codex does not endorse it.
