# Chinese skink — 石龙子 *Shilongzi* (Shennong bencao jing), with 蜥易, 守宫, 石蜴, 山龙子
## *A lizard, in the modern identification Plestiodon chinensis (Gray) (syn. Eumeces chinensis), the Chinese skink, of medium confidence: Plestiodon elegans is also cited, and the classic's own aliases (守宫) point also to a wall gecko, so the text does not settle which reptile is meant* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** (Other lizards on the spine, III_181 Skink, from Dioscorides' *skinkos*, III_27 Lizard and III_174 Seps lizard, are from other traditions and are not this animal.) The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The identification is ours.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the middle (中) classic, reads 味咸，寒 ("salty, cold"). It governs 五癃邪结气 ("the five forms of strangury and perverse bound qi"), "breaks stone strangury" (破石淋), "brings down blood" (下血) and "frees the urine and the water passages" (利小便水道). The alias is 一名蜥易, and the habitat 生川谷. There is no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 石龙子 *shilongzi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2767)

**Wu Pu (3rd century).** 《吴普》曰 gives the aliases 守宫, 石蜴 and 石龙子. **Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 gives 山龙子, 守宫 and 石蜴, the habitat 生平阳及荆山石间 ("level sunny ground and among the rocks of Mount Jing") and the harvest: 五月取，着石上，令干, "taken in the fifth month, put on a stone to dry".

**Sun's note (案).** Sun collects glosses that distinguish and then run together several small reptiles. Shuowen says that of the 蜓 type, the one on a wall is the 蜓 and the one in grass is the 蜥易 (the transcription has gaps), and equates 守宫 ("palace guardian", the wall lizard) with 蜓; the Guangya, Erya and Mao commentary give 蜥蜴, 蝾螈, 蜓 and 守宫; Lu Ji describes the 虺蜴 as 蝾螈, "blue-green, as big as a finger, ugly in form"; and the Fangyan lists regional names for 守宫, including, in Guilin, a large one that sings, the 蛤蚧. He does not choose among them.

**Identity.** The glosses cover wall geckos (守宫), lizards in grass (蜥易), newts (蝾螈) and the large singing gecko (蛤蚧); the Wu Pu and Mingyi layers give 守宫 as an alias of 石龙子. The modern drug named 石龙子 is a skink (*Plestiodon chinensis*), which is not a gecko. The text therefore supports "a small reptile of rock and level ground" and the Codex does not treat the species as settled. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The text prints no constituent. In general terms a reptile's body is protein and fat; reptiles can carry *Salmonella* on their skin. Nothing more specific is stated without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records a salty, cold animal-drug for strangury, urinary stone, bleeding and to "free the urine". The Codex gives no protocol and recommends none. Urinary stone, blood in the urine and difficulty passing urine all need medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives the harvest only: taken in the fifth month and dried on a stone (Mingyi layer). It gives no preparation, route, quantity or course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

In general knowledge, skinks and geckos are insectivorous lizards, and reptiles can carry *Salmonella*. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 石龙子 in the middle grade (中品); its preface says middle-grade drugs nourish the nature, and some are toxic. The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise; it lists urinary complaints and bleeding. The aliases, habitat and harvest come from the Wu Pu and Mingyi layers. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 石龙子 *shilongzi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2767)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A middle-grade animal-drug for urinary complaints. SHORT entry: the sources give little.

### Contraindications

- **Urinary stone, bleeding.** Need medical assessment.
- **Hygiene.** Reptiles can carry *Salmonella*; do not handle or eat them for these uses.
- **Conservation and law.** Wild reptile collection is regulated in many places. [USER: verify local rules]
- **Identification.** The classic's animal may be a gecko, a skink or another reptile.
