# Pundarika [SS] — Pundarika (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna tr.), "red-lotus" in the translator's gloss

## *A lotus (the name means a lotus bloom in Sanskrit) listed as a member of the Utpaladi group; the translator glosses it "(red-lotus)" in one place, where the standard Ayurvedic reading of the name is the white lotus, so species and colour are unresolved; the candidates are *Nelumbo nucifera* Gaertn. (sacred lotus) or a *Nymphaea* water lily. Confidence: low. It is not the same drug as Prapaundarika (CPU-00378)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Pundarika [SS]"; this entry replaces it. The text is Bhishagratna's English, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, with Vols. 2 and 3. The existing entry for Prapaundarika (CPU-00378, `I_425_Prapaundarika_Nymphaea_lotus.md`) states that Sushruta lists Prapaundarika separately from Pundarika; the two are kept apart here.

**Ayurveda (SS).** The group. Sutrasthana ch. 38, the Utpaladi group (Vol. I line 15717): "The drugs known as Utpala, Raktotpala, Kumuda, Saugondhika, Kuvalaya, Pundarika and Madhuka constitute the group known as the Utpaladi Gana." Its stated effect (lines 15721 to 15727): "This group is possessed of the therapeutic virtue of allaying thirst and corrects the deranged Pittam and the vitiated blood. It assuages the burning sensation of the body and proves curative in cases of vomiting, in Hridroga (Angina pectoris), in syncope, in haemoptysis and in cases of poisoning as well." The group's other members include Utpala (CPU-00391) and Kumuda (CPU-00392), which have their own entries.

The flower. In the Uttaratantra's account of fever treatment the patient's chamber is to be scented and decorated with "Mansi, Tamala, Musta, Kunkuma, Padma-leaves, Jati flower, Utpala (blue-lotus), Priyangu, Kesara (Bakula), and Pundarika (red-lotus), Punnaga, Naga-kesara, and Karavira" (Vol. III line 12633). The gloss "red-lotus" is the translator's and sits beside "Utpala (blue-lotus)"; no botanical name is printed.

The homonyms, set aside. The word Pundarika is also used in the text for (a) a kind of Shali rice in the list at Vol. I line 19716, (b) a type of skin disease (Kushtha), described at Vol. II line 3455 ("the patches resemble the petals" of the lotus; line 3401 lists it among the types), (c) a kind of snake (Vol. II line 30286), and (d) a poisoning, "Pundarika-poisoning", with enlargement of the abdomen and redness of the eyes (Vol. II line 29472). These are not this drug. Pundarika is also listed with Padma, Utpala, Nalina and Kumuda among the aquatic plants whose decomposed matter is said to give rise to the non-venomous leeches (Vol. I line 7244). That is a plant reference to the lotus, not a use of the drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Through the Utpaladi group: thirst, Pitta and blood disorders, burning of the body, vomiting, cardiac pain, syncope, haemoptysis and poisoning; and as a scented flower in a fever chamber.
- **What the text does not say.** It prints no effect, preparation or dose for Pundarika alone.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation of Pundarika alone is given in the lines read; the flower is used for scenting the patient's chamber (Vol. III line 12633). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Lotuses and water lilies are aquatic plants whose flowers have long been used as scents and in traditional drinks and infusions. Which species is meant here is not established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Pundarika alone. The Utpaladi group as a whole allays thirst and corrects Pitta and blood (lines 15721 to 15727). The cooling quality is the group's, and is not attributed here to the single flower.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The flower sits in the Utpaladi gana of Sutrasthana ch. 38, with a cooling therapeutic account for Pitta and blood. No rasayana or vajikarana assignment for it was found in the passages read, and no *ojas* claim is made. The status is unattested.

**Practitioner's note.** A lotus bloom in the cooling group, used against thirst, heat and bleeding, and as a scent in a fever room. Nothing here makes it a cultivation substance.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** Unresolved; the "red-lotus" gloss conflicts with the usual white-lotus reading. [USER: verify against a commentary.]
- **Haemoptysis, syncope, cardiac pain, poisoning.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Conservation.** Wild water lilies and lotuses should not be gathered for medicine on the strength of this entry.
