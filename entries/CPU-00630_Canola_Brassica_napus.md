# Rape — Canola, Colza, Rapeseed, Yun Tai (芸薹)
## *Brassica napus* L. | Family: Brassicaceae
**Tradition Tags:** AT (Assyrian), CM (Avicenna), DMM (Dioscorides), SBJ (Shennong lineage)
**Energetic Classification:** Warm and moist (Galenic 1st–2nd degree) — a pungent, dispersing seed-oil
**Training Phase:** Foundation (dietary lipid base), with pressed leaf/poultice as a Purification-phase topical

---

### Origin & Tradition

A necessary distinction frames this entry: **historical rapeseed and modern canola are not the same oil.** Traditional *Brassica napus* seed was high in **erucic acid** (a long-chain fatty acid linked in animal studies to cardiac lipidosis) and high in glucosinolates. Modern **canola** ("Canadian oil, low acid") is a mid-20th-century low-erucic, low-glucosinolate cultivar bred specifically to remove those hazards. Every traditional citation below refers to the old high-erucic seed and its lamp-oil, condiment and topical uses — *not* to the modern edible oil, whose evidence is reviewed separately.

**Dioscorides (De Materia Medica):** Cultivated *Brassica* and rape (*napus/gongylis* group) appear as pot-herbs and seed-drugs; the pungent seed was used, mustard-like, as a warming, dispersing rubefacient in poultices and its oil as a base for other medicines. The warming, drawing quality of the crushed seed is the consistent theme.

**Avicenna (Qanun fi'l-Tibb):** Rape seed (*shaljam*/colza group) is classed hot and dry-to-moist, carminative and emmenagogue, the pressed oil used as an emollient carrier and the seed as a warming digestive and topical resolvent for swellings — the standard Brassica seed profile in Unani medicine.

**Assyrian (cuneiform materia medica):** Brassica/mustard-family oilseeds are among the pressed-oil and seed drugs of the Mesopotamian tablets, used as unguent bases and warming topical applications — an early instance of seed-oil as pharmaceutical vehicle.

**Shennong lineage (Chinese materia medica):** *Yun tai* (芸薹, oilseed rape) is a recognised Chinese vegetable-medicine; the greens and seed are pungent-warm, used to move blood and disperse stasis, resolve swellings and abscesses topically, and the seed-oil as a household lipid — a dispersing, circulation-moving food-medicine.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Across traditions rape reads as a *pungent disperser* — the crushed seed warms, draws, and moves stagnation, exactly the signature of its mustard-family glucosinolate/isothiocyanate chemistry. The symbolic "moving oil" also tracks the real role of the pressed oil as a carrier that carries other actives into tissue. Modern breeding kept the useful dietary lipid and deliberately stripped the doctrinally "hot/sharp" erucic and glucosinolate load.

### Active Components

- **Modern canola oil:** low in saturated fat; high in **monounsaturated oleic acid**; a notable plant source of **alpha-linolenic acid (ALA, omega-3)**; contains tocopherols and phytosterols.
- **Erucic acid** — high in traditional/HEAR rapeseed; deliberately bred **out** of canola (regulated to low levels).
- **Glucosinolates** (e.g. progoitrin, gluconapin) — pungent, goitrogen-precursor sulphur compounds; high in old cultivars, low in modern canola/double-low varieties; the seed's rubefacient/dispersing chemistry.
- **Isothiocyanates** — glucosinolate breakdown products; antimicrobial, pungent, the topical "drawing" agents.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Canola oil (Foundation phase):** a dietary lipid base — a low-saturated, ALA-containing cooking oil used as the everyday fat in a Foundation nutritional protocol, where the goal is a favourable baseline lipid environment. Its clinical evidence (below) supports modest LDL/total-cholesterol improvement versus less favourable fats.

**Seed/leaf (Purification phase, topical):** the traditional pungent poultice — a warming, dispersing external application for stagnation and swellings, consistent with its glucosinolate/isothiocyanate chemistry. Internal use of the high-erucic traditional seed-oil is **not** endorsed; only modern low-erucic canola belongs in the diet.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- Pourrajab et al. (2022), *Critical Reviews in Food Science and Nutrition* — systematic review and meta-analysis of 13 RCTs: canola oil consumption significantly reduced serum LDL-c (WMD −6.13 mg/dl), total cholesterol (WMD −8.92 mg/dl) and the LDL-c/HDL-c ratio compared with olive oil, supporting a cardioprotective dietary role. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10408398.2022.2100314)
- Maleki Sedgi et al. (2024), *British Journal of Nutrition* — 12-week RCT (n=110) in non-alcoholic fatty liver disease: replacing ghee with rapeseed (canola) oil significantly reduced hepatic steatosis, liver enzymes (ALT, GGT), triglycerides, LDL, fasting glucose, insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) and anthropometric measures. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114524000564)

**Evidence grade:** Moderate-to-good clinical evidence for **modern low-erucic canola oil** as a favourable dietary fat (RCTs and meta-analysis for lipid and metabolic endpoints). Traditional seed/leaf and topical uses are traditional-only. The historical high-erucic material is superseded on safety grounds and should not be conflated with canola.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** *Yun tai* — pungent, warm; moves blood, disperses stasis, resolves swellings/abscesses (topical); seed-oil a warming household lipid.
- **Ayurveda (Brassica seed-oil analogy):** *rasa* katu (pungent); *guna* tikshna (sharp), ushna (hot); *virya* ushna (heating); *vipaka* katu (pungent); kapha- and vata-pacifying, pitta-increasing in excess.
- **Galenic / Unani:** Warm and moist-to-dry, 1st–2nd degree; carminative, emmenagogue, resolvent; pressed oil an emollient carrier (Avicenna).

### Contraindications

- **⚠️ Erucic acid (historical/HEAR oil):** high-erucic traditional rapeseed oil is associated with cardiac lipidosis in animal models and is regulated in food; **do not use high-erucic oil internally** — only modern low-erucic canola is food-safe.
- **Glucosinolate/goitrogen load:** high-glucosinolate old cultivars have goitrogenic potential; relevant for the traditional material and for thyroid-compromised individuals, not for modern double-low canola in ordinary amounts.
- **Topical seed poultice:** pungent isothiocyanates are irritant/rubefacient — risk of blistering with prolonged skin contact; patch-test and limit exposure.
- **Brassica allergy / mustard allergy:** possible cross-reactivity; caution.
- **Not a substitute for lipid-lowering medical care;** dietary benefit is modest and adjunctive.
