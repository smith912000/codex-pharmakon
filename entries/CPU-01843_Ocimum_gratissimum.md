# Ocimum gratissimum — Ajagandha, Aja-gandha (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Ocimum gratissimum, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Ocymum  gratissimum.", line 31210) against the Sanskrit name Ajagandha (OCR "Ajagandh££", line 31153). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print Ajagandha (and Aja-gandha) in many recipes, with no Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The chapters name Ajagandha in about a dozen recipes and plasters, and one footnote ties the name to a heat-making group; they give no Latin and do not call it an Ocimum, so the Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed and the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 357 gives the name as "Ajagandh££", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31153; id line 31210)" and the Latin as "Ocymum  gratissimum." (the batch note records the merged printed form "Ocymum gratissimum"), with the note "Appendix 3 two-column OCR paired by position (block of 31)". The OCR garbles the last letters of the name; the chapters print Ajagandha.

**The pairing, checked by counting.** The first page of the appendix prints thirty-one Sanskrit names (lines 31126 to 31181) and thirty-one Latin entries (31184 to 31235), with Amalaka and Amalaki bracketed as one. Ajagandha is the sixteenth Sanskrit name (Abhaya, Abhraka, Adhaki, Aditya-vaktra, Ardraka, Agni-dhama, Agasti, Agastya, Agni, Agniksa, Agni-mantha, Aguru, Ahifena, Ahigandha, Airavata, **Ajagandha**) and "Ocymum gratissimum." is the sixteenth Latin entry. The start of the column agrees name for name ("Terminalia Chebula" for Abhaya, "Talc" for Abhraka, "Zingiber" for Ardraka, "Soot" for Agni-dhama, "Aquilaria agallocha" for Aguru, "Papaver Somniferum. Opium." for Ahifena), and the names after it fit: Ajamoda with "Pimpinella Involucrata" (31212), Aja-shringi with "Gymnema Sylvestre" (31214), Alarka with "Calotropis gigantia" (31219) and Alavu with "Lagenaria Vulgaris" (31220). The same page is counted in the entries on Aristolochia indica (I_1373), Cleome viscosa (I_1381), Pavonia odorata (I_1376) and Andropogon acicularis (I_1390). [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Ajagandha and its OCR and hyphenated forms (Ajagandha, Aja-gandha, Ajagandhd) and by fuzzy matching of every word. The name is printed in these places; Ajagara (a python) and Ajagari (a plant of Chikitsa 30) are different words.

*Plugs, plasters and oils for ulcers and swellings.* In the chapter on preliminary measures (Sutra ch.5, `SS_Sutra_ch05.txt` lines 146 to 152): a lint plug "plastered over with the (paste) Kalka of sesamum, honey and clarified butter, and soaked in disinfectant (lit: purifying medicines such as Ajagandha, etc.) should be inserted deep into the cavity of the wound." In the chapter of miscellaneous remedies for swellings (Sutra ch.36, the *Mishraka* chapter), lines 41 to 45: "A plaster, composed of Ajagandha, Ashvagandha, Kala, Asarala, Ekaishika, and Ajashringi pasted together, and applied to the spot, leads to the resolution of a Kaphaja swelling (appearing at any part of the body)"; lines 96 to 100, the *Shodhana Varti*: "A lint saturated with a plaster of Ajagandha, Ajashringi, Gavakshi, Langalahvaya, Putika, Chitraka, Patha, Vidanga, Ela, Renuka, Tri-katu, Yavakshara, the five kinds of salt, Manahshila, ..."; and lines 122 to 126: "Oil or clarified butter prepared with the aforesaid Ajagandha, Ajashringi, etc, and Kasisa, Katurohini, Jatikanda, and the two kinds of Haridra, and applied to an ulcer or open swelling, purifies its interior." In Chikitsa ch.1 (lines 553 to 558) the powders "of the drugs with which the purifying plug or the lint has been enjoined to be plastered (Ajagandha, &c.)" are used to purify deep, foul-smelling ulcers covered with layers of deranged fat. In Chikitsa ch.17 (lines 118 to 121), Kaphaja Visarpa "readily yield to a proper application of a medicinal plaster (Pradeha) composed of Aja-gandha, Ashva-gandha, Sarala, Kala, Ekaishika and Aja-shringi pasted with the urine of a cow." In Chikitsa ch.18 (lines 470 to 478), for Kaphaja goitre, a plaster of "Aja-gandha, Ati-visha, Vishalya, Vishanika, Kushtha, Shukahvaya, Gunja (taken in equal parts)" pasted with the alkaline water from the ashes of Palasha wood and applied hot.

*A heat-making group.* A translator's footnote in Chikitsa ch.19 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch19.txt` lines 82 to 83) says of the "heat-making drugs" used in a poultice: "Some say that the heat-producing drugs are those comprised in the Aja-gandhadi group mentioned in the Mis'raka chapter, Sutra Sthanam." The Sutra 38 group lists do not print a group under that name; the Mishraka chapter is Sutra 36, the one that lists the Ajagandha plasters above. The Codex records the footnote and does not equate the group with a Sutra 38 list.

*A fever powder (Sutra ch.44).* `SS_Sutra_ch44.txt` lines 140 to 143, in the chapter on the choice of purgatives: "A compound consisting of equal parts of sugar and powdered Ajagandha, Tvakkshiri, Vidari and Trivrit and licked with honey and clarified butter, proves curative in a fever with thirst and a burning sensation of the body."

*Compounds for Vayu and Gulma.* In Chikitsa ch.5 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt` lines 677 to 690), the *Hingvadi-vati*: a powder of asafoetida, Trikatu, Vacha, Ajamoda, Dhanya, Ajagandha and about twenty other drugs, treated many times with the expressed juice of Matulunga, "should be made into boluses, each weighing an Aksha (two Tolas) in weight. One (such) pill should be taken (in an empty stomach) every morning in all diseases of the deranged Vayu. This compound proves curative in cough, asthma, internal tumour (Gulma), ascites, heart-disease, ..." In Uttara ch.42 (lines 186 to 192), the *Hingvadi Ghrita*, clarified butter cooked with Hingu, Sauvarchala salt, Ajaji, Vit salt, Dadima, Dipyaka, Pushkara, Vyosha, Dhanyaka, Amla-vetasa, Yava-kshara, Chitraka, Sathi, Vacha, Ajagandha, Ela and Surasa as Kalka with curd, "proves efficacious in a case of Vataja Gulma, colic-pain and suppression of stool and urine". In Chikitsa ch.37, the "Third Taila" (lines 66 to 76), an oil of Chitraka, Ativisha, Patha, Danti, Vilva, Vacha, Guggulu, Sarala and about twenty others including Ajagandha, cooked with milk and oil, "highly efficacious in the disorders of the deranged Vayu", and used as an *Anuvasana* enema.

*The ear (Uttara ch.21).* `SS_Uttara_ch21.txt` lines 32 to 39: Karna-shula (ear-ache) of Vayu and Kapha "yield to the application of fomentation to the affected part with (the fumes of) Vilva, Eranda-roots, Arka, Varshabhu, Kapittha, Dhustura, Shigru, Ajagandha, Ashvagandha, ..., barley and bamboo, boiled in Aranala (fermented rice-gruel) and administered in the manner of Nadi-sveda (fomentation through a pipe)."

*Two infant chapters (Uttara ch.34 and 35).* In the chapter on an attack by Shita-putana (`SS_Uttara_ch34.txt` lines 21 to 25): "Dungs of an owl and a vulture, the cast-off skin of a snake as well as Ajagandha and Nimba leaves and Yashti-madhu should be used for fumigating purposes, and Lamba (Tiktalavu), Gunja and Kakadani should be worn (as a charm) by the child." In the chapter on Mukha-mandika (`SS_Uttara_ch35.txt` lines 13 to 16): "Oil and fat (in equal parts) should be duly cooked with the expressed juice of the Bhringaraja, Ajagandha and Harigandha (Ashwagandha) and be used in anointing the child's body." The first is a fumigation and an amulet and is recorded, not endorsed.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Ajagandha found nothing.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Ocimum gratissimum* and Ajagandha found no entry on this plant. **I_398** (*Cleome gynandra*, Tarkari, tagged SS) says the names Suvarchala and Ajagandha "appear in wound-cleansing washes, plugs and potherb lists (ch. 5, 36, 46)" and that which of the names belongs to *Cleome gynandra* "is a later identification"; it overlaps with this entry on the Sutra 5 and Sutra 36 passages and the Codex does not reconcile the two. Three entries cover other basils and are not this plant: **I_237** (Tulsi), **I_463** (Holy Basil, *O. tenuiflorum*) and **I_442** (sweet basil). **I_1381** (*Cleome viscosa*, Aditya-vaktra) and **I_1390** (*Andropogon acicularis*) print Ajagandha only as a neighbour in the page count. Nothing is merged. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Ocimum* is a genus of the mint family (Lamiaceae), and its members are aromatic herbs rich in volatile oils. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. In the Sushruta text, Ajagandha is a recipe ingredient: in plugs, plasters and oils for Kaphaja swellings and ulcers, a goitre plaster, a powder for fever with thirst, the compounds against Vayu and Gulma, a fomentation for ear-ache and two infant remedies. The text gives it no action singly. The Codex does not recommend any of these, gives no protocol, and notes that the plant behind the name rests on a translator's appendix pairing.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters give these preparations that include the name: lint plugs and plasters pasted with cow's urine or alkaline water (Sutra 5 and 36; Chikitsa 17 and 18); oil or ghrita (Sutra 36; Uttara 42; Chikitsa 37); a licked powder with sugar, honey and ghee (Sutra 44); a bolus; a fumigation; the juice in an anointing oil. The one dose printed is for a compound containing Ajagandha: of the *Hingvadi-vati* "each [bolus] weighing an Aksha (two Tolas)", "one (such) pill ... every morning in all diseases of the deranged Vayu" (Chikitsa 5). No quantity is given for Ajagandha itself. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** none is given for Ajagandha singly. The chapters read print no *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* under that name. The one quality attached to it is indirect: a translator's footnote in Chikitsa 19 (lines 82 to 83) says "some say" the heat-producing drugs are those of the Ajagandhadi group.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Ajagandha only in therapeutic contexts: ulcer and swelling plugs and plasters, goitre, Visarpa, fever, Vayu and Gulma, ear-ache and two infant remedies. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with this herb; it is an ingredient in treatments of illness, and the Latin name behind Ajagandha is a translator's appendix pairing. The infant fumigation and amulet are recorded in the text and are not a recommendation.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this herb. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Wound plugs, enemas and plasters with alkaline water or caustic salts are clinical measures and not home ones; fever, goitre, ear-ache and infant illness need medical care. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
