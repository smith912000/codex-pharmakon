# Bark-of-the-Pomegranate [EP] — "Bark-of-the-Pomegranate" and "Bark-of-the-Pomegranate-tree-soaked-in-Beer" (Bryan 1930, English rendering of the Papyrus Ebers)

## *The bark (in one remedy the root bark) of the pomegranate tree, Punica granatum L.; the identification is Bryan's own English ("Pomegranate"), repeated in the working list `_sources/ebers.csv` (Pomegranate (root), Punica granatum, citing Nunn 1996), and is of medium confidence: the tree is a well-defined plant and the English word is unambiguous, but the Egyptian word is not printed in the text on disk, so the match rests on the translator* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** Pomegranate root and stem bark contain alkaloids that are toxic in quantity; the bark was the historic worm remedy. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take this bark on the strength of it.

**Named only by the Ebers papyrus among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry, and the text is a modern English rendering, not the hieratic: Bryan 1930, `Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`. The name index (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`) holds the row "Bark-of-the-Pomegranate, Bryan 1930, Ch. VI Plant Remedies (line 1321)", with the note "forms listed: Bark-of-the-Pomegranate | Bark-of-the-Pomegranate-tree-soaked-in-Beer". The fruit itself is not a separate Ebers entry in Bryan's list (line 1321 and the lines about it hold only the bark).

**Egyptian (EP).** In Bryan's alphabetical list of "recognizable Plants" (Chapter VI, "Plant Remedies"; the intro is at lines 1231-1233) the pomegranate appears twice, as "Bark-of-the-Pomegranate" and "Bark-of-the-Pomegranate-tree-soaked-in-Beer" (lines 1321-1322), just after "Blossoms-of-the-Peppermint" and before "Poppy-plant". Bryan prints no Egyptian word and gives no grade.

*Remedy 1, for the roundworm* (Bryan's chapter on indigestion and the worms; text at lines 1950-1956). Bryan writes that the roundworm "was expelled by means of mixtures and draughts, two of the latter meriting commendation". The first is headed "TO KILL THE ROUNDWORM" and lists "Bark-of-the-Pomegranate-root" and "Water", with the directions "Keep moist, strain, and take for a day." The ingredient is here "Pomegranate-root", a slightly different form from the list entry, so the match to the list item is by sense and is ours.

*Remedy 2, for the "uxedu"* (text at lines 3262-3270; Bryan's page headed "THE AAA DISEASE", printed page 121). Bryan says that for the uncomplicated *uxedu* the Scribe gives "a series of mixtures to be taken, several ointments, and various suppositories", and that "one mixture is in the nature of an infusion": "ANOTHER FOR THE UXEDU / Bark-of-the-Pomegranate-tree-soaked-in-Beer / Keep moist in a jug of Water / ... Strain a sufficient amount each morning and let the Patient drink." Bryan does not identify the *uxedu* as one modern disease; at line 3919 he translates a passage in which it is "like beans, discharging abscesses arise in his skin". The identification of the complaint is therefore open.

No quantity is printed with either remedy in Bryan's rendering. No recitation was found attached to them in the lines read.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the bark and root bark of the pomegranate carry tannins and a group of piperidine alkaloids (the best known is pelletierine), which were the basis of the tree's long use against tapeworm in later pharmacy, and which are toxic in excess. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Toxic in quantity.**

- **What the text says it is for.** To kill the roundworm (draught of the root bark in water); and, soaked in beer and then in water, a morning drink for the *uxedu* (Bryan's rendering).
- **What the text does not say.** The quantity, the strength, the number of days, or what the *uxedu* is.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this bark. Intestinal worms are a medical condition that needs proper diagnosis and treatment.

### Preparation & Dosing

Preparations in the sources read: the root bark kept moist in water, strained, taken for a day (line 1954-1956); the bark of the tree "soaked in Beer", then "kept moist in a jug of Water", a sufficient amount strained each morning and drunk (lines 3269-3271). "A sufficient amount" is the only measure printed. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Pomegranate (*Punica granatum*) is a long-cultivated Mediterranean and Near Eastern tree. The bark and root bark have a long record in later pharmacy as a taenifuge, and their alkaloids are toxic at higher intake, so a historical worm remedy of this kind agrees in kind with the pharmacology. This does not make any quantity safe. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian (EP).** *(c) Absent.* The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution. The two remedies read are a worm drug and a drink for a complaint Bryan does not identify, and neither is placed in the plural person (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) or presented with *heka*. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** A bitter, tannin-rich, alkaloid-bearing bark used against worms. There is nothing here for a cultivator to take or do; the only discipline it teaches is that an effective worm drug is also a poison, and that the Ebers text gives no quantity to tell the two apart.

### Contraindications

- **Toxic in quantity.** The alkaloids of the bark are poisonous in excess (see Active Components). Do not take it.
- **Pregnancy.** Avoid. No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
