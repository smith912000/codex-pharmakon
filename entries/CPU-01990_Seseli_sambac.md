# Seseli sambac — Vana-yamani (Sushruta, Bhishagratna's Appendix 3, as aligned by position)

## *An umbellifer (carrot-family herb) named only by the Latin synonym "Seseli Sambac" in Bhishagratna's appendix; no accepted binomial of that form is known to us, so the identification is unresolved: family plausible, genus and species low confidence, and the Sanskrit name is ours by position in a damaged list* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The only attestation is the Sushruta Samhita in Bhishagratna's English translation. A search of the other texts on disk for *Seseli sambac* found nothing. The Codex has a different entry for *Seseli tortuosum* (CPU-00961, Dioscorides, Avicenna, Assyrian), which is not this row.

**Sushruta (SS).** The Latin name stands once, in "Appendix No. 3. Names of Drugs (with Latin synonyms)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, line 33251): ".  Seseli  Sambac." The appendix prints the Sanskrit names in one column and the Latin synonyms in another, and the OCR has separated them. In the V section the Sanskrit names run from *Vacha* (line 33214) to *Vata-patri*; the Latin synonyms run from "Acorus Calamus" (line 33240) to "Colcus Ambjnicus". The two lists differ in length by about two (the Sanskrit list has *Vacha* and *Vacha'*, *Vams'a* and *Vams'aka*, *Va'saka* and *Va'sa'* where the Latin list has one line each). Counting down with those pairs merged, "Hibiscus Vitiolius" (line 33250) falls against *Vana-ka'rpa'sa* (line 33224) and "Seseli Sambac" against *Vana-yama'ni* (line 33225), and the next line, "Calamus Rotong", against *Vanjula*. The Sanskrit name is therefore ours by counting, not printed beside the Latin. [USER: verify against the printed page.]
