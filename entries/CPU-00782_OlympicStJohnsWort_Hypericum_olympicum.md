# Olympic / Mount-Olympus St John's Wort

## *Hypericum olympicum* L. | Family: Hypericaceae

**Tradition Tags:** DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Bitter, resinous-aromatic, faintly astringent; cool-drying. A mountain Hypericum carrying the genus wound/light/nervine doctrine — but standing on its own genuine claim: it is the source plant of a notable anti-MRSA phloroglucinol.

**Training Phase:** Peripheral / Reference, with a Compound-Discovery footnote — catalogued as a boundary Hypericum (not the medicinal *H. perforatum*), but flagged as chemically important in its own right.

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Hypericum olympicum* is a small, cushion-forming, woody-based perennial of the mountains of the Balkans and Anatolia (its epithet points to Mount Olympus, and its range runs through Greece, North Macedonia, Serbia, and Turkey). Like *H. calycinum* it is grown widely as a hardy rock-garden ornamental with large golden flowers. It is a genuine Balkan/Anatolian montane wildflower, and much of its literature comes from Serbian, Greek, and Macedonian phytochemists working on the region's Hypericum flora.

**The hard distinction (again, first).** *H. olympicum* is **not** *Hypericum perforatum*, the medicinal St John's Wort in this codex. In the Balkans the whole genus is called "St John's wort" ("*spathohorto*", "*valsamo*" in Greek; according to PubMed, Pyrka et al. 2021, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-1379-3249)), and *H. olympicum* is swept up in that vernacular — but the antidepressant/antiviral clinical evidence base belongs to *H. perforatum* and does **not** transfer. The DNA-barcoding literature exists precisely because these species are visually and commercially confused and need molecular discrimination (Pyrka et al. 2021, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-1379-3249)). Do not import *H. perforatum* — or *H. calycinum* — data as species-specific to *H. olympicum*.

**Doctrine of the mountain wort (DMM — genus / signatures stream).** *H. olympicum* inherits the full genus doctrine: the solar midsummer flowering, the "light-through-perforate-glands" signature, the blood-red hypericin exudate read as a wound/blood correspondence, and the guardian-nervine associations bound up with St John's Eve. Its montane, Olympus-named habitat has, in the folk-symbolic reading, only intensified the "high, clean, solar" character attributed to it. As with its ornamental cousins, this is attribution-by-genus: the doctrine arrives with the family name, not with a demonstrated species-specific medicine.

**Established/attested medicinal use (CM — Continental/Balkan folk stream).** Honest statement: species-specific traditional indications for *H. olympicum* are **sparse and poorly documented**. It is used regionally as a "St John's wort" tea within the general genus reputation, but robust, olympicum-specific ethnomedical claims are not well attested in the retrieved literature. The plant is catalogued here mostly for its chemistry and as a genus-boundary marker — the entry says so plainly rather than dressing it in *H. perforatum*'s reputation.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** *H. olympicum* is the most interesting of the three ornamental-Hypericum boundary entries, because here the deflation partly *reverses*. The genus doctrine (solar wound-and-light herb) is, as usual, a correspondence argument that predicts healing from photoactive red pigment — and, as usual, the same pigment predicts phototoxicity as much as cure. But *H. olympicum* also carries something the doctrine never anticipated and modern chemistry did find: a specific acylphloroglucinol, **olympicin A**, with real, potent activity against drug-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* (see below). So the honest doctrinal reading is layered: (1) the romantic "light-herb" signature does not license the plant's folk reputation — that remains under-evidenced; but (2) the underlying phloroglucinol chemistry that the genus signature crudely gestures at (resinous, antimicrobial gland-exudate) turns out to contain a genuinely antibacterial molecule. The signature is wrong about *why*; the chemistry is right that something in the glands fights infection. Map the doctrine onto that phloroglucinol chemistry, not onto the hypericin mysticism.

### Active Components

Confirmed, species-specific where cited:

- **Acylphloroglucinols — including olympicin A.** The defining compound. Bioassay-directed work on the genus isolated **olympicin A** *from H. olympicum*, a geranyloxy-substituted acylphloroglucinol with strong anti-staphylococcal activity (according to PubMed, Rahman et al. 2018, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejmech.2018.05.038)). This is the plant's genuine chemical signature and its claim to scientific relevance.
- **Naphthodianthrones** — hypericin and hyperforin/hyperforin-class compounds are present and have been quantified in *H. olympicum* by LC-MS/MS among nine Balkan Hypericum species (according to PubMed, Smelcerovic & Spiteller 2006, PMID 16599273, *Pharmazie* 61(3):251–2 — no DOI available for this record).
- **Flavonoids and phenolic acids** — hyperoside, quercitrin, quercetin glycosides, catechin; *H. olympicum* is characterised as a *potent source of phenolic metabolites* (according to PubMed, Kovačik et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plaphy.2022.06.009)) and is rich in flavonoid-family secondary metabolites with strong antioxidant activity (according to PubMed, Kakouri et al. 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox12040899)).

Note: presence of hypericin/hyperforin markers again does **not** equal therapeutic equivalence to standardised *H. perforatum* extract. Content varies markedly by species and locality (Smelcerovic & Spiteller 2006; Kovačik et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plaphy.2022.06.009)).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Within Kenetica, *H. olympicum* is a **reference/boundary entry with a compound-discovery footnote.** As a whole-plant remedy it is not a validated protocol tool — its folk indications are too thin. Its real place in the system is instructive and chemical:

1. It reinforces the "key on species, not genus" discipline alongside *H. calycinum*.
2. It flags the phloroglucinol lane of the genus as the scientifically live one: *H. olympicum* is a source organism for olympicin A, one of the concrete data points behind the modern interest in Hypericum phloroglucinol derivatives as antimicrobials against resistant staphylococci.

For a cultivator, that reframes the plant: not a self-medication tea, but a montane ornamental whose gland-exudate chemistry is of genuine antibacterial-lead interest.

### Scientific Correlation

Graded honestly, including nulls:

**Anti-staphylococcal (olympicin A) — STRONG at the molecule level, species-linked.** This is the standout, genuinely species-specific result. Olympicin A, isolated *from H. olympicum*, showed MICs of roughly 0.5–1 mg/L against a panel of clinical multi-drug-resistant (MDR) and methicillin-resistant *S. aureus* (MRSA) isolates; total synthesis and analogue work mapped the structure-activity requirements (an 8–10-carbon alkyloxy group ortho to a five-carbon acyl substituent on the phloroglucinol core) (according to PubMed, Rahman et al. 2018, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejmech.2018.05.038)). This is a compound-level, in-vitro result — a strong antibacterial-lead finding, **not** a demonstration that whole-plant *H. olympicum* is a clinical antibiotic. But it is real, potent, and traceable to this species.

**Antioxidant / radical-scavenging — MODERATE-to-STRONG, in-vitro.** *H. olympicum* extracts show strong antioxidant activity (DPPH/ABTS) among Greek Hypericum species (according to PubMed, Kakouri et al. 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox12040899)), and Balkan *H. olympicum* often matched or exceeded *H. perforatum* on antioxidant assays (according to PubMed, Božin et al. 2013, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules181011733)). Genuine but generic — a shared polyphenol property.

**Anticholinesterase (Alzheimer's-relevant) — WEAK/EXPLORATORY, in-vitro.** Balkan Hypericum species including *H. olympicum* showed notable in-vitro acetylcholinesterase inhibition, flagged only as a reason for *further* investigation (Božin et al. 2013, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules181011733)). This is a preliminary screen signal, not a therapeutic claim; do not overstate.

**Antidepressant / nervine — NULL for this species (evidence belongs to *H. perforatum*).** No retrieved study demonstrates antidepressant or clinically meaningful nervine activity for *H. olympicum*. Recording any would be fabrication.

**Phototoxicity — assumed present (genus/compound property).** As a hypericin-bearing species, phototoxicity is the expected class property (documented across the genus; the closely comparative work on Hypericum photoactivity is cited in the *H. calycinum* entry). Treated as a real caution, not decoration.

**Hyperaccumulation note (cultivation-relevant).** *H. olympicum* takes up and retains nickel and other elements in a species-specific way (Kovačik et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plaphy.2022.06.009)) — relevant to a cultivator's guide: material grown on metal-contaminated soil may carry an elemental load. Source clean-soil material.

**Bottom line:** a lightly-attested montane ornamental that shares the genus chemistry, shows solid in-vitro antioxidant activity, and — uniquely among these boundary entries — is the traceable botanical source of a potent anti-MRSA phloroglucinol (olympicin A). It does **not** carry *H. perforatum*'s therapeutic evidence. Whole-plant clinical data are sparse; stated as such.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Genus doctrine (DMM):** solar, montane wound-and-light herb; nervine/guardian by inheritance — attribution by genus, deflated above.
- **Folk (CM):** bitter, cool-drying; minor regional "St John's wort" use, poorly attested for this species.
- **Kenetica synthesis:** a signature-bearing mountain wort whose real gift is chemical, not devotional — cool, drying, antibacterial in its gland resin, phototoxic at the pigment.

### Contraindications

- ⚠️ **Photosensitisation (class caution).** As a hypericin-bearing species, *H. olympicum* can act as a photosensitiser; avoid pairing internal use with strong sun/UV, and treat fair-skinned or photosensitive individuals with care.
- ⚠️ **CYP450 / P-glycoprotein induction (class caution).** The genus — driven by hyperforin-class acylphloroglucinols — is a potent inducer of CYP3A4 and P-gp, causing serious herb-drug interactions (loss of efficacy of many co-medications: anticoagulants, oral contraceptives, immunosuppressants, antiretrovirals, etc.). Because *H. olympicum* contains these compounds, assume the induction risk until species-specific data exclude it. Do not combine with prescription drugs without professional review. - **Not a substitute for *H. perforatum*.** Do not use *H. olympicum* expecting antidepressant or standardised medicinal effect; the evidence does not exist.
