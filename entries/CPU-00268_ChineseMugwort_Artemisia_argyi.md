# Chinese Mugwort / Moxa — Ài Yè (艾葉) · "longevity grass" (cháng shòu cǎo)

## *Artemisia argyi* H.Lév. & Vaniot | Family: Asteraceae

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ (Shennong Ben Cao Jing lineage — *ài yè*, warming/hemostatic/gynaecological; the canonical moxibustion herb)

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese (Shennong / SBJ):** *Ài yè* is the single most important herb of **moxibustion (jiǔ, 灸)** — the burning of compressed mugwort ("moxa") over acupoints to drive warmth into the channels. Its documented medicinal use spans more than two millennia, and it is embedded in Chinese culture beyond medicine: hung at doorways at the Dragon Boat Festival as a protective, cold-dispelling herb. In the internal (decoction) tradition it is a **warming hemostatic** and a **gynaecological anchor** — used for cold-deficient uterine bleeding, dysmenorrhoea, cold in the lower abdomen, and threatened miscarriage (calming the fetus), classically in formulas such as *Jiao Ai Tang* (mugwort + donkey-hide gelatin). Its dual identity — burned externally as moxa, decocted internally as *ài yè* — makes it one of the most versatile single drugs in the Chinese pharmacopoeia.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Grass that Carries Fire:** The Codex reads *ài yè* as the tradition's chosen **vector of warmth**. Moxa is not merely a fuel; it is doctrinally understood as the herb that most cleanly conducts *yang* heat into a cold, stagnant, or bleeding channel. The "longevity grass" epithet ties directly to this: warmth restored to cold-deficient patterns is, in the tradition's logic, the restoration of life-heat. That the smoke and essential oil are both bioactive — antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory — and potentially irritant folds hazard and virtue into one plant, the same pattern the Codex tracks across warming drugs.

### Active Components

- **Essential oil (AAEO)** — the principal medicinal fraction; >200 volatile constituents reported, dominated by monoterpenoids and sesquiterpenoids including **1,8-cineole (eucalyptol), camphor, borneol, β-caryophyllene, and terpinen-4-ol**.
- **Flavonoids** (including eupatilin-type and jaceosidin-type) — anti-inflammatory/antioxidant fraction.
- **Terpenoids** — numerous, including newly characterised sesquiterpenes.
- **Combustion products (moxa smoke)** — bioactive but also a source of particulate and irritant exposure.

### Scientific Correlation

Attribution: findings from PubMed.

- **Essential-oil chemistry & pharmacology (review).** A comprehensive review of *Artemisia argyi* essential oil (AAEO) catalogued >200 constituents (terpenes, ketones/aldehydes, alcohols/phenols, acids/esters) and summarised anti-microbial, anti-inflammatory, analgesic and anti-cancer activity — while explicitly warning that **the toxicity of AAEO cannot be ignored** and mechanisms remain unclear (Liu et al., 2021, *J Ethnopharmacol*) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2021.114404).
- **Botany, phytochemistry & clinical application (review).** A 2024 review of *Artemisia argyi* Folium identified 136 compounds (including 23 new terpenoids and 2 new flavonoids) and mapped pharmacological effects to anti-inflammatory, anti-tumour, antioxidant and antibacterial actions, with clinical use across respiratory, immune, digestive, nervous-system and gynaecological indications (Wang et al., 2024, *Naunyn Schmiedebergs Arch Pharmacol*) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00210-024-03122-7).

**Evidence grade:** Chemistry — well characterised. Pharmacology — largely **preclinical / mechanistic** (anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, analgesic). Moxibustion clinical efficacy is a separate literature not assessed in these two reviews; do not overstate. Toxicity of the essential oil is flagged in the primary source.

### Contraindications

- **Yin-deficiency / heat patterns** — warming, drying nature aggravates heat and dryness.
- **Essential-oil toxicity** — AAEO toxicity is explicitly flagged (Liu et al., 2021); do not ingest neat, dilute for topical use.
- **Moxa smoke** — particulate/irritant exposure; ventilate; caution in asthma/respiratory sensitivity.
- **Pregnancy** — although traditionally used to "calm the fetus" in specific cold-deficient patterns, this is a supervised indication; self-use in pregnancy is not advised.
- **Bleeding disorders / anticoagulants** — hemostatic action may interact; supervise.
- **Burns** — direct moxa carries thermal-injury risk.
