# Winged spindle tree stem — 卫矛 *weimao*, also 鬼箭 ("ghost arrow")

## *Euonymus alatus (Thunb.) Siebold, the winged spindle tree, whose twigs bear corky wings; the identification is ours, made from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence. The classic names no part; "stem" is the Codex's label from the 案 below* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on one text in one edition, Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). A search of the other texts on disk for *Euonymus* found nothing.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The name line is 卫矛 (line 2531). The classic's own paragraph is at line 2533: 味苦，寒。主女子崩中下血，腹满汗出，除邪，杀鬼毒、虫注。一名鬼箭。生山谷。 In English: "Flavour bitter, cold. Governs in women 'collapse and descent of blood' (崩中下血, flooding and bleeding from the womb in the Codex's plain reading), fullness of the belly with sweating (腹满汗出); removes evil (除邪); kills ghost-poison and *chongzhu* (杀鬼毒、虫注; the second is a classical term for a worm-infestation illness that the Codex keeps untranslated). Also called 鬼箭. Grows in mountain valleys." The paragraph has no 久服 clause and names no part. The classic places the entry in the middle class (中经; heading 卷二 中经 at line 2541). The preface (line 1677) describes the middle class as nourishing the nature, either non-toxic or toxic, to be chosen as fit.

**Wu Pu (《吴普》, third century) layer.** Line 2535: "鬼箭, also called 卫矛. Shennong, Huangdi, Tongjun: bitter, non-toxic (无毒). The leaf is like a peach's, like a feather (叶，如桃如羽); gathered in the first, second and seventh months, dried in the shade. Or it grows in the fields" (quoted from the *Yulan*). This layer states "non-toxic" outright, in the same breath as the classic's flavour, for a middle-class drug whose preface allows both. The Codex records it as Wu Pu's.

**Mingyi bielu (《名医》, later additions) layer.** Line 2537: "grows at Mount Huo; gathered in the eighth month, dried in the shade" (生霍山。八月采，阴干).

**Sun Xingyan's note (案).** Line 2539: "The *Guangya* says: 鬼箭 is 神箭" (ghost arrow is spirit arrow). "Tao Hongjing says: its stalk has three feathers (vanes), shaped like arrow feathers" (其茎有三羽，状如箭羽). That is the only part named in the whole entry, the 茎 (stalk), and it is the reason for the Codex's label "stem"; the three vanes agree with the corky wings of *Euonymus alatus*, which is a general fact. The statement is Tao's, a later commentator's, and not the classic's.

### Active Components

None is stated in the text. As a general fact, *Euonymus* is a genus of the staff-tree family (Celastraceae), and the genus is known to contain cardenolide-type and other bioactive compounds in various species. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The classic records a bitter, cold material for uterine flooding and bleeding in women, a full belly with sweating, and ghost-poison and worm-infestation, without a preparation. The Codex gives no protocol and does not turn these into modern diagnoses. Uterine bleeding is a condition that needs medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic names no preparation and no quantity. Wu Pu and the Mingyi layer give gathering months (the first, second and seventh; the eighth) and drying in the shade; Tao names the stalk. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

The identification is ours; Tao's three-vaned stalk is the main descriptive support. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Divine Farmer's Classic).** 味苦，寒, "bitter, cold" (line 2533). Wu Pu: bitter, non-toxic (line 2535). No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle class (中经), the class the preface describes as nourishing the nature and sometimes toxic. Its 味 paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 卫矛 *weimao*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2533). Its status is therapeutic only. The Wu Pu, Mingyi and 案 layers add none.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator has no practice here. The classic offers a bitter, cold material for women's bleeding and worm-illness in the middle class, with no long-use claim, no preparation and no dose.

### Contraindications

The classic gives no warning, and Wu Pu says "non-toxic". The Codex did not read any source that confirms this for the plant. The Codex has not retrieved a safety source on this species. Modern Chinese sources are generally cautious about the drug in pregnancy, which the Codex states from general knowledge and not from a source read.
