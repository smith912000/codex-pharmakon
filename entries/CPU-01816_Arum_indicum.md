# Arum indicum — Manaka, Mana, Kachu (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3)

## *Arum indicum, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints against the Sanskrit names Mana and Manaka (line 32295, "Arum Indicum.") and, on an earlier page, against Kachu with the parenthesis "(Mana)" (line 31867, "Arum Indicum ; A. Colocasia."). ### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The chapters name the drug only by the Sanskrit name Manaka. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Tibetan text folders for *Arum indicum* found no hit; those folders hold other *Arum* names under their own chapters (for example *Arum maculatum* and *Arum colocasia* in the Dioscorides index, and the Codex's I_516). The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. Quotations keep the translator's words; the OCR's garbled diacritics are normalised. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The name in the chapters.* "Manaka" occurs in one place in the chapter files and "Kachu" in none. The word "Kachuris" (Sutra 46, line 2622) is a confection ("Luchis and Kachuris of our modern confectioners"), a different thing. A search of the chapter files for "Mana" as a drug name found only the Manaka passage below. [USER: verify]

*The bulb group (Sutra 46, lines 1918 to 1996).* In the chapter on food and drink, "The Bulb Group: Now we shall discourse on the virtues of edible bulbous plants or herbs (Kandas)." Manaka is in the second list: "The bulbs (Kanda) known as Sthula-Kanda, Shurana-Kanda, and Manaka, etc. have a slightly astringent and pungent taste, and tend to produce a state of dryness in the organism. They are heavy of digestion, and subdue the Pittam, and are long retained in the stomach in an undigested condition." The metrical verses add: "The species known as the Manaka is sweet, and cooling in its potency and heavy of digestion, while the one called the Sthula Kanda is not inordinately heat-making in its potency. The species Surana is usually found to be curative in cases of piles and rectal polypi and condylomata." The text gives two tastes for the group (slightly astringent and pungent) and a different one for Manaka itself (sweet); the Codex records both and does not resolve the difference.

*What the chapters do not say.* The text gives no preparation, no dose and no use of Manaka beyond its place in the bulb list, and names it in no recipe, no group of Sutra 38 and no rasayana or vajikarana chapter. The Latin pairing is the appendix's alone.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Arum indicum*, *Manaka*, *Mana* and *Kachu* found no entry on this plant under that binomial. I_487 (Taro, *Colocasia esculenta*; tags DMM, CM, SBJ, SS) prints *Kachu* in its heading and takes the Sushruta *kachu* to be taro without a chapter locus; the appendix's "A. Colocasia" on the Kachu line (31867) is the one point of contact, and whether the Sushruta Manaka, the appendix's Kachu and the Codex's taro are one drug is not shown by the lines read. I_516 (Cuckoo-pint, *Arum maculatum*) and I_780 (Jack-in-the-pulpit, *Arisaema erubescens*) are entries on other aroids. Nothing is merged; the decision is Smith's. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, the aroid family (Araceae) to which *Arum* and *Colocasia* belong commonly carries calcium oxalate crystals in its tissues, which irritate the mouth and throat unless the plant is cooked or otherwise treated. That is a statement about the family, not about what Sushruta ate or used under the name Manaka. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Manaka as an edible bulb of the bulb group, heavy of digestion, long retained in the stomach, drying and Pitta-subduing, and sweet and cooling according to the verses. These are the text's statements about a Sanskrit name, and the Latin name that the appendix attaches to it is the translator's, by position. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation and no dose is given in the sources retrieved; Manaka appears only in a list of edible bulbs. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Sushruta terms (Pittam, heavy, drying, cooling potency) are not measured outcomes, so no modern finding can be set beside them. The identity rests on a positional pairing in a translator's appendix, and several aroids are edible only after careful cooking, so a study of any one plant would not bear directly on the Sushruta drug.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** the chapters read print for Manaka, as a member of the bulb list (Sutra 46, lines 1983 to 1988): slightly astringent and pungent taste, drying, heavy of digestion, subduing Pittam and long retained in the stomach; and, in the verses (lines 1990 to 1993), sweet, cooling in potency and heavy of digestion. No *vipaka* is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text names Manaka only as an edible bulb in the bulb list of the food and drink chapter. A search of the chapter files found it in none of the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) or the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30), so the text assigns it no rejuvenative or generative status in the lines retrieved. Other bulbs in the same chapter carry such words (the neighbouring list names Vidari-kanda and Shatavari, "spermatopoietic" bulbs, and the Vrahakanda "rejuvenating"), but Manaka is not among them, and the Codex does not carry the words across. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The Latin identification is the appendix's. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. In the Sushruta scheme Manaka is a food bulb with a recorded heaviness and drying quality, and the text rates it among the hard-to-digest bulbs. Nothing should be gathered or eaten on the strength of a Latin name that the chapters do not give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read print no warning for the drug itself beyond its being "heavy of digestion" and "long retained in the stomach in an undigested condition". Aroids as a family can burn the mouth and throat when raw, and some are poisonous, and the identity here is open between *Arum indicum*, a *Colocasia* and the Codex's taro, so no plant should be gathered or eaten on the strength of this entry.
