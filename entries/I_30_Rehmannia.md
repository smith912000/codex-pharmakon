## Section 1 — Nomenclature

### Primary Scientific Name
*Rehmannia glutinosa* (Gaertn.) DC. (Orobanchaceae; formerly Scrophulariaceae)

### Nomenclature Table

| Tradition | Language | Name | Transliteration | Meaning |
|-----------|----------|------|-----------------|---------|
| Chinese (SBJ) | 文言文 | 熟地黃 | Shú Dì Huáng | "Cooked earth yellow" — the wine-steamed processed form; primary tonic form |
| Chinese (SBJ) | 文言文 | 生地黃 | Shēng Dì Huáng | "Raw earth yellow" — the fresh/dried unprocessed form; cooling, Blood-clearing |
| Chinese (SBJ) | 文言文 | 乾地黃 | Gān Dì Huáng | "Dried earth yellow" — the air-dried intermediate form |
| Arabic (CM) | العربية | — | — | Referenced in Islamic pharmacy as "Chinese earth root" via trade contacts |
| Tibetan (FMT) | བོད་སྐད། | ལྕམ་སྤ | Lcam pa | Standard Tibetan pharmaceutical name |
| English | — | Rehmannia / Chinese Foxglove | — | "Chinese Foxglove" from leaf resemblance to European foxglove (*Digitalis*) — no pharmacological relation |

### Botanical Identity
Perennial herb 20–30cm; sticky-hairy leaves and stem; red-purple tubular flowers; thick fleshy tuberous roots (the drug); yellow-orange interior when fresh; blackens when processed. Native to northern China; major cultivation in Henan province (Di Huang of Henan — *huai di huang* — is the highest quality designation). Three processing stages yield three distinct drugs: fresh root (*xian di huang*), dried root (*gan di huang* / *sheng di huang*), and wine-processed root (*shu di huang*) — each with different pharmacological character.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ)

*Shú Dì Huáng* (processed, upper class):
> *"Sweet; slightly warm. Enters Heart, Liver, Kidney channels. Nourishes Yin and Blood, tonifies Jing and marrow, strengthens bones and sinews. The primary herb for profound Yin-Jing deficiency: emaciation, lower back weakness, night sweats, vertigo, premature greying, spermatorrhoea, impaired hearing. The foundation of all Kidney-nourishing formulas."*

*Shēng Dì Huáng* (raw/dried):
> *"Sweet, bitter; cold. Clears Heat, nourishes Yin, cools the Blood, stops bleeding. Used for febrile disease with Blood Heat, epistaxis, haematuria, excessive uterine bleeding, thirst, and wasting conditions (xiaoke/diabetes)."*

The raw-to-processed transformation again (see He Shou Wu, Peony) converts a cooling/clearing herb into a warming tonic — one of the most sophisticated pharmaceutical principles in classical Chinese medicine.

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Islamic medicine references "Chinese earth root" in trade contexts as a Yin-nourishing and haematopoietic substance. The Jing-tonification framework is specific to Chinese/Tibetan traditions; CM describes the application in humoral terms as moistening, cooling, and strengthening.

### 2.3 Four Medical Tantras (FMT)

*Rgyud bzhi*:
> *"Lcam pa (rehmannia): sweet; neutral-cooling. Nourishes kidney essence, cools blood heat, strengthens the lower body and bones. Used in compound preparations for Kidney Yin deficiency patterns — the primary root for Jing tonification in Tibetan compound formulas."*

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Haematopoietic

**Catalpol + RGPs**: Stimulate haematopoietic stem cells in bone marrow → increase RBC, WBC, platelet production. RGPs activate erythropoiesis via erythropoietin upregulation. This validates the classical "nourishes Blood and Jing" — rehmannia genuinely stimulates blood cell production.

### 5.2 Anti-diabetic

**Catalpol**: AMPK activation → GLUT4 translocation → improved glucose uptake. Protects pancreatic beta cells from oxidative damage. Reduces advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) → anti-diabetic + anti-ageing. This maps to the classical *xiaoke* (wasting-thirsting disease = diabetes mellitus) application of raw rehmannia.

### 5.3 Neuroprotective

**Catalpol**: Elevates BDNF and NGF in hippocampus → promotes neurogenesis → protects against Alzheimer's pathology. Reduces β-amyloid aggregation and tau hyperphosphorylation. Anti-inflammatory in CNS (NF-κB, microglial modulation).

### 5.4 Renal / Kidney Protective

**Acteoside + catalpol**: Nephroprotective — reduce renal tubular oxidative damage; anti-fibrotic (TGF-β inhibition → reduces renal scarring). The "Kidney tonic" classification has a genuine pharmacological correlate in renal protection.

### 5.5 Processing Effect

Steaming with wine converts stachyose → 5-HMF and alters iridoid ratios. The cooked form (*shu di huang*) has: reduced catalpol, increased 5-HMF, altered polysaccharide profile, and higher sweet/warm character. The switch from cooling to warming is real and documented biochemically.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Diabetic nephropathy**: Multiple Chinese RCTs — rehmannia-containing formulas (especially *Liu Wei Di Huang Wan*, "Six Ingredient Pill with Rehmannia") significantly reduce proteinuria and creatinine in diabetic nephropathy; mechanistically supported.

**Anaemia / haematopoiesis**: Clinical evidence from Chinese hospital practice; catalpol haematopoietic stimulation in animal and in vitro studies.

**Alzheimer's / cognitive**: Catalpol animal studies very strong; some human data from formula studies.

**Anti-ageing**: AGE-inhibition, AMPK, BDNF — multiple mechanism studies; clinical evidence emerging.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The foundation formula**: *Liu Wei Di Huang Wan* (Six-Ingredient Pill with Rehmannia) — rehmannia as primary with cornus, dioscorea, poria, alisma, moutan — is the most widely prescribed formula in Chinese medicine history, covering the Kidney Yin deficiency pattern: hot flashes, night sweats, vertigo, tinnitus, lower back weakness, and reduced sexual function. This formula has been continuously prescribed for approximately 800 years (first formulated by Qian Yi, Song Dynasty, c. 1070 CE) and remains among the most commonly used in modern Chinese clinical practice. Rehmannia's centrality in this foundational formula explains its position as the primary Jing tonic herb.

**Earth yellow**: The Chinese name *di huang* (earth yellow) captures the root's appearance — a yellow-orange root emerging from earth. The deep earth association is pharmacologically apt: rehmannia addresses the deep constitutional (Jing/marrow/bone) level of deficiency, the "earth" of the body's fundamental resources.

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM (Shu Di Huang)**: Sweet; slightly warm. Channels: Heart (心), Liver (肝), Kidney (腎). Actions: Nourish Yin and Blood, tonify Jing, strengthen marrow. Foundation of Kidney Yin formulas.

**TCM (Sheng Di Huang)**: Sweet, bitter; cold. Clears Heat, cools Blood, nourishes Yin. For febrile and Blood-Heat conditions.

**Tibetan**: Sweet; neutral-cooling. Kidney Jing tonic; bone strengthening.

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Spleen deficiency / loose stools**: *Shu di huang* is heavy and greasy — causes digestive weakness in Spleen-deficient constitutions. Typically formulated with digestive herbs (Chen pi, sha ren) to buffer this effect.
- **Phlegm-damp patterns**: Avoid in excess dampness — worsens obstruction
- **Blood sugar monitoring**: Hypoglycaemic activity — adjust medication in T2DM
- **Drug interactions**: CYP3A4 modulation by iridoids — potential interactions with drugs metabolised by CYP3A4
