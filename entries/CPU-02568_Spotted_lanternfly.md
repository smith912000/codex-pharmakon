# Spotted lanternfly — 樗鸡 *chuji*, "Chu Ji" (the "Ailanthus fowl")

## *A small insect found on the 樗 (Ailanthus) tree, taken in modern works to be Lycorma delicatula (White), the spotted lanternfly; the identification is ours, of medium confidence, and the old commentaries describe the insect in terms that fit it only in part, so the modern name is a later reading and not the classic's* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Chu ji* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The spine had no row for it before this one (CPU-02568). Yang's English translation was not found to carry the entry on its readable pages (the animal pages of `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` are not readable), so Sun's text is the only witness read.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 樗鸡 in the middle classic (卷二 中经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2745, 味 paragraph; name line 2743). The paragraph reads: 味苦，平，主心腹邪气，阴痿，益精强志，生子，好色、补中、轻身。生川谷。In English: "Flavour bitter, balanced. Governs evil qi of the heart and abdomen; impotence (阴痿); boosts the essence (益精) and strengthens the will (强志); [aids] begetting children (生子); [makes one] fond of women (好色); supplements the centre (补中) and lightens the body (轻身). It grows in rivers and valleys." There is no 久服 clause. Sun's edition lists the entry in the middle classic's preface under the spelling 椿鸡 (line 1677).

*Wu Pu layer.* None.

**Mingyi bielu layer** (《名医》, later additions; line 2747). "Grows at Henei, on 樗 trees. Gathered in the seventh month and dried in the sun" (生河内樗树上。七月采，曝干). This is the layer that names the host tree and the harvest month. It does not say which stage of the insect or which part is taken; the Codex reads the whole dried insect.

**Sun Xingyan's note** (案, line 2749). The note is entirely philological. The *Guangya*: 樗鸠 (*chujiu*) is 樗鸡. The *Erya*: [a lost glyph] is "heavenly fowl" (天鸡); Li Xun says another name is "sour fowl" (酸鸡). Guo Pu: "a small insect, black body, red head; another name is 莎鸡, and it is also called 樗鸡" (小虫，黑身赤头，一名莎鸡，又曰樗鸡). The *Mao shi*: "in the sixth month the 莎鸡 vibrates its wings". Lu Ji: "the 莎鸡 is like a locust but mottled, with several layers of feathered wings, the hind wing quite red; some call it 'heavenly fowl'; in the middle of the sixth month it flies and vibrates its wings, making a rasping sound; the people of Youzhou call it 蒲错" (莎鸡，如蝗而斑色，毛翅数重，某翅正赤，或谓之天鸡。六月中，飞而振羽，索索作声。幽州人谓之蒲错). One glyph in Lu Ji's passage is damaged in the file ("某翅" is probably "其翅"), and the Codex reads it as the hind wing. Sun draws no conclusion.

**Identity.** The Codex's name, *Lycorma delicatula*, rests on the Mingyi layer's host tree (the Ailanthus, which this planthopper feeds on, a general fact) and on the red hind-wing and the harvest season in the commentaries. It fits only in part. Guo Pu's "black body, red head" does not describe the adult spotted lanternfly well, and Lu Ji's insect makes a rasping sound by vibrating its wings, which a planthopper does not do. The commentaries are about the 莎鸡 of the *Erya* and *Odes*, and Sun's note mixes it with the drug name; the equivalence is itself confused. Other red-winged insects have also been sold under the name 樗鸡, among them a red-bodied cicada relative sometimes called *hongniangzi*. Neither fact was read in the sources on disk. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, balanced insect for evil qi of the heart and abdomen, for impotence, for "boosting the essence" and strengthening the will, for begetting children and for being "fond of women", for supplementing the centre and lightening the body. The Codex gives no protocol and does not translate "boosting the essence" into any modern term. The material is an insect of uncertain identity and uncertain safety. The spotted lanternfly is, in some regions, a recognised invasive pest, and its handling is subject to local rules. This entry is not a basis for collecting or ingesting any insect.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the middle classic (中经), which the preface describes as "to nourish the nature, in correspondence with man; non-toxic or toxic, to be weighed as fit; for those who wish to hold off disease and make good the weak" (中药...主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经; line 1677). The grade therefore does not come from the class. It comes from the drug's own 味 paragraph, which promises "lightens the body" (轻身) and also "boosts the essence and strengthens the will" (益精强志); on the grading rule used for this series a 轻身 clause is a life-nourishing promise, with or without a 久服 prefix. The paragraph has no 久服 clause, so the promise is not tied to long taking, and the emphasis is therapeutic and generative (impotence, begetting children). 轻身 is the classic's term and is not rewritten here into any pharmacological effect. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 樗鸡 *chuji*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2745) The Mingyi and 案 layers add no cultivation claim and never count toward the grade.

**Practitioner's note.** There is no cultivation practice here. This is a middle-class record of an insect of uncertain identity, praised for sexual function and offered with no dose. A cultivator should read it as history.

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity unknown.** The classic does not warn, and the preface says middle-class drugs may be toxic or not (line 1677). The Codex has no source on the safety of the insect. Insects sold under related names include irritant species; ingestion could cause serious harm. Do not ingest any insect on the strength of this entry. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The name has been applied to more than one insect. Do not gather insects from trees for use.
- **Sexual function and fertility.** The impotence, essence and "begetting" claims are historical record only. A man with impotence, or a couple with fertility problems, needs medical care.
- **Pest species.** The spotted lanternfly is subject to local quarantine rules in some regions; follow them. [USER: verify]
