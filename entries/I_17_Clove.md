## Section 1 — Nomenclature

### Primary Scientific Name
*Syzygium aromaticum* (L.) Merr. & L.M.Perry (Myrtaceae); also *Eugenia caryophyllata* Thunb. (older name)

### Nomenclature Table

| Tradition | Language | Name | Transliteration | Meaning |
|-----------|----------|------|-----------------|---------|
| Sanskrit (SS) | संस्कृत | लवङ्ग | Lavaṅga | Sanskrit name for clove; exact etymological origin uncertain |
| Sanskrit (SS) | संस्कृत | देवकुसुम | Devakusuma | "Divine flower" — for the bud's aromatic quality |
| Arabic (CM) | العربية | قرنفل | Qaranful | Standard Arabic; from Sanskrit/Tamil root *karnaful* |
| Persian (CM) | فارسی | میخک | Mikhak | "Small nail" — for the nail-shaped dried bud appearance |
| Greek (DMM) | Ἑλληνική | κάρυον ἀρωματικόν | Karyon arōmatikón | "Aromatic nut" — Dioscorides references it as an Eastern aromatic |
| Chinese (SBJ) | 文言文 | 丁香 | Dīng Xiāng | "Nail fragrance" — same nail-shape observation as Persian |
| Tibetan (FMT) | བོད་སྐད། | གི་ཝམ | Gi wam | Standard Tibetan pharmaceutical designation |
| English | — | Clove | — | Via Old French *clou* (nail) — the nail-shaped bud |

### Botanical Identity
Evergreen tree, 10–20m; native to the Maluku Islands (Moluccas, Indonesia) — the original Spice Islands; now cultivated in Zanzibar (Tanzania), Madagascar, Indonesia, Sri Lanka. The dried unexpanded flower buds are the commercial "cloves." Deep-red when fresh and dried — the colour comes from high tannin content. The essential oil is among the highest eugenol-containing plant sources (70–90% eugenol in clove bud oil).

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Sushruta Samhita (SS)

**Charaka Samhita**:
> *"Lavaṅga: acrid; hot; reduces Kapha and Vata. Kindles digestive fire, improves digestion, removes bad breath, benefits the lungs, relieves thirst and burning sensations (paradoxically — due to its cooling post-effect). Used in compound preparations for respiratory conditions, digestive weakness, and pain."*

Clove is one of the most important spices in Ayurvedic compound formulas — appearing in *Chaturjata*, *Trijata*, and numerous dental/oral preparations. The classical Ayurvedic oil preparation *Lavanga Taila* (clove oil in sesame oil) is the primary traditional dental analgesia.

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā:
> *"Qaranful: temperament hot and dry in the third degree. Strengthens the brain, stomach, heart, and liver. Used as an analgesic for toothache when applied directly. Promotes digestion, removes bad breath, stops vomiting, reduces nausea. Its aroma strengthens weak sight and hearing."*

### 2.3 De Materia Medica (DMM)

Dioscorides references the "aromatic nut from India" with warming and digestive properties — the identification with clove is contextually supported though species disambiguation from the East African/Indonesian import trade is difficult for this era.

Galen references warming Eastern aromatics with analgesic dental application — consistent with clove.

### 2.4 Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ)

> *"Dīng xiāng (Clove): acrid, warm. Enters the Stomach, Spleen, Kidney channels. Warms the Stomach and Kidneys, descends rebellious Qi, stops hiccups. Used for cold-type hiccup, nausea, and vomiting; warming the Stomach for cold-Qi stagnation conditions."*

The TCM application for hiccup is pharmacologically specific — eugenol's smooth muscle relaxant action on the diaphragm and gastric cardia reduces the esophageal-gastric junction spasm that underlies intractable hiccup.

### 2.5 Four Medical Tantras (FMT)

*Rgyud bzhi*:
> *"Gi wam (clove): acrid, hot. Warms the stomach and kidney vital fire. Reduces bad-kan. Used in compound formulas for cold-type nausea and vomiting, and as a tooth pain remedy applied locally."*

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

Five-tradition convergence on:
1. **Digestive warming / carminative** — all five
2. **Dental analgesic** — SS, CM, SBJ, FMT
3. **Respiratory** — SS, CM
4. **Anti-emetic / anti-hiccup** — SBJ, CM, SS
5. **Breath-freshening** — SS, CM

The dental analgesic application is one of the most precisely convergent functional uses in the Codex — four independent traditions independently observed that clove (applied to the gum) relieves toothache. This is validated by eugenol's local anaesthetic mechanism.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Local Anaesthetic / Dental Analgesia

**Eugenol** → reversible sodium channel (Na+) blockade in sensory nerve fibres → inhibition of action potential generation → local anaesthesia.

Eugenol is still used in modern dentistry as a component of zinc oxide-eugenol (ZOE) cement — the most widely used dental cement globally. The 5-tradition convergence on dental application predated this pharmaceutical use by 2,000 years. This is one of the clearest examples in the Codex of ancient empirical observation being validated and adopted by modern medicine.

### 5.2 COX-2 Inhibition / Anti-inflammatory

Eugenol inhibits COX-2 with selectivity over COX-1 (similar to selective NSAIDs) → reduced prostaglandin E2 → anti-inflammatory, analgesic, antipyretic.

β-Caryophyllene → CB2 receptor agonism → additional anti-inflammatory pathway.

### 5.3 Antimicrobial

One of the most potent antimicrobial essential oils:
- Active against MRSA, *E. coli*, *Pseudomonas*, *Candida*, *Aspergillus*
- Anti-biofilm: disrupts *S. mutans* oral biofilm → basis of traditional oral hygiene application
- Antifungal: disrupts fungal membrane ergosterol

### 5.4 Carminative / Anti-emetic

Eugenol → smooth muscle relaxant (calcium channel antagonism) → reduces gastric and esophageal spasm → carminative, anti-emetic, anti-hiccup.

The TCM-specific application for hiccup is mechanistically supported: eugenol relaxes the lower esophageal sphincter and reduces diaphragmatic spasm.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Dental analgesic**: Clove oil as eugenol-source is a standard of care reference material in dental pain research. Multiple RCTs confirm eugenol-based preparations equivalent to benzocaine for dental analgesic applications.

**Premature ejaculation**: Topical eugenol-containing cream (from clove + other botanicals) — significant delay in ejaculatory latency in RCTs (likely via local anaesthetic mechanism on penile sensory nerves).

**Antimicrobial / oral**: Multiple in vitro studies; Shakena et al. (2011): clove mouth rinse comparable to chlorhexidine for plaque and gingivitis.

**Liver protection**: Oleanolic acid in clove — hepatoprotective in CCl₄ models.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**Numbing warmth**: The immediate pharmacological experience of clove in the mouth is unambiguous — a brief intense warmth (TRPV1 activation) followed rapidly by numbness (Na+ channel blockade). Every tradition that used clove for toothache was observing this same sensory sequence. The almost immediate analgesia makes this the most pharmacologically obvious application in the traditional pharmacopoeias — no multi-century observation was required; one application to an aching tooth delivers the result within minutes.

**The nail metaphor**: The Persian "mikhak" (little nail) and English "clove" (from French *clou*, nail) both name the spice by its shape — not its pharmacological action. But the nail metaphor is accidentally apt: eugenol "nails down" (blocks) sodium channels in sensory nerves with a precision and selectivity that modern pharmaceutical research has validated.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Whole bud in tea | 3–5 buds per cup | Digestive, carminative, warming |
| Ground clove | ¼–½ tsp in food/beverages | Culinary antimicrobial, digestive |
| Clove oil (dental) | 1–2 drops on cotton, apply to tooth | Toothache — established first aid |
| Clove water | 5–10 buds in 500ml water, steep 15 min | Digestive, antimicrobial, blood sugar |
| Clove EO (diluted topical) | 2–5% in carrier oil | Muscle pain, antimicrobial skin |

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Acrid, warm. Channels: Stomach, Spleen, Kidney. Actions: Warm middle, descend rebellious Qi, stop hiccup, warm Kidney Yang. Key indication: cold-type hiccup and nausea.

**Ayurvedic**: Pungent; hot; pungent post-digestive. Reduces Kapha ↓↓, reduces Vata ↓, increases Pitta. Deepana, Krimighna, Hrdya.

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Blood clotting / warfarin**: Eugenol has antiplatelet activity — caution at high doses with anticoagulants
- **Undiluted EO on mucous membranes**: Clove EO is caustic at high concentrations (>1%) — always dilute; can cause oral ulceration if used repeatedly undiluted
- **Liver toxicity**: High-dose eugenol is hepatotoxic — avoid therapeutic EO use > 1 month continuous
- **Children**: Avoid EO applications in children < 2 years
