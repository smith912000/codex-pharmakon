# Kukkuta flesh — Kukkuta, flesh of the cock or fowl, a bird of the scatterer (*Vishkira*) group, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the bird Bhishagratna prints as "Kukkuta" and glosses as "(cock)" in Uttaratantra ch.39; his Vol. 3 glossary reads "A cock" by column order; ch.46 describes "a wild cock" and "a domesticated cock or fowl"; no binomial is printed* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Kukkuta is named in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 167 lists "Kukkuta (cock/fowl)", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1"). The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The high-ground (*Jangala*) animals fall into eight sub-groups, "Of these the Janghala and the Viscira are the most important" (lines 403-411). The Kukkuta is listed in the second: "Birds such as the Lava, Tittiri, Kapinjala, Vartira ... Mayura, Krakara, Upachakra, Kukkuta ... belong to the Vishkira species", so called "from the fact of their picking up their food after scattering it first with their bills and claws" (lines 450-463; names omitted where the OCR is damaged). The group text reads: "The flesh of a bird of this group is light, cooling, sweet and astringent in taste and tends to pacify the deranged humours of the body" (lines 463-466).

The fowl is then described without the Sanskrit name printed: "The flesh of a wild cock is demulcent, heat-making, and spermatopoietic. It acts as a diaphoretic, imparts tone to the voice and the organism, subdues the deranged Vayu, and is useful as a good constructive tonic. The flesh of a domesticated cock or fowl is possessed of properties similar to those of its wild prototype with the exception that it is heavy, and proves curative in rheumatism, consumption, vomiting and chronic (Vishama-Jvara) fever" (lines 496-504). The sentences sit in the run of Vishkira birds, after the Mayura and before the Pratuda group, which is why they are read here as the Kukkuta's; the text does not name it in them. [USER: verify]

Other places. *Uttaratantra* ch.39 (fever) says that "several authorities" do not recommend "the meat of Sarasa, Krouncha, Mayuta (peacock), Kukkuta (cock) and of Tittira in cases of fever, owing to its heaviness (as regards digestion), as well as to its heat-making potency", and that the translator subscribes "with a certain limitation": the flesh may be given in moderate quantity at the proper time if the fever has a preponderance of deranged *Vayu* (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt`, lines 908-917). The after-drinks passage of ch.46 says "Kola and Vadara wines should be used after having taken the cooked flesh of the fowls of the Vishkira species" (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 2828-2840). *Sutrasthana* ch.20 says the pot-herbs Nadima and Siddhi "should not be simultaneously eaten with curd, and the flesh of a cock" (`SS_Sutra_ch20.txt`, lines 148-150), where "cock" is printed without the Sanskrit. The "Kukkutandaka" in ch.46 line 111 is a name in the list of *Vrihi* rice grains, a different thing, and "Kukkuti" in *Uttaratantra* ch.33 (line 28, among roots) and ch.60 ("a kind of bulb", line 292) is a plant, not the bird. The Dioscorides and Tibetan entries on poultry are in the entry Domestic fowl (III_34); this entry is Sushruta only.

**Identity.** The translator glosses the Kukkuta as "(cock)" in *Uttaratantra* ch.39, and his Vol. 3 glossary (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, name at line 30661) gives "A cock" in a separate OCR column; the pairing is by column order. The spine list's "cock/fowl" matches. No binomial is printed in any of these places. The chapter's "wild cock" and "domesticated cock or fowl" are the translator's English. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Fowl flesh is mostly water and muscle protein with fat that is concentrated in the skin, and supplies iron, zinc and B vitamins including B12. No analysis specific to this entry was read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Fowl is an ordinary food in many cuisines, lawful where slaughter and sale are lawful. As a medicine, the sourced record is the Sushruta classification above, a dietary one: it describes the cock and the fowl with a set of qualities and indications, and with some cautions. The Codex does not recommend the flesh as a therapy for rheumatism, consumption, vomiting or fever, and gives no protocol. A reader following a vegetarian or other diet for ethical, religious or health reasons has no need of it on the strength of this text. The text's own cautions (heaviness in fever; not with certain pot-herbs and curd) are the more practically relevant content.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation or dose is given for the flesh in ch.46. The text elsewhere says it may be taken in fever in moderate quantity at the proper time when *Vayu* predominates (*Uttaratantra* ch.39), and that wine of Kola or Vadara follows a meal of Vishkira fowls (ch.46 after-drinks). Ch.46 also describes ways of preparing meat in general, but none is attributed to the Kukkuta here. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Poultry must be cooked through and handled hygienically: it is a recognised source of *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter* infection, and poultry can carry avian influenza viruses. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 463-504). For the *Vishkira* group: light; cooling; sweet and astringent in taste; pacifies the deranged humours. For the wild cock: demulcent; heat-making; spermatopoietic; diaphoretic; improves the voice; subdues *Vayu*; a constructive tonic. For the domesticated cock or fowl: similar, but heavy; curative in rheumatism, consumption, vomiting and chronic fever. The group is called cooling and the cock heat-making; *Uttaratantra* ch.39 repeats "heavy" and "heat-making" for the Kukkuta among birds some authorities avoid in fever. The text does not reconcile "cooling" with "heat-making"; both are recorded as printed. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Kukkuta is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food, not about this flesh. The text calls the wild cock's flesh "spermatopoietic" and "a good constructive tonic", which are properties listed among foods and not a placement in a *vajikarana* or *rasayana* chapter; no line read puts the Kukkuta in either. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta the cock and fowl are a food with stated qualities, and the passages that bear on a cultivator are the cautions: heavy and heating, so not an easy meal in fever, and not combined with curd and certain pot-herbs. A person who eats meat may read the classification as the tradition's own description of the food; a person who does not has nothing to adopt.

### Contraindications

- **Food safety:** raw or undercooked poultry carries *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*; cook through and keep raw and cooked foods apart.
- **The text's own cautions:** heavy and heat-making, so avoided by some authorities in fever (*Uttaratantra* ch.39); not with curd and the pot-herbs Nadima and Siddhi (*Sutrasthana* ch.20, lines 148-150).
- **Identity:** the chapter's "wild cock" is the translator's English and is not named as the Kukkuta in the sentence; do not substitute a species on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Legal status:** the domestic fowl is a legally farmed animal in most countries; wild jungle fowl are protected in some. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food is a moral question for many traditions, and meat is restricted in some; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
