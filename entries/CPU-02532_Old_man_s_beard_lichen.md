# Old man's beard lichen — 松罗 *songluo* (Shennong bencao jing), also called 女萝 *nüluo*

## *A pendant, beard-like lichen, our identification (medium confidence) as Usnea diffracta Vain. (the genus Usnea, a lichen: a fungus living with an alga, not a flowering plant); the Shennong text names the thing only as "pine-gauze" and the only description comes from Lu Ji in Sun's note* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Shennong bencao jing (SBJ).** Under the name *songluo* the substance is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. A different name in another text on disk does list the same binomial: Clark's 1995 translation of the rGyud-bzhi, in its materia medica chapter, gives *gser.skud* ("gold thread") as "Cuscuta sinensis: Usnea diffracta: Parmelia saxatilis" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, line 1029). That is a different name with three binomials, the first of them a dodder, and it is not tagged here and not treated as the same substance. The Chinese entry's identification as a lichen is ours.

The source for the Chinese entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). It stands in the middle classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 松罗 *songluo*, same file, line 2523). The preface's list of middle-class wood drugs includes it (line 1677).

**The Shennong text proper** (line 2523): "Flavour bitter, balanced. It governs the evil qi of rage and anger (嗔怒邪气); stops deficiency sweating and head-wind; for women, cold in the yin and swelling illness. One name, *nüluo* (女萝). It grows in mountain valleys." (味苦，平。主嗔怒邪气，止虚汗、头风，女子阴寒、肿病。一名女萝。生山谷。)

**The Mingyi bielu layer** (line 2525): "Grows on Mount Xiong'er" (生熊耳山). That is all the later layer adds. There is no Wu Pu layer for this entry.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 2527).** Sun quotes the *Guangya*: "*nüluo* is *songluo*" (女萝，松萝也); the *Mao shi*: "*niao* and *nüluo*" (茑与女萝); the commentary: "*nüluo* and *tusi* are *songluo*"; and Lu Ji's description: "*songluo* climbs by itself on the pine, its branches straight and green, unlike *tusi*" (自蔓松上，枝正青，与菟丝异). *Tusi* is the dodder. So the note shows that *nüluo* and *tusi* were run together in the commentary, and that Lu Ji separated them. Only Lu Ji gives a description, and it does not say "lichen"; the reading as a lichen is ours.

**Preface.** The middle class "mainly nourishes the nature, to correspond to man; toxic or non-toxic, to be weighed as appropriate; for one who wishes to check disease and fill out the thin, take from the middle classic" (line 1677).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, lichens of the genus *Usnea* are known to contain usnic acid, a lichen metabolite, and other lichen acids and polysaccharides. This is general chemistry of the genus and is not a finding of the text. No analytical source for *U. diffracta* was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records it for "evil qi of rage and anger", deficiency sweating, head-wind, and for women's cold in the yin and swelling. The Codex does not turn these into modern indications and gives no protocol. Persistent sweating, headache and swellings need diagnosis. Any use of lichen as a medicine should rest on a source this entry does not have.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives no preparation and no dose; the Mingyi layer gives the place of growth only. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

*Usnea* lichens grow on trees in cool, clean-air regions and are slow-growing. Usnic acid has been studied for antibacterial properties; that is a general statement and not a study cited here. Whether the lichen affects sweating, headache or swelling has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong).** One flavour and one nature are printed: bitter (苦), balanced (平). Middle class by placement. No further quality is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry is in the middle classic, whose preface (line 1677) describes drugs to be "weighed as appropriate" for checking disease and filling out the thin. The paragraph has no *jiu fu* ("taken for long") clause and no life-nourishing wording. All its claims are therapeutic. The only item with the flavour of a state of the person is the "evil qi of rage and anger" (嗔怒邪气), which the classic lists as a condition the substance governs; it is not a cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing here to cultivate. The classic offers a bitter, balanced lichen for a short list of complaints and gives no dose or course. Treat it as a record. Do not read the "rage and anger" clause as an instruction for anger management.

### Contraindications

- **Identification.** The text names the thing only as *songluo* or *nüluo*; the commentary tradition confused it with dodder. Do not substitute one for the other.
- **Lichen acids.** Usnic acid has been reported as harmful to the liver when taken in supplements; this is a general caution and I did not read the report. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, children, medicines.** The text says nothing; no use is supported. [USER: verify]
- **Conservation.** *Usnea* is slow-growing and sensitive to air pollution; wild stripping of host trees should be avoided.
