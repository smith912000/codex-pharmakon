# Goat flesh — Aja (goat), flesh of the domestic-animal group, Sushruta Samhita
## *Meat of the domestic goat; the translator prints "the flesh of the goat" and "goat's flesh", with no Sanskrit name for the flesh itself in the file read* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Goat flesh is described in the *Sushruta Samhita*, *Sutrasthana* chapter 46, the chapter "of food and drink" (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 235 cites "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1"). The chapter opens by recalling that "food determines the origin of beings and forms the chief source of their bodily strength and complexion, as well as of the albuminous (Ojas) principle in their organisms" (lines 9-12), and then runs through the food groups, including flesh of different animals.

Goat belongs to the "Domestic Animal Group": "Animals such as horses, mules, cows, bullocks, asses, camels, goats, sheep, and *Medapuchhas* (fat tailed or Turkish sheep) etc., belong to the group of domestic animals (*Gramyas*)" (line 615-618). The group's metrical text says: "The flesh of domestic animals is possessed of constructive, tonic and appetising properties, is sweet in taste and digestion. It destroys the deranged Vayu and produces the Kapham and Pittam." It then singles out the goat (lines 625-628): "Of these the flesh of the goat is moderately cooling in its potency, does not increase the secretions of the internal organs, is heavy and demulcent, subdues the Pittam and the Kapham, and is beneficial in nasal catarrh." Next come sheep ("constructive, tonic and heavy, and generates the Pittam and Kapham"), the *Medapuchcha* ("aphrodisiac and has properties similar to those of mutton"), beef ("holy and refrigerant", curative in dyspnoea, catarrh, cough, chronic fever and morbid craving for food), and animals with unbifurcated hoofs (lines 628-649).

Goat flesh also appears in the chapters on regimen. In *Sutrasthana* ch.19 (management of a patient with an ulcer) "goat's flesh, mutton" is in the list of foods to be avoided by an ulcer patient (line 80). In ch.20 (*Hitahitiya*, wholesome and unwholesome regimen) "goat's flesh, mutton" is among articles which, "being eaten simultaneously with milk", can "act as deadly poisons through incompatible" combination (lines 88-91). In ch.21 (humours as causes of ulcers) "the flesh of a Godha or a goat or mutton" is among foods which may aggravate a condition "if taken injudiciously" (line 300). The condition aggravated is not identified in the lines read. [USER: verify]

Parts of the goat other than the flesh are separate substances: the testes of a he-goat appear in the *Vajikarana* chapter (*Chikitsasthana* ch.26, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt`, about lines 94-100 and 130-135), and goat's-milk ghee in ch.45 (see the Ghee entry). They are not folded into this one.

### Active Components

Meat is mostly water and muscle protein, with fat that varies with the cut and the animal, and contains iron (largely as haem iron), zinc, and B vitamins including B12. The proportions depend on age, feed and cut. No analysis specific to this entry was read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Goat meat is an ordinary food in many cuisines, lawful where slaughter and sale are lawful, and needs no special licence in those places. As a medicine, the sourced record is the Sushruta classification above, a dietary one: it describes goat flesh as one food among the domestic animals, with a set of qualities, and with some cautions. The Codex does not recommend goat flesh as a therapy for nasal catarrh or any other condition, and gives no protocol. A reader following a vegetarian or other diet for ethical, religious or health reasons has no need of it on the strength of this text. The text's own warnings (ulcer regimen; incompatibility with milk) are recorded in Origin & Tradition and are the more practically relevant content.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Goat meat is a source of protein, iron and vitamin B12, as other red meats are. Meat must be cooked through and handled hygienically to avoid foodborne illness. Goats can carry *Brucella*, *Coxiella* and other zoonoses, so unpasteurised dairy and undercooked meat are recognised risks. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed qualities in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 619-628). For domestic-animal flesh generally: constructive, tonic, appetising; sweet in taste and in digestion; destroys deranged *Vayu*, produces *Kapham* and *Pittam*. For goat flesh specifically: "moderately cooling in its potency", "heavy and demulcent", "does not increase the secretions of the internal organs", subdues *Pittam* and *Kapham*, "beneficial in nasal catarrh". There is a tension in the text as translated: domestic-animal flesh "produces" *Kapham* and *Pittam* generally, while goat flesh specifically "subdues" them. The file read gives no explanation; it is recorded as printed. [USER: verify] No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Goat flesh is named in a food-and-drink chapter whose opening lines tie food generally to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (lines 9-12). That is a claim about food as a class, not about goat flesh; no line read places goat flesh in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter. The he-goat's testes, not the flesh, appear in the *Vajikarana* chapter (ch.26). Where goat flesh does appear in the regimen chapters it is as a food to avoid (ulcer regimen, ch.19; incompatible with milk, ch.20; possible aggravator, ch.21). There is therefore no *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim for goat flesh in the passages read. The popular lists that sort foods into *sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* trace to *Samkhya* and later material, not to Sushruta, and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. Goat flesh in Sushruta is a food with stated qualities, and the passages that bear on a cultivator are the cautions: avoid it in an ulcer regimen, and do not combine it with milk. A person who eats meat may read the classification as the tradition's own description of the food; a person who does not has nothing to adopt.

### Contraindications

- **Food safety:** undercooked goat meat can carry bacteria and parasites; brucellosis and Q fever are goat-associated zoonoses.
- **The text's own cautions:** avoid in an ulcer patient's diet (ch.19, line 80) and with milk (ch.20, lines 88-91).
- **Dietary context:** red meat intake may be limited on medical advice, for example in gout or kidney disease; consult a clinician. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food is a moral question for many traditions, and meat is prohibited or restricted in some; this entry takes no position beyond noting it.
- **Legal status:** goat is a domestic, legally farmed animal in most countries; slaughter and sale rules vary. [USER: verify]
