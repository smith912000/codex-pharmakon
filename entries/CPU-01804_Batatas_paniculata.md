# Batatas paniculata — Vidari, Vidari-kanda, Bhumi-kushmanda (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3)

## *Batatas paniculata, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints against the Sanskrit names Vidari and Vidari-kanda (line 33303) and, with Ipomoea digitata (OCR "Diijilata"), against Bhumi-kushmanda (line 31473). ### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` (Sutra, Chikitsa, Uttara, Kalpa, Nidana, Sharira) and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The chapters themselves name the drug only by Sanskrit name (Vidari). A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Tibetan text folders on disk for *Batatas* found no hit. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** The row has three source rows in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, all keyed by a **Sanskrit name** as the OCR prints it: line 438, "Bhumi-Kuslimanda" (appendix line 31417, Latin line 31473, "Batatas paniculata ; Ipomoea Diijilata."); line 643, "Vida'ri" (appendix line 33273, Latin line 33303, "Batatas paniculata"); and line 644, "Vidstri-kanda/" (appendix line 33273, Latin line 33303). **Identity doubt.** The Sushruta chapters do not give a Latin name, and the Codex already holds an entry that reads Sushruta's Vidari as a different plant. **I_351** (Vidari, *Pueraria tuberosa*) takes the Sanskrit name to a leguminous tuber, and its own heading prints "Bhūkūṣmāṇḍī" among Vidari's names (from its own sources, not from the Sushruta chapters read here), so the Codex already treats Vidari and Bhumi-kushmanda as names of one drug. Bhishagratna's appendix gives a morning-glory-family Latin name (*Batatas* is a genus name under which the sweet potato group was once placed; the Codex has not checked its current standing) and, for Bhumi-kushmanda, *Ipomoea digitata*, which I_351 mentions (its line 80) only as a market substitute for Vidari. The Codex cannot say which plant Sushruta meant, and this entry stands for the Sanskrit drug under the appendix's Latin. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The name in the chapters.* The Sanskrit "Bhumi-kushmanda" is not in any chapter file: a search of the chapter files for "Bhumi", "kushmanda", "kusmanda" and "Bhu-kus" found only the plain "Kushmanda" (Sutra 42 line 299; Sutra 46 lines 1380 and 2879; Chikitsa 31 line 121), which is a different drug in the appendix. "Vidari" is in the chapters, in the following places read for this entry. (The string also occurs as disease names, *Vidarika* in Nidana 13 and Chikitsa 20, and *Vidari* in Nidana 16, lines 420 to 424, which are not the drug.)

*The group (Sutra 38, the Vidari-gandhadi group, lines 12 to 23).* "The Vidarigandha'di Group:— The drugs known as Vidarigandha, Vidari, Sahadeva, Vishvadeva, Shvadanstra, Prithakparni, Shatavari, Sariva, black Sariva, Jivaka, Rishavaka, Mahasaha, Kshudra-Saha, Vrihati, Kantakari, Punarnava, Eranda, Hansapadi, Vrishchikah, and Rishavi, form the group known as the Vidari-gandhadi." The "Metrical Text" that follows says that the group "subdues the action of the deranged Vayu and Pittam and proves beneficial in phthisis (Shosha), Gulma, aching of the limbs, Urdha Shvasa and cough". The extraction's own row for the name is `names.csv` line 3 ("Sutra 38, Vidarigandha'cli group", with the note "no identification given here").

*Two more group mentions (Sutra 39).* Line 93 names "the drugs forming the group of Vidarigandhadi-Gana" among drugs "possessed of the general virtue of soothing (restoring to its normal state) the deranged Vayu"; line 105 lists "Manjishtha, Payasya, Vidari, Shatavari, Gundra ..." in the Pitta-Samshamana-Varga, whose drugs "generally prove soothing to the deranged Pittam". Sutra 42 (line 297) lists "Vala, Ativala, Atmagupta, Vidari, Payasya, Gokshuraka ..." among the drugs "generally included within the Madhura group", the sweet-taste group.

*A therapeutic mention (Sutra 15, line 663).* "Payasya, Ashvagandha, Vidari, Vidarigandha, Shatavari, Vala, Ativala, Nagavala and such other drugs of sweet taste" are named in a "medicated compound" in the passage that follows an account of extreme emaciation (lines 636 to 662); the sentence that introduces the compound is printed in Bhishagratna as "A case of patent obesity should be checked with ...", which does not sit easily with the passage before it, and the Codex does not resolve the difference.

*The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26).* The chapter is headed "the medical treatment for increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons (Kshina-Valiyam Vaji-Karana)" (lines 4 to 7). It names "Viddri" (the OCR's spelling) in four recipes. Line 81 (page 511): "Powders of sesamum, Masha-pulse, Viddri, or Sali-rice should be mixed with Saindhava salt and pasted with a copious quantity of the expressed juice of the sugarcane of the Paundarika species," mixed with hog's lard, and made into an *Utkarika* with clarified butter. Line 114 (page 512): "Powdered Viddri successively soaked in the expressed juice of the same and dried, should be licked with honey and clarified butter." Lines 142 to 144 (page 513): "The powdered bulbs of Viddri, weighing an Udumbara (one Tola) in measure, and taken with milk and clarified butter would make an old man young again." Lines 186 to 188 (page 514): "the milk boiled with Masha-pulse, Viddri, or Uchchata should be taken with honey, clarified butter and sugar."

*The rasayana chapter (Chikitsa 27).* The chapter opens (lines 4 to 9) with the "recipes and modes of using elixirs and rejuvinators ... which will make it invulnerable to the inroads of any disease or of decay (Sarvopaghata-shamaniya-Rasayanam)" and its first metrical text (lines 10 to 21) says that a physician should prescribe a tonic (*Rasayana*) in youth and middle age only "after having their systems (properly) cleansed" by oil and by emetics and purgatives, because an uncleansed person's tonics "would fail to produce the wished-for result". After the *Vala-kalpa* (lines 153 to 169, page 519), whose course is said to arrest "premature old age and senile decay" for twelve years when taken for twelve days, and for a hundred years when extended to a hundred days, the text continues (lines 169 to 179): "Ati-valai, Naga-valai, Vidatri, Satatvari may be similarly taken for the same purposes, with this distinction that the (powders of) the Ati-vala should be taken with water, those of the Naga-vala, with honey, whereas Viddri and the Satavari powders should be taken with milk. The regimen of diet and conduct as well as the beneficial results produced therefrom should be the same (as from the use of Vala). The present remedies are recommended to persons seeking strength or suffering from an attack of Haematemesis or Haematochezia." ("Vidatri" and "Vz'ddrz" are the OCR's spellings of the one name, on lines 170 and 173.)

*Further hits.* The name also occurs in recipe lists and group references that the Codex has not read for context beyond the line (Chikitsa 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 14, 22, 37 and 38; Sharira 10; Kalpa 8; Uttara 9, 17, 24, 31, 39, 40, 41, 42, 45, 48, 51, 52, 58, 59 and 61). ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Batatas* and *Ipomoea* are names of genera of the morning-glory family (Convolvulaceae), whose members include the sweet potato, a starchy storage root, and the purgative-resin plants such as jalap (the Codex's I_582); which of these the Sushruta drug resembles is not shown by the lines read. Nothing is stated here about the drug's composition. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Vidari as a member of the sweet-taste group and of the Vidari-gandhadi group, which it says subdues deranged Vayu and Pitta; as an ingredient of vajikarana recipes in Chikitsa 26; and as a drug that "may be similarly taken" as an elixir in a rasayana chapter (Chikitsa 27). These are the text's claims about a Sanskrit name, and the Latin name that the appendix attaches to it is the translator's. They are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose, and nothing in this entry should be taken by mouth on the strength of a Latin name that the text does not itself give.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives these preparations, each with its locus. Chikitsa 26, line 81: powder with other powders pasted with sugarcane juice and lard, made into an *Utkarika* with clarified butter. Chikitsa 26, line 114: the powder "successively soaked in the expressed juice of the same and dried" and licked with honey and clarified butter. Chikitsa 26, lines 142 to 144: "the powdered bulbs of Viddri, weighing an Udumbara (one Tola) in measure," taken with milk and clarified butter. Chikitsa 26, lines 186 to 188: milk boiled with the drug and taken with honey, clarified butter and sugar. Chikitsa 27, lines 169 to 179: the powder taken with milk in the manner of the *Vala-kalpa*, after cleansing, with the same regimen of diet and conduct. The only measure printed for the drug is "an Udumbara (one Tola)" at Chikitsa 26 line 142; the *Vala-kalpa* gives "A Pala or half a Pala" of the powdered roots of Vala (line 153), and the text does not say whether the same weight is meant for Vidari. No frequency or duration is given for Vidari itself. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** The chapters read print for Vidari: membership of the Madhura (sweet) group (Sutra 42, line 297); membership of the Vidari-gandhadi group, said to subdue deranged Vayu and Pittam (Sutra 38), and a place in the Pitta-soothing list (Sutra 39, line 105). No individual *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for it in the lines read. The group-level statements are the text's, and the Codex does not attach them to a particular Latin plant.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(a) Attested for the Sanskrit name Vidari: named in the rasayana chapter and in the vajikarana chapter.* Chikitsa 27, the chapter on elixirs and rejuvenators (*Rasayana*), says (lines 169 to 179, page 519) that Vidari "may be similarly taken for the same purposes" as the *Vala-kalpa* and with the same regimen, with milk; and Chikitsa 26, the chapter on virile power (*Vaji-Karana*), names it in four recipes (lines 81, 114, 142 and 187). The status therefore rests on the Sanskrit name in two chapters that I read. It does not rest on the Latin name, which comes from Bhishagratna's appendix by position and which the Codex's I_351 reads differently. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for this drug (a search of Chikitsa 26 to 30 for *ojas* found nothing), and none is asserted here. The text makes the rasayana course conditional on prior cleansing (Chikitsa 27, lines 10 to 21), and the Codex repeats that condition. The name "Bhumi-kushmanda" is not in the chapters, so no cultivation claim is made for it. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** In the Sushruta scheme the drug named Vidari is a rasayana and vajikarana ingredient, taken under a physician, after cleansing and with a prescribed regimen, and the text gives its measure only in one vajikarana recipe. The Latin identification is a translator's appendix pairing and the chapters do not confirm it; nothing here supports gathering or taking a plant on the strength of the Latin name.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read print no warning for the drug itself. The text's own condition for the rasayana course is that the body is first cleansed, and a regimen of diet and conduct is prescribed with it (Chikitsa 27, lines 10 to 21 and 156 to 158). Weakness, emaciation, bleeding disorders and decline in virile power need medical care. The identity is open between two families, a leguminous tuber (I_351) and a morning-glory-family root (this appendix), and the second family includes strongly purgative plants, so no root should be gathered or taken on the strength of this entry.
