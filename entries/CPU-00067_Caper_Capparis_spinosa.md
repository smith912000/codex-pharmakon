### Origin & Tradition

The caper is a tough, sprawling, spiny shrub that grows from cracks in rock and old walls across the Mediterranean and the dry Near East — a plant of poor, hot, stony ground. Its familiar product is the **flower bud**, picked before opening and pickled in salt or vinegar as the sharp, piquant condiment; the larger fruit ("caper berries") and the young shoots are likewise preserved. But the classical and medieval pharmacy valued above all the **root-bark**, a bitter, warming, drying drug. Dioscorides treats the caper in Book II and assigns it a clear therapeutic identity that the later traditions preserved.

That identity is, before all else, the **deobstruent of the spleen** — the remedy for the hardened, enlarged, "obstructed" spleen and liver. In the humoral framework the caper's bitter-warming-drying quality made it the classic agent for resolving indurations and obstructions of the abdominal viscera, particularly splenic enlargement (a common consequence of chronic malaria and other endemic disease in the ancient Mediterranean). Alongside this it served as a stomachic and appetite-stimulant (the pickled bud before meals), an emmenagogue, a diuretic, an anti-rheumatic, and — applied to the gums — a remedy for toothache.

In **Unani** medicine *kabar* holds exactly this place: a hot-dry deobstruent and resolvent for the spleen and liver, for "cold" rheumatic and sciatic pain, and as a digestive. Modern interest has centred on the bud's and leaf's rich **flavonoid content** (notably rutin and quercetin) and antioxidant/anti-inflammatory activity, giving a phytochemical footing to the traditional anti-rheumatic and hepatosplenic uses. The caper thus runs from the humble pickle jar to a genuine drug of the classical materia medica.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM / Galenic humoral degree:** Hot and Dry (commonly hot in the second degree) — a bitter, warming, drying deobstruent; the spleen-resolving drug of the classical pharmacy.
- **Unani/Arabic mapping:** *kabar* — hot-dry; muhallil/mufattit (resolvent of indurations), deobstruent of liver and spleen, digestive, anti-rheumatic.
- **Kenetica Qi/Shen tier:** ◉ **Qi** — on the warming-drying, deobstruent/resolvent axis (resolving hard visceral obstruction), with a digestive-bitter aspect; a robust "open the obstruction" agent.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy — caution.** Emmenagogue/deobstruent reputation; medicinal bark doses traditionally avoided (pickled buds are ordinary food). [USER: verify.]
- **Salt load (pickled product).** Commercial capers are high in salt — relevant in hypertension and salt-restricted diets.
- **Hot/inflamed acute conditions.** A warming-drying herb is less suited to frankly hot acute states.
- **Allergy.** Possible sensitivity in those allergic to Brassicales/mustard-family pungents.
- **GI irritation.** Strong bark preparations can irritate.
