### Origin & Tradition

*Onosma echioides* is a low, bristly, rough-haired perennial of dry stony hillsides across the Mediterranean and western Asia, its pale yellow tubular flowers ("golden-drops") sitting above a root whose bark bleeds a deep red-violet dye. That red root is the whole story of the plant in the old pharmacy. Across the Boraginaceae — alkanet (*Alkanna tinctoria*), gromwell (*Lithospermum*), *Arnebia*, *Onosma* — the same family signature recurs: a colourless plant above ground, a blood-red root below, prized simultaneously as a cosmetic and textile dye and as a wound- and skin-drug. The ancient mind read that redness as a signature, and the plant's medicine was built on it.

**Egyptian (EP).** The Egyptian materia medica of the Ebers tradition worked extensively with red mineral and plant colourants and with root-based salves for wounds, burns and skin eruptions; the red dye-roots of the Boraginaceae belong to this world of coloured wound-unguents, though attribution of *Onosma echioides* specifically to a named Ebers prescription cannot be made with confidence. [USER: verify any specific Ebers citation before asserting.] What is secure is the doctrinal category: a red root worked into fat or oil as a dressing for damaged and inflamed skin.

**Assyrian (AT).** The cuneiform plant lists of Mesopotamia record numerous root-drugs used as poultices and salves for "the sore" and for skin complaints, several of them dye-yielding. A red-rooted Boraginaceous drug plausibly sits among them, but the Akkadian plant names resist confident binomial identification, and no invented equivalence should be offered here. [USER: verify — treat the Assyrian attribution as general rather than a specific named entry.]

**Greek (DMM).** Dioscorides describes both *onosma* and the closely allied red-rooting alkanets. The red-root drugs of this family are given a consistent Greek identity: the root, worked in wax or oil, is a remedy for burns, spreading sores, ulcers and skin lesions, and the plants carry an emmenagogue/abortifacient reputation attached to *onosma* in particular — a caution the tradition preserved. The dyeing root and the wound-salve are one and the same drug.

**Avicenna / Unani (CM).** In the Arabic-Persian synthesis the red dye-roots (*shanjar* / *abu khalsa* group, the alkanet complex) are classed as cooling and drying, detergent and cicatrising: applied to burns, ulcers, erysipelas and inflamed skin, and reputed to "cool the blood" and settle hot eruptions. This is the clearest surviving statement of the doctrine the whole family shares.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** The organising idea is chromatic sympathy: a root the colour of blood is a drug *for* the blood and for the bleeding, raw or inflamed surface. "Cooling for the blood" is the humoral reading of a plant whose signature is red-in-the-cool-earth — it draws heat out of an inflamed wound and closes it. Mapped onto real chemistry, this doctrine is unusually well-founded, because the red pigment is not inert. The colour is due to **naphthoquinones of the alkannin/shikonin type** — the same naphthazarin-cored molecules that give alkanet and *Lithospermum* (Chinese *Zicao*, "purple herb") their dye and their documented wound and antibacterial activity. The tradition, in effect, isolated a bioactive pigment by eye and used it exactly where its chemistry acts: on the wounded skin surface. The one place the doctrine over-reaches is the internal "blood-cooling" claim, where — as the Contraindications make plain — the family's *other* chemistry (pyrrolizidine alkaloids) makes internal use hazardous rather than cooling.

### Active Components

- **Naphthoquinone pigments (alkannin / shikonin / arnebin-type naphthazarins)** — the red root-bark colourants; the bioactive signature of the family. In *Onosma* these are congeneric to the well-characterised alkannin (*Alkanna*) and shikonin (*Lithospermum*); the specific naphthoquinone profile of *O. echioides* is less fully quantified than its relatives. [USER: verify quantitative content before quoting figures.]
- **Pyrrolizidine alkaloids (PAs)** — the family-wide Boraginaceae liability; hepatotoxic on internal exposure (see Contraindications). Their presence in *Onosma* should be assumed on family grounds.
- **Phenolics, phenylpropanoids and flavonoids** — antioxidant constituents typical of the genus.
- **Fixed oils / waxes of the root** — the lipophilic matrix that carries the naphthoquinones into a salve and into skin.
- **Rosmarinic acid and related caffeic-acid esters** — anti-inflammatory phenolics broadly distributed in Boraginaceae.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Wound and burn dressing (primary, topical).** The red root in oil, wax or fat as a cicatrising salve for burns, ulcers and slow-healing sores — the defining classical use, and the one with real chemical support.
2. **Cooling anti-inflammatory for hot skin.** Applied to erysipelas, inflamed eruptions and "hot" lesions; the "cools the blood" doctrine expressed topically.
3. **Dye / cosmetic colourant.** The root as a red-violet dye for textiles, oils and lip/cheek colour — culturally inseparable from the medicinal use.
4. **Emmenagogue (traditional, cautioned).** A recorded reputation carrying the abortifacient warning; not a recommendation. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

The traditional profile — a red root-pigment that dresses wounds, closes burns and resists infection — maps onto a genuinely active class of molecules, though most of the hard data is on *Onosma*'s Boraginaceous relatives rather than on *O. echioides* itself. This congeneric borrowing is stated plainly rather than hidden.

- **Antibacterial mechanism of the pigments.** According to PubMed, Watson et al. (2023, *International Journal of Biological Macromolecules*) showed that shikonin and alkannin — the naphthoquinones of the dye-root family — inhibit bacterial ATP synthase, with shikonin causing complete inhibition of *E. coli* ATP synthase, tying the antibacterial reputation to a defined molecular target [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2023.127049). Grade: **moderate for the pigment class; congeneric, not *O. echioides* specifically.**
- **Wound healing, family-level in vivo.** According to PubMed, Shen et al. (2017, *Zhonghua Shao Shang Za Zhi / Chinese Journal of Burns*) found that *Arnebia* (a red-root Boraginaceous congener) root oil accelerated full-thickness wound healing in rats, with raised VEGF and bFGF expression — a plausible mechanism for the cicatrising tradition [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.issn.1009-2587.2017.09.008). Grade: **moderate, congeneric animal model.**
- **Onosma-genus burn healing.** According to PubMed, Hemmati et al. (2018, *World Journal of Plastic Surgery*) reported that an *Onosma bulbotrichum* root extract cream improved second-degree burn healing in rabbits, comparable to silver sulfadiazine at 5% (PMID 29651389; no DOI). Grade: **weak–moderate; same genus, small animal study, different species.**
- **Redox/antitumour chemistry of the scaffold.** According to PubMed, Beretta et al. (2016, *Archiv der Pharmazie*) characterised alkannin/shikonin-inspired naphthoquinones as topoisomerase-I inhibitors whose potency rests on the redox-active naphthazarin core [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ardp.201600286) — establishing that these pigments are pharmacologically real molecules, not inert dyes. Grade: **strong for the chemistry; not a clinical wound claim.**
- **Honest deflation.** No controlled human trial establishes *O. echioides* as a wound therapeutic; the internal "blood-cooling" doctrine has no toxicological safety and is contradicted by the family's PA hepatotoxicity (below). The topical doctrine is well-supported *in principle*; the internal doctrine is deflated.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM / Galenic humoral degree:** Cooling and drying; a detergent, cicatrising root-drug for hot and ulcerated skin.
- **Avicenna / Unani mapping:** cold-dry; mubarrid (refrigerant) and mujaffif (desiccant), cicatrising and "cooling to the blood," for burns, erysipelas and inflamed lesions.
- **Kenetica Blood tier:** ◉ **Blood** — a cooling, wound-closing, surface-detergent agent on the red-root/naphthoquinone axis; topical strength, internal caution.

### Contraindications

- **⚠️ Internal use — pyrrolizidine-alkaloid hepatotoxicity.** As a Boraginaceae member, *Onosma* must be presumed to contain hepatotoxic pyrrolizidine alkaloids. According to PubMed, Fan et al. (2024, *Frontiers in Pharmacology*) document that Boraginaceous PAs (in *Cynoglossum amabile*) drive hepatotoxicity linked directly to their PA content [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2024.1325283), and Kimel et al. (2023, *Molecules*) show comfrey PA levels can exceed safe daily doses [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules28166171). **Do not use internally.**
- **Pregnancy — avoid.** Emmenagogue/abortifacient reputation attached to *onosma* in the classical sources; the PA hepatotoxicity risk compounds this. [USER: verify.]
- **Prolonged/large-area topical use — caution.** PAs show limited but non-zero skin permeability; reserve for short courses on limited areas.
- **Allergy / sensitisation.** Possible contact sensitivity to the bristly herbage or root pigments.
