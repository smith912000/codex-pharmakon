### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The only locus given is `_sources/ebers.csv` line 140: "Pelican,,animal,"Ebers Papyrus; Ghalioungui 1987 trans., Glossary I (Medicaments)",yes". The row names no binomial and no papyrus column or formula, and its last field flags the identification as uncertain ("yes"). Its source field cites the Glossary of Medicaments in Ghalioungui's 1987 translation; that book is not among the sources on disk, and I did not read it. So the attestation of the pelican rests on a list row alone.

I searched the Ebers material that is on disk. The Bryan 1930 text (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`) has no occurrence of "pelican" (a search for "pelic" and "pelik" returned nothing), and `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` (Bryan's alphabetical lists of minerals, plants and animals) has no pelican row. Bryan's list of animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (line 1388 and the list that follows, lines 1389-1400) gives the lion, hippopotamus, crocodile, ox, cow, calf, mouse, porcupine, serpent, tortoise, lizard, mole, vulture, bird-of-passage, swallow, pigeon, goose, duck, stag, gazelle, ram, goat, ass, hog, dog, cat, frog, tadpole, scorpion, eel, electric eel, crab, swordfish, ostrich, locust, tarantula, beetle, bat, wasp, fly and tapeworm; the pelican is not among them. Bryan also says (line 1388) that "the identity of a considerable number of the animals mentioned is still beyond us". The extract README (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/README.md`) records that many substances "are completely hidden from us under the guise of meaningless words" and are not in the lists.

Bryan's text does print untranslated bird names in prescriptions, for example "the adu-Bird" (line 1362), "the mesa-Bird" (line 1963) and "the per-baibait-bird" (line 2335), and "the gabgu-bird" (line 1377). None of these is identified with the pelican in the text I read, and I do not identify any of them with it. [USER: verify]

Whether the pelican appears in the papyrus under that rendering therefore depends on Ghalioungui's glossary, which I could not read. In this Codex the Ebers pelican is a list-row attestation with an uncertain identification. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

No part, preparation or prescription is attested in the sources read, so no active component can be assigned to an Ebers pelican material. As a general fact, a pelican is a large water bird whose tissues (flesh, fat, bile, feathers, bone) contain the usual proteins, lipids and minerals of birds; its pouch is skin and muscle. The attestation does not say which part, if any, was used.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and a thin one. The sources read give no indication, no preparation and no part of the pelican used. The Codex does not recommend any use of pelican material, and gives no protocol. Pelicans are protected in many countries, and wild water birds can carry avian influenza and other infections.

Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); no recitation, spell or ritual is linked to the pelican in any text I read, and no deity is attributed to it here, since none was read. ### Contraindications

Wild pelicans are protected in many jurisdictions, and killing or possessing them or their parts can be an offence; check the law of the jurisdiction. [USER: verify] Wild birds can carry avian influenza, *Salmonella* and parasites. This entry gives no medical advice.
