# Bhargi [SS] — Bhargi (Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch. 38 Arka'di and Pippalya'di groups, ch. 39 Vata-samshamana-varga); "Padma (Bhargi)" in Bhishagratna's glosses

## *Clerodendrum (Clerodendron) siphonanthus, as Bhishagratna's plant index (Vol. III, Appendix No. 3) glosses the name; a shrub of the verbena family known in Indian practice under names of the Bharangi type. The identification is the translator's, at medium-low confidence at species level: the first-pass guess, Clerodendrum serratum (L.) Moon, is also a plant sold as Bharangi, and the text on disk does not choose between them* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry, and the text is Bhishagratna's English translation, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, with the other two volumes read for the further occurrences. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it.

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Sutra ch. 38, the Arka'di group* (Vol. I, lines 15443-15450). The group is given as: "The drugs known as the Arka, Alarka, the two kinds of Karanja, Nagadanti, Mayuraka, **Bhargi**, Rasna, Indrapushpi, Kshudrashveta, Mahashveta, Vrishchikali, Alavana and Tapasha-Vriksha enter into the composition of the group known as the Arkadi Gana." The metrical text (lines 15453-15458) reads: "The group known as the Arkadi destroys Kaphani, fat, and the effects of poison. It acts as a vermifuge and a specific aseptic agent in the case of an ulcer and proves curative in diseases of the skin."

*Sutra ch. 38, the Pippalya'di group* (line 15495). Bhargi is also in this group: "Pippali, Pippali mulam, Chavya, Chitraka, Shringavera, Maricha, Hasti-Pippali, Harenuka, Ela, Ajamoda, Indrayava, Patha, Jiraka, Sarshapa, Maha-Nimva-Phala, Hingu, **Bhargi**, Madhurasa, Ativisha, Vacha, Vidanga and Katurohini." The metrical text (lines 15498-15505) says the group "acts as a good appetiser and is an absorbant of intestinal mucous and unassimilated lymph chyle", and that its therapeutic range "includes catarrh, deranged Kapham and Vatam, non-relish for food, abdominal glands, colic and gastralgia." No property is given for Bhargi alone in either group; its action is the group's.

*Sutra ch. 39, the Vata-samshamana-varga* (line 16010, in the chapter headed "CHAPTER XXXIX", line 15921). Among the drugs that soothe a deranged Vata the text lists "... Kanchanaka, **Bhargi**, Karpasi, Vrishchikali, Pattura, Vadara, Yava, Kola, Kulattha, etc. and the drugs forming the group of Vidarigandhadi-Gana, as well as those belonging to the first two groups of Panchamula, ... are possessed of the general virtue of soothing (restoring to its normal state) the deranged Vayu Vata" (lines 16004-16016).

*The gloss "Padma (Bhargi)".* In three places Bhishagratna translates the Sanskrit *Padma* as "Padma (Bhargi)": Vol. II, line 16906 (in the preparation of medicinal *arishtas*, *asavas* and lambatives, Chap. XL), and Vol. III, lines 9507 and 9746 (in the treatment of bloody stool and of dysenteric discharges, Uttara-tantra Chap. XL). At line 9507 the decoction is "of Payasya, Chandajia [sic], Padma (Bhargi), Sita (sugar), Musta and the pollen of lotus", for a case of "Pakvatisara marked by bloody stool". Whether Padma and Bhargi are one drug in the Sanskrit is the translator's reading and is not tested here. [USER: verify against the Sanskrit and Dallana.]

*The index.* Bhishagratna's plant index (Vol. III, Appendix No. 3; name "Bhargi" is printed with an OCR error as "Bhsirgi" at line 31408) answers with "Clerodendron Siphonanthus" (line 31462). The two columns are separated by the OCR and the alignment is ours, supported by the neighbouring pairs (for instance "Bhavya" with "Dillenia Indica" and "Bhringa-raja" with "Wedelia Calendulacea; Eclipta Erecta"). ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** It is a member of three groups, each with a stated action: the Arka'di (against Kapha, fat and poison; a vermifuge; an aseptic for ulcers; skin disease), the Pippalya'di (an appetiser; catarrh, Kapha and Vata, colic), and the Vata-soothing list.
- **What the text does not say.** The part used, any single action of Bhargi alone, any preparation of its own.

The Codex does not translate the Sanskrit terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text on disk gives no preparation of Bhargi as such. The "Padma (Bhargi)" decoctions in Vol. III (lines 9503-9509 and 9742-9748) name it with other drugs and give no weight for it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Clerodendrum* is a large genus of shrubs and climbers of warm regions, and at least two species are sold in India as Bharangi; they are not the same plant, which is why the identification here is held loosely. [USER: verify the trade identities] The classic's *Kapha*, *Vata* and "intestinal mucous" have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Bhargi sits in therapeutic groups (chapter 38) and a Vata-soothing list (chapter 39), all classified by action on the humours and on disease. None of the places found is a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no passage read names it in either discipline or relates it to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. Its status in the tradition is therapeutic only, and the cultivation status is unattested. The popular *sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas* lists come from Samkhya and later material, not from Sushruta, and none is applied here.

**Practitioner's note.** A plant that appears in groups against Kapha, Vata, skin disease and colic, and as "Padma (Bhargi)" in astringent recipes for bloody stool. It is a Vata-soothing member of a list, not a rejuvenative. A cultivator takes nothing from it, and should not use a plant sold under a similar name on the strength of this entry.
