# Mayura flesh — Mayura (also printed Myura, Mayuta), the peacock, flesh of a bird of the scatterer (Vishkira) group, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the bird Bhishagratna prints as "Mayura" and glosses "(peacock)" in ch.46, ch.20 and Uttaratantra ch.39; no binomial is printed for the bird* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Mayura is named in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 164 lists "Mayura (peacock)", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", id_uncertain "no"). The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The Mayura is a high-ground (*Jangala*) bird of the second of eight sub-groups, the *Vishkira*: "Birds such as the Lava, Tittiri, Kapinjala, Vartira, Vartika, Vartaka, Naptrika, Vatika, Chakora, Kalavinka, Mayura, Krakara, Upachakra, Kukkuta, Saranga, Shata-Patraka, Kutittiri, Kuruvahuka and Yavalaka belong to the Vishkira species" (lines 450-454). The group text: "The flesh of a bird of this group is light, cooling, sweet and astringent in taste and tends to pacify the deranged humours of the body" (lines 463-466).

The Mayura itself: "The flesh of the Mayura is astringent and saline in taste, and is beneficial to the skin, helps the growth of hair, improves the voice, intellect, appetite and relish for food, and imparts strength and vigour to the organs of sight and hearing" (lines 486-491, across a page break). In the summary verses it stands among the animals whose flesh is "the best of those of all other animals respectively belonging to their own species": "the Lava, Tittiri, Saranga, Kuranga, Ena, Kapinjala, Mayura (peacock), Varmi and Kurma (tortoise)" (lines 2160-2165). In the after-drinks passage, "Kola and Vadara wines should be used after having taken the cooked flesh of the fowls of the Vishkira species" (lines 2837-2840).

Other places. *Sutrasthana* ch.20 warns that the flesh of Kapinjala, "Myura (peacock", Lava, Tittira and Godha, "boiled with castor oil and on a fire of the twigs of castor plants, should not be eaten" (`SS_Sutra_ch20.txt`, lines 178-182). *Chikitsasthana* ch.39 gives the soup of "an Ena, Lava, hare, peacock, Tittiri, or deer" as light food after emetics and purgatives (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch39.txt`, line 240). *Uttaratantra* ch.40 (diarrhoea) allows food "with an unsalted soup (Yusha) of a peacock or of a Lava bird" (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch40.txt`, lines 770-772). In *Uttaratantra* ch.39 (fever), "Several authorities, however, do not recommend the use of the meat of Sarasa, Krouncha, Mayuta (peacock), Kukkuta (cock) and of Tittira in cases of fever, owing to its heaviness (as regards digestion), as well as to its heat-making potency"; the translator subscribes "with a certain limitation", and allows moderate quantities at the proper time (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt`, lines 909-915). *Kalpasthana* ch.1, on protecting food and drink from poison, includes the meat and soup of "a peacock, mungoose, Godhi (a species of lizard), or Prishata deer" in a king's regular diet, and gives a recipe: the flesh of a peacock "should be similarly cooked and spiced with sugar, Ativisha and Sunthi" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch01.txt`, lines 445-449 and 455-463). That chapter's own frame is protection from poison in the king's food: the text speaks of "similar antitoxic properties" and says "An intelligent king should always use food and drink of poison-destroying properties" (lines 465-469). Other peacock parts occur elsewhere and are not the flesh: peacock's bile for a case of *Svitra* in *Chikitsasthana* ch.9 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch09.txt`, line 355). *Sutrasthana* ch.29 (line 186) and ch.30 (line 120) also mention a peacock; those lines were not read in context and are not used. No Dioscorides, Tibetan or other text is attached to this entry; the tag is SS only.

**Identity.** The translator glosses the bird as "(peacock)" in ch.46, ch.20 and *Uttaratantra* ch.39, and his Vol. 3 index has "Mayura (see 'Birds')" (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, line 22335). His Vol. 3 glossary line "Mayura (Celosin creslola)" (line 30787) is the plant name, the OCR of a binomial resembling *Celosia cristata*, and does not describe the bird. No binomial is printed for the bird. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Peafowl flesh is mostly water and muscle protein with some fat, and supplies iron, zinc and B vitamins. No analysis specific to this entry was read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text recommends the peacock's flesh for the skin, hair, voice, appetite and the senses of sight and hearing, and includes it in a king's anti-poison diet; the Codex does not recommend it for any of these. The peacock is a national bird and a sacred or protected animal in some countries, its sale is regulated, and no supply can be recommended. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the text gives: a soup or *yusha* of the flesh, unsalted in the diarrhoea diet (*Uttaratantra* ch.40); a soup after emetics and purgatives (*Chikitsasthana* ch.39); the king's soup cooked and spiced with sugar, *Ativisha* and *Sunthi* (*Kalpasthana* ch.1). Kola and Vadara wines are the after-drink for the *Vishkira* group (*Sutrasthana* ch.46). The one prohibited preparation is boiling it with castor oil on a fire of castor twigs (*Sutrasthana* ch.20). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Peafowl and other game birds must be cooked through and handled hygienically: birds can carry *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*, and avian influenza viruses. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 463-491). For the *Vishkira* group: light; cooling; sweet and astringent in taste; pacifies the deranged humours. For the Mayura: astringent and saline in taste; beneficial to skin; helps hair growth; improves voice, intellect, appetite and relish for food; strengthens sight and hearing. Ranked among the best of its kind (lines 2160-2165). *Uttaratantra* ch.39 reports that some authorities hold the peacock's meat heavy and heat-making in potency, against the group's "cooling" in ch.46; the text gives that as an opinion with a qualification and does not reconcile it. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Mayura is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food in general, not about this flesh. The text credits the flesh with benefit to skin, hair, voice, intellect, sight and hearing, and ranks it among the best of its kind; these are dietary properties and a dietary ranking, not a cultivation grade, and no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter read names the Mayura. The Mayura has no "spermatopoietic" claim in ch.46, unlike the Tittiri, Krakara and Upachakra that stand beside it. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta the peacock is a food whose stated benefits are to skin, hair, voice and the senses, and the passages that bear on a cultivator are the cautions: some authorities avoid it in fever as heavy and heating, it is taken unsalted in diarrhoea, and it is never boiled in castor oil. A person who eats meat may read the classification as the tradition's own description of the food; a person who does not has nothing to adopt.

### Contraindications

- **Food safety:** poultry-type birds carry *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*; cook through and keep raw and cooked foods apart.
- **The text's own cautions:** heavy and heat-making according to some authorities, so avoided in fever except in moderate quantity (*Uttaratantra* ch.39); not boiled with castor oil on a castor-twig fire (ch.20); unsalted in the diarrhoea diet (ch.40).
- **Legal and conservation status:** the Indian peafowl is legally protected in India, and the wild status and trade of peafowl species vary by country. [USER: verify]
- **Identity:** the plant glossary line "Mayura" is a different thing from the bird; do not read the glossary binomial as the bird's.
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food is a moral question for many traditions, and the peacock is sacred in some; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
