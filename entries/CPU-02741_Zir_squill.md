# Zir squill — بصل الزير (*baṣal al-zīr*)

## *A squill-like bulb ('onion of the *zīr*') that resembles rat's squill in power and taste but is weaker, used in its place; no modern species is assigned (confidence low; a squill relative is only the Canon's own comparison)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk, so far as searched. Source file: `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`.

**Locus (CM).** *Canon* Book II, entry *baṣal al-zīr* (بصل الزير), heading at Arabic text line 11473; entry to line 11476 (the next heading is *banāt wardān*, line 11477).

**What it is (CM).** 'It resembles *baṣal al-fār* (rat's onion) in its power and taste, and is used in its place, and it is weaker than it' (يشبه بصل الفأر في قوته وطعمه ويستعمل بدله وهو أضعف منه; lines 11474-11475). Rat's onion has its own entries in the Canon (lines 10045 and 16465); they were not read for this entry. The Codex's Squill (CPU-00041, I_222) is a related but separate record. **Identity doubt:** *al-zīr* is not explained; no species is assigned. [USER: verify]

**What the text says (CM).** Poisons: it helps against poisons and against the sting of the scorpion and of the *rutaylāʾ* (a venomous spider), drunk or as a poultice when mixed with figs (lines 11475-11476). Nothing else is given.

### Active Components

General chemistry: squills (*Drimia* and related) contain cardiac glycosides (bufadienolides). This is general knowledge about squill; it is not stated in the Canon and is not asserted for this plant. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The entry records a weaker substitute for rat's squill, used drunk or as a poultice with figs for venomous stings. It is not a recommended practice.

### Preparation & Dosing

Forms named: a drink, and a poultice mixed with figs. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General fact: squill bulbs are cardiotoxic.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No temperament is printed. Its power and taste are said to be like rat's onion, but weaker (line 11474).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(b) Framed.* The entry is a drug-by-drug list of qualities and uses inside the *Canon*'s materia medica. It assigns no cultivation status and says nothing of innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruh*. Nothing is said of the faculties.

**Practitioner's note.** A weaker squill used in place of rat's squill, noted for scorpion and spider bite. Nothing supports a cultivation use.

### Contraindications

If the bulb is squill-like, it is cardiotoxic in general terms, and the text calls it a substitute for a drug with that character; do not ingest. The text states no contraindication. [USER: verify]
