# Lava flesh — Lava (also printed Ldva), flesh of a bird of the scatterer (Vishkira) group, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the bird Bhishagratna prints as "Lava" and "Ldva" and calls a bird; the spine list glosses it "quail"; his Vol. 3 glossary prints a binomial in damaged OCR as "Perdix chirtensis"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Lava is named in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 154 lists "Lava (quail)", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", flagged id_uncertain). The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The Lava is a high-ground (*Jangala*) bird of the second of eight sub-groups, the *Vishkira*: "Birds such as the Lava, Tittiri, Kapinjala, Vartira, Vartika, Vartaka, Naptrika, Vatika, Chakora, Kalavinka, Mayura, Krakara, Upachakra, Kukkuta, Saranga, Shata-Patraka, Kutittiri, Kuruvahuka and Yavalaka belong to the Vishkira species" (lines 450-454). They are so called, the translator's bracket says, "from the fact of their picking up their food after scattering it first with their bills and claws", and "The flesh of a bird of this group is light, cooling, sweet and astringent in taste and tends to pacify the deranged humours of the body" (lines 454-466).

The Lava itself: "The flesh of the Lava is light, has a sweet and astringent taste, is pungent of digestion, and possessed of astringent and appetising properties. It is highly efficacious in diseases due to the concerted humours of the body" (lines 468-472). Further down, in the summary verses, the Lava heads a list of animals whose flesh is "the best of those of all other animals respectively belonging to their own species": "The flesh of the Lava, Tittiri, Saranga, Kuranga, Ena, Kapinjala, Mayura (peacock), Varmi and Kurma (tortoise) should be regarded as the best" (lines 2160-2165). In the after-drinks passage, "Kola and Vadara wines should be used after having taken the cooked flesh of the fowls of the Vishkira species" (lines 2837-2840).

Other places. *Sutrasthana* ch.20 (*Hitahitiya*, on wholesome and unwholesome regimen) names the flesh of the Lava among the "flesh of the Ena, the Harina ... Mriga-matrika ... the Lava, the Tittiri, the Kapinjala, the Varttira, and the Varttika" within "Articles or substances which may be safely included within the food stuffs of all human beings" (`SS_Sutra_ch20.txt`, lines 43-55), and warns that the flesh of Kapinjala, peacock, Lava, Tittira and Godha, "boiled with castor oil and on a fire of the twigs of castor plants, should not be eaten" (lines 178-182). Lava appears in invalid and seasonal diets: after emetics and purgatives, the soup of "an Ena, Lava, hare, peacock, Tittiri, or deer" with light food (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch39.txt`, line 240); in the spring regimen, soup "of the meat of the animals of the Vishkira class, such as Lava, etc." (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch64.txt`, line 173); in the diet of a fever patient used to animal food, "The meat of Lava, Kapinjala, Ena ... " (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt`, lines 905-907); for diarrhoea, "unsalted soup of a peacock or of a Lava bird" (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch40.txt`, lines 770-772); as meat-juice of "Lava, Ena and Tittira unmixed with any acid" with sugar and ghee (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch47.txt`, line 184; chapter subject not checked); as boiled flesh of fish, cock or Lava in a hot poultice, *pinda-sveda* (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch21.txt`, line 49); and among "meat of birds (such as Lava, etc.)" counted as invigorating to eyesight (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch17.txt`, line 442). *Uttaratantra* ch.65, a chapter explaining the text's own method, uses "Lava and Tittira (two kinds of birds)" as examples of the principal meats (line 284). The Dioscorides, Tibetan and other texts are not attached to this entry.

**Identity.** The translator's glossary prints "Lava [Perdix chirtensis]" (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, line 30721; the binomial is OCR-damaged and its spelling cannot be relied on), and the Vol. 3 index has "Lava (bird)" (line 21998). The spine list's "quail" is the list's own gloss. A partridge-type bird is suggested by the glossary genus, a quail by the list; the two do not agree and the text does not decide. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Game-bird flesh is mostly water and muscle protein with some fat, and supplies iron, zinc and B vitamins. No analysis specific to this entry was read, and the species is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Lava is a wild game bird of uncertain identity, ranked in the text among the best meats of its kind and recommended in several invalid diets, but nothing in the sources supports eating or sourcing it today. Wild game birds are subject to hunting and trade law, and the species cannot be chosen from this text. The Codex does not recommend the flesh as a therapy and gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the text gives: a soup or meat-juice of the Lava, unsalted in the diarrhoea diet (*Uttaratantra* ch.40) and without acid in ch.47; a soup after emetics and purgatives (*Chikitsasthana* ch.39); boiled flesh in a hot poultice (*Uttaratantra* ch.21). Kola and Vadara wines are the after-drink for the *Vishkira* group (*Sutrasthana* ch.46). The one prohibited preparation is boiling it with castor oil on a fire of castor twigs (*Sutrasthana* ch.20). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Game-bird flesh must be cooked through and handled hygienically: birds can carry *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*, and wild birds can carry avian influenza viruses. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 463-472). For the *Vishkira* group: light; cooling; sweet and astringent in taste; pacifies the deranged humours. For the Lava: light; sweet and astringent in taste; pungent of digestion; astringent and appetising; highly efficacious in diseases due to the concerted humours. Ranked among the best of its kind in the summary verses (lines 2160-2165). No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Lava is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food in general, not about this flesh. The summary verses rank the Lava among the best flesh of its kind (lines 2160-2165), which is a dietary ranking and not a cultivation grade. The text's properties for the Lava (light, astringent, appetising) include no "spermatopoietic" and no tonic claim, and no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter read names it. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta the Lava is a light food for the sick, the convalescent and the spring season, and the passages that bear on a cultivator are those on restraint: unsalted for diarrhoea, never boiled in castor oil. A person who eats meat may read the classification as the tradition's own description of the food; a person who does not has nothing to adopt.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** the bird is not settled (quail on the list, a *Perdix* name in the damaged glossary); do not substitute a species on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Food safety:** game birds carry *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*; cook through and keep raw and cooked foods apart.
- **The text's own cautions:** not boiled with castor oil on a castor-twig fire (ch.20); unsalted and without acid in the stated diets.
- **Legal and conservation status:** hunting, sale and import of wild game birds are regulated in many countries. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food is a moral question for many traditions, and meat is restricted in some; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
