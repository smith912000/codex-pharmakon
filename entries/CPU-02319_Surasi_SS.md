# Surasi — Surasi (Sushruta Samhita)

## *Unresolved: a drug named Surasi in the Surasadi group of the Sushruta Samhita, whose flowers are an ingredient in two antivenom compounds in the Kalpasthana; the translation prints no Latin name; a basil-type reading is a lead only, and confidence in any identification is low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The name is printed *Surasi* in Bhishagratna's translation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder with the locus "SS Sutra 38, Surasa'di group (Vol. I line 15463)"; this entry replaces it. A search of the chapter files and of the three Bhishagratna volumes found the name in the *Sutrasthana* list and in two chapters of the *Kalpasthana*. Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 (Latin synonyms, Vol. III) does not list the name.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The Surasadi group (Sutra ch. 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 119 to 130; Vol. I line 15463).* "The Surasa'di Group: — The drugs known as Surasa, white Surasa, Fainjjhaka, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika form the group known as the Surasadi Gana." The metrical text: "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough." The same chapter list prints both *Surasa* (with a "white Surasa") and *Surasi* as separate members, so the two names are not treated as one drug here. The group also appears in chapter 42 among those that "collectively form the pungent group" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` lines 324 to 331), and Bhishagratna's footnote to *Uttaratantra* ch. 39 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt` lines 1534 to 1535) reports Dallana as explaining the "heat-making group" (*Ushna-varga*) to mean "the Bhadradatvadi, Surasidi and the Elddi groups".

*The antivenom compounds (Kalpasthana).* In ch. 5, the snake-bite chapter (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch05.txt` lines 411 to 418), a compound of powders of Mansi, Triphala, Murangi, Manjishtha, Yashti-madhu and others, with "flowers of Surasi and of Bhallataka, well pasted with honey and with the bile of a boar (Vardha), Godha, Peacock, Sallaka, cat, Prishata (deer) and of mungoose, should be preserved inside a horn. This anti-venomous medicine is called the Rishabha'gada." In ch. 7 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch07.txt` lines 120 to 146), Surasi stands in a long list of flowers and other drugs ("Kumuda, Utpala, Padma, flower of Arka, flowers and fruits of Champaka, Asoka, Sumanas, Tilaka, Patali, Salmali, Selu, Sirisha, Surasi ...") that is "pasted with honey, clarified butter and the bile of a cow and should be kept inside a horn"; the text calls the compound of "eighty-five ingredients" the Mahasugandhi Agada. In both places Surasi supplies flowers.

**What the sources do not give.** No Latin name and no description. The name resembles Surasa, and in later Sanskrit usage *surasa* is reported to denote a basil, but this was not found in any text opened for this entry, and the lists here keep Surasa and Surasi apart. The basil idea is a lead only. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a group member: vermifuge, aseptic, subduing Kapham, catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma, cough (ch. 38). As flowers in a compound: an ingredient in two antivenom preparations (Kalpa ch. 5 and ch. 7).
- **What the text does not say.** Anything about the plant itself. The antivenom claims belong to the compounds as a whole and are not assigned to Surasi.

The Codex gives no protocol. Snake-bite and poisoning are medical emergencies, and nothing here is a treatment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparations are the two compounds above, in which the flowers are pasted with honey and animal bile (and, in ch. 7, ghee) and kept in a horn. No quantity for Surasi is printed in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Surasi alone. Through its group it falls in the pungent group (ch. 42) and its group subdues Kapham (ch. 38).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Surasi is named in the Sushruta Samhita as a member of a therapeutic group (*gana*) (the Surasadi group, Sutra ch. 38) and, as a flower, in two antivenom compounds of the *Kalpasthana*. A *gana* is a grouping by therapeutic action. Neither the group nor an antivenom compound is a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status, and the text read assigns Surasi neither. The chapters of the *Chikitsasthana* on *rasayana* and *vajikarana* (ch. 26 to 31 on disk) were searched for the name and it does not occur in them. Nothing links it to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The status is unattested; only a therapeutic one is recorded.

**Practitioner's note.** A flower in two antivenom pastes and a name in a pungent group. A cultivator has nothing to use from it until it is identified, and nothing in the record supports use outside the compounds named.
