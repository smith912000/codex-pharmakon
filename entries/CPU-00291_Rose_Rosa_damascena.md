### Origin & Tradition

The Damask rose is one of the oldest cultivated aromatics of the temperate Old World, a hybrid of *Rosa gallica* × *R. moschata* lineage carried westward from Persia and the Levant. Its dried petals, distilled water (*gulāb*, rose-water), and steam-distilled attar (*ruh gulab*, otto of rose) became trade goods that crossed every major medical tradition, which is why Rose sits in this Codex as a genuine cross-tradition substance rather than a single-lineage dravya.

**Ayurvedic (Nighaṇṭu tradition):** Rose is not a classical Vedic-era Sushruta dravya in the way the *daśamūla* roots are; it enters Ayurveda most strongly through the later *nighaṇṭu* (materia medica lexicon) literature and the Mughal-era exchange with Unani practice, under the names *Gulāb*, *Śatapatrī* ("hundred-petalled"), and *Tāruṇī*. In this stream it is classed *hṛdya* (cardiotonic, gladdening to the heart), *raktaprasādana* (blood-clarifying), *dāhaśāmaka* (heat/burning-relieving), and *manasika balya* (tonic to the mind). *Gulkand* — rose petals macerated with sugar and sun-cured — is the most widely used Ayurvedic-Unani preparation, given as a gentle laxative, a Pitta-cooling summer tonic, and a remedy for burning sensations, mouth ulcers, and mental heat/irritability.

**Unani (Avicenna and successors):** Here Rose has its deepest documented history. In the Unani temperament system Rose (*Gul-e-surkh*) is classed as **cold and dry** in the first degree, *muqawwi-e-qalb* (heart-strengthener), *muqawwi-e-dimagh* (brain/cephalic-strengthener), and *mufarrih* (exhilarant — a mood-lifting, spirit-gladdening agent). Avicenna's *Canon* and later Unani formularies use rose-water and rose oil for palpitations, headache, inflamed eyes, and disturbances of the "vital spirit." *Gulqand* and *arq-e-gulab* (rose distillate) remain first-line Unani mufarrih remedies today.

**Persian & folk tradition:** In Iran the plant is *Gol-e-Mohammadi*, "the flower of the Prophet Muhammad," and the Kashan rose-harvest and *golabgiri* (rose-water distillation) is a centuries-old ritual. Rose-water carries devotional and purificatory significance across Islamic and Hindu practice alike — sprinkled in shrines, mosques, and temples.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the Cooling Heart-Opener:** Rose is the archetypal *hṛdya* in the double Ayurvedic sense: it strengthens the physical heart (*hṛdaya*, the organ) and gladdens the emotional/spiritual heart (the *hṛt*, seat of *manas* and *ojas*). Its doctrine sits at the intersection of the two systems it belongs to: Unani *tafrih* (exhilaration of the spirit) and Ayurvedic *prasāda* (serene clarity of mind). The signature is legible — a soft, cool, moist, sweetly-scented flower with a subtle astringent bite maps to an action that cools inflamed Pitta, soothes agitated Vāta-manas, opens the emotional heart, and yet gently astringes and tones (the petals' tannins) rather than merely relaxing. Symbolically the rose is love, devotion, and the opened heart across Sufi, Christian, and bhakti traditions alike — a rare case where the doctrine-of-signatures reading and the cross-cultural symbolism converge on the same organ and the same emotional register.

### Active Components

- **Volatile oil (attar / otto):** dominated by the monoterpene alcohols **citronellol** and **geraniol** (the principal aroma and bioactivity carriers), with nerol, linalool, and the trace odour-impact compound β-damascenone.
- **Phenylethyl alcohol** — a major constituent of rose-water (largely water-soluble, so concentrated in the distillate rather than the oil).
- **Flavonoids** — quercetin, kaempferol and glycosides; **anthocyanins** (petal pigments).
- **Hydrolysable tannins** (gallotannins, ellagitannins) — the astringent, *raktaprasādana* fraction.
- **Vitamin C** and organic acids in petals and hips.

Per PubMed (Mahboubi 2015, review), citronellol and geraniol are identified as the constituents chiefly responsible for the plant's pharmacological activity.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Rose is filed as a **[RESET] / [SLEEP]** agent — a cooling, heart-opening de-escalator for phases dominated by heat, agitation, and sympathetic over-arousal.

- **[RESET]:** after a high-Pitta, high-stress load — irritability, "burning" mental states, inflamed mood — *gulkand* or rose-water calms the manas and cools the system without sedating heaviness. This is Rose's exhilarant-yet-cooling (*mufarrih* + *dāhaśāmaka*) profile in application.
- **[SLEEP]:** rose aromatics used at wind-down (tea, distillate, or the essential oil by inhalation/olfactory route) to lower anxious arousal before sleep. The anxiolytic literature (below) is the strongest evidence base for this use.

Rose does **not** build tissue or drive output; it is a corrective/soothing agent, best paired with tonics rather than used as one.

### Scientific Correlation

Attribution: findings from PubMed.

- **Anxiety, depression, and stress (clinical — the strongest evidence base):** A systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (Rasooli et al., 2021, *Phytotherapy Research*, 32 studies pooled) found that *Rosa damascena* administration significantly reduced **state anxiety** (SMD −1.74, 95% CI −2.29 to −1.20; p<0.001), **depression** (SMD −0.87, 95% CI −1.47 to −0.28; p=0.004), and **stress** (SMD −1.33, 95% CI −1.98 to −0.69; p<0.001), but had **no significant effect on trait anxiety**. The authors graded evidence quality as very low to moderate (GRADE) and called for further high-quality RCTs. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.7243)
- **Ethnopharmacology & phytochemistry (review):** Mahboubi (2015, *Journal of Traditional and Complementary Medicine*) reviews confirmed antimicrobial, antioxidant, analgesic, anti-inflammatory, anti-diabetic, and anti-depressant properties, attributing activity chiefly to citronellol and geraniol. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtcme.2015.09.005)

**Evidence grade:** For anxiety/stress/mood — **clinical (meta-analytic), but graded very-low-to-moderate quality**; effect on state (situational) anxiety is more robust than on trait anxiety. For antioxidant/anti-inflammatory/antimicrobial actions — **preclinical to early clinical**. The traditional cardiotonic (*hṛdya* / *muqawwi-e-qalb*) claim is longstanding but not established by robust modern cardiac trials — treat as traditional.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurvedic (Nighaṇṭu / Unani-influenced):**
- **Rasa (taste):** *Madhura* (sweet), *Tikta* (bitter), *Kaṣāya* (astringent)
- **Guṇa (quality):** *Laghu* (light), *Snigdha* (slightly unctuous) — with a cooling refinement
- **Vīrya (potency):** *Śīta* (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** *Madhura* (sweet)
- **Prabhāva (specific/idiosyncratic effect):** *Hṛdya* + *mufarrih* — the specific heart-gladdening, spirit-exhilarating action not fully explained by rasa/vīrya/vipāka alone
- **Doṣa action:** *Pitta-śāmaka* (primary), *Vāta-śāmaka* (via its unctuous, soothing quality on manas); Kapha-neutral to mildly increasing in large sweet doses (e.g. heavy gulkand)
- **Karma (actions):** *Hṛdya* (cardiotonic), *raktaprasādana* (blood-clarifying), *dāhaśāmaka* (relieves burning), *manasika balya / medhya-adjacent* (calms and clears the mind), *anulomana / mṛdu-recana* (gentle laxative, as gulkand), *varṇya* (complexion-enhancing)

**Unani temperament:** Cold & dry (1st degree); *muqawwi-e-qalb*, *muqawwi-e-dimagh*, *mufarrih*.

**Dantian / subtle note (optional):** Works at the middle/heart centre — an opener and cooler of the emotional heart rather than a lower-centre builder.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian (EP).** No secure Egyptian cultivation framing for rose is attested. The Egyptian corpus is indication-organised — it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution — and in that tradition drug and spoken *heka* form a single intervention, so any Egyptian "use" separated from its recitation is already only half the record. Substance identity in the papyri is itself a modern hypothesis. **(c) absent.**

**De Materia Medica (DMM).** Dioscorides records the rose (*rhodon*) among astringent, cooling aromatics, and offers no subtle-body system into which a cultivation status could be set. What he gives the cultivator is the discipline of attention — the quality of the petals, their handling and preparation — not a claim about the flower's action on a vital principle. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status; the discernment is the practice.

**Canon / Unani (CM).** This is the genuine claim, and it must be located precisely. Rose (*gul-e-surkh*) is attested as a *mufarriḥ* — an exhilarant, spirit-gladdening cardiac drug held to strengthen the *rūḥ* — but the *mufarriḥ* class belongs to Ibn Sīnā's ***Kitāb al-Adwiya al-Qalbiyya*** (the treatise on cardiac remedies), **not** the *Canon*; rose appears among the drugs of that work in the scholarship reviewing it. Within the Unani frame of innate heat and the vital spirit this is a cultivation-adjacent status: a drug held to gladden and strengthen the *rūḥ*. **(a) attested** — as a *mufarriḥ* of the *Adwiya al-Qalbiyya*, expressly not the *Canon*, and kept separate from the devotional/aromatic uses.

**Bencao (TCM).** Rose (*méi guī huā*) is claimed by the later Chinese materia medica as an aromatic that regulates and moves qi, but no *san pin* 三品 grade was retrieved for it, so no cultivation status can be stated — only a therapeutic, qi-regulating one. **(b) framed** — attested, no cultivation grade.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS).** Rose is explicitly **not** a classical Vedic-era Sushrutan *dravya*; it enters Ayurveda through the later *nighaṇṭu* lexicon tradition and the Mughal-era exchange with Unani, as *gulāb / śatapatrī*. It is classed *hṛdya* (cardiotonic, heart-gladdening) and *manasika balya* (mind-soothing), but no formal *rasāyana* or *vājīkaraṇa* discipline is attested for it; its status is therapeutic and soothing, not a formal cultivation regimen. **(b) framed** — claimed, but no rejuvenative discipline attested.

**Four Tantras (FMT).** No formal Tibetan subtle-body-practice category exists for rose, and none is asserted. **(c) absent.**

**Practitioner's note.** Two things must be kept apart. The *cultivation* status is thin and singular: only the Unani tradition assigns rose a genuine vital role — a *mufarriḥ* that gladdens and strengthens the *rūḥ* — and even that lives in the *Adwiya al-Qalbiyya*, not the *Canon*. The *devotional and aromatic* life of the rose — rose-water in shrine and mosque, the Kashan *golābgiri*, the Sufi and bhakti symbolism of the opened heart — is real and ancient but is ritual and cultural, not a materia-medica cultivation assignment, and the two should never be blurred. What a cultivator actually does with rose is corrective and calming: a cooling, heart-soothing aromatic for states of heat and agitation, best paired with tonics rather than used as one.

### Contraindications

- Generally very safe in culinary/traditional doses. Essential oil (otto) must **never be ingested neat** and should be well-diluted for topical use.
- Rose can be mildly cooling/drying and laxative in excess — caution in those with a very cold, sluggish (high-Kapha, low-agni) constitution or loose stools.
- Possible allergy/contact sensitivity to rose oil in susceptible individuals.
- Pregnancy/lactation: culinary rose-water is traditionally used, but concentrated essential oil should be avoided without professional guidance. `[USER: verify]`
- Not a substitute for prescribed treatment of clinical depression or anxiety disorders; the RCT evidence supports adjunctive/situational use, not monotherapy for major psychiatric illness.
