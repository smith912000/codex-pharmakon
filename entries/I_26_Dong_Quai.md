## Section 1 — Nomenclature

### Primary Scientific Name
*Angelica sinensis* (Oliv.) Diels (Apiaceae/Umbelliferae)

### Nomenclature Table

| Tradition | Language | Name | Transliteration | Meaning |
|-----------|----------|------|-----------------|---------|
| Chinese (SBJ) | 文言文 | 當歸 | Dāng Guī | "The one who should return" — possibly referring to the return of blood/qi, or a husband's return to his wife (reflecting gynecological use) |
| Chinese | 文言文 | 全歸 / 歸尾 | Quán Guī / Guī Wěi | Whole root vs root tip — different parts used for different actions |
| Tibetan (FMT) | བོད་སྐད། | གླ་སྒང | Gla sgang | Standard Tibetan pharmaceutical name |
| Arabic (CM) | العربية | — | — | Closely related *A. archangelica* (angelica root) is the Islamic medicine equivalent; *A. sinensis* attested in later Islamic texts via trade |
| Hindi | हिन्दी | — | — | Ayurvedic tradition uses *A. glauca* (choraka) — different species but related pharmacologically |
| English | — | Dong quai / Female ginseng | — | "Female ginseng" reflects its reputation as the primary women's tonic herb of East Asian medicine |

### Botanical Identity
Perennial herb 40–100cm; compound umbel flowers (white); large tuberous root aromatic with characteristic sweet-spicy celery-like odour; native to southwestern China (Gansu, Yunnan, Sichuan); cultivated primarily in Gansu province (Minxian Dang Gui — highest quality). Three parts: head (*gui tou*) — Blood tonifying; body (*gui shen*) — mixed tonic/moving; tail (*gui wei*) — Blood-moving. This three-part differential is a level of pharmaceutical precision unusual even in classical TCM.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ)

> *"Dāng guī: sweet, acrid, bitter; warm. Enters Heart, Liver, Spleen channels. Tonifies the Blood, invigorates Blood circulation, regulates menstruation, alleviates pain, moistens the intestines. The foremost herb for Blood deficiency patterns, dysmenorrhoea, amenorrhoea, irregular menstruation, postpartum Blood stasis. Used in all conditions where Blood must be simultaneously tonified and moved."*

Dang Gui is the most widely used herb in classical Chinese gynecology — it appears in virtually every formula addressing gynecological blood disorders. The unique capacity to simultaneously *tonify* and *move* Blood (normally opposing pharmacological actions) is the defining characteristic that makes it irreplaceable in classical formulation.

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā references *Angelica archangelica* (European angelica) extensively for similar applications — warming, emmenagogue, carminative — with the same energetic characterisation. *A. sinensis* entered Islamic medicine via the silk road trade and is referenced in later Islamic medical texts for identical gynecological applications.

### 2.3 Four Medical Tantras (FMT)

*Rgyud bzhi*:
> *"Gla sgang (angelica/dong quai): sweet, acrid; warm. Tonifies Blood, relieves pain, warms and moves the channels. Used in compound formulas for cold Blood deficiency patterns with pain — post-partum weakness, menstrual irregularity, joint pain from Blood deficiency."*

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

| Compound | Content | Activity |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Z-Ligustilide** | 5–18% of EO | Primary active; smooth muscle relaxant (uterus, vasculature); antispasmodic; crosses BBB; antidepressant potential |
| **Ferulic acid** | 0.1–0.3% of root | Blood thinning (antiplatelet); anti-inflammatory; antioxidant; estrogenic |
| **Phthalides** (senkyunolide, butylidene phthalide) | EO fraction | Antispasmodic; smooth muscle relaxant; sedative |
| **Polysaccharides (ASP)** | 15–35% | Hematopoietic (red blood cell production); immunostimulatory |
| **Succinic acid, vanillin** | Minor | Anti-inflammatory |
| **Vitamin B12 analogue** | Trace | Hematopoietic (haemopoietic support — may explain erythropoietic effect) |
| **Coumarins** (oxypeucedanin) | Minor | Anticoagulant; anti-spasmodic |

**Z-Ligustilide** is the pharmacological anchor — the compound responsible for the characteristic dong quai aroma and the primary antispasmodic action. **Ferulic acid** is responsible for the antiplatelet and estrogenic activity.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Menstrual Regulation (Dual Mechanism)

**Stimulatory phase** (Blood-moving): Ferulic acid → inhibits platelet aggregation (TXA2 inhibition) → reduced uterine blood stasis → allows normal menstrual flow. Uterotonic activity of certain phthalides (low-dose uterine contraction stimulation).

**Relaxant phase** (Pain-relieving): Z-Ligustilide → smooth muscle relaxant → uterine relaxation → anti-spasmodic → relieves dysmenorrhoea. Also vasodilatory → improves uterine perfusion.

This bidirectional action — the traditional "tonifies and moves Blood simultaneously" — is pharmacologically coherent: ferulic acid moves blood (antiplatelet), phthalides relax spasm (anti-dysmenorrhoea), polysaccharides build blood (haematopoietic). Each constituent addresses a different aspect of the pattern.

### 5.2 Haematopoietic (Blood-Tonifying)

**ASP polysaccharides**: Stimulate erythropoiesis (red blood cell production) — shown in multiple animal studies to increase haemoglobin and erythrocyte count. This validates the "Blood tonic" classification — dong quai may genuinely increase red cell production, explaining why it was used for anaemia and post-haemorrhagic recovery.

### 5.3 Phytoestrogenic

**Ferulic acid + coumestans**: Weak estrogen receptor (ERβ) agonism → mild estrogenic effect. This explains the traditional menopause/menopausal syndrome application but also creates the contraindication in estrogen-sensitive cancers.

### 5.4 Neuroprotective / Antidepressant

**Z-Ligustilide**: Crosses BBB; BDNF elevation in hippocampus → neuroprotective. Monoamine oxidase inhibition → mild antidepressant. This may explain the TCM application for heart/mind conditions accompanying Blood deficiency.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Dysmenorrhoea**: Multiple Chinese RCTs confirm dong quai preparations (alone and in formula) significantly reduce dysmenorrhoea pain scores; comparable to NSAIDs in some studies.

**Anaemia**: Clinical use widely accepted in TCM practice; limited RCT data specifically for haematopoietic effect.

**Menopause symptoms**: Mixed evidence; some studies show significant reduction in hot flash frequency; estrogenic mechanism is plausible but effect size modest.

**Atherosclerosis / cardiovascular**: Ferulic acid component has significant antiplatelet evidence; multiple small human studies confirm antiplatelet effect.

---

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The return of blood**: The Chinese name *Dāng Guī* — "the one who should return" — encodes a clinical observation: when the Blood (in the TCM sense — including red cell mass, menstrual regularity, tissue perfusion) has departed or become deficient, Dang Gui returns it. The name is poetic, mnemonic, and pharmacologically accurate simultaneously.

**Gender-specific primary herb**: Dong Quai occupies in Chinese gynecology the position that Ginseng occupies in general tonic practice. This reflects the observation that most tonification practices were male-centred (ginseng for Qi/Yang); dong quai was developed as the foundational female Jing-Blood tonic, recognising that Blood deficiency is the characteristic deficiency pattern of reproductive-age women.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Decoction | 6–15g dried root per 500ml | Classic gynecological tonic |
| *Si wu tang* (Four Substance Decoction) | Dang Gui + Rehmannia + Peony + Ligusticum | Foundational Blood tonic formula |
| Capsule/tablet | 500–1500mg/day | Western commercial form |
| Tincture | 2–4ml 1:5 tincture | Amenorrhoea, dysmenorrhoea |
| Whole root in bone broth | 15–30g per pot | Postpartum recovery traditional preparation |

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Sweet, acrid, bitter; warm. Channels: Heart (心), Liver (肝), Spleen (脾). Dual action: tonify + move Blood. Primary gynecological herb. Key formula: *Si Wu Tang* (four substance) = foundation of Chinese Blood nourishment.

**Tibetan**: Sweet, acrid; warm. Blood tonic, channel-warming. Pairs with warming herbs in cold Blood patterns.

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Pregnancy**: Uterotonic — contraindicated in pregnancy (first trimester especially)
- **Estrogen-sensitive cancers**: Phytoestrogenic activity — avoid in breast/uterine cancer
- **Heavy menstrual bleeding**: Blood-moving activity may worsen flooding
- **Anticoagulants**: Ferulic acid antiplatelet + potential warfarin interaction — monitor INR
- **Hot/damp patterns**: Warming nature — avoid in excessive heat patterns (Yin deficiency with Heat)
