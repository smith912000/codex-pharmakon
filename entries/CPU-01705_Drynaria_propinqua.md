# Drynaria propinqua — ldum.bu.re.ral, the "king fern" (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Drynaria propinqua, the first of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for ldum.bu.re.ral (with Polystichum squarrosum), in an entry that gives identifications and a rank among ferns and prints no taste and no action; the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text has no line for this Tibetan name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT), and under this name only by Clark.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. Both Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance", and here the line prints two, of which the row takes the first (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, line 180). The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1084).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The whole entry reads:

"ldum.bu.re.ral: Drynaria propinqua: Polystichum squarrosum: is said to be the 'king fern', whilst brags.spos (Lepisorus) is the 'queen fern' and brags.skya.ha.wo (Corallodiscus) is the 'minister fern']."

It is printed with a closing bracket and no opening one, so the text as it stands on disk cannot show where the commentary layer begins; Clark's preface (line 82) says that "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". The entry gives the Tibetan name, the two Latin names, which are Clark's identifications and not the root text's, and a ranking of three ferns. It prints no taste, no power, no part used and no action.

*The entry before it (line 1083).* The line before names *ldum.bu.re.ral* among the names of another drug:

"re.ral: a type of Fern: Adiantum pedatum L.: Adiantum aspidium: [has five names, for example rgyal.po.re.ral(lit. 'king fern') and ldum.bu.re.ral. It grows on the trunks of trees, and has a furry root similar to a monkey's tail, slightly sweet taste, cool power and the shape of the leaf is similar to a tongue of flame.]"

So line 1083 calls *rgyal.po.re.ral* ("king fern") and *ldum.bu.re.ral* two of the five names of *re.ral* (given binomials *Adiantum pedatum* and *Adiantum aspidium*), while line 1084 treats *ldum.bu.re.ral* as an entry of its own, with different binomials. The text does not reconcile the two, and the Codex does not. The only action printed next to the ferns is in the next entry (line 1085), for the Tibetan tamarisk: "Adiantum and Myricaria neutralize meat poison and compounded poison [and cure bile fevers ...]". That sentence names *Adiantum* and not *Drynaria*, and the Codex does not carry it over.

*The other two ferns.* Line 1068 is the "minister fern" entry (*brag.skya.ha.wo*: "Corallodiscus Kingianus (Craib) Burtt: Dryopteris fragrans: Woodsia glabella"), which says that it "neutralizes poisons, halts dysentery with fever", and line 1069 is the "queen fern" entry (*brag.spos*: "Lepisorus waltonii ching: Lepisorus scolopendrium"), which says that "Lepisorus heals wounds, dries pus, sustains bone resin". Those two entries carry actions and *ldum.bu.re.ral* does not.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011.* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. A search for "ldum bu", "Dumbu" and "Dhumbu" found no line for this name (the only hits are the term "dumbu karpo", lines 2384 and 4856, which is a different name). The glossary does set a fern against a nearby Tibetan name: in the "R" block the phonetic names are "Rekon Rerel Risho Ru-ta" (line 5668), the transliterations include "re skon re ral ri sho" (line 5673), and the scientific run (line 5676) reads "Polygonatum cirhifolium Stellera chamaejasme Corydalis nepalense Polystichum squarros Ligularia cymbulifera Saussurea lappa", against an English-name run that includes "Fern". Counting in order, "Rerel" (*re ral*) stands fourth against "Polystichum squarros[um]", which is Clark's second binomial for *ldum.bu.re.ral*. In the running text "Rerel" is in the poison classes: "Rerel and Vombu cure meat poisoning and compounded poisoning" (line 3456) and, in chapter 21, "Rerel, Vombu, Pang gyen ..." in the group that cures poisoning (line 3605). Clark's entry for *re.ral* is the one that carries the name *ldum.bu.re.ral* among its names, and the Codex does not decide whether the glossary's *Polystichum* pairing, or those two poison passages, belong to *ldum.bu.re.ral*; they belong at most to the name *re.ral*. The sibling entry I_1225 treats that name. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Drynaria propinqua*, *ldum.bu.re.ral* and *Polystichum squarrosum* found two entries that bear on this plant. I_1225 (*Adiantum pedatum*) quotes lines 1083 and 1084 and the glossary "R" block in its prose and discusses the *re.ral* name; it is not an entry on this plant. I_803 (Gu Sui Bu, *Drynaria fortunei*, spine CPU-00721, tags SBJ and FMT) says that the only *Drynaria* in the Clark translation is *Drynaria propinqua* glossed under the fern *ldum.bu.re.ral*, "a different species", and marks its own FMT tag unconfirmed; that FMT tag is therefore the same Clark line as this entry's, at genus level, and the two spine rows (CPU-00721 and this one) share one attestation. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Drynaria* is a genus of epiphytic and rock-growing ferns (family Polypodiaceae) whose creeping rhizomes are covered in scales. Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained, because its Latin name is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Clark's entry prints no action for this drug: it gives only identifications and the rank "king fern" among three ferns (line 1084), and the Men-Tsee-Khang text has no line for the name. There is no effect to report, and the Codex invents none. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no part used, no quantity, no frequency and no duration. The commentary in the neighbouring *re.ral* entry mentions a "furry root", and that is a description of another drug's root and not a part taken. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Clark's entry places the drug in a ranking of three ferns ("king", "queen", "minister") and in a list of herbs; that is a naming and ordering frame inside the materia medica, and it carries no therapeutic action and no cultivation status. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this name places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the lines cited above were read in full and none mentions one. The "king fern" rank is a rank of names and is not a grade of cultivation use.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a fern named in Clark with a rank and two Latin names and no action, and there is no preparation and no dose. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. The identity is uncertain, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
