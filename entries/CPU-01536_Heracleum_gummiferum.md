# Heracleum gummiferum — Mekon aphrodes, the frothy poppy (Dioscorides 4-67; "Papaver spumeum, called heracleum by some")

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk names this plant. The entry rests on one chapter of one text, and the chapter is short. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed under the chapter heading, and not Dioscorides' own identification. The Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED line gives three *Heracleum* species with one English name, and the entry takes the first. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-67. MEKON APHRODES* at line 8612, SUGGESTED line at 8613, text at line 8614. The chapter stands in Book Four, "Other Herbs & Roots", after 4-66 *MEKON KERATITES* and before 4-68 *UPEKOON*, both different chapters. The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 647.

The whole chapter reads: "Papaver spumeum (called heracleum by some) has a stalk twenty centimetres long, and the leaves especially small, similar to radicula [radish], the seed white between them, and the entire small herb is white and frothy. The root is thin for the most part. The seed of this is gathered in the summer when it is fully-grown, and when dried falls away. A vinegar cruet of the seed (taken with honey and water) purges by vomiting, and such a purging is effectively good for epilepsy."

So the chapter gives a description (a stalk of twenty centimetres, very small leaves like radish, white seed, the whole herb "white and frothy", a thin root), a note on gathering (the seed is gathered in summer when fully grown, and falls away when dry) and one use (the seed, taken in honey and water, purges by vomiting, and that purging is said to be good for epilepsy). The only other name given is the one in the first line: "Papaver spumeum", "called heracleum by some". The bracket "[radish]" is the translators'.

*Overlap.* The existing entry I_981 (*Heracleum panaces*) rests on a different chapter, 3-55 *PANAKES HERAKLEION* (heading at line 7021), and shares only the name *heracleum*. It is not merged with this one. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records an emetic seed drink and a claim for epilepsy. The Codex gives no protocol. The identity is a translators' suggestion among three species, and inducing vomiting as a treatment for epilepsy is not something to attempt.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives one preparation: "A vinegar cruet of the seed (taken with honey and water)". That is the only quantity printed, and it is expressed in a household measure whose size the text read does not define. No frequency or duration is printed. The text also gives a gathering rule: the seed is "gathered in the summer when it is fully-grown". [USER: verify]

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] It is a general, well-established fact that epilepsy is a medical condition that requires diagnosis and treatment, and that induced vomiting is not a treatment for it.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** chapter 4-67 prints no hot, cold, moist or dry quality and no taste. The only quality printed is the colour of the herb ("white and frothy").

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a description for recognition (a short stalk, small radish-like leaves, white seed, a whole herb "white and frothy"), a harvest rule (seed gathered in summer, when fully grown, before it falls) and a measure (a vinegar cruet). It is not a cultivation status. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The transferable point is the harvest rule: take the seed when it is fully grown and before it falls. The text offers nothing more, and the abstentive answer is the right one.

### Contraindications

Chapter 4-67 prints no warning, but the only use it records is an emetic. The identification is only the translators' suggestion among three species, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Epilepsy needs medical care, and vomiting should not be induced as a treatment. The text gives no dose beyond a household measure. [USER: verify]
