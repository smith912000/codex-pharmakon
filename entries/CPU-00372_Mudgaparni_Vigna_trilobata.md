# Mudgaparni — मुद्गपर्णी (Mudgaparṇī); Wild Gram; Pillipesara; Mudga-parni

## *Vigna trilobata* (L.) Verdc. | Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)

**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā) — cooling pulse; Vidārigandhādi / Śālaparṇyādi gaṇa; *NOT an Aṣṭavarga herb*

**Training Phase:** Cooling / Recovery · Anti-inflammatory maintenance · Post-exertion settling

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (Ayurveda).** Mudgaparni — literally "the plant with mudga (green-gram) leaves" — is a trailing wild pulse counted among the classical cooling, light legumes. Suśruta and the later nighaṇṭus group it (with its partner Māṣaparṇī) in the **Vidārigandhādi** and **Śālaparṇyādi** style clusters of sweet, cooling, balya herbs used against Pitta and Vāta-Pitta disorders. Its therapeutic reading is that of a *dāhapraśamana* (heat-quelling), *jvaraghna* (fever-allaying) and mildly *balya* (strengthening) agent — a "cool pulse" whose leaves and whole plant are used, distinct from the great tonic rasāyanas.

Classical indications include *dāha* (burning sensations), *jvara* (fever), *raktapitta* (bleeding/heat disorders), *kṣaya* (as a gentle, non-heavy nutritive), and general Pitta aggravation. Unlike the heavy Aṣṭavarga rasāyanas, Mudgaparni is *laghu* (light) and mildly *rūkṣa* (dry) — it cools and settles without the congesting heaviness of the milk-tonics, which is precisely its niche. Suśruta's surgical-medical tradition valued such herbs in convalescence and in the febrile, inflamed states following injury.

**Charaka and later usage.** In the broader classical corpus Mudgaparni appears among *balya* and *pittaśāmaka* dravyas and in Vātaraktahara (gout/inflammatory) contexts. Regionally, *Vigna trilobata* ("pillipesara," "wild gram") is a fodder and green-manure legume across the Indian subcontinent, and its sprouts and pulse are eaten — an ethnobotanical continuity with the Ayurvedic reading of it as a nourishing yet cooling food-medicine.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Leaf that Cools the Blood:** Where the Aṣṭavarga herbs are lunar reservoirs of *ojas*, Mudgaparni belongs to a humbler and more everyday register: the cooling *anna* (food-as-medicine) that keeps the blood clean and the heat banked. Its doctrinal signature is *laghu* — lightness. In Ayurvedic thought lightness is close to *sattva*: a light, cooling, astringent pulse clears rather than clogs, settles inflammation without smothering the digestive fire beneath a blanket of unctuousness. In the Kenetica frame Mudgaparni is a *maintenance* herb, not a heroic one — the cooling green you take through a hot, inflamed, overworked stretch to keep the system from tipping into *dāha*, rather than the reservoir you draw on once already emptied. It embodies the principle that the most sustainable medicine is often the lightest.

### Active Components

Compound-level data specific to authenticated *Vigna trilobata* is limited but real. Documented and genus-consistent constituents include: **phenolic acids and flavonoids** (the basis of measured antioxidant activity), **protease inhibitors** — a Bowman–Birk-type inhibitor with sequence homology reported for *V. trilobata* — plant proteins and lysine-rich legume storage proteins, saponins, and standard pulse carbohydrates/fibre. Antioxidant capacity of the sprouted seed has been directly measured (see Scientific Correlation). ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Mudgaparni is a **cooling / recovery-phase** dravya:

- **Anti-inflammatory maintenance:** for hot, inflamed, Pitta-dominant patterns — during rather than after depletion, keeping burning and reactivity in check.
- **Post-exertion settling:** a light, cooling nutritive after heat-generating work, where a heavy tonic would congest.
- **Antioxidant support (food-grade):** the sprouted pulse itself is a functional food with measured radical-scavenging capacity — a low-drama, dietary-tier tool rather than a concentrated extract.

Its lightness makes it combinable and low-risk; it is the cooling background note, not the lead.

### Scientific Correlation

**Evidence grade: preclinical / food-science — real antioxidant and biochemical data exist for the plant, but no retrieved clinical trials for classical indications.** The measured pharmacology is at the level of antioxidant capacity and molecular characterisation, not human outcomes.

According to PubMed:

- Rama Rao, Prakash, Rajkumar, Raju, Srilatha & Reddy (2018), *Tropical Animal Health and Production* — directly quantified **wild gram (*Vigna trilobata*) sprouts** as the highest-phenolic (102 mg GAE/g) and highest anti-radical (61%) of the pulses tested, and dietary supplementation reduced lipid peroxidation and raised glutathione peroxidase, glutathione reductase and superoxide dismutase activity in vivo (broiler model). This is concrete antioxidant evidence, in an animal-production model. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11250-018-1543-5)
- Katoch, Singh, Thakur, Dutt, Yadav & Shukle (2014), *Gene* — characterised a Bowman–Birk-type protease inhibitor sharing **99% homology with the *Vigna trilobata* BBI**, evidencing the plant's protease-inhibitor / plant-defence protein complement (potential relevance to anti-inflammatory and digestive-protective actions, though not clinically demonstrated). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gene.2014.05.055)

The antioxidant signal is genuine and quantified; classical *dāhapraśamana*/*jvaraghna* claims remain attributed to the Ayurvedic tradition rather than to trial evidence.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa:** Madhura (sweet), Kaṣāya (astringent)
- **Guṇa:** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry)
- **Vīrya:** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka:** Madhura (sweet)
- **Prabhāva:** Dāhapraśamana beyond taste alone — a specific heat-settling action on rakta/Pitta
- **Doṣa action:** Pitta–Kapha śāmaka; mildly Vāta-vardhaka (due to laghu-rūkṣa) if overused dry
- **Karma:** Balya, Dāhapraśamana, Jvaraghna, Raktapittahara, Śīta/Grāhi (mild), Vātaraktahara
- **Gaṇa / group:** Vidārigandhādi / Śālaparṇyādi-associated cooling balya group (Suśruta); *not Aṣṭavarga*
- **Dhātu:** rasa, rakta (cooling and cleansing)
- **Srotas:** rasavaha, raktavaha

### Contraindications

- **High Vāta / very dry constitutions:** its laghu-rūkṣa nature can aggravate Vāta if used dry and in excess; pair with unctuous anupāna.
- **Weak digestion for the raw pulse:** legumes can produce *āma*/flatulence in low-Agni states; prefer sprouted or cooked forms.
- **Botanical identity:** *Vigna* species are readily confused (V. mungo, V. radiata, V. umbellata substitutions are common in trade and even in genetics literature); confirm species before therapeutic use.
- Not a treatment for febrile or inflammatory disease in place of medical care. `[USER: verify with a qualified practitioner.]`
