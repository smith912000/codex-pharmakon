# Shirisha — Śirīṣa, Bhaṇḍī, Kapītana, Lebbeck, Siris tree

## *Albizia lebbeck* (L.) Benth. | Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae; subfamily Mimosoideae)

**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā), CS (Caraka Saṃhitā), AH (Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya), Nighaṇṭus (Bhāvaprakāśa, Dhanvantari)
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa — Tikta (bitter), Kaṣāya (astringent), Madhura (sweet, secondary) · Vīrya — Anuṣṇaśīta / mildly cooling · Vipāka — Kaṭu (pungent) · Guṇa — Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry) · Tridoṣaghna with a marked action on Kapha–Pitta; the classical **Viṣaghna** (antitoxic) and **Śvāsahara / Kāsahara** (anti-dyspnoeic, antitussive) agent.
**Training Phase:** Clearing / Purification (Śodhana-adjacent) · Respiratory conditioning · Resilience & anti-reactivity (allergic-diathesis work)

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** Śirīṣa is one of the most emphatically praised anti-toxic drugs in the surgical canon. In the Kalpasthāna (the toxicology section), Suśruta ranks Śirīṣa among the foremost **Viṣaghna** dravyas — plants that counter poisons, envenomations, and the systemic derangement that follows bites and stings. The seeds, bark, flowers, and leaves are each deployed: seed paste as a topical and nasal application (nasya) in envenomation and in obstinate skin and sinus disease, bark decoction as an internal astringent and detoxicant. Suśruta groups Śirīṣa within the broad astringent-and-antitoxic cluster used across *kuṣṭha* (skin disorders), *viṣa* (toxicology), and *śvāsa-kāsa* (respiratory) chapters.

**Caraka Saṃhitā (CS):** Caraka lists Śirīṣa among **Viṣaghna** and **Kaṇḍūghna** (anti-pruritic) drugs. Its bitter-astringent profile places it in formulations for *kuṣṭha* and for allergic, itching, weeping skin conditions — the phenotype that later vaidyas would map onto urticaria and contact reactions.

**Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya & the Nighaṇṭus (AH, Bhāvaprakāśa, Dhanvantari Nighaṇṭu):** The synonyms tell the doctrinal story. *Bhaṇḍī* and *Kapītana* denote the tree's stature and utility; *Viṣaghna* is used almost as an epithet. Bhāvaprakāśa fixes Śirīṣa as the household antidote — the tree one plants near the home so that bark, flower, and seed are always at hand against poison, cough, and skin eruption. The white-, yellow-, and dark-flowered varieties (śveta, pīta, kṛṣṇa Śirīṣa) are distinguished, the white generally regarded as the most potent Viṣaghna.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Threshold Guardian:** Śirīṣa occupies a specific doctrinal niche: it is the plant of the *boundary*. Toxin, allergen, and venom are, in Āyurvedic terms, all species of *āgantu* — that which comes from outside and violates the organism's integrity. Śirīṣa is the drug that stands at the membrane between inner and outer, neutralising what crosses without permission. Its dense, fast-growing canopy — planted at village edges and along roads throughout the subcontinent — makes it a literal threshold tree, and the doctrine mirrors the ecology: a guardian at the perimeter. In Kenetica this reads cleanly onto the **anti-reactivity** work of the Clearing phase — the training of a nervous and immune system that no longer over-responds to every crossing signal. Śirīṣa is not a sedative that dulls the alarm; it is the drug that raises the threshold at which the alarm fires. The mild cooling (*anuṣṇaśīta*) virya is doctrinally important: heat is reactivity, and Śirīṣa's near-neutral temperature lets it quiet Pitta-driven inflammation without the depleting coldness of a frank refrigerant.

### Active Components

According to phytochemical literature, *Albizia lebbeck* bark, seed, and leaf carry: **saponins** (echinocystic-acid and oleanolic-acid glycosides, the albizia-saponins), **flavonoids** and flavonol glycosides, **condensed and hydrolysable tannins**, **triterpenoid glycosides**, sterols (β-sitosterol), and the characteristic macrocyclic **budmunchiamine alkaloids** of the seed. The bark is notably rich in tannins and antioxidant phenolics. These classes plausibly underlie the astringent (tannin), mast-cell-stabilising/anti-anaphylactic (saponin-flavonoid), and antioxidant actions attributed to the drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Śirīṣa is Kenetica's **anti-reactive respiratory and immune conditioner**. It is placed where the training goal is to reduce the amplitude of allergic, inflammatory, and toxic over-response — the reactive breath, the itching skin, the system that treats every crossing as an assault. Its triple action — Viṣaghna (detoxicant), Śvāsahara (breath-easing), Kaṇḍūghna (anti-itch) — makes it the connective tissue between the Clearing phase and the respiratory-conditioning track. It pairs naturally with drying, Kapha-clearing agents in damp respiratory pictures and with cooling antipruritics in Pitta-heat skin pictures.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the pharmacological literature on *Albizia lebbeck* is real but modest, dominated by animal and in-vitro work rather than human trials — the evidence grade for the classical anti-allergic/anti-toxin claims is therefore **preliminary**.

- Saha & Ahmed (2009), *Pakistan Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences*, demonstrated dose-dependent **anti-inflammatory and analgesic** activity of an *A. lebbeck* bark extract in rodents: 400 mg/kg inhibited carrageenan-induced paw oedema by ~37% and acetic-acid writhing by ~52%, with significant tail-flick analgesia. PMID 19168425.
- Tasneem et al. (2021), *Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology*, reported strong **antioxidant** activity of an *A. lebbeck* bark extract (~85% DPPH scavenging) and demonstrated measurable effects on human skin parameters (melanin, erythema, moisture, elasticity) from a 3% topical emulgel over 8 weeks. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.14244)

These findings corroborate the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory *direction* of the traditional Viṣaghna/Kaṇḍūghna use. The specific anti-asthmatic and anti-anaphylactic reputation rests largely on older animal studies not retrieved in this run and should be presented as **traditionally attributed, not clinically established**. `[USER: verify against a dedicated anti-asthmatic RCT if making a respiratory claim]`

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Tikta, Kaṣāya, with secondary Madhura
- **Vīrya (potency):** Anuṣṇaśīta — mildly cooling / near-neutral
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu, Rūkṣa
- **Doṣa action:** Pacifies Kapha and Pitta; broadly tridoṣaghna in the toxicological context
- **Karma (actions):** Viṣaghna (antitoxic), Śvāsahara & Kāsahara (anti-dyspnoeic, antitussive), Kaṇḍūghna (antipruritic), Kuṣṭhaghna (anti-dermatosis), Grāhī/Saṅgrāhī (astringent-binding via tannins)
- **Srotas (channels):** Prāṇavaha (respiratory), Rasavaha, Raktavaha (blood/skin), and the toxicological handling of āgantu-viṣa
- **Prabhāva (specific effect):** Its signature is the raising of the reactive threshold — the anti-anaphylactic prabhāva that distinguishes it from ordinary astringents

### Contraindications

- Traditionally drying (Rūkṣa, Laghu) — use with caution in frank Vāta depletion, dryness, and debility without suitable anupāna.
- Human safety data are limited; **pregnancy, lactation, and paediatric use are not established** — avoid without practitioner supervision. `[USER: verify]`
- Genuine envenomation and anaphylaxis are medical emergencies: Śirīṣa's classical antitoxic reputation does not substitute for emergency care or antivenom.
- Possible additive effects with anti-diabetic, anti-inflammatory, or immunomodulatory drugs — monitor. `[USER: verify]`
