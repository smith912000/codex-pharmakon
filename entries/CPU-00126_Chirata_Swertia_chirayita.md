### Origin & Tradition

Chirata is an annual gentian-family herb of the temperate Himalaya, and one of the great **bitters** of the Indian and Himalayan materia medica — so intensely bitter that it is a byword for bitterness. The whole dried plant is the drug, known in Ayurveda as **Kiratatikta** ("the bitter of the Kirata mountain people"), in Unani as *chirayata*, and used across Tibetan and Himalayan folk medicine. It belongs to the bitter-tonic gentian family (with gentian itself, this volume, and the Tibetan "bitter" herbs), and shares their cooling, digestive, hepatic and febrifuge identity in a particularly concentrated form.

In Ayurveda and Unani Chirata is a premier **bitter febrifuge and bitter tonic**: used for **fevers** (including intermittent/malarial-type fevers — a classic anti-periodic), for **liver complaints and jaundice** (the bitter cholagogue/hepatic action), for **inflammatory and "hot blood" skin conditions**, for diabetes and metabolic ("prameha") conditions, for digestive weakness and worms, and as a general blood-purifying, heat-clearing bitter tonic. Its action across all these is the classic bitter one — stimulating digestion and bile, cooling heat, clearing damp-heat from liver and blood, and lowering fever.

The intense bitterness and activity come from **secoiridoid glycosides** (amarogentin — among the most bitter substances known — and swertiamarin) and **xanthones**, the characteristic gentian-family constituents, well studied for hepatoprotective, antimalarial, anti-inflammatory and hypoglycaemic activity.
