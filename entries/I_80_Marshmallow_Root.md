### Botanical Identity
Perennial 60–120cm; soft, velvety leaves covered in star-shaped (stellate) hairs; pale pink five-petalled flowers; native to marshes and damp meadows of Europe, western Asia, and North Africa. The root is the primary drug material — harvested in autumn, peeled, dried; the mucilage content is highest in the root (up to 35% polysaccharide by dry weight). The leaf and flower are secondary sources. The plant's entire softness — the velvety leaves, the emollient root — reflects the pharmaceutical identity: this is the great soothing plant of the Western and Islamic traditions. The original marshmallow confection (French *pâte de guimauve*) was made by whipping *Althaea* root extract with sugar — the mucilage created the soft, foam-like texture. The modern confection replaced root extract with gelatine; the name remained.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 De Materia Medica (DMM)

Dioscorides (*Book III, Chapter 146*):
> *"Althaia: the root boiled in wine or water softens and relaxes all swellings and irritations. Applied to wounds it draws out thorns and splinters. Taken with honey wine it soothes hoarseness and dry cough. The root boiled in oil, applied, relieves inflammation of sinews and nerves."*

Pliny further specifies: *"A spoonful of the root juice drunk daily in water cures all diseases."* — a hyperbole that nonetheless encodes the broad demulcent and anti-inflammatory utility.

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā on *khatmī*:
> *"The root is warm and moist. Resolves all swellings and hardenings. The decoction of the root and seed is appropriate for dryness and roughness of the throat, trachea, and lungs. It is the best medicine for cough, dry bronchitis, and urinary pain. Applied to burns and inflammations of the skin it quickly relieves. When mixed with oil of roses, it draws out splinters."*

Ibn Sīnā's moisture categorisation accurately reflects the mucilage mechanism — the root's demulcent action coats inflamed mucous membranes exactly as classical "moistening" therapy predicted.

### 2.3 Ebers Papyrus (EP)

Marsh plants with mucilaginous properties in Egyptian preparations for skin conditions and "cooling the throat." The Egyptian term for cooling, soothing preparations (*qebḥ*) aligns with the demulcent category.

### 2.4 Assyrian Medical Tablets (AT)

Mucilaginous plant preparations for throat pain, urinary difficulty, and "burning of the gut" in Babylonian texts — consistent with the demulcent application across all traditions.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Demulcent / Mucosal Protection (Primary)

**Mucilage polysaccharides**: On contact with mucous membranes (throat, oesophagus, gastric mucosa, urinary epithelium), *Althaea* polysaccharides form a viscous protective film:
- Physically coats irritated epithelium → reduces friction and direct irritant contact
- Stimulates mucin secretion → augments endogenous mucosal defence
- The film creates a "second mucosa" — a temporary protective layer over inflamed tissue

This mechanism is not pharmacological in the traditional sense — it is purely physical chemistry. The polysaccharide film adheres to the mucosa via electrostatic and hydrogen-bonding interactions. Clinical effect begins within minutes and lasts 1–3 hours per dose.

### 5.2 Anti-Inflammatory (Secondary)

**Pectin + phenolic acids + flavonoids**: Multiple anti-inflammatory mechanisms:
- Pectin stimulates TLR-4 on macrophages → paradoxically reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine cascade (immunomodulatory, not immunostimulatory)
- Caffeic acid derivatives inhibit COX-2 and NF-κB
- Quercetin + luteolin: 5-LOX inhibition → reduced leukotriene-driven inflammation

### 5.3 Antitussive / Bronchial

**Mucilage + scopoletin**: The viscous polysaccharide layer on the pharyngeal and tracheal mucosa reduces the irritant stimulus that triggers the cough reflex → peripheral antitussive mechanism (distinct from codeine/dextromethorphan central mechanisms). Scopoletin adds smooth muscle relaxation (antispasmodic) → reduces bronchospasm component of cough.

### 5.4 Urinary Tract Soothing

**Polysaccharides + asparagine**: Mucilage reaches the bladder epithelium intact (undigested in small intestine) → coats urothelium → reduces dysuria and urgency in lower UTI and interstitial cystitis. Asparagine is a mild diuretic — increases urine flow → dilutes irritants.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Dry cough / throat irritation**: Büechi (2005): *Althaea officinalis* syrup significantly reduced dry cough frequency and severity vs placebo in 822 patients over 3 weeks. The largest marshmallow RCT.

**Pharyngeal irritation**: Karlovits (2019): *Althaea* root spray significantly reduced throat pain and irritation scores vs placebo — fast onset (within minutes) consistent with the physical film mechanism.

**Irritable bowel / gastritis**: Kia (2021): *Althaea* root extract significantly improved GI symptoms (heartburn, regurgitation, epigastric pain) vs placebo in patients with gastro-oesophageal reflux — consistent with mucosal protective mechanism.

**Wound healing**: German Commission E approved for minor skin inflammation and irritation — the topical emollient application confirmed in multiple observational studies.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The confection that became a medicine's namesake**: The etymology of marshmallow travels from Greek *althainein* (to heal) → plant name *Althaea* → Arabic *khatmī* → French *guimauve* → French confectionery. Apothecaries in 18th-century France whipped *Althaea* root mucilage with sugar and egg whites to create *pâte de guimauve* — originally a cough lozenge and throat medicine. The soft, pillowy texture was a side effect of the pharmaceutical mucilage. When the confection was industrialised in the 19th century, the actual plant was replaced by gelatine — but the name *marshmallow* remained. Every child's campfire marshmallow carries the name of a 2,500-year-old throat remedy without knowing it. The medicine became a sweet; the sweet remembered the name but forgot the origin.

**The elemental healer**: *Althaea* was the most widely recommended demulcent in both European and Islamic traditions not because of complex pharmacology but because of an elegant simple mechanism — a physical film that protects. Where other medicinal plants compete through alkaloids, terpenoids, and multi-target receptor pharmacology, marshmallow works by coating. The Galenic concept of *epipastic* (a substance that coats the body surface) describes exactly what the mucilage does at the cellular level. Classical medical theory that had no concept of polysaccharide chemistry still correctly identified the mechanism as "softening," "moistening," and "coating" — functional descriptions that map precisely to the physical chemistry discovered two millennia later.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Cold infusion (root, 8h) | 2–4g root in 250ml cold water overnight | Maximum mucilage extraction — heat degrades polysaccharides |
| Decoction (leaf/flower) | 3–5g per cup, 10-min steep | Throat; cough; mild demulcent |
| Syrup (cold extract + honey) | 15–30ml 3–4× daily | Dry cough; throat; children's preparation |
| Tincture (1:5, 25% ethanol) | 5–10ml 3× daily | Lower potency demulcent — ethanol reduces mucilage content |
| Topical poultice (root powder + water) | Applied to affected area | Wound; burn; skin inflammation |
| Capsule (root powder) | 400–800mg 3× daily | GI inflammation; gastritis; IBS |

**Cold infusion note**: Unlike most herbs, marshmallow root is best extracted in cold or room-temperature water. Heat (above 60°C) degrades the mucilage polysaccharides. The traditional overnight cold infusion preserves maximum demulcent activity. Modern preparations often use cold extraction specifically for this reason.

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Sweet, bland; cool. Channels: Lung (肺), Stomach (胃), Bladder (膀胱). Moistens the Lung, clears heat from the throat, promotes urination, soothes the Stomach.

**Ayurvedic**: Sweet, bland; cooling. Reduces Pitta ↓↓, reduces Vata ↓. *Snigdha* (unctuous/emollient). *Balya* (strengthening), *Vṛṣya* (tonic). Primary *Bṛṃhaṇa* (nourishing) herb for dry tissues and inflamed membranes.

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Diabetes / blood sugar**: High polysaccharide content may affect post-prandial glycaemia at very high doses — monitor blood glucose in insulin-dependent diabetes
- **Oral medications**: Mucilage may slow drug absorption — take marshmallow preparations 1–2 hours apart from pharmaceutical medications
- **Pregnancy**: Generally regarded as safe at culinary/moderate doses; avoid very high therapeutic doses (insufficient safety data)
- **Generally one of the safest herbs in the Codex**: no significant toxicity, no known serious interactions, broad traditional use in infants and elderly
