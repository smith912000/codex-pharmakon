# Satureia thymbra — "Satureia thymbra" (translator's suggestion for Dioscorides 3-35 *Tragoriganos*, 5-55 *Oinos tragoriganites* and 5-60 *Oinos thumbrites*)

## *Satureja thymbra L. (the spelling Satureia is the translator's), a small aromatic shrub of the eastern Mediterranean known as Cretan or thyme-leaved savory; the identification is the translator's, printed first in a list of four suggestions for the shrub Dioscorides calls tragoriganos, so it is one candidate and not a settled name; confidence low to medium* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Dioscorides translation only, as a suggested identification.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Satureia thymbra [DMM]". The name is the translator's (Osbaldeston, 2000; `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`), printed in the "SUGGESTED" line under each chapter heading. The spine already has a Thyme entry (I_233, CPU-00414); the first pass called this row a duplicate of it and the audit disagreed, since *Satureja thymbra* is a distinct species and the Dioscorides chapters here are about *tragoriganos*, not about thyme. This entry keeps them apart. [USER: verify whether to merge.]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Three chapters are given as loci.

*3-35, Tragoriganos* (heading line 6828; suggestion, line 6829: "Satureia thymbra, Thymus tragoriganum, Thymus graveolens, Micromeria thymbra — Savory of Crete, Candian Savory"). The text (line 6831): "Tragoriganum is a little shrub similar to origanum or wild serpyllum [3-46] in its leaves and small branches. Some is found that is more prosperous and broaderleaved, gluey enough, depending on the location. Another (which is also called prasium) has small shoots and thin leaves. The best is the Cilician and those in Co, Chios, Smyrna, and Crete. All are warming, urinary, and good for the intestines (in a decoction taken as a drink) for they drive down depression. Taken in a drink with vinegar they are effective for the spleen, and are given as an antidote with wine for those who have taken a drink of ixia [3-103]. They expel the menstrual flow, and are given as linctuses [syrups] with honey for coughs and pneumonia. A liquid medicine of it is mild; as a result it is given to the squeamish, for gastric [disorders], unsavoury belchers, and those who have seasickness, nausea and heartburn. It dissolves oedema applied with polenta."

*5-55, Oinos tragoriganites* (heading line 10066; same suggestion line). "Tragoriganites. Let down four teaspoonfuls of tragoriganum (bound in a linen cloth) into four pints of must [grape pulp] for three months and then jar it. It is good for griping, convulsions, hernias, pains of the side, the movement of winds, and difficulty to digest." (line 10069)

*5-60, Oinos thumbrites* (heading line 10091; same suggestion line). "Thymbrites is made in the same way and is good for whatever thymites is." (line 10095) The preceding chapter, thyme wine, is "good for digestive difficulties, lack of appetite, dysentery, disorders of the nerves, and hypochondria [nervous gastric disorder]; for winter shiverings, and for poison from venomous creatures" (line 10090).

Dioscorides' own chapter on *thymbra* (3-45, heading line 6914, the same row as spine CPU-02326) carries a different suggestion ("Sisymbrium ... Serpyllum ... Thymus serpyllum") and is not folded in here. A search of the other texts on disk for *Satureia thymbra* and *Satureja thymbra* found only the Dioscorides lines and the translation's index (line 17418, "Satureia thymbra 403, 771, 772"). No other tradition's text names it.

### Active Components

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The shrub (3-35): urinary, good for the intestines, the spleen, as an antidote after *ixia*, to expel the menstrual flow, for coughs and pneumonia, and for gastric disorders, belching, seasickness, nausea and heartburn; a poultice with polenta dissolves oedema. The wine (5-55): griping, convulsions, hernias, pains of the side, winds, and difficult digestion.
- **What the text does not say.** It does not say which species the shrub is.

The Codex gives no protocol. Pneumonia and convulsions are medical emergencies, and the menstrual use is not a recommendation.

### Preparation & Dosing

Decoction taken as a drink; drink with vinegar; linctus with honey; poultice with polenta (3-35, line 6831). For the wine, "four teaspoonfuls" of the herb in "four pints" of must for three months (5-55, line 10069). These are quantities for making a batch, not doses. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] **Do not use any quantity from this entry.**

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Savories and thymes are culinary herbs, and thymol and carvacrol are antiseptic phenols. Concentrated essential oils are irritant. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** Chapter 3-35 prints "All are warming, urinary, and good for the intestines" (line 6831) and calls a liquid medicine of it "mild". The wine chapters print no quality of their own beyond their uses. No other classification is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is manufactured here. What the chapter offers a practitioner is a discipline of attention: it separates broad-leaved from thin-leaved plants "depending on the location" and says "The best is the Cilician and those in Co, Chios, Smyrna, and Crete" (line 6831), which is provenance and sensory judgement. The frame is that of a therapeutic herb with a graded source. No cultivation status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A warming herb and its wine, chosen by origin. A cultivator takes from the chapter only the habit of asking where a material was grown and which kind it is.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy.** The text says the herb expels the menstrual flow; avoid in pregnancy.
- **Alcohol.** The wine is alcoholic; not suitable in pregnancy, for children, or for people with liver disease.
- **Essential oil.** Concentrated oil is an irritant; do not take it internally on the strength of this entry.
- **Pneumonia, convulsions, hernia.** These need medical care.
- **Identity.** The species is the translator's first suggestion of four. [USER: verify]
