# Flat azurite — 扁青 *Bian Qing* (Shennong bencao jing); Yang 1998 prints "Bian Qing (Azuritum)"
## *The blue copper carbonate ore azurite, Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2, in the standard modern identification of 扁青 as flaky or massive azurite (蓝铜矿); medium confidence, because the classic's text gives no description of the stone and chrysocolla has also been proposed* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on two witnesses of that classic: Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`), and, as a second witness attributed to him, Bob Flaws's translation of Yang Shou-zhong (Yang 1998, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`). The identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern view, and the sources read do not confirm it. (The spine already carries other azurite-type rows from the same classic: Kong Qing, entry II_118, and the layered and white varieties; this is a separate monograph.)

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the upper (上) classic, reads: 味甘，平 ("sweet, balanced"). It governs 目痛 ("eye pain"), "brightens the eyes" (明目), 折跌 ("fractures from falls"), 痈肿 ("welling abscesses and swellings"), 金创不疗 ("incised wounds that do not heal"), "breaks accumulations and gatherings" (破积聚), then 解毒瓦斯 (as printed; the Yulan quotation, per Sun's own bracket, reads 辟毒, "ward off poison"), and 利精神 ("makes the essence-spirit flow freely"). The life-nourishing clause follows: 久服，轻身、不老 ("taken long, it lightens the body and one does not age"). The habitat is 生山谷 ("grows in mountains and valleys"). (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 扁青 *bian qing*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 243.) The printed 瓦斯 ("gas") looks like a conversion artefact in the transcription of 毒气 ("toxic qi"); Yang renders the clause "resolves toxic qi". That reading is ours. [USER: verify]

**Wu Pu (3rd century).** 《吴普》曰 gives: 神农、雷公：小寒，无毒 ("Shennong and Lei Gong: slightly cold, non-toxic"), habitat 生蜀郡 (Shu commandery), and indications 治丈夫内绝，令人有子 ("treats a man's internal exhaustion, makes a person able to father children"). A Yulan quotation in brackets gives another list (治痈脾风痹) and again 久服，轻身. (Sun ed., line 245.)

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 adds the places 生朱崖、武都、朱提 (Zhuya, Wudu, Zhuti) and 采无时 ("gathered at any time"). (Line 247.)

**Sun's note (案).** Sun cites the *Fanzi Jiran* for the origin: 出宏农、豫章 (Hongnong, Yuzhang). Nothing more is said about the stone. (Line 249.)

**Yang 1998 (second witness).** Under "Jades and Stones: Superior Class", PDF page 24 (line 948), Yang prints: "Bian Qing (Azuritum) is a little cold and nontoxic. It mainly treats eye pain, brightens the eyes, and [heals] fracture [caused by] falls as well as welling abscesses and swellings, and refractory incised wounds. It breaks accumulations and gatherings, resolves toxic qi, and disinhibits the essence spirit. Protracted taking may make the body light and prevent senility. It is produced in mountains and valleys." Yang's version gives "a little cold and nontoxic" where Sun's 味 paragraph gives "sweet, balanced"; the Yang wording matches the Wu Pu quotation in Sun, so the two witnesses differ on the nature. Yang's footnote 37, printed on the preceding page (lines 927-931) and attached to Kong Qing, says that Kong Qing "and the next three" (Ceng Qing, Bian Qing, Bai Qing) "are all derived from the same mineral source": the ore "may be found in different shapes", Kong Qing round and hollow, Ceng Qing in layers, Bian Qing "in short cylinders", and Bai Qing "the same substance as Bian Qing but is white." That is the translator's statement and not the Classic's.

**Identity.** The classic never describes the stone. The name 扁青 means "flat blue" or "flaky blue", and Yang's footnote speaks of "short cylinders" for the same name, so the shape reading differs between sources. The ore identification as azurite comes from Yang's "Azuritum" and the standard modern view; Yang uses the one Latin term for four different entries. Chrysocolla (a blue-green copper silicate) has sometimes been proposed. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Azurite is a deep blue basic copper carbonate, Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2, found in the weathered zone of copper ore bodies and often with green malachite, to which it alters over time. It has been used as a blue pigment. Its active component in a medical sense would be copper in a poorly soluble form, together with whatever impurities the ore carries (arsenic, lead and others occur in some copper ores). The sources read give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records a copper ore for eye pain, falls and fractures, abscesses and wounds, and for "breaking accumulations", and says that taken long it lightens the body and prevents ageing. The Codex does not endorse those claims and gives no protocol. Copper compounds taken by mouth are poisonous, and the Classic's "non-toxic" in the upper-class frame does not make them safe.

### Scientific Correlation

Copper is an essential trace element in very small amounts, and copper salts are toxic in larger ones: they irritate the gut and can damage the liver, kidney and blood. Copper minerals are not a source of the nutrient. No property of azurite as a medicine is established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong text proper, Sun ed.).** 味甘，平: sweet; balanced. No toxicity statement is printed in the 味 paragraph.
- **SBJ (Wu Pu layer, Sun ed.; Yang).** "Slightly cold, non-toxic" (小寒，无毒).
- Class: upper (上). No organ affinity is printed. No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: an upper-grade drug (上品).* 扁青 is listed in the upper jade-and-stone group of the preface list (Sun ed. line 45; summary line 251 "上，玉、石，上品一十八种"). The preface (line 43) says upper drugs "mainly nourish life to correspond with heaven, are non-toxic, and taking much or long does not harm people", and that for lightening the body, adding to the qi, and not ageing, "extending the years" one should base oneself on the upper classic (欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经). The monograph's own clause, 久服，轻身、不老 ("taken long, it lightens the body and one does not age"), fits that frame, and the Wu Pu layer repeats 久服，轻身. 轻身 ("lighten the body") has no pharmacological equivalent and is not weight loss. The grade is the Classic's statement as reconstructed by Sun in 1799; no later claim is carried back. (Sun ed. line 243; Yang line 948.)

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade drug in the Chinese scheme, which the Classic says can be taken long. That statement cannot be followed here: this is a copper ore, and long-term ingestion of copper minerals is a poisoning risk. For a cultivator the entry is a record of how the Classic ranked a blue ore, not a practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Copper toxicity.** Ingested copper compounds can cause vomiting, abdominal pain, and liver and kidney injury. The Classic's "non-toxic" and its long-term-use clause are not a safety statement.
- **Impurities.** Mineral drugs sold as azurite may be a different mineral or carry arsenic, lead or other metals. They are unstandardised.
- **Wounds, abscesses, fractures and eye pain.** Need medical care.
- **Pregnancy and fertility.** The Wu Pu layer's claim about fathering children is historical record only.
