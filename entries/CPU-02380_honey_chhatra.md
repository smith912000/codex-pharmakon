# Honey, Chhatra (Chhatram Madhu) — Sushruta Samhita

## *Honey from the umbrella-shaped hives of the Chhatra bees, one of the eight honeys of Sushruta; the translator says "bees of the Chhatra species" and no species is identified; the English prints "Chhatram"; the honeybee identification is ours, of medium confidence* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta Samhita only, among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The English text is Bhishagratna's translation, Sutra ch.45, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`. The honey group opens (line 1076) "Honey is sweet, and leaves an astringent after-taste. It is parchifying, cold, stomachic, cosmetic, tonic, light, softening, palatable, liquefacient (Lekhanan), and fermenting (Sandhanam)." The metrical text then says (lines 1093-1102): "Eight different kinds of honey are commonly used such as, the Pauttikam, Bhramaram, Kshaudram, Makshikam, Chhatram, Arghyam, Auddalakam and Dalam." The translator's footnote (lines 1120-1142) names the bee or hive of each kind.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The translator's footnote (lines 1132-1133) gives the fifth kind: "The kind of honey obtained from the umbrella shaped hives of bees of the Chhatra species is called Chhatram." The text describes it (lines 1118 and 1151-1153): "Honey, known as the Chhatram, is sweet in digestion, heavy, cooling and slimy. It acts as a vermifuge, proves curative in haemoptysis, psoriasis and Meha, and is possessed of a high efficacy." *Meha* is the Sanskrit disease-term left untranslated by the translator. The chapter's general rules apply to every kind. Honey "is not collected from the flowers of any particular species" but from flowers and plants that are incompatible in nature, taste, potency and effect; "for these reasons, and further from the fact of it being prepared by poisonous bees, honey becomes positively injurious after contact with heat or fire, and accordingly the use of hot or boiled honey is forbidden" (lines 1183-1192, with the metrical verses following). Fresh honey is "constructive and aphrodisiac" and old honey "astringent and liquefacient" (lines 1165-1169).

### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms honey is mostly the sugars fructose and glucose with water, and small amounts of enzymes, organic acids and pollen-derived material; the composition varies with the flowers and the bee. These are well-established general facts, not read in the source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Worms, haemoptysis, psoriasis and *meha* (the Sanskrit term is left as printed); the text calls it "possessed of a high efficacy".
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, and no species of bee.

The Codex does not translate the Sanskrit disease terms into modern diagnoses, and gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation for this honey beyond the general rules on honey. The chapter's rules: do not heat or boil honey; honey with rain-water is injurious (all kinds, except Arghya, lines 1203-1206); and for emetic purposes honey may be given with a hot substance, since the aim is that it be thrown up (lines 1208-1213). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Honey is a sweet food made by bees from nectar; the Sushruta text's claim that it becomes injurious when heated is not a modern pharmacological finding, and heating honey is not generally regarded as poisonous. Honey must not be given to infants under twelve months (a general statement, not from the source). No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (ch.45).** Rasa: not printed. *Vipaka*: sweet in digestion. Qualities: heavy, cooling, slimy (lines 1118, 1151-1152). Action: vermifuge; curative in haemoptysis, psoriasis and *meha* (lines 1152-1153).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The Chhatra honey is described in the dietetic chapter by taste, potency and effect on the *doshas*. It is not named in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter and no *ojas* claim is made for it. The honey group's own sentences call honey "tonic" and say that fresh honey is "constructive and aphrodisiac" (lines 1076-1088, 1165); these are group statements and are not carried over to a single kind. The phrase "a high efficacy" is a statement of potency in treating disease and not a cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** A heavy, cooling honey that the text rates highly for disease; it gives a cultivator nothing to act on.

### Contraindications

- **Heated honey.** The text forbids hot or boiled honey and says it can be fatal like poison (lines 1183-1200).
- **Infants.** Honey is not given to infants under twelve months; this is a general statement, not from the source.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The kind of bee is the translator's footnote and the English prints the Sanskrit names only; no species of bee is identified by us beyond *Apis*-type honeybees in general, which is itself ours.
