# Welsh onion seed — 葱实 *congshi*

## *The seed (实) of the Welsh onion, Allium fistulosum L.; the identification is ours, made from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence. The classic's paragraph also speaks of the stalk of the same plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk as a monograph.** The entry rests on one text in one edition, Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The existing onion entry I_174 (*Allium cepa*) has a paragraph on *Allium fistulosum* that cites the Bencao gangmu and a later formula, and does not cite this entry; that is a different source and a different species label, and nothing is merged. I_506 (shallot) mentions the Welsh onion only as a cross-reference.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The name line is 葱实 (line 2903). The classic's whole entry is one paragraph, at line 2905: 味辛，温。主明目，补中不足。其茎可作汤，主伤寒寒热，出汗，中风面目肿。 In English: "Flavour acrid, warm. Governs brightening the eyes (明目) and replenishing the centre where it is insufficient (补中不足). Its stalk can be made into a decoction (其茎可作汤), which governs cold-damage with cold and heat (伤寒寒热), brings out sweat (出汗), and wind-strike with swelling of face and eyes (中风面目肿)." The paragraph has no habitat clause and no 久服 clause. The entry has no Wu Pu, Mingyi or 案 layer of its own in this edition: the next line is the heading 卷二 中经 (line 2907), then a different entry. "Cold-damage" (伤寒) and "wind-strike" (中风) are classical disease names that the Codex keeps in the text's words and does not turn into modern diagnoses.

The classic places the entry in the middle class (中经; heading 卷二 中经 at line 2907). The preface (line 1677) describes the middle class as nourishing the nature, non-toxic or toxic, chosen as fit, for those who want to hold off illness and replenish the lean.

**A comment on the neighbouring entry.** The entry after this one has an unreadable heading (printed "KT" in the transcription; line 2909). Its 案 (line 2915) quotes Tao Hongjing as saying: "葱 and [that plant] are different things, but now they share one entry. The *Bencao jing* has no leek, and they are of the same kind, so..." (葱、[ ]异物，而今共条。《本经》既无韭，以其同类，故也). The Codex records only that Tao's comment shows 葱 sharing an entry with a neighbouring allium in his edition,. It is Tao's comment, and not part of the Shennong text.

### Active Components

None is stated in the text. As a general fact, the Welsh onion, like other alliums, contains organosulfur compounds that give its smell and acrid taste. The Codex retrieved no analytical source for the seed in particular and names no compound. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The classic records the seed for brightening the eyes and replenishing the centre, and the stalk decoction for cold-damage with chills and fever, and for facial swelling after wind-strike. The Codex gives no protocol and does not translate these into modern diagnoses. The Welsh onion is also an ordinary food, which is general knowledge. A food use is not a recommendation of the seed.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic gives one preparation: the stalk "can be made into a decoction" (其茎可作汤), taken for cold-damage and the other complaints. No water, time, quantity or frequency is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle class (中经), the class the preface describes as nourishing the nature and sometimes toxic. Its 味 paragraph carries no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise: it promises bright eyes and a replenished centre in the present tense, not a long-use status (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 葱实 *congshi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2905). The status is therapeutic. The Codex adds nothing from the later formula literature.

**Practitioner's note.** There is no cultivation practice in the text. A cultivator reads a short, plain entry for the seed of a kitchen plant and a stalk decoction for the first stage of a cold. It is a minimal record.

### Contraindications

The classic does not warn. No safety information for pregnancy, children, allergy or other medicines was read, and none is asserted. Fever, chills and facial swelling need medical assessment. [USER: verify]
