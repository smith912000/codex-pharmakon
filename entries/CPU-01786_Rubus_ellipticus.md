# Rubus ellipticus — Gadra, ga bra; ga.dra, "Brown Salamin" (rGyud-bzhi, Men-Tsee-Khang glossary by column position)

## *Rubus ellipticus, the second of four binomials on line 5282 of the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary, which column order sets against "Gadra" (ga bra, English "Raspberry"); Clark 1995 prints ga.dra with Rubus idaeus first, then Debregeasia edulis and Solanum indicum, and no Rubus ellipticus* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi, read in two modern editions.** The editions are Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, both in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. They are two translations of one tradition's text and are not two independent traditions. A search of the Shennong (Yang 1998), Sushruta, Ebers and Assyrian texts on disk for *Rubus* found no hit. The Dioscorides text prints *Rubus* in translators' SUGGESTED lines for Greek drugs (lines 4444, 8323 to 8324 and 8328: *Rubus fruticosus* and *Rubus idaeus*), which are not read as this plant. The row was added from a printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (line 275) the row is keyed by the **scientific name** "Rubus ellipticus", with the locus "Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients (line 5282)" and the note "Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)". Line 5282 prints **four** binomials on one line ("Cinnamomum camphora Rubus ellipticus Bergenia stracheyi Hedychium spicatum"). The key is the **second**, a later name on the line and not the first, and each of the other three has its own row. Clark prints no *Rubus ellipticus*. The Tibetan name *ga.dra* is reached from this row by column position and by the meaning of the sentence, as set out below.

**Identity doubt.** The binomial is set against the Tibetan name by column position only. Clark's entry *ga.dra* (line 973) prints "Brown Salamin: Rubus idaeus: Debregeasia edulis: Solanum indicum", so its first species is a different *Rubus* and two of its four names are not *Rubus*. The two editions therefore agree on the Tibetan name and the genus (or one of Clark's genera) and differ on the species, and the Codex does not choose. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance" and (line 68) that "the data given in some of these is occasionally suspect". The Men-Tsee-Khang editors' note (line 686) says that its botanical terms rest on the information of its own Materia Medica Department, and it names no earlier flora. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (the "G" block).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients. The glossary prints its columns in separate runs. The phonetic names are ten (lines 5274 to 5278): "Gabur", "Gadra", "Gadur", "Gakya", "Gangga chhung", "Gokya", "Goje", "Gongthokpa", "Go nyoe" and "Gudue". The transliterations (line 5280) are "ga bur", "ga bra", "li ga dur", "sga skya", "gang ga chung", "sgog skya", "go bye", "gong thog pa", "go snyod" and "rgu drs". The scientific names are ten (lines 5282 to 5287): "Cinnamomum camphora", "Rubus ellipticus", "Bergenia stracheyi", "Hedychium spicatum" (all four on 5282), "Gentiana umula" (5283), "Allium sativum" (5284), "Senecarpus sp." and "Erysimum melicentaea" (both on 5285), "Carum carvi" (5286) and "Senecio scandens" (5287). The English column (lines 5289 to 5297) reads "Camphor", "Raspberry", "-", "Wild ginger", "-", "Garlic", "-", "-", "Caraway", "-". Counted in order, "Gadra" is the second phonetic name, "ga bra" the second transliteration, *Rubus ellipticus* the second scientific name and "Raspberry" the second English name. The count is checked on "Gabur" against camphor (first), "Gokya" against *Allium sativum* and "Garlic" (sixth) and "Go nyoe" against *Carum carvi* and "Caraway" (ninth), where Clark's *go.snyod* (line 1122) prints "Fennel: Foeniculum vulgare: Carum carvi". The glossary does not print the pairing in one row, so it is by position only. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text.* Chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225; colophon at line 3583), line 3423: "Ga dra possesses the same effect as Laetae and Kanta kari and is effective against contagious diseases." The sentence before it (lines 3422 to 3423) reads: "Laetae and Kanta kari cure hot disorders associated with loong." The index lists "Ga dra 205" (line 7371). "Loong" is a Tibetan term that the editors keep untranslated (note at line 686). The sentence gives no part used, no preparation and no taste.

*Clark 1995, the same Tibetan name (read through the name, not the binomial).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 ("The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines", line 792), within "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (heading at line 997), line 973, immediately after *kanda.ka.ri* (line 972): "ga.dra: Brown Salamin: Rubus idaeus: Debregeasia edulis: Solanum indicum: [has fourteen names, for example stag.tsher (Salsola collina) and brihati]. It too is beneficial [in wind fever and] infectious fever." Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries, so the fourteen names, the examples of them and "in wind fever and" are the commentary layer. Outside the brackets, as Clark's marks stand, are the Tibetan name, the English name, the four Latin names, which are Clark's identifications, and "It too is beneficial ... infectious fever", a clause that points back to the preceding drug. Clark's name index lists *ga.dra* (line 1680).

The two editions agree in kind: Clark's drug is "beneficial in wind fever and infectious fever" and "too" like *kanda.ka.ri*, and the Men-Tsee-Khang *Ga dra* has "the same effect as Laetae and Kanta kari" and is "effective against contagious diseases", where "infectious" and "contagious" are the two translators' words. The Codex reads *Gadra* and *ga.dra* as one drug from the spelling and that agreement; neither text says so. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Rubus ellipticus*, *ga.dra*, *Gadra*, *Debregeasia* and *Rubus idaeus* found no FMT entry on this drug. I_1243 (*Bergenia stracheyi*), I_1293 (*Gentiana umula*) and I_1189 (*Senecio scandens*) print other binomials of the same glossary "G" block and name *Rubus ellipticus* in doing so. I_837 (*Rubus idaeus*, Raspberry; tags CM, DMM) is an entry on Clark's first species in other traditions, with no FMT tag. I_360 (*Brihati*, *Solanum indicum*; tag SS) is an entry on Clark's fourth species and the commentary name "brihati" in another tradition, and is not merged. I_1349 (*Rubus biflorus*, CPU-01785) is the sibling row from the same batch, for the neighbouring name *Kanta kari* and Clark's line 972. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Rubus* is a genus of the rose family (Rosaceae) whose berries are eaten, that *Debregeasia* belongs to the nettle family (Urticaceae) and that *Solanum* belongs to the nightshade family (Solanaceae), so Clark's four names point to plants of three families. The lines read give no composition for the Tibetan drug, and nothing is stated here about what it contains.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a drug that is "beneficial in wind fever and infectious fever" (Clark, line 973) and has "the same effect as Laetae and Kanta kari and is effective against contagious diseases" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3423). These are the texts' claims, attached to this binomial only through column position, and they are not Codex protocols. Fever and infection need medical care, and the Codex gives no protocol and no dose for them. The English "Raspberry" the glossary prints is not evidence that the Tibetan drug is the fruit of the garden plant.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica gives this drug by what it does: it is beneficial in wind fever and infectious fever, and the Men-Tsee-Khang text ranks it with *Laetae* and *Kanta kari*. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. A search of both Tibetan texts for the drug's names (*ga.dra*, *Gadra*, *Ga dra*, *Rubus*) found it only in the lines cited above, the glossary and the indexes. It is not in the rejuvenation chapter (Men-Tsee-Khang chapter 23, colophon at line 3950), and the herb entries of Clark's chapter 20 that mention essence extraction (lines 971, 1043, 1078, 1103, 1121 and 1144) do not include it. Its framing is therapeutic only, and it attaches to the Tibetan name more securely than to this binomial.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a fever drug defined by its likeness to another drug, with no preparation and no dose, and its Latin name is a positional guess among several. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. Fever and infection need medical care; the identity is open among plants of different families, no preparation or dose is given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry.
