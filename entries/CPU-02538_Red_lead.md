# Red lead — 铅丹 *Qiandan* (Shennong bencao jing), also 铅华 *qianhua* (Mingyi layer); minium
## *Red lead (minium), the red-orange lead oxide Pb3O4, the identification we give to 铅丹; of high confidence, supported by Tao Hongjing's gloss in Sun's note that it is the yellow-red 黄丹 made by boiling lead. This is a lead compound: historical record only, plainly toxic* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Toxic substance: historical record only.** Red lead is a lead compound and a cumulative poison. Nothing in this entry is a recommendation. The Codex's separate entry II_101 (Lead) treats lead and its oxides from several traditions, including the Shennong *Qian Dan* from Yang 1998; this entry is the monograph from Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`) and does not repeat the other traditions.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the lower (下) classic, reads 味辛，微寒 ("acrid, slightly cold"). It governs 土逆胃反 (the characters as printed; the Yulan variant in Sun's bracket reads 吐下, which suggests "vomiting counterflow and stomach reflux" [USER: verify]), 惊痫…疾 ("fright epilepsy" and one further illness; one character is lost in the transcription), and it "removes heat and brings down qi" (除热下气). Then come two sentences that are not indications: 炼化还成九光 ("refined and transformed, it returns to become nine lights/radiances") and **久服，通神明**: "taken long: communicates with the spirit-light / understanding". Sun's bracket adds that the Yulan quotes the passage as 吐下 and says 久服成仙, "taken long, one becomes an immortal (*xian*)". The habitat is 生平泽, "level marshes". (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 铅丹 *qiandan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2967)

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 adds the alias 铅华 and the origin 生蜀郡 ("Shu commandery"). The Mingyi layer states no toxicity here.

**Sun's note (案).** Sun cites Shuowen, which calls lead "blue-green metal" (青金), and Tao Hongjing, who says that 铅丹 "is today's 黄丹 made by boiling (熬) lead".

**Identity.** Tao's gloss is the only description given: the red-yellow powder made from lead. It matches red lead, and the modern name 铅丹 (and 黄丹) refers to red lead (Pb3O4). The Shennong text itself does not give the chemistry. No contradiction found.

### Active Components

Red lead is lead(II,IV) oxide, Pb3O4, an orange-red powder used as a pigment. Lead and its compounds are cumulative poisons that affect the nervous system, blood and kidneys; this is well established. Nothing else is stated here.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice, and not to be taken. The Shennong classic records red lead for vomiting and reflux, fright epilepsy, to remove "heat" and to "bring down qi", and adds a claim that long use communicates with the spirit-light (and, in the Yulan quotation, makes one an immortal). The Codex records these as claims of the classic and nothing more. A lead compound taken in the body causes lead poisoning, and the long-use claim is the most dangerous sentence in the text: a cumulative poison taken over a long time is the opposite of the "nourish life" the claim imagines.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives the habitat and one phrase of processing: 炼化还成九光, "refined and transformed, it returns to become nine lights". It gives no recipe, quantity or course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved, and the Codex does not supply one. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Lead(II,IV) oxide is a pigment and was once used in paints and glazes; lead exposure causes anaemia, neurological damage and kidney damage, and no safe level of exposure is known for children. Its use in medicines and cosmetics is banned or restricted in many places. The classic's claims for vomiting, epilepsy or immortality have no modern support. ### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested, with a tension the Codex records.* The classic files 铅丹 in the lower grade (下品); its preface says lower-grade drugs treat disease, are mostly toxic, and are not for long use. Yet its own paragraph carries a 久服 clause: 久服，通神明 ("taken long: communicates with the spirit-light"), and the Yulan variant quoted in Sun's bracket goes further (久服成仙, "taken long, one becomes an immortal"). The clause is therefore a long-use cultivation promise attached to a lower-grade drug, which contradicts the preface's own frame for that grade (not for long use). The Codex records (a) because the paragraph promises a life-nourishing status, and records the contradiction. The Mingyi bielu, not the Shennong text, gives the alias 铅华 and the origin Shu. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 铅丹 *qiandan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2967)

**Practitioner's note.** There is no practice here. The classic's long-use promise attaches to a compound of lead, a cumulative poison; the preface itself says lower-grade drugs are not for long use; and the Codex gives no dose and no recommendation. For a cultivator the only use of this entry is as a warning: the claim "taken long, communicates with the spirit-light" is the classic's, and following it would be poisoning.

### Contraindications

- **Lead poisoning.** Red lead is a lead compound. Do not ingest, inhale or apply it. Children and pregnancy are the most at risk. Lead is a cumulative poison.
- **Heat and fumes.** Heating lead compounds releases toxic fume.
- **Law.** Use in medicines and cosmetics is banned or restricted in many places. [USER: verify local rules]
- **The classic's claim.** The "long use" and "immortal" statements are not a safe practice under any reading.
