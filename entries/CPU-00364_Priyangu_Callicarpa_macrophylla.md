# Priyangu — प्रियङ्गु (Priyaṅgu), Phalinī, Gandhaphalī, Śyāmā, Kāntā

## *Callicarpa macrophylla* Vahl | Family: Lamiaceae (formerly Verbenaceae)

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā ( Āyurvedic surgical canon):** Priyaṅgu is one of the classical *raktastambhaka* (haemostatic) and *saṃdhānīya* (union-promoting / wound-knitting) dravyas of the surgical tradition. It appears repeatedly in the treatment of *rakta-pitta* (haemorrhagic disorders and bleeding tendencies), *atisāra* (diarrhoea / dysentery), and *dāha* (burning sensation). Its astringent, cooling character makes it a paradigmatic Pitta-and-Kapha pacifier: it is deployed wherever heat, laxity of tissue, and unchecked flow (of blood, of stool, of discharge) must be gathered and sealed. Classical formulary groupings place Priyaṅgu among the *Priyaṅgvādi gaṇa* (the group named for it) and it recurs in *Puṣpādi* and cooling-astringent assemblages used topically as *lepa* (paste) for wounds, ulcers, and inflammatory swellings, and internally in decoctions for haemorrhage and flux.

**Bṛhat-trayī continuity (Caraka / Vāgbhaṭa):** The later compendia retain Priyaṅgu among *saṃgrāhī* (absorbent, flow-checking) and *varṇya* (complexion-improving) agents. It is a fragrant dravya — the epithets *Gandhaphalī* ("fragrant-fruited") and *Gandhapriyaṅgu* mark it — and is used in cosmetic and dermatological pastes as well as in obstetric and gynaecological contexts for excessive menstrual and post-partum bleeding.

> **Nomenclature caution — the "Priyaṅgu" ambiguity.** The Sanskrit name *Priyaṅgu* is applied in different regional and historical traditions to more than one plant. In much of the North Indian and classical Āyurvedic material-medica tradition, Priyaṅgu is identified as *Callicarpa macrophylla* Vahl. However, *Priyaṅgu* has also historically denoted *Prunus mahaleb*, *Aglaia* spp., and (as "kāṅgu/priyaṅgu" the millet) *Setaria italica*. This entry follows the *Callicarpa macrophylla* identification per the given binomial. Practitioners sourcing raw drug should confirm botanical identity, because the substitutes differ pharmacologically. **Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Gathering Hand:**
In the symbolic grammar of Kenetica, Priyaṅgu is the *closing gesture* — the hand that draws scattered things back to centre. Where a stimulant scatters and a purgative expels, the astringent *gathers*: it contracts the mouth of the vessel, tightens the weave of the tissue, and stems what would otherwise run out and be lost. Blood that stays in the vessel is vitality retained; flux that is checked is essence conserved. Priyaṅgu therefore belongs to the family of "sealing" substances whose doctrinal function is *conservation of the vital fluid* — the refusal of unnecessary loss.

Its fragrance (*gandha*) adds a second layer. Fragrant astringents in the tradition are not merely styptic; they are *varṇya* and *saumanasya-kara* — complexion-brightening and mind-gladdening. The cooling seal is thus also a beautifying, composing act: the same contraction that stops bleeding smooths and clarifies the surface it touches. In the training arc, Priyaṅgu is the substance of the *recovery and consolidation* phase — invoked after heat, after exertion, after loss, to knit the edges back together and hold what has been rebuilt.

### Active Components

Reported constituents of *Callicarpa macrophylla* (from the wider phytochemical literature on the genus *Callicarpa*) include **clerodane and related diterpenoids** (a chemotype characteristic of the genus), **flavonoids** (which underlie the astringent, antioxidant, and haemostatic actions attributed to it), **phenylethanoid glycosides**, **triterpenoids**, and **volatile terpenoid constituents** giving the fragrant character (*Gandhaphalī*). The astringency classically ascribed to the drug is consistent with a **tannin / polyphenol** fraction.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

- **Haemostatic sealing (raktastambhana):** the core use — checking bleeding, both topically on wounds/ulcers and internally in haemorrhagic states. Deployed in the Cooling-and-Sealing phase.
- **Cooling of Pitta heat (dāha, rakta-pitta):** reduces burning, inflammatory heat, and the "boiling-over" of the blood.
- **Astringent flow-control (saṃgrāhī):** in diarrhoea/dysentery to firm the stool and reduce excess discharge.
- **Wound-knitting and complexion (saṃdhānīya, varṇya):** topical paste for ulcer margins, inflammatory swelling, and skin clarity.

In Kenetica's phase model Priyaṅgu is a **consolidation agent**: never a driver or opener, always a closer — used to hold, seal, cool, and conserve after the active work is done.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** kaṣāya (astringent) predominant; tikta (bitter); madhura (mildly sweet)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** laghu (light), rūkṣa (dry) — consistent with the astringent, drying, flow-checking action
- **Vīrya (potency):** śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** kaṭu (pungent)
- **Doṣa action:** Pitta-śāmaka and Kapha-śāmaka; in excess or in the constitutionally dry, may aggravate Vāta (owing to rūkṣa–laghu qualities)
- **Karma (actions):** raktastambhaka (haemostatic), saṃgrāhī (absorbent/flow-checking), saṃdhānīya (wound-uniting), varṇya (complexion-improving), dāha-praśamana (relieves burning), viṣaghna (classically also cited as toxin-allaying)
- **Srotas / dhātu:** acts primarily on rakta (blood) and māṃsa/tvak (flesh and skin); used in raktavaha and purīṣavaha srotas disorders
- **Gaṇa membership:** eponymous head of *Priyaṅgvādi gaṇa*; appears in cooling-astringent and *puṣpādi*-type classical groupings

### Contraindications

- **Constitutional dryness / Vāta excess:** astringent-drying character can worsen constipation, dryness, and Vāta-type pain; balance with unctuous (snigdha) anupāna if used.
- **Deep-seated āma (undigested toxin) with obstruction:** flow-checking action may not suit conditions requiring drainage.
- **Botanical-identity risk:** because "Priyaṅgu" is a name shared by several unrelated plants, misidentified raw drug is a real hazard — source against a verified *Callicarpa macrophylla* voucher.
