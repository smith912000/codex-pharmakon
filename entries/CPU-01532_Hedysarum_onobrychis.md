### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk names this plant. The entry rests on one chapter of one text. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed under the chapter heading, and not Dioscorides' own identification. The Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED lines give *Hedysarum onobrychis* and two *Onobrychis* names, then a second plant, *Onobrychis christagalli*. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *3-170. ONOBRUCHIS* at line 7949, SUGGESTED lines at 7950-7951, a printed cross-reference "see 3-41" at line 7952, text at line 7953. The chapter stands in Book Three, "Roots", after 3-169 *ALISMA* and before 3-171 *UPERIKON*. The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 576.

The whole chapter reads: "Onobrychis has leaves similar to lens [lentils] but a little longer, a stalk twenty centimetres long, a purple flower, and a small root. It grows in moist undisturbed places. The herb (pounded into small pieces and smeared on) is able to dissolve tubercles [growths], and a decoction (taken as a drink with wine) cures slow painful urination. Rubbed on with oil it encourages sweating. It is also called onobrochilos, eschasmene, hypericum, corion, or chamepitys, the Romans call it opaca, some, brichilata, lopta, or iuncinalis, and the Dacians, aniassexe."

*The cross-reference.* The chapter 3-41 is *EDUOSMOS EMEROS* (heading at line 6882), whose SUGGESTED line gives mint (*Mentha sativa*) and whose text describes a warming, astringent little herb. Nothing in 3-170 explains why it points there, and nothing from the mint chapter is read into this entry. The closing list of names includes *hypericum*, and the next chapter is headed 3-171 *UPERIKON* (line 7954); that chapter was not read for this entry and the two are not merged. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records a poultice for growths, a drink in wine for difficult urination and an oil rub to bring on sweating. The Codex gives no protocol for any of these. The identity is a translators' suggestion, and the text's own list of names includes *hypericum* and *chamepitys*, which is a further reason for caution.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a description for recognition (leaves like lentils but a little longer, a stalk of twenty centimetres, a purple flower), a habitat (moist, undisturbed places) and an unusually long list of names in several languages (Greek, Roman, Dacian). The names show how widely the plant was named. It is not a cultivation status. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The transferable point is that a long list of names for a plant is a reason to check the description before trusting the name. The text offers nothing more, and the abstentive answer is the right one.

### Contraindications

Chapter 3-170 prints no warning. The identification is only the translators' suggestion, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Painful or slow urination needs medical assessment, and nothing here is advice. [USER: verify]
