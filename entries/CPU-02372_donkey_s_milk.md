# Donkey's Milk — Ass's Milk (Bryan's Ebers)

## *Milk of the donkey or ass (Equus asinus); Bryan prints "Ass's Milk" among the forms of milk used in the Ebers prescriptions; the identification as the domestic donkey is ours, of medium confidence, since Bryan's list of animals prints "Ass" with no Egyptian word and no binomial; the name is not an item with its own prescription in the text read* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Ebers Papyrus only, among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 *The Papyrus Ebers*, a translation with summaries (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). The Egyptian words are not printed. In the chapter on vehicles Bryan says that milk "appears as Fresh Milk, Sour Milk, Spoiled Milk, Cooked Milk, Ass's Milk, Cow's Milk, Man's Milk, Milk-of-a-Woman, Milk-of-a-Woman-who-has-borne-a-Son, Milk-of-the-Sycamore, and Milk-juice" (line 1131). The project's list `_sources/ebers.csv` has the matching row, line 66, "Donkey's milk", botanical column "Equus asinus", class animal, source reference "Ebers; sae.saw-leipzig.de", not flagged id-uncertain. The second reference is a database that is not on disk and was not opened.

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The word is in the list of forms of milk; Bryan gives no prescription for it by name in the lines read. In his animal list "Ass" appears (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, row "Ass", Bryan Ch. VII, line 1404), and the prescriptions he summarises use the ass's fat for the headache (line 2110-2112) and the ass's dung (lines 2181, 2338), once "mix in Fresh Milk", but not as a milk. The ass's ear is also an ingredient of an ear injection (line 3032). What the milk of the ass was used for is therefore not stated in the text on disk. ### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms, donkey milk is low in fat and, among the common milks, low in casein relative to whey proteins and high in lactose; these are general facts and no figure was read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Nothing specific. It is one of the milks named as a vehicle for other drugs.
- **What the text does not say.** The complaint, the dose, the preparation, any recitation.

The Codex gives no protocol for this material.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Bryan text on disk prints no preparation and no dose for the ass's milk as such; milk in general is described as a vehicle (line 1131). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Donkey milk is consumed in some regions and sold as a speciality product; interest in it as a substitute for cow's milk in infants with cow's-milk protein allergy exists, but no clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **EP.** Bryan prints no energetic quality for any milk. The corpus is organised by complaint.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* The Ebers material read records what was done for a complaint and names ass's milk only as a form of milk used as a vehicle. It does not say what the milk does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. In Egyptian practice spell and drug are components of one intervention (*heka*); Bryan summarises prescriptions and omits much of the recitation, so what the ass's milk did within a *heka* intervention cannot be read from him.

**Practitioner's note.** The entry records that a donkey's milk was one of the milks in the papyrus's list of vehicles. There is no cultivation use to report, and the identification is a modern gloss on Bryan's English.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene.** Raw milk of any animal can carry infection.
- **Allergy.** People allergic to cow's-milk protein may react to other milks; this is a general statement, not from the source.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** "Ass's milk" is Bryan's English; the Egyptian word is not printed. The modern list's species name is not confirmed here.
