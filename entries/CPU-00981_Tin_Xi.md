# Tin — Trapu (Bhishagratna) / Xi Tong Jing Bi, "Compound of Tin & Copper"; Tie Xi, "Disintegratable Tin" (Yang 1998)

Tin occurs twice, as a material for vessels. In the quicksilver chapter 5-110 (line 10405), hydrargyrum "is kept in glass, lead, tin or silver jars for it eats through all other matter and runs out". In 1-33 ELAION SIKUONION, Sicyonian oil (heading at line 3815), the oils are boiled "into a broad-mouthed kettle covered with tin" (line 3820); that oil is "somewhat warming, suitable for fevers and affected nerves". **Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** No entry for tin metal. Two printed items carry the character or word. Xi Tong Jing Bi, printed in the OCR as "Xi Tong ling Bi (Compound of Tin & Copper)", in "Jades and Stones: Inferior Class" (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 1350): it "mainly treats females' blood block, concretions and conglomerations lying in the intestines, and infertility. [Galenite] is also named Tie Xi (Disintegratable Tin). It is produced in the mountains and valleys of Gui Yang" (lines 1350-1354); Yang's note 74: "This was a compound of lead and copper from which mirrors were made in olden times. In those times, tin was often confused with lead." The preceding entry, Fen Xi, is rendered "Galenitum Praeparatum" (line 1321), which the spine files under lead. Tin is also named in other entries only as something other drugs act on: Shui Yin "kills the toxins of gold, silver, copper, and tin" (line 880), and Kong Qing "is able to transform copper, iron, lead, and tin into gold" (line 912). Whether the spine's "Xi" refers to Fen Xi, to Xi Tong Jing Bi, or to both is not settled by the text. [USER: verify]

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Trapu* heads the *Trapvadi* group of *Sutrasthana* ch.38 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 440): "Trapu, Sisa, Tamra, Rajata, Krishna-Lauha, Suvarna and Lohamala", a group "regarded as a good vermifuge" that neutralises "the effects of chemical poison originated through incompatible combinations" (lines 443-448). The metal group of ch.46 (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 2121-2141) has gold, silver, copper, bell-metal, iron, "zinc and lead", and no tin by name. In *Chikitsasthana* ch.13 (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch13.txt` lines 24-36), "the six kinds of metal, such as tin, lead, copper, silver, gold and black-iron" are present in *Silajatu*, and "as tin, lead and iron, etc., are progressively more and more efficacious, so the different varieties of Silajatu, originated from the essence of tin, lead, iron, etc., are progressively more efficacious". In a passage on worms (*Uttara* ch.54, `SS_Uttara_ch54.txt` lines 190-193) "Trapu (killed lead) should be rubbed in the upper liquid part of curd (Mastu) and should be taken for a week"; there the translator's gloss reads *Trapu* as lead, while `_sources/sushruta.csv` lists *Trapu* as "tin Sn" (Sutra 38). The translator's gloss and the list therefore disagree on what *Trapu* is. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Tin is the element Sn, a soft, silvery metal that resists corrosion and is alloyed with copper to make bronze and bell-metal. The metal and its simple inorganic salts are of low toxicity compared with lead, and organotin compounds are toxic. Tin alloys such as pewter and solder have often contained lead.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. In the sources tin is a vessel material (Greek), a named member of a vermifuge and antitoxic group and a constituent of *Silajatu* (Sanskrit), and a component of a mirror-metal compound with a stated gynaecological use (Chinese). None supports taking tin for health. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only preparations the texts give. SS: "Trapu (killed lead)" rubbed in the upper part of curd and taken for a week (Uttara ch.54); no method of "killing" and no quantity is given in the lines read. DMM: none. SBJ: none for Xi Tong Jing Bi. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Metallic tin is poorly absorbed and has little pharmacological activity; its historical uses against worms and as a "metal" medicine are not supported by clinical evidence. Old vessels, solders and alloys sold as tin may contain lead. No clinical studies were retrieved for the traditional uses. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied, and tin has no chapter. The only trace is the tin-lined vessel, which is the discipline of attention in its smallest form: a vessel is chosen so that the contents do not eat through it (5-110).

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent, and the attestation rests on a spine row.* No Avicenna text or list row was found. Innate heat and radical moisture cannot be tied to tin without a source and are not tied to it here. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* There is no tin entry and so no *san pin* grade for tin. The nearest printed item, Xi Tong Jing Bi, stands in the inferior-class pages (line 1350); the preface (lines 391-396) says of that class that it "mainly treat[s] disease ... usually toxic and cannot be taken for a long time". That grade belongs to the compound as Yang renders it, and Yang's own note says the compound is as much lead as tin. It is not extended to tin as a substance.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* *Trapu* is claimed by *dravyaguna* (the *Trapvadi* group) and by the *Silajatu* passage in the diabetes chapter (*Chikitsasthana* ch.13). It is not named in the *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapters searched (*Chikitsasthana* ch.26-30), and no *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is printed for it. What *Trapu* is, tin or lead, is unsettled in the translator's own glosses.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator to take. Tin is, in these sources, a vessel, a compound metal, a named member of a vermifuge group and a minor constituent of a mountain resin. The honest takeaway is an identity warning: the old names for tin and lead cross, and a "tin" remedy may have been lead. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Because the old names for tin and lead cross, treat any traditional "tin" or *Trapu* preparation as a possible lead preparation. "Killed" or "ash" metal preparations are unstandardised. Organotin compounds are toxic. Use of metal preparations in medicine is regulated in many places.
