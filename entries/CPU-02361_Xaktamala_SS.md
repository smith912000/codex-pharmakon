# Xaktamala — Naktamala (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907; printed "Xaktamala" in the OCR of the group lists and "Naktamala" elsewhere)

## *A tree or shrub named in two drug groups of Sushruta chapter 38 (the Varunadi and the Salasaradi), in the list of woods burnt for alkali, and in a blood-letting plaster. The "X" is an OCR misreading of "N", shown by the same name printed "Naktamala" in other lines. The text gives no description and no Latin name, and the translator's appendix has no row for it in the lines searched. Identification: unresolved. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The name is a bare entry in drug lists. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should gather or take a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder keyed on the OCR form "Xaktamala"; this entry replaces it. The first-pass guess was "unresolved (Xaktamala)". That stands at species level. The reading of "X" as "N" is made here from the text itself (points 3 and 4 below), and the name has no other row on the spine: no row for *Naktamala* was found in `canonical_index.csv`.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

1. **Group list, Varunadi.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15385 to 15391; `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 44 to 50). Bhishagratna prints: "The drugs known as Varuna, Artagala, Shigru, Madhu-Shigru, Tarkari, Mesha-Shringi, Putika, Xaktamala, Morata, Agnimantha, the two kinds of Sairiyaka, Vimvi, Vasuka, Vasira, Chitraka, Shatavari, Mlva, Ajashringi, Darbha, and the two kinds of Vrihati form the group known as the Varunadi." (The OCR also damages "Bilva" to "Mlva".) The metrical text (lines 52 to 56) says: "The group is possessed of the efficacy of reducing the deranged Kapham and fat and proves efficacious in cases of cephalaegia [sic], Gulma and internal abscesses." This is the group's virtue, not the drug's own.
2. **Group list, Salasaradi.** The same chapter (Vol. I line 15421; `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 75 to 82) lists "Salasara, Ajakarna, Khadira, Kadara, Kalaskandha, Kramuka, Bhurjja, Meshashringi, Tinisha, Chandana, Kuchandana, Shinshapa, Shirisha, Asana, Dhava, Arjuna, Tala, Shaka, Xaktamala, Putika, Ashvakarna, Aguru and Kaliyaka". The metrical text (lines 83 to 88) says that the group "destroys the germ of Kushtha, absorbs the deranged fat and Kapham and proves beneficial in morbid discharges from the urethra (Meha), chlorosis or jaundice (Pandu)". Again this is the group's virtue.
3. **The name printed with an N.** In the chapter on the preparation of alkali (*Sutrasthana* ch.11, `SS_Sutra_ch11.txt` lines 124 to 132) the wood, leaves, roots and fruits of a list of trees, "Kutaja, Palasha, Ashvakarna, Paribhadra, Vibhitaka, Aragvadha, Tilvaka, Arka, Snuhi, Apamarga, Patala, Naktamala, Vrisha, Kadali, Chitraka, Putika, Indra-Vriksha, Asphota, Ashvamaraka, Saptachchhada, Agnimantha, Gunja, and the four species of Koshataki", are burnt to ashes. The name here is "Naktamala", and it stands beside *Putika* and *Agnimantha* just as "Xaktamala" does in the group lists.
4. **A plaster for blood-letting.** In the chapter on blood (*Shonita-Varnaniya*, *Sutrasthana* ch.14, `SS_Sutra_ch14.txt` lines 350 to 358) "A plaster composed of Ela, Shitashiva, Kustha, Tagara, Patha, Agaradhuma, Bhadradaru, Vidanga, Chitraka, Trikatus, Ankura, Haridra, Arka, and Nakta-mala, or three, or four, or as many of them as are available, pasted together and soaked in mustard oil saturated with common salt, should be rubbed over the mouth of the incision. By this means the blood will fully come out." The word is split across a line break in the OCR ("Nakta-" and "mala"). The plaster is rubbed over an incision for blood-letting and is a compound; no property is given to the drug alone.
5. **Identity.** Bhishagratna's appendix of drug names with Latin synonyms (Vol. III, Appendix 3) was searched for *Naktamala* and its variants; no row was found. The machine-made index `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` records the name as "group list; no identification given here". Other entries in this Codex that name *Naktamala* in these same lists (for example the Putika and Sairiyaka entries) do not give it a species either. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unresolved identity.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Varunadi group: reducing *Kapham* and fat, and against headache, *Gulma* and internal abscesses. As a member of the Salasaradi group: against the germ of *Kushtha*, deranged fat and *Kapham*, urinary discharges (*Meha*) and jaundice (*Pandu*). Both statements are for the groups. In the blood-letting plaster the drug is one of fourteen named ingredients, applied to the cut.
- **What the text does not say.** No description, no part used when it stands alone, no dose.

The Codex does not translate *Kapham*, *Meha* or the other terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend any plant under this name.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations in which the name appears are the burning of the wood, leaves, roots and fruits to ashes for alkali (ch.11) and the oil-and-salt plaster of "three, or four, or as many of them as are available" ingredients (ch.14). ### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified. Do not substitute any tree on the strength of the name.
- **Alkali and caustic preparations.** The ashes in ch.11 make an alkali (*Kshara*), a caustic. This is a historical record and not a recipe.
- **Blood-letting plaster.** The plaster is rubbed on an incision to make blood flow. Do not use it.
- **Skin disease, urinary discharge, jaundice, abscess.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
