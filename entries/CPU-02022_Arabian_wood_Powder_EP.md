# Arabian-wood Powder — as printed in Bryan's Ebers (1930); also "Powdered-wood-from-Arabia"

## *A powdered wood from Arabia, named in two eye remedies of the Papyrus Ebers. Identification: unresolved. Bryan's list prints only this English rendering, with no Egyptian word and no binomial, and does not equate it with the aloes-wood printed just above it; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** The recipes that name this wood also contain red lead, verdigris, calamine and sulphur, which are not safe in the eye. This entry is a historical record only. Nobody should put any of these in the eyes.

**Named only by the Papyrus Ebers (through Bryan's 1930 English) among the texts on disk. This is a single-text entry.** The source is `Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`. The name is a head word in Bryan's chapter VI, "Plant Remedies" (line 1246), in the extract row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 44, which records it as "Bryan's English identification". The extract README says that the identifications are Bryan's English renderings only, and that "no Egyptian word and no binomial is given in these lists". Bryan himself says of the substances in his lists that they are the "recognizable" plants; he also says that many others are "completely hidden from us under the guise of meaningless words" (line 1233).

**Papyrus Ebers (EP).** The list entry stands alone between the Aloes group and the Balsam group:

"Aloes / Grain-of-Aloes / Resin-of-Aloes / Wood-of-Aloes // Arabian-wood Powder // Balsam / Real Balsam" (lines 1241 to 1248).

Because "Wood-of-Aloes" is a separate item in the group above, Bryan does not call the Arabian wood aloes-wood. He gives no description of it.

Bryan's chapters on the diseases of the eye name it twice. (1) For Bloodshot Eyes: "A simple poultice of Incense and Crocus, equal parts, often sufficed to clear up Bloodshot Eyes. If it did not a more complicated poultice composed of Onions, Verdigris, Collyrium, and Arabian-wood powder, crushed 'to a powder' with Ink and Water was recommended" (line 2747). (2) For pterygium, under the heading "ANOTHER TO DRIVE OUT PTERYGIUM": "Red-lead / Powdered-wood-from-Arabia / Iron-from-Apollonopolis-parva / Calamine / Egg-of-an-Ostrich / Saltpetre-from-Upper-Egypt / Sulphur / Honey. Make into one and apply to the Eyes" (lines 2848 to 2850). Bryan prints the second item as "Powdered-wood-from-Arabia"; it may be the same ingredient as the "Arabian-wood powder" of the list and of line 2747, and he does not say. [USER: verify]

No other occurrence was found by searching for "Arabia" and "Arabian" in the file. The Ebers text on disk has no Egyptian transliteration, no papyrus column numbers for these remedies, and no spell for them.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Bloodshot eyes (poultice), and pterygium (a mixed application "to the Eyes").
- **What the text does not say.** No dose and no amount; the wood is one item among several in each recipe.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this material.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations given are: Arabian-wood powder crushed to a powder with ink and water, in a poultice with onions, verdigris and collyrium (line 2747); and powdered wood from Arabia made into one with red lead, iron, calamine, ostrich egg, saltpetre, sulphur and honey, then applied to the eyes (lines 2848 to 2850). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Red lead and verdigris are poisonous metal compounds and must not be applied to the eye. The identity of the wood is unresolved, so no modern correlation is attempted. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution (the plural person of ka, ba, akh and ib is not engaged by these remedies in the passages read). In the Egyptian frame a spell (heka) and a drug are parts of one intervention; Bryan's English summaries give the ingredients and no recitation for these two eye remedies, and whether the papyrus attaches one is not recoverable from the text on disk. **Practitioner's note.** An unidentified imported wood powder named in two eye remedies. Nothing here supports any cultivation use, and the recipes are unsafe. A cultivator has no use for it.

### Contraindications

- **Eyes.** Do not apply any recipe in this entry to the eyes. The companions (red lead, verdigris, calamine, sulphur) are harmful.
- **Identity.** Unresolved; do not substitute any wood.
- **Eye disease.** Bloodshot eyes and pterygium need an eye doctor.
