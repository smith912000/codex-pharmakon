# ʿArqūn — Dioscorides (عرقون)

## *A plant with split anemone-like leaves and a round, eaten root, quoted from Dioscorides; a second kind is also described; neither is identified; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. Book II, entry *ʿarqūn*, Arabic text from line 16749 of `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk was not run beyond the spine check. 

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** "Dioscorides claimed that *ʿarqūn* is a plant whose leaf is like the leaf of the anemone (*shaqāʾiq al-nuʿmān*), split and long, and it has a round root, [*ḥamās*], which is eaten; and if a drachm's weight of it is drunk with wine it dissolves winds." The word *ḥamās* (حماس) is not clear to me and may be a copyist's form; I leave it untranslated. [USER: verify] "He also mentioned that there is another kind of it, with thin branches on which are seen leaves like the leaves of *mulūkhiyya* (Jew's mallow) and at the ends of the branches something protruding like the head of the crane (*kurkī*) and its beak; it has no use in the craft of medicine but in another craft, and it is not fitting for us to mention it here." After a page break the entry adds, under excretory organs: "a drachm's weight of it with wine dissolves the winds that inflate the womb."

**Identity doubt.** The anemone-like leaf, the round edible root and the crane-beak second kind do not fix a species. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** It records a root eaten and taken in wine against wind in the gut and womb. No protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

One drachm's weight (*wazn dirkhamī*, وزن درخمي) in wine for winds, twice stated. A drachm is the Canon's measure; no modern equivalent is given. This is the Canon's figure, not advice. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to practise. It is a lost Greek plant with a carminative root.

### Contraindications

- The plant is not identified; do not eat wild roots on the strength of this entry.
- Pain, swelling or bloating of the womb or gut needs medical care.
