# Kuvalaya — Kuvalaya, Kuvalayam (Sushruta Samhita)

## *A water-lily named Kuvalaya in the Utpaladi gana, called in the flower chapter a variety of the same species as Utpala; no Latin name is printed, and a Nymphaea species such as N. nouchali or N. stellata is the likely reading at low to medium confidence, from the text's own pairing with Utpala* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** Single-text entry, with the caution that the closely related water-lilies have their own entries (see the cross-references). The name is printed *Kuvalaya* in the gana and *Kuvalayam* in the flower chapter in Bhishagratna's translation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder with the locus Sutra 38, Vol. I line 15717. A first-pass check proposed that it duplicated the blue-lotus row, and the audit disagreed: the text itself gives Kuvalayam as a separate named variety of the same species as Utpalam. That reading is the text's, and the identification with a Latin name is left open. No Latin name is printed for Kuvalaya in the passages read.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The gana (Sutra ch. 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 375 to 378; Bhishagratna Vol. I line 15717).* "The Utpala'di Group:— The drugs known as Utpala, Raktotpala, Kumuda Saugondhika, Kuvalaya, Pundarika and Madhuka constitute the group known as the Utpaladi Gana." The metrical text (lines 380 to 386): "This group is possessed of the therapeutic virtue of allaying thirst and corrects the deranged Pittam and the vitiated blood. It assuages the burning sensation of the body and proves curative in cases of vomiting, in Hridroga (Angina pectoris) in syncope, in haemoptysis and in cases of poisoning as well."

*The flowers (Sutra ch. 46, `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1795 to 1805).* "The Padma has a bitter and sweet taste, is cooling in its potency, and subdues the deranged Pittam and Kapham. The Kumuda has a sweet taste, and is slimy, demulcent, pleasing and cooling in its potency. The two varieties of the same species known as the Kuvalayam and the Utpalam, slightly differ from the preceding varieties in their properties." The text does not say how they differ.

**Overlap.** The entries for Utpala (`I_479_Utpala_Nymphaea_stellata.md`), Kumuda (`I_913_Kumuda.md`) and the blue lotus (`I_494_BlueLotus_Nymphaea_caerulea.md`) treat their own names. Kuvalaya is kept apart because the text names it apart, and because the text calls it a variety of the same species as Utpala. The audit's view that Kuvalaya corresponds to *Nymphaea nouchali* or *N. stellata* rather than *N. caerulea* is the audit's and was not checked against a source for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As part of the Utpaladi group: thirst, deranged Pitta, vitiated blood, burning of the body, vomiting, heart disease, fainting, haemoptysis and poisoning (group action). As a flower: close to Utpala and Kumuda in property, 'slightly' different.
- **What the text does not say.** The plant's description, the part used, or any single-drug recipe.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Kuvalaya itself. The neighbouring flowers in the same passage (Sutra ch. 46, lines 1795 to 1805) are given as cooling in potency and soothing to Pitta (Padma, Kumuda), and the text says Kuvalayam and Utpalam "slightly differ" from them. The Utpaladi group is described in terms of thirst, Pitta and blood (Sutra ch. 38, lines 380 to 386).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Kuvalaya is named in the Sushruta Samhita as a member of a therapeutic group (gana) and in the other places listed under Origin & Tradition. The gana is a grouping by therapeutic action. It is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status, and the text read assigns Kuvalaya neither. A search of the whole chapter set on disk (sutra, chikitsa, kalpa, uttara, nidana, sharira) for the name found no occurrence in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters. It is also named in the flower chapter (ch. 46) as a variety of the same species as Utpala. Nothing links it to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The status is unattested, and only a therapeutic one is recorded.

**Practitioner's note.** A water-lily flower and a gana member, close to Utpala and Kumuda. It is recorded in the tradition for cooling and for Pitta. No cultivation status is attested.
