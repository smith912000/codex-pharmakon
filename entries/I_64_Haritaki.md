### Botanical Identity
Deciduous tree 15–30m; ovate grey-green leaves; cream-coloured flowers; greenish-yellow to blackish oval fruit with prominent ridges. Native to South Asia (India, Nepal, Sri Lanka) extending to Myanmar, Thailand, China. The fruit, harvested unripe and dried, is the drug material — commercial preparations use dark brown-black dried fruits graded by size and quality (seven grades in classical Ayurveda corresponding to different applications). The tannin content (24–32%) is among the highest of any medicinal fruit — the basis for the powerful astringent and antimicrobial effects.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Sushruta Samhita / Classical Ayurveda (SS)

*Harītakī* in Charaka Samhita:
> *"Astringent, sour, sweet, bitter, pungent — like *āmalakī*, contains five of the six tastes. Tridoshic. The king of medicines among fruits. Benefits the eyes, promotes intelligence, digestive and carminative, anti-ageing. To be taken daily as a rasāyana — morning with rock salt (Vata season), noon with sugar (Pitta season), evening with ghee (Kapha season)."*

**Seven Sanskrit names** of haritaki, each corresponding to a pharmacological application: *harītakī* (removes disease); *abhayā* (fearlessness); *amṛtā* (immortality); *hemavat* (golden); *pathyā* (wholesome/healing); *kayastha* (promotes longevity); *shivā* (auspicious). The seven-name system is an Ayurvedic mnemonic for breadth of therapeutic application.

### 2.2 Four Medical Tantras / Tibetan (FMT)

**Supreme in Tibetan medicine**: *A ru ra* is called *rGyal po'i sman* — "King of Medicines." In Tibetan thangka paintings, the Medicine Buddha (*Sangye Menla*) is depicted holding an *a ru ra* fruit in his right hand — it is the defining symbol of Tibetan medicine. No other substance in Sowa Rigpa has this iconographic status.

> *"A ru ra — the fruit of the Medicine Buddha. Of all medicines, the supreme. Clears all diseases of the three humours, renews the body, promotes intelligence, benefits the eyes, prolongs life."*

### 2.3 Shennong Bencao Jing / TCM (SBJ)

*Hé zǐ*:
> *"Sour, astringent; neutral. Enters Lung, Large Intestine channels. Astringes and stabilises Lung Qi, stops cough, astringes the intestine, stops chronic diarrhoea and prolapse. Used for chronic cough, hoarse voice, chronic diarrhoea, rectal prolapse."*

The Chinese application is more limited — focused on the astringent (Lung and Large Intestine) actions.

### 2.4 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā on *halīlaj*:
> *"Of moderate degree. Benefits the brain and stomach, promotes digestion, useful in jaundice and liver conditions, beneficial for the gums and teeth. One of the most important digestive tonics."*

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Bidirectional Digestive (Dose-Dependent)

**Anthraquinones + tannins**: Classic dose-dependent bidirectional mechanism:
- **Low dose (0.5–1g)**: Tannins predominate → mild astringency + digestive enzyme stimulation → improved digestion; mild carminative (reduces gas)
- **High dose (2–6g)**: Anthraquinones predominate → cathartic laxative effect
- This bidirectional action is the pharmacological basis for the Ayurvedic "tridoshic" classification: the correct dose for each dosha state is different.

### 5.2 Antimicrobial (Exceptionally Broad-Spectrum)

**Chebulinic acid + chebulagic acid + gallic acid**: Membrane disruption + tannin-protein binding → broadest antimicrobial spectrum of the Terminalia genus. Active against:
- *Staphylococcus aureus* including MRSA
- *Helicobacter pylori* (directly inhibits and blocks adhesion)
- *Candida albicans* and other fungal pathogens
- *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (in vitro MIC 62.5–125 µg/mL)

Chebulagic acid specifically: anti-HSV-1, anti-HIV-1 (reverse transcriptase inhibition), anti-influenza A mechanisms confirmed in vitro.

### 5.3 Hepatoprotective

**Corilagin + chebulagic acid**: Activate Nrf2 → hepatocyte antioxidant defence. Direct hepatoprotection in CCl4 and paracetamol models. The traditional "liver benefit" in Islamic and Ayurvedic medicine is mechanistically supported.

### 5.4 Anti-Ageing / Rasāyana

**Ellagic acid + chebulagic acid**: Collagen MMP inhibition → skin collagen preservation. Telomerase activation (preliminary). SIRT1 pathway activation. The *rasāyana* longevity mechanism most likely reflects these anti-ageing molecular targets.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Dental / oral**: Rahman (2014): Terminalia chebula extract mouthwash significantly reduces *Streptococcus mutans* counts — comparable to chlorhexidine 0.2% at equivalent concentrations. Multiple studies confirm dental antimicrobial efficacy.

**Diabetes**: Multiple animal studies: haritaki extract significantly reduces blood glucose and HbA1c. Human trial data limited but mechanistically credible (aldose reductase inhibition reduces diabetic complications).

**H. pylori**: Multiple in vitro studies: haritaki tannins among the most potent natural *H. pylori* inhibitors identified (MIC 0.5–4 µg/mL for purified chebulinic acid). Clinical trials in progress.

**Anti-inflammatory**: Kannan (2009): Haritaki extract significantly reduced CRP and pro-inflammatory cytokines in patients with systemic inflammation.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The King of Medicines and the Medicine Buddha**: The depiction of the Medicine Buddha holding an *a ru ra* fruit is the single most powerful cultural embedding of a plant in the world's medical traditions. Not as an ingredient in a formula or a supporting herb — but as the defining symbol of healing itself, held in the divine physician's hand. This iconographic choice reflects the Tibetan medical tradition's supreme evaluation of *a ru ra* based on 2,000 years of clinical observation: a substance with bidirectional digestive activity, broad-spectrum antimicrobial function, hepatoprotection, anti-ageing rasāyana effects, and adaptable dosing. The Medicine Buddha's hand holds what the Tibetan tradition considers the most complete single medicine: one that adjusts to the need, treats across organ systems, and can be taken daily.

**Seven names**: The seven Sanskrit names of haritaki constitute an unusual pharmacological mnemonic system. Instead of a single identity, the plant was given seven names encoding seven distinct applications — each name a clinical indication. *Abhayā* (fearless) = psychological resilience / adaptogen application. *Amṛtā* (immortality nectar) = rasāyana / longevity. *Hemavat* (golden-coloured) = the hepatic/bile application (jaundice, liver clearing). *Pathyā* (wholesome) = daily tonic safety. *Kayastha* (body-sustaining) = constitutional tonic. The naming system is a pre-literate clinical database.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Powder (churna) 0.5–1g | Daily digestive tonic | Improved digestion; mild astringent |
| Powder 2–4g | Laxative/purgative | Constipation; cleansing |
| Decoction | 5g in 200ml water | Antimicrobial; hepatoprotective; throat gargle |
| Triphala (1/3 haritaki component) | 1–3g/day | Combined rasāyana formula |
| Mouthwash (10% extract) | Gargle 1 min | Dental antimicrobial |
| Seasonal dosing (Ayurvedic) | With salt (autumn), sugar (summer), ghee (spring) | Dosha-adjusted seasonal rasāyana |

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Astringent, sour; neutral. Channels: Lung (肺), Large Intestine (大腸). Astringes Lung, stabilises Large Intestine.

**Ayurvedic**: Five tastes (sour, sweet, astringent, bitter, pungent); heating (post-digestive). Tridoshic. The supreme *tridoshic rasāyana*. *Cakṣuṣya* (eye-beneficial), *Medhya* (brain-beneficial), *Rasāyana* (rejuvenating), *Krimighna* (anti-parasitic).

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Pregnancy**: Avoid therapeutic doses; culinary amounts via diet acceptable
- **Severe dehydration / diarrhoea (acute)**: High dose is laxative — contraindicated in acute diarrhoea
- **Children under 2**: Potent tannin content — avoid
- **Concurrent medications**: Tannins bind medications reducing absorption — separate doses by 2 hours
- **Inflammatory conditions (acute)**: Astringent tannins may aggravate acute inflammatory gut conditions at high doses
- Daily low-dose (0.5–1g) use: one of the safest substances in the Codex
