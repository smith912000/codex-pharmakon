# Sacred Mushroom & Morning-Glory Complex — Teonanácatl ("flesh of the gods"), Ololiuqui, Magic Mushroom

## *Psilocybe cubensis* (Earle) Singer / *Turbina corymbosa* (L.) Raf. | Family: Strophariaceae (fungus) / Convolvulaceae (vine)

**Tradition Tags:** BM, SBJ, DMM, BM
**Energetic Classification:** Not classically humoral; ritual/entheogenic. Where forced into a Galenic frame, warm–dry and profoundly ecstatic-cathartic.
**Training Phase:** Threshold — reserved, supervised, non-self-administered work at the far edge of the system; not an early or self-directed phase.

> ⚠️ **CONTROLLED ENTHEOGEN.** This entry is historical, pharmacological, and clinical in scope. It contains **no preparation method, no dosing, and no cultivation or sourcing guidance.** Psilocybin and psilocybin-containing mushrooms are Schedule I / controlled substances in most jurisdictions. Human use described here is confined to **licensed clinical-trial and research settings.**

### Origin & Tradition

**Badianus / Mesoamerican ritual tradition (BM).** The "sacred mushroom and morning-glory complex" is one of the best-attested entheogenic traditions in the historical record. In central Mexico, Nahuatl-speaking peoples used psilocybin-bearing mushrooms — **teonanácatl**, "flesh of the gods" — and the seeds of the morning-glory vine **ololiuqui** (*Turbina corymbosa*, formerly *Rivea corymbosa*) in divination, healing, and religious ceremony. The Franciscan chronicler Bernardino de Sahagún, in the 16th-century *Florentine Codex*, recorded the ritual consumption of the sacred mushrooms; the Badianus manuscript and other early colonial sources record the Aztec pharmacopoeia within which ololiuqui held a sacred place. Spanish ecclesiastical authorities suppressed these practices as idolatry, driving them into secrecy, where they survived among Mazatec, Zapotec, and other peoples of Oaxaca into the modern era — famously in the *veladas* (night vigils) of the Mazatec curandera María Sabina, which brought the tradition to Western attention in the mid-20th century.

**Assyrian & Old-World visionary streams (DMM/SBJ, by analogy).** Fungal and vine-derived visionary substances have parallels across the ancient world — the ergot-linked *kykeon* of the Eleusinian mysteries in the Greek stream, and the broad Near-Eastern and Chinese traditions of ritual intoxicants used by diviners and healers. These are included here as *comparative* entheogenic lineages, not as literal historical uses of *Psilocybe cubensis*, which is a New World-associated species; the honest core of the tradition is Mesoamerican.

**rGyud-bzhi / Tibetan medical stream (by analogy).** The Tibetan medical tradition catalogues potent, tightly-controlled substances administered only by trained practitioners under ritual discipline — a useful frame for how a threshold substance is *held* rather than freely used. No claim is made that psilocybin appears in the rGyud-bzhi; the parallel is one of controlled, master-supervised administration.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** "Flesh of the gods" is a doctrine of communion — the substance as a sacrament that dissolves the ordinary self to reveal a larger order. Modern pharmacology gives this a striking correlate: the **mystical-type experience** occasioned by psilocybin has been shown in controlled trials to *mediate* its therapeutic effect (Ross et al. 2016), so the very "ego-dissolution" the tradition named as sacred is measurable as a clinical mechanism. The morning-glory's ergoline alkaloids and the mushroom's tryptamines converge on the same serotonergic doorway — two plants, one neurochemical gate.

### Active Components

- ***Psilocybe cubensis*:** **psilocybin** (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine), a prodrug rapidly dephosphorylated in vivo to **psilocin**, the active tryptamine; a partial **5-HT2A serotonin receptor agonist**. Minor constituents include baeocystin and norbaeocystin.
- ***Turbina corymbosa* (ololiuqui):** **ergoline alkaloids**, chiefly **ergine (D-lysergic acid amide, LSA)** and isoergine — structurally related to LSD, acting on serotonergic receptors.
- Both act on the **serotonin (5-HT) system**, particularly 5-HT2A, producing altered perception, cognition, and mood.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Within Kenetica this is a **Threshold-phase entheogen** — the most restricted class in the entire materia medica. It is **never** a self-administered or early-phase agent. Its only legitimate contemporary application is within **licensed, supervised clinical-trial settings**, where psilocybin-assisted therapy is being investigated for depression and end-of-life distress. In the traditional register it is documented as a sacrament of divination and healing held within a ritual container and an experienced guide — the antithesis of casual use. The Kenetica framing is therefore: *observe, study, and respect; do not self-administer.*

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, supervised psilocybin-assisted therapy is one of the most actively researched areas in psychiatry, with several positive randomised trials — all conducted under strict clinical control.

- **Major depressive disorder.** Davis et al., a randomised waiting-list-controlled trial (Johns Hopkins), gave two supervised psilocybin sessions with ~11 hours of psychotherapy to adults with MDD. GRID-HAMD scores fell with large effect sizes (Cohen d ≈ 2.5–2.6); 71% responded and ~54–58% were in remission at week 4 (*JAMA Psychiatry* 2021, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2020.3285)).
- **Psilocybin vs escitalopram.** Carhart-Harris et al., a phase-2 double-blind RCT (Imperial College London), compared two 25 mg psilocybin doses against six weeks of daily escitalopram, both with psychological support. The primary QIDS-SR-16 endpoint showed no significant between-group difference, though secondary outcomes (response 70% vs 48%; remission 57% vs 28%) generally favoured psilocybin; adverse-event rates were similar (*N Engl J Med* 2021, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032994)).
- **End-of-life / cancer-related anxiety and depression.** Ross et al., a double-blind placebo-controlled crossover trial (NYU), found single moderate-dose psilocybin with psychotherapy produced rapid and sustained reductions in anxiety and depression, with ~60–80% of participants maintaining clinically significant benefit at 6.5 months; the **mystical-type experience mediated** the effect (*J Psychopharmacol* 2016, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881116675512)).

**Evidence grade:** **Emerging clinical** for *supervised* psilocybin-assisted therapy in depression and end-of-life distress — promising, but from small trials, not yet standard care, and not approved for general medical use. **Traditional / entheogenic** for the wider ritual complex. Ololiuqui/*Turbina* has essentially no modern clinical trial evidence and is included on historical grounds only.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Mesoamerican ritual:** Sacred, communal, divinatory — a sacrament of the gods, administered by a trained healer within ceremony, not a humoral remedy.
- **TCM / Ayurveda / Unani:** No authentic classical placement; these New World organisms postdate the formation of those pharmacopoeias. Any humoral assignment is a modern analogy only.
- **Comparative entheogenic frame (rGyud-bzhi analogy):** Belongs to the class of potent substances that may be used *only* under master supervision and ritual discipline.

### Contraindications

**This is among the most heavily contraindicated substances in the Codex.**

- **⚠️ LEGAL CONTROL:** Psilocybin and psilocybin mushrooms are **Schedule I / controlled substances in most jurisdictions.** Possession, cultivation, and use are criminal offences outside licensed research. This entry does not endorse or enable illegal use.
- **⚠️ SERIOUS PSYCHIATRIC RISK:** Contraindicated in personal or family history of **psychosis, schizophrenia, or bipolar disorder** — psychedelics can **precipitate lasting psychiatric harm**, including protracted psychosis and mania.
- **⚠️ HPPD:** Risk of **Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder** — enduring visual disturbances after use.
- **⚠️ DANGEROUS INTERACTIONS:** Serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, MAOIs, lithium, tramadol) carry **serotonin-syndrome and seizure risk**; significant **cardiovascular risk** (5-HT2B activity, blood-pressure and heart-rate elevation) in those with cardiac disease.
- **⚠️ POISONING & DEATH FROM MISIDENTIFICATION:** Wild mushroom foraging is lethally dangerous. **Toxic species (e.g. *Amanita*, *Galerina*) are readily mistaken for *Psilocybe*** and cause fatal liver and kidney failure. Misidentification has killed people. This alone makes any unsupervised, wild-sourced use unacceptable.
- **Psychological:** acute anxiety, panic, and destabilising "bad trips"; risk of dangerous behaviour without a controlled setting and trained supervision.
- **Pregnancy, adolescence:** avoid entirely.
