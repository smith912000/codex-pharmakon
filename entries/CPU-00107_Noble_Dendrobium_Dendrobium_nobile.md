### Origin & Tradition

Noble dendrobium is an epiphytic orchid of East and South Asia whose stems are the Chinese materia medica drug **Shi Hu** (石斛) — literally "stone dendrobium," for its habit of growing on rocks and trees. It is one of the classic **yin- and fluid-nourishing** herbs of Chinese Medicine, and an esteemed (and, for the finest "Tie Pi Shi Hu"/iron-skin grades, costly) tonic with a reputation reaching back to the earliest materia medica, where it was counted among the superior longevity tonics.

In Chinese Medicine Shi Hu is sweet, slightly cold and moistening, used to **nourish stomach yin and generate fluids** (for dry mouth, thirst, dry tongue, and the fluid-depletion of febrile illness recovery), to **clear deficiency heat**, to nourish kidney yin and "brighten the eyes," and to support the depleted, dry, overheated constitution. It is the herb for the dry, thirsty, yin-deficient picture — particularly dryness of the stomach and the aftermath of fever — restoring moisture and quelling the low-grade heat that accompanies fluid depletion. It is gentle, tonifying and food-like, often cooked into restorative soups and teas.

Modern interest centres on the orchid's **polysaccharides** (immunomodulatory, much studied) and its **dendrobine** alkaloid and bibenzyl constituents, giving a phytochemical footing to its tonic and fluid-nourishing reputation.
