# Tian Ma (天麻) / Gastrodia

## *Gastrodia elata* Blume | Family: Orchidaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ, FMT
**Energetic Classification:** Sweet, neutral (slightly warm on the tongue after steaming); Liver-channel; wind-extinguishing, ascending-then-settling
**Training Phase:** Stabilisation / Settling (a corrective, not a stimulant — deployed when the internal "wind" of tremor, spin and agitation must be quieted)

---

### Origin & Tradition

Few drugs in the Kenetica materia medica carry a stranger biology than Tian Ma. *Gastrodia elata* is a **leafless, rootless, chlorophyll-less orchid** — it has abandoned photosynthesis entirely. It cannot feed itself from light. Instead the tuber is a parasite twice over: it germinates only in partnership with a small fungus (*Mycena*), then spends its adult life digesting the invading mycelium of **Armillaria** (honey fungus), the same aggressive wood-rotting fungus that kills forest trees. Gastrodia, in effect, eats the eater. What surfaces briefly above ground is only a pale flowering stalk — no green tissue at all. A plant that lives in the dark, on a fungus, and rises only to flower: the doctrine that grew around it is unusually faithful to that image.

**Chinese medicine (SBJ — Shen Nong / classical materia medica lineage).** Tian Ma ("heavenly hemp/marsh") is one of the premier drugs to **extinguish Liver wind and stop tremor** (息風止痙, *xī fēng zhǐ jìng*). "Wind" in this system is the internal principle of sudden, involuntary movement — the shaking, spinning, spasming and convulsing that arrive without warning, as wind arrives. Tian Ma is the classical answer to that whole family: **headache and migraine, dizziness and vertigo, convulsions, childhood fright-wind (infantile convulsion), tetany, numbness and, in modern TCM practice, the "wind" of hypertension and post-stroke sequelae.** It is the emperor herb of formulas such as Tian Ma Gou Teng Yin (for ascendant Liver yang with dizziness and headache) and Ban Xia Bai Zhu Tian Ma Tang (for phlegm-wind vertigo). Crucially it is held to be neither strongly cooling nor strongly warming — it settles wind without draining the patient, which is why it can be given to the depleted and the young.

**Korean and Japanese Kampo use (SBJ, shared East Asian lineage).** As *cheonma* (Korea) and *tenma* (Japan) the same tuber is used for headache, vertigo and "dizziness with heaviness of the head," and Korea in particular developed large-scale cultivation on inoculated oak logs. The indications track the Chinese ones closely — this is one materia medica shared across a region, not three independent traditions, and the honest reading treats them as one attested lineage.

**Folk / cultivator's tradition (FMT).** Because wild Tian Ma was scarce and its fungal partnership was not understood, the tuber was long treated as almost mythic — "hard to obtain, easy to counterfeit." The folk knowledge that *is* verifiable and load-bearing is preparatory rather than doctrinal: **raw Tian Ma is acrid, unpleasant and hard on the stomach, and is traditionally steamed (or boiled) and dried before use.** Steaming is not cosmetic; it is the standard processing step that renders the drug palatable and stable, and it is reflected in the pharmacopoeial preparation.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** The wind-extinguishing doctrine maps onto real chemistry with unusual cleanness. "Internal wind" is the phenomenology of **uncontrolled neuronal firing and cerebrovascular instability** — seizure, tremor, migraine aura, the spinning of vertigo, the surge of a hypertensive head. Tian Ma's principal actives, **gastrodin** and its aglycone **p-hydroxybenzyl alcohol** (with 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde), act on exactly those axes: they raise the seizure threshold, damp neuronal over-excitation, protect neurons under ischaemic and oxidative stress, and relax vascular tone. "Extinguishing wind" and "raising the GABAergic seizure threshold / stabilising cerebral perfusion" are, to a first approximation, the same claim in two vocabularies. The symbolic layer — a plant that thrives in darkness by *containing* a destructive fungal force rather than fighting it in the open — is a neat mnemonic for a drug that settles rather than suppresses, but that is analogy, not evidence, and is flagged as such.

### Active Components

- **Gastrodin** (gastrodine; p-hydroxybenzyl alcohol 4-O-β-D-glucoside) — the marker and principal active; a phenolic glucoside that is hydrolysed in vivo to its aglycone.
- **p-Hydroxybenzyl alcohol (HBA)** — the active aglycone; crosses into the CNS more readily than the glucoside.
- **4-Hydroxybenzaldehyde, vanillin, vanillyl alcohol** — related phenolics contributing to the sedative/anticonvulsant profile.
- **Parishins (A/B/C)** — citric-acid-linked gastrodin conjugates that act as slow-release reservoirs, converting to gastrodin in vivo.
- **Gastrodia polysaccharides** — the other major fraction, associated with antioxidant and immunomodulatory activity.

According to PubMed, a comparative pharmacokinetic study found that giving parishin or whole *G. elata* extract — rather than pure gastrodin — produced a **longer elimination half-life and mean residence time** for circulating gastrodin, because the conjugates and polysaccharide matrix act as a depot (Tang et al., *J Ethnopharmacol* 2015, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2015.10.007)). This is a real pharmacological argument for using the processed whole tuber rather than isolated gastrodin.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Tian Ma is a **settling / anti-wind** agent — filed in the Stabilisation phase, not the activation phase. Its use case is the practitioner or patient presenting with **"too much internal movement": tension or vascular headache and migraine, positional or central dizziness and vertigo, tremor, and the agitated, over-firing nervous system** that classical practice calls ascendant Liver yang or Liver wind. In a modern cultivator-and-clinician frame it is deployed adjunctively (never as sole therapy) around **hypertension-associated headache/dizziness and post-stroke recovery**, on the strength of its neuroprotective and vasomodulatory chemistry.

The intended felt effect is a *lowering of the ceiling of excitability* — fewer spikes, less spin, a quieter head — rather than sedation into dullness. That distinction matters, and so does its shadow: pushed too far, or stacked with other CNS depressants, Tian Ma over-sedates.

**Cultivator's note.** Tian Ma cannot be grown like an ordinary crop: it requires a two-fungus relationship — a germination fungus (*Mycena*) to start the seed, then *Armillaria* to feed the growing tuber. Commercial production inoculates hardwood logs/billets with Armillaria and plants Gastrodia tubers against them in shaded, well-drained beds; harvest is of the underground tuber, which is then **steamed and dried immediately** to fix quality. Skip the steaming and you get an acrid, poorly-storing, low-value drug.

### Scientific Correlation

The preclinical evidence is **strong and mechanistically coherent**; robust human evidence is **thinner** and is the honest weak point.

- **Anticonvulsant / antiepileptic (preclinical — strong).** According to PubMed, gastrodin reduced seizure severity and protected hippocampal neurons in the rat lithium–pilocarpine temporal-lobe epilepsy model, and did so by **reversing degradation of the GABA_A receptor and enhancing GABAergic transmission** (Yang et al., *J Ethnopharmacol* 2021, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2020.113751)). This is a direct molecular correlate of "raising the seizure threshold" — i.e. extinguishing wind.
- **Neuroprotective / anti-inflammatory (preclinical — strong).** In a PTZ-induced epilepsy model, gastrodin reduced neuronal apoptosis, oxidative stress and inflammation by regulating the **NOX2/Nrf2 balance**, with molecular-docking evidence of gastrodin binding NOX2 (Zhou et al., *Neuropharmacology* 2025, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropharm.2025.110620)).
- **Broader neurobiology (review — supportive).** A neurology review catalogues gastrodin's neuroprotection after ischaemia and toxin exposure, reduction of amyloid-β and excess glutamate, elevation of BDNF and stimulation of neurogenesis, plus reported **clinical efficacy in post-stroke depression** and improvement of memory/learning (Berezutsky et al., *Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr* 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.17116/jnevro202212208127)). The post-stroke-depression claim is a genuine human signal but is reported second-hand in a review and should be treated as promising, not settled.
- **Ethnopharmacological consolidation.** A dedicated review confirms the classical indication set (headache, migraine, dizziness, epilepsy, infantile convulsion, tetany) and catalogues >81 compounds, while explicitly calling for **more human toxicological and clinical study** — i.e. the field itself flags the human-data gap (Zhan et al., *J Ethnopharmacol* 2016, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2016.06.057)).

**Honest grade.** Anticonvulsant, neuroprotective and GABAergic mechanisms: **strong preclinical, mechanistically specific.** Vasomodulatory/antihypertensive and cognitive claims: **plausible, preclinical-dominated.** Rigorous, large, placebo-controlled human trials for the headline indications remain sparse — a real limitation, and the wind-extinguishing doctrine should be presented as *supported at the mechanism level, under-tested at the bedside*, not as proven clinical fact.

**Null / deflation note.** The traditional list also includes asthma, paralysis and "immune dysfunction." These are not backed by the specific mechanistic evidence above and are **not** carried forward as endorsed Kenetica indications — they are legacy breadth of the classical drug, flagged and deflated.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Taste/nature:** sweet, neutral (SBJ).
- **Channel:** Liver.
- **Action:** extinguishes wind, stops tremor/spasm, calms ascendant Liver yang, unblocks the collaterals (for numbness/headache).
- **Kenetica reading:** a *ceiling-lowering settler* — reduces excitatory/vascular over-activity without heavy sedation, best matched to over-firing, spinning, agitated presentations.

### Contraindications

- **Over-sedation / additive CNS depression.** Tian Ma settles and, at higher doses or stacked with sedatives, alcohol, benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants, can cause drowsiness and blunting. Do not combine casually.
- **Antiepileptic and antihypertensive therapy.** Because its mechanisms overlap with these drug classes, use alongside prescribed anticonvulsants or antihypertensives only under clinician supervision — it is an adjunct, never a replacement, and monitoring is required.
- **Deficiency without wind.** Classical caution: not indicated for headache/dizziness arising from pure blood/yin deficiency without a "wind" pattern.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, paediatric use.** Traditionally given to children for convulsions, but modern controlled safety data are limited; use in pregnancy, lactation and young children only under professional guidance. [USER: verify]
- **Raw tuber.** Do not use unsteamed — it is acrid, gastric-irritant and off-specification.
- **Quality/adulteration.** Historically counterfeited; source verified, steamed, correctly-identified tuber only.
