# Honey, Bhramara (Bhramaram Madhu) — Sushruta Samhita

## *Honey of the Bhramara bees, one of the eight honeys of Sushruta; the translator says only "bees of the Bhramara species" and no species is identified; the English prints "Bhramaram" and "Bhr^maram"; the honeybee identification is ours, of low confidence* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta Samhita only, among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The English text is Bhishagratna's translation, Sutra ch.45, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`. The honey group opens (line 1076) "Honey is sweet, and leaves an astringent after-taste. It is parchifying, cold, stomachic, cosmetic, tonic, light, softening, palatable, liquefacient (Lekhanan), and fermenting (Sandhanam)." The metrical text then says (lines 1093-1102): "Eight different kinds of honey are commonly used such as, the Pauttikam, Bhramaram, Kshaudram, Makshikam, Chhatram, Arghyam, Auddalakam and Dalam." The translator's footnote (lines 1120-1142) names the bee or hive of each kind.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The translator's footnote (lines 1123-1124) gives the second kind: "The kind of honey obtained from hives of bees of the Bhramara species is called Bhramaram." The text on the kind is brief (lines 1111-1113): "Honey, known as the Bhramaram, is extremely heavy owing to its extremely sweet taste and slimy character." No further property, disease or caution is printed for it. The chapter's general rules apply to every kind. Honey "is not collected from the flowers of any particular species" but from flowers and plants that are incompatible in nature, taste, potency and effect; "for these reasons, and further from the fact of it being prepared by poisonous bees, honey becomes positively injurious after contact with heat or fire, and accordingly the use of hot or boiled honey is forbidden" (lines 1183-1192, with the metrical verses following). Fresh honey is "constructive and aphrodisiac" and old honey "astringent and liquefacient" (lines 1165-1169).

### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms honey is mostly the sugars fructose and glucose with water, and small amounts of enzymes, organic acids and pollen-derived material; the composition varies with the flowers and the bee. These are well-established general facts, not read in the source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Nothing specific. It is "extremely heavy", sweet and slimy.
- **What the text does not say.** No indication, no dose, no preparation, and no species of bee.

The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation for this honey beyond the general rules on honey. The chapter's rules: do not heat or boil honey; honey with rain-water is injurious (all kinds, except Arghya, lines 1203-1206); and for emetic purposes honey may be given with a hot substance, since the aim is that it be thrown up (lines 1208-1213). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Honey is a sweet food made by bees from nectar; the Sushruta text's claim that it becomes injurious when heated is not a modern pharmacological finding, and heating honey is not generally regarded as poisonous. Honey must not be given to infants under twelve months (a general statement, not from the source). No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (ch.45).** Rasa: extremely sweet. Qualities: extremely heavy, slimy (lines 1111-1113). No *virya*, *vipaka* or *dosha* effect is printed for this kind.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The Bhramara honey is described in the dietetic chapter by taste, potency and effect on the *doshas*. It is not named in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter and no *ojas* claim is made for it. The honey group's own sentences call honey "tonic" and say that fresh honey is "constructive and aphrodisiac" (lines 1076-1088, 1165); these are group statements and are not carried over to a single kind. The only property printed is its heaviness.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing here for a cultivator beyond the name and the one property.

### Contraindications

- **Heated honey.** The text forbids hot or boiled honey and says it can be fatal like poison (lines 1183-1200).
- **Infants.** Honey is not given to infants under twelve months; this is a general statement, not from the source.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The kind of bee is the translator's footnote and the English prints the Sanskrit names only; no species of bee is identified by us beyond *Apis*-type honeybees in general, which is itself ours.
