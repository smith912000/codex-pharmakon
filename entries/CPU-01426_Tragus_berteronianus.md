# Tragus berteronianus — Tragos (Dioscorides 2-115, 4-51)

## *Tragus berteronianus* (the Dioscorides translators' suggested identification; carrot seed grass) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** This plant is named only by Dioscorides (DMM). The binomial is the suggestion printed on the SUGGESTED lines of the translation by Osbaldeston and Wood (2000), not Dioscorides' own: "Tragus berteronianus — Carrot Seed Grass", with the note "see 2-115" at 4-51. The two chapters headed TRAGOS describe different things: 2-115 is a grain, compared with *chondrus* and *zea* among the cereals; 4-51 is a leafless seashore shrub with red kernels. The one binomial therefore merges two forms of the same Greek word and the identity is doubtful. [USER: verify] Source: `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).**

*2-115 TRAGOS* (heading at line 5468; Book Two, among the grains). The whole chapter: "Tragus is somewhat similar in shape to chondrus [below] but is much less nourishing than zea [above] because it has much chaff. As a result it is hard to digest and softens the bowels."

*4-51 TRAGOS* (heading at line 8446; Book Four, Other Herbs & Roots; the text runs across a page break at lines 8449 and 8452). "Tragus grows particularly near the sea. It is a little shrub, on the ground, somewhat long, not large, about twenty centimetres tall or more. It has no leaves, but on the branches there hang (as it were) many little red kernels about the size of wheat, sharp on the top, especially astringent to the taste. Ten kernels of the seed of this (taken as a drink with wine) help the abdomen and women having their discharges [menstrual flow]. Some also beat it and make it into tablets for storage to use later." Other names: *scorpion*, *traganos*.

The preceding chapter, 4-50 *tragion alterum*, is a separate plant (glossed *Artemisia vulgaris*) and is not attributed to this row.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides records a poorly nourishing, bowel-softening grain (2-115) and an astringent seed for the abdomen and for women's discharges (4-51), as his claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

*4-51:* "Ten kernels of the seed of this (taken as a drink with wine)"; some beat it into tablets to store. This is the only quantity printed. *2-115:* a food, no preparation given. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** 4-51 prints the kernels as "sharp on the top, especially astringent to the taste". 2-115 prints the grain as hard to digest and bowel-softening. No warming, cooling or degree is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. 4-51 is a short discipline of attention: seashore habitat, low habit, no leaves, red wheat-sized kernels, astringent taste, and storage as tablets. 2-115 places the grain among cereals by comparison with *chondrus* and *zea* and judges it a poor food. No status is assigned. The binomial is a translators' gloss merging two chapters. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the source read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. This is a short record of an unsettled identity, and nothing is to be done with it.

### Contraindications

The identity is doubtful and no safe preparation can be stated. 4-51 is recorded as acting on women's discharges; do not use in pregnancy. The traditional uses are not advice.
