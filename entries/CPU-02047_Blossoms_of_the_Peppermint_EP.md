# Blossoms-of-the-Peppermint [EP] — "Blossoms-of-the-Peppermint" (Bryan 1930, English rendering of the Papyrus Ebers)

## *The flowers of a mint-like aromatic herb that Bryan's English calls "Peppermint"; the Egyptian word is not printed in the text on disk and no species is given. The identification is unresolved, at low confidence: modern peppermint (Mentha x piperita L.) is a hybrid of recent European origin and cannot be what an Egyptian scribe meant, so Bryan's word is an English label for some fragrant herb, perhaps a mint (Mentha), perhaps not. "Mint-of-the-Mountains" and plain "Peppermint", which occur elsewhere in Bryan, are separate items and are not folded in* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Ebers papyrus among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry, and the text is a modern English rendering, not the hieratic: Bryan 1930, `Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`. The name index (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`) holds the row "Blossoms-of-the-Peppermint, Bryan 1930, Ch. VI Plant Remedies (line 1319)", with the note "Bryan's English identification".

**Egyptian (EP).** In Bryan's alphabetical list of "recognizable Plants" (Chapter VI; intro at lines 1231-1233) the entry "Blossoms-of-the-Peppermint" (line 1319) comes after "Papyrus-plant" and before "Bark-of-the-Pomegranate". The list has no plain "Peppermint" item. Bryan prints no Egyptian word, no source plant and no grade.

The remedy in which the blossoms occur is a poultice for the tapeworm (Bryan's chapter on indigestion and the worms; lines 1984-1992). Bryan has just said that the tapeworm "proved more obstinate" than the roundworm, that "wormcakes were recommended first", then a draught, "and desperate cases could resort to a poultice". The poultice is headed "TO DRIVE OUT THE DISEASES CAUSED BY THE TAPEWORM" and lists: "Resin-of-Acanthus, Blossoms-of-the-Peppermint, Wild Lettuce, t'as-plant", with the direction "Stir, mix into one, and apply as a poultice to the ..." (the line continues past the cut at line 1992 and the object is not read here). No quantity is printed.

*Items with similar names, not folded.* Plain "Peppermint" is an ingredient in other remedies in Bryan (lines 1667, 1825, 3677 and 3754 are ingredient lists; at line 2518 a "REMEDY TO CAUSE A WOMAN TO BE DELIVERED" is "Peppermint. Let the woman apply it to her bare posterior"; at line 3088 coryza is treated by "rubbing the Nose with Peppermint-in-Dates"). "Mint-of-the-Mountains" is a separate item in the list (line 1305). Whether these name the same plant as the blossoms is not stated in the text on disk. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** One ingredient of an external poultice "to drive out the diseases caused by the tapeworm" (line 1988).
- **What the text does not say.** What the blossoms do by themselves, which plant they come from, or any quantity.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this poultice. Intestinal worms are a medical condition that needs proper diagnosis and treatment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the sources read is the poultice: the four ingredients "stirred, mixed into one", and applied (lines 1984-1992). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify against a scholarly edition before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Aromatic volatile oils from mints are used today as flavours and in some topical products; they are not recognised as a treatment for tapeworm, and an external poultice would not reach a tapeworm in the gut. The papyrus is better read as a record of what the scribe tried than as a drug trial. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian (EP).** *(c) Absent.* The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution. The blossoms are one ingredient in a poultice for the tapeworm, and nothing in the lines read places them in the plural person (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) or presents them with *heka*. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** One fragrant flower in a four-ingredient poultice for worms, under an English name that cannot be literally correct. There is nothing here for a cultivator to take or do, and the entry serves mainly as a warning that Bryan's plant words are hypotheses.

### Contraindications

- **Identity unknown.** Do not substitute modern peppermint oil or any other plant on the strength of this entry. Concentrated mint oils can be harmful, especially to infants and small children.
- **Worms.** A medical condition; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Companion ingredients.** The poultice also holds resin of acanthus and a plant Bryan leaves untranslated; nothing is known of them here.
