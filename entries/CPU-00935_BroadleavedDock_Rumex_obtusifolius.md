# Broad-leaved Dock / Bitter Dock
## *Rumex obtusifolius* L. | Family: Polygonaceae
**Tradition Tags:** DMM, CM, SBJ
**Energetic Classification:** Cooling, Drying (root: purgative-bitter); leaf: mucilaginous-cooling
**Training Phase:** Phase II — Clearing & Downward Drainage (bowel, "blood-cleansing" register)

---

### Origin & Tradition

Broad-leaved dock is two remedies wearing one name. Above ground it is the child's-playground plant — the broad leaf slapped onto a nettle sting. Below ground it is a member of the *Rumex/Rheum* anthraquinone family, sharing chemistry with rhubarb and yellow dock, and used as a mild bowel purge and "blood cleanser." The tradition mostly ran the two together; Kenetica separates them, because one doctrine is chemically real and the other almost certainly is not.

**Greco-Roman — Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides describes several *lapathon* (dock/sorrel) plants in *De Materia Medica* (Book II), noting the boiled leaves loosen the belly and the roots are more strongly active — decoctions used for the bowel, for itching/scaly skin conditions (applied as a wash), and for "hardness." The recurring theme is a downward, cleansing, mildly laxative action from the root and a softening, cooling action from the leaf. Classical species identification within the docks is loose, but the *functional* attribution — dock root as a gentle mover of the bowel and a remedy for eruptive skin — is stable across the Greek and Latin materia medica.

**Medieval/Early-Modern English — Culpeper (CM).** Culpeper places the docks under Jupiter and keeps the split use: the root "cleanses the blood," is "good for the itch," scabby and eruptive skin, and works as a gentle purge; the bruised leaf is a cooling application. The "blood-cleansing"/alterative framing — dock as a spring depurative that clears "foul humours" through the bowel and skin — becomes the dominant folk indication from this period forward.

**British / Northern-European folk (SBJ).** This is the living oral tradition: *"Nettle in, dock out."* The broad, cool leaf of *R. obtusifolius* is crushed and rubbed on the wheal raised by a stinging nettle (*Urtica dioica*), reliably found beside it. The same folk register keeps dock root as a bitter "spring tonic" and the leaf as a rough field poultice.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** Two doctrines, opposite fates.

*Doctrine 1 — downward drainage / purgation (root).* The "cleansing" and "blood-purifying" language maps onto real chemistry. Dock roots contain **anthraquinone glycosides** — chiefly emodin, chrysophanol, and physcion derivatives — the same purgative pharmacophore that defines senna, rhubarb, and cascara. The doctrine "it draws foulness downward and out" is a symbolic reading of a genuine mechanism: anthraquinone aglycones, released by colonic bacteria from their glycosides, act on the large-bowel mucosa to increase secretion and stimulate peristalsis, producing a laxative effect. The "blood cleansing" is the pre-modern language for bowel and biliary elimination; the mechanism underneath it is real even if the humoral framing is not.

*Doctrine 2 — the nettle-sting cure (leaf).* Here the doctrine and the chemistry part company. The relief a dock leaf gives on a nettle sting is best explained by **cooling, moisture, the mechanical act of rubbing, and expectation (placebo)** — not by any chemical neutralisation of the sting. There is no established "alkaline dock sap neutralising nettle acid" mechanism (nettle stings are a cocktail of histamine, serotonin, acetylcholine and formic-acid-like irritants, not a simple acid), and the folk claim of a specific antidote is chemically unsupported. Kenetica teaches this explicitly as a **deflated doctrine**: a real, kindly, self-limiting comfort with a non-chemical explanation.

A third folk claim — dock (especially its cousin yellow dock) as an **iron tonic for anaemia** — is also deflated here. Dock leaves do contain iron, but they are simultaneously high in **oxalate and phenolics**, both of which bind non-heme iron and impair its absorption. The plant is far more plausibly an *anti*-iron food than an iron tonic. This claim should be presented as folklore, not physiology.

**Do not confuse with Curly Dock (*Rumex crispus*).** *R. crispus* is the more commonly monographed medicinal dock (narrow, wavy-edged leaves; the standard "yellow dock" of Western herbalism). *R. obtusifolius* has broad, rounded-based leaves and is the classic nettle-companion. They share anthraquinone chemistry and are used interchangeably in folk practice, but they are distinct species and should be labelled correctly.

### Active Components

- **Anthraquinone glycosides / aglycones:** emodin, chrysophanol, physcion (and related) — concentrated in the root; the purgative fraction.
- **Phenolic compounds & flavonoids:** quercetin/kaempferol derivatives, catechins, proanthocyanidins; leaves are a documented high-phenolic, high-antioxidant tissue.
- **Oxalic acid / soluble oxalates:** notable in the leaves — the principal toxicological concern.
- **Tannins:** contribute astringency and, with oxalate and phenolics, to iron-binding.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Filed as a **Phase II clearing/downward-drainage agent**, dock root is the *mild* end of the anthraquinone purgative shelf — gentler and less reliable than senna or true rhubarb, used traditionally as a bitter, gently laxative "alterative" in eruptive-skin and sluggish-bowel pictures. Kenetica keeps three teaching points attached to the entry: (1) the root purgative action is real but should be used sparingly and short-term, exactly as for any stimulant laxative; (2) the leaf's nettle-sting use is a legitimate comfort measure taught *as* cooling/placebo, not as pharmacology; (3) the iron-tonic claim is retired. The leaf's genuine, evidence-backed property is antioxidant/antifungal phenolic content — interesting chemistry, but not the basis of the famous folk uses.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- **The anthraquinone purgative mechanism is real and well-characterised as a class.** Wang et al.'s review of anthraquinone pharmacokinetics documents that anthraquinone glycosides (emodin, chrysophanol, aloe-emodin, rhein, sennosides) are hydrolysed by intestinal flora to active aglycones and exert **purgation** among their principal actions ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2021.638993)). This is the chemistry the dock-root "downward cleansing" doctrine names. **Grade: STRONG** for the mechanism as a class; **MODERATE→WEAK** specifically for *R. obtusifolius*, since I retrieved no RCT of dock-root laxative efficacy this run — the inference is from shared chemistry, not from a dock trial.
- **The leaf's antioxidant/phenolic doctrine is confirmed.** Bineshian et al. showed *R. obtusifolius* leaf hydroalcoholic extract has strong DPPH-scavenging and reducing activity correlated with its phenolic/flavonoid content, plus anti-*Candida* activity (MIC ~200–250 µg/µL) (PMID 31278700, no DOI issued). Feduraev et al. confirmed high phenolic accumulation and antioxidant activity across *Rumex* organs, maximal in reproductive parts and leaves ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox8070237); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox11020311)). **Grade: STRONG** for antioxidant chemistry — but note this is *not* the property the famous folk uses invoke.
- **The nettle-sting cure is chemically unsupported — deflated.** I retrieved **no study this run** demonstrating a chemical neutralisation of nettle-sting mediators by dock sap. The plausible mechanisms (cooling, moisture, rubbing, placebo) require no special dock chemistry — any cool leaf would do comparably. **Grade: NULL** for the specific "dock antidote" claim; the *comfort* is real, the *chemistry* is not.
- **The iron-tonic claim is deflated.** Dock leaves are high in oxalate and phenolics, and phenolics/tannins are potent inhibitors of non-heme iron absorption — up to ~90% reduction in human studies (Tuntawiroon et al., [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/53.2.554); Glahn et al., [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/jf011046u)). The plant's own chemistry works *against* the iron-tonic doctrine. **Grade: NULL/CONTRARY** — folklore, not physiology.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Cooling and drying overall; root bitter and downward-draining (purgative), leaf cooling and mildly mucilaginous. Planetary attribution: Jupiter (Culpeper). Kenetica reads the root as a centrifugal/downward eliminative and the leaf as a cooling topical — two vectors in one plant.

### Contraindications

- **Oxalate load (leaves):** *R. obtusifolius* leaves contain soluble oxalates; avoid large or raw quantities. Contraindicated in oxalate kidney-stone formers and in renal impairment; large ingestions of oxalate-rich *Rumex* foliage can be toxic.
- **Stimulant-laxative (anthraquinone) cautions (root):** not for prolonged or habitual use — risk of dependence, electrolyte (potassium) loss, and colonic changes with chronic anthraquinone abuse. Contraindicated in intestinal obstruction, undiagnosed abdominal pain, inflammatory bowel disease, pregnancy, lactation, and young children.
- **Iron deficiency:** do not rely on dock as an iron source; its phenolics/oxalate impair iron absorption.
- **Drug interactions:** stimulant laxatives can potentiate potassium loss with diuretics and corticosteroids and affect cardiac glycoside sensitivity.
