# Abyssinian stone — حجر حبشي (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *A stone brought from the land of Abyssinia (al-Ḥabasha), yellowish, which on scraping gives a sharp-tasting powder "like milk"; the stone itself is not identified (confidence low). The identification as a mineral eye-remedy is the text's; any modern mineral name would be a guess and none is given* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *ḥajar ḥabashī* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 13437-13440). Under "what it is" (الماهية): حجر يجلب من بلاد الحبشة يضرب إلى الصفر يستحك منه حكاكة لاذعة للسان شبيه باللبن (13438-13439): "a stone brought from the land of Abyssinia, tending to yellow; what is rubbed off it is a scraping that stings the tongue, resembling milk". Whether "resembling milk" refers to colour or to the look of the powder is not stated.

Eye (أعضاء العين, 13439-13440): ينفع غشاوة العين إذا لم تكن مع ورم ورمد وينفع من آثار القروح فيها وينفع الظفرة اللينة: "it benefits the film (*ghishāwa*) of the eye when it is not with swelling and ophthalmia, and benefits the scars of ulcers in it, and benefits the soft pterygium (*ẓufra*)". No temperament, no source author, no preparation and no dose are printed. A page marker (`PageV01P496`) follows the entry.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Canon records a scraped stone powder for a film, ulcer scars and a soft pterygium of the eye. The Codex gives no protocol, cannot identify the material, and does not recommend putting any mineral powder in the eye. The terms of the *Canon* are the tradition's own and are not translated into modern diagnoses.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation implied is scraping the stone to a powder (13438-13439); how it was applied is not stated. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only: unidentified mineral dust is an eye irritant, and the stinging taste noted by the Canon agrees with that in kind. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Eye.** Do not put any unidentified mineral powder in the eye; infection and corneal injury. The text itself says the powder stings.
- **Identity.** Not established; do not substitute another stone. [USER: verify]
