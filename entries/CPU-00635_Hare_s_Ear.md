# Hare's Ear — Chai Hu (Shennong: "Radix Bupleuri"; Di Xun "Earth Fuming")

## *Bupleurum falcatum* (spine botanical; Yang 1998 prints only "Radix Bupleuri", naming the genus and no species) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM, CM, SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity warning.** Only the Shennong tag has a real locus, and it names the genus, not *B. falcatum*. A finished entry already exists for the same Chinese drug under another species: I_531, *Bupleurum chinense*, "Thorowax / Chai Hu" (CPU-00634), tagged CM, DMM, SBJ and FMT. The two rows probably overlap and may need to be merged. [USER: verify] The four Dioscorides chapters that the genus-level extraction attached to this row are not about *Bupleurum*, as shown below.

**Chinese — Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ).** In Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) the entry is at lines 1818 to 1824, on PDF page 47, within the run of pages headed **"Herbs: Superior Class"** (class opening at line 1383; running headers on the neighbouring pages at lines 1775 and 1856). It reads: "Chai Hu (Radix Bupleuri) is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats bound qi in the heart, abdomen, intestines, and stomach, drink and food accumulation and gathering, cold and heat, and evil qi. It weeds out the stale to bring forth the new. Protracted taking may make the body light, brighten the eyes, and boost the essence. Its other name is Di Xun (Earth Fuming). It grows in rivers and valleys." Yang's footnote 103 quotes Ye Gui (1667 to 1746 CE) on how Bupleurum "is able to lift and set free the gallbladder qi", and names the Da and Xiao Chai Hu decoctions. That is later commentary, not the classic, and is not relied on. The Chai Hu entry does not appear in `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` or `_sources/shennong.csv`; it was found by searching the text.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** No chapter for this plant was found. The four chapters cited for this row in `EXTRACTION_GAPS.md` are 1-4 KUPEIROS (line 3569, glossed *Cyperus*), 2-72 ENTERA GES (line 5223, earthworms), 2-158 KORONOPOUS (line 5893, buckshorn plantain) and 2-214 MUOS OTON (line 6463, chickweed and mouse-ear); none is a *Bupleurum*. The only mention of the genus in the text is a translator's note under 4-123 KAKALIA (line 9128, with the note at line 9131): "According to Sprengel, this is Bupleurum longifolum [Loudon]." That is a different species, a speculative identification of a plant glossed *Cacalia*, and the chapter says only that the root "cures coughs and roughness of the throat" (line 9132). It is mentioned for completeness and not attributed to this row. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and no chapter for this plant was found. Nothing in the four cited chapters concerns it.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The tag rests on a spine row, not on any list row or text read. No attestation and no cultivation framing was found.

**Chinese — Shennong (SBJ).** *(a) Attested, in the upper grade, for the genus as printed.* The entry sits under the printed heading "Herbs: Superior Class" (line 1383; running headers at lines 1775 and 1856). The preface (lines 352 to 355 and 383 to 384) says of that class: "There are 120 superior class medicinals which are used as sovereigns. They mainly nourish life and correspond to heaven. They are nontoxic and taking them in large amounts and for a long time will not harm people. If one intends to make one's body light, boost the qi, prevent aging, and prolong life, one should base [one's efforts] on the superior class." This is the *san pin* upper grade (上品). The entry itself says "Protracted taking may make the body light, brighten the eyes, and boost the essence." Yang renders the Chinese as "essence", and the character is not shown in the text read, so this entry does not equate it to *jing*. The claim is for "Radix Bupleuri" as printed, and not for *B. falcatum* in particular.

**Practitioner's note.** In the Chinese scheme this is an upper-grade drug, a grade of long-term nourishing, but the grading belongs to the genus-level drug Chai Hu, and the tag for *B. falcatum* depends on a species identification the text does not make. Nothing read supports a particular long-term regimen or dose. The Dioscorides and Avicenna tags are unsupported.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Do not treat the classic's uses as advice, and do not self-prescribe long-term use on the strength of an upper-grade label. Seek a qualified practitioner, and seek ordinary medical care for abdominal pain, fever or digestive illness. Species of *Bupleurum* are numerous, and the drug should not be substituted by wild collection.
