# Katphala — Kaṭphala, Kaṭu-phala, Somavalka, Kumbhī, Bhadra (Sanskrit); Kaphal, Box Myrtle, Bayberry (Kaphal)

## *Myrica esculenta* Buch.-Ham. ex D.Don | Family: Myricaceae
**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā), CS (Caraka Saṃhitā); Bhāvaprakāśa and later Nighaṇṭus
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa — Kaṣāya (astringent), Tikta (bitter), Kaṭu (pungent); Vīrya — Uṣṇa (heating); Vipāka — Kaṭu (pungent); Guṇa — Laghu (light), Tīkṣṇa (sharp/penetrating), Rūkṣa (dry). Pacifies Kapha and Vāta; may aggravate Pitta owing to its uṣṇa-tīkṣṇa nature.
**Training Phase:** Clearing / Mobilising — a respiratory and ENT decongestant used to break stagnation, not to nourish.

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** Katphala's officinal part is the **bark (tvak)**, a warming, sharp, astringent-pungent drug directed at the head, throat and chest. In the classical framework it is a **Kapha-hara** decongestant: employed for *kāsa* (cough), *śvāsa* (dyspnoea/breathlessness), *pīnasa/pratiśyāya* (rhinitis, catarrh), *svarabheda* (hoarseness), and diseases of the *kaṇṭha* (throat) and *nāsā* (nose). Suśruta's tradition uses it in gargles (kavala/gaṇḍūṣa), nasal applications, and as a fumigant/dhūma for upper-respiratory and ENT complaints. Its sharp warmth makes it a natural agent for liquefying and expelling accumulated Kapha in the head and chest.

**Caraka Saṃhitā (CS) & Nighaṇṭus:** The broader corpus additionally lists Katphala among agents for *jvara* (fever), *arśas* (haemorrhoids), * āmadoṣa*, and as a *dīpana-pācana* (kindling/digestive) drug — consistent with its uṣṇa-tīkṣṇa profile. Bhāvaprakāśa fixes the bark as the drug of record and reiterates the throat/respiratory emphasis. The name *Kaṭu-phala* ("pungent fruit") records the sharp taste; the fruit itself is edible (hence *esculenta*) and eaten as a sub-Himalayan hill fruit, but the **medicinal drug is the bark, not the fruit**.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Warm Astringent of the Threshold:** Katphala sits at a doctrinally interesting junction. It is simultaneously **astringent** (kaṣāya — a taste that contracts, holds, dries) and **hot-pungent** (uṣṇa-kaṭu-tīkṣṇa — tastes that open, move, penetrate). In dravyaguṇa logic these usually pull in opposite directions, and a substance that holds both is prized precisely for the **threshold work** of the head-and-throat: it must simultaneously *dry* the wet stagnation of Kapha catarrh and *open/expel* it. The plant's ecological signature reinforces the reading — a hardy sub-Himalayan tree of cold, misty mid-altitude slopes, its warming bark is the tradition's answer to the cold-damp Kapha conditions those very climates produce. In Kenetica it is filed as a **head-and-throat clearing agent**: a mobiliser of the uppermost srotas, a substance of the passageways (nose, throat, voice) — the gates through which breath and speech pass. Its domain is the threshold between inner and outer air.

### Active Components
Bark constituents reported for *M. esculenta* include the diarylheptanoid/aryl compound **myricanone** and the flavonoid glycoside **myricitrin** (with its aglycone **myricetin**), plus the pentacyclic triterpenoid **myricadiol** and its acetate derivative **3-acetylmyricadiol** (see Scientific Correlation). The bark is rich in condensed tannins (the material basis of the strong astringency), together with proanthocyanidins and phenolic acids. This tannin-plus-triterpenoid-plus-flavonoid matrix underlies the reported astringent, anti-inflammatory and antioxidant behaviour.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica
Katphala is positioned as an **upper-respiratory and ENT clearing agent** — the reference drug for cold-damp (Kapha-type) congestion of the nose, throat and chest: productive cough, catarrhal rhinitis, hoarseness and sore/inflamed throat. As a warm astringent it both dries secretion and mobilises it for expulsion, and its documented anti-inflammatory action supports its use in inflamed throat tissue. It is a clearing-phase, short-course agent — used to break a congestive pattern, not as a long-term tonic (its uṣṇa-tīkṣṇa nature makes prolonged high-dose use Pitta-provoking).

### Scientific Correlation
According to PubMed:

- **3-Acetylmyricadiol**, a pentacyclic triterpenoid isolated from the **bark of *M. esculenta***, showed dose-dependent **anti-inflammatory activity** in LPS-stimulated RAW 264.7 macrophages, inhibiting nitric oxide, IL-6 and TNF-α (up to ~52–63% inhibition at 20 µM) — a mechanistic correlate of the traditional throat/respiratory anti-inflammatory use. Ahmad et al., 2022, *Comb. Chem. High Throughput Screen.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/1386207324666210319122650).
- **Myricitrin**, a flavone abundant in the root/bark of *Myrica* species including *M. esculenta*, is reviewed as an **antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and anti-nociceptive** agent with cytoprotective effects across cardiovascular and neural models — supporting the antioxidant/anti-inflammatory character of the drug matrix. Zhang et al., 2020, *Curr. Stem Cell Res. Ther.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/1574888X14666181126103338).

**Evidence grade — Low (preclinical / in vitro and review).** Both citations are mechanistic (macrophage assay; narrative review). No human clinical trial for the respiratory/ENT indication is cited here. The anti-inflammatory signal is consistent with, but does not clinically establish, the traditional throat-and-chest use.

### Traditional Energetic Classification
- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṣāya (astringent), Tikta (bitter), Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Tīkṣṇa (sharp), Rūkṣa (dry)
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha-Vāta-śāmaka (pacifies Kapha and Vāta); Pitta-vardhaka (may aggravate Pitta)
- **Dhātu (tissue) focus:** Rasa; acts on the *prāṇavaha* (respiratory) and upper channels
- **Karma (actions):** Kaphahara / Śvāsahara (relieves cough/dyspnoea), Kaṇṭhya (throat-benefiting), Svarya (voice-improving in the congestive sense), Dīpana-Pācana (digestive-kindling), Śothahara (anti-inflammatory), Nāsā-vairecana (nasal decongestant/purge)
- **Srotas:** primarily Prāṇavaha (respiratory) and the head/throat srotas; also Annavaha

### Contraindications
- **Uṣṇa-tīkṣṇa nature:** contraindicated or used cautiously in Pitta-predominant states, hyperacidity, bleeding disorders driven by heat, and inflammatory GI conditions.
- Not for prolonged high-dose use — the sharpness can irritate mucosa and aggravate Pitta over time.
- **Pregnancy/lactation:** safety not established from the evidence here; avoid self-directed use `[USER: verify]`.
- Distinguish the **medicinal bark** from the **edible fruit**; dosing and effect are not interchangeable.
- Respiratory symptoms that are severe, febrile or persistent require medical evaluation, not self-treatment.
