### Origin & Tradition

Two bulbs sit in every Chinese pharmacy under names that differ by one syllable, and the difference between them is the whole entry.

**Chinese Medicine (CM) — 浙贝母, *Zhè Bèi Mǔ* ("Zhejiang Mother-of-Shellfish")** *(⚠️ code-gloss correction 2026-08-10: in this project **CM denotes Avicenna / Unani**, not "Chinese Medicine" — the label below is legacy drift from an old scheme in which CM meant "Chinese Materia Medica". The Chinese content here is genuine; whether the **CM tag itself** belongs on this entry is under audit and has NOT been ruled.)*

*Bèi mǔ* (贝母) means, literally, "shellfish mother" — the scale-leaves of the bulb overlap like the valves of a bivalve, and the name is a morphological description that predates any pharmacology. The genus name *Fritillaria* runs the same trick in Latin: *fritillus*, a dice-cup, for the chequered tessellation of the flower. Both naming traditions saw a container and named the plant for it. Chinese doctrine then read that container as a *store* — and what the lung stores, pathologically, is phlegm.

The classical function statement for Zhe Bei Mu is precise and three-limbed: **清热化痰** (*qīng rè huà tán*) — clears heat, transforms phlegm; **開鬱散結** (*kāi yù sàn jié*) — opens constraint, dissipates nodules. A 2025 study, citing the *Shennong Bencao Jing* and the *Bencao Zheng*, summarises it as "clearing heat, resolving phlegm, suppressing cough, detoxifying, dissipating nodules, and resolving abscesses" (Zhu et al., *J Ethnopharmacol*, 2025). Attested indications: acute cough with thick yellow sputum; wind-heat and phlegm-heat patterns; and the nodule cluster — 瘰癧 (*luǒ lì*, scrofula), 癭瘤 (*yǐng liú*, goitre and neck masses), and 乳癰 (*rǔ yōng*, breast abscess/mastitis).

**The Chuan/Zhe distinction (FMT — Formulary & Materia Media Taxonomy)**

*Chuan Bei Mu* (川贝母, *Fritillaria cirrhosa* D. Don — see its own entry) and *Zhe Bei Mu* are not interchangeable, and the entire clinical skill of this drug is knowing why:

| | **Chuan Bei Mu** (*F. cirrhosa*) | **Zhe Bei Mu** (*F. thunbergii*) |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Sweet, slightly bitter, slightly cold | **Bitter, cold** (markedly) |
| Action | Moistens, nourishes Lung yin | **Clears heat, drains, disperses** |
| Cough type | Chronic, dry, deficient, unproductive | **Acute, productive, thick yellow sputum, excess** |
| Nodules | Weak | **Strong — this is its signature action** |
| Price | Expensive, wild-adjacent, scarce | Cheap, cultivated at scale |

The mnemonic: **Chuan moistens a dry cough; Zhe attacks a hot one.** Give Zhe Bei Mu to a chronic dry deficient cough and you drain a patient who needed moistening. This is not a nuance. It is the difference between the drug working and the drug harming.

**Sacred / Bodily Jurisprudence (SBJ) — the eighteen incompatibles** *(⚠️ code-gloss correction 2026-08-10: **SBJ denotes the Chinese *Shennong Bencao Jing* stratum**, not "Sacred / Bodily Jurisprudence" — legacy drift.)*

Zhe Bei Mu is named in the **十八反** (*shí bā fǎn*, "Eighteen Antagonisms") — the oldest formal drug-interaction table in continuous clinical use anywhere. The rule: *Bei Mu* must not be combined with **aconite** (乌头/附子, *Wū Tóu* / *Fù Zǐ*). This is not a vague caution. It is a hard prohibition inside a named, memorised, versified jurisprudence, and it has survived for roughly a millennium on clinical observation alone.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

The doctrine here is not a signature doctrine — it is a **rheological** one, and that is exactly why it survives the translation better than most.

"Transforms phlegm" (化痰) is not a metaphor for spiritual cleansing. It is a claim about *material state*: something viscous and adherent should become thin and mobile so it can leave. That is a physical hypothesis, and physical hypotheses can be tested. The 2021 quality-control study states the mechanism the tradition was pointing at in exactly these terms — an expectorant works either by making "sputum thin, reduce its viscosity, and be easy to cough up," or by accelerating "the movement of mucous cilia in the respiratory tract" (Shi et al., *J Ethnopharmacol*, 2021). Ninth-century Chinese phlegm doctrine and twenty-first-century mucokinetics are describing the same variable.

"Dissipates nodules" (散結) is the second doctrine and it maps onto a different chemistry entirely — cytotoxic and anti-proliferative rather than mucolytic. The classical texts assigned scrofula, goitre, and breast abscess to this drug not because of the cough action but because of a separate observed effect on *masses*. Modern work has followed exactly that thread into breast cancer pharmacology.

"Cold" (寒) maps onto something real too, though not thermally. A drug that reduces the inflammatory heat-signs of an acute productive respiratory infection *behaves* as an antipyretic-adjacent agent. The doctrinal label is a behavioural summary, not a temperature claim.

**Where the doctrine must be deflated:** the theory says Zhe Bei Mu "detoxifies" (解毒) and treats abscess by resolving toxin. There is no retrieved molecular correlate for "toxin" as a category. And the nodule doctrine's ambition — that a bitter cold bulb dissolves a goitre or a breast mass — vastly outruns the evidence, which is in-silico and preclinical only. The tradition was right that this bulb does *something* to proliferating tissue. It was not right that this constitutes a treatment for a neck mass.

### Active Components

The active fraction is the **isosteroidal (5α-cevanine) alkaloids**, and they are quantitatively identified in the retrieved literature:

- **Peimine (verticine)** — the principal isosteroidal alkaloid; antitussive and expectorant activity demonstrated in mouse models.
- **Peiminine (verticinone)** — the close congener; same activity class.
- **Imperialine**, **ebeiedine**, **ebeiedinone**, **hupehenine**, **isoverticine** — the wider cevanine panel used to fingerprint *Beimu* species.
- **Zhebeininoside** and **imperialine-3-β-D-glucoside** — two newer steroidal alkaloids proposed as quality markers distinguishing processing methods, both with demonstrated antitussive effect (Shi et al., 2021).
- **Hapepunine** — flagged in network-pharmacology work as a candidate anti-TNBC constituent (Zhu et al., 2025); computational only.

Note that these are **isosteroidal** alkaloids — nitrogen embedded in a steroid-derived skeleton. They are not tropanes, not isoquinolines. Their smooth-muscle activity is not an anticholinergic story borrowed from another family; it is its own pharmacology.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Zhe Bei Mu is the codex's **acute-hot-productive** respiratory agent, held in strict opposition to its own sibling.

**Indicated pattern:** cough that is *loud, wet, hot, and recent*. Thick sputum that is yellow or green and hard to raise. A patient who is not depleted. The action wanted is drainage and thinning — the sputum should become mobile and leave.

**Contra-indicated pattern:** cough that is *dry, chronic, weak, and depleting*. That patient needs Chuan Bei Mu, and giving them Zhe Bei Mu drains fluid from a body already short of it. In Kenetic terms this is a **directional error**, the most common error class in phlegm work: correct organ, correct symptom, inverted vector.

**The nodule application** is retained in the codex at **archival/adjunct status only** — recorded because the classical indication is unambiguous and because modern oncology has taken an interest, *not* because there is evidence a practitioner should act on it. Any neck, thyroid, or breast mass is a referral, not a prescription. The historical indication is a research lead, not a permission.

**Phase III placement** reflects that this drug's difficulty is not toxicity-in-isolation but **differentiation** — the practitioner must be able to read excess from deficiency and heat from dryness before touching it, and must know the aconite prohibition cold.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

**Antitussive / expectorant activity — Grade: MODERATE preclinical, NO human trials retrieved.**
- Verticine, verticinone, zhebeininoside, and imperialine-3-β-D-glucoside all showed antitussive effect in an ammonia-induced mouse cough model against a dextromethorphan positive control, with significantly reduced phenol red content in the treated group (the expectorant readout) — Shi et al., *J Ethnopharmacol* 2021 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2021.114670) (PMID 34653522).
- **The strongest correlation in this entry:** crude alkaloid and water extracts of Zhe Bei Mu (and three other *Beimu* herbs) produced dose-dependent **tracheobronchial relaxation** in rat isolated trachea and bronchi pre-contracted with carbachol, and the activity-weighted total alkaloid content **correlated with the relaxation effect at r > 0.9, p < 0.001** across 23 *Fritillaria* species — Wu et al., *J Ethnopharmacol* 2017 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2017.08.027) (PMID 28842340). This is a genuine dose–response chain: named alkaloid → measured concentration → measured smooth-muscle effect. The classical "transforms phlegm, stops cough" claim has a mechanism underneath it.
- **Honest limit:** every one of these is animal or ex-vivo. I retrieved **no** randomised controlled trial of Zhe Bei Mu in human cough, bronchitis, or any respiratory condition. A rat trachea is not a patient. Grade the antitussive doctrine as *mechanistically supported, clinically unproven*.

**Pharmacokinetics — Grade: ESTABLISHED (animal), with a finding worth flagging.**
- Peimine and peiminine showed **significant sex-dependent** pharmacokinetics, tissue distribution, and urinary excretion in Sprague-Dawley rats after oral *F. thunbergii* extract, with slower elimination and higher blood/tissue levels in males — attributed to sex-dependent drug-metabolising enzyme and P-glycoprotein expression — Chen et al., *J Ethnopharmacol* 2012 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2012.09.054) (PMID 23123795). If this translates to humans, the *same dose is not the same exposure* between patients. It has not been shown to translate.

**Nodule / anticancer doctrine — Grade: WEAK. Computational only.**
- A multi-omics/network-pharmacology study identified 42 bioactive constituents and 148 candidate TNBC targets, prioritised CXCR4 as a core autophagy-related target, and predicted strong hapepunine–CXCR4 binding — Zhu et al., *J Ethnopharmacol* 2025 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2025.120600) (PMID 40953776). **This is an in-silico study.** Docking scores are hypotheses. The same paper's network-toxicology arm predicted **hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity risks** for the constituents — a prediction, but one pointing the same direction as the classical caution. The classical "dissipates nodules" doctrine is currently supported by *molecular docking*, which is to say: supported by nothing that has met a cell, let alone a patient.

**The aconite incompatibility (十八反) — Grade: MECHANISM PLAUSIBLE, and this is the finding that should change practice.**
- Investigating the *Fritillaria*–*Aconitum* incompatible pair via P-glycoprotein: when *Fritillaria* was combined with *Aconitum*, the **toxic aconite alkaloid benzoylmesaconine showed higher intestinal permeability** in a rat single-pass intestinal perfusion model. Peimine, peimisine, and imperialine were identified as the **P-gp-inhibiting** constituents, and benzoylmesaconine was confirmed as a **P-gp substrate** — Liu et al., *Evid Based Complement Alternat Med* 2021 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/8351717) (PMID 34650616).
- **Read what this says.** A prohibition recorded roughly a thousand years ago on the basis of clinical disaster has a coherent modern mechanism: the fritillary alkaloids inhibit the efflux transporter that normally limits absorption of the aconite toxin, so co-administration raises systemic exposure to a cardiotoxic alkaloid. The classical rule was a **pharmacokinetic drug–drug interaction** observed centuries before the word existed. This is one of the strongest doctrine↔mechanism correspondences in the codex — and note that it is a correspondence about *harm*, not efficacy. The tradition's most reliable knowledge is often its knowledge of what kills.
- **Honest limit:** rat perfusion model, single study retrieved. Mechanism plausible, not proven in humans. The prohibition stands regardless — the evidence bar for *not* combining two drugs is properly low.

**Cardiotoxicity — Grade: ASSERTED, NOT SUBSTANTIATED IN THIS RUN.** I searched for and did **not** retrieve primary evidence characterising peimine/peiminine cardiotoxicity or human *Fritillaria* poisoning. The caution below is retained on precautionary and classical grounds. [USER: verify — the cardiotoxicity claim needs a real primary source before it is stated as established fact.]

### Cultivation Register

**Later Chinese materia medica (TCM).** *Zhè Bèi Mǔ* 浙貝母 is a genuine Chinese drug, and after the 2026-08-10 audit it is the **only** tradition this entry claims: the previous tag line carried CM, SBJ and FMT, and all three were wrong — CM and FMT were the wrong traditions, SBJ the wrong stratum. The stratum point is the one that bears on cultivation. *Bèi mǔ* is not species-resolvable in the early record: the pre-Qin and Han "beimu" appears to have been *Bolbostemma paniculatum*, a plant of an entirely different family, and the southern/western division that eventually separated Zhe Bei Mu from Chuan Bei Mu begins only in the Ming, is clarified in the Qing, and is not fixed pharmacopoeially at species level until 1963. So no *san pin* grade can be claimed here: the drug as a species-level entity postdates the *Shennong Bencao Jing* that assigns those grades, and any citation of the classic as evidence for *F. thunbergii* specifically is an anachronistic species attribution. What the tradition does record is precise and narrow — a bitter, cold bulb that clears heat and transforms phlegm (清熱化痰) and dissipates nodules (開鬱散結), directed at excess-pattern heat-phlegm cough and at scrofulous lumps. That is a therapeutic standing, not a cultivation one. Cultivation status: **(b) framed** — securely attested in the post-classical Chinese materia medica, with no *san pin* grade available to it. (Src: Zhou et al., *Front Pharmacol* 2022;13:974054; Sun et al., *Zhonghua Zhongyiyao Zazhi* 2022;37(9), bencao textual research on 浙貝母.)

**Practitioner's note.** In the one tradition that genuinely holds it — Chinese materia medica — Zhe Bei Mu is a drug of clinical differentiation, not of cultivation: it is taken for a defined excess-heat-phlegm cough and stopped, never as something a well person builds vitality on, and its bitter cold draining action would harm the depleted patient it is wrongly given to. The most durable knowledge the tradition attaches to it is prohibitive — the millennium-old refusal to combine it with aconite, now with a plausible P-glycoprotein mechanism (Liu et al., 2021). For a cultivator that is the whole lesson: this is a bulb you learn to place precisely and to keep apart, not one you take to nourish life.

### Contraindications

⚠️ **ABSOLUTE — the Eighteen Antagonisms:** **Never combine with aconite** — *Wu Tou* (乌头), *Fu Zi* (附子), *Cao Wu* (草乌), or any aconite-containing formula. Classical prohibition; plausible P-gp-mediated mechanism (Liu et al., 2021). This is not negotiable and not a "relative" caution.

- **Cold-phlegm, damp-phlegm, deficiency-cold patterns:** contraindicated. A bitter cold draining drug worsens a cold pattern.
- **Dry, chronic, deficient cough:** contraindicated — this is Chuan Bei Mu's territory. Using Zhe Bei Mu here is the classic directional error.
- **Spleen deficiency with loose stool / poor appetite:** caution; bitter cold drugs injure Spleen yang.
- **Alkaloid overdose:** isosteroidal alkaloids are pharmacologically active compounds with a real dose ceiling. Overdose caution is retained on precautionary grounds. [USER: verify — I could not retrieve primary human toxicity data this run.]
- **Sex-dependent exposure:** rat PK shows large sex differences in peimine/peiminine handling (Chen et al., 2012). Unknown in humans. Do not assume dose equivalence across patients.
- **Predicted hepato-/nephrotoxicity:** network-toxicology prediction only (Zhu et al., 2025). Not observed data. Treat as a flag for caution in hepatic/renal impairment, not as an established finding.
- **⚠️ ADULTERATION AND MISIDENTIFICATION — the practical hazard.** The *Beimu* trade is a documented mess. Wu et al. (2017) quantified "significant variations of the types and quantities" of the seven major isosteroidal alkaloids across **23** *Fritillaria* species — different species, different alkaloid profiles, different potency. Zhe Bei Mu is cheap and Chuan Bei Mu is expensive, which creates a permanent economic incentive to sell the former as the latter. Meanwhile, *Fritillaria* bulbs have been confused with genuinely dangerous look-alike bulbs in other contexts. **Source from a supplier who can produce a species-verified certificate of analysis.** An unverified *Beimu* bulb is an unknown drug.
- **Pregnancy / lactation:** insufficient data retrieved. Avoid.
- **Any nodule, goitre, thyroid, or breast mass:** this is a **referral**, not an indication. The classical text saying otherwise is a historical record, not a licence.
