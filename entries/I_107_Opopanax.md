### Botanical Identity
Two distinct plants are sold under the name "opopanax":

1. **True opopanax**: *Opopanax chironium* (Apiaceae) — large perennial umbelliferous herb 1–2m; native to eastern Mediterranean and western Asia; root and stem exude an oleo-gum-resin with a distinctive pungent-aromatic odour; this is Dioscorides' opopanax.

2. **Sweet myrrh / Bisabol opopanax**: *Commiphora erythraea* var. *glabrescens* (Burseraceae) — small African tree related to myrrh (*C. myrrha*); resin has a sweeter, more balsamic character; used interchangeably with true opopanax in the incense and perfume trade from antiquity onwards.

The Codex treats the resin material (pharmacological properties) as the entry subject, noting the botanical duality. Both resins contain overlapping pharmacologically active compounds (sesquiterpenes, coumarins, gum arabics).

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Dioscorides / Greco-Roman (DMM)
Book III, Chapter 48: "Opopanax — the best comes from Boeotia and Arcadia." Applications: softens hardened masses (tumours, lymph nodes); resolves nerve pain; antispasmodic; for sciatica; in compound preparations for skin conditions; draws out thorns and splinters; for coughs. Dioscorides emphasises its nervine and resolvent applications.

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine / Ibn Sīnā (CM)
*Jāwishīr* — warming, drying. Applications: for paralysis and weakness of nerves; antispasmodic for epilepsy; for sciatica; carminative; dissolves hardened masses; for respiratory conditions. Ibn Sīnā's neurological applications (paralysis, epilepsy, sciatica) are more systematised than in DMM.

### 2.3 Ebers Papyrus (EP)
Aromatic resins consistent with opopanax appear in EP compound formulas; the Egyptian trade access to East African and Arabian resins made opopanax-type materials available.

### 2.4 Assyrian Medical Tablets (AT)
Aromatic resin preparations in AT for neurological conditions (nerve pain, spasm) and wound healing are consistent with opopanax identification.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Analgesic / Nerve Pain (β-Caryophyllene Primary)

**β-Caryophyllene**: Selective CB2 (cannabinoid receptor type 2) agonist — one of the few plant-derived CB2 agonists that is not a cannabinoid:
- CB2 activation → reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine release in peripheral nervous tissue → analgesic
- Reduces neuroinflammation (microglial activation suppression)
- Anti-allodynic effects in animal models of neuropathic pain

This explains the sciatica/nerve pain application in DMM and CM: β-caryophyllene acts on the peripheral and central CB2 receptors in the pain processing pathway.

### 5.2 Antispasmodic (Coumarins + Sesquiterpenes)

**Osthole + umbelliferone + isabolene**:
- Calcium channel antagonism (osthole — well-studied mechanism for this coumarin)
- Smooth muscle relaxation in intestinal and neurological smooth muscle
- The antiepileptic application in CM maps to the CNS calcium channel effects of ostehole-type coumarins (anticonvulsant in animal models)

### 5.3 Resolvent / Anti-fibrotic

**Opopanaxin + sesquiterpene lactones**:
- Dissolve fibrin deposits and fibrous adhesions (fibrinolytic-like activity via protease activation)
- Anti-inflammatory in lymph node tissue (reduces chronic lymphadenopathy)
- The "softening hardened masses" application in DMM and CM maps to anti-fibrotic mechanisms

### 5.4 Antimicrobial / Wound Healing

**Falacarinol + falcarindiol + β-caryophyllene**:
- Antimicrobial (gram-positive; antifungal)
- Anti-inflammatory in wound tissue
- Wound healing support via anti-inflammatory + antimicrobial combined action

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Formal RCTs**: Absent. The pharmacological mechanisms (β-caryophyllene CB2 analgesia, osthole antispasmodic) are well-characterised in vitro and in animal models. β-Caryophyllene has attracted significant research interest as a non-psychoactive CB2 agonist with potential for neuropathic pain and inflammation.

**Historical pharmacopoeial listing**: Opopanax was listed in the British Pharmacopoeia through the 19th century, primarily as a compound antispasmodic (in Compound Opopanax Pill for hysteria, flatulence, and spasmodic conditions). It was removed when galbanum and assafoetida largely replaced it.

**Aromatic/incense use**: Sweet myrrh (*Commiphora erythraea*) remains an important commercial incense ingredient; the aromatic tradition is unbroken from antiquity.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The all-healer juice**: The etymology *opopanax* (juice + all-healer) represents the ancient pharmaceutical aspiration of the universal remedy — the herb so therapeutically broad that it addresses every complaint. The Greek *panax* root (same as Panax ginseng) carries this meaning across cultures. Dioscorides, who was generally cautious about hyperbolic claims, used this name for a resin he valued specifically for its neurological applications. The "all-healer" designation may reflect the convergent traditional observation that opopanax resin was genuinely useful across multiple distinct conditions — nerve pain, spasm, hard masses, skin disease, coughs — without a single dominant application.

**Chiron's herb**: Some classical sources attribute the discovery of opopanax to Chiron the centaur — the mythological teacher of medicine who taught Asclepius (the god of medicine) the art of healing. The specific attribution of opopanax to the centaur who taught medicine is a mythological encoding of the herb's perceived identity as a fundamental medicine — a plant whose properties were so important they belonged to the origin story of medical knowledge itself.

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM (cross-reference)**: Acrid, bitter; warm. Channels: Liver (肝), Kidney (腎). Moves Blood, relieves constraint, softens hard masses, resolves phlegm.

**Ayurvedic (cross-reference)**: Pungent; warming. Reduces Vata ↓↓, Kapha ↓. Parallels *Hingu* (assafoetida) in antispasmodic and carminative action. Not a classical Ayurvedic herb.

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Pregnancy**: Emmenagogue/uterotonic potential from sesquiterpene content — avoid
- **Apiaceae allergy**: *Opopanax chironium* is in the carrot family; potential cross-reactivity with carrot, celery, fennel, coriander
- **Anticoagulants**: Coumarin content (umbelliferone, osthole) — additive with warfarin; monitor INR
- **Dosage**: Pungent and irritant at high doses — gastric irritation; start at low doses
