# Selinusian earth — Selinousia, Dioscorides 5-175

## *An earth from Selinus, called in the herbal "glittering, white and brittle" and quickly dissolved by wet, whose properties are given only by reference to the earths above it; the translator gives no identification ("UNKNOWN"), and we offer none* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-175, *Selinousia* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 10739-10741; the Codex's name-index row in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` gives locus "5-175", class mineral, identification uncertain; list row `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 900; the book index sends the reader to page 827). The whole chapter was read. It is the shortest kind of entry in the book (line 10741):

"Selinusian earth does the same things [as those above]. The best glitters extremely, is white and brittle, and very quickly dissolved if it gets wet."

The translator's heading line is "UNKNOWN" (line 10740). The chapter therefore gives a place-name (in the adjective), a rule for the best (it "glitters extremely", is "white and brittle"), one behaviour (it dissolves quickly in wet), and no uses of its own. The bracket "[as those above]" is the translator's gloss.

**What "those above" are.** The chapters directly above are the earths: Eretrian earth (5-171, line 10718), Samian earth (5-172, line 10722), Lithos Samios (5-173, line 10732) and Chian earth (5-174, line 10735). The text does not say which of them it means, and the translator's bracket does not either. For the reader's convenience, and without choosing among them: Samian earth (5-172) "stops the spitting up of blood, and is given with flowers of wild pomegranate for women's menstrual flows", is smeared on with water for inflammation "from stones [urinary, kidney]" and of the breasts, "represses sweating" in water, and "helps those bitten by venomous creatures, and those who have taken a deadly medicine [antidote]" (lines 10726-10730). Chian earth (5-174) "has the same properties as Samian earth", "makes the face and the whole body without wrinkles and clear, and used in a bath instead of saltpetre [potassium nitrate] it scours" (line 10738). These are the properties of the neighbours, quoted as context; they are not asserted for Selinusian earth, whose chapter says only "the same things". Which earth is meant is not settled by the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

**The place name.** The translation's gazetteer defines "SELINUS, SELINUSIAN" as "a Greek city, now in ruins, near Castelvetrano on the southwest coast of Sicily" (line 3527). The chapter itself does not say where the earth is dug.

**Identity.** The translator offers no identification ("UNKNOWN"), and the Codex list marks the identification uncertain. A white, brittle, glittering earth that dissolves in water might be several minerals; the chapter gives too little to name one. None is chosen here. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated. The earth is unidentified, so no composition is asserted. In general terms, "earths" in this part of the herbal are fine mineral materials of varied chemistry, and the translator's own notes at 5-171 to 5-174 variously suggest alkaline earths, bicarbonate of soda and clay minerals; none of those suggestions is made for this chapter.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The herbal records an unidentified white earth from Selinus as doing "the same things" as the earths above it. Since the properties are borrowed from neighbours and the earth cannot be identified, the Codex states no use. Mineral earths of unknown composition should not be swallowed, bathed in or applied to wounds or the eyes on this record. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives no preparation, no way of burning or washing (these are given for some of the other earths, for example Eretrian earth at 5-171), no vehicle and no quantity. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] As a general fact, white mineral earths vary widely in composition, some being clays, some carbonates and some alkaline or metal-bearing; unidentified material cannot be assumed safe.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM).** The chapter prints no quality for this earth. No quality is carried across from the neighbouring chapters, whose own qualities (for example "astringent and cooling" for the stone of 5-173, line 10734) belong to those substances.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. The chapter's one contribution to the discipline of attention that the herbal does offer is a sensory assay: the best earth glitters extremely, is white and brittle, and dissolves quickly in wet.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation. The chapter is a single sentence of assay attached to a borrowed list of uses, for a material nobody can now identify. It is not a recommended practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Unidentified mineral.** Do not swallow, bathe in or apply an unknown earth. Composition and contaminants are unknown.
- **Borrowed uses.** The neighbouring earths' uses include pregnancy-related and bite-related conditions, which are medical matters.
- **Eyes and open wounds.** Non-sterile mineral powders are unsafe there.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
