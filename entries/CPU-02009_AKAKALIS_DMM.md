# AKAKALIS — Akakalis, "Acacalis" (Dioscorides 1-118)

## *The fruit of a shrub growing in Egypt, "similar to the fruit of the myrica" and mixed into eye medicines; the translator marks the chapter UNKNOWN and gives no suggested binomial, so the plant is unidentified and the identification is open: no species is proposed here* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The attestation is Dioscorides, *De Materia Medica*, in the English translation on disk. A search of the other texts on disk for *Akakalis* and *Acacalis* found nothing, and the name row (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 120) gives the identification as "UNKNOWN". The Codex's "check" for this row is "Unresolved (Akakalis)", which is confirmed.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter is 1-118, AKAKALIS, in Book One on aromatics and related plants, between 1-117 EREIKA (heath) and 1-119 RAMNOS TRISSUS (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, lines 4413-4416). The translator's line under the heading reads "UNKNOWN" (line 4414). The whole text of the chapter (line 4415) is: "Acacalis is the fruit of a shrub growing in Egypt, similar to the fruit of the myrica [1-116]. A dilution of it is mixed with eye medicines which are good for sharpening the sight." The book's page index lists "AKAKALIS 117" (line 12006).

What the chapter says is short: where the shrub grows (Egypt), what part is used (the fruit), a comparison (with the fruit of myrica), a preparation (a dilution) and a use (as an addition to eye medicines "good for sharpening the sight"). It names no tree, gives no description of leaf or flower, and gives no property.

The comparison sends the reader to 1-116, MURIKE, MURRIS (line 4395), where Dioscorides describes myrica as a well-known tree of marshy ground and standing waters, some planted in gardens in Egypt, which "bears fruit like a gall ... unequally astringent to the taste, and used instead of galls in medicines for the mouth, eyes and spitting of blood" (lines 4404 ff.). That is the myrica chapter and says nothing of Akakalis, but it shows what a reader of the time had in mind by "the fruit of the myrica": an astringent, gall-like fruit. The Codex does not infer that Akakalis is astringent; the chapter does not say. The Codex has separate entries for Tamarisk (CPU-00991, with a Dioscorides attestation) and other plants, and no identification is made between them and this row. [USER: verify.]
