# Jew's stone — حجر اليهود (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *Ḥajar al-Yahūd: a small nut-like stone with parallel and cross lines that leave bright scale-like facets. The usual modern identification is the fossil spine of a sea urchin (*lapis judaicus*); that is ours, from the name and the shape described, of medium confidence* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

*What it is.* الماهية: كالجوز الصغير إلى طول يسير يقطعها خطوط تأتي من طرفها وخطوط آخرى معارضة لها متوازية فيتقاطع ويبقى منها كالتفاليس الصغار لامعة (13399-13400): "like a small walnut, a little elongated, cut by lines that come from its end and other lines crossing them in parallel, so that they intersect and leave small bright scale-like facets". The word *tafālīs* is read here as "little scales" (our reading). [USER: verify]

*Digestive organs.* يضعف المعدة ولا يوافقها ويسقط الشهوة (13400-13401): "it weakens the stomach, does not suit it, and drops the appetite".

*Excretory organs.* ينفع من حصاة الكلية ويخرجها (13401-13402): "it benefits kidney stone and brings it out". The draught is "ten *anūlūsāt*" (the unit is not explained here) in hot water (13402). Someone claimed it helps bladder stone, "and it is not so", and it is said to cut the rectum (13402-13403).

### Active Components

The passage names no constituent. If the modern identification as a fossil sea-urchin spine is right, the material is mainly calcium carbonate, a general fact about such fossils; that is not stated by the text. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Canon records a small lined stone drunk in hot water for kidney stone, with the warning that it harms the stomach and is said to cut the rectum. Historical record only. The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this material.

### Preparation & Dosing

A draught of "ten *anūlūsāt*" in hot water for kidney stone (line 13402); the unit is not explained in the passage. No other dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Under the digestive organs, Avicenna says the stone weakens the stomach, does not suit it and drops the appetite, so the stomach and appetite are the claimed sites. Under the excretory organs, the kidney is the claimed target: it is said to benefit kidney stone and bring it out. The bladder is named for bladder stone, a claim the text itself records and then rejects ('it is not so'). The rectum is named as a site the stone is said to cut. The draught is taken in hot water. The passage gives no temperament, so no hot or cold quality is mapped. In modern anatomy the claimed sites are the stomach, the kidney and ureter, the bladder, and the rectum, which together form the digestive and urinary tracts. The shape described has scale-like facets; the identity as a fossil is ours and only of medium confidence.

### Contraindications

- **Stomach.** The source says it weakens the stomach and drops appetite.
- **Rectum.** The source says it is said to cut the rectum.
- **Kidney and bladder stone.** Medical conditions; use proper care.
