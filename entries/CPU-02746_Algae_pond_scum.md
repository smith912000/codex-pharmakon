# Algae / pond scum — طحلب (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *Ṭuḥlub: a freshwater and sea growth of the kinds the Canon calls "river", "sea" and "lens-like" (ʿadasī); the lens-like kind is, on the usual reading, duckweed. The identification to algae and duckweed is ours, from the Arabic names and description, and is of medium confidence; no genus is given* | *(Volume IV — Fungal/Algal, shelved in Volume I)*

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *ṭuḥlub* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 13537-13543).

*What it is.* معروف والنهري مائي أرضي والبحري أشد قبضا (13538): "well known; the river kind is watery-earthy, and the sea kind is stronger in astringency". Rock *ṭuḥlub* is said to be *ḥarrār al-ṣakhr* "and we have mentioned it" (13538-13539): a cross-reference to another entry, not read here.

*Temperament and action.* الطبع: بارد (13539): "cold". الخواص: حابس للدم في كل موضع طلاء والبحري أشد (13539-13540): "stops blood in every place, as a coating, and the sea kind is stronger".

*Swellings.* يجعل على الأورام الحارة والحمرة والنملة وكذلك العدسي من الطحلب مع السويق (13540-13541): "it is put on hot swellings, on *ḥumra* (erysipelas-like) and *namla* (a spreading skin sore), and likewise the lens-like kind of *ṭuḥlub* with barley meal (*sawīq*)".

*Joints and nerves.* On hot gout and hot pains of the joints, and إذا أغلي بالزيت العتيق لين العصب (13541-13542): "when boiled in old oil it softens the nerve".

*Excretory organs.* يضمد به قيلة الأمعاء فيضمرها (13542-13543): "a poultice of it is applied to intestinal hernia (*qīla*) and it shrinks it". No source author is named in the lines read.

### Active Components

The passage names no constituent. In general terms freshwater algae and duckweed are mostly water with cell-wall polysaccharides and some protein; sea algae are rich in mucilaginous polysaccharides and, depending on the species, iodine. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Canon uses *ṭuḥlub* as a cooling external application for hot swellings, hot joint pain and hernia, and as a styptic coating. The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend collecting pond growth for use on the skin or wounds. The terms of the *Canon* (cold, astringent) are the tradition's own and are not translated into modern diagnoses.

### Preparation & Dosing

Preparations given: applied as a coating or poultice; the lens-like kind with barley meal; boiled in old oil for the joints (13540-13542). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Pond growth can carry bacteria and, in some blooms, cyanobacterial toxins; wild seaweed varies by species. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **CM.** *Cold* (بارد, 13539), with astringency (the sea kind "stronger", 13538) and a blood-stopping action (13539). No degree is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Avicenna / Unani (CM).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested in the passage read. The entry is organised by temperament and by organ, and says nothing of the *ruh*, of innate heat or radical moisture, or of any life-lengthening status. Nothing is imported from outside the passage. The *Canon* is read here from the Arabic text on disk, not from the English adaptation.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do. A cold external remedy for hot swellings; it is not a food or a cultivation material in this text.

### Contraindications

- **Wild-collected algae.** Contamination and cyanobacterial toxins in stagnant water; do not apply to broken skin. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The "river", "sea" and "lens-like" kinds are not tied to species by the text.
