# Cyrtomium rhizome — Guan Zhong 贯众 (Shennong bencao jing); aliases Guan Jie 贯节, Guan Qu 贯渠, Bai Tou 百头, Hu Juan 虎卷 and Bian Fu 扁符

## *Cyrtomium fortunei J.Sm., Fortune's holly fern, one of the ferns whose rhizome is sold as the Chinese drug guan zhong; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence at best, since the classic gives no usable description (its Wu Pu layer describes flowers and fruit, which a fern does not have) and Yang 1998's commentary says several different plants supply this one name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** The principal text read is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Its other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), also has the drug, as "Guan Zhong (Radix Aspidii Falcati)" (line 3453), and is read here as a second witness, attributed to Yang. The row began as an unverified placeholder, "Aspidium sp.", an old genus name for shield ferns. Other ferns are treated from other traditions in I_982 (male fern, *Polypodium filix-mas*, Dioscorides 4-186) and I_983 (*Polypodium lonchitis*); those entries do not cite the Shennong text, and the Codex does not equate their plants with this drug.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 贯众 in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 贯众 *guan zhong*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3347). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，微寒 ("bitter, slightly cold"); 主腹中邪热气 ("governs evil heat qi in the abdomen"), 诸毒 ("the various poisons"), 杀三虫 ("kills the three worms", a category the text does not define); aliases 一名贯节, 一名贯渠, 一名百头 (the *Yulan* writes 白, "white"), 一名虎卷 and 一名扁符; habitat 生山谷 ("grows in mountain valleys"). There is no 久服 clause. As printed, Sun's paragraph does not say "toxic"; Yang's English does (below).

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 3351) lists further aliases and the flavours as judged by several authorities: "Shennong and Qi Bo: bitter, toxic; Tongjun and Bian Que: bitter; one text: sweet, toxic; Huangdi: salty, sour; one text: bitter, non-toxic" (神农、岐伯：苦，有毒；桐君、扁鹊：苦；一经：甘，有毒；黄帝：咸，酸；一经：苦，无毒). It also gives the only description of the plant: 叶黄，两两相对；茎，黑毛聚生。冬夏不老。四月花，八月实，黑聚相连，卷旁行生 ("the leaves are yellow, paired, facing each other; the stalks have black hairs growing in clusters; it does not age in winter or summer; it flowers in the fourth month and fruits in the eighth, the black [fruits] joined in clusters, curling and creeping sideways"), and the harvest: 三月、八月采根，五月采药 ("the root is gathered in the third and eighth months, the 'medicine' in the fifth"). A fern does not flower or bear fruit in the botanical sense; this passage does not describe a fern as a botanist would, and the Codex does not resolve what the Wu Pu author saw. [USER: verify]

The Mingyi bielu layer (line 3353) adds the aliases 伯萍 and 药藻, the remark 此谓草鸱头 ("this is called 'grass owl-head'", not explained), the habitat 生元山及冤句、少室山 ("grows on Mount Yuan, at Yuanju and on Mount Shaoshi"), and the harvest: 二月、八月采根，阴干 ("the root is gathered in the second and eighth months and dried in the shade").

Sun Xingyan's note (案, lines 3355-3357) is philological and is the commentator's, not the Shennong text's. It cites the *Shuowen* (a plant name, character lost), the *Guangya* (贯节 is guan zhong), and the *Erya* with Guo Pu's gloss: 叶，圆锐；茎，毛黑。布地，冬夏不死 ("the leaves are round and pointed; the stalks have black hair; it spreads over the ground and does not die in winter or summer"). Sun proposes that the *Erya* should read "bian fu, stops; luo, guan zhong", because, he says, the classic gives 篇符 as an alias of this drug (据《经》云：一名篇符，即此也).

Yang 1998 (line 3453) renders the paragraph as "Guan Zhong (Radix Aspidii Falcati) is bitter, slightly cold, and toxic. It mainly treats evil heat qi in the abdomen and various toxins. It kills the three [kinds of] worms." and lists the aliases (Guan Jie, Guan Qu, Bai Tou, Hu Juan and Bian Fu). Yang therefore prints "toxic" where Sun's paragraph does not; the Wu Pu layer carries the toxic judgement in Sun's edition. His footnote 212 (line 3469) is commentary: "Several different plants supply this single-named medicinal depending on locale. Hsu identifies it as Rhizoma Blechni, while Bensky and Gamble identify it as anyone of Rhizoma Dryopteridis Crassirhizomae, Rhizoma Woodawardiae Unigemmatae, Rhizoma Osmundiae Japonicae, or Rhizoma Matteucciae Struthiopteridis." That note is why this identification is held with caution.

**Appendix.** An appendix of drug relations, later than the main text, says of it: 贯众 菌为使 ("guan zhong: jun is its envoy", line 4445, the second term as printed). It names guan zhong as the envoy of long dan (line 4311) and says that shu qi "dislikes guan zhong" (line 4449).

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the rhizomes of shield ferns such as male fern (*Dryopteris*) contain phloroglucinol derivatives that kill intestinal worms and are toxic to the person taking them; whether this is true of *Cyrtomium fortunei* in particular is not established by any source read here. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, slightly cold rhizome for abdominal heat, for "the various poisons" and for killing the three worms, and the Wu Pu layer notes authorities who call it toxic. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose and recommends no use. The "three worms" are a historical category and are not translated into a modern diagnosis.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparations in the sources read are the harvests: the root in the third and eighth months (Wu Pu) or the second and eighth months, dried in the shade (Mingyi, line 3353). No quantity, form or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] A fern rhizome used against worms must not be dosed from a historical entry.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Rhizomes of male fern and related shield ferns have a long history as worm remedies and are poisonous when taken in excess; they are no longer used this way in most medical systems because safer drugs exist. The classic's "three worms" and "poisons" have no direct modern equivalent. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味苦，微寒, "bitter, slightly cold" (line 3347). The Wu Pu layer records "bitter, toxic" (Shennong and Qi Bo), "bitter" (Tongjun and Bian Que), "sweet, toxic" (one text), "salty, sour" (Huangdi) and "bitter, non-toxic" (another text) (line 3351); the Codex does not settle the disagreement, and the weight of the named authorities is toward "toxic".

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph for 贯众 stands in the lower classic, whose preface says the lower medicines "treat disease, correspond to the earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to remove cold and heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and cure illness" (主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经; line 2935). The paragraph has no 久服 clause. The register is (b): the lower grade is not a life-nourishing frame. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 贯众 *guan zhong*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3347)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. This is a lower-class anti-worm and "poison" drug that several authorities in the Wu Pu layer call toxic and that the classic's own preface says is not for long use. The honest use of the entry is as a record.

### Contraindications

- **Toxic:** the Wu Pu authorities and Yang's English call the drug toxic; fern rhizomes used against worms can poison the person who takes them. Do not take any form of this entry's drug.
- **Identity:** several ferns are sold under this name (Yang's note 212); they are not interchangeable and the text describes no usable plant.
- **Intestinal worms and abdominal pain:** need medical assessment and modern treatment.
- **Appendix pairings:** the appendix says shu qi "dislikes" guan zhong (line 4449); this is a historical pairing and not a modern interaction list.
- **Pregnancy, nursing, children:** no safety information was read; do not use. [USER: verify]
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given.
