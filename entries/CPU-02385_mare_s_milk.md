# Mare's milk — Ekashapha milk, "the mare" (Sushruta Samhita)
## *The milk of a mare; Bhishagratna's English "mare" in the Sushruta list of the milks of animals "with unbifurcated hoofs (Ekashapha)", identification as *Equus caballus* milk ours, confidence medium (the text says "mare, etc.", so the passage may also cover other single-hoofed animals)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** On the project's spine (CPU-02385) only the *Sushruta Samhita* is attested for mare's milk, and no other text on disk was searched to find it. The row began on 13 October 2026 as an unverified placeholder from `_nonplant/nonplant_master.csv` (curated 8 October, confidence clear); this entry replaces it. The general milk entry (`III_285_Milk.md`) covers milk across traditions; this entry treats the mare's milk named in Sushruta.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The locus is *Sutrasthana* ch.45 (the chapter on liquids) in Bhishagratna's English, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`. The milk group opens (lines 413-416, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`): "The milk of a cow, she-goat, she-camel, ewe, she-buffalo, mare, she-elephant, or of a woman, is what generally comes to the use of man." The text then describes milk in general (lines 419-462) as "the white fluid essence of drugs and cereals ... the best of all nutritive substances", "heavy, sweet, slimy, cold, glossy, emollient, laxative and mild", and, among a long list of benefits, "rejuvenating and aphrodisiac" and one that "increases the duration of life" (lines 450-457). Those lines belong to milk as a group and are not specific to any one animal. The mare is named in that opening list at line 415. The animal-by-animal passage then gives, at lines 504-508: "The milk of a she-animal with unbifurcated hoofs (Ekashapha) such as, the mare, etc., is tonic, light, parchifying, sweet and acid in taste, leaving a saline after-taste, and proving curative in cases of rheumatism restricted to the extremities." The "etc." is in the English; which other animals it covers is not stated in the passage. Food chapter ch.46 uses the same term for "the horse and the mule etc." in the meat group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 647), which suggests that the mule is one of the "etc." animals, but that is our inference from a different chapter and the milk passage does not say it.

The chapter's general rules for milk also apply: milk milched in the morning is heavy and cold, milk milched in the evening is "refrigerant and eye-invigorating", and "Cold or unboiled milk is extremely heavy ... whereas by boiling it is freed from those injurious traits" (lines 523-540); "milk, which emits a fetid smell, or has become discoloured and insipid, or has acquired an acid taste and looks shreddy and curdled, or tastes saline, should be regarded as unwholesome and injurious" (lines 550-553).

### Active Components

The Sushruta text names no constituent. Milk in general is an emulsion of fat in a watery solution of lactose with proteins, minerals and vitamins; mare's milk is relatively low in fat and protein and high in lactose compared with cow's milk. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Rheumatism restricted to the extremities (line 507); the milk is called tonic and light.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation specific to the mare's milk and no duration.

Mare's milk is a food in some cultures and is sold as a product in some places. The Codex gives no protocol and does not translate "rheumatism restricted to the extremities" into a modern diagnosis.

### Preparation & Dosing

The passage on the milk of the Ekashapha animals gives no preparation and no dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] The general rules above (boiling, time of milking, spoilage) are for milk as a group.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Milk is a nutritious fluid; lactose intolerance and milk allergy are common, and raw milk can carry pathogens such as *Salmonella*, *Listeria* and *Brucella*, so heat treatment matters. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The group description of milk says it is "rejuvenating", "increases the duration of life" and "augments the quality of bodily albumen (Ojah)" (lines 450-461, Bhishagratna's words), which are the nearest terms in the chapter to *rasayana*-type language, but they are said of milk as a group in the chapter on liquids, and the mare's milk paragraph itself calls it "tonic" and names only a remedial use. No line read places the mare's milk in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter. No claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* is made for it specifically. The sattvic, rajasic and tamasic food lists are Samkhya and later categories and are not Sushrutan.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. The text names a remedial use for the milk of the single-hoofed animals and no cultivation purpose, and it gives no way of obtaining or preparing the milk. A cultivator should not draw a rejuvenation claim for the mare from the general praise of milk.

### Contraindications

- **Spoilage.** Sushruta's own rule: milk with a fetid smell, discolouration, an acid taste or curdling is unwholesome (lines 550-553).
- **Raw milk** carries infection risks; boil it, as the chapter itself advises.
- **Lactose intolerance and milk allergy.**
- **Identity.** The English "mare, etc." leaves open which animals are meant. [USER: verify]
