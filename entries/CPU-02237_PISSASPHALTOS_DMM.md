# PISSASPHALTOS [DMM] — pissasphaltos, "pitch-bitumen" (Dioscorides, De materia medica 1-100)

## *A natural bitumen that Dioscorides describes as smelling "of pitch mixed with bitumen" and found as knobs cast up on the shore near Apollonia; the translators suggest "Pissasphaltos — Bitumen — Asphalt — Mineral Pitch". Identification: a natural pitch-bearing bitumen; basis, Dioscorides's own description and the translators' suggestion; confidence medium. It is not equated with asphaltos (1-99) or naphtha (1-101), which he describes separately* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** Bitumen and pitch fumes are irritant and are generally regarded as harmful to breathe, and Dioscorides's inhalation uses are not a recommendation. This entry is a historical record only.

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "PISSASPHALTOS [DMM]"; this entry replaces it. The text is the Osbaldeston and Wood English, `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The spine's existing Asphalt / Mumia entry (CPU-00295, `II_110_Asphalt_Mumia.md`) is tagged Avicenna, Assyrian and Ebers, not Dioscorides; this entry is kept apart.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter. Book 1, chapter 100 (line 4281): "1-100. PISSASPHALTOS ... There is some called pissasphaltos found in Apollonia near Epidamnus, which is carried down from the Ceraunian mountains by the violence of the river and cast on the shore, growing into knobs which smell of pitch mixed with bitumen." The translators' heading gives "Pissasphaltos — Bitumen — Asphalt — Mineral Pitch [2]". The chapter has no property list of its own.

The shared properties. Chapter 1-101 (naphtha) is followed by a paragraph headed "Properties of Asphaltos" (line 4292) that covers the group, and ends: "Pissasphaltos can do as much as pix [organic pitch] and bitumen mixed together." The paragraph says of bitumen in general (the group of 1-99, 1-100, 1-101): it "is able to repress inflammation, close open cuts and sores, and disperse and soften, and is effective for congested vulvae and prolapse when applied, smelled, or the smoke inhaled"; the smoke "reveals those troubled with epilepsy if the smoke is inhaled like [burning] gagate [jet] stone"; taken as a drink with wine and castoreum it "draws out the menstrual flow"; it "helps obstinate coughs, and those troubled with asthma and difficult breathing, also snakebites, hip pains and pains of the side"; "given to those troubled with colic as a catapotium [pill]"; "taken as a drink with vinegar it dissolves clots of blood"; melted with barley water as a suppository for dysentery; "wrapped around teeth it soothes toothache"; and a plaster with barley meal, wax and saltpetre is applied for podagra, arthritis and lethargy. Whether each use belongs to pissasphaltos alone is not stated; the closing sentence says it does as much as pitch and bitumen together.

The neighbouring chapters. 1-99 Asphaltos: Judean bitumen is best, found in Phoenicia, Sidon, Babylon and Zacynthus; 1-101 Naphtha: "strained Babylonian asphaltus, white in colour".

### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms, a bitumen is a mixture of heavy hydrocarbons, and pitch is a tarry residue from resinous woods or from distillation; a "pitch-bitumen" is a mixture of the two kinds of material. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Do not inhale, swallow or apply.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The group paragraph above: wounds and sores, coughs and breathing, colic, dysentery, toothache, gout and joint pain, menstrual flow, epilepsy test.

### Preparation & Dosing

The group paragraph names a drink with wine and castoreum or with vinegar, a pill, a suppository in barley water, a plaster with barley meal, wax and saltpetre, and smoke. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Bitumen and tar contain polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, some of which are carcinogenic, and their fumes are irritants to the airways; neither material is a modern medicine for internal use. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** No warming, cooling or other quality is printed for pissasphaltos in the translation. The group paragraph says bitumen "disperses and softens". No other classification is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is attributed to this material. What the chapter offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention: provenance (Apollonia, cast up by the river), a sensory assay (it "smells of pitch mixed with bitumen"), and a comparison with its neighbours; the adjacent chapter warns that black foul bitumen is adulterated with added pitch (1-99).

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator beyond the habit of naming source and smell. The material is an irritant and no use is recommended.

### Contraindications

- **Fumes and contact.** Bitumen and pitch smoke are irritants; do not inhale or ingest. Skin contact may blister or sensitise.
- **Plaster with saltpetre.** The plaster in the text contains saltpetre and wax and is a historical formula only.
- **Pregnancy.** The text itself lists it as drawing out the menstrual flow; avoid. 
- **Epilepsy "test".** The inhaled-smoke test for epilepsy is not a diagnostic method.
- **Identity.** The deposit named is a local one; do not substitute any commercial bitumen. [USER: verify]
