# Tagara (Indian Valerian) — Tagarā, Nata, Vakra, Kuṭila, Sugandhā, Tagar

## *Valeriana jatamansi* Jones (≡ *V. wallichii* DC.) | Family: Caprifoliaceae (Valerianaceae s.l.)

**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā) · Ayurvedic classical materia medica · Himalayan aromatic-root ethnomedicine

**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa (taste): Tikta (bitter), Kaṭu (pungent), Kaṣāya (astringent) · Vīrya (potency): Uṣṇa (warming) · Vipāka (post-digestive): Kaṭu (pungent) · Guṇa (qualities): Laghu (light), Snigdha (slightly unctuous) · Doṣa action: Tridoṣa-śāmaka with emphasis on Vāta-Kapha-śāmaka (calms Vāta and Kapha).

**Training Phase:** Settling & consolidation phases — the nervine used to quiet an agitated, over-mobile nervous system so that integration can occur. A grounding herb for the come-down and the rest.

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** Tagara is a classical Ayurvedic aromatic root recognised as a *medhya* / nervine and calmative agent. In the classical corpus it is associated with *unmāda* (mental disturbance/insanity), *apasmāra* (seizure/epileptiform states), *nidrānāśa* (insomnia), and disorders of aggravated Vāta affecting the mind and nerves. It is grouped among the fragrant, Vāta-pacifying drugs used to steady the mind, promote sleep, and settle tremor and restlessness. Its warming, light, slightly unctuous nature makes it a corrective for the cold, dry, erratic quality of deranged Vāta in the nervous system.

**Nomenclatural note — the Tagara/Jaṭāmāṃsī neighbourhood:** Classical "Tagara" is identified with *Valeriana jatamansi* (the Indian valerian), a Himalayan valerian distinct from — though repeatedly confused with — *Nardostachys jatamansi* (spikenard, "Jaṭāmāṃsī"). Both are aromatic Himalayan roots used as calmatives, and the shared epithet *jatamansi* has produced centuries of overlap in the trade and the texts. In this entry the referent is fixed: *Valeriana jatamansi* Jones, synonym *V. wallichii* DC.

**Himalayan ethnobotany:** *Valeriana jatamansi* is a perennial of the temperate Himalaya, prized for its aromatic rhizome and roots. Across Himalayan folk practice the root is a standard remedy for nervous unrest, insomnia, hysteria, and cramp — the same nervine territory the classical texts assign it.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Root that Anchors the Wandering Mind:**
Tagara's symbolic identity is that of the *anchor for a mind that has slipped its moorings*. Ayurveda reads insomnia, anxiety, tremor, and mental agitation as the signature of Vāta — the wind principle — grown wild: light, cold, dry, mobile, erratic. The corrective must be its inverse in some measure while sharing its subtlety: a substance fragrant enough to reach the mind (aromatics are held to touch the *manas*), warm enough to counter the cold of anxious dryness, and grounding enough to still the wandering. The valerian root — dense, earth-bound, pulled from cold Himalayan ground, and carrying a heavy, insistent aroma — becomes the natural emblem of this settling. Where a stimulant scatters attention outward, Tagara draws it back down and in. In Kenetica it is filed as the **consolidation nervine**: the herb of the settling phase, taken after exertion, disturbance, or an intensive practice to return the system to rest, so that what was opened can close and integrate rather than fray. Its symbol is the tangled, fragrant root — *kuṭila* and *vakra*, "twisting" and "crooked," names the tradition gave it — that nonetheless holds fast in the ground: complexity that anchors rather than agitates.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Tagara is the **grounding nervine and sleep-settler** of the materia medica — deployed where an over-mobile, anxious, sleepless, or tremulous state (deranged Vāta of the mind) is the presenting picture. Traditional and preliminary experimental uses:

- **Anxiolytic / calmative** — reduction of anxiety-type behaviour (see Scientific Correlation).
- **Sleep support** (*nidrā-janana*) — the classical insomnia indication.
- **Nervine for tremor, restlessness, cramp** — settling of Vāta-driven agitation.
- **Adjunct in agitated mental states** (classical *unmāda* context).

Use-case framing: a **settling-phase agent**, best matched to the end of a working arc — the wind-down, the rest, the integration — rather than the opening.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- A controlled study in mice found that **compound *Valeriana jatamansi* Jones** produced a significant **anxiolytic effect** in the elevated plus-maze and light–dark box (increased open-arm entries/time and light-compartment transitions) **without sedative impairment of spontaneous locomotor activity**; the effect was reduced by the benzodiazepine antagonist flumazenil, implicating **GABA/benzodiazepine-receptor mediation** (You et al., *BMC Complement Altern Med*, 2012) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6882-12-223).

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical (animal behavioural pharmacology), and note the tested agent was a **multi-herb compound formula containing *V. jatamansi***, not the isolated single herb — so the effect is not cleanly attributable to *V. jatamansi* alone. The anxiolytic/GABAergic signal is suggestive and mechanistically framed, but **human clinical evidence for the single herb is not established here**. Treat the sedative/nervine claim as traditionally grounded and preliminarily supported, not clinically proven.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Tikta (bitter), Kaṭu (pungent), Kaṣāya (astringent).
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (warming) — counters the cold of anxious, dry Vāta.
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** Kaṭu (pungent).
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Snigdha (slightly unctuous) — the unctuousness distinguishing it from harshly drying bitters, allowing it to soothe rather than deplete.
- **Doṣa action:** Primarily *Vāta-Kapha-śāmaka* (calms Vāta and Kapha); described as broadly *tridoṣa*-balancing in its nervine role. Its warmth means excess can aggravate Pitta.
- **Karma (actions):** *Medhya* (mind-supporting), *nidrā-janana* (sleep-promoting), *vāta-anulomana* (regulates Vāta's downward flow), *śūla-praśamana* (antispasmodic/pain-settling), *dīpana* (mildly kindling digestion).

- Warming potency may **aggravate Pitta** / heat conditions if overused.
