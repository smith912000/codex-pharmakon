# Sardinian honey — MELI SARDOON (Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 2-102)
## *Honey from Sardinia, which Dioscorides says is bitter because the bees feed on wormwood; the Greek heading is MELI SARDOON and the translator's SUGGESTED name is "Sardinian Honey"; a regional honey, from bees kept or wild on the island, with no binomial in the text; the sense "Apis mellifera honey" is ours and of medium confidence* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** On the project's spine (CPU-02392) only Dioscorides is attested for Sardinian honey. The row began on 13 October 2026 as an unverified placeholder from `_nonplant/nonplant_master.csv` (curated 8 October, confidence clear); this entry replaces it. The general honey entry (CPU-00446, `III_13_Honey.md`) covers honey across traditions, and Dioscorides' main honey chapter (2-101) is treated there; this entry holds only the separate chapter on the honey of Sardinia.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The English is the 2000 edition of *De Materia Medica* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`). Book 2, chapter 102, headed *MELI SARDOON* with the translator's heading "SUGGESTED: Sardinian Honey" (line 5394; `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` row for "MELI SARDOON", locus "2-102 (line 5394)"), is a single sentence (line 5396): "The honey that is made in Sardinia is bitter because the bees feed on wormwood [3-26] yet it is good rubbed on the face for sunburn and spots." The bracketed reference is the translator's cross-reference to chapter 3-26, *APSINTHION*, which he glosses "Artemisia absinthium [Linnaeus] — Wormwood" (line 6750-6751). The chapters before and after are the general honey chapter, 2-101 *MELI* ("Honey"), and 2-103 *MELI PONTIKON*, the honey of Heraclea in Pontus (line 5397), which Dioscorides says makes those who eat it "beside themselves with sweating" (the separate row CPU-02376).

The introduction to the same edition lists "Sardinian honey" among the cleansing and beautifying lotions for the complexion used in Roman times (line 355). That is the editor's summary and is not a second attestation by Dioscorides; it agrees with his use on the face.

### Active Components

Dioscorides names no constituent beyond the bitterness, which he explains by the bees' feeding on wormwood. In general terms, honey is mainly the sugars fructose and glucose with water, small amounts of organic acids, enzymes and pollen; a bitter taste in a honey comes from the plants the bees visit, and wormwood (*Artemisia absinthium*) is known to contain bitter sesquiterpene lactones and a volatile oil that contains thujone. Whether the Sardinian honey carried any of these is not stated, and no analysis was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Honey applied to skin is a humectant and has antimicrobial properties from its sugar content and acidity. Some Sardinian honeys, such as the bitter strawberry-tree (*Arbutus unedo*) honey of the island, are known for a bitter taste. That point is general knowledge, not from Dioscorides, who gives wormwood as the cause. [USER: verify] Honey can cause an allergic contact reaction. There is no evidence retrieved that honey treats sunburn or "spots". No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. What the chapter offers is a short discipline of attention to provenance: the same substance, honey, is described by where it was made and what the bees fed on, and the place changes the taste and the use. The neighbouring chapters give the Attic honeys as the best (2-101) and the honey of Pontus as one that can make eaters sweat and lose their senses (2-103). The chapter assigns no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. The lesson that survives is Dioscorides' habit of asking where a honey came from and what the bees fed on, and the text itself records a cosmetic use. A cultivator has no use for it.

### Contraindications

- **Allergy** to bee products or to the plants in a honey.
- **Infants.** Never give honey to a child under one year (infant botulism); modern general knowledge, not from Dioscorides.
- **Identity.** The text says only "the honey that is made in Sardinia". Any modern bitter Sardinian honey sold under the name is not shown to be what Dioscorides meant. [USER: verify]
- **Wormwood** in large amounts is toxic, though the text does not claim that the honey carries it in any quantity.
