# I-248: Salvia rosmarinus / Rosmarinus officinalis (Rosemary)

**Codex Pharmakon Universalis — Volume I: Plant-Based Substances**
**Entry I-248 | Lamiaceae (Labiatae)**

---

## Section 1: Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Primary Binomial:** *Salvia rosmarinus* Schleid. (syn. *Rosmarinus officinalis* L.)
**Family:** Lamiaceae (Labiatae)

**Nomenclature note:** Reclassified from *Rosmarinus officinalis* to *Salvia rosmarinus* by molecular phylogenetics (2017); many sources still use *R. officinalis*. Both names are valid in current literature.

**Common Names:**
- English: Rosemary, Sea Dew, Compass Plant
- Latin: *Ros marinus* — "dew of the sea" (grows on Mediterranean coastal cliffs)
- Arabic: *Iklīl al-jabal* (إكليل الجبل) — "crown of the mountain"
- Persian: *Romārin* (Ibn Sīnā)
- Hebrew: *Rozmarinus* (post-Roman loan)
- Ancient Greek: *Libanotís* (λιβανωτίς) — "frankincense-like" (from the resinous aromatic quality); also *Romanos*

**Morphology:** Aromatic evergreen shrub; 0.5–2 m; narrow needle-like leaves, grey-green above, white below; small blue-violet flowers; highly resinous, piney-camphoraceous aroma. Mediterranean coastal native; drought-tolerant; cultivated globally.

**Part Used:** Leaves and flowering tops (fresh and dried); essential oil; hydroalcoholic extract.

**Growth Region:** Native to Mediterranean basin; widespread in southern Europe, North Africa, Turkey. Cultivated globally for culinary and medicinal purposes. Major producers: Spain, France, Morocco, Tunisia.

**Convergence Score: 7/9**

| South American Indigenous / SA | — | Not indigenous to South America; absent |

**7/9 convergence** — restricted only by New World inaccessibility. All Old World traditions document rosemary for remarkably consistent indications (memory/cognitive, circulatory, respiratory, fumigant).

**Historical uses of note:**
- **Memory and scholarship:** Dioscorides, Ibn Sīnā, and European herbalists consistently link rosemary to memory enhancement — "there's rosemary, that's for remembrance" (Shakespeare, Hamlet IV.v). This 2,000-year tradition received partial modern validation.
- **Plague fumigant:** Burned in rooms of the ill during medieval European plague epidemics; carried in posies ("Ring Around the Rosie" — rosemary posy against plague smell/miasma). Antimicrobial air-purification rationale partly valid (1,8-cineole + camphor vapor).
- **Wedding tradition:** Mediterranean brides wore rosemary crowns (*corona romarin*) for fidelity and memory of vows — cultural convergence of cognitive and symbolic meaning.

## Section 3: Convergence Analysis

**Primary Convergence Domain:** Cognitive/memory enhancement; circulatory stimulant; nervine — consistent across all 6 confirming traditions in Old World.

**Secondary Convergence Domain:** Respiratory / antimicrobial fumigant — EP, AT, DMM, CM.

**Tertiary Convergence:** Antispasmodic; digestive carminative — CM, DMM, European.

**Mechanistic Coherence:**
The traditional "brain and memory" indications are now mechanistically validated — rosemary's AChE inhibition (carnosic acid, rosmarinic acid) and 1,8-cineole's established cognitive effects make the pre-pharmacological intuition pharmacologically accurate. Cultures working independently arrived at the same cognitive application because they were observing the same real effect.

**Energetic Classification:**
- ◉ Qi (functional): Primary — circulatory Qi activation; digestive; antispasmodic
- ☿ Shen (neuromodulatory): Strong secondary — cognitive enhancement; memory; AChE inhibition
- ◈ Jing (structural): Secondary — antimicrobial; antioxidant; anti-tumor

**Primary Tier: ◉ Qi + ☿ Shen** (circulation and cognition dual action)

**Thematic Archetype:** The Rememberer — cognitive activation, circulatory stimulation, and the plant that has carried the symbolic weight of memory across human cultures for three millennia.

## Section 4: Phytochemical Composition

### Essential Oil (0.5–2.5%)

**1. 1,8-Cineole (Eucalyptol) — 15–55% (chemotype-dependent)**
- Primary cognitive and respiratory active
- Mechanism: AChE inhibition (IC₅₀ ~40 μM); bronchodilation; mucolytic; antimicrobial
- CNS penetration: Crosses BBB rapidly; serum levels from inhaled cineole detectable in 20 minutes

**2. Camphor — 5–25%**
- CNS stimulant at low concentrations (cortical activation); convulsant at high concentrations (never use camphor-rich preparations in epilepsy)
- Circulatory stimulant: peripheral vasodilation; cardiac inotrope at therapeutic concentrations
- Contraindicated in pregnancy (uterine stimulant), infants

**3. α-Pinene — 5–25%**
- AChE inhibition; anxiolytic (via GABA-A); antimicrobial; anti-inflammatory

**4. Borneol — 2–10%**
- Mild analgesic; antimicrobial; sedative at high doses (paradox with camphor stimulant)

**5. Limonene, β-Pinene, Linalool — minor terpenes**

### Diterpene Phenolics (primary antioxidants; non-volatile)

**6. Carnosic Acid — 1.5–2.5% dry weight**
- Primary antioxidant; neuroprotective; Nrf2 activator → GSH upregulation
- AChE inhibition (IC₅₀ ~10 μM) — contributes to cognitive effects
- Anti-tumor: selectively toxic to cancer cells in vitro (NF-κB inhibition)
- Degradation product: carnosol (also active)

**7. Carnosol — 0.2–0.7%**
- Diterpene quinone; anti-inflammatory; anti-tumor; antioxidant
- NF-κB inhibition; induces Nrf2/HO-1 pathway; triggers apoptosis in cancer cells in vitro
- Anti-estrogenic: Potential in hormone-sensitive cancers (aromatase inhibition)

**8. Rosmarinic Acid — 0.5–3%**
- Caffeic acid ester; GABA-T inhibition; anti-inflammatory; antiviral; antioxidant (parallel to Lemon Balm; I-242)
- Synergistic with carnosic acid for antioxidant protection

### Other Constituents

**9. Ursolic Acid — 0.5–1.5%**
- Pentacyclic triterpene; anti-inflammatory; anti-tumor; anabolic (myostatin inhibition in animal models)

**10. Luteolin, Apigenin, Diosmetin, Hesperidin — flavonoids**

## Section 5: Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Acetylcholinesterase (AChE) Inhibition — Cognitive Mechanism
Multiple rosemary constituents inhibit AChE:
- Carnosic acid: IC₅₀ ~10 μM
- 1,8-Cineole: IC₅₀ ~40 μM
- α-Pinene: moderate AChE inhibition
- Rosmarinic acid: mild AChE inhibition
Combined: synergistic AChE inhibition increasing ACh availability at synapses → enhanced cholinergic neurotransmission → memory consolidation, attention, and recall enhancement.

This mechanism parallels pharmaceutical acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, galantamine) used in Alzheimer's disease, though rosemary acts through different binding sites and at lower potency.

### 2. Cerebrovascular Circulation Enhancement
- Camphor: peripheral vasodilation; possible cerebrovascular blood flow increase
- Borneol: vasodilatory effects documented in regional brain blood flow studies
- Overall: increased cerebral perfusion, which may contribute to acute cognitive performance effects observed in controlled studies

### 3. Nrf2 / Antioxidant Defense Pathway Activation (Carnosic Acid)
Carnosic acid and carnosol strongly activate Nrf2 (Nuclear Factor Erythroid 2-Related Factor 2), the master regulator of cellular antioxidant response:
- Nrf2 → HO-1 (heme oxygenase-1) upregulation
- Nrf2 → NQO1 (quinone oxidoreductase) upregulation
- Nrf2 → GSH (glutathione) synthesis upregulation
Net: enhanced cellular antioxidant capacity; neuroprotective; cancer-preventive; hepatoprotective

### 4. Anti-Inflammatory (Multi-Target)
- NF-κB inhibition (carnosol, carnosic acid, ursolic acid): reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine transcription
- COX-2 inhibition (carnosol, ursolic acid): reduces PGE₂
- 5-LOX inhibition (carnosic acid): reduces LTB4
- Net: comprehensive anti-inflammatory across enzymatic and transcriptional pathways

### 5. Estrogenic / Anti-Estrogenic (Carnosol)
- Carnosol inhibits aromatase → reduces estrogen synthesis
- May act as weak ER-β agonist in some contexts
- Relevant to hormone-sensitive cancer research; complex in clinical context

### 6. Circulatory and Hepatic Effects
- Rosmarinic acid + carnosic acid: hepatoprotective (Nrf2; anti-inflammatory)
- Camphor: mild positive inotrope (cardiac stimulant; therapeutic window narrow)
- Ursolic acid: improves skeletal muscle insulin sensitivity; anti-obesity in animal models

## Section 6: Clinical Evidence

### Cognitive Enhancement

**Moss et al. 2012 (Therapeutic Advances in Psychopharmacology):**
- Aromatherapy study; 66 healthy participants; rosemary essential oil aroma vs. lavender vs. no aroma in cubicle
- Rosemary group: Significant improvements in speed of memory, accuracy of attention tasks, secondary memory factor (p<0.05 vs. control)
- 1,8-Cineole serum concentration correlated positively with performance scores (r=0.45; p<0.05) — first direct demonstration of inhaled cineole penetrating blood-brain barrier and correlating with cognitive benefit
- Level B (one RCT; reasonable design)

**Pengelly et al. 2012 (Therapeutic Advances in Psychopharmacology):**
- Elderly participants (N=28); rosemary powder dose escalation (750 mg, 1,500 mg, 3,000 mg, 6,000 mg)
- 750 mg: Improved speed of memory vs. placebo (p<0.05)
- Higher doses (3,000–6,000 mg): Impaired performance (inverted-U dose-response)
- Implication: Culinary doses of rosemary (750 mg powder equivalent to typical cooking use) are cognitively beneficial; higher doses counterproductive
- Level B

### Antimicrobial / Antifungal

**In vitro evidence (extensive, Level D for clinical use):** Rosemary essential oil effective against MRSA, Candida, Aspergillus at concentrations achievable topically but not systemically.

### Antioxidant / Anti-tumor

**Level D:** Carnosol, carnosic acid, and rosmarinic acid show consistent anti-tumor activity in vitro across multiple cancer cell lines (breast, colon, prostate); in vivo animal models positive; human clinical trials lacking.

### Hair Growth

**Panahi et al. 2015 (Skinmed):** Rosemary oil (6 months, topical) vs. minoxidil 2% for androgenetic alopecia; equivalent hair count increase at 6 months; comparable efficacy to minoxidil with fewer adverse effects (scalp itching); Level B (single RCT).

**Mechanism:** Proposed inhibition of 5α-reductase by carnosic acid (reduces DHT-mediated follicle miniaturization) + circulatory stimulation of scalp vasculature.

## Section 7: Phenomenological Record

### Qualitative Experience

Rosemary's phenomenological profile is distinctively activating and clarifying — the most cognitively oriented of the major culinary herbs. Its traditional association with memory is not metaphorical but reflects genuine neurophysiological action observable by practitioners and users across millennia.

**Aromatherapy / olfactory exposure:**
- Immediate (0–5 min): Sharp, piney, camphoraceous aroma; immediate sense of mental clarity; "head-clearing" sensation (consistent cross-cultural description)
- 10–20 minutes: Measurable cognitive improvements in reaction time, memory recall, and attention (validated by Moss 2012 serum cineole correlation); heightened wakefulness without stimulant agitation
- Subjective character: Alert without anxious; clear without cold; the "rosemary effect" is a qualitative improvement in processing speed rather than sedation or stimulation — distinctively different from caffeine (which adds energy with mild anxiety) or GABA-ergics (which reduce anxiety with possible sedation)

**Oral (tea or extract):**
- Tea: Warm, piney, slightly bitter; digestive warmth; mild circulatory stimulation (peripheral vasodilation → skin warmth)
- 30–60 minutes: Improved cognitive clarity at low dose; possible mild headache at high dose
- Culinary dose (750 mg equivalent in food): Cognitive benefit window (Pengelly 2012); pleasant; no adverse effects

**Topical (hair/scalp):**
- Scalp tingling within minutes of application (camphor + 1,8-cineole + α-pinene)
- Warming/circulatory effect at application site; empirically reported hair growth benefit over 3–6 months
- No systemic absorption at scalp application doses

**Traditional military/competitive use:**
Roman soldiers and Spartan warriors reportedly wore rosemary as ritual protection before battle. Medieval scholars placed rosemary on their desks. The consistent "cognitive activation" phenomenology across martial and intellectual contexts is unified by the mechanism: AChE inhibition + cerebrovascular enhancement producing measurably faster cognitive processing.

## Section 8: Practical Application

### Formulation and Dosing

**Cognitive / memory enhancement:**
- Aromatherapy: 3–4 drops rosemary essential oil in diffuser; 30-minute exposure during study/work
- Tea: 1.5–2 g dried leaves per 150 mL; 1–2 cups/day (culinary-dose range = cognitive benefit range)
- Standardized extract (carnosic acid/rosmarinic acid standardized): 200–400 mg/day
- **AVOID HIGH DOSES:** >3,000 mg rosemary powder/day — inverted-U response; impairs cognition

**Hair loss (androgenetic alopecia):**
- Rosemary essential oil 2% in jojoba/MCT carrier oil; massage into scalp 2 minutes; leave 30 minutes minimum; daily application
- 3–6 months minimum for visible hair count change
- Evidence: Equivalent to minoxidil 2% at 6 months (Panahi 2015)

**Digestive / antispasmodic:**
- Tea 1.5–2 g 3× daily; or 1–2 mL tincture (1:5 in 45% ethanol) 3× daily

**Anti-inflammatory / antioxidant:**
- Rosemary extract standardized to ≥5% rosmarinic acid or ≥2% carnosic acid
- 400–800 mg/day (preventive) or up to 1,200 mg/day (acute anti-inflammatory)

### Safety Profile

**Generally very safe** at culinary and low-moderate medicinal doses.

**Adverse effects:**
- GI irritation (rare at tea/culinary doses; possible at essential oil ingestion)
- **Seizure risk:** Camphor content — high-dose rosemary essential oil oral ingestion can provoke seizures; therapeutic window narrow for camphor; **AVOID oral essential oil in epilepsy**
- Allergy: Lamiaceae family sensitivity; cross-reaction with other Lamiaceae herbs

**Drug interactions:**
- **Anticoagulants (warfarin):** Rosemary has anticoagulant properties (rosmarinic acid, ursolic acid platelet inhibition); additive with warfarin; monitor INR with heavy use
- **Iron absorption:** Rosemary polyphenols chelate iron; avoid with iron-deficiency anemia management at meal times
- **CYP1A2 induction:** Carnosic acid/rosmarinic acid may induce CYP1A2; theoretical reduction of drugs metabolized by CYP1A2 (certain antidepressants, antipsychotics); clinical significance at typical doses uncertain
- **Antidiabetics:** Ursolic acid improves insulin sensitivity; additive hypoglycemia risk

**Contraindications:**
- Pregnancy (high dose): Camphor is uterine stimulant; culinary amounts safe; avoid supplements during pregnancy
- Epilepsy: Camphor proconvulsant; avoid essential oil
- Infants under 2 years: Camphor and 1,8-cineole facial application risks (parallel to peppermint/menthol concern)
