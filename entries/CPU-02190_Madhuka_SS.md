# Madhuka (Maula) — printed "Madhuka (Maula)", "Madhuka (Moula)" and "Maduhka (Maul)" (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *A sweet, astringent tree product that the Sushruta text lists as a second item beside plain Madhuka; the translation gives no Latin name and does not explain the bracketed word "Maula". The identification is ours and unresolved, at low confidence: the translator's Appendix No. 3 prints two entries for Madhuka whose Latin names, as paired by position in the OCR, read Glycyrrhiza glabra (liquorice) and Bassia latifolia (the mahua tree), and nothing read says which of these, if either, the "(Maula)" item is* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, in four places.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; the 30 October check pass kept it separate from plain Madhuka, because the first pass's suggestion of a duplicate was not supported. The text itself prints the bracketed form as a second item next to Madhuka in the same list, which is the reason it is not folded into another row. [USER: verify the relation to Madhuka]

**Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.38, the Nya'grodha'di group.** Chapter 38 classifies drugs "usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 9). The group is printed at lines 344 to 351 (Vol. I line 15688 for the bracketed item): "Nyagrodha, Audumvara, Ashvattha, Plaksha, Madhuka, Kapitana, Kakubha, Amra, Koshamra, Chorakapatra, the two sorts of Jamvu, Piyala, Madhuka (Maula), Rohini, Vanjula, Kadamva, Vadari, Tinduki, Sallaki, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Bhallataka, Palasha, and Nandi-Vriksha, combinedly form the group known as the Nyagodhradi Gana." So the group holds both a plain Madhuka and Madhuka (Maula), the second after Piyala. The metrical texts (lines 353 to 360) say the group "proves beneficial in cases of ulcer, cures all disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones and all sorts of secretions of the body in addition to its astringent properties (Sangrahi) and proves curative in a case of haemoptysis. It is an anti-fat and assuages the burning sensation of the body." These are group claims, not claims for this item alone.

**Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.39, errhines and trees.** In the chapter on "medicinal drugs possessed of cleansing (cathartic) or soothing effects", the list of head-purging drugs (errhines, *shirovirechana*) includes "Shala, Tala, Madhuka (Maula), Laksha and Hingu" (`SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` line 55). The later sentence on parts used speaks of "the essence (Sara) of Shala, Tala ... and Maduhka (Maul) trees" (lines 69 to 71), which is the only place that calls the item a tree and says which part is meant (the *sara*, translated "essence", of the tree).

**Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.42, the sweet taste.** In the chapter on "the specific properties of flavours (Rasa-Vishesha-Vijnaniya)", the sweet (Madhura) group includes "Madhuka (Moula), Draksha, Kharjura, Rajadana, Tala, Narikela" and others (line 295), the list ending "are generally included within the Madhura group" (line 300).

**Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.45, oils.** "The Oils of the Madhuka (Maula), Kishmarya, and Palasha seeds are sweet and astringent. They pacify the deranged Kapham and Pittam" (`SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` line 998; Vol. I line 18884, printed p.447). That makes the seed an oil source.

**Sushruta (SS), Uttarasthana ch.46, fainting fits.** In the cooling measures for fainting (*murchchha*), "cordials prepared with the expressed juice of Ikshu, Piyala and Draksha and with sugar, or with the expressed juice of Draksha and Madhuka (Maula)" are named (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch46.txt` lines 71 to 74; Vol. III line 12148). This points to a sweet fruit or part that yields a juice.

Taken together, the text uses the item as a tree (ch.39), a sweet-class drug (ch.42), an oil seed (ch.45) and a juice for cordials (Uttara ch.46). Those uses fit a sweet, oil-bearing tree better than a root. The translator does not say, and this entry does not say, that they all refer to one plant.

**Appendix No. 3.** The translator's index of drug names with Latin synonyms (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, the M page around lines 32150 to 32200) prints two Madhuka lines; by position in the OCR columns the first pairs with *Glycyrrhiza glabra* (liquorice) and the second with *Bassia latifolia*. The Codex treats such pairings as proposals only, since a dropped OCR line shifts a pairing by one. Neither is attached to "(Maula)". [USER: verify against the printed page]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, if the item is a seed-oil tree such as *Bassia latifolia* (mahua), the seed is oil-rich and the flower is sugar-rich; if it is liquorice, the root carries glycyrrhizin, a sweet triterpenoid saponin. Because the identification is open, neither can be asserted for this item. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Nya'grodha'di group: ulcers, uterine and vaginal disorders, fractured bones, haemoptysis and burning; as an errhine ingredient (ch.39); as a sweet-class drug (ch.42); as a sweet, astringent oil that pacifies Kapham and Pittam (ch.45); and as a juice for cooling cordials in fainting (Uttara ch.46).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no yield, and no statement that it is the same as plain Madhuka.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this item.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources give a cordial of its expressed juice with that of Draksha (Uttara ch.46, lines 71 to 74), an oil from the seed (ch.45, line 998), and the *sara* of the tree as a head-purging ingredient (ch.39). No quantity is printed for any of them. Chapter 38 ends with a general instruction to use "two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group", or the whole group, as the physician decides (lines 561 to 568). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Liquorice root is a recognised sweetener and demulcent that can raise blood pressure and lower potassium when taken in quantity; the mahua tree (*Bassia latifolia*, now *Madhuca longifolia*) yields a food oil and a fermentable flower. Because the identification of this item is open, none of this is carried over to it. The classic's *Kapham*, *Pittam* and "fainting fits" have no modern equivalents and are not translated into any. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Bhishagratna 1907).** The seed oil is "sweet and astringent" and "pacifies the deranged Kapham and Pittam" (ch.45, line 998). In ch.42 the item sits in the sweet (*madhura*) taste group (line 295). The Nya'grodha'di group is credited with astringent (*sangrahi*) action (ch.38, lines 353 to 360). No potency (*virya*) or post-digestive effect (*vipaka*) is printed for the item.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* The item appears in a therapeutic group, a taste-class list, an oil list and a fainting-treatment cordial. It was not found in a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) chapter, and no *ojas* effect is assigned to it. Being in the sweet (*madhura*) class is a statement about taste, not a cultivation grade.

**Practitioner's note.** A sweet, astringent tree product of unsettled identity, used by Sushruta as a seed oil, a juice for cooling cordials, and a head-purging ingredient. A cultivator has nothing here to take up; the entry's use is to keep the name, and its four contexts, on record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity unresolved.** Do not substitute liquorice or mahua products for it, and do not assume it is plain Madhuka.
- **Liquorice caution, if that is the identification.** Liquorice root taken in quantity can raise blood pressure; this is a general fact, not a statement about this item.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Head-purging use.** The errhine use (ch.39) is a clinical procedure; do not attempt it.
- **Fainting, haemoptysis, ulcers.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
