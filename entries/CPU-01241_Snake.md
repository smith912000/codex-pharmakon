# Snake — "Serpent" (Bryan 1930's English rendering of the Ebers animal list)
## *The serpent; Bryan prints only the English word "Serpent", with no Egyptian name, no species and no binomial* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The Ebers material reaches this Codex through Cyril P. Bryan's 1930 English version, *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). In his Chapter VII, "Organic Remedies", Bryan says that a complete list of the animal materia medica "would not be possible", because the identity of many of the animals is "still beyond us", and he then gives the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (lines 1382-1390). The list is printed in alphabetical groups, and the snake stands in the line "Serpent" (line 1393), between the group of "Crocodile Ox Cow Calf Mouse Porcupine" and that of "Tortoise Lizard Mole Vulture". The extract file records it as "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, row for "Serpent", locus line 1393). No species is named; the project's source list (`_sources/ebers.csv`, line 73) enters it as "Serpent / snake fat, unspecified snake sp.", citing "Ebers (baldness pomade)" and a general-interest website; the website is not relied on here.

Bryan also prints a second list, of the parts that the animals "were forced to yield" (hair, feathers, horns, hoof, hide, fat, flesh and so on; lines 1408-1415). It is not sorted by animal, so it does not say which parts of the snake were used. For that the text has to be searched under the name, and the serpent appears in four places.

The first is the hair remedy. Bryan prints "ANOTHER FOR THE GROWTH OF THE HAIR ON A HEAD WHICH IS BECOMING BALD", with the ingredients "Fat-of-the-Lion, Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus, Fat-of-the-Crocodile, Fat-of-the-Cat, Fat-of-the-Serpent, Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat", and the instruction "Make into one and rub the head of the Bald One therewith" (lines 4023-4029). He introduces the group of remedies with the remark that the "array of remedies suggested" is such that "the very mention of which is almost sufficient to activate the hair-cells out of sheer terror" (line 4015). The fat of the serpent is one of six fats, with no quantity and no method of rendering. The use is the one the extract file means by "baldness pomade".

The second is a set of remedies against the snake and not of it. Bryan reports that "we had to be on the look-out for ... a Serpent creeping out of his hole", for which "we had the choice of three remedies": dried ant-fish set against the entrance of the lair, natron put into the hole, and a garlic-ball inserted into it, with the Scribe adding to each, in words Bryan prints in capitals, that it will not creep out (lines 4225-4233). Bryan's introduction mentions the same recipes (line 1039-1041, "to prevent the serpent creeping out of his hole"). The snake here is the thing kept away, and none of its parts is used.

The third is a simile. In a passage on tumours of the *met* the text says the swelling "winds itself like snakes while it forms many prominences" (line 3950). That is a description of how the disease looks and not a use of the animal.

### Active Components

Fat from an unspecified snake is a mixture of triglycerides, with the fatty acids depending on the species and what it has eaten. The sources name no active principle, and none specific to this entry is claimed.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced use is a pomade of six animal fats, serpent among them, rubbed on the head against baldness. The Codex gives no protocol for it. Hair loss has many causes (inherited pattern loss, illness, medicines, scalp disease), and an ointment of wild-animal fat is not a treatment for any of them. The snake remedies against the snake are likewise recorded and not recommended: snakes that are dangerous should be left to those trained to remove them.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only one preparation is given: the six fats are combined ("Make into one") and rubbed on the head (line 4029). There is no quantity, no method of preparing the fats, no frequency and no duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Animal fats serve as ointment bases because they spread and hold moisture, and they have no recognised effect on hair growth. Pattern baldness is largely a matter of hormones and inheritance, and the treatments with evidence behind them are medicines prescribed for it. Reptiles commonly carry *Salmonella*, and wild-animal fat of unknown origin is not a sanitary preparation. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** None is given. Bryan's list names the animal and his prescriptions name the fat; he prints no taste, temperature, humoral or other quality for the serpent. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, here baldness, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution, and the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis resting on Bryan's English word. None of the Egyptian terms for the person (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is attached to the serpent in any line read. The anti-serpent remedies carry the Scribe's own remarks on whether they worked (that the serpent will not creep out), but no spell is printed with them in the lines read, so no *heka* frame is claimed for them. Serpent deities and the uraeus are not discussed, because no source read attributes them to this entry. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this. It is one fat among six in a hair pomade, with no quantity, and Bryan himself calls the group of such remedies "mighty bewildering" (line 4021). The only use the entry has is as a record of what Ebers lists.

### Contraindications

- **Wild snakes:** do not handle or attempt to catch wild snakes; bites can be serious. Seek emergency care after any snakebite.
- **Hygiene and infection:** reptile fat and tissue can carry *Salmonella* and other pathogens; do not apply raw animal fat to the scalp or broken skin.
- **Hair loss:** sudden or patchy loss, or loss with scalp pain, scaling or illness, should be assessed by a clinician.
- **Legal and conservation status:** many snake species are protected, and their capture and trade are controlled in many countries; the text names no species. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** killing a wild animal for a substance with no supported benefit is hard to justify.
