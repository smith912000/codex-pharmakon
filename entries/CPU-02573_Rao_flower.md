# Rao flower — 荛花 *Rao Hua* (Shennong bencao jing); Yang 1998 prints "Yao Hua (Aos Wikstroemiae Japonicae)"
## *The flower of a Wikstroemia shrub (Thymelaeaceae), Wikstroemia chamaedaphne Meisn. in the standard modern identification of 荛花 (W. canescens is also proposed); medium confidence, because the classic's text gives no description of the plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`) and, as a second witness attributed to him, Yang's 1998 English translation (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`). The identification is ours.

**Toxicity caution.** The classic files this drug in the lower grade, whose preface calls the drugs mostly toxic and not for long use, and the plant belongs to a family of irritant and toxic shrubs. Nothing here is a recommendation. It is a strong purgative drug in the text's own account ("precipitates the twelve waters"), and it is not to be taken on the strength of this entry.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the lower (下) classic, reads 味苦，平，寒 ("bitter; balanced, cold" as printed). It governs 伤寒温疟 ("cold damage and warm malaria"), "precipitates the twelve waters" (下十二水), "breaks accumulations and gatherings, great hardness, concretions and conglomerations" (破积聚、大坚、症瘕), "washes away what has lodged in the stomach and intestines, abiding rheum and food, cold and heat evil qi" (荡涤肠胃中留癖饮食、寒热邪气), and 利水道 ("frees the water passages"). The habitat is 生川谷 ("grows in river valleys"). There is no 久服 clause. No toxicity word is printed in the paragraph. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 荛花 *rao hua*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3363.)

**Wu Pu.** No Wu Pu paragraph is printed for this entry.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰：生咸阳及河南中牟。六月采花，阴干: "grows at Xianyang and at Zhongmu in Henan; the flowers are gathered in the sixth month and dried in the shade." (Line 3365.) This is the only guidance on gathering. There is no 案 by Sun for this entry.

**Yang 1998 (second witness).** Yang's page 89, under "Herbs: Inferior Class" (line 3293), prints the same monograph under the heading "Yao Hua (Aos Wikstroemiae Japonicae)": "bitter and cold. It mainly treats cold damage and warm malaria, precipitates the 12 waters, breaks gatherings, accumulations, great hardness, concretions, and conglomerations, flushes abiding and aggregated food and drink away from the stomach and intestines as well as cold and heat evil qi, and disinhibits the water passageways. It grows in rivers and valleys." The match with Sun's 荛花 paragraph is close, and Yang prints Yuan Hua (*Daphne genkwa*, 芫花) as a separate entry earlier on page 88 (line 3260), so "Yao Hua" in Yang is the 荛花 monograph and not the other drug; the spelling "Yao" for "Rao" is as printed. Yang's footnote 203 explains: "The 12 waters refer to all categories of swelling, for example, enlarged abdomen, swollen face, and swelling starting from below with a tendency to extend upward." His Latin "Aos Wikstroemiae Japonicae" is the translator's.

**Identity.** The name 荛花 means "flower of the 荛"; the Mingyi layer's gathering of the flowers in the sixth month fits a flower drug, and the shrub is placed in *Wikstroemia* by the standard modern view; Yang's Latin points to *Wikstroemia japonica*. The classic itself does not describe the plant. The spine's placeholder CPU-02021 ("Aos Wikstroemiae Japonicae") is the Yang printing of this same monograph, though the spine maps it to 芫花, which Yang treats as another entry. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Plants of the Thymelaeaceae, including *Wikstroemia* and *Daphne*, are generally known to contain irritant diterpenoid esters and coumarins, which make them toxic and skin-irritating. The constituents of this species were not retrieved for this entry, and none is asserted. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records a bitter flower that drives out accumulated water, hard masses and food stagnation, and treats fevers and agues. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends none. Any drug said to "precipitate the twelve waters" is a purgative strong enough to cause dehydration and collapse.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives only the harvest: the flowers gathered in the sixth month and dried in the shade (Mingyi layer). It gives no preparation, route, quantity or course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Thymelaeaceae shrubs are widely regarded as poisonous, and several are used as fish poisons and for the fibre of their bark. Whether this species' flowers relieve any condition the Classic names has not been tested in sources retrieved. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong text proper).** 味苦，平，寒: bitter; the printed "balanced, cold" is as transcribed and is not resolved here (Yang: "bitter and cold"). No toxicity statement is printed in the paragraph.
- Class: lower (下). No organ affinity is printed. No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 荛花 in the lower grade (下品); its preface (Sun ed. line 2935) says lower drugs are "for treating disease to correspond with earth", "mostly toxic, not to be taken long" (多毒，不可久服). The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise; it lists fevers, swellings and hard masses. Its place in the lower grade is a warning and not a status. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 荛花 *rao hua*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3363.)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A lower-grade purgative flower, at most a short course against a defined complaint, as the preface's grading warns. The Codex does not recommend it.

### Contraindications

- **Toxic plant.** Thymelaeaceae shrubs are poisonous and irritating to skin and gut; do not take this plant by mouth and do not handle it casually.
- **Dehydration.** A strong purgative can cause severe fluid and salt loss. Swelling of the abdomen, face or legs needs medical assessment.
- **Fever and malaria-like illness.** Need diagnosis and treatment.
- **Identification.** Wikstroemia species are easily confused with one another and with other shrubs.
