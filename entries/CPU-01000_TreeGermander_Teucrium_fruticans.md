# Tree Germander / Shrubby Germander / Olivilla / Germandrée arbustive / Camedrio arbustivo

## *Teucrium fruticans* L. | Family: Lamiaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Bitter, dry, cooling-to-neutral; downward and clearing in action, with a drying astringency in the leaf

**Training Phase:** Consolidation — a bitter tonic of the digestive corridor, admitted only under hepatic supervision; never a Foundation herb

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Teucrium fruticans* is a silver-leaved evergreen shrub of the western Mediterranean — Iberia, southern France, Italy, Sicily, North Africa — with felted white-tomentose stems and pale blue-lilac labiate flowers. It belongs to a genus whose name is traditionally referred to Teucer, the Trojan figure credited in classical herbal lore with first using the plant. Unlike its low-growing relatives *T. chamaedrys* (wall germander) and *T. polium* (felty germander), *T. fruticans* is a true shrub, and its history is more horticultural than pharmacopoeial. This is the honest starting point for the entry: **most of what is written about "germander" in the historical record belongs to other species of the genus, and *T. fruticans* has been carried along by association.** Kenetica treats it accordingly — as a genus-member with an inherited doctrine and an inherited hazard, not as a well-attested drug in its own right.

**Dioscoridean / Mediterranean Materia Medica (DMM)**
The Greek herbal tradition knew the germanders as *khamaidrys* (χαμαίδρυς, "ground-oak") — the name preserved in *T. chamaedrys* — from the oak-like lobing of the leaf. The classical indication is consistent and narrow: a bitter for the belly and the liver-region, given for sluggish digestion, for obstruction of the viscera, for chronic intermittent fevers, and for the joint-disease later called gout. The doctrine is one of **bitter incision** — the herb was held to cut and thin thick, stagnant humours, opening obstructed passages so that what was congealed could move. Germander wine and germander decoction persisted in the Mediterranean domestic pharmacy under exactly this reading, and shrubby Mediterranean *Teucrium* species — *fruticans* among them where it grows — were gathered as local substitutes for the same purpose. The attestation for *T. fruticans* specifically is regional and thin; the doctrine attached to it is DMM's germander doctrine, borrowed wholesale.

**European Common / Folk Medicine**
Germander entered the European folk and later official pharmacopoeias as a bitter tonic, a stomachic, an antiseptic wash, and — most famously and most disastrously — a slimming and "liver-supporting" botanical. In the Iberian and Italian countryside, *T. fruticans* (Spanish *olivilla*, from the olive-like silver leaf) was used as an aromatic bitter infusion and as a wash for wounds and mouth complaints. The gout indication travelled with the genus: germander was a standing ingredient of the celebrated eighteenth-century gout remedies, and this is the historical thread that gives the genus its persistent reputation as an anti-arthritic bitter. **The folk record for *T. fruticans* is real but small, and it is overwhelmingly about the aerial parts as a bitter and as a topical antiseptic.**

**Contemporary / Modern-Ethnobotanical (AT — Applied Tradition)**
In present-day use, *T. fruticans* is best known as an ornamental hedge shrub, cultivated far beyond its range — including in China, where it has been grown and phytochemically examined. Its modern applied profile is as an essential-oil-bearing Lamiaceae aromatic. Recent characterisation of cultivated Italian material found the aerial-part essential oil dominated by sesquiterpene hydrocarbons — β-caryophyllene (28.30% in February material) and germacrene D (19.16%), with β-myrcene rising to 13.77% in April harvest — and localised terpene production to peltate and medium-stalked capitate glandular trichomes (Giuliani et al., *Chem Biodivers* 2023; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbdv.202200913)). Sicilian and Maltese collections likewise showed germacrene D (29.4–50.0%), (*E*)-β-caryophyllene (19.6–21.9%) and 1-octen-3-ol as the volatile signature (Gagliano Candela et al., *Nat Prod Res* 2020; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2019.1709193)). This is a genuine, retrievable modern profile — and it is a profile of an aromatic, not of a validated liver drug.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

The germander doctrine is *bitter incision*: a taste that cuts, a herb that opens obstruction, an agent that moves what has congealed in the liver-region and the joints. Read against chemistry, this doctrine has two halves — one that maps cleanly and one that inverts into the plant's central hazard.

The half that maps: the bitterness is real and it is chemically located. *T. fruticans* is a neo-clerodane diterpenoid plant. Seven new neo-clerodanes — teufruintins A–G — plus eight known compounds were isolated from the aerial parts of Chinese-cultivated material (Lv et al., *Phytochemistry* 2015; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2015.09.011)), and earlier work established fruticolone, isofruticolone, 8β-hydroxyfruticolone, difuranofruticol, deoxyfruticolone and 10-hydroxyteucjaponin B in the species (Coll & Tandrón, *Phytochemistry* 2005; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2005.07.003)). Neo-clerodanes are intensely bitter and are the genus's insect-antifeedant chemistry — they exist to make the plant taste unbearable. A bitter tonic doctrine built on this class is a doctrine built on a real molecular fact: the taste that the tradition read as "cutting" is the taste of a diterpene defence system. Bitter receptor stimulation on the tongue provoking cephalic-phase digestive secretion is the plausible, mechanistically modest reading of the stomachic use, and it does not require the diterpenes to do anything systemic at all.

The half that inverts: the tradition read the germanders as *liver herbs*. The chemistry says the opposite. The very furan ring that decorates many germander neo-clerodanes is the structure that the liver converts into a reactive, protein-alkylating metabolite. **The doctrine of hepatic opening and the fact of hepatic injury share a single molecular address.** This is the most important sentence in the entry: the plant was given for the liver because it was bitter, the bitterness comes from furano-neo-clerodanes, and furano-neo-clerodanes are what damaged the liver. The symbolic layer here is not a decoration — it is a warning about how doctrine of taste can carry a class of molecules into exactly the organ least able to tolerate them.

### Active Components

- **neo-Clerodane diterpenoids** — teufruintins A–G, fruticolone, isofruticolone, 7β- and 8β- and 11-hydroxyfruticolone, deacetylfruticolone, difuranofruticol, deoxyfruticolone, 10-hydroxyteucjaponin B, 6-acetylteucjaponin B. The bitter principle and the antifeedant chemistry. Several bear furan or furan-derived rings — the toxicophore of concern.
- **Sesquiterpene hydrocarbons (essential oil)** — β-caryophyllene, germacrene D, α-humulene, β-myrcene, 1-octen-3-ol. Produced in peltate and capitate glandular trichomes; the aromatic, not the bitter, fraction.
- **Phenolics and flavonoids** — the genus is phenolic-rich; in *Teucrium* generally, phenylethanoid glycosides (notably teucrioside) and iridoid glucosides (harpagide, 8-*O*-acetylharpagide) carry the antioxidant activity. Species-specific quantification for *T. fruticans* is not established. [USER: verify species-specific phenolic profile]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

*T. fruticans* enters Kenetica as a **Consolidation-phase bitter of last resort** — which in practice means it enters as a teaching case rather than a working herb. Its legitimate profile is: an intensely bitter aromatic aerial part, appropriate as a *taste* stimulus to the digestive corridor in small, short, oral-contact doses; and a topical antiseptic wash of the folk register.

Its illegitimate profile — the one the tradition offers and Kenetica declines — is: a systemic hepatic or anti-gout agent taken in sustained internal doses. The genus's history is the reason. Kenetica's rule for *T. fruticans* is therefore **taste, not dose**: whatever value the bitterness has is delivered at the tongue and requires no accumulation, while every documented harm in the genus is a function of sustained systemic exposure to reactive diterpene metabolites.

The Consolidation placement reflects the phase where practitioners have enough discrimination to distinguish a bitter stimulus from a hepatic therapy and to refuse the second. Foundation-phase practitioners have no business here.

### Scientific Correlation

**The bitter-tonic doctrine → neo-clerodane chemistry: WELL-SUPPORTED as chemistry, UNTESTED as therapy.** The presence of a large, structurally diverse neo-clerodane inventory in *T. fruticans* is firmly established (Lv et al. 2015, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2015.09.011); Coll & Tandrón 2005, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2005.07.003)). That these are bitter antifeedants is consistent with the assay data — though notably, the tested compounds showed only *low* antifeedant activity against *Spodoptera littoralis* and no significant antifungal activity against *Rhizoctonia solani* (Coll & Tandrón 2005). **Grade: chemistry confirmed; the leap from "contains bitters" to "is a digestive therapy" is unbridged by any trial in this species.**

**The antiseptic doctrine → essential oil: PLAUSIBLE, INDIRECT.** The oil is dominated by β-caryophyllene and germacrene D (Giuliani et al. 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbdv.202200913); Gagliano Candela et al. 2020, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2019.1709193)). Both authors note antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory *potential* on the basis of literature about the isolated constituents — not on the basis of testing this oil against pathogens. That is an inference, and the papers are honest that it is one. **Grade: mechanistically plausible, not demonstrated for this species' preparation.**

**⛔ NULL RESULT — the anti-cancer and anti-inflammatory hope collapses.** All neo-clerodanes isolated from *T. fruticans* were tested against three human cancer cell lines and for inhibition of LPS-induced nitric oxide production in RAW 264.7 macrophages. **None displayed cytotoxic activity, and only one of fifteen compounds showed weak NO inhibition** (Lv et al. 2015, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2015.09.011)). This is a clean negative and it is reported here as such. **Grade: NEGATIVE.**

**⛔ DOCTRINE DEFLATED — the "liver herb" reading.** There is no evidence that any *Teucrium* species supports the liver, and abundant evidence that the genus's characteristic diterpenes injure it. In mice, the neo-clerodane **teucrin A reproduced the midzonal hepatic necrosis caused by whole germander extract**; the injury was *worsened* by phenobarbital (CYP induction) and by glutathione depletion, and *attenuated* by the CYP inhibitor piperonyl butoxide. Decisively, the tetrahydrofuran analogue of teucrin A — the furan ring chemically reduced — **was not hepatotoxic**, establishing furan oxidation as the initiating event (Kouzi, McMurtry & Nelson, *Chem Res Toxicol* 1994; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/tx00042a020)). In humans, germander tea caused an epidemic of cytolytic hepatitis in France; rechallenge produced early recurrence, and patients' sera contained autoantibodies against microsomal epoxide hydrolase, which was shown to be present on the hepatocyte surface and alkylated by a CYP3A-derived reactive metabolite of teucrin A (De Berardinis et al., *Mol Pharmacol* 2000; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1124/mol.58.3.542)). *T. chamaedrys*-based supplements were consequently banned (Piccolella et al., *Biomolecules* 2021; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biom11050690)). **Grade: the hepatotoxicity is STRONG — mechanism, animal model, human epidemic, rechallenge, and immune target all converge. The "liver support" doctrine is REFUTED.**

**Species-specific caveat, honestly stated.** The human hepatitis epidemic and the teucrin A mechanism belong to *T. chamaedrys*, with *T. polium* also implicated in reported liver injury. **No case of hepatotoxicity attributable to *T. fruticans* was retrieved, and the compound teucrin A has not been reported from this species.** Furthermore, genus-membership does not guarantee the toxicophore: a *Teucrium* species (*T. polium* group) from Iraqi Kurdistan, traditionally used for jaundice and liver disorders, was found on analysis to lack detectable hepatotoxic furano-clerodane diterpenoids, its activity residing instead in the iridoid glucosides harpagide and 8-*O*-acetylharpagide (Abdullah et al., *Molecules* 2022; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27185963)). Likewise, *T. chamaedrys* cell cultures produce abundant teucrioside but no detectable neo-clerodanes (Antognoni et al., *Phytochemistry* 2012; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2012.05.027)). **The honest position: absence of case reports for *T. fruticans* is absence of evidence, not evidence of safety, and the species is confirmed to carry a rich furan-bearing neo-clerodane load. Kenetica therefore applies the genus warning as a class warning. Grade for species-specific safety: DATA-SPARSE — treat as hazardous by inheritance.**

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian (AT).** *Teucrium fruticans* is a shrub of the western Mediterranean — Iberia, southern France, Italy, Sicily, North Africa — outside the geographic and textual reach of the Mesopotamian cuneiform materia medica, and no *asû* or *āšipu* prescription can be tied to this species. Illness in the Assyrian corpus is classified by causal agency (divine hand, ghost, oath), and a substance is partly defined by which agency it opposes; nothing of the sort is recorded for this plant. Under the standing blocking warning in force for every Assyrian substance identification and tablet reference, no cultivation status is claimed. **(c) absent.** (See attestation flag: the "AT" tag on this entry is used in the local sense "Applied Tradition / modern ethnobotanical," not the canonical Assyrian code, and the modern ornamental-aromatic material carries no cultivation framing either.)

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The germanders reach the Greek herbal as *khamaidrys* (χαμαίδρυς, "ground-oak"), read as a bitter of incision that cuts and thins stagnant humours of the belly and liver-region; *T. fruticans* is carried along only as a regional shrubby substitute where it grows, its doctrine borrowed wholesale from the genus rather than attested for the species. Dioscorides assigns no subtle-body system that could give any germander a cultivation status — *pneuma* is context, and the Galenic three spirits are later and post-Dioscoridean. What the tradition genuinely offers a cultivator here is his discipline of attention: correct gathering of the aromatic aerial parts, sensory assay of the bitterness, and guarding against the substitution of one *Teucrium* for another — a real practice of provenance and judgement, sharpened in this case by the genus's hepatic hazard. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The germander doctrine that reaches this entry is humoral — hot and dry in the second degree, attenuating and aperient of visceral obstruction — but its transmission for *T. fruticans* specifically runs through European folk and Galenic domestic practice (the Iberian *olivilla* bitter, the eighteenth-century gout remedies), not through any attested Avicennan account of this species. The strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus — the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture as a finite vital endowment — is a longevity frame of the whole regimen, not a status the tradition assigns to a cooling, drying, furan-bearing bitter, and no *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed for it. No genuine Unani attestation for *T. fruticans* was retrieved. **(c) absent.** (See attestation flag: the CM tag on this species is likely better coded EF, European folk / phytotherapy.)

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does effectively nothing with this plant, and that is the point of the entry. Its only defensible use is gustatory and topical — a brief bitter contact at the tongue to stimulate the digestive corridor, or an external antiseptic wash — neither of which is a cultivation practice in any of the three traditions tagged. Every documented harm in the genus is a function of sustained internal exposure to reactive furano-neo-clerodane metabolites, so the tradition's implied "liver herb" is precisely the use a careful practitioner refuses. Taste, not dose; and no long-term taking.

### Contraindications

⚠️ **HEPATOTOXICITY — CLASS WARNING, GENUS *TEUCRIUM*.** This is the governing constraint of the entry.

- **Do not use internally in any person with existing liver disease, raised transaminases, viral hepatitis, alcohol-related liver disease, or any history of drug-induced liver injury.** The genus mechanism is CYP3A-mediated furan bioactivation to a protein-alkylating metabolite, with glutathione as the limiting defence (Kouzi et al. 1994; De Berardinis et al. 2000).
- **Do not use with CYP3A inducers, or in the setting of glutathione depletion** (malnutrition, fasting, paracetamol overdose recovery, chronic alcohol use). Both were shown experimentally to *amplify* germander hepatotoxicity.
- **Do not use concurrently with any hepatically metabolised drug of narrow margin,** or with other botanicals of known hepatic risk.
- **No prolonged or repeated-course internal use, at any dose.** The human germander epidemic followed *sustained* tea consumption. Immune sensitisation was demonstrated — meaning the injury may not be dose-linear on re-exposure, and rechallenge caused early recurrence.
- **Contraindicated in pregnancy and lactation.** No data; diterpene-rich Lamiaceae bitters are excluded by default.
- **Contraindicated in children.**
- **Not for use as a slimming or "detox" botanical.** This is the precise historical use-pattern that produced the hepatitis epidemic and the regulatory ban on *T. chamaedrys* supplements.
- **Do not substitute for, or accept as a substitute from, other *Teucrium* species.** Genus-level material trade is unreliable; *T. chamaedrys*, *T. polium*, *T. scordium* and *T. fruticans* are not interchangeable and their diterpene loads differ.
- **Discontinue immediately and seek medical assessment** on any nausea, anorexia, right-upper-quadrant pain, dark urine, or jaundice.

**Kenetica default: external and gustatory use only. Internal courses are a supervised exception, not a practice.**
