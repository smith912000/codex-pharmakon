# Sariva — Sanskrit: Sāriva, Anantamūla, Gopī, Śveta-sāriva; Indian/White Sarsaparilla, Nannari

## *Hemidesmus indicus* (L.) R.Br. ex Schult. | Family: Apocynaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SS
**Tags (previous line, retained for reference):** Suśruta Saṃhitā (raktaprasādana — blood-clarifying; *sārivādi gaṇa*); Caraka Saṃhitā (dāhapraśamana, stanya-śodhana); Unani (as *Ushba hindi*); Siddha; South Indian folk & European pharmacopoeial *Sarsaparilla* surrogate

**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Tridoṣaghna with primary action on Pitta and Rakta; *madhura-vipāka*, *śīta-vīrya* — pacifies Pitta and Kapha, mildly grounding to Vāta

**Training Phase:** Cooling & Clarifying (raktaśodhana) · Consolidation · Recovery / Restoration

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (the surgical-Ayurvedic root):** Sāriva is the eponymous head of the *sārivādi gaṇa*, a cooling group Suśruta assembles for burning sensation (*dāha*), thirst (*tṛṣṇā*), fever (*jvara*), and disorders of the blood. In Suśruta's dravyaguṇa the drug is *raktaprasādana* — it "clears" or "settles" the blood, the tissue (*dhātu*) most susceptible to Pitta's heat. Its territory is therefore the skin (viewed as an outward expression of blood), the mucous membranes, and the urinary channel: skin eruptions, itching (*kaṇḍū*), chronic low fevers, and the burning of inflamed *mūtravaha srotas*. The root — long, slender, fragrant, hence *Anantamūla*, "the endless root" — is the officinal part.

**Caraka Saṃhitā:** Caraka classes Sāriva among *dāhapraśamana* (heat-quenching) and *stanyaśodhana* (breast-milk purifying) drugs, extending its cooling logic from blood to lactation and to the settling of thirst and vomiting. It is a standing ingredient of restorative *rasāyana* and cooling *ghṛta*/milk decoctions.

**Later nighaṇṭus & pan-Indian folk use:** As *Nannari*, the fragrant root is the base of South Indian cooling syrups (*nannari sharbat*) drunk against summer heat — a folk continuation of the classical *śīta-vīrya* reading. The root's 2-hydroxy-4-methoxybenzaldehyde gives it a vanilla-sarsaparilla aroma that made it the Indian substitute for New-World *Smilax* sarsaparilla in colonial European practice, where it entered materia medica for syphilis, rheumatism, and "blood impurity."

**Unani & Siddha:** In Unani (*Ushba*) it is a *muṣaffī-e-khūn* (blood purifier) of cold-dry temperament used in skin disease and venereal complaints; Siddha texts deploy it similarly for *ratta* (blood) and skin.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Endless Root that Cools the Blood:** Sāriva's Sanskrit names encode its doctrine. *Anantamūla*, "the endless root," images a remedy that reaches down without limit into the deep, hidden channel of the blood — the tissue Ayurveda treats as the seat of vitality, complexion (*varṇa*), and the fire of Pitta. Its fragrance (*sugandhi-mūla*) is doctrinally load-bearing: aromatic cooling drugs are held to move *and* pacify, so Sāriva does not merely dampen heat (a purely *śīta* action would risk stagnation and Kapha) but circulates its coolness, "clarifying" rather than "extinguishing." This is *raktaprasādana* in its exact sense — *prasādana* means to render clear, calm, gracious, the same word used for a mind made serene. The blood is settled the way silt settles from disturbed water. Symbolically Sāriva is the drug of restored complexion and quiet heat: where Pitta has flushed the skin, inflamed the urine, or soured the milk, the endless root is drawn down to make the blood *prasanna* — clear and unangry.

### Active Components
Aromatic aldehydes and phenolics dominate the root — chiefly **2-hydroxy-4-methoxybenzaldehyde** (the characteristic aroma/marker) and related methoxy-salicylaldehydes and benzoic acids; **triterpenoids** (lupeol acetate, α- and β-amyrin acetates, β-amyrin palmitate); **phytosterols** (β-sitosterol, stigmasterol); **pregnane glycosides** (hemidesmosides); flavonoid glycosides (rutin, hyperoside, isoquercetin); coumarinolignoids; and a range of volatile terpenes. According to PubMed, this profile is compiled in the ethnopharmacology review by Nandy et al. (2020), *J Ethnopharmacol* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2020.112609).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica
Sāriva is Kenetica's principal **cooling/clarifying (raktaśodhana)** agent for the Cooling & Clarifying phase: used where heat has accumulated in the blood-and-skin axis — flushing, eruptive or itching skin, hot low-grade inflammation, burning urination, and the internal "heat of overexertion." It is a settling, complexion-restoring drug rather than a stimulant: reach for it in Consolidation and Recovery to quench residual inflammatory heat and support the skin and urinary channels after intense loading. Its aromatic character makes it a pleasant vehicle/adjuvant in cooling syrups and milk decoctions.

### Scientific Correlation
According to PubMed:
- **Comprehensive review** — Nandy et al. (2020), *J Ethnopharmacol*, catalogue hepatoprotective, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-diabetic, neuroprotective, anti-venom, and antimicrobial activities across the (largely preclinical) literature and map the phytochemistry [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2020.112609).
- **Anticancer / proteasome** — Turrini et al. (2019), *Sci Rep*, show a standardised root decoction induces apoptosis in human leukaemia cells via proteasome inhibition and reactive-oxygen generation, with intestinal absorption of phytomarkers in a Caco-2 model [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-43609-5).
- **Bone / anti-osteoporotic (traditional-use corroboration)** — Desai et al. (2017), *J Ethnopharmacol*, report that an ethanolic root extract preserved bone strength and turnover markers in ovariectomised rats without uterine hypertrophy, supporting the classical use in female hormonal/"bone-loss" indications [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2017.01.031).

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical (in vitro and rodent) with one systematic ethnopharmacological review. No robust human clinical trials; classical cooling/blood-clarifying uses are supported at the mechanistic level (antioxidant, anti-inflammatory) but not clinically confirmed in humans.

### Traditional Energetic Classification
- **Rasa (taste):** Madhura (sweet), Tikta (bitter); some sources add Kaṣāya (astringent)
- **Guṇa (quality):** Guru (heavy), Snigdha (unctuous)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Madhura (sweet)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** *Raktaprasādana* — a specific blood-clarifying/complexion-restoring effect exceeding what rasa-guṇa-vīrya alone predict
- **Doṣa action:** Pacifies Pitta and Kapha; the sweet-unctuous-cooling profile is mildly Vāta-settling — broadly *tridoṣaghna* with a Pitta-Rakta focus
- **Karma (actions):** Raktaśodhana/raktaprasādana, dāhapraśamana (anti-burning), kaṇḍūghna (anti-itch), jvaraghna (antipyretic), mūtrala (diuretic), stanyaśodhana (milk-purifying), varṇya (complexion-enhancing), rasāyana (rejuvenative)
- **Gaṇa:** *Sārivādi gaṇa* (Suśruta) — eponymous head

### Contraindications
Cooling and heavy; may aggravate Kapha-cold conditions, sluggish digestion (*mandāgni*), or Vāta-cold complaints if used long-term without warming adjuvants. Not established as safe in pregnancy/lactation at therapeutic doses `[USER: verify]`. Human safety data are limited. Ensure correct botanical identity — *Hemidesmus indicus* (Śveta/white Sāriva) is distinct from the "black Sāriva" (*Cryptolepis buchanani*) with which it is traditionally paired and sometimes substituted.
