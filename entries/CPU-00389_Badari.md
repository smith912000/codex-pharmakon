# Badari — Vadari, Vadara, Badara, Kola (Sushruta, Bhishagratna 1907; spine spelling "Badari")

## *Ziziphus Jujuba* (as printed against "Vadara" in Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 glossary; the spine row carries *Ziziphus mauritiana* Lam.) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** This entry needs a ruling. The spine row names the Indian jujube, *Ziziphus mauritiana*. The Latin printed against the Sanskrit word in the text on disk is *Ziziphus jujuba*, which is the plant of the finished Chinese-jujube entry `I_086_Da_Zao_Ziziphus_jujuba.md` (tagged AT, CM, DMM, EP, SBJ, SS and FMT). That entry's own Sushruta paragraph treats *Badara* as *Z. mauritiana*, "a close relative of *Z. jujuba*", and it assigns qualities and a *rasayana* grade that were not found in the Sushruta chapters read for this entry. This entry does not edit it. [USER: verify] Two other Volume I entries cover other jujubes (`I_675_ChristsThornJujube_Ziziphus_spina-christi.md`, `I_692_LotusJujube_Ziziphus_lotus.md`). The Sushruta spelling also varies. The gana verse prints *Vadari* (Sutra ch.38, line 348), most chapters print *Vadara*, and Uttara ch.40 prints *Badari* and *Badara* (lines 476 and 661). These are taken here as one name, which is an inference. [USER: verify] Sushruta also lists *Kola* beside *Vadara* in its fruit and drug lists (Sutra ch.39, line 92; Sutra ch.46, lines 988 to 989), so the two are not simply the same word in those lists, yet a translator's bracket at Uttara ch.40 (line 293) reads "Kola (Vadara)". Whether one species or two kinds of jujube are meant is not settled in the text read. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

*The gana (Sutra ch.38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`).* In the Nyagrodhadi group (lines 344 to 351) the list reads "... Rohini, Vanjula, Kadamva, Vadari, Tinduki, Sallaki, Rodhra ..." (line 348). The group's metrical text (lines 353 to 360) says it is beneficial in ulcer, cures disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones, is astringent (*Sangrahi*), is curative in haemoptysis, is "an anti-fat", and "assuages the burning sensation of the body". Membership of the group is a grouping by therapeutic action, grade (b) at most.

*Appendix 3 glossary (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`).* Two Sanskrit spellings are printed. "Badara" stands at line 31353 on the page of B-names, with "See Vadara." (line 31359) in the Latin block. "Vadara" stands at line 33216 on the page of V-names, and the Latin block of that page, counted down by position, gives "Acorus Calamus," (line 33240) for the Vacha entries, "Ziziphus Jujuba." (line 33242) for Vadara and "See 'Somar^ji'" (line 33243) for the next name. The count was checked against the neighbouring names and agrees. The pairing is by OCR column position, not by a printed link. The glossary prints neither "Vadari" nor "Badari".

*Passages read, in the order of the book.*

- **Sutra ch.39** (`SS_Sutra_ch39.txt`, lines 85 to 95), the *Vata-Samshamana* list: "... Bhargi, Karpasi, Vrishchiaali, Pattura, Vadara, Yava, Kola, Kulattha, etc. and the drugs forming the group of Vidarigandhadi-Gana, as well as those belonging to the first two groups of Panchamula (Mahat and Svalpa), are possessed of the general virtue of soothing (restoring to its normal state) the deranged (Vayu) Vata."
- **Sutra ch.46** (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`), the fruit group: Vadara and Kola are both named in the list (lines 988 to 989). At lines 1029 to 1034 the text reads "The Karkandhu, like the Kola or the Vadara, generates the Kapham and Pittam in its unripe or immature stage, while in its ripe or matured stage it is sweet, demulcent, purgative and subdues the Vayu and Pittam. The fruit known as the Sauvira is possessed of properties similar to those of the Vadara; moreover it is sweet, demulcent, and subdues the Vayu and Pittam." The text gives the properties of Vadara only through these comparisons. At lines 2831 to 2838, in the after-drinks, "The acid soup of Indian Jujubes (Kola) should be taken after a meal consisting of any of the afore-said cereals" and "Kola and Vadara wines should be used after having taken the cooked flesh of the fowls of the Vishkira (OCR: \'ishkira) species."
- **Chikitsa ch.10** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch10.txt`, lines 67 to 70), an *Arishta* (a fermented preparation) for skin disease: "one Kudava weight of Vadara and Triphala should be powdered" with a list of other drugs.
- **Chikitsa ch.19** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch19.txt`, lines 289 to 293): a wash for an ulcer with "the barks of S'allaki, Vadara, Vilva, Palas'a ..." and the Kshiri trees.
- **Chikitsa ch.20** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch20.txt`, lines 351 to 363), the infant ailment *Ahi-putana*: plasters of various drugs "pasted together with Kanjika, or of Vadari bark and rock-salt, should be applied".
- **Chikitsa ch.34** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch34.txt`, lines 325 to 334), after emetics or purgatives have gone wrong: "Milk boiled (according to the Kshira-paka-vidhi) with Kasmari fruit, Vadari fruit, Usira and Durva grass subsequently cooled and mixed with the cream of clarified butter and Anjana, should be syringed into the rectum in the manner of an Asthapana-Vasti in the case of an excessive flow of red" (the sentence runs on past the lines read).
- **Sharira ch.10** (`SS_Sharira_ch10.txt`, lines 77 to 79 and 390 to 393): an enema "composed of a decoction of Vadara mixed with Vala, Ativala, Satapushpa" for a pregnant woman, and a newborn "should be fanned with the branches of a Pilu, Nimba, Vadari, or Parushaka tree."
- **Uttara ch.32** (`SS_Uttara_ch32.txt`, lines 22 to 25), in the chapter on child-seizing spirits: a fumigation of "Gandhmidkidi [OCR], Kumbhika, marrow of Vadara-fruits, shells of crabs, and mustard-seeds pounded together and mixed with clarified butter".
- **Uttara ch.40** (`SS_Uttara_ch40.txt`), diarrhoea: "The Ghrita duly cooked with Nagara, Changeri and Kola (Vadara) and with milk, curd (Dadhi) and Amla (Kanjika)" for *Atisara* with pain (lines 291 to 293); "Barks of Badari, Arjuna, Jambu, Amra, S'allaki and Vetasa taken with honey and sugar would cure a case of Atisara" (lines 476 to 478); and "Badara-[OCR: irwxts, read as fruits] boiled with oil and treacle, or cakes prepared from them" for frothy motions (lines 659 to 662).
- **Uttara ch.50** (`SS_Uttara_ch50.txt`, lines 152 to 156), hiccup: a lambative with Pippali, Amalaka, Shunthi, sugar and honey, or with "the kernel (of the stone of) Vadara-fruit".
- **Uttara ch.52** (`SS_Uttara_ch52.txt`, line 154), cough: "Leaves of Vadara pasted with Saindhava salt and fried in clarified butter should be taken".
- **Uttara ch.58** (`SS_Uttara_ch58.txt`, lines 186 to 190), suppression of urine: "Abhaya, Amalaka and Aksha (Vibhitaka) together weighing a Vadara measure should be taken with salt and water". Here *Vadara* is a unit of weight named after the fruit, and the lines read do not give its value.

No rasayana or vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) was found to name Vadari, Vadara or Badara, so no grade (a) status is claimed.

### Active Components

Jujube fruits are generally known to contain sugars, organic acids and mucilage, and the barks of such trees are generally known to contain tannins, which are astringent. The composition of the tree named in the text was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Sushruta records Vadara as a fruit eaten raw and ripe, a wine, a kernel, a leaf, a bark and a branch, in remedies for ulcers, diarrhoea, cough, hiccup, urinary trouble and after-effects of purging, and as a fumigant and a fan for infants. These are the text's own claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol for this tree.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted above: a medicated ghee (Uttara ch.40), fruit boiled in oil and treacle (Uttara ch.40), milk boiled with the fruit and used as an enema (Chikitsa ch.34), leaves fried in ghee with rock salt (Uttara ch.52), bark pasted with rock salt (Chikitsa ch.20), and a fermented preparation (Chikitsa ch.10). Chikitsa ch.10 (line 69) gives "one Kudava weight of Vadara" in a powder for an *Arishta*, and Uttara ch.58 (line 188) gives a "Vadara measure" for a different mixture. No dose of Vadari for a patient is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** The text prints taste and effect, but no *virya* or *vipaka*, for Vadara in the lines read. Sutra ch.39 (line 92) puts Vadara in the list of drugs that soothe the deranged Vayu. Sutra ch.46 (lines 1029 to 1034) says the Karkandhu, "like the Kola or the Vadara", generates the Kapham and Pittam when unripe and is "sweet, demulcent, purgative" and subdues the Vayu and Pittam when ripe, and says the Sauvira has properties similar to the Vadara. Whether the ripe-fruit description applies to the Vadara itself is not clear from the sentence.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Vadari is a named tree in the Nyagrodhadi group and the Vata-soothing list, a fruit of the food chapter, and a bark, leaf, fruit or kernel in many remedies. The Sushruta frame for it is therapeutic and dietetic. The ch.38 gana is a grouping by action, not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. Nothing read ties it to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*, and none of the rasayana or vajikarana chapters searched (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) names it. The status is unattested here, even where another entry assigns a *rasayana* grade to the jujube from the *Charaka Samhita*, a text outside the sources read for this entry. The identification rests on an OCR-paired appendix that prints a different binomial from the spine. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this fruit tree. In Sushruta it is a fruit and a wine, and a leaf, bark or kernel in remedies. The abstentive answer is the correct one: any use is a matter for a qualified practitioner, and the text supplies no long-term regimen.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Sushruta says that the unripe fruit it compares to the Kola or Vadara "generates the Kapham and Pittam" (Sutra ch.46, line 1029). The infant, urinary, bleeding and hiccup uses above are conditions needing medical care, and none of them is advice. Do not give medicinal preparations to a newborn. Do not use in pregnancy. Seek a qualified practitioner, and seek ordinary medical care for diarrhoea with blood, retention of urine or any wound that does not heal.
