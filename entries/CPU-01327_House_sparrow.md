# House sparrow — *Passer domesticus* (Men-Tsee-Khang glossary "Sparrow"); Tibetan *mchhil.pa* ("Chhilpa")
## *The sparrow, as a meat in the Tibetan materia medica; the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints "Sparrow" twice, once with* Passer moutanus *[sic] and once with* Passer domesticus *Hodgson, while Clark's* Quintessence Tantras *prints "mchhil.pa: Sparrow: Passer montanus"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The spine row rests on the glossary of animals in the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`; `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, "Passer domesticus", line 403, citing glossary line 6920). In the glossary's bird list the English column prints "Sparrow" at line 6862 and the transliteration column "Chhilpa" at line 6856, with the Latin "Passer moutanus S." at line 6890; a second "Sparrow" at line 6918 has "Passer domesticus Hodgon" at line 6920. The OCR splits the columns, so these pairings are by order and names.csv records that no Tibetan name is paired to the row. The glossary therefore prints both a tree-sparrow and a house-sparrow name under "Sparrow". The passages below say only "sparrow".

*In the meat classification.* Explanatory Tantra ch.16 (principles of diet) sorts meats into nine sections (Clark 1995, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, lines 659-672). Section (b) is "creatures which excavate the earth with their beaks; these include parrot, cuckoo (Cuculus canorus), stockdove, magpie, Pomatorhinus swinhoei and sparrow" (line 672). Men-Tsee-Khang has "The parrot, cuckoo, pigeon, magpie, Tibetan lark and sparrow are the group of birds which use their beak to dig for food" (line 2905). Meats in general "are sweet tasting and have a sweet post-digestive taste" (Clark line 673). On the flesh of particular animals: "Flesh of chicken and sparrow increases semen and is beneficial for wounds" (Clark line 678); "Chicken meat and meat from a sparrow increase semen production and heals wounds" (Men-Tsee-Khang line 2931).

*In the materia medica of chapter 20.* Under "Meat" (animal products): "mchhil.pa: Sparrow: Passer montanus: [has seven names]. nas.zan bird (lit. 'barley feeder'): Asian sparrow: [has ten names]. rtsangs.pa: a type of Gecko: Agama himalayana sacra: [has eleven names, for example sder.chhags. The flesh of] these three creatures increases semen, [is aphrodisiac, tonic and anodyne, and drains pus]" (Clark line 1170). Men-Tsee-Khang: "The meat of sparrows and mountain-dwelling lizards increase semen" (line 3536). Clark states that square brackets mark interpolations from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries (introduction, line 82), so "aphrodisiac, tonic and anodyne, and drains pus" is commentary, not the root text as Clark gives it. Clark's identification is *Passer montanus*, the tree sparrow, not the house sparrow; the same passages are the basis of the separate entries Sparrow (III_187) and Asian sparrow (III_225). Whether the house-sparrow row should stand beside them or be folded into III_187 is not settled here. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

A sparrow is a very small bird, eaten whole or in part; the flesh is muscle protein with little fat, and the skeleton adds calcium. No analysis was read for the Tibetan materia medica item, and the species is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts say sparrow flesh increases semen and is good for wounds, as one item in a list of meats. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no use for fertility, wound care or any other purpose. Wild songbirds are protected by wildlife law in many countries, a whole small bird is an impractical and unhygienic food source, and Clark observes that "nowadays only very few animal products are used, although many are documented in the traditional texts" (introduction, line 57).

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given for the sparrow beyond "meat" in the two lists. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Wild birds can carry enteric bacteria such as *Salmonella* and avian pathogens, and thorough cooking with hygienic handling is the standard precaution. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Printed qualities: the sparrow, as meat, has the sweet taste and sweet post-digestive taste printed for all meats (Clark line 673; Men-Tsee-Khang line 2923). Its action is "increases semen" and "beneficial for wounds" (Clark line 678). The sparrow belongs to the group of birds that dig with their beaks (Clark line 672; Men-Tsee-Khang line 2905). The Tantra's separate qualities for the dry-land, wet-place and both groups depend on group order, and one group line is unreadable in the OCR (Men-Tsee-Khang line 2915), so the sparrow's place in that scheme is not asserted. ### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The sparrow is a meat in the Tantra's diet chapter and an animal product in its materia medica. Clark's introduction says the three humours are "rooted in attachment, hatred and obscuration respectively" (line 51), so disease is not only bodily in this tradition; but nothing says the sparrow treats that root. "Increases semen" is a therapeutic action listed with the flesh, not a cultivation status. The essence-extraction (*bcud len*) compounds Clark's introduction mentions are made from plants, flowers, calcite and mineral exudate (line 69), not from bird flesh. There is no category of substances for subtle-body practice in this tradition, and none is claimed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. Sparrow flesh is one line in a list of meats and animal medicines; a cultivator has no regimen to take from it, and the tradition's own comment that few animal products are used today argues for leaving it as history.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** wild birds can carry bacteria and avian pathogens; cook any flesh through.
- **Identity:** the Tibetan text names a "sparrow"; Clark identifies it as *Passer montanus*, the glossary prints both species. Do not substitute on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Legal and conservation status:** songbirds are protected in many countries, and the house sparrow's status varies by region; status was not checked. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food or medicine is a moral question for many traditions; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
