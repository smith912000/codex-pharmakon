### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** The spine names this tree *Ficus lacor*. The only Latin name printed against the Sanskrit word in the text on disk is "Ficus Infectoria." (glossary, see below). Neither source read says that the two Latin names are one tree, so the link is partly an inference. [USER: verify] The same glossary prints "Ficus Infectoria." against a second Sanskrit name, *Parkati* (line 32555), so one Latin name stands against two Sanskrit names. Four finished Volume I entries name Plaksha as *Ficus lacor* in a closing cross-reference line only (`I_428_Jivanti_Leptadenia_reticulata.md`, `I_429_Nyagrodha_Ficus_benghalensis.md`, `I_430_Udumbara_Ficus_racemosa.md`, `I_431_Ashvattha_Ficus_religiosa.md`). A search found no entry of its own for this tree.

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

*The gana (Sutra ch.38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`).* Line 344 opens "The Nya'grodha'di Croup:— The drugs known as Nyagrodha, Audumvara, Ashvattha, Plaksha, Madhuka, Kapitana, Kakubha, Amra, Koshamra, Chorakapatra, the two sorts of Jamvu, Piyala, Madhuka (Maula), Rohini, Vanjula, Kadamva, Vadari, Tinduki, Sallaki, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Bhallataka, Palasha, and Nandi-Vriksha, combinedly form the group known as the Nyagodhradi Gana" (lines 344 to 351; "Croup" and "Nyagodhradi" are the OCR and the translator's own spellings). The metrical text that follows (lines 353 to 360) says the group is beneficial in ulcer, cures disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones and "all sorts of secretions of the body in addition to its astringent properties (Sangrahi)", is curative in haemoptysis, is "an anti-fat", and "assuages the burning sensation of the body". Membership of the group is a grouping by therapeutic action, grade (b) at most.

*Appendix 3 glossary (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`).* The name "Plaksha" is printed at line 32527 in the Sanskrit column of its page, and the page's Latin column is printed as a separate block. Counted down both columns by position, the entries just before it are "Piyala" (line 32525, OCR "Piyjtla") against "Buchanania Latifolia." (line 32581), and the entries just after it are "Prachinamalaka" against "Flacourtia cataphracta." (line 32583) and "Prapaundarika" against "Nymphoea Lotus." (line 32584). So "Plaksha" pairs with "Ficus Infectoria." at line 32582. The count was checked from both ends of the block and agrees. The pairing is by OCR column position, not by a printed link.

*Plaksha as a Vanaspati.* Sutra ch.1 (`SS_Sutra_ch01.txt`, lines 420 to 422): "Those trees which bear fruit without blossoming are called the Vanaspatis such as, the Plaksha and the Oudumvura". A translator's footnote at Chikitsa ch.18 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt`, line 125) says "The Vanaspati class consists of Vata, Plaksha, As'vattha and Udumbara trees."

*Uses in the chapters read.*

- **Sutra ch.12** (`SS_Sutra_ch12.txt`, lines 217 to 220), in the treatment of burns: "A plaster composed of Tugakshiri, Plaksha, Chandana, Gairika, and Amritam Guduchi), pasted together with clarified butter, should be applied over a burn of the Samyag-Dagdha type". The part of the tree used is not stated.
- **Sutra ch.46** (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 1120 to 1137), the fruit group: "The fruit of such trees as the Ashvatha, the Plaksha, the Audumvura, etc., which pass by the general name of Kshira-Vrikshas as well as those which are known as the Jamva, Rajadana, Todana, Tinduka, Vakula ... are cooling, and astringent in their effect. They subdue the Pittam and Kapham, tend to produce a condition of parchedness in the system, and have a sweet and astringent taste." The next sentence says that the fruit of the Kshiri-Vrikshas "is heavy and cooling, and is long retained in the stomach in an undigested state. It has a sweet, acid and astringent taste and does not inordinately derange the Vayu."
- **Kalpa ch.7** (`SS_Kalpa_ch07.txt`, line 25), in the antivenom chapter: "Plaksha" stands in a long list of powders (beside Varuna, Lavana, Nichula and Vardhamana) that are added to a boiled alkaline solution, the Ksharagada, with which "Dundhubhis (drums), banners and the gate ways of houses should be smeared" (lines 25 to 47). The role of Plaksha inside the compound is not separately stated.
- **Uttara ch.40** (`SS_Uttara_ch40.txt`, lines 607 to 611), in diarrhoea: "The discharge of blood (in a case of Atisara) may be stopped by a potion consisting of the barks of Piydla, S'dhnali, Plaksha, S'allaki, and of Tinis'a pasted together with milk and mixed with honey." (OCR spellings as printed)

No rasayana or vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) was found to name Plaksha, so no grade (a) status is claimed.

### Active Components

Barks and fruits of this kind of tree are generally known to be rich in tannins, which are astringent. The composition of the tree named in the text was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Sushruta records Plaksha as a bark in a diarrhoea potion, an ingredient in a burn plaster, a member of an astringent group of trees, and a fruit that is cooling and astringent. These are the text's own claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol for this tree.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted above: a plaster with clarified butter (Sutra ch.12, lines 217 to 220) and a bark potion pasted with milk and honey (Uttara ch.40, lines 607 to 611). No quantities are given in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** For the fruit of the Kshira-Vrikshas, which Sutra ch.46 (lines 1120 to 1137) names with Plaksha, the chapter prints "cooling, and astringent", and "a sweet and astringent taste", and for the Kshiri-Vrikshas separately "heavy and cooling" with "a sweet, acid and astringent taste". Whether the first line applies to Plaksha specifically or to the whole list is not stated. No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Plaksha alone in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Plaksha is a named tree in the Nyagrodhadi group, a Vanaspati (Sutra ch.1), a cooling-astringent fruit (Sutra ch.46) and a bark or plaster ingredient in wound, burn and diarrhoea remedies. The Sushruta frame for it is therapeutic. The ch.38 gana is a grouping by action, not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. Nothing read ties Plaksha to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*, and none of the rasayana or vajikarana chapters searched (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) names it. The status is unattested, and the identification with *Ficus lacor* rests on an OCR-paired appendix. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this tree. In Sushruta it is an astringent, cooling tree used in remedies for burns and bleeding diarrhoea. The abstentive answer is the correct one: any use is a matter for a qualified practitioner, and the text supplies no long-term regimen.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. The traditional uses above are for burns and bleeding diarrhoea, which are conditions needing medical care, and none of them is advice. Do not apply a bark or latex plaster to a burn at home, and do not treat bloody diarrhoea with a herbal drink without medical care. Do not use in pregnancy. The identity of the tree is uncertain, and the Latin name should not be used to buy or harvest material.
