### Botanical Identity
Evergreen tree 10–25m; clusters of small spherical fruits (1.5–2.5cm); thin tan/brown shell; translucent white-grey aril (flesh) around dark brown seed — the transparent flesh over the dark seed gives the "eye" appearance; native to South/Southeast Asia; cultivated in southern China, Thailand, Vietnam, India. The dried aril (*long yan rou*) is the drug material; the seed (*long yan he*) is a minor astringent drug.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ)

Listed in upper class:
> *"Lóng yǎn ròu: sweet; warm. Enters Heart and Spleen channels. Nourishes Heart Blood, tonifies Spleen Qi, calms the Shen, benefits intelligence and memory. Used for Heart-Spleen deficiency patterns: palpitations, insomnia, forgetfulness, excessive dreaming, fatigue from Blood deficiency. Prolonged use strengthens the will and body."*

Longan is the specific herb for the *Heart-Spleen deficiency* TCM pattern — a pattern characterised by anxiety, insomnia, poor memory, palpitations, and digestive weakness occurring together, caused by excessive mental/emotional labour depleting both Heart Blood and Spleen Qi simultaneously. This is one of the most clinically prevalent patterns in modern Chinese medicine practice.

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Islamic medicine references longan primarily via trade medicine context — the fruit entered the Middle Eastern medical lexicon through Indian Ocean trade. Ibn Sīnā references tropical fruits from India with warming, nourishing, heart-strengthening properties in a category consistent with longan.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Shen Calming / Anxiolytic

**GABA (free)**: Longan flesh contains measurable free GABA — directly inhibitory neurotransmitter. **Tryptophan**: Serotonin precursor → enhanced serotonergic tone → mood stabilisation, sleep support. **Adenosine**: A1 receptor agonism → sedative/calming; reduces neuronal excitability.

Combined, these three compounds provide a nutritional anxiolytic effect — not pharmacologically potent alone, but collectively supporting the calming applications when consumed regularly.

### 5.2 Haematopoietic / Blood-Tonifying

**Iron + Vitamin C**: Iron (for haemoglobin synthesis) with co-present Vitamin C (enhances non-haem iron absorption by up to 6×) → haematopoietic. This nutritional combination genuinely supports red cell production and validates the Blood-tonifying classification.

### 5.3 Neuroprotective / Cognitive

**Longan polysaccharides (LPs)**: BDNF elevation → hippocampal neurogenesis → memory and learning improvement. Anti-cholinesterase activity → increased acetylcholine → cognitive support.

**Corilagin**: Neuroprotective against oxidative stress; anti-inflammatory (NF-κB inhibition).

### 5.4 Immunostimulatory

LPs → macrophage and NK cell activation → enhanced innate immunity → anti-fatigue and anti-ageing effects.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Anti-fatigue / cognitive**: Multiple animal studies; limited human RCTs but consistent traditional use in postpartum recovery and mental exhaustion.

**Antioxidant**: Human studies confirm significant increase in serum antioxidant capacity with longan consumption.

**Sedative/sleep**: Mechanistically supported; limited clinical trials.

**Neuroprotective**: Animal models suggest protection against cognitive decline with LP administration.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The dragon's eye**: The appearance of the peeled longan — a sphere of translucent white flesh around a dark central seed, exactly resembling an eye — is one of the most striking visual analogies in botanical medicine. Traditional Chinese medicine took this seriously: the *dragon's eye* is given to nourish the Shen (mind-spirit), which in Chinese cosmology is associated with the eyes and with the Heart-Mind complex. The visual signature mapped onto the application domain.

**Heart-Spleen deficiency in modernity**: The TCM pattern that longan specifically addresses — Heart-Spleen deficiency from excessive intellectual labour — is arguably the most prevalent pattern in modern knowledge-economy workers. The combination of cognitive overwork, irregular eating, and insufficient rest creates precisely this pattern: anxiety, insomnia, palpitations, poor memory, and digestive weakness occurring together. That a subtropical fruit became the foundational food-medicine for this pattern suggests long experience with its effects.

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Sweet; warm. Channels: Heart (心), Spleen (脾). Actions: Nourish Heart Blood and Yin, tonify Spleen Qi, calm Shen, benefit intelligence. Primary herb for *Heart-Spleen Deficiency* pattern. Pairs with: *dang gui*, *da zao*, *yuan zhi* (Polygala), *bai zi ren* (arborvitae seed).

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Phlegm-damp / obesity**: Sweet/warm nature — avoid in damp-heat or phlegm-excess patterns
- **High blood sugar**: High simple sugar content — caution in T2DM; use dried form in small amounts only
- **Mania / excess fire**: Warming nature contraindicated in excess Yang/fire patterns
- Very safe at food doses; the food/medicine boundary is minimal for longan
