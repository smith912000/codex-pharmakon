# Actinolite (Yang Qi Shi) — Yang Qi Shi, "Actinolitum" (Yang 1998); other name Bai Shi (White Stone); list row 陽起石
## *Actinolite (a calcium-magnesium-iron amphibole silicate) in the Divine Farmer's Classic; Yang 1998 prints "Actinolitum" for Yang Qi Shi and no footnote doubts the identification* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yang Qi Shi is printed in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) on PDF page 29, in the run of "Jades and Stones: Middle Class". The page itself prints no heading in the OCR; the heading is printed at the top of page 28 (line 1097) and at the top of page 30 (line 1168), and the entry lies between them. The text (lines 1143-1148): "Yang Qi Shi (Actinolitum) is sour and nontoxic. It mainly treats flooding and leaking,55 breaking the blood in the uterus, and concretion and conglomeration bound qi. [It also treats] cold and heat, abdominal pain, infertility, and impotence, and it supplements insufficiency. Its other name is Bai Shi (White Stone). It is produced in the mountains and valleys of Qi Shan.56" The bracketed "[It also treats]" is the translator's.

Yang's footnote 55 (lines 1154-1161) explains "flooding and leaking" as damage of the chong and ren vessels: "Flooding refers to sudden, profuse vaginal bleeding, while leaking refers to continual, usually scanty dribbling of blood from the vagina", and says the translator prefers to translate the words and not gloss the meaning in modern terms. Footnote 56 (after it) says that "Qi Shan is in the suburbs of Jinan, the capital of present-day Shandong Province". The OCR of the footnote lines is damaged in places; the quotations restore only what is clear.

The list `_sources/shennong.csv` carries the row as 陽起石 (line 125), and `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` has the row "Yang Qi Shi" (line 23) with the identification "Actinolitum". The spine's note is "middle", with no identity doubt. The other name Bai Shi (White Stone) is also the fourth of the five names printed for the halloysite clays (line 1022, as "Bai Shi"), so the two should not be confused. The sources read give no further description of the stone.

### Active Components

Actinolite is a calcium-magnesium-iron silicate of the amphibole group, with the formula Ca2(Mg,Fe)5Si8O22(OH)2 (the spine writes the iron and magnesium together). It forms green to grey, needle-like or fibrous crystals. A fibrous form of actinolite is one of the minerals regulated as asbestos. The sources read give no composition for the stone Yang translates.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records the stone for flooding and leaking (uterine bleeding as Yang explains the terms), for blood in the uterus, for bound qi masses, abdominal pain, infertility and impotence, and says that it "supplements insufficiency". The Codex does not endorse these claims and offers no protocol. Because fibrous actinolite is an asbestos mineral and the sources give no way to tell the forms apart, swallowing or handling it as a powder is not recommended.

### Scientific Correlation

Inhaled asbestos fibres, among them fibrous actinolite, are a well-established cause of asbestosis, lung cancer and mesothelioma; that is a fact about inhaled fibres and not about swallowing a stone. Non-fibrous actinolite is a rock-forming mineral with industrial uses as a decorative stone. No medical benefit of swallowing actinolite for the conditions the Classic names is established. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- SBJ: "sour and nontoxic" (Yang line 1143). Class: Middle (headings lines 1097 and 1168). The text states a toxicity here ("nontoxic"), which is not a hot or cold quality; no temperature is printed. Yang's footnote 32 (lines 849-855) explains how he renders flavour and qi.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: a middle-grade drug (中品).* Yang Qi Shi lies between two printings of "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (Yang lines 1097 and 1168); the page itself prints no heading, and the entry is placed by bracketing, as Mica (Yun Mu) is in the Superior run. Yang's preface says the medium class medicinals "mainly nurture personality and correspond to humanity. They may or may not be toxic, and [therefore,] one should weigh and ponder before putting them to their appropriate use. If one intends to control disease, supplement vacuity, and replenish exhaustion, one should base [one's efforts] on the middle class" (lines 385-390). The monograph prints "it supplements insufficiency" (lines 1145-1146), an English phrase close to the preface's aim for the class ("supplement vacuity"); whether one Chinese term stands behind both is not shown by Yang's English. It prints no longevity, lightness or immortality line and no phrase about protracted taking, and its own word is "nontoxic". The grade is a statement of the Classic as translated by Yang in 1998 and is not carried forward from later materia medica works.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: not a "nourish life" drug for long use, but a drug for controlling disease and supplementing vacuity under care. Nothing in the sources supports taking actinolite, and the asbestos hazard of its fibrous form is a reason against. For a cultivator the entry is a record of how the Classic ranked a white fibrous stone. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Fibrous actinolite is an asbestos mineral; inhaled fibres cause serious lung disease. Powdered stone sold as a remedy may be a different mineral or may be contaminated. Mineral medicines are unstandardised and their sale is regulated in many places. The Classic's "nontoxic" is not a claim of fact.
