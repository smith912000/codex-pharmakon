### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** The spine names this tree *Diospyros peregrina*. The Latin printed in the glossary on the line after "Tinduka" is "Dyospyros Glutinosa. Eubyopteris." (OCR, line 33131). Neither source read says that the spine's binomial and the glossary's are the same tree, so the link is partly an inference. [USER: verify] The Sushruta spelling varies: *Tinduki* in the gana verse and in Sutra ch.12, *Tinduka* in most other chapters, and *Timduka* (OCR) in Sutra ch.42. These are taken as one name, which is an inference. [USER: verify] Two lookalike words occur and are not treated here: *Kakadani (Vayasa-tinduka)* in a plaster at Chikitsa ch.18 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt`, line 118), and *Atimukta (Tinduka)* among pot-herbs in Uttara ch.45 (`SS_Uttara_ch45.txt`, line 130), where the translator's bracket equates a second plant with the word. [USER: verify] No earlier Volume I entry for this tree was found.

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

*The gana (Sutra ch.38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`).* In the Nyagrodhadi group (lines 344 to 351) the list reads "... Kadamva, Vadari, Tinduki, Sallaki, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra ..." (line 348). The group's metrical text (lines 353 to 360) says it is beneficial in ulcer, cures disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones, is astringent (*Sangrahi*), is curative in haemoptysis, is "an anti-fat", and "assuages the burning sensation of the body". Membership of the group is a grouping by therapeutic action, grade (b) at most.

*Appendix 3 glossary (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`).* On the page that holds this name, the glossary prints each Latin name on the line after its Sanskrit name: "Tinduka" (line 33129) is followed by "Dyospyros Glutinosa. Eubyopteris." (line 33131), "Tinisa" (line 33133) by "Dalbergia Oujeinensis." (line 33135) and "Tintida" (line 33137) by "Tamarindus Indica." (line 33139). This page is therefore not a pairing by column position, and the Latin sits directly under the name.

*Passages read, in the order of the book.*

- **Sutra ch.12** (`SS_Sutra_ch12.txt`, lines 227 to 246), in the treatment of burns of the *Ati-Dagdha* (over-burnt) type: "the affected part should be dusted over with pulverised Shali rice, or a plaster composed of the pulverised skin of Tinduki and clarified butter pasted together, should be applied over its surface." A translator's footnote adds, "Several authorities prescribe Tinduki bark and human cranium powdered together and mixed with clarified butter, while others prescribe a decoction of Tinduki bark." The human cranium is an ingredient of those other authorities, not of the main text, and it is recorded here as history and not as a practice.
- **Sutra ch.42** (`SS_Sutra_ch42.txt`, lines 350 to 353), the astringent group: "... Triphala, Shallaki, Jambu, Amra, Vakula, Timduka fruits, Katakha fruits ..."
- **Sutra ch.46** (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`), the fruit group: Tinduka is named among the fruits that are "cooling, and astringent" (lines 1120 to 1129; see the entry for Plaksha, `I_908_Plaksha.md`, for the full sentence). At lines 1144 to 1151 the text reads "Unripe Tinduka fruit is astringent, and heavy in digestion and produces Vayu in the organism, while in its ripe or fully matured stage it is sweet, and subdues the Kapham and Pittam."
- **Chikitsa ch.2** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch02.txt`, lines 486 to 496), the chapter on traumatic wounds: an oil cooked with a long list of drugs including "Piyala seeds, raw and tender Tinduka fruit, or with as many of them as would be available, should be regarded as a good healing remedy in respect of all non-malignant traumatic sores or ulcers."
- **Chikitsa ch.11** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch11.txt`), the chapter on *Prameha* (urinary disorders): "a decoction of Guduchi, seeds of Tinduka, Kasmarya and Kharjura, mixed with honey, in a case of Sonita-meha" (lines 150 to 153), and "a decoction of Tinduka, Kapittha, Sirisha, Palasa, Patha, Murva and Dusparsa mixed with honey" for *Hasti-meha* (lines 173 to 177). At lines 236 to 243 the chapter lays down a regimen for a rich man with *Prameha*, who "should live on Syamaka, Kapittha, Tinduka and Asmantaka and live among the deer", straight after the passage that sends a poor and friendless patient to "live on alms, lead a life of perfect continence like an ascetic, forego the use of shoes and umbrella and walk a hundred Yojanas or more on foot". That is a treatment regimen for a disease and is not a *rasayana*.
- **Chikitsa ch.21** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch21.txt`, lines 69 to 71), in the chapter on sores of the penis: sores "healed up with the application of an oil, cooked with Dhava, Asva-Karna, Paitanga, S'allaki and Tinduka".
- **Uttara ch.21** (`SS_Uttara_ch21.txt`, lines 275 to 278): "Powders of the barks of Tinduka, Abhaya, Rodhra, Samanga and of Amalaka mixed with honey and the expressed juice of Kapittha should be similarly used."
- **Uttara ch.31** (`SS_Uttara_ch31.txt`, lines 15 to 19), in the chapter on an attack by Revati: "Medicated Ghrita duly cooked with the decoction of Dhava, Asva-karna, Kakubha, Dhataki and Tinduka and with the drugs of the Kakolyadi group (as Kalka) should be employed internally as a draught."
- **Uttara ch.40** (`SS_Uttara_ch40.txt`, lines 233 to 236), diarrhoea: one of the listed drug groups is "barks of Eranda, Tinduka, Dadima, Kutaja and of Sami".
- **Sharira ch.10** (`SS_Sharira_ch10.txt`, lines 105 to 109): the lying-in chamber for the four social orders is made "of Vilva, Vata, Tinduka and Bhallataka wood", the four woods being matched in order to a Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya and Shudra mother.

No rasayana or vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) was found to name Tinduka or Tinduki, so no grade (a) status is claimed.

### Active Components

The unripe fruits and barks of trees of this kind are generally known to be rich in tannins, which are astringent, and the ripe fruits of the ebony genus (*Diospyros*) are generally sweet. The composition of the tree named in the text was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Sushruta records Tinduki as an astringent fruit, a bark for burns, a seed or bark in remedies for urinary disorders, sores and diarrhoea, and a timber for the lying-in chamber. These are the text's own claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol for this tree, and in particular the burn, urinary and sore uses are not self-treatment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted above: a plaster of the skin with ghee for an over-burnt area (Sutra ch.12), an oil cooked with the fruit (Chikitsa ch.2) or the bark (Chikitsa ch.21), a decoction of seeds or of the plant with honey (Chikitsa ch.11), and bark powder with honey and Kapittha juice (Uttara ch.21). No quantity is given for Tinduki in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** Sutra ch.46 prints "cooling, and astringent" and "a sweet and astringent taste" for the group of fruits that includes Tinduka (lines 1120 to 1129), and separately "Unripe Tinduka fruit is astringent, and heavy in digestion and produces Vayu", and when ripe "sweet, and subdues the Kapham and Pittam" (lines 1148 to 1151). The sentence just before it (the Todana, lines 1144 to 1148) also prints "(heat-making) in its potency" and cannot be tied to Tinduka. Sutra ch.42 puts the fruit in the astringent group. No *vipaka* is printed for it in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Tinduki is a named tree in the Nyagrodhadi group, an astringent fruit in the flavour chapter, a skin, bark, seed or fruit in remedies for burns, sores, urinary disorders and diarrhoea, and a timber in the lying-in chamber. The Sushruta frame for it is therapeutic. The ch.38 gana is a grouping by action, not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. The Chikitsa ch.11 passage that sets a rich man with *Prameha* to live on its fruit among the deer belongs to the treatment of a disease, and it is a behavioural regimen, not a rasayana. Nothing read ties Tinduki to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*, and none of the rasayana or vajikarana chapters searched (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) names it. The status is unattested, and the identification with *Diospyros peregrina* rests on an appendix that prints a different binomial. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this tree. In Sushruta it is an astringent fruit and a bark or seed in remedies, and in one disease regimen a forest food. The abstentive answer is the correct one: any use is a matter for a qualified practitioner, and the text supplies no long-term regimen.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Astringent preparations may constipate, and Sushruta says the unripe fruit "produces Vayu". The burn, sore, urinary and diarrhoea uses above are conditions needing medical care, and none of them is advice. A burn must not be dressed with bark and ghee at home, and the human-cranium preparation recorded in the footnote to Sutra ch.12 is history only, is not a remedy, and must not be reproduced; human remains are legally and ethically protected. Do not use in pregnancy.
