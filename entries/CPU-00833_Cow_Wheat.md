### Origin & Tradition

**Identity warning.** This row was promoted from MASTER_INDEX (two traditions: Avicenna, Dioscorides) and neither tag has a locus for *Melampyrum pratense* itself. The Dioscorides tag was attached by a stem and genus match, and the Avicenna tag has no list row. The entry is therefore a record of what was searched and what was not found. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), and the name index is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`. No chapter was found whose translator's identification is *Melampyrum*, cow wheat or any related plant. Four chapters were attached to this row by the genus-level extraction (`EXTRACTION_GAPS.md`, line 278): 2-107 PUROI (heading at line 5422), 2-112 KRIMNON (5454), 2-114 ATHERA (5463) and 2-118 CHONDROS (5497). All four are cereal chapters, glossed *Triticum* (wheat) and spelt wheat (*Triticum spelta*). They share the English word "wheat" with the common name and nothing else, and they are not about *Melampyrum*.

Two places in the text do carry the Greek name:

- **4-117 MUAGROS** (heading at line 9079; text at line 9081). The translator's suggestion is "Myagrum sativum, Camelina sativa — Camelina, Gold Of Pleasure". The text reads: "Myagros is a brushy kind of herb, two feet tall, with pale leaves similar those of rubia [dyer's madder]. The fat seed is like a neck or whorl. They use it, scorching and bruising it, and rubbing the stems, and using them instead of a candle. It seems that the fat from them makes sleek and smooth any roughness of the body. It is also called melampyrom." The synonym index lists "melampyrom 667" (line 11142). This is a synonym attached to a plant the translator identifies as camelina, so it does not attest *Melampyrum pratense*. Whether the ancient name was ever applied to the cow wheat of the botanists is not shown in the text read. [USER: verify]
- **3-139 KRATAIOGONON** (heading at line 7749; text at line 7752). The chapter is identified by the translator as hawthorn (*Crataegus*). It says only that the plant "has leaves similar to melampyrum", used as a comparison for the leaf. No identification of the comparison plant is given.

The only Dioscorides content that touches the name is therefore a leaf-shape comparison and a synonym. Neither gives a medicinal use for cow wheat.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** No locus was found. `_sources/avicenna.csv` (56 rows) has no *Melampyrum* or cow wheat row, and no Canon chapter text is on disk (`Sources/CM_Avicenna` holds an overview and a README only). The tag rests on a MASTER_INDEX row (idx 1693), whose source for the claim is not shown.

**Related entries.** No earlier entry for *Melampyrum* exists in Volume I. The hemiparasitic relatives I_836 Yellow Rattle (*Rhinanthus minor*), I_856 Lousewort and I_828 Red Bartsia are in the same family, and I_828 names *Melampyrum* in a cross-reference. They are not merged here.

### Active Components

*Melampyrum* is a genus of semi-parasitic annual herbs of the family Orobanchaceae. Plants of this family are generally reported to contain iridoid glycosides such as aucubin. The composition of *M. pratense* specifically was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and for this plant not even that. The sources read give no use for cow wheat. The Dioscorides uses at 4-117 belong to the plant he calls *myagros*, which the translator takes to be camelina, and they are not carried over. The Codex gives no protocol for *Melampyrum*.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation for this plant is given in the sources retrieved. The only preparation near the name is at 4-117 (seed scorched and bruised, stems rubbed, the fat used on the skin and as a lamp fuel), and it is attributed there to *myagros*. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications
