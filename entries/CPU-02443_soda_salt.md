# Soda salt — lba.tsha (Clark 1995, "Soda Salt: Saltpetre"); Wa tsha (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011), rGyud-bzhi Explanatory Tantra ch.20

## *A salt "which resembles crystalline lumps of 'rock sugar'" with an extremely strong salty taste, said to dispel goitre; Clark glosses it "Saltpetre" and lists it in the salty group as "soda salt (saltpetre)", while the name "soda" and a neighbouring entry for "salsoda (sodium bicarbonate)" point elsewhere, so the identification is unresolved (low confidence)* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The Codex's list row is `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 215, "Wa tsha, , mineral, rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Efficacy of Medicinal Substances); Four Tantras of Tibetan Medicine (English trans.)", flagged as an uncertain identification. Two texts were read.

*Clark 1995* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). Chapter 20 lists the salts in part (3), the earth medicines (line 802). The entry (line 935) reads:

"lba.tsha: Soda Salt: Saltpetre: [is a type of salt which resembles crystalline lumps of 'rock sugar.' It has an extremely strong, salty taste and] dispels goiters. [Moreover when soda salt is not available the salt from cave floors is a suitable substitute]."

By the bracket convention of Clark's introduction (line 82; square brackets carry the commentaries), the tantra's own words are "dispels goiters"; the look, the taste and the substitute are commentary. The convention was not checked against the Tibetan. [USER: verify] The salty-taste group, stated earlier in the chapter (line 767), is: "rock salt, wood salt, horn salt, black 'sanchal' salt, white mineral salt, mirabilite, soda salt (saltpetre), white rock salt, ash salt, aragonite (Tartar salt) and salsoda (sodium bicarbonate)". Soda salt is thus listed beside, and separately from, salsoda; and the entry for mirabilite (ze.tsha, line 860) is glossed "Saltpetre: Mirabilite", so the English word "saltpetre" is used in this translation for two different Tibetan entries.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`). The Explanatory Tantra's salty-taste group lists "Wa tsha" with a note (line 3211, with footnote 1 at line 3274): "Wa tsha is an extremely bad tasting salt found in northern Tibet. It looks like a mass of sugar and cures and prevents goitre." In the salt-medicine summary (line 3400, page 203): "Wa tsha eliminates goitre." The match of Clark's *lba.tsha* to Men-Tsee-Khang's *Wa tsha* rests on the same action (goitre), the same sugar-like look and the sound of the names; neither text says they are the same. [USER: verify]

**What is not given.** In the passages read, neither text gives a potency or post-digestive taste for the salt by itself beyond "extremely strong, salty" (Clark's commentary), a dose, a preparation or a formula containing it.

**Identity.** The Codex name "soda salt" is Clark's translation of *lba.tsha*; his gloss "Saltpetre" would in ordinary usage be potassium nitrate, whereas "soda" suggests a sodium salt, and Clark separately lists "salsoda (sodium bicarbonate)". The Men-Tsee-Khang note describes a "bad tasting" salt of northern Tibet that "looks like a mass of sugar". Salts of that description in the region include natron-type sodium carbonate deposits (a general fact, not read in the texts). The sources retrieved do not settle it. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None is stated in the texts. As a general fact, the candidates named by the translations are potassium nitrate (saltpetre), sodium bicarbonate and sodium carbonate; all are water-soluble salts. Which one the tantra means is not settled.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** Both texts record the salt as a remedy for goitre. Goitre (an enlarged thyroid) has several causes, some of them serious, and needs medical assessment. Concentrated alkaline or nitrate salts are irritants when swallowed in quantity. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation for soda salt. Clark's commentary names only a substitution: "when soda salt is not available the salt from cave floors is a suitable substitute" (line 935). No form, route, measure or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] As general facts: goitre is commonly linked to iodine deficiency in mountain regions, among other causes, and no mineral salt of the kinds named is a treatment for it; sodium bicarbonate is an antacid, and saltpetre is an oxidising salt that is toxic in large amounts.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT).** The salt is placed in the salty-taste group (Clark line 767; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3211). Clark's commentary gives an "extremely strong, salty taste". The Men-Tsee-Khang note calls it "extremely bad tasting". The salty taste is, in this tradition's own statement, one of those that pacifies wind (*rlung*) disorders (Men-Tsee-Khang, the passage after line 3211: "sweet, sour, salty and hot tastes pacify loong disorders"); that is stated of the taste in general and is not an action printed for this salt. No other classification is carried across.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The tantra lists the salt among the salty-taste medicines and gives it one therapeutic action. The only substance in the same chapter called "unsurpassed as an essence-extraction substance" is *brag.zhun* (Clark line 870); nothing like this is said of soda salt, and no *bcud len* use of it was found. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation. It is a salt the tantra names for goitre, of uncertain identity and with no preparation given, and goitre needs medical assessment. It is not a recommended practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Goitre and neck swellings:** medical conditions; see a doctor.
- **Identity unsettled:** potassium nitrate, sodium bicarbonate and sodium carbonate differ in toxicity; nothing should be bought or used on the strength of the name.
- **High-sodium or nitrate salts:** harmful in quantity, especially for people with heart, kidney or blood-pressure problems and for infants.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
