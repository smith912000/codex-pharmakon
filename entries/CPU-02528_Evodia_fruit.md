# Evodia fruit — 吴茱萸 *wu zhuyu* (Shennong bencao jing; Sun's note says the early text had 茱萸 *zhuyu* without 吴)

## *The fruit of a small aromatic tree, our identification (high confidence) as Tetradium ruticarpum (A.Juss.) T.G.Hartley, synonym Evodia rutaecarpa (A.Juss.) Benth., from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification; the text describes the plant only through Guo Pu's remark in Sun's note* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Shennong bencao jing (SBJ).** Evodia is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The identification is ours. The source is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`); the entry is in the middle classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 吴茱萸 *wu zhuyu*, same file, line 2385). The preface's list of middle-class wood drugs names it (line 1677). Under the head Sun adds a bracketed note that the *Taiping yulan* quotes the entry "without the character 吴, which is right" (引无吴字，是); that is Sun's textual opinion that the early name was *zhuyu*.

**The Shennong text proper** (line 2385): "Flavour pungent, warm. It governs warming the middle, bringing qi down, stopping pain, cough with counter-flow, cold and heat; removing damp and blood-obstruction (血痹); driving out wind evil; opening the *couli* (the texture or interstices of the flesh; the file notes that the old text wrote a different character and the *Yulan* another, and Sun chooses 凑). The root kills the three worms. One name [a character is missing in the file]. It grows in mountain valleys." (味辛，温。主温中，下气，止痛，咳逆，寒热，除湿、血痹，逐风邪，开凑理。根：杀三虫。一名 ，生山谷。) The fruit is not named in the paragraph; the paragraph speaks of the drug and then of "the root", which implies that the first part is for the fruit or another part, but the text does not say so. [USER: verify]

**The Mingyi bielu layer** (line 2387): "Grows at Yuanju. Gather on the ninth day of the ninth month; dry in the shade." (生冤句。九月九日采，阴干。)

**Sun Xingyan's note (案, lines 2389 and 2391).** Sun gathers philological quotations. The *Shuowen* defines the name as of the *zhuyu* kind; the *Han lü* (Han statutes) say that Kuaiji presented a *dou* (a measure) of a related product (a character is missing); the *Guangya* and the *Sancang* give equivalents (characters missing). The *Erya* names it with Guo Pu's gloss that the fruit "grows in clusters forming a tuft, and today Jiangdong also calls it [name missing], like *zhuyu* but smaller and red" (聚生成房貌… 似茱萸而小，赤色). He cites the *Liji* ("the three sacrificial animals use it", 三牲用) with Zheng Xuan's gloss that the [character missing] *jian* named there is *zhuyu* (the sense of *jian* is not clear from the file), the *Fan Zi Jiran* ("*zhuyu* comes from the Sanfu"), and Tao Hongjing's remark on the *Liji* name versus the common name for the fruit. Many characters are dropped in the file; I have quoted only legible words.

**Preface.** The middle class "mainly nourishes the nature, to correspond to man; toxic or non-toxic, to be weighed as appropriate; for one who wishes to check disease and fill out the thin, take from the middle classic" (line 1677).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, the fruit of *Tetradium ruticarpum* contains indoloquinazoline alkaloids (evodiamine and rutaecarpine are the best known), together with volatile oil, and the genus belongs to the rue family (Rutaceae), many members of which are aromatic. These are general statements of plant chemistry, not findings of the text.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records a pungent, warm drug for pain, cough, "cold and heat", damp and blood-obstruction, and wind evil, and a root that "kills the three worms". The Codex does not turn these into modern indications. Cough, pain and obstruction in the limbs have many causes and call for diagnosis. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong paragraph gives no preparation and no dose. The only handling is the Mingyi harvest rule: gather on the ninth day of the ninth month and dry in the shade (line 2387). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Evodia fruit is a recognised drug in Chinese pharmacy today and its alkaloids have been studied in laboratory settings; that is a general statement and not a study cited here. Whether it acts on pain, cough or damp as the text says has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong).** One flavour and one nature are printed: pungent (辛), warm (温). Middle class by placement. No further quality is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle classic, the class the preface (line 1677) says is for nourishing the nature, toxic or non-toxic, to be weighed as appropriate, and for checking disease. The paragraph has no *jiu fu* ("taken for long") clause and no life-nourishing wording of the upper-class kind. The statements it makes are therapeutic (warming the middle, bringing qi down, stopping pain). Its middle-class placement frames it as a drug whose use is to be weighed, not as one to take for long.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator has no regimen here. The classic places the drug among those to be "weighed as appropriate", gives no dose and no course, and says nothing about taking it for long. Treat it as a course drug for a defined complaint, if at all, and stop when the complaint is gone.

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity and dose.** The classic's middle class is "toxic or non-toxic, to be weighed as appropriate". The fruit is a pungent drug with alkaloids; no safe dose can be stated from the text. Do not take it on this entry's authority. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, children, medicines.** The text says nothing on any of these, and no use is supported. [USER: verify]
- **Identification.** *Evodia* and related *Tetradium* fruits are treated as one drug in current pharmacy, but this text does not say which species it means; the Guo Pu quotation is about the *Erya* plant, not a species.
- **Root.** The paragraph assigns "kills the three worms" to the root only. The Codex does not recommend any root preparation.
