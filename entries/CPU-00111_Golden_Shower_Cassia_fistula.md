### Origin & Tradition

Golden shower is a spectacular flowering tree of the Indian subcontinent, hung in season with cascades of golden blossom (hence the name and its status as a celebrated ornamental and the state flower of Kerala). Its medicine is the **fruit** — a long, cylindrical, woody pod ("pudding-pipe") whose dark, sweetish pulp between the seeds is a classic **gentle laxative**. In Ayurveda the drug is **Aragvadha** ("disease-killer"), and in the Unani and later European pharmacy the pod pulp was "purging cassia" (*cassia fistula*), a recognised mild aperient.

The defining virtue of Cassia fistula pulp is that it is a **mild, sweet, cooling laxative gentle enough for children, the elderly, the pregnant (with care) and the debilitated** — a contrast to the drastic purgatives (colocynth, scammony, the spurges, this volume). It relieves constipation softly, cools "heat," and was given for biliousness and as a vehicle to soften the action of stronger purgatives. Ayurveda also uses the leaf, bark and flowers — for skin diseases (the leaf paste, *Aragvadha* being a noted skin remedy), and for fever and inflammatory conditions.

The gentle purgative action comes from **anthraquinone glycosides** (the same broad class as senna and rhubarb, but milder in the sweet pulp), supported by the pulp's sugars and mucilage which soften and bulk the stool.

### Contraindications

- **Intestinal obstruction / inflammatory bowel disease / undiagnosed abdominal pain.** As with all stimulant laxatives.
- **Prolonged habitual use.** Anthraquinone laxatives should not be used chronically (dependence, electrolyte loss).
