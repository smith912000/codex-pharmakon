# Ganoderma, black — Hei Zhi 黑芝 / 黑芝 (Shennong bencao jing); alias Xuan Zhi 玄芝 ("Dark Ganoderma")

## *One of the six colour-named "zhi" 芝 of the Shennong classic, a fungus of the zhi group; the species is not settled. The pre-filled guess "Ganoderma sinense" is not supported by any text on disk: Yang 1998 prints only the Latin-form colour label "Ganodenna Nigra" (OCR-damaged), and the purple zhi, not the black, is the one the Codex files under G. sinense (I_284). Identification: fungus of the zhi group, by the Chinese name and Yang's label; species unresolved; confidence low* | *(Volume IV — Fungal/Algal, shelved in Volume I)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Black zhi is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 English edition (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) and a plain e-text (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_plain_daizhige.txt`) are read as second and third witnesses. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "ganoderma, black", from `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` (row "Hei Zhi", "Herbs: Superior Class, PDF p.37 (line 1431)", Latin label "Ganodenna Nigra"); line 1431 is a line of Yang's text.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), Sun Xingyan edition.** The entry is 黑芝 in the upper classic (卷一 上经). In Sun's e-text it is run on to the end of the red zhi's paragraph at line 613 (一名丹芝。黑芝味咸，平。…), and the paragraph breaks off at 延年、 where the file's line ends, so the closing words and the alias are lost in this file. What survives reads: 味咸，平 ("salty, balanced"); 主癃 ("governs urinary dribbling block", Yang's rendering); 利水道 ("disinhibits the water passageways"); 益肾气 ("boosts kidney qi"); 通九窍 ("frees the nine orifices"); 聪察 ("sharp hearing and perception", Yang: "sharpens the hearing"); 久食，轻身、不老、延年 ("taken for a long time: lightens the body, no ageing, prolongs years") and then it stops. The plain e-text, `Shennong_plain_daizhige.txt` line 111, gives the whole paragraph: ...久食轻身不老，延年神仙。一名玄芝。生山谷, that is, the clause ends with 神仙 ("immortal-spirit"), the alias is Xuan zhi 玄芝, and it "grows in mountains and valleys". The Mingyi bielu layer (Sun line 643) says 黑芝，生恒山 ("black zhi grows on Mount Heng") and, of the six zhi together, 皆无毒，六月、八月采 ("all non-toxic, gathered in the sixth and eighth months"). Sun's note (案, line 645) quotes the *Baopuzi*, "Xian yao pian" (as Sun cites it), for the colours, and for black it reads 黑者，如泽漆 ("the black is like *zeqi*"). The comparison is given without further explanation, and the plant it points to is not identified in the sources read. Line 639 records that the old text had "six kinds" which Sun merged (旧作六种，今并).

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 1431 onward, his page 37): "Hei Zhi (Ganoderma Nigra) is salty and balanced. It mainly treats urinary dribbling block, disinhibits the water passageways, boosts the kidney qi, frees the nine orifices, and sharpens the hearing. Protracted taking may make the body light, prevent senility, and prolong life so as to make one an immortal. Its other name is Xuan Zhi (Dark Ganoderma). It grows in mountains and valleys." Yang's footnote 79 (line 1400) says: "In this book, Ganoderma is divided into six types depending on its color... it is called Immortal Weed. It is not only able to prevent and treat disease but is said to even cultivate virtues." That is Yang's commentary, not the classic.

**What this drug is not.** The name "Hei Zhi" is also used in modern sources for other fungi. None is attested in the texts on disk. The sources on disk give no species and describe no plant beyond the Baopuzi colour comparison quoted by Sun. A search of the other texts on disk for the Chinese and pinyin names found nothing further.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Urinary dribbling block ("癃"), freeing the water passageways, boosting kidney qi, freeing the nine orifices, sharpening hearing.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no manner of eating it, no species.

The Codex does not translate these terms into modern diagnoses and gives no protocol. Nobody should forage or buy an unidentified fungus because of a classical colour name.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph says 久食 ("taken for a long time") and gives no preparation. The only handling statement is the Mingyi layer's harvest note for all six zhi (sixth and eighth months, line 643). The classic's upper-class preface (Sun line 43; Yang lines 352 to 358) says that upper drugs may be taken in large amounts and for a long time without harming people. That is a statement about the class and is not a dose for this fungus. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* Black zhi stands in the upper classic (上经), whose preface (Sun line 43) reads 上药一百二十种，为君，主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经: the upper drugs are "sovereigns, mainly nourishing life (养命) to correspond with heaven, non-toxic; taken in large amounts and for a long time they do not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body (轻身), boost qi, not age (不老) and prolong years (延年), base [the effort] on the upper classic". The drug's own paragraph carries the promise: 久食，轻身、不老、延年 (Sun line 613, which survives to this point), and with 神仙 in the plain e-text (line 111) and in Yang (line 1431 onward). Sun and Yang place the drug in the same class (upper), so there is no difference to state. *Qing shen* 輕身 is the tradition's own phrase and is not weight loss or any pharmacological effect. The attestation is for the black zhi named in the classic and does not establish that any mushroom now sold or foraged is that zhi. The Neidan (inner alchemy) reading of the zhi is an overlay that cultivators bring and is not a *bencao* category; it is not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade drug in the classic's own scheme, the kind taken long-term for nourishing life, but with no identified species, no dose and no preparation in the record. A cultivator can read it as the tradition's own account of an upper-grade fungus and should not act on it by eating any fungus.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The black zhi has no settled species. Do not gather or eat wild fungi on the strength of this entry; many are poisonous and some are lethal.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, people on medicines.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Urinary retention and sensory loss.** "Urinary dribbling block" and loss of hearing are medical conditions; see a clinician.
- **Damaged text.** Sun's e-text breaks off in this paragraph; the end of the paragraph and the alias are taken from the plain e-text and from Yang.
