# Lion fat — "Fat-of-the-Lion" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus)
## *The fat of the lion, the first animal on Bryan's animal list; Bryan prints no Egyptian word, no species and no preparation of the fat, and it occurs once in the running text, as the first of six fats in a hair pomade* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter VII "Organic Remedies (animals)". "Lion" is the first animal on Bryan's list of those "whose identity is not in doubt, and who were called upon to sacrifice themselves in the cause of the Healing Art" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1390; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 92, class animal, "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). Bryan's list of animal parts (lines 1405-1413) includes "Fat" and "Tallow" but does not tie either to an animal. The spine row ("Lion fat, Panthera leo", with "Ebers (baldness pomade); history.com") was not used as authority: the binomial is the list-maker's, and the website was not opened.

**The one prescription for the fat.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "lion". The word occurs twice: in the animal list (line 1390) and in the hair remedy at line 4025. The remedy is in Chapter XXII "The Hair" (heading line 3994): "ANOTHER FOR THE GROWTH OF THE HAIR ON A HEAD WHICH IS BECOMING BALD. Fat-of-the-Lion, Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus, Fat-of-the-Crocodile, Fat-of-the-Cat, Fat-of-the-Serpent, Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat. Make into one and rub the head of the Bald One therewith." (lines 4023-4029). The lion fat is the first of six fats in one compound, which the entries for the hippopotamus fat and the crocodile fat share. The word "ANOTHER" shows that the remedy follows an earlier hair remedy.

**The chapter around it, as Bryan reports it.** Bryan opens the hair chapter (lines 3995-3997) with the remark that the first remedy for baldness recorded was prepared "for a woman", "Ses, Mother of His Majesty the King of Upper and Lower Egypt, Teta, deceased" (OCR capitals normalised), composed of "Toes-of-a-Dog Refuse-of-Dates Hoof-of-an-Ass". He says that in this section the scribe "was simply copying from other sources, for he has observed no order whatever in the entries but simply dashed down the remedies as they came to him", and that the simpler remedies, such as oil without admixture, were tried before "they resorted to desperate remedies" (the order is his rearrangement). He introduces the six-fat pomade, with others, as "mighty bewildering". These are Bryan's comments on the papyrus, not statements of the papyrus. The lion appears in no other prescription in his text.

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say how lion fat was obtained, whether the fat was of a captive or wild animal, how it was rendered, in what quantity it was used, or why the lion was chosen. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. No Ghalioungui glossary row for the lion is in `_sources/ebers.csv`. A Codex project file (`Sources/EP_Ebers/EP_Overview.md` line 190) prints a similar pomade under a translator's label, listing a fat of a hippopotamus, a crocodile, a serpent, an ibex and a lion; it disagrees with Bryan's six fats, gives no page, and was not used. ### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it. In general terms, the fat of a large carnivore is mainly triglycerides, and rendered fat is an ointment base. Nothing in the sources retrieved says that lion fat contains any component that acts on hair. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show lion fat once, as one of six fats rubbed on the head of a bald man. No quantity is legible. Lions are a protected species over their range and have no established medicinal use. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution, and a hair pomade is a remedy for an external complaint. The lion's association in other Egyptian contexts is not in Bryan's text and is not supplied here. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*). Bryan prints no recitation with the six-fat pomade, though he summarises, and that is no proof the papyrus gives none; the one spoken text he prints in the hair chapter, "TO CHARM AWAY ALOPECIA O Shining One, Thou who hoverest above! [...] To be spoken over Iron Red-lead Onions Alabaster Honey" (lines 4069-4072), is attached to a different mixture and is not transferred to the fat. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The record is one fat among six in a hair pomade, with no quantity and no cultivation role. A cultivator has nothing to do with it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** animal fat on the scalp or on broken skin carries infection risk.
- **Conservation and law:** the lion is protected and trade in its products is controlled in many countries; its present legal and conservation status was not retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** lion products come from killing a large wild predator and are not recommended.
