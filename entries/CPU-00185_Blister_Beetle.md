No preparation, dose, taste or potency is printed for the beetle.

### Active Components

Blister beetles of the genus *Mylabris* contain cantharidin, a terpenoid compound that is a strong vesicant (it raises blisters on skin and mucous membranes) and is poisonous when swallowed. The sources read give no composition.

### Scientific Correlation

Cantharidin, the active principle of blister beetles, is a known toxic vesicant. Swallowed, it irritates and damages the gut and the urinary tract and can be fatal; a small amount of its chemical derivative is used under medical supervision on the skin to remove warts (that is a controlled modern use of the pure compound, not of the dried insect). No clinical studies were retrieved for the Tibetan use. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Blister beetles are poisonous. Swallowing or handling them can cause blistering of skin and mouth, kidney and urinary-tract damage, and death. They should not be taken by mouth, put on the skin or handled bare-handed. Dried insect preparations are not standardised in strength. Pregnancy and breastfeeding add risk. Sale of such preparations is regulated in many places. The species identification (*Mylabris phalerata* or *M. cichorii*) is the translator's and the list's, and an uncertain one.
