# Chalcanthite — Shi Dan (石膽) "Cuprus Sulphate" (Yang 1998) / Tuttha, Tutthaka, "sulphate of copper" (Bhishagratna)
## *Blue copper sulphate, CuSO4 (hydrated, chalcanthite); Yang 1998 prints "Cuprus Sulphate" for Shi Dan, and Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 and his running text print "Sulphate of Copper" for Tuttha* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ, SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Shi Dan is printed in the readable part of Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), under the heading "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (PDF p.28, line 1097). Lines 1098-1105: "Shi Dan (Cuprus Sulphate) is sour and a little cold. It mainly brightens the eyes [and treats] eye pain, incised wounds, and all [kinds of] epilepsy and tetany. [It treats] genital erosion and pain in females, stone strangury, cold and heat, flooding and precipitation of blood, and various kinds of evil and toxic qi. It makes pregnancy possible. Taking it after it is sublimated may prevent senility, while protrated [sic] taking may increase longevity and make one an immortal. It may change iron into copper, gold, or silver. Its other name is Bi Shi (Green Stone). It is produced in mountains and valleys." Yang's footnote 52 (lines 1118-1119) adds: "This medicinal is toxic and erosive. It is only used to treat tooth decay, nasal polyps, sores, and flat abscesses. Occasionally, it is applied to eye diseases." That footnote is the translator's, and states the later use of the drug, not the classic's.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Sanskrit name is *Tutthaka* in the group list and *Tuttha* in the recipes. In *Sutrasthana* ch.38 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 274-278): "The Ushaka'di Group: The medicinal drugs and substances known as Ushaka (alkaline earth) Saindhava salt, Shilajatu, the two kinds of Kasisa, Hingu and Tutthaka enter into the composition of the group known as the Ushakadi Gana." The metrical text which follows says of the group: "It destroys kapham (mucous), absorbs the fat of the body and proves curative in cases of stone or gravel in the bladder (urinary calculi), dysuria and abdominal glands (Gulma)" (lines 280-283). This is a property of the whole group and not of *Tutthaka* alone. Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 identifies *Tuttha* as "Sulphate of Copper." (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` lines 33170 and 33196). In the recipes, which are all external, the translator renders the word as "sulphate of copper": for destroying soft marginal growths of an ulcer, "Proper drugs or articles (such as sulphate of copper, etc.) powdered and pasted with honey" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch01.txt` lines 705-708); for purifying an ulcer, in a list ending "Triphala and sulphate of copper (Tuttha)" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch08.txt` lines 252-253); in plasters for skin conditions, including one for *Svitra* beginning "sulphate of copper, Haritala (yellow oxide of arsenic)" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch09.txt` lines 201 and 360-362); in a plaster for a *Vrishana-Kachchhu* sore (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch20.txt` lines 360-364); and in eye preparations, as an *Anjana* with *Rasanjana* in equal parts and as a powder soaked in a decoction (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch17.txt` lines 333-336), with *Kajjala* rubbed in ghee on a copper vessel for a swollen lid (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch12.txt` lines 353-355), and as an ingredient of an *Anjana* applied with an iron pipe (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch11.txt` lines 96-102). In *Uttara* ch.14 it is applied with honey to an incised *Lagana* (lines 20-25).

### Active Components

Chalcanthite is hydrated copper(II) sulphate, the blue crystalline salt. It dissolves in water to give copper(II) ions, which are astringent, corrosive to tissue in strong solution, and toxic when swallowed. Natural material may carry other metal salts. Nothing in the sources read gives a composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Both texts use the mineral: the Chinese text lists it as a middle-class drug with a printed longevity line, and the Sanskrit text uses it in external ulcer, skin and eye applications. Copper sulphate is a toxic salt, and the translator's own footnote says it is "toxic and erosive" (Yang line 1118). The longevity line in the classic is the one cultivation claim, and no safe use of the mineral by mouth can be inferred from it. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation.

### Preparation & Dosing

SBJ: "after it is sublimated" (line 1102-1103); no method of sublimation and no quantity is printed in the lines read. SS: ground and pasted with honey, with *Kanjika* (Chikitsa ch.20), with the urine of a goat in a hollowed *Kapittha* rind buried a month under an *Arjuna* tree (Chikitsa ch.1 lines 798-806, there called *Tuttham* "(Sulphate of copper)"), or rubbed in ghee on a copper vessel (Uttara ch.12). No quantity of *Tuttha* is given in the lines read except "equal parts" with *Rasanjana* in Uttara ch.17. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Copper sulphate is a known emetic in large amounts, an astringent and caustic on tissue, a fungicide and an algicide, and a poison; ingestion can cause gastrointestinal injury, haemolysis and kidney and liver damage. These are general facts and no study was retrieved for the traditional uses. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- SBJ: "sour and a little cold" (Yang line 1098). Class: Middle (heading line 1097).
- SS: none printed for *Tuttha* by itself. The *Ushakadi* group, which includes *Tutthaka*, "destroys kapham" and "absorbs the fat of the body" (Sutra ch.38 lines 280-283); the text gives no rasa, virya or vipaka for the single drug in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: a middle-class (中品) drug, with a printed longevity line.* Shi Dan stands under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (Yang line 1097). The line quoted above says "Taking it after it is sublimated may prevent senility, while protrated taking may increase longevity and make one an immortal" (lines 1102-1104); the classic does not state this of the class as a whole, and it is the drug's own line. Yang's preface describes the middle class as "mainly nurture personality" and "may or may not be toxic" and says that "one should weigh and ponder before putting them to their appropriate use" (lines 385-387); it ties the superior class, not the middle, to "nourish life" (line 353). Yang's footnote to the preface says that the early materia medica were largely made by Daoists concerned with "the achievement of 'immortality' ... through the ingestion of various 'elixirs'" (lines 375-378). The same classic prints "It may change iron into copper, gold, or silver" (lines 1104-1105), the transmutation line also found for Kong Qing and Ceng Qing. The sublimation was required, and the translator's own footnote warns that the drug is toxic and erosive. This is not a *jing*, *qi* or *shen* claim; none is printed.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* *Tutthaka* is claimed by the *Ushakadi* group in the materia-medica chapter (*Sutrasthana* ch.38) and by the surgical, skin and eye chapters as an external application. It does not occur in the lines of *Chikitsasthana* ch.27-30 (the rasayana and vajikarana chapters) which were searched; no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status and no *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is printed for it. The *shodhana* (purification) of the mineral is not described in the lines read.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator to swallow. The one cultivation claim is a Chinese line, "may increase longevity and make one an immortal" after sublimation, and the translator warns in the same place that the drug is "toxic and erosive". The Sanskrit text uses it as an ulcer, skin and eye ointment. The take-away is historical: a middle-class stone with a longevity line, and a method nobody in the sources lets us follow. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Copper sulphate is poisonous by mouth, corrosive to the gut and skin in strong solution, and harmful to the eye; it can cause vomiting, haemolysis, kidney and liver damage. "Sublimated" material and other mineral preparations are not standardised, and the classic's method is not given. Pregnancy: Yang prints "It makes pregnancy possible" (line 1102); this must not be read as a recommendation, since the same drug is toxic. Mineral and metal preparations are regulated in many places. The 'transmutation' line is a line about alchemy, not therapy.
