# Tuber album — Hudnon (Dioscorides 2-175, translator's suggestion)

## *A round, pale, leafless underground body eaten as a vegetable, which the translator's heading to Dioscorides 2-175 suggests is Tuber album, Rhizopogon album or Choiromyces meandriformis, a truffle or false truffle; the chapter text itself gives no binomial, so the identification is the translator's, of low confidence at species level and medium at the level of a hypogeous (underground) fungus. The Codex's main truffle entry is CPU-01280 (I_871_Truffle.md); this row was kept apart because the Latin name "Tuber album" is one specific suggestion and the audit judged the target to be the generic truffle, not a species* | *(Volume IV — Fungal/Algal, shelved in Volume I)*

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter is 2-175, HUDNON, in the Book Two section on vegetables (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 6048). The translator's line (line 6050) reads "SUGGESTED: Tuber album, Rhizopogon album, Choiromyces meandriformis — White Truffle, False Truffle". The whole text of the chapter (line 6051) is: "Tuber is a round, pale, yellow root without leaves or stalk. It is dug up in the spring and is edible eaten either raw or boiled [vegetable]." The bracketed word is the translator's. The chapter gives no medicinal use, no property, no preparation as a drug and no dose. It is the shortest kind of entry in the book: a description, a season and a note that it is food.

The names in the heading are three different organisms. *Tuber* is a genus of true truffles (Ascomycota); *Choiromyces* is a separate genus of truffle-like fungi, and *Rhizopogon* a genus of false truffles. The text does not choose among them, and neither does the Codex. Dioscorides' word "root" is his word for a leafless thing that comes out of the ground; the text does not say more. [USER: verify] Fungi are treated by Dioscorides at 4-83 (mushrooms), which does not name the truffle.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, truffles are the fruiting bodies of fungi that grow underground, usually in association with tree roots; their tissue is mostly water, with chitin, glucans, a little protein and volatile compounds responsible for the smell. Nothing more can be stated without fixing the species. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The text gives a food, not a remedy. The Codex states no use, and it does not encourage foraging for underground fungi.

### Preparation & Dosing

Dug in the spring and eaten "either raw or boiled" (2-175). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Truffles are edible fungi prized in cooking; several truffle-like fungi that are not true truffles are not eaten. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** Several underground fungi are sold or collected under truffle-like names, and the species here is not fixed. Never eat a wild fungus on the strength of this entry.
- **Raw consumption.** The text says raw or boiled; the Codex advises nothing. Fungi should be properly identified and cooked. [USER: verify]
