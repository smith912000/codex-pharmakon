### Botanical Identity
Deciduous shrub/small tree 1–5m; palmate aromatic leaves with 5–7 lanceolate leaflets; long spikes of blue-violet flowers; small dark peppercorn-like berries; native to Mediterranean and Central Asia; widely cultivated ornamentally. The berries (dried) are the drug material. Long associated with monasteries — planted to reduce libido in male monastics (the "chaste" application) — while simultaneously used medicinally in women for premenstrual and menstrual conditions. The apparent contradiction (anti-aphrodisiac for men, menstrual regulator for women) reflects the dopaminergic mechanism operating differently across sex hormone contexts.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 De Materia Medica (DMM)

Dioscorides (*Book I, Chapter 134*):
> *"Lygos / Agnos: cooling and drying. The leaves and seeds and ripe fruit reduce carnal desires when drank in water. Athenian women put the leaves in their beds during the festival of Thesmophoria (when celibacy was required). The seed taken in wine promotes menstruation and the birth, and helps those with hardness of the spleen."*

Two apparently contradictory uses appear simultaneously in Dioscorides: anti-aphrodisiac + menstrual promoter. Both reflect prolactin normalization via dopaminergic agonism — different presentations of the same mechanism.

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā on *kaff maryam*:
> *"Cooling and drying. Reduces libido and sexual thoughts. Useful for women with menstrual irregularity and uterine conditions. Relieves inflammations of the uterus. The aroma applied to the genitals reduces desire."*

### 2.3 Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ — minor, *V. trifolia*)

The Chinese *màn jīng zǐ* (*Vitex trifolia*) is used primarily for Wind-Heat headache and eye conditions — pharmacologically distinct from *V. agnus-castus* applications, though sharing the genus.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Dopaminergic / Prolactin Suppression (Primary)

**Rotundifuran (diterpene)**: D2 receptor (dopamine receptor subtype 2) agonism → suppresses pituitary prolactin release → normalises prolactin levels. This is the central mechanism linking all traditional applications:

- **Women with elevated prolactin**: Normalised prolactin → restored LH pulsatility → normalised progesterone levels → resolves PMS/PMDD, normalised cycle length, reduced premenstrual mastalgia
- **Men**: Prolactin suppression → reduced libido (prolactin is permissive for sexual arousal; suppressing it reduces desire) — the monastic anti-aphrodisiac application
- **Galactagogue/anti-lactation paradox**: Low dose (≤20mg/day extract) — mild D2 agonism within normal range → maintains lactation; high dose → D2 overstimulation → prolactin suppression → reduces milk

This dose-dependent reversal of effect (low dose pro-lactation; high dose anti-lactation) is one of the most important hormetic relationships in plant medicine.

### 5.2 Progesterone Pathway

**Casticin (flavonoid)**: Weak progestogenic activity at the uterine receptor level. Contributes to luteal phase support independent of prolactin pathway. Combined with prolactin normalization → improved progesterone:oestrogen ratio → reduced PMS.

### 5.3 Opioidergic / Pain

**Beta-endorphin stimulation**: Agnuside and related iridoids stimulate mu-opioid receptor pathway → reduce pain sensitivity. Explains mastalgia (breast tenderness) relief in addition to hormonal mechanism.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**PMS/PMDD**: Schellenberg (2001, *British Medical Journal*): Standardised Vitex extract (Ze 440, 20mg/day × 3 cycles) vs placebo — significant reduction in all PMS symptoms (irritability, mood changes, anger, headache, breast fullness). Effect size comparable to fluoxetine for PMDD.

**Mastalgia (cyclic breast pain)**: Wuttke et al. (2003): Vitex extract vs pyridoxine vs placebo — Vitex significantly reduced breast pain, superior to pyridoxine. Prolactin normalization confirmed as mechanism.

**Menstrual normalisation**: Multiple cohort studies: Vitex normalises cycle length in women with polymenorrhoea (short cycles) or oligomenorrhoea (long cycles) — bidirectional normalisation consistent with prolactin-normalising mechanism.

**Infertility**: Small RCTs: Vitex improved progesterone levels in luteal phase and increased pregnancy rates in women with luteal phase deficiency over 3-month treatment.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The monastery and the midwife**: Vitex sits at the intersection of two opposed social uses — the monastery (where monks planted it to suppress desire, calling it *agnus castus*, "the chaste lamb") and the midwife's practice (where women were given it for menstrual and fertility conditions). The plant was simultaneously valued for anti-aphrodisiac effects in celibate men and fertility-promoting effects in women seeking conception. The dopaminergic mechanism produces both outcomes from the same compound acting in different hormonal environments. The Athenian women of Dioscorides' Thesmophoria account who lay on beds of *agnos* leaves were using the same plant for ritual celibacy enforcement that midwives prescribed for menstrual restoration.

**The prolactin hypothesis**: The identification of Vitex's mechanism as D2 agonism → prolactin suppression in the 1990s resolved 2,000 years of apparent contradiction between classical sources. Anti-aphrodisiac + menstrual promoter + galactagogue + anti-galactagogue — all follow from the same molecular target. The traditional pharmacopoeia preserved the right treatment even when the mechanism was unknown.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Standardised extract (Ze 440, BNO 1095) | 20–40mg/day | PMS/PMDD; mastalgia; menstrual regulation |
| Dried berry tincture (1:5) | 3–5ml/day | Same as above (titrate to 20mg equivalent) |
| Dried berry (morning dose) | 150–300mg crude herb | Luteal phase support; cycle normalisation |
| Note: Take in morning (morning dose synchronises with pituitary LH release pattern) | — | — |

**Dosing principle**: Always take as single morning dose — aligns with circadian prolactin pattern and LH pulsatility (strongest from 6–8am). Split doses are less effective.

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM (V. trifolia — headache application)**: Acrid, bitter; slightly cold. Channels: Liver (肝), Stomach (胃), Urinary Bladder (膀胱). Disperses Wind-Heat, clears head, relieves pain.

**Ayurvedic**: Bitter, astringent; cooling. Reduces Pitta ↓↓. *Artavajanana* (menstrual promoting), *Stanyajanana* (galactagogue, low dose).

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Pregnancy**: Dopaminergic agonism + menstrual stimulating — avoid; first trimester especially
- **Hormonal contraceptives / HRT**: May interfere with dopaminergic modulation of pituitary feedback — caution
- **Dopaminergic medications (antipsychotics, levodopa)**: D2 agonism → potential additive or antagonistic effects
- **Prolactinoma**: Theoretical benefit (prolactin suppression) but clinical management should not replace medical supervision
- Onset of action: 3 menstrual cycles minimum — counsel patient on realistic timeline
- Generally very safe for non-pregnant adults
