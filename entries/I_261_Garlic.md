## Section 1: Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Primary Binomial:** *Allium sativum* L.
**Family:** Amaryllidaceae (previously classified in Alliaceae; now included in Amaryllidaceae)

**Common Names:**
- English: Garlic, Common Garlic
- Sanskrit: *Laśuna* (लशुन) / *Rasona* — foundational Ayurvedic name; *Laśuna* in Charaka Samhita
- Hindi: *Lahsun*
- Arabic: *Thūm* (ثوم) — extensively documented in Hadith ("a cure for every disease except death" — though this is attributed to black seed/Nigella in most authoritative sources; garlic association in Tibb Nabawi is also strong)
- Persian: *Sīr* (سیر)
- Mandarin: *Dà suàn* (大蒜) — "great bulb"
- Ancient Greek: *Skórodon* (σκόρoδον) — Homer's *Odyssey* documents garlic consumption
- Hebrew: *Shum* (שום) — Numbers 11:5: Israelites in the wilderness cry for the "shum" (garlic) they had in Egypt — one of the earliest biblical food mentions
- Egyptian: *stpty* / Ebers name: *iw* (abundant documentation)
- Latin: *Allium sativum* from Celtic *all* (burning, pungent) + *sativum* (cultivated)
- Russian: *Чеснок* (chesnok)

**Taxonomy and Varieties:**
Two main subspecies:
- *A. sativum* var. *ophioscorodon* (Hardneck garlic): Stronger flavor; 5–10 cloves per bulb; bolt-producing; richer in allicin
- *A. sativum* var. *sativum* (Softneck garlic): Milder; 10–30 cloves; longer storage; supermarket standard

Wild ancestor: *Allium longicuspis* (Central Asia — Tian Shan mountains).

**Morphology:** Bulbous perennial; 30–90 cm tall; flat solid leaves (vs. hollow leaves of onion); compound bulb comprising 5–20 cloves enclosed in papery white/purple tunics; white-pink umbellate flowers; fibrous roots.

**Part Used:** Bulb (fresh cloves; dried powder; aged garlic extract; essential oil; oil macerate).

**Growth Region:** Native to Central Asia (Kyrgyzstan/Tajikistan/northwestern China). Among the earliest cultivated plants in human history; now cultivated globally. Major producers: China (>75% of world production), India, South Korea, Russia, Egypt.

## Section 4: Phytochemical Composition

### Organosulfur Compounds (primary bioactives — unique to Allium genus)

**Alliin → Allicin System (CRITICAL UNDERSTANDING):**
Fresh garlic does not contain allicin. When garlic is **crushed, chopped, or chewed**, the enzyme **alliinase** (compartmentalized in vacuoles) contacts its substrate **alliin** (S-allyl cysteine sulfoxide, compartmentalized in cytoplasm) → rapid synthesis of **allicin** (diallyl thiosulfinate). This enzymatic reaction is the pharmacological activation step:

*Alliin + alliinase → Allicin + pyruvic acid + ammonia*

**1. Alliin (S-allyl cysteine sulfoxide) — 1.0–1.5% fresh weight**
- Precursor; inactive itself; stable in intact garlic
- After alliinase contact: converted to allicin within 10–60 seconds

**2. Allicin (Diallyl Thiosulfinate) — primary active; formed only upon crushing**
- Formed at 0.1–0.5% equivalent in freshly crushed garlic
- **Primary antimicrobial mechanism:** Reacts with thiol groups (—SH) of bacterial cysteine residues → inhibits critical enzymes containing cysteine at active sites → bacteriostatic/bactericidal
- **Active against:** S. aureus (MIC 0.24 mg/mL), MRSA (MIC 0.5 mg/mL), E. coli, Helicobacter pylori (MIC 0.06 mg/mL), Candida (MIC 0.03 mg/mL), many intestinal parasites
- **Highly reactive:** Allicin decomposes rapidly (half-life: minutes to hours in solution; days in oil)
- Converts to: Diallyl disulfide (DADS), diallyl trisulfide (DATS), and ajoene (secondary actives)

**3. Diallyl Disulfide (DADS) — allicin breakdown product**
- Stable allicin metabolite; antimicrobial; lipid-lowering; anti-tumor; anti-thrombotic

**4. Diallyl Trisulfide (DATS) — allicin breakdown product**
- Stronger anti-tumor than DADS; H2S donor (mechanism — see below); anti-atherosclerotic

**5. Ajoene ((E,Z)-4,5,9-trithiadodeca-1,6,11-triene 9-oxide)**
- Formed from allicin in oil/heat; potent anti-platelet (IC₅₀ < aspirin); anti-tumor; anti-fungal
- Most stable allicin transformation product; retained in garlic oil preparations

**6. S-Allyl Cysteine (SAC) — primary Aged Garlic Extract active**
- Water-soluble; stable; formed during long-term aging of garlic (Aged Garlic Extract — AGE — developed by Wakunaga/Kyolic)
- Neuroprotective; antioxidant; cholesterol-lowering; well-absorbed; no odor
- SAC has >98% oral bioavailability; extensively studied in Kyolic AGE preparation

**7. S-Allyl Mercaptocysteine (SAMC) — in AGE**
- Anti-tumor (prostate, colon); cell cycle arrest; anti-metastatic

### Flavonoids and Other Constituents

**8. Quercetin, Kaempferol, Myricetin — flavonols**
- Anti-inflammatory; antioxidant; anti-tumor

**9. Allixin — non-sulfur compound; anti-tumor; neuroprotective**

**10. Selenium compounds — organic selenium**
- Anti-cancer (selenocysteine, selenium-allyl compounds); antioxidant

## Section 5: Mechanisms of Action

### 1. H2S Donation — Cardiovascular Primary Mechanism
Recent landmark discovery: Garlic-derived polysulfides (DATS, ajoene, allicin) are metabolized in the cardiovascular system to **hydrogen sulfide (H2S)**, a gasotransmitter (alongside NO and CO) that:
- Relaxes vascular smooth muscle (vasodilation → reduced blood pressure)
- Protects cardiac myocytes from ischemia-reperfusion injury
- Reduces vascular inflammation
- Inhibits platelet aggregation

This H2S-donation mechanism explains garlic's cardiovascular benefits at the molecular level — garlic is one of the primary dietary sources of H2S bioavailability. This mechanism was only discovered/characterized in 2007 (Benavides et al., PNAS) — 3,500 years after Ebers documented garlic's cardiovascular benefit.

### 2. Allicin-Mediated Antimicrobial (Broad-Spectrum)
Allicin covalently modifies thiol groups of bacterial enzymes, particularly:
- Cysteine protease active sites
- Acetyl-CoA synthetase (energy metabolism)
- Alcohol dehydrogenase (in parasites)
- NADH dehydrogenase (electron transport chain)

This non-specific thiol-reactive mechanism explains the extraordinary breadth of antimicrobial activity — nearly every bacterial, fungal, and parasitic organism with cysteine-dependent enzymes is susceptible.

### 3. Cholesterol / Lipid Lowering
- DADS + DATS: Inhibit HMG-CoA reductase (same enzyme targeted by statins) → reduced hepatic cholesterol synthesis
- Allicin: Inhibits cholesterol biosynthesis at multiple steps
- AGE (SAC): Reduces LDL oxidation (prevents oxidized-LDL atherosclerotic plaque formation)
- Combined: Modest but consistent LDL reduction (−10 to −15% from baseline)

### 4. Anti-Platelet / Anti-Thrombotic
- Ajoene: Inhibits fibrinogen binding to glycoprotein IIb/IIIa on platelets (same target as clopidogrel / abciximab)
- Allicin: Reduces thromboxane A2 synthesis (anti-platelet; parallel to aspirin mechanism)
- DATS: Cyclooxygenase inhibition
- Combined: Significant anti-platelet effect; additive with anticoagulants — **CRITICAL DRUG INTERACTION**

### 5. NF-κB Anti-inflammatory Pathway
- Allicin, DADS, SAC: NF-κB inhibition → reduced TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6; reduced CRP (C-reactive protein)
- Consistent anti-inflammatory across multiple studies
- Reduces endothelial inflammation central to cardiovascular disease

### 6. Anti-tumor (Multiple Mechanisms)
- DATS: H2S donation → mitochondrial apoptosis in cancer cells
- SAMC (AGE): Cell cycle arrest at G2/M
- Allicin: DNA strand break induction in tumor cells
- Selenium compounds: Anti-cancer (prostate cancer specific epidemiological evidence)

### 7. Anti-Hypertensive (Blood Pressure)
- H2S vasodilation (primary mechanism, above)
- NO synthesis enhancement (allicin → eNOS activation)
- ACE inhibition: Allicin-derived peptides inhibit angiotensin-converting enzyme
- Net: Consistent blood pressure reduction (~10 mmHg systolic in clinical trials)

## Section 6: Clinical Evidence

### Hypertension — Level A

**Ried et al. 2010 (Maturitas meta-analysis):**
- 10 RCTs, 440 hypertensive patients; garlic preparations vs. placebo
- Significant reduction: Systolic BP −8.4 mmHg; Diastolic BP −7.3 mmHg vs. placebo (p<0.001)
- Effect most pronounced in hypertensive patients (vs. normotensive)
- Level A

**Ried et al. 2016 (Journal of Nutrition):**
- N=88 hypertensive patients; Aged Garlic Extract (AGE/Kyolic) vs. placebo; 12 weeks
- Systolic −10 mmHg vs. placebo (p=0.006); enhanced effect in poorly controlled hypertension
- Level A

**Cochrane Review on garlic for hypertension (Stabler 2012):** Consistent positive finding; Level A designation for hypertension management as adjunct.

### Cholesterol / Cardiovascular

**Warshafsky et al. 1993 meta-analysis (Annals of Internal Medicine):**
- 5 RCTs; garlic reduces total cholesterol 9% (vs. placebo; p<0.001); Level A

**Ried et al. 2013 meta-analysis (Nutrition Reviews):**
- 39 trials; garlic reduces total cholesterol −17 mg/dL, LDL −9 mg/dL; Level A for lipid effect

**Atherosclerosis regression (Koscielny 1999):** Aged Garlic Extract 900 mg/day vs. placebo × 4 years; significantly slower carotid intima-media thickness progression; Level B.

### Anti-Infection / Common Cold

**Josling 2001 (Advances in Therapy):** N=146; allicin-yielding garlic supplement × 12 weeks; significantly fewer colds vs. placebo (24 vs. 65 episodes; p<0.001); faster recovery; Level A.

**Lissiman et al. 2014 Cochrane Review:** Only 1 high-quality RCT identified (above); evidence suggestive but insufficient to draw firm conclusions; Level B.

### H. pylori Eradication

**Multiple in vitro studies:** Allicin MIC against H. pylori 0.06 mg/mL; among most active natural agents.

**Clinical (Dehghani et al. 2018):** Garlic supplementation + triple therapy vs. triple therapy alone; improved eradication rate (from 73% to 86%); Level B (adjunct; not monotherapy).

### Anti-Cancer (Epidemiological)

**Iowa Women's Health Study (Steinmetz 1994):** Higher garlic consumption significantly inversely associated with colon cancer risk (OR 0.68); Level C (epidemiological).

**Meta-analysis (Zhou 2011):** Garlic consumption inversely associated with gastric cancer risk (OR 0.54); Level B.

**Overall Evidence: Level A (hypertension, cholesterol); Level B (cold prevention adjunct, H. pylori adjunct, cancer epidemiology); Level C–D (most other indications).**

## Section 7: Phenomenological Record

### Qualitative Experience

Garlic's phenomenological signature is among the most powerful and immediately recognizable in the Codex — the sharp, pungent, sulfurous aroma of allicin that has defined human culinary and medicinal practice for 5,000+ years. No other botanical medicine is simultaneously: the most universally consumed herb, the most cross-traditionally documented, and among the most pharmacologically validated for major disease endpoints.

**Fresh garlic (raw):**
- Crushing/chewing: Immediate sharp, burning warmth from allicin; pungent sulfurous aroma (allicin volatilizing)
- Oral mucosa: Mild burning; slight anesthesia from thiosulfinates on oral epithelium
- GI: Warmth spreading downward; distinctive sensation of "heat moving through the body"
- 15–30 minutes: Whole-body warmth; slight diaphoresis (perspiration) — garlic's warming quality has been used for chills, cold exposure, and respiratory illness onset across all traditions
- 1–4 hours: Allicin/DADS excreted through lungs (breath odor) and skin (body odor) — the garlic "smell" that follows consumption is pharmacologically meaningful (indicates systemic absorption and excretion of active compounds)

**Chronic daily consumption (Mediterranean/Asian dietary pattern):**
- Week 1–4: Gradual blood pressure reduction (not felt directly); progressive platelet aggregation reduction
- Month 2–3: Measurable cholesterol reduction
- Year 1+: Atherosclerotic plaque progression slowing (measurable by ultrasound IMT)
- Subjective: "circulation improvement" reported by regular consumers; improved extremity warmth in cold patients; general sense of systemic robustness

**Cultural phenomenology:**
Garlic's universal consumption creates an unusual phenomenological context — it is simultaneously the most ordinary food and the most venerated medicine. Ancient Egyptians wore garlic amulets for protection; Odysseus used garlic (*moly*) against Circe's transformation spell; medieval plague doctors carried garlic in their beaks; modern Italian nonnas insist it "cures everything."

The common thread across 5,000 years and all human cultures: the subjective sense that garlic makes the body more robust, more resistant, and more vital. This multi-millennium phenomenological consensus is now pharmacologically validated — H2S donation, allicin antimicrobial, NF-κB anti-inflammatory, HMG-CoA reductase inhibition, anti-platelet aggregation, and anti-hypertensive mechanisms together explain why humans across all cultures have independently converged on garlic as the quintessential "protection and vitality" herb.

## Section 8: Practical Application

### Formulation and Dosing

**CRITICAL: Allicin Yield — Preparation Method Determines Activity**
- Raw crushed/minced garlic: Maximum allicin yield; 1 clove = ~5 mg allicin yield if properly crushed and left 10 minutes before consumption
- **"Crush and Wait" protocol:** Crush garlic; wait 10 minutes before cooking — allows alliinase reaction to complete; allicin is then more heat-stable as it has already formed
- Cooked whole garlic: Low allicin yield (alliinase denatured by heat before alliin contact)
- Garlic oil: Contains DADS, ajoene but minimal allicin (heat-processed)
- Aged Garlic Extract (Kyolic®): No allicin; high SAC/SAMC; well-absorbed; odorless; best long-term cardiovascular supplement

**By indication:**

**Hypertension (clinical standard):**
- Aged Garlic Extract (Kyolic): 240–960 mg/day (Ried 2016 protocol: 480 mg 2× daily)
- OR standardized allicin powder (minimum allicin yield 3,600–5,400 mcg): 600–900 mg/day
- 8–12 weeks minimum for measurable BP change

**Cholesterol:**
- Standardized powder: 900 mg/day standardized to allicin yield; 3+ months
- AGE: 2.4–4.8 g/day (Koscielny atherosclerosis study: 900 mg/day for 4 years)

**Infection (cold, URI prevention):**
- Josling protocol: Allicin-yielding supplement (allicin 180 mg equivalent) 1 capsule/day for 12 weeks through cold season
- Acute illness: 2–4 crushed raw garlic cloves/day (maximum allicin exposure)

**Culinary (preventive):**
- Mediterranean dietary pattern: 2–4 cloves daily in food; crush and wait before cooking
- This dose level provides consistent low-dose allicin/H2S/DADS exposure

### Safety Profile

**Excellent safety record** — among the most extensively consumed plants in human history.

**Adverse effects:**
- **Breath/body odor (primary complaint):** Allicin/DADS excreted via lungs and skin; cosmetically significant; AGE (Kyolic) is odorless
- **GI irritation:** Raw garlic in quantity (>4 cloves/day): heartburn, nausea, diarrhea, flatulence; cooked garlic much better tolerated; take with food
- **Contact dermatitis:** Topical raw garlic → chemical burns (allicin direct tissue damage); never apply undiluted to skin long-term
- **Occupational asthma:** Garlic processing workers; aerosolized allicin; prevent with ventilation

**Drug Interactions (CRITICAL):**
- **Anticoagulants (warfarin, heparin, aspirin, clopidogrel):** ADDITIVE ANTI-PLATELET + ANTICOAGULANT — **most important garlic drug interaction**; INR monitoring essential; **pre-operative discontinuation 7–14 days minimum** (multiple case reports of excessive bleeding from garlic + anticoagulant combination); affects platelet function AND clotting factors
- **HIV antiretrovirals (especially saquinavir, ritonavir):** Case reports of significant saquinavir plasma level reduction with high-dose garlic supplementation; CYP3A4 induction mechanism; AVOID high-dose garlic supplements in HIV patients on PI-based regimens
- **Antidiabetics:** Mild glucose-lowering effect; additive; monitor
- **Antihypertensives:** Additive BP lowering; monitor BP
- **Cyclosporine:** Possible reduction via CYP3A4 induction (high-dose supplements); transplant patients: discuss with physician

**Contraindications:**
- Pre-operative: Discontinue supplements 7–14 days before surgery (antiplatelet effect)
- Bleeding disorders: Significant antiplatelet activity; caution
- **Pemphigus patients:** Case reports of garlic triggering pemphigus vulgaris flares
- Allium family allergy (relatively rare; cross-react with onion, leek, chives)
- Gastroesophageal reflux: Raw garlic aggravates (LES relaxation); cooked garlic generally tolerated

**Pregnancy:** Culinary amounts safe; medicinal supplements (high-dose allicin-yielding) avoided in first trimester (mild uterotonic activity historically documented); anecdotal but recommended caution.
