### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** In the Sutra chapter on purgatives the Samhita uses this drug as a purgative. This entry is a historical record, not a recommendation, and nobody should take it.

**Named by the Sushruta translation only.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder.

**Sushruta (SS).** The name is printed at line 31433 of `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` in the Sanskrit-name block of Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs", heading at lines 31119-31121): "Briddha daraka", between "Brahmi" and the end of the "Bh-" and "Br-" run. The Latin column is a separate block in this e-text, and the Latin gloss for this name cannot be paired with it. The appendix therefore supplies the name and no identification. [USER: verify against the printed page.]

The spelling in the chapters is Vriddha-daraka ("Vriddhadaraka"). We read "Briddha daraka" as a spelling variant of it, which is our inference from the sound and the spelling and is not stated by the translator. Chapter text (chapter files in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`): Sutra ch.44, line 161, "A compound consisting of equal parts of powdered Trivrit, Shyama (Vriddha-Daraka), Yavakshara, Shunti and Pippali, and taken with honey, acts as one of the most effective purgatives in diseases due to the action of the deranged Kapham." Line 195: a compound of equal parts of "powdered Trivrit, Shyama (Vriddhadaraka), Pippali and Triphala", made into a confection (*Modaka*) with honey and sugar, "should be regarded as one of the most potent cures (purgatives)" for *Sannipata*. Line 220: a purgative of "Shyama (Vriddhadaraka), Trivrit, Nili, Katvi, Musta, Duralabha, Chavya, Indrayava and Triphala" in clarified butter, meat essence or water, "commended to persons of dry temperament". Uttara ch.43, line 112: a patient "should be purged with clarified butter mixed with powdered Shyama (Vriddha-daraka) and Trivrit". In Chikitsa ch.37, line 217, a translator's note reads "Dallana explains 'Shyama' as 'Vriddha-daraka'". No quantity is given in these passages.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the source says it is for.** A purgative ingredient: with Trivrit and other drugs, for diseases of deranged Kapha, for *Sannipata*, for dry-tempered persons, and in ghee for a purge (Sutra ch.44, lines 161, 195, 220; Uttara ch.43, line 112).
- **What the source does not say.** The plant's identity, a quantity, a course or a condition for stopping.

The Codex gives no protocol for this material and does not recommend it.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters give compounds of equal parts, powdered, with honey (line 161), or as a confection with honey and sugar (line 195), or in clarified butter, meat essence or water (line 220); and powdered in ghee for a purge (Uttara ch.43, line 112). "Equal parts" is a ratio, not a dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] **Do not use any dose from this entry.**

### Contraindications

- **Purgative.** Strong evacuation can harm the weak, the dehydrated, the pregnant and the very young or old. The text itself limits the compound at line 220 to persons of dry temperament in ghee.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** Not addressed in the passages read; avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** Unresolved. Do not guess a plant and do not gather one.
