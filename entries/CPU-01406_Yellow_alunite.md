# Yellow alunite (ser tshur) — ser tshur (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011); ser.mtsur (Clark 1995, "the yellow type"), rGyud-bzhi Explanatory Tantra ch.20
## *The yellow type of the mineral mtsur/tshur, which Clark glosses as "ser.mtsur (fibroferrite), the yellow type" beside "nag.mtsur: Black Alunite: Vitriol"; used for dyeing wool, and with black alunite said to halt necrosis and extract tumours; the "alunite" in this entry's name is the translator's gloss of mtsur, and for the yellow type Clark's own gloss is fibroferrite* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The Codex's list row is `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 165: "Ser tshur, , mineral, rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Efficacy of Medicinal Substances); Four Tantras of Tibetan Medicine (English trans.)", flagged as an uncertain identification. That row gives only a name and a chapter; the following text loci were read to support it.

*Clark 1995* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). Chapter 20, the powers of medicines, has a specific section on individual natural substances in eight parts: precious metals, stones, earths, trees, resins, plants, herbs and animal products (lines 801-802). Part (3) is "The Exposition of the Power of Earth Medicines" (line 857), and ser tshur is dealt with there, inside the entry for black alunite (line 867):

"nag.mtsur: Black Alunite: Vitriol: [has six names and is greenish-yellow. The bubbles of this mineral when boiled will turn copper red on contact, and, if sprinkled in a decoction of chebulic myrobalan, it will turn it black. [ser.mtsur (fibroferrite), the yellow type,] is used for dyeing wool and a solution of chebulic myrobalan will turn yellow when it is added. This and black alunite (nag.mtsur) halt necrosis, extract tumors [and are also beneficial for falling hair]."

Clark's introduction says he "interpolated liberally" from commentary (the Blue Aquamarine) in rendering the tantra (line 80), and in the earth-medicine entries the square brackets carry such commentary, while the unbracketed words are the tantra's own. On that reading the tantra's own words about this substance are only that "this and black alunite (nag.mtsur) halt necrosis, extract tumors". Everything about colour, the boiling test, the myrobalan test and the wool dye sits in brackets, and "[and are also beneficial for falling hair]" is also bracketed. The yellow type is named only inside that bracket: "ser.mtsur (fibroferrite), the yellow type". The brackets' attribution is a convention of the translation as inferred from line 80 and from the layout, and was not checked against the Tibetan. [USER: verify]

The same mineral is used as a comparison elsewhere in the section: lake salt (rgya.tsha) "has a hot taste similar to mtsbur (alunite)" (line 927), and white rock salt (mdze.tsha) "resembles clear, dried bubbles of mtshur (alunite) on rocks" (line 933). Chalcanthite (big.pan) "has similar qualities to mTsur and is blue", with a whitish type that "tastes, like mtsur" (line 868). These lines give a hot taste and a bubbled, crystalline look, but they speak of mtsur in general (the spellings differ, "mtsbur", "mtshur", "mTsur", "mtsur"), not of the yellow type. Borax (tsha.la) is said to have "five names, dar.tshur, dar.sman, etc." (line 934); that is a name of another mineral and is not this entry.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`). In the earth-medicine summary of the Explanatory Tantra (line 3373) the text reads "Nag tshur and Ser tshur heals gangrene and dissolves tumours." The two are named together, as black and yellow (*nag* and *ser*) tshur, with one action stated for both. The index lists "Nag tshur 200" (line 7449) and "Sertshur 200" (line 7486), so the passage is on page 200. This is the only passage found that names *ser tshur* in this spelling, and it is the Men-Tsee-Khang counterpart of Clark's "halt necrosis, extract tumors".

**What is not given.** In the passages read, neither text gives a taste, a potency or a post-digestive taste for ser tshur by itself, a dose, a preparation (beyond the wool dye, which is a dyeing use, and the myrobalan test) or a formula that contains it. The hot taste is stated for mtsur only by comparison with the salt (line 927). Whether other parts of the Explanatory Tantra (for example the compounding chapter) or the Oral Instruction Tantra name the yellow type was not searched beyond these hits. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The heading of this entry, "Yellow alunite", is the Codex's name; it combines the Tibetan *ser* (yellow) with Clark's "alunite" for *mtsur*. Clark's own glosses are different: the black type he calls "Black Alunite: Vitriol" and the yellow type, in a bracket, "fibroferrite". Alunite and fibroferrite are different minerals (see Active Components), so "yellow alunite" is not what Clark prints for *ser.mtsur*. The Tibetan spelling also varies: *ser tshur* (Men-Tsee-Khang and the list), *ser.mtsur* (Clark line 867), *mtshur/mtsbur/mTsur* (Clark lines 868, 927, 933). Which material the tradition means by the yellow type is not settled by the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated from the text. The tradition's names, as translated, point to sulphate minerals: Clark glosses the yellow type as fibroferrite, a hydrated iron sulphate mineral of yellow colour, and the black type as vitriol; and his "alunite" is a potassium aluminium sulphate mineral. These are statements about those minerals in general, not readings of the text, and which of them (if any) is the *ser tshur* of the tantra is not settled. The tests Clark records are in line with metal sulphates in general: a decoction of chebulic myrobalan, which contains tannins, is said to turn black with the black type and yellow with the yellow type. No composition is claimed for this entry.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts read record it as a mineral for gangrene or necrosis and for tumours (and, in a bracketed note, falling hair), and in a bracketed commentary as a wool dye. It is an unidentified or uncertainly identified mineral sulphate, the texts give no dose and no preparation, and mineral sulphates are irritants and can be toxic. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT).** For ser tshur by itself no taste, potency or post-digestive taste is printed in either text read. For mtsur in general, a hot taste is implied only by comparison (lake salt "has a hot taste similar to mtsbur (alunite)", Clark line 927) and the tantra's own words give its actions: it halts necrosis and extracts tumours (Clark line 867; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3373, "heals gangrene and dissolves tumours"). No wind, bile or phlegm (*rlung*, *mkhris pa*, *bad kan*) action is printed for it in the passages read. No classification is carried across from the Chinese or Indian systems.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The tantra places the mineral among the earth medicines of Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Clark line 857) and assigns it therapeutic actions (halting necrosis, extracting tumours). The same section names one substance, shilajit-type *brag.zhun*, as "unsurpassed as an essence-extraction substance" (Clark line 870); that sentence is not said of this mineral, and no *bcud len* use of ser tshur was found. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition and none is asserted. The text read presents it as a medicine for defined conditions and, in a bracketed note, a dye.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation. It is a mineral sulphate the texts use for necrosis and tumours; its identity is uncertain, no preparation is given, and the conditions it is named for need medical care, not self-treatment. It is not a recommended practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Mineral sulphate:** sulphates of iron and aluminium are irritants; do not swallow, inhale as dust, or apply to open wounds or the eyes. Large amounts of iron salts are toxic.
- **Gangrene and tumours:** these need prompt medical care; the tantra's statements are not a substitute for treatment.
- **Unsettled identity:** the Tibetan name, the translator's "alunite" and "fibroferrite", and the Codex name do not agree; nothing should be collected, bought or used on the strength of the name.
- **Preparation unknown:** no processing is given in the sources retrieved; Tibetan mineral medicines are traditionally prepared with specialist methods, and this entry does not state any. [USER: verify]
