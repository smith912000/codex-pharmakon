### Origin & Tradition

Ox bezoar is the rare **biliary concretion (gallstone)** that occasionally forms in the gallbladder or bile ducts of cattle — a golden-brown, layered stone of crystallised bile that the Chinese named *Niú Huáng* ("ox yellow") and the Indian tradition *Gorochana*. Its scarcity (found in perhaps one in a thousand slaughtered cattle) and its dramatic clinical reputation made it one of the most precious substances in the animal materia medica — literally worth its weight in gold — and the defining ingredient of the great "cool-opening" emergency formulas for high fever with clouded consciousness and convulsion.

**Chinese Tradition (SBJ — Shén Nóng Běncǎo Jīng):**
Niú Huáng is recorded in the *Shén Nóng Běncǎo Jīng* and is the paradigmatic **cool orifice-opener**. Where musk (I_337) opens the orifices by warm, penetrating aroma, Niú Huáng opens them by *clearing heat* — the distinction between the warm and cool resuscitative methods. Its actions: it **clears heat and resolves toxicity**, **opens the orifices and awakens the spirit** in heat-induced clouding, **extinguishes internal wind and stops convulsions** (childhood convulsive fever, epilepsy), and **clears heat-phlegm** obstructing the Heart. It is the monarch of **Ān Gōng Niú Huáng Wán** ("Peaceful Palace Bezoar Pill"), the pre-eminent emergency formula for high fever with delirium, convulsion, and coma (used in stroke, encephalitis, and febrile crises).

**Ayurvedic (SS — Gorochana) and Tibetan (FMT — gi-wang):**
*Gorochana* is a precious, rare, cooling, auspicious substance in Ayurveda, used for fevers, poisoning, mental disturbance, and as a sacred/ritual pigment (a tilaka mark of blessing). Tibetan medicine's *gi-wang* is a treasured cooling ingredient of precious pills for hot disorders, liver heat, poisoning, and disturbed consciousness.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the golden stone born of the body's fire:**
Niú Huáng is a profound symbolic object: a **precious golden stone generated within the living animal by disease** — like agarwood (I_307) and pearl, a treasure born of pathology. The tradition read its golden colour and biliary origin as signatures of its power over the "fire" and the bile/Heat of the body: a concretion of the very bile that governs heat, turned into the medicine that *clears* heat and quells the fire-driven madness of high fever. Its auspicious, almost talismanic status across China, India, and Tibet (a blessing-mark, a treasure-pill ingredient) reflects the awe attached to a rare golden stone that could pull a convulsing, delirious patient back from the brink of fever-death. The Codex reads it as the **cool counterpart to musk in the resuscitative pair** — the two supreme orifice-openers, one warm and aromatic, one cool and heat-clearing.

### Active Components

- **Bile pigments — bilirubin and biliverdin** — the principal and characteristic constituents (bilirubin gives the golden colour); associated with the sedative, anticonvulsant, anti-inflammatory and antioxidant actions, though high concentrations of bilirubin are themselves **neurotoxic** (the basis of both efficacy and a real toxicity ceiling).
- **Bile acids — cholic, deoxycholic, taurocholic acids** — chol  eretic, anti-inflammatory, and the source of much of the digestive and hepatobiliary action; the configuration and concentration of bile acids differ among the four commercial sources (natural, cultured, in-vitro, artificial) and drive their pharmacological differences.
- **Cholesterol, amino acids (incl. taurine), and trace minerals** — the concretion matrix; taurine and amino acids contribute to the neuro-sedative profile. Over 44 constituents have been characterised.

### Scientific Correlation

*Attribution: findings below are from a review retrieved from PubMed; DOI linked.*

A comprehensive review of *Calculus bovis* (Yu et al., 2020, *Journal of Ethnopharmacology*) documents its traditional use for high fever, convulsion and stroke; identifies its four commercial sources (natural, cultured-in-vivo, in-vitro-cultured, and artificial) with differing constituent profiles; catalogues **>44 constituents** (mainly **bile pigments, bile acids, cholesterols, and amino acids**); and summarises pharmacological effects across the **nervous, cardiovascular, respiratory, digestive and immune systems** — while noting that **high concentrations of bilirubin cause neurotoxicity and hearing impairment**, and that pharmacokinetic data are still lacking. Critically for sustainability, the review concludes that **cultured bezoar (CBS) contains analogous constituents and exerts similar effects to natural bezoar** and may serve as a sustainable substitute ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2020.112649)).

*Evidence-grade note: the pharmacology is real but largely preclinical/constituent-level; clinical use is as a minute component of compound emergency formulas, and PK data are lacking. Bilirubin's dose-dependent neurotoxicity is a genuine ceiling. No efficacy statistics are asserted beyond the review's scope.*

### Contraindications

- **Cold / deficiency patterns; no true heat:** contraindicated — Niú Huáng is cool and for excess heat/toxic-heat only.
- **Pregnancy:** contraindicated (strong cool-opening/wind-extinguishing agent).
- **Bilirubin toxicity ceiling:** high-concentration/prolonged exposure risks neurotoxicity and hearing impairment — a real dose limit; not for extended use.
- **Acute/specialist only:** a minute-dose emergency formula component, not a self-care supplement.
- **Sourcing/conservation:** use cultured or artificial substitute; natural bezoar is rare and its trade raises sourcing concerns.
- **Evidence honesty:** pharmacology is preclinical/constituent-level; PK data lacking; use is traditional-clinical within formula.
