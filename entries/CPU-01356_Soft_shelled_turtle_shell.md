# Soft-shelled turtle shell — 鱉甲 Biē Jiǎ (the project's SBJ reconstruction prints "Soft-shell Turtle Shell")
## *The shell of the soft-shelled turtle, as the on-disk reconstruction of the Shennong Bencao Jing names it; the texts read give no binomial and the source list's English cell is cut to "Soft"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The list row is `_sources/shennong.csv` line 234, "Soft (鱉甲 Biē Jiǎ)", class animal, source SBJ. The English cell of that row is truncated to the single word "Soft"; the Chinese name 鱉甲 is intact, and the name is the evidence for the row.

The one text on disk that prints the entry is the project's reconstruction `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/SBJ_Original_Chinese.md`, under "蟲獸部中品 (Chóng Shòu Bù — Creatures and Animals, Middle)" (heading at line 569), inside "卷二 中品 (Book Two: Middle Class)" (line 399). At lines 595-597 it reads:

"**鱉甲 Biē Jiǎ** (Soft-shell Turtle Shell)
味鹹，平。主心腹症瘕堅積，寒熱，去痞，息肉，陰蝕，痔，惡肉。
*Flavor: salty; nature: balanced. Governs: hard accumulations and abdominal masses in the heart and abdomen; cold-heat; removing glomus; polyps; genital erosion; hemorrhoids; malign flesh.*"

The English line is the reconstruction's own, not Yang's. This file is not a critical edition. Its closing notes (lines 746-748) say it follows Mori (1972) and Yang (1998), and that entries "reconstructed from later sources" carry the mark "[traditional attribution]"; this entry carries no such mark. Elsewhere in the same middle-class animal section the file prints items, such as royal jelly and bee pollen, that its own marks flag as later attributions, so the file is useful as a lead and not as an authority.

I could not check the entry against Yang's 1998 translation (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`). Its table of contents lists "Animals: Superior Class" (p.111), "Animals: Middle Class" (p.119) and "Animals: Inferior Class" (p.129) (lines 770-776), but the OCR pages 110-129 (from line 3882) hold only page markers, so none of the animal entries is readable. The reading of this entry therefore rests on the reconstruction file and on the list row. [USER: verify against Yang p.119ff or the Chinese edition]

The neighbouring entry in the reconstruction (lines 591-593) is 龜甲 Guī Jiǎ, tortoise plastron, which has its own row in the project's list (CPU-00998). It is a different material; it is the entry that carries a long-term clause ("久服輕身不饑"), and this one does not. The identity of 鱉 as the soft-shelled turtle is the reconstruction's gloss. The project's master table names a binomial for it, but with no cited source, and it is not relied on here.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, turtle shell is bone covered by a horny or leathery layer; bone is mainly calcium phosphate in a collagen matrix, and the covering is mostly protein. Nothing more specific can be said without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic lists the shell for a group of complaints, namely hard accumulations and masses in the abdomen, cold-heat, glomus, polyps, genital erosion, haemorrhoids and "malign flesh". The Codex does not prescribe it. Turtles are heavily traded and many species are threatened, the sources give no preparation, and the entry is a record of what the classic says.

### Preparation & Dosing

The reconstruction gives no preparation of the shell (no roasting, vinegar or powdering) and no quantity. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

### Cultivation Register

**Chinese / Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; the middle-class placement is unconfirmed.* The reconstruction prints this entry in the middle-class animal section (中品). If that placement is right, the frame is Yang's preface (lines 352-396), which reads the middle class as the drugs that "mainly nurture personality", used "to control disease, supplement vacuity, and replenish exhaustion", which "may or may not be toxic" and call for weighing, as against the superior class, which is non-toxic and may be taken long-term to nourish life. That is a grade for the class, not a statement about this shell. I have not graded it (a) because the placement comes from a project reconstruction I could not check against Yang's animal pages, which are unreadable. The entry's own sentence assigns it a list of disease actions (masses, cold-heat, glomus, polyps, erosion, haemorrhoids, malign flesh). It carries no long-term-use clause and no *qing shen* ("lighten the body") clause, unlike the tortoise plastron beside it. No *jing*, *qi* or *shen* action is asserted for it, and the neidan overlay (*dantian*, *yuan shen*) is not a bencao category and is not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. At most this is a middle-class drug taken for a defined complaint with care, if the placement is confirmed, and the entry's own sentence is a list of disease actions with nothing about long-term use. The Codex does not recommend it, and the conservation status of turtles is a reason for restraint. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Contraindications in the classic:** none are printed in the lines read. The preface says that middle-class drugs "may or may not be toxic" and should be weighed (Yang, lines 352-396); that applies to the class and is not a statement about this shell.
- **Conservation and law:** turtle species are widely threatened, and trade in many of them is restricted internationally; do not obtain a turtle product of unknown origin.
- **Identity and quality:** the species is not named in the texts read, so the material cannot be identified from them; animal-derived products of unknown origin can be adulterated or contaminated.
- **Ethics:** the Codex does not recommend harming an animal for these uses.
