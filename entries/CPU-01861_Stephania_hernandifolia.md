# Stephania hernandifolia — Ambashtha; Amvashtha (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Stephania hernandifolia, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Stephania  hernandifolia.", line 31229) against the Sanskrit name that the OCR gives as "AmbasbtbsC" (line 31177), read here as Ambashtha, and prints a second time (OCR "Stephanea  Hernandifolio.", line 32564), which the count below sets against an OCR-garbled name on the P page, read as Patha. Both pairings of a Sanskrit name with its Latin line are positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; neither is printed on one line. The chapters print Ambashtha (as Amvashtha) only as the first-named drug of a group, the Ambashthadi group, and give no Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed and the Sanskrit name is an OCR reading, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 370 gives the name as "AmbasbtbsC", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31177; id line 31229)" and the Latin as "Stephania  hernandifolia.", with the note that the pairing was positional in a block of 31. The OCR garbles this page's letters throughout (for example "Akbu-parni" for Akhu-parni, line 31161), and the chapters print the group name Ambashthadi (as "Amvashtha'di", Sutra ch.38 line 326), so "AmbasbtbsC" is read as Ambashtha. The second Latin line, "Stephanea  Hernandifolio." (line 32564), has no row in the CSV, neither paired nor unpaired.

**The second pairing, checked by counting.** The page of the P names holds thirty-six Latin entries (lines 32549 to 32592) and, once "Tsitali" (line 32492) and "P^tali" (line 32493) are taken as two entries, thirty-six Sanskrit names (lines 32479 to 32546). The OCR prints those two lines with stray bracket marks and no legible "Patha". "Stephanea Hernandifolio." is the tenth Latin entry, and the tenth Sanskrit line is "P^tali". The neighbours fit: the ninth Latin entry, "Stereopermiim  Suaveolens." (32562), stands against "Tsitali", which reads as Patali; the eleventh, "Tricosanthus  Diocia." (32565), against Patola (32495); and "See  Tejpatra." (32566) against Patra (32497). The alignment is firm at both ends of the column, where Pundarika stands against "Nelurabium  speciosum." (32592). Read by position and alphabetical order, the line is Patha (which follows Patali), but the OCR does not print that name, and the Codex does not state it as a reading. If it is right, the appendix pairs *Stephania hernandifolia* with two Sanskrit names, Ambashtha and Patha. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Ambashtha with its OCR and spelling variants (B and V for each other, "Amboshtha", "Ambashthadi", "Amvashtadi", the letters split across a line) and for the English words "Stephania" and "Cissampelos". The name occurs only as the first drug of the Ambashthadi group and as the group's name, in five chapters, and the translator never glosses it or names a plant for it.

*The group list (Sutra ch.38).* `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 326 to 335: "The Amvashtha'di Group:— Drugs known as Amvashtha, Dhataki flowers, Samanga, Katvanga, Madhuka, Vilva-peshika, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Palasha, Nandi-Vriksha and Padma keshara, enter into the composition of the group known as the Amvashthadi Gana." (The OCR prints "Amvashth^" and "Xandi-Vriksha".) The metrical text that follows, lines 337 to 342, covers this group and the Priyangvadi group before it: "The two medicinal recipes or groups prove beneficial in a case of deranged Pitta, favour the healing of ulcers, bring about the adhesion of fractured bones and prove curative in cases of dysentery where the stools are found to consist of lumps of thick and matured mucous (Pakvatisara.)" The text gives no use for Ambashtha alone. This is a list mention.

*The groups used (Sutra 40 and 42, Chikitsa 38, Uttara 40).* `SS_Sutra_ch40.txt` lines 346 to 348, in the argument that a physician should keep to tested recipes: "No amount of logic will alter the nature of things, nor persuade the drugs of the Amboshtha group to exercise a purgative virtue." `SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` lines 349 to 352, the astringent group: "The component members of the groups known as the Nyagrodhade-Gana, the Amvashtadi-Gana, and the Priyangvadi and the Rodhradi Ganas, Triphala, Shallaki, Jambu, Amra ..." `SS_Chikitsa_ch38.txt` lines 603 to 607, a Vasti: "A paste of the drugs of the Ambashthadi group, dissolved in a decoction of those of the Priyangvadi group and mixed with honey and clarified butter, may be used as a Vasti". `SS_Uttara_ch40.txt` lines 454 to 456, in a long-standing Atisara with blood and a predominant Kapha: "The drugs of the Ambashtadi group as well as those of the Pippalyadi group taken with honey would be similarly beneficial."

*Patha.* The name Patha, which the second pairing may concern, is a different Sanskrit name in the chapters (for example the Aragvadhadi group, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 28) and was not graded for this entry, because its link to the appendix line is an inference from a garbled OCR line.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Ambashtha found nothing.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Stephania hernandifolia*, Ambashtha, Amvashtha and *Cissampelos* found **I_367** (Patha, *Cissampelos pareira*, tags SS and others), whose title lists "Ambaṣṭhā" as a name for Patha and whose SS paragraph cites the Aragvadhadi group and two Sutra chapters for Patha but not the Ambashthadi group; the appendix pairing for the P line above, if read as Patha, differs from that entry's binomial. **I_1362** (*Stephania tetrandra*, Fang Ji, tag SBJ) is the Shennong entry on another *Stephania* and is not this plant. **I_1414**, **I_1418** and **I_1420** count the same first page of the appendix. No entry on Ambashtha was found. Nothing is merged. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Stephania* is a genus of the moonseed family (Menispermaceae), a family known for isoquinoline alkaloids, and plants of the genus are used as tuberous climbers. That is a statement about the genus and not a finding of the Sushruta text. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta chapters give no use for Ambashtha alone. They give a group built on its name, to be used for Pitta, ulcers, fractures and dysentery (Sutra 38), as an astringent group (Sutra 42), as a Vasti with honey and ghee (Chikitsa 38) and in a bloody, Kapha-predominant Atisara (Uttara 40). The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the chapters give are for the group and not for Ambashtha: the paste of the group's drugs dissolved in a decoction of the Priyangvadi group with honey and clarified butter, as a Vasti (Chikitsa 38, lines 603 to 607), and the group "taken with honey" (Uttara 40, lines 454 to 456). No quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Ambashtha only as the first drug of a group, used in the lists, the Vasti and the Atisara passages above. Those are therapeutic and group contexts, and the text says no more of the single drug. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The plant therefore carries no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status in the text as retrieved. The grade rests on a positional match of names. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with this plant: it is the name of a group of astringent drugs, used by a physician for Pitta, ulcers and dysentery. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of a Latin name that the chapters do not give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for Ambashtha or its group. The identity is open, and the genus has alkaloid-bearing members, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Dysentery and bleeding need medical assessment.
