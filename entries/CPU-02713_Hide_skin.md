# Hide / skin — جلد (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

**Avicenna, *Canon* of Medicine (CM).** (Canon Book II, entry *Jild*, Arabic text line 11735–11745). *Choice:* the best are skins of sucklings, for their moisture. *Actions:* its nourishment is little and viscous and close in its condition to the trotters; scrapings of goat skin laid on a bleeding stop it. *Cosmetic:* burnt viper skin as a salve for alopecia (*dāʾ al-thaʿlab*). *Swellings:* "it is said" that the skin of the water-horse laid on a pustule disperses it. *Wounds:* the ashes of mules and the like on fire burns and hot sores when there is no swelling, and for chafing of the foot and thighs and for piles; the skin flayed from a sheep laid at once on a blow prevents the injury, and is good for malignant sores, scab and gangrene. *Digestive:* the inner skin of the gizzards and crops of birds, especially cocks, dried, pounded and drunk with *ṭilāʾ* (boiled grape syrup) benefits stomach pain. *Poisons:* "it is said" a goat's flayed skin, warm, laid on a viper bite draws out the venom. No temperament is printed. No authority is named.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

The Canon maps animal skin onto bleeding, sores, burns, hair, the feet and thighs, piles, stomach pain and viper bites. Scrapings of goat skin laid on a bleeding stop it. Burnt viper skin, as a salve, is recorded for alopecia, named in the text as dāʾ al-thaʿlab. Skin of the water-horse, laid on a pustule, is said to disperse swellings. Ashes of mules and the like, burnt on fire, are used on burns and hot sores without swelling, for chafing of the foot and thighs, and for piles. Skin flayed from a sheep and laid at once on a blow prevents injury and helps malignant sores, scab and gangrene. The dried inner lining of cocks' gizzards, drunk with grape syrup, benefits stomach pain. A warm flayed goat skin laid on a viper bite draws out venom. The Canon gives no temperament, saying only that the nourishment of skin is little and viscous, and reports these uses with 'it is said'.

### Contraindications

- **Infection.** Raw or unclean hide on an open wound risks infection.
- **Snakebite.** The reported skin remedy is not a treatment; seek emergency care.
- **Animal source and ethics.** Several of the animals named are protected or not available; the Codex does not recommend any.
