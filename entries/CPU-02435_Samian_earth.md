# Samian earth — Samia (SAMIA), Dioscorides 5-172

## *A very white, light, soft and brittle earth that holds fast to the tongue like glue, of which Dioscorides records two kinds (the ordinary kind, and a crusty, thick one called *aster*); the translator's gloss is a list of alkaline earths and of *aster* (asteriated sapphire, chrysoberyl). The material is identified by name and tests only; the chapter does not name a mineral, so the identification is of medium confidence as a white clay-like earth* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder. It is **kept as a separate entry** from Samian stone (5-173, CPU-01402, `II_82_Samian_stone.md`): the first-pass check treated them as duplicates and the audit disagreed, because Dioscorides gives them separate chapters with separate headings, different marks and different uses (the stone is one that goldsmiths use to polish gold; the earth is a very white, soft, light earth that holds to the tongue). A search of `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` for "Samian", "Samia" and "Samios" found the two chapters, the Chian earth chapter (5-174) that refers to the Samian, and the index.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-172, *Samia* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10722; name-index row in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` at row 945, translator gloss quoted below, class guess mineral; the list row in `_sources/dioscorides.csv` is at line 897, where the entry is mis-labelled as a plant; the book index lists the heading under 827). The whole chapter was read. It reads (lines 10726-10731):

"Samian earth is preferred which is very white and light, and on touching the tongue holds it fast like glue. Additionally it is full of liquid, soft and brittle, such as that which some call collyrium [eye salve, suppository]. There are two kinds; besides that previously mentioned there is one called aster, crusty and thick like whetstone. It has the same properties and is burnt and broken like eretrias [5-171]. It stops the spitting up of blood, and is given with flowers of wild pomegranate for women's menstrual flows. Smeared on with water it soothes inflammation from stones [urinary, kidney], and inflammation of the breasts. Taken in a drink of water it represses sweating; it helps those bitten by venomous creatures, and those who have taken a deadly medicine [antidote]."

The chapter thus gives: a rule for the best (very white, light, holds fast to the tongue "like glue"); a texture ("full of liquid, soft and brittle", like what some call *collyrium*); two kinds (the first, and *aster*, "crusty and thick like whetstone"); a preparation (the *aster* "is burnt and broken like eretrias [5-171]" and has the same properties); and uses (it stops the spitting of blood; given with wild-pomegranate flowers for women's menstrual flows; smeared on with water for inflammation "from stones" and of the breasts; taken in a drink of water it represses sweating; it helps those bitten by venomous creatures and those who have taken "a deadly medicine").

**The two kinds.** The ordinary Samian earth is the white, light, tongue-sticking sort described first. The second kind, *aster*, is "crusty and thick like whetstone", and has the same properties. Dioscorides names no place for either beyond "Samian". The two kinds are two forms of one chapter and are kept inside this entry.

**The place name.** The chapter says "Samian" in the translation and *Samia* in the heading. The translator's gazetteer, as noted in the Samian-stone entry, defines SAMIA as a town of Elis; the chapter does not say whether the island of Samos or that town is meant. No choice is made here. [USER: verify]

**The neighbours.** The Chian earth chapter after it (5-174) says that Chian earth "which is white and almost an ashy colour like the Samian [above] should be used" and "has the same properties as Samian earth" (line 10738), and says it is used in the bath in place of saltpetre (line 10738). The chapter before (5-171, Eretrian earth) is the one the *aster* is burnt like. The stone of 5-173 is a different chapter.

**Identity.** The translator's gloss is "Alkaline Earths; Baryta — Barium monoxide; Lime — Calcium oxide; Strontia — Strontium monoxide; Aster — Asteria — Asteriated Sapphire, Chrysoberyl" (a list shared with the Eretrian earth chapter and extended with *aster*). It is a list of possibilities and not a single identification. The chapter's marks (very white, light, soft, brittle, sticks to the tongue) are those of a fine, absorbent, white clay-like earth, but the text does not name a mineral. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated from the chapter. The translator's gloss lists oxides of barium, calcium and strontium and a sapphire or chrysoberyl, but these are possibilities and not a finding. In general terms, a soft, light, white earth that sticks to the tongue is a fine-grained, water-absorbing earth such as a clay or a chalky earth. This is a general fact and is not asserted of this earth. Barium compounds in particular are toxic when swallowed, so the translator's gloss is not a reason to take any of them.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** Dioscorides records Samian earth as an earth that stops the spitting of blood, regulates women's flows, soothes inflamed breasts and "stones", represses sweating, and helps those bitten by venomous creatures or who have taken a deadly medicine. Spitting blood, heavy menstrual flow, breast inflammation, kidney stones, bites and poisoning are conditions that need medical care, and an earth of unknown composition should not be swallowed for them. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives: smeared on with water (for inflammation from stones and of the breasts); taken in a drink of water (for sweating, bites and poisons); given with flowers of wild pomegranate (for women's menstrual flows); and for the *aster* kind, "burnt and broken like eretrias [5-171]" (line 10726 onward). The Eretrian method is in 5-171: pound, settle, strain, dry in the sun, beat again, and form into tablets (lines 10721-10725). No quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any quantity from this entry.** [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] As general facts: clays and chalky earths absorb water and some substances in the gut, which is the basis of their historical reputation against poisons, but they are not reliable antidotes; swallowing earth carries a risk of infection, contamination with metals, and intestinal blockage; and the oxides of barium named in the translator's gloss are poisonous. No topical earth is an established treatment for breast inflammation, bleeding, bites or sweating. These points are not drawn from the chapter.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM).** The chapter prints no humoral quality of the earth. It states its actions only: it "stops the spitting up of blood" and "represses sweating". No warming, cooling, drying or moistening quality is printed. The "astringent and cooling" printed for the Samian stone (5-173) is that chapter's and is not carried over.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What the herbal offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention (provenance, season of gathering, sensory assay, adulteration detection), and this chapter shows the sensory-assay part clearly: very white, light, holding fast to the tongue like glue, soft and brittle, and a second form identified by its crust and thickness. The chapter is therapeutic and carries no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation beyond the model of assay by eye, weight and tongue, and the habit of naming the kinds of a material. The earth is not a recommended practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Spitting blood, heavy menstrual flow, breast inflammation, kidney stones, bites, poisoning:** these need medical assessment or emergency care; do not rely on an earth.
- **Swallowing earth:** risk of infection, contamination with metals, and blockage; the oxides listed in the translator's gloss include toxic barium compounds.
- **Pregnancy:** the chapter gives it for women's flows; an earth should not be swallowed in pregnancy. [USER: verify]
- **Identity:** the material is not named by the chapter; do not substitute any commercial earth.
