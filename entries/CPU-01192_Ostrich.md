# Ostrich — "Ostrich", "Ostrich Egg" / "Egg-of-an-Ostrich", "Ostrich Oil" (Bryan 1930, Ebers Papyrus; no Egyptian word or binomial printed in the list)
## *The ostrich, chiefly its egg and its oil, as Bryan's English renders the Ebers material; his list gives no Egyptian word and no binomial* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The only text on disk for this tradition is C. P. Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). In his chapter VII, "Organic Remedies", Bryan lists the animals "whose identity is not in doubt, and who were called upon to sacrifice themselves in the cause of the Healing Art". The list ends "... Swordfish Ostrich Locust Tarantula Beetle Bat Wasp Fly Tapeworm" (line 1404; row 125 of `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, "Bryan 1930, Ch. VII Organic Remedies (animals) (line 1404)", which notes "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). The Egyptian word Bryan renders "Ostrich" is not printed, and I have not read Ghalioungui's translation, which is not on disk. The identification is Bryan's.

The ostrich appears in several recipes in Bryan's text, always as the egg or the oil, never as meat. In each case below the text is Bryan's rendering and summary, and the quotations are his.

- **Face-cream** (line 1380, in his survey of strange ingredients): "a Face-cream of Bullock's Bile and Ostrich Egg, beaten up with Fresh Milk". The fuller recipe is in his chapter on cosmetics (lines 4115-4122): "ANOTHER REMEDY / Bullock's Bile / Whipped-up Ostrich Egg / Oil / Dough / Refined Natron Hautet-resin / Mix, make into a pap, mix in Fresh Milk, and wash the face therewith daily." Bryan introduces it as reserved "for those from whom the second and third, as well as the first bloom of youth had departed".
- **Headache** (line 2112): among poultices and lotions for a stubborn headache, "One consisted of Crocodile-earth beaten up with Egg-of-the-Ostrich ... and still another of Ostrich Oil whipped up with Bile-of-the-Black-abdu-fish". The fish is not identified by Bryan.
- **Stinking ulcer**: "ANOTHER REMEDY TO DRIVE OUT A STINKING ULCER IN THE BODY OF A MAN OR WOMAN / Ege-of-an-Ostrich Tortoise-shell" (line 2362; the OCR misprint "Ege" is as on disk), and a second form at lines 2692-2694: "Ostrich Egg Tortoise-shell Thorns-of-the-am-tree / Warm and anoint therewith. But DON'T GET TIRED DOING IT."
- **Excessive granulation** (line 2411): Bryan describes a remedy "to check excessive granulation" as "an unguent concocted of Ostrich-egg ground down with Tortoise-shell and Thorns-of-the-am-tree".
- **Pterygium** (lines 2847-2851): under "ANOTHER TO DRIVE OUT PTERYGIUM" a long list of ingredients includes "Egg-of-an-Ostrich" together with red lead, powdered wood from Arabia, iron, calamine, saltpetre from Upper Egypt, sulphur and honey: "Make into one and apply to the Eyes."

I searched Bryan's text for "ostrich" and found only these passages (lines 1380, 1404, 2112, 2362, 2411, 2694, 2849, 4119). Bryan's book is a summary with selected quotations, not a full translation, so the absence of further recipes in it proves little. ### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, an egg is mostly water, protein (the albumen is rich in ovalbumin) and, in the yolk, lipids, cholesterol, vitamins A and D and minerals. Ostrich egg shell is calcium carbonate. Ostrich oil is a rendered body fat, a mixture of triglycerides with oleic, palmitic and linoleic acids. Beaten egg white acts as a binder and a protein film on skin.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Ebers recipes use the ostrich egg and its oil as a base for a face-cream, a headache poultice, ulcer and granulation unguents and an eye application. The Codex records them and does not recommend them. Raw egg and unpurified animal fat on an ulcer or in the eye risk infection, and a stinking ulcer or an eye disease is a case for clinical care. The recipes also contain lead, saltpetre and sulphur compounds that are not safe in the eye. An ostrich egg is also an ordinary food; the Codex gives no medicinal use for it from these sources. No modern protocol is invented here.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted above: beaten up or whipped egg mixed with other ingredients to a pap or unguent and applied (face, head, ulcer, eye), and ostrich oil whipped with fish bile. Bryan prints no quantities in the passages read, apart from the direction "Warm and anoint therewith" (line 2694). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Raw or lightly cooked eggs may carry *Salmonella*, and applying them to broken skin or to the eye risks infection. Eggs and animal fats do moisturise skin and can act as binders, but have no known effect on ulcers or eye disease. Lead compounds such as red lead are toxic by every route and must not be applied to the eye. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Egyptian (EP):** None. Bryan's text gives no quality (hot, cold, moist, dry) for the ostrich, its egg or its oil. The Ebers corpus is organised by complaint and records what was done for a condition, not a classification of the substance.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. The ostrich recipes are for the complexion, headache, an ulcer and an eye complaint. The Egyptian practice of treating spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*) is relevant in general, and Bryan prints "words to be spoken" elsewhere in the papyrus (for example over verdigris with beetle-wax, line 2839); none is reported with the ostrich recipes, and since Bryan summarises, that is no proof that the papyrus gives none. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The ostrich is the source of an egg and an oil used as a base in a handful of remedies for skin, head, ulcer and eye, and the Codex does not recommend them. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Eyes:** do not apply eggs, animal fats or any of the mixtures above to the eye; the lead, saltpetre and sulphur compounds in the pterygium recipe are harmful there.
- **Ulcers and wounds:** raw egg and animal fat on an open ulcer risk infection; seek clinical care for any non-healing or foul-smelling ulcer.
- **Allergy and food safety:** egg allergy is common; raw egg can carry *Salmonella*.
- **Conservation and ethics:** wild ostrich populations and trade in their parts are regulated in some countries; the Codex does not recommend taking eggs or animals for these uses.
