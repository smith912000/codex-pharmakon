# Euphorbia root — 大戟 (Da Ji); also [one glyph lost]钜 (Ang Ju, "Raised Hook" in Yang)

## **Euphorbia pekinensis* Rupr. (Euphorbiaceae), the root, the plant of the Chinese drug 京大戟 *jing da ji* (high confidence from Guo Pu's gloss "the medicinal halberd" and from the name); Yang 1998 prints a different Latin name, *Cirsium japonicum* (a thistle), for the same Chinese name, and that conflict is flagged below* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** A lower-class drug. Euphorbia roots are strongly purgative and irritant, and *da ji* is a drastic drug that the classic itself says "reverses" liquorice. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and it is not for self-treatment.

*Da ji* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The placeholder row it replaces was named "Herba sen Radix Cirsii Japonici," which is Yang's Latin and is a different plant.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 大戟 in the lower classic (卷三 下经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3321, 味 paragraph; name line 3319). The Shennong text proper: "bitter, cold" (味苦，寒). It "mainly treats" "gu poison, the twelve waters, swelling and fullness with acute pain, accumulations and gatherings, wind stroke, skin pain, and vomiting" (主蛊毒、十二水，肿满急痛，积聚，中风，皮肤疼痛，吐逆). "One name: [a glyph lost] 钜." Sun's note in the text: "that the phrase 'grows in rivers and marshes' is absent here suggests that anciently this was perhaps one entry with 泽漆." No 久服 clause and no toxicity statement in the paragraph.

*Wu Pu layer.* None for this entry.

*Mingyi bielu layer* (line 3323). "Grows at Changshan. The root is gathered in the twelfth month and dried in the shade."

*Sun's note* (案, line 3325, cut short in the e-text). *Erya*: "[a lost glyph] 钜"; Guo Pu: "today's medicinal 'halberd' (戟)." Then a *Huainanzi* citation (Miu cheng xun), truncated. The Guo Pu gloss is the one piece of identification: *da ji* is "the halberd," a name that fits the plant's form and the later identification.

*Neighbouring entry.* The next entry, 泽漆 (name line 3329; not covered here), is described by the Mingyi layer as "the seedling of 大戟" (大戟苗也); Sun's note above suggests the two were once one entry.

*Drug-relations table* (Sun's edition): "大戟 reverses (反) liquorice" (line 4433); liquorice "reverses 甘遂, 大戟, 芫花 and seaweed" (line 4295); 石龙芮 and 秦皮 have 大戟 as envoy (使; lines 4381, 4509); 白薇 "dislikes" 大戟 (line 4397).

**Yang 1998 as second witness, and the identity conflict.** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 3289) prints "Da Ji (Herba seu Radix Cirsii Japonici) is bitter and cold. It mainly treats gu toxins, the 12 waters, abdominal fullness and acute pain, accumulations and gatherings, wind stroke, skin aching pain, and counterflow vomiting. Its other name is Ang Ju (Raised Hook)." The indications are the same as in Sun's text. But the Latin is *Cirsium japonicum*, a thistle (Asteraceae), which is not a drastic water-purging drug. Yang's own footnote 202 (line 3307) reads: "This medicinal is able to drastically drain water," which fits the euphorbia drug and not a thistle, so his Latin and his note do not agree; the footnote 203 (line 3329) says "the 12 waters refer to all categories of swelling." The entry here follows Guo Pu's "halberd" gloss and the drastic-purgative reading and takes the thistle name as an error in Yang's Latin. This is a judgement, flagged for the spine.

### Active Components

Euphorbia species carry a white latex rich in irritant diterpene esters, and the roots of the genus are known as drastic purgatives and skin and mucosal irritants. This is a general, well-established statement about the genus. No analytical source was retrieved for *E. pekinensis* in this entry, and no compound is asserted. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Toxic.**

- **What the text says it is for.** "Gu poison," "the twelve waters" (swelling and fullness), accumulations, and vomiting.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no duration.

Swelling, fullness with acute pain and vomiting are conditions for medical care. No Kenetica protocol is derived from this entry, and none should be.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest: the root, twelfth month, dried in the shade (line 3323). **Do not use any dose from this entry.** No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic, which the preface (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2935) defines as "the left envoys, mainly treating disease to correspond with earth; mostly toxic and not to be taken long." The entry has no 久服 clause. No cultivation status is attested; the grade is itself the warning.

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug for defined, severe swelling and accumulation. The tradition's own grading is the warning, and nothing in the record supports any cultivation use. A cultivator should not use it.

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC AND IRRITANT.** The latex and root of Euphorbia species injure skin, eyes and gut. Do not ingest or handle without protection.
- **Liquorice.** The classic's table lists liquorice as "reversing" this drug; do not combine.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Given the drastic action, avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** Do not substitute any other plant on the strength of Yang's Latin name (*Cirsium*). [USER: verify]
