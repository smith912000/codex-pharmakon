### Origin & Tradition

Xinjiang asafoetida is the fetid oleo-gum-resin tapped from the root of a giant fennel native to the Xinjiang region of Central Asia, and one of the botanical sources of the Chinese materia medica drug **A Wei** (阿魏) — the Chinese form of "asafoetida." It is a close relative of the classic Persian asafoetida (*Ferula assa-foetida*, this volume), sharing the same powerfully fetid, sulphurous-savoury character and the same *Ferula* gum-resin chemistry, and entered Chinese medicine along the Silk Road trade in aromatics and drugs.

In Chinese Medicine A Wei is valued for its strong, warm, acrid, fetid nature as an agent to **dispel "food stagnation" and abdominal masses, move stagnant qi, warm the cold abdomen, and kill parasites** — used for hard abdominal accumulations (including parasitic and food-stagnation masses), cold-type abdominal pain and distension, and as an antiparasitic. This is a slightly different emphasis from the Persian/Ayurvedic asafoetida's everyday carminative-culinary role, leaning more toward the dispersing of stubborn masses and parasites, but the underlying warming-carminative-antispasmodic action is shared across the whole *Ferula* asafoetida group.

Like its Persian cousin it owes its action to **sulphur-containing volatiles** and **sesquiterpene coumarins**, and like it the raw resin is intensely malodorous, mellowing on cooking.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy — avoid medicinal doses.** Mass-dispersing/emmenagogue reputation; culinary pinches are food, medicinal doses avoided. [USER: verify.]
- **Weakness/deficiency without stagnation (CM).** A dispersing herb is inappropriate where there is deficiency and no accumulation to break.
- **Heat patterns / hyperacidity.** A warm acrid herb can aggravate heat and acid dyspepsia.
- **Bleeding risk.** Antiplatelet activity reported; caution with anticoagulants.
- **Allergy / gluten (adulteration).** Commercial asafoetida is often cut with wheat flour.
