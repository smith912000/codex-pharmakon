# Tithymalus acutifolius — Pituousa, "Pityusa" (Dioscorides 4-166)

## *Tithymalus acutifolius, the first name on the translators' SUGGESTED line for 4-166 ("Tithymalus acutifolius, Euphorbia pithyusa, Tithymalus pithyusa — Pithyusa")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk lists the plant under this name (the name indexes in `Sources/*/extract/names.csv` were searched). The entry rests on one chapter of one text. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed under the chapter heading, and not Dioscorides' own identification. The Codex does not state the identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-166. PITUOUSA* at line 9556, SUGGESTED line at 9557, text at line 9558. A page number (723) and the running header "Book Four: Other Herbs & Roots" fall inside the chapter (lines 9559 and 9561), and the text resumes at line 9563 ("seed is broad like lentils"). The chapter stands in Book Four, after *4-165. TITHUMALOS* (the seven tithymals, 4-165a to 4-165g) and before *4-167. LATHUROS* (line 9565). The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 744, and the translators' indexes print "Tithymalus pithyusa 720, 723" (line 17884) and "PITUOUSA 723" (line 12511).

The whole chapter reads: "Pityusa sends out a stalk longer than a foot, very knotted, surrounded with sharp thin little leaves like those of pine flowers, small, and as it were, purple. The seed is broad like lentils; the root (which they call turpeth) is white, thick, and full of juice. This shrub is found considerably large in some places. Two teaspoonfuls of the root (given with honey water) purges downward. One teaspoonful of the seed (and as much as a spoonful of the juice) is taken with meal in a catapotium [pill] but three teaspoonfuls of the leaves are given. It is also called clema, crambion, paralion, or canopicon, and seems to differ from the Cyparissian tithymal, although it is considered one of them."

The description is for recognition: a stalk "longer than a foot", "very knotted", surrounded by "sharp thin little leaves" compared with "pine flowers", small and "as it were, purple"; a seed "broad like lentils"; a root that is "white, thick, and full of juice" and that "they" call *turpeth*; and a shrub that is "found considerably large in some places". The single claim is that it purges. Three parts are given for it, each with its quantity: two teaspoonfuls of the root with honey water, which "purges downward"; one teaspoonful of the seed and "as much as a spoonful of the juice", taken with meal in a *catapotium* (the translators' bracket says "pill"); and "three teaspoonfuls of the leaves". The chapter gives no other use. The last sentence is the text's own comment on classification: it "seems to differ from the Cyparissian tithymal, although it is considered one of them", which places the plant among the tithymals while saying that it may differ from the *cyparissias* kind (the fifth of the seven, 4-165c). The other names printed are *clema*, *crambion*, *paralion* and *canopicon*.

**Why the genus name is the translators'.** The preceding chapter, 4-165, is headed *TITHUMALOS* and its four SUGGESTED lines (9516 to 9519) include this chapter's own line, "Tithymalus acutifolius, Euphorbia pithyusa, Tithymalus pithyusa — Pithyusa" (line 9518). So the translators print the same binomials both at the head of the general tithymal chapter and at this one. The seven kinds listed at line 9521 (*characias*, *myrsinites*, *paralius*, *helioscopius*, *cyparissias*, *dendroides*, *platyphyllos*) do not include *pityusa*. The name *paralion* printed among this chapter's other names is close to the *paralios* kind of 4-165e. The Codex does not decide whether the two are the same, and the text of 4-166 says only that the plant "seems to differ" from one of the seven.

**Overlap.** A chapter-level search of the entries folder for 4-166, the Greek heading, *Pityusa* and *Pithyusa* found no entry on this chapter. I_1085 (*Euphorbia lathyris*, 4-167) and I_1087 (*Euphorbia resinifera*, 4-165) mention it only as a neighbour or as one of the four suggestion lines at the head of 4-165. This entry is the chapter itself. The sister entry I_1209 (*Tithymalus diffusis*, 4-163) is a separate chapter in the same group. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The text prints no constituent, and no analysis of the plant Dioscorides describes was read. It is a general fact that spurges (*Euphorbia*) belong to the spurge family (Euphorbiaceae), that most of them carry a milky latex, and that the latex of many species is a strong irritant to skin and eyes. Nothing is stated about the composition of the translators' *pithyusa*. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records a drastic purgative made from the root, seed, juice and leaves of a spurge-like shrub. These are Dioscorides' claims and not Codex protocols. A strong purgative of an unidentified plant can cause serious harm, and the Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives three preparations, with quantities as printed in the translation: "Two teaspoonfuls of the root (given with honey water)"; "One teaspoonful of the seed (and as much as a spoonful of the juice) ... with meal in a catapotium [pill]"; and "three teaspoonfuls of the leaves". It does not say how often or for how long, and it does not say how the juice is obtained or how the leaves are given. The teaspoonful is the translators' unit and the chapter gives no weight. The Codex offers no modern dose. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] It is a general, well-established fact that many spurge latexes are skin and eye irritants and that a strong purgative can cause dehydration and loss of salts.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** chapter 4-166 prints no hot, cold, moist or dry quality and no taste. The only functional statement is that the root "purges downward". The colours of the leaves ("as it were, purple") and the root ("white") are physical.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a description for recognition (a knotted stalk, small purple leaves, a seed like a lentil, a white juicy root), a note that the shrub is large in some places, and a remark on classification that the plant is counted among the tithymals but "seems to differ" from the Cyparissian one. The chapter assigns the plant no cultivation status. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a purgative, and a purge is the opposite of a conserving practice. The transferable point is the text's willingness to say that a plant is classed with a group while seeming to differ from it. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

Chapter 4-166 prints no warning. For safety on the group the Codex can point only to a neighbouring chapter: 4-165a, on the first of the seven tithymals, directs that one "must not stand against the wind, nor put his hands to his eyes" when juicing it, and that the body be rubbed beforehand with grease or oil and wine, "especially the face, neck and scrotum". That is a precaution for a different plant, and nothing here says that it applies to this one. The identification is only the translators' suggestion, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. The text gives no safety information for pregnancy, children or any other medicine, and gives doses only as quoted. [USER: verify]
