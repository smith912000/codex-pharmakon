### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The attestation rests on one row of the project's list `_sources/shennong.csv`, line 105: "Dragon Bone (龍骨 Lóng Gǔ)", class animal, source reference "SBJ", not flagged id-uncertain. This is the only list row with the characters 龍骨. Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) lists "Animals: Superior Class", "Middle Class" and "Inferior Class" in its contents (lines 770-776, the first at p.111), but the text layer is readable only to PDF p.104 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/README.md`); the pages after p.104 are empty in the file (from line 3872), so no animal monograph could be read, and `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` has no animal rows. Nothing of what the Classic says of Long Gu was read: no flavour, no indication, no note on how it was gathered or prepared. [USER: verify]

The only readable lines of Yang with the word Dragon are other names of plants: Long Zhi "Dragon Ganoderma" (line 1388), Long Dou "Dragon Bean" (line 2433), Long Sha "Dragon Sand" (line 2640) and Long Zao "Dragon Date" (line 3029), and "Dragon's Beard" (line 2021). They are not this drug.

**Position in the list.** The list is arranged by class, and Shi Dan (row 119) is the first row of the Middle Class stones, which Yang prints under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (heading line 1097; Shi Dan at line 1098). Row 105 stands in the animal block (rows 104-118) before that point. By position this suggests the Superior Class, which matches the spine's own note ("high"). It is an inference from row order and not a reading of any class heading, and the Animals pages were not read. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The spine names the row "Dragon bone / fossil bone" and labels it "fossilized mammal bone". No source read for this entry states that the Classic's drug is fossil bone; the list prints only "Dragon Bone". The project file `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/SBJ_Original_Chinese.md` has a heading for this drug, but it describes itself as a reconstruction, it contains entries (bee pollen, royal jelly) that are not in the Classic, and it marks some entries "[traditional attribution]"; it is a project note and not an edition, and it is not used here for any statement. ### Active Components

If the material is fossil bone, it is bone whose organic part has largely been replaced or lost, leaving mainly calcium phosphate (apatite) and calcium carbonate with traces of other minerals from the ground. The composition of any particular piece is unknown. The sources read give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. No text read says what Long Gu was used for. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation, and nothing here supports swallowing ground fossil bone. Fossil collecting is also regulated in many places, and fossils may be protected heritage.

### Contraindications

Powdered fossil or bone material sold as a remedy is unstandardised and may be contaminated, or may be a different mineral or an animal product that is protected. Fossils can be protected heritage, and export or sale is restricted in many places; the status of the material has not been checked here.
