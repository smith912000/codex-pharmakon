# Asian sparrow (nas.zan) — nas.zan, "barley feeder" (Clark 1995: "nas.zan bird (lit. 'barley feeder'): Asian sparrow")
## *A small bird that Clark's rendering of the Explanatory Tantra calls "Asian sparrow", with the Tibetan name nas.zan literally "barley feeder"; Clark prints no binomial for it, and the Men-Tsee-Khang translation does not name it* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The nas.zan bird is entered in the Explanatory Tantra (*bshad rgyud*), chapter 20, in Clark 1995 (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`), in the section "(8) Medicines of Animal Origin" (line 1147), under the heading "Meat" (line 1167). The entry's only locus is line 1170, where the following run of entries is printed: "mchhil.pa: Sparrow: Passer montanus: [has seven names]. nas.zan bird (lit. 'barley feeder'): Asian sparrow: [has ten names]. rtsangs.pa: a type of Gecko: Agama himalayana sacra: [has eleven names, for example sder.chhags. The flesh of] these three creatures increases semen, [is aphrodisiac, tonic and anodyne, and drains pus.]" The Tantra therefore groups three animals, a sparrow (*mchhil.pa*, with Clark's binomial *Passer montanus*), the *nas.zan* bird, and a lizard Clark calls a gecko. The one action stated for all three is that their flesh increases semen. Clark's square brackets mark wording not in the main verse; I did not confirm his exact convention, but his introduction says he interpolated liberally from the *Blue Aquamarine* commentary (line 80), so "aphrodisiac, tonic and anodyne, and drains pus" is likely commentary and not the verse. [USER: verify] The phrase "has ten names" is the commentary's count; the names themselves are not printed in the lines read.

*What the name says.* Clark's gloss, "lit. 'barley feeder'", is the only information he gives about the bird beyond the English "Asian sparrow" and the count of names. No Latin name is printed for it. The Tibetan name appears again in Clark's index of Tibetan names (line 1710, "nas.zan."), which is a list and adds nothing about the bird. The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, line 3536) reads at the corresponding place "The meat of sparrows and mountain-dwelling lizards increase semen", and it does not name a "barley feeder" bird. The glossary of animals in that book has two sparrows with Latin names (line 6890, "Passer moutanus S."; line 6920, "Passer domesticus Hodgon"), but the Tibetan names are not paired with them in the OCR, and the nas.zan bird is not found by name. So which bird nas.zan names, and whether it is a different species from the *mchhil.pa*, is not settled by the lines read. The sister entries are the Sparrow entry (III_187, *Passer montanus*) and the House sparrow entry on the spine (CPU-01327, *Passer domesticus*). [USER: verify]

*Sparrow flesh in the food chapter.* The chapter on food (Clark lines 672 and 678) names the sparrow in the list of creatures that "excavate the earth with their beaks" and then says: "Flesh of chicken and sparrow increases semen and is beneficial for wounds." Men-Tsee-Khang has the same two statements (lines 2905 and 2931). These sentences say "sparrow" and do not name the *nas.zan* bird, so they are not credited to it here.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, the flesh of a small bird is mostly water and muscle protein with little fat. Nothing links any constituent to the one action the Tantra gives, that the flesh increases semen.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced content is a single action of the flesh of three small animals, that it increases semen, in a list of animal medicines. The Codex gives no protocol. Wild small birds are protected in many countries, the flesh has no recognised effect on semen, and the Tantra gives no preparation or dose. The entry exists to record that the Tantra names this bird and groups it with a sparrow and a lizard.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation of the bird and no way of taking it. The line read says only that the flesh of the three creatures increases semen. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Semen production is governed by the testes and the hormonal system, and no common meat is established to raise it. Wild birds can carry bacteria such as *Salmonella* and parasites, and eating the flesh is a hygiene risk if it is not well cooked. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT):** The Materia Medica line gives the action "increases semen" (with the bracketed commentary additions above) and no taste, potency or quality for the bird. Qualities are given in the food chapter for meats as a class and by group, not for this bird by name: all meats in the groups are "sweet tasting and have a sweet post-digestive taste" (Clark line 673; Men-Tsee-Khang line 2923). The sparrow is in the group of beak-diggers (Clark line 672; Men-Tsee-Khang line 2905), but no quality is printed for that group in the lines read, and nothing says the *nas.zan* belongs to it. No energetic classification of the *nas.zan* bird is given in the sources retrieved.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(c) Absent.* Neither translation read gives this bird any cultivation framing. "Increases semen" is a therapeutic action of the flesh, listed with two other creatures in the animal-origin chapter, and it is not a status. The wider frame is the tradition's own: the three humors are rooted in attachment, hatred and obscuration (Clark line 51), these are "the three causes" of wind, bile and phlegm disorders (line 147), and the chapter on routine behaviour calls for refraining from the ten negative actions, the first being "taking life" (line 616). Those lines say nothing about this bird. The rejuvenation passage in chapter 23 (lines 1262-1277) does not name it, and no *bcud len* use of it was found. The Tibetan tradition has no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice, and none is asserted here.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this. It is a list entry for a small wild bird's flesh, with no preparation and no dose, and the Tantra's own first precept is to refrain from taking life. The honest use of the entry is as a record of what the text says. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** the flesh of wild birds can carry bacteria and parasites; it should be fully cooked, and wild-caught meat is not recommended.
- **Conservation and law:** wild small birds are protected in many countries; the identity of the *nas.zan* is uncertain, so check local law before taking any bird.
- **Fertility:** a concern about semen or fertility needs a clinician, and the Tantra's "increases semen" is a historical statement and not a treatment.
- **Ethics:** the Tibetan text itself lists "taking life" first among the ten negative actions (Clark line 616).
- **Identity:** "Asian sparrow" is Clark's English; no binomial is printed for the bird in the lines read.
