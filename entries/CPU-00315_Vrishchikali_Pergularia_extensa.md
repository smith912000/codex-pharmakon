# Vrishchikali — वृश्चिकाली (Vṛścikālī), Uttamarani, Veliparuthi, Utaran

## *Pergularia extensa* (Jacq.) N.E.Br. (≡ *Pergularia daemia* (Forssk.) Chiov.) | Family: Apocynaceae (Asclepiadoideae)

**Tradition Tags:** Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS) · Caraka Saṃhitā (CS, as Uttamārṇī) · Folk Siddha (Veliparuthi) · Yoruba/West-African ethnomedicine (as *P. daemia*)
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Kaṭu–Tikta rasa · Uṣṇa vīrya · Kaṭu vipāka — *kapha-vāta-śāmaka*, sharply *pitta-vardhaka* in excess

**Training Phase:** Purification (Śodhana) · Kindling (Dīpana–Pācana) · Wind-clearing (Vāta-anulomana)

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (Ayurveda, surgical corpus).** Vṛścikālī — literally "the scorpion-plant," named for the hooked, scorpion-tail curve of its slender twining shoots and its stinging, acrid latex — is a twining perennial of dry scrub across the Indian subcontinent. Suśruta and the later nighaṇṭus place it among the *tīkṣṇa* (sharp, penetrating) creepers used for *śodhana*: the emetic (*vamana*) and gentle purgative preparations that open obstructed channels before the main therapy. Its bruised leaf, warmed and applied, was a classic *vāta-hara* poultice over cold, aching, immovable swellings and over the gravid or post-partum abdomen to encourage *anulomana* — the downward, outward movement of wind. The whole plant carries the reputation of an * āmapācana* and *krimighna* (digestive-corrective and anthelmintic) herb, and its expressed juice was dropped warm as a decongestant in catarrh and infantile chest colds.

**Caraka Saṃhitā & the Nighaṇṭu tradition.** Under the synonym Uttamārṇī the plant appears in the later materia medica (*Bhāvaprakāśa*, *Rāja Nighaṇṭu*) as a bitter tonic-alterative — an herb for intermittent fevers, for "*yakṛt-plīhā*" (liver-spleen disorders), and for the sluggish, kapha-laden digestion where a hot, scraping bitter is wanted. The consistent thread across texts is *movement*: Vṛścikālī does not build tissue, it dislodges and expels.

**Folk Siddha & tribal use.** As Veliparuthi in Tamil Siddha practice, and across tribal pharmacopoeias, the leaf juice is an established emmenagogue, lactagogue, and remedy for asthma and whooping-cough; the plant recurs in fertility-regulating and uterotonic folk formulae — a use that maps directly onto the modern smooth-muscle findings below. As *Pergularia daemia* it is equally embedded in West-African and Arabian ethnomedicine for rheumatism, respiratory catarrh, and as a febrifuge.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Scorpion as Purifier:** The scorpion is an ambivalent sign across the traditions that touched this plant: venom and antidote in one tail. Vṛścikālī embodies the Ayurvedic paradox that the sharpest, most nearly-toxic bitters are precisely the agents of purification — *viṣa* handled correctly becomes *auṣadha*. The plant that stings when mishandled is the plant that stings the disease out. Its scorpion-curl shoots and acrid milk are a botanical emblem of *tīkṣṇa guṇa* itself: penetration that must be respected, dosed low, and steered downward and outward, never allowed to turn back on the healer. In the Kenetica frame it sits with the "controlled venom" pharmaka — substances whose whole value lies in the operator's discipline over a live edge.

### Active Components

- **Cardenolide / cardiac-glycoside fraction** — calactin, calotropin, calotropagenin-type glycosides and related uzarigenin derivatives (shared with the Asclepiad kin *Calotropis*); these underlie the emetic, uterotonic and cardiotonic edge and the plant's real toxicity ceiling.
- **Flavonoids** — a flavonoid-rich fraction correlates with the documented anti-arthritic/antioxidant activity.
- **Sterols & triterpenes** — β-sitosterol, lupeol, α- and β-amyrin.
- **Pergularinine and related alkaloidal constituents**; plus tannins, saponins and a pungent latex.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Vṛścikālī is filed as a **Purification-phase mover, not a builder.** Its role is to open a blocked channel — digestive, respiratory or uterine — through controlled, bitter-hot stimulation, then step aside. Indicated where the picture is *cold, damp, stuck, and kapha-vāta*: sluggish digestion with *āma*, boggy chest catarrh, cold immovable joint swellings, and delayed or obstructed downward flow. It is a *short-arc* pharmakon — used in bounded courses to break stasis, never as a daily tonic. Because its glycoside load gives it a live cardiotonic/emetic edge, it belongs in the operator's hands as a *steered* agent: low dose, downward direction, defined endpoint.

### Scientific Correlation

Evidence is **early-stage (preclinical animal/in-vitro only); no human trials.** Per PubMed:

- Kavaye Kandeda A et al., 2021, *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* — a hydro-ethanolic *Pergularia daemia* extract reduced pentylenetetrazole kindling-induced seizures and attenuated oxidative stress and neuroinflammation in mice. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2021.114338)
- Ananth DA et al., 2016, *Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy* — a flavonoid-rich *Pergularia daemia* extract ameliorated Complete Freund's Adjuvant-induced arthritis in rats, with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopha.2016.03.019)

These support the traditional anti-inflammatory/anti-rheumatic and CNS-modulating uses at a mechanistic, animal level. **Evidence grade: LOW** — small rodent studies, no clinical data, no standardised dosing; the traditional uterotonic/emetic uses remain phytochemically plausible (cardenolides) but clinically unverified.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṭu (pungent), Tikta (bitter)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry), Tīkṣṇa (sharp/penetrating)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** *Tīkṣṇa-vamana/anulomana* — a distinctive sharp, downward-and-outward moving effect on obstructed channels disproportionate to its bulk; scorpion-signature penetration.
- **Doṣa action:** ↓ Kapha, ↓ Vāta (via anulomana); ↑ Pitta in excess. *Kapha-vāta-śāmaka.*
- **Karma (actions):** Vamana (emetic), Dīpana–Pācana (kindling–digestive), Krimighna (anthelmintic), Vātānulomana, Ārtavajanana (emmenagogue), Kāsahara/Śvāsahara (anti-cough/anti-asthmatic), Śothahara (anti-swelling).
- **Suśruta gaṇa membership:** Aligned with the *tīkṣṇa/vamana* purification drugs of the Suśruta corpus rather than a named nutritive gaṇa; grouped with the sharp channel-openers.

### Contraindications

Pregnancy — **absolute contraindication** (uterotonic/emmenagogue, documented folk abortifacient use). Cardenolide content: caution with any cardiac-glycoside therapy (digoxin), in cardiac disease, and in electrolyte disturbance. Not for high pitta, ulcer, hyperacidity, or inflammatory GI states (heating, sharp). Not for children, the debilitated, or the dehydrated except under expert supervision. Overdose risk: vomiting, purging, cardiac effects. Lactation — folk lactagogue use notwithstanding, avoid without supervision.
