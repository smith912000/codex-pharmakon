# Belamcanda chinensis — She Gan 射干 (herb), also Wu Shan "Black Fan" and Wu Pu "Black Palmtree Leaf" (Shennong Bencao Jing)

## *Belamcanda chinensis, as the pharmaceutical Latin "Rhizoma Belamcandae Chinensis" that Yang 1998 prints for the entry "She Gan"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong text (SBJ).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The entry is in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`). The Latin name is the translator's pharmaceutical Latin and was matched to the binomial by stem in the extraction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv`, row 169). The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The characters 射干 come from the Codex's own list of Shennong names (`_sources/shennong.csv`, line 269), which prints them alone, and not from Yang's English text. [USER: verify]

**Chinese — Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ).** *She Gan (Rhizoma Belamcandae Chinensis), PDF page 92.* The page marker `[[p92]]` is at line 3414. The entry begins at line 3423 and runs to line 3431, and it follows the entry for Yin Yu (*Folium Skimmiae Japonicae*), which itself follows the closing lines of an entry begun on the previous page. The text file's OCR has damaged three words (it prints "cou~h", "affectin~" and "breathin~"); they are read here as cough, affecting and breathing. The entry reads:

"She Gan (Rhizoma Belamcandae Chinensis)211 is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats cough and counterflow qi ascent, throat impediment, and sore throat affecting breathing. It disperses binding qi, counterflow evil in the abdomen, and great heat due to food and drink. Its other name is Wu Shan (Black Fan). Another name is Wu Pu (Black Palmtree Leaf). It grows in rivers and valleys."

The next entry, Yuan Wei (*Radix Iridis Tectori*, line 3432 onward), is a separate drug with its own Latin name and is not treated here. Yang's note 211 (lines 3440 to 3448) is the translator's modern commentary and not part of the classic. It says that the drug "is able to drain fire, disperse swelling, and resolve phlegm binding", that it "is often used to treat throat impediment, sore throat, chest fullness, abdominal distention, panting, menstrual block, constipation, mounting conglomeration, and mother of malaria", and that it "can be used to level the liver to brighten the eyes". The note describes present-day use and does not say that these uses are the classic's. Where the entry and the note agree, on throat impediment and sore throat, the entry is the older layer.

*The class heading.* Yang's table of contents prints "Herbs: Inferior Class" (line 729). On the page of this entry no heading is printed under the page marker: the page opens (line 3415) with the running title "The Divine Farmer's Materia Medica Classic". The heading "Herbs: Inferior Class" is printed at the top of the page before (line 3374, under `[[p91]]`) and at the top of the page after (line 3452, under `[[p93]]`). The entry therefore stands under the inferior-class heading by position between two repeated page-top headings, and not directly beneath one. The preface (lines 391 to 395) says of the inferior class: "There are 125 inferior class medicinals which are used as assistants and envoys. They mainly treat disease and correspond to earth. They are usually toxic and cannot be taken for a long time." It adds that one bases one's efforts on this class "to eliminate cold and heat and evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and cure disease". Yang's commentary (lines 362 to 365) says that inferior-class medicinals "specifically treat disease" and "are usually at least slightly toxic". The entry itself does not say "toxic" and prints no warning.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Belamcanda chinensis* is a plant of the iris family (Iridaceae) with a rhizome, and the iris family is generally known to include isoflavone compounds in its rhizomes. Nothing here says what the Shennong drug contained, and the Latin name is the translator's. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Shennong text records, in its own terms, a bitter and balanced drug for cough with counterflow qi ascent, throat impediment, sore throat affecting breathing, binding qi, counterflow evil in the abdomen and great heat due to food and drink. These are the text's claims and not modern diagnoses, and they are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The entry gives no preparation, no part of the plant, no quantity, no frequency and no duration. The translator's Latin name says "Rhizoma" (rhizome), and the Codex takes that as the translator's, not as the classic's. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ:** "bitter and balanced" (line 3423). No toxicity is printed in the entry, and no meridian or other system of later Chinese medicine is printed in it. The preface describes the inferior class as usually toxic (line 393), and that is a statement about the class and not about this entry.

### Cultivation Register

**Chinese — Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: the inferior class (下品), by position between repeated page-top headings.* The class is assigned by the printed heading "Herbs: Inferior Class", at line 3374 (`[[p91]]`) and again at line 3452 (`[[p93]]`), and the entry stands between them on page 92, with no heading of its own. It is a printed class and not a promise of cultivation use. The preface (lines 391 to 395) gives the inferior class a purpose: it mainly treats disease, it is usually toxic and it "cannot be taken for a long time". The Codex reads the class as the classic's grade for a drug used against a complaint and then stopped, and not as a *yang sheng* ("nourishing life") drug.

The entry carries no sentence on protracted taking, no mention of making the body light and no mention of prolonging life. It asserts no *jing*, *shen* or *neidan* action, and none is added here.

**Practitioner's note.** An inferior-class entry in the Chinese scheme, listed for a throat and cough complaint and for binding in the abdomen, with no preparation and no dose. The class warns against long use. Nothing here supports taking this plant as a cultivation practice. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The Shennong entry prints no warning, and its silence is not a statement of safety; the preface says of the inferior class that it is "usually toxic and cannot be taken for a long time". The identity rests on a translator's Latin name, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. A sore throat that affects breathing needs medical care.
