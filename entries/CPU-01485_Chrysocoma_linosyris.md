# Chrysocoma linosyris — Chrusokome (Dioscorides 4-55)

## *Chrysocoma linosyris (one of the translators' suggestions; "Goldylocks")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) printed on the chapter's SUGGESTED line, not Dioscorides' own identification. The row was added from that line (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Identity doubt.** The SUGGESTED line (line 8486) reads "Chrysocoma [Bedevian] — Goldylocks ALSO: Chrysocoma linosyris, Chrysocoma villosa". The translators' main suggestion is the genus *Chrysocoma* from Bedevian, and the row's species is one of two listed under "ALSO". The text does not choose between them. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-55. CHRUSOKOME* at line 8484, text at line 8487.

The whole chapter reads: "Chrysocome is a small shrub twenty centimetres long with filaments like corymbi [flattened inflorescences] resembling hyssop [3-30]; a slender thick root like black hellebore — not unpleasant to the taste, equal to cyprus [1-124], somewhat sour in its sweetness. It grows in shady, rocky places. The root is warming and binding — of suitable use for the liver and pneumonia. It is taken (boiled with honey water) for cleansing the womb. It is also called chysitis, chrysanthemon, amarantum, or the beard of Jupiter, while the Romans say Iovis barba, the Africans, dubath, and some, burchumath."

So Dioscorides describes a small shrub twenty centimetres long, with flower clusters compared to hyssop, and a "slender thick" root likened to black hellebore in appearance and to *cyprus* (chapter 1-124, not read) in taste: "not unpleasant", "somewhat sour in its sweetness". It grows in shady, rocky places. The root is "warming and binding" and used for the liver and for pneumonia, and, boiled in honey water, for cleansing the womb. Names printed: *chysitis*, *chrysanthemon*, *amarantum*, "the beard of Jupiter" (Roman *Iovis barba*), African *dubath* and *burchumath*. The translators' index lists *Chrysocoma linosyris* at page 595.

**Overlap.** No entry covers this chapter. 4-56 Chrusogonon is I_1028 *Bongardia chrysogonum*, which reports 4-55 as a different plant. The names printed here recur elsewhere: 4-57 Elichruson (line 8495) is also called *chrysanthemon* and *amarantum*, and 3-154 Anthemis (line 7855) lists *chrysocome* among its alternative names. These are shared names across chapters, not evidence of one plant. The preceding chapter, 4-54 Paronuchia, is a separate plant.

### Active Components

The constituents of the plant Dioscorides describes were not read. The translators' *Chrysocoma* and *linosyris* names point to the daisy family (Asteraceae), but no composition is given in any source retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records a warming, binding root used for the liver and for pneumonia, and boiled in honey water to cleanse the womb. These are the text's claims. The Codex gives no protocol: the identification is split, no dose is given, and pneumonia is a serious illness.

### Preparation & Dosing

4-55 gives the root, "boiled with honey water", for cleansing the womb; the other uses are given without a preparation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No study of the plant was opened. Pneumonia is an infection that needs medical treatment. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints "warming and binding" for the root. It also prints "not unpleasant", "somewhat sour in its sweetness" as taste.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter offers the discipline of attention that the cultivation spec describes: size, habitat (shady, rocky places), the look and taste of the root, its quality (warming and binding) and a long list of names. It assigns the plant no status. The identification is the translators' and partly generic. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. Its record is therapeutic and gynaecological only.

### Contraindications

Pneumonia and liver complaints need medical assessment. The preparation is said to cleanse the womb, so it should not be used in pregnancy. The plant's identity is split, so no wild plant should be gathered or drunk on this basis. None of the traditional uses above is advice. [USER: verify]
