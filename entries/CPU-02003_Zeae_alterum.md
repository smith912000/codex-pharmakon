# Zeae alterum — Zeia (Dioscorides 2-111; "Zeae alterum genus [Fuchs]" in the translator's heading)

## *The single-grained kind of zea, a hulled cereal; the translator's heading lists "Zeae alterum genus [Fuchs], Zea briza dicta, Zea monococcus germanica [Brunfels], Triticum monococcum — Small Spelt, Engrain, One-grained Wheat" for this chapter, so the identification is the translator's, of medium confidence: einkorn wheat (Triticum monococcum L.) for the "single" kind, with the other kind of the chapter being emmer or spelt* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The attestation is Dioscorides, *De Materia Medica*, in the English translation on disk. The name "Zeae alterum" is not Dioscorides' own: it is the Latin label of a 16th-century botanist, Fuchs ("alterum genus", the second kind of zea), which the translator prints in the heading as one of several suggestions. A search of the other texts on disk for it found nothing. The Codex has separate rows for emmer wheat (*Triticum dicoccum*, CPU-00426, Ebers), common wheat (CPU-01015) and *Triticum romanum* (CPU-01650, from 2-113).

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter is 2-111, ZEIA, in Book Two on cereals ("FRUMENTACEA: CEREALS") (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 5446). The translator's suggestion lines (5447-5448) give two groups: the first, with "Zeae alterum genus [Fuchs]" and *Triticum monococcum*, is "Small Spelt, Engrain, One-grained Wheat"; the second, "Zeae primum genus [Fuchs], Zeae dicoccus major [Bauhin]", *Triticum dicoccum*, is "Emmer Wheat, Two-grained Wheat", followed by *Triticum zea* and *Triticum spelta*, "Spelt Wheat, Dinkel Wheat". The whole text of the chapter (line 5449) is: "There are two kinds of zea — one single and the other dicoccous, having the seed joined in two husks. It is more nourishing than barley, pleasant-tasting to the mouth, but made into bread it is less nourishing than wheat." The "single" kind is what the translator's first group suggests this row refers to. The text itself does not link its two kinds to the Latin names; that pairing is the translator's.

The neighbouring chapters give uses of zea and its products. These apply to zea in general, or to the dicoccous kind, not specifically to the single kind, and are given here only as context:

- 2-112, KRIMNON (line 5456 ff.): crimnum, the coarsest meal made from zea and wheat, from which porridge is made, is "abundantly nourishing and easy to digest, but that from zea is more astringent to the bowels, especially dried by the fire beforehand."
- 2-113, OLURA: olyra is very similar to zea but somewhat less nourishing (line 5462).
- 2-114, ATHERA: made of zea ground very small, "a spoonmeal ... suitable for children, and it is good in plasters" (line 5467).
- 2-118, CHONDROS: made of "zea dicoccos", with medicinal uses, including boiling with vinegar for leprosy and a decoction as a suppository in dysentery (line 5501). The text names the dicoccous kind only.
- 2-123, AMULON (starch): starch is also made from zea, "not fit for bodily use but for other uses it is fit enough" (line 5546).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** For this row the text gives a food grain with a comparison of nourishment. The Codex gives no protocol. It notes that cereals of the wheat group contain gluten and are unsuitable for people with coeliac disease.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives no preparation of its own. Related chapters give meal (2-112), a fine-ground spoonmeal (2-114) and starch (2-123), as above. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Einkorn is one of the oldest cultivated wheats and bears a single grain in each spikelet; emmer bears two. Both are hulled wheats. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** "More nourishing than barley ... less nourishing than wheat" in bread (2-111). Zea meal is "more astringent to the bowels" (2-112). No warming or cooling quality is printed for the chapter.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. The chapter is a comparison of foods by how much they nourish, a small act of attention to ordinary material, and it makes no cultivation claim.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do. This is a grain.

### Contraindications

- **Gluten.** Wheat-group grains are unsuitable for coeliac disease and wheat allergy.
- **Identity.** The Latin name is a 16th-century label chosen by the translator. Do not treat the row as a statement about einkorn specifically. [USER: verify]
