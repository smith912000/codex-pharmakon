# Slug-moth cocoon — 雀瓮 *Que Weng* ("sparrow jar"; alias 躁舍 *Zao She*)

## *The hard egg-shaped cocoon of the slug moth Cnidocampa flavescens (Walker) (syn. Monema flavescens), a stinging-caterpillar moth, the identification we give to 雀瓮; of medium confidence: the Mingyi layer says it is the "house" of a caterpillar on tree branches, taken in the eighth month and steamed, and Sun's note says the name is from its resemblance to a sparrow's egg and a pupa, but the characters naming the caterpillar are lost in the transcription, and no species is given* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** *Que weng* is a monograph of the Divine Farmer's Classic. It has no chapter in Sushruta, Dioscorides, Ebers or the Assyrian texts. Yang 1998 does not carry this monograph in its readable text (its animal pages are not readable on disk), so the monograph rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction alone.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the lower (下) classic, reads 味甘，平 ("sweet, balanced"). It governs 小儿惊痫 ("fright epilepsy of small children"), 寒热结气 ("cold and heat, bound qi") and 蛊毒鬼注 (*gu* poison and ghost *zhu*, two classical affliction names). It gives the alias **一名躁舍** (*zao she*, "restless house"). **There is no 久服 ("taken long") clause**, and no habitat appears in the paragraph. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 雀瓮 *que weng*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3941)

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 gives the place 生汉中 ("Hanzhong") and the instruction **采，蒸之** ("gather it; steam it"), and describes it: 生树枝间，蛄…房也 ("born among tree branches; the 'house' or chamber of the *gu*-[something]"; one character of the animal's name is lost in the transcription, and the standard materia medica reads it as the name of the stinging slug caterpillar, 蛅蟴 *zhan si* [USER: verify]). The harvest is 八月取 ("taken in the eighth month") (line 3943).

**Sun's note (案).** Sun cites the *Shuowen*, the *Erya* and Guo Pu on the caterpillar's name, but every key character in the quoted clauses is lost in the transcription, so the glosses cannot be read. One line survives: Guo Pu says that people in Qingzhou call it by a name ending in a lost character (今青州人呼为蛄…). Sun's own reasoning is plain: "The *Ben jing* names it *que weng* because *weng* ('jar') and *yong* ('pupa') sound near each other; since it is like a sparrow's egg and also like the pupa of a cocoon-worm, they called it so" (按：《本经》名为雀瓮者，瓮与蛹，音相近，以其如雀子，又如茧虫之蛹，因呼之) (line 3945). This is the only description of the object: a sparrow's egg-sized, pupa-like case on a tree.

**Identity.** The description, a hard egg-like case on a tree branch, gathered in the eighth month and steamed, and the standard modern identification of 雀瓮 with the cocoon of the slug moth (the "saddleback" or stinging-caterpillar family, Limacodidae) agree. The Codex follows it with medium confidence. *Cnidocampa flavescens* is the species usually named; the text does not distinguish it from other limacodid cocoons. The cocoon is a hard shell, wrapped round a pupa or an empty case, and not the stinging caterpillar itself.

### Active Components

The cocoon is silk, protein and the secretions the caterpillar uses to harden its case, with a pupa or its remains inside; this is a general statement about moth cocoons and does not come from the texts read. The caterpillars of slug moths carry hollow stinging spines connected to venom glands, which cause pain and a rash on contact; this is well established for the family. Whether the cocoon retains spines or irritant fragments is not stated in any text read. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records the cocoon for children's fright seizures, "cold and heat" with bound qi, and *gu* poison and ghost *zhu*. The Codex records these as the claims of the classic. Seizures in small children are an emergency and need medical care. The Codex does not recommend giving insect material to children.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation the text gives is that of the Mingyi layer: gather the cocoon (in the eighth month) and **steam** it. The paragraph gives no dose, course or manner of taking. No dose is given in the sources retrieved, and the Codex does not supply one. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Slug-moth caterpillars (Limacodidae) sting through their spines, and the stings are painful but seldom serious; this is general zoology. No source read shows any effect of the cocoon on seizures or fever. Febrile seizures and epilepsy in children are conditions with known causes and treatments. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong text proper).** 味甘，平: sweet; balanced. No organ or channel affinity is printed. The paragraph prints no toxicity word; the lower-class placement carries the preface's general statement that lower drugs are "mostly toxic" (多毒) and "not for long taking" (不可久服) (line 2935).
- No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph stands in the lower classic. The preface says lower drugs are "assistants" that "treat disease to correspond with the earth", are mostly toxic, may not be taken for long, and are for those who want to remove cold-and-heat evil qi, break accumulations and cure illness (line 2935). The paragraph has a flavour, a nature, indications and an alias, and **no 久服 clause**. It therefore carries only the lower class's therapeutic frame and no cultivation status. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 雀瓮 *que weng*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 2935 and 3941)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The entry is a record of a lower-grade insect drug for a child's complaints, and the Codex gives it for information only. The one durable point in the text is Sun's: the name is a likeness (a sparrow's egg, a pupa), which is the only reason any reader can tell what the drug is. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Contraindications

- **Children.** The classic's chief use is for small children's seizures; do not give insect material to children. A child's seizure needs urgent medical care.
- **Stinging caterpillars.** Do not handle the caterpillars or freshly collected cocoons barehanded; the spines sting and may break off in the skin.
- **Allergy.** Insect material can trigger allergic reactions in sensitive people.
- **Identity and hygiene.** Wild-collected cocoons are not of assured identity or cleanliness; steaming does not make them a safe medicine.
- **Animal welfare.** The Codex does not recommend collecting living animals for these uses.
