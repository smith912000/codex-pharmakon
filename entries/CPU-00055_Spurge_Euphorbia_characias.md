### Origin & Tradition

The spurges are a vast genus of plants united by one unmistakable feature: a **copious milky latex** that wells from any broken surface and is, in many species, intensely **caustic and irritant.** *Euphorbia characias* is a robust, blue-green Mediterranean shrub-spurge with the family's dense terminal flower-heads and its abundant acrid sap. The genus name itself is a fragment of classical pharmacy: the Roman author Pliny records that the plant **euphorbia** was named by King **Juba II of Numidia/Mauretania after his Greek physician Euphorbus** — making *Euphorbia* one of the few plant genera to preserve, in its very name, the memory of an ancient doctor and his use of the drug.

Dioscorides treats the spurges among the strong, dangerous plants of Book IV. The classical and folk tradition used the caustic latex and the drastic root chiefly in two registers: as a **violent purgative** (a hydragogue drastic of the harshest grade, evacuating "water and phlegm" — used historically in dropsy and obstinate constipation), and as a **corrosive/escharotic** applied externally to **burn off warts, corns and unwanted growths** and as a counter-irritant. The latex's power to raise blisters and destroy tissue made it both a tool (for removing warts and as a depilatory or rubefacient) and a hazard. Some spurges' latex was also used as a fish poison and, dangerously, in folk wart and skin-lesion treatment to the present day.

The same caustic chemistry that makes spurge latex useful makes it dangerous: it is a serious **eye hazard** (latex splashed in the eye causes severe, potentially sight-threatening keratoconjunctivitis), a skin irritant and blistering agent, and — because many *Euphorbiaceae* latices contain **tumour-promoting diterpene esters** — a substance handled with real caution. Spurge is therefore firmly a specialist/external material, its drastic internal use essentially obsolete.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Recorded as **historical and specialist/external material.** The caustic latex is not for self-administration; internal drastic use is obsolete and dangerous.

### Contraindications

- **Eyes and mucous membranes — absolute.** Latex contact causes severe, potentially sight-threatening injury; ophthalmic emergency.
- **Skin — caustic.** Blisters and burns healthy tissue; protect surrounding skin; not for large or broken areas.
- **Internal use — avoid.** Corrosive, toxic, drastic; obsolete and dangerous.
- **Pregnancy — absolute.** Drastic/caustic plant; contraindicated.
- **Children.** Keep away; the latex is a serious hazard.
- **Tumour-promotion caution.** The diterpene esters' co-carcinogenic potential argues against repeated/careless skin exposure.
- **No casual use of any kind.** Specialist/external framing throughout.
