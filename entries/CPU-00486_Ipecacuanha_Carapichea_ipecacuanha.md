# Ipecacuanha / Ipecac

## *Carapichea ipecacuanha* (Brot.) L.Andersson (syn. *Cephaelis ipecacuanha*, *Psychotria ipecacuanha*) | Family: Rubiaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SA
**Energetic Classification:** South American ethnobotanical framing — a "downward-and-outward expelling" root in folk practice (a purge that drives sickness up and out); the Galenic hot/cold humoral axis is *not native* here and is marked inferred/NA.
**Training Phase:** Foundation — Signature Study

---

### Origin & Tradition

Ipecacuanha is a low, creeping forest herb of the Rubiaceae (the coffee and cinchona family), native to the humid Atlantic and Amazonian forests of Brazil, extending into Bolivia, Colombia and Central America. The drug is the dried **root and rhizome**, thin and annulated ("ringed"), and the plant has passed under several botanical names — *Cephaelis ipecacuanha* and *Psychotria ipecacuanha* are older synonyms for what is now placed as *Carapichea ipecacuanha*.

The name is Tupí-Guaraní, commonly rendered as something like "the small road-side plant that makes you sick" — a folk gloss that is *plausible but not securely documented*, recorded here because the root's signal action is vomiting. Indigenous Brazilian peoples used the root against **dysentery** and bloody flux, and Portuguese colonists carried it to Europe in the seventeenth century, where it entered the pharmacopoeias first as a secret antidysenteric remedy and then, once the emetine alkaloids were characterised in the nineteenth century, as two distinct medicines: an **emetic** (syrup of ipecac) and an **amoebicide/antidysenteric** (emetine and its derivatives). Both of those historical uses have since been sharply curtailed by modern evidence — a deflation this entry treats as central rather than incidental.

### Active Components

- **Emetine** and **cephaeline** — the principal **isoquinoline alkaloids** of the root. Emetine is the more studied; cephaeline is the more emetic per unit weight. Together they account for the root's two actions: local gastric irritation plus central chemoreceptor-trigger-zone stimulation (emesis), and inhibition of protein synthesis in *Entamoeba histolytica* and mammalian cells (amoebicidal and cytotoxic).
- **Psychotrine** and minor congener alkaloids.
- The alkaloids are **protein-synthesis inhibitors** (blocking ribosomal translocation) — the same mechanism underlies both the antiparasitic effect and the systemic toxicity.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica ipecacuanha is filed as the archetypal **expellant / emetic root** — the plant whose signature is forced upward evacuation. Two historical therapeutic identities hang off that signature, and the Codex grades them honestly:

1. **Emetic (syrup of ipecac).** For most of the twentieth century syrup of ipecac was the standard household and emergency means of inducing vomiting after poison ingestion. **Modern toxicology has abandoned this.** Position statements from the American Academy of Clinical Toxicology and the European Association of Poisons Centres and Clinical Toxicologists conclude that ipecac should *not* be administered routinely to poisoned patients: the amount of marker removed is highly variable and falls with time, there is no clinical evidence it improves outcomes, and it can delay or impair activated charcoal, oral antidotes and whole-bowel irrigation. This is a key deflation — the once-canonical use is now explicitly deprecated.

2. **Amoebicide / antidysenteric.** Emetine (and dehydroemetine) were genuinely effective against **invasive amoebiasis**, matching the indigenous anti-dysentery use at the molecular level. But emetine has largely been *superseded by metronidazole* and related nitroimidazoles, which are safer and better tolerated. Emetine now survives mainly as a reserve agent where alternatives fail.

The Kenetica reading keeps the folk-to-pharmacology thread — "the sickening root" really is an emetic and really does hit *Entamoeba* — while refusing to present either use as current best practice. Ipecac is here as a **historical and cautionary** signature, not a recommendation.

### Preparation & Dosing

Ipecacuanha is **not a home remedy** and inducing vomiting is not endorsed. The table is for reference and historical record only; no dose is offered for self-use.

| Form | Historical context | Status now |
|---|---|---|
| Syrup of ipecac | Induced emesis after poisoning | **Deprecated** — not for routine use (AACT/EAPCCT position statements); do NOT induce vomiting at home |
| Emetine / dehydroemetine (parenteral) | Invasive amoebiasis | Largely superseded by metronidazole; reserve/specialist use only |
| Ipecacuanha as an expectorant (very low dose, older compound preparations, e.g. "ipecac in cough mixtures") | Loosening secretions | Marginal historical use; low evidence |

Safety caveats: never used to treat "toxins" or for "cleansing"; any therapeutic use of emetine is inpatient/specialist with cardiac monitoring. Chronic or repeated dosing is dangerous at any level (see Contraindications).

### Scientific Correlation

- **Emetine/cephaeline are emetic and inhibit protein synthesis** — *Established (mechanism).*
- **Routine syrup of ipecac for poisoning** — *Deprecated / evidence against* — no outcome benefit; officially abandoned (AACT/EAPCCT).
- **Emetine amoebicidal in invasive amoebiasis** — *Established but superseded* by metronidazole.
- **Emetine cardiotoxicity (cardiomyopathy, arrhythmia) with cumulative exposure** — *Established* — including a documented, mechanistically-explained pattern of harm from chronic ipecac abuse in eating disorders (sodium-channel blockade; reversible in some cases, but the threshold for irreversible myocardial damage is undefined).
- **Emetine antiviral/anticancer repurposing (incl. SARS-CoV-2)** — *Preclinical only* — nanomolar in-vitro potency and a proposed eIF4E/cap-binding mechanism, but no established clinical antiviral role; its narrow toxic margin is the barrier.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

In Brazilian and colonial-era folk practice ipecacuanha is a **downward-rooted expellant used paradoxically upward** — the medicine of forced discharge, given to void dysenteric matter and later, in dilute form, to loosen chest secretions. Mapped onto the Codex grid it reads as a **cold, dispersing, strongly evacuant** agent. This placement is analogical and inferred; the plant's home tradition did not use Galenic humoral categories.

### Contraindications

Ipecacuanha's alkaloids are protein-synthesis inhibitors with a genuinely narrow toxic margin. Real safety flags:

- **Cardiotoxicity (the defining hazard)** — emetine causes dose-cumulative cardiomyopathy, conduction abnormalities and arrhythmia via sodium-channel blockade. This is the mechanism behind the well-documented, sometimes fatal, cardiac harm seen with **chronic ipecac abuse in bulimia and other eating disorders**. Any repeated exposure is dangerous.
- **Do NOT induce vomiting** — deprecated in poisoning; contraindicated outright where consciousness is decreased or failing, and after ingestion of **corrosives** or **hydrocarbons with high aspiration potential** (risk of chemical burn on the way back up, or aspiration pneumonitis).
- **Eating-disorder populations** — ipecac is a drug of abuse in this group; access and use are a specific red flag warranting cardiac assessment.
- **Skeletal-muscle weakness and myopathy** — reported with chronic emetine exposure.
- **Not a home remedy, not a detox, not a cleanse** — no legitimate self-care indication.
- **Pregnancy/lactation** — avoid; emetine is cytotoxic.
