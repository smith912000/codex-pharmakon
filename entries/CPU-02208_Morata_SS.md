### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, in four places.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from an extraction of the translation's group lists; the 30 October check pass kept it as a distinct row with the identification open.

**Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.38, the Varuna'di group.** The chapter classifies drugs "usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 9). The group is printed at lines 44 to 50 (Vol. I lines 15385 to 15391): "The drugs known as Varuna, Artagala, Shigru, Madhu-Shigi-u, Tarkari, Mesha-Shringi, Putika, Xaktamala, Morata, Agnimantha, the two kinds of Sairiyaka, Vimvi, Vasuka, Vasira, Chitraka, Shatavari, Mlva, Ajashringi, Darbha, and the two kinds of Vrihati form the group known as the Varunadi." The metrical text (lines 52 to 55) says the group "is possessed of the efficacy of reducing the deranged Kapham and fat and proves efficacious in cases of cephalaegia, Gulma and internal abscesses." These are group claims, not claims for Morata alone.

**Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.38, the Viratarva'di group (a different row).** The next group prints "Morata Vasuka" as a pair (lines 57 to 62; Vol. I line 15402). The Codex keeps that name as its own row (CPU-02209); whether it is two drugs, Morata and Vasuka, or one, is not settled by the text, and Vasuka also appears in the Varuna'di list above as a separate member. The Viratarva'di group's metrical text, at lines 66 to 70 and the page that follows, says it "subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles". That is a claim for the group and applies to the Morata Vasuka row, not to this one. [USER: verify the relation]

**Sushruta (SS), Chikitsitasthana ch.7, stone disease.** In the treatment of *Ashmari* (urinary calculus), a decoction of "Kusha, Kasha, Shara, Gundra, Itkata, Morata, Ashmabhid, Shatavari, Vidari, Varahi, Shali-mula, Trikantaka, Bhalluka, Patala, Patha, Pattura, Kuruntika, Punarnava, Shirisha", with a paste (*Kalka*) of Shilajatu, Madhuka (flower) and the seeds of Indivara, Trapusha and Ervaruka, "would speedily bring about the disintegration of Pittaja Ashmari (calculi, etc.)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch07.txt` lines 49 to 55; Vol. II line 15011). The text adds that "an alkali, Yavagu (gruel), soup, a decoction, or milk, properly prepared with the above Pitta-subduing drugs, should also be prescribed as food and drink" in the same condition. Morata stands among the Pitta-subduing drugs of the decoction; the line gives it no separate action.

**A related name: Kshira-morata.** The same translation prints "Kshiramorata" in the sweet (*Madhura*) taste group of Sutrasthana ch.42 (`SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` line 298; Vol. I line 16994) and "Kshira-morata" in a potion of Sariva, the two kinds of Yashti-madhu, Draksha, Payasya, Kshira-morata, Vidari and Gokshura with honey in the chapter on insect bites (Kalpasthana ch.8, `SS_Kalpa_ch08.txt` lines 773 to 775; Vol. II line 32458). These are printed as one word, and the text does not say whether Kshira-morata ("milky Morata") is Morata, a variety of it, or a different plant. The Codex does not merge them. [USER: verify]

The text on disk gives no taste, no part used and no habitat for Morata, and no Latin name. The translator's general index of names (the volume-three index page that prints "Morata", `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 29293) is a page-reference list and gives no identification.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Through its group and formula only: reducing Kapham and fat, headache, abdominal lumps and internal abscesses (Varuna'di); and, in a decoction with other Pitta-subduing drugs, the disintegration of Pitta-type urinary calculi (Chikitsa ch.7).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no amount in the decoction, and no action of Morata alone.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this item. Urinary stone disease is a medical condition that needs proper care.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources give it as one of at least nineteen herbs in a decoction taken with a paste (Chikitsa ch.7). The decoction's proportions are not printed in the passage read. Chapter 38 ends with a general instruction for all its groups: they "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", using "two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group", or a whole group, as the physician decides (lines 561 to 568). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Bhishagratna 1907).** No taste (*rasa*), potency (*virya*) or post-digestive effect (*vipaka*) is printed for Morata. The Varuna'di group's metrical text gives only the group's actions (lines 52 to 55). The Chikitsa ch.7 decoction is described as made of "Pitta-subduing drugs" as a whole.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* Morata appears in a therapeutic group and in a stone-disease decoction. It was not found in a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) chapter, and no *ojas* effect is assigned to it. The related Kshira-morata sits in the sweet taste group, which is a statement about taste and not a cultivation grade.

**Practitioner's note.** A name without a plant behind it, in a group for Kapham and in a decoction for stone disease. There is nothing to source, take or cultivate. The honest outcome is to keep the name and its contexts on record and leave it.

### Contraindications

- **Identity unresolved.** Do not substitute any product for it, and do not assume it is the same as Kshira-morata or Morata Vasuka.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Urinary stones, pain on passing urine, abscesses.** These are medical conditions; use proper care. Do not rely on a herbal decoction for a stone.
