# Euphorbia antiquorum — Vajri, Sushruta Samhita Appendix 3

## *Euphorbia antiquorum L. (triangular spurge), a succulent, spiny, milky-juiced shrub, as the translator's Latin gloss to the Sanskrit name Vajri; the Latin is Bhishagratna's, the pairing with the Sanskrit name is ours from the two OCR columns, and the identification of the plant is of high confidence. The first-pass note that equated it with Snuhi is not supported by the appendix, which pairs Snuhi with a different spurge* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** The milky latex of spurges (Euphorbia) is a strong irritant to skin, eyes and mucous membranes and is toxic if swallowed. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take this plant.


**Named in the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) only through the translator's Appendix No. 3, "Names of drugs with Latin synonyms".** This is a single-text entry. The appendix is printed in two columns, Sanskrit names on the left and Bhishagratna's Latin or English glosses on the right, and the e-text `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` splits the two columns into separate runs of lines. In that file the Sanskrit name stands at line 33218 as "Vajri" (the OCR garbles diacritics) and the Latin gloss "Euphorbia  Antiquorum." at line 33244. The extraction file `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` lists the Latin line as unpaired ("OCR column split"). The pairing here is ours, made by counting entries in the two columns from points where the pairs are unambiguous: Vacha to Acorus Calamus, Vadara to Ziziphus Jujuba, Va'guji to "See Somaraji", then Vajri to "Euphorbia Antiquorum", Vakula to Mimusops Elengi, Vamsa to Bamboo and Bandhujiva to Pentapetes Phoenicea. The count held at every checked anchor, but it remains a reconstruction from OCR and must be checked against the page scan. [USER: verify] 

**Not Snuhi in this glossary.** The same appendix pairs the name Snuhi (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 613, Vol. III Appendix 3 line 33025) with "Euphorbia Neriifolia.", and a second spurge name, read as Sehunda and Sehundi (name at line 32802, Latin at line 32874), with the same Latin. In the Samhita the Snuhi is the plant with the milky juice: in the chapter on caustics and purgatives "an electuary composed of sugar, clarified butter and the milky juice of a Snuhi plant, should be used for purgative purposes", and Pippali powder is soaked in the milky juice of the same plant (`SS_Sutra_ch44.txt` lines 548 to 556); Snuhi is also among the trees burnt to ashes for alkali (`SS_Sutra_ch11.txt` line 126). Those uses belong to Snuhi, which this glossary glosses as another species, and are not carried over to Vajri.

**The name in the main text.** The Sanskrit form Vajri was not found in the chapter files of `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`. The attestation rests on the appendix line alone.

**No other text.** A search of the other source texts on disk for *Euphorbia antiquorum* found nothing.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Euphorbia antiquorum* is a succulent spurge of South Asia often grown as a hedge; its latex is a skin and eye irritant. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC and IRRITANT.** The latex can injure skin and eyes and is dangerous if swallowed. Do not ingest or handle it carelessly.
- **Identity.** The pairing with Vajri is ours, and Snuhi is glossed as a different spurge in the same appendix. [USER: verify]
