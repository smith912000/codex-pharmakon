# Euryale seed — 鸡头实 *jitoushi* ("chicken-head fruit"), also 雁啄实, 芡

## *Euryale ferox, the prickly water lily (fox nut); the identification is ours, made from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence. The synonym 芡实 is the modern drug name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on one text in one edition, Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). A search of the other texts on disk for *Euryale* found nothing.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The name line is 鸡头实 (line 1585). The classic's own paragraph, the only layer that is "Shennong", is at line 1587: 味苦，平。主湿痹，腰脊膝痛，补中，除暴疾，益精气，强志，令耳目聪明。久服，轻身、不饥、耐老、神仙。一名雁啄实。生池泽。 In English: "Flavour bitter, level. Governs damp-blockage (湿痹), pain of loins, spine and knees; replenishes the centre (补中); removes sudden illness (除暴疾); adds to essence-qi (益精气); strengthens the will (强志); makes ears and eyes sharp (令耳目聪明). Taken long: lightens the body, no hunger, resists ageing, 'divine immortal' (久服，轻身、不饥、耐老、神仙). Also called 雁啄实 ('goose-peck fruit'). Grows in pools and marshes." "Divine immortal" (神仙) is the classic's own word and is given as printed. The compound 精气 ("essence-qi") is a single term in this text and is not split here. The classic places the entry in the upper class (上经; the group tally at line 1593 reads "upper fruits, upper class, five kinds"). The preface (line 43) describes the upper class as nourishing life, non-toxic, and safe for much and long use.

**Mingyi bielu (《名医》, later additions) layer.** Line 1589: "also called 芡; grows at Leize; gathered in the eighth month" (一名芡，生雷泽。八月采).

**Sun Xingyan's note (案).** Line 1591 is a chain of glosses on the name. The *Shuowen* and *Guangya* give "芡 is 鸡头". The *Zhou li*, in the office of the basket-bearer, lists the added basket contents including 芡, and Zheng Xuan glosses 芡 as 鸡头. The *Fangyan* gives regional names: the northern Yan call it by another name (written with a character lost in the transcription); between Qing, Xu, Huai and Si it is 芡; in the south of Chu, along the Jiang and Xiang, it is 鸡头, "or 雁头, or 乌头". The *Huainanzi* has 鸡头 in a sentence about curing, and Gao You says "the 芡 in the water; in You province called 雁头". The *Gujin zhu* (a later work) adds: "leaf like a lotus but larger, the leaf crinkled on top as if boiling; the fruit has awns and thorns; inside is a 'rice' that can be used to get through hunger" (叶似荷而大，叶上蹙绉如沸，实有芒刺，其中有米，可以度饥). That passage ends with a word printed 茑子 in the transcription, which the Codex does not interpret. The glosses show an aquatic plant with a spiny fruit and an edible inner seed, and the Codex notes that 乌头 is also the name of the aconite plant elsewhere; it is only a regional name for this plant in this quotation.

### Active Components

None is stated in the text. As a general fact, the seed of *Euryale ferox* is a starchy seed, eaten as a food in East and South Asia. The Codex retrieved no analytical source and names no other compound. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The classic ascribes to the seed relief for damp-blockage and pain of the loins and back, a replenishing action, and a series of long-use effects. The Gujin zhu quotation in Sun's note says the inner seed "can be used to get through hunger", which agrees with the classic's 不饥 only loosely: the classic's phrase belongs to the 久服 clause, the later note to a famine food. The Codex gives no protocol and does not turn "damp-blockage" into a modern diagnosis. The seed is also an ordinary food, a general fact.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic names no preparation and no quantity. The Mingyi layer gives the gathering month (the eighth); the classic's own paragraph gives the habitat (pools and marshes). The Gujin zhu remark in the 案 names an inner "rice" of the fruit. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the upper class (上经), and its 味 paragraph carries a 久服 clause with a life-nourishing status: 久服，轻身、不饥、耐老、神仙, "taken long: lighten the body, no hunger, resist ageing, divine immortal" (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 鸡头实 *jitoushi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1587). The same paragraph says the seed 益精气，强志, "adds to essence-qi, strengthens the will", which the Codex records as the classic's wording for this substance; it does not build a *jing-qi-shen* account on it. "Lighten the body" (轻身) is the classic's effect category and not a weight claim. Neidan overlays are not a part of this text. The Mingyi and 案 layers add no cultivation clause.

**Practitioner's note.** The classic's 久服 clause for this plant is long, and the plant is a food seed. The text gives no preparation, no daily amount and no duration. A cultivator can say what the classic says, and that it names a seed that people ate, and no more.

### Contraindications

The classic does not warn. No safety information for pregnancy, children or other medicines was read, and none is asserted. Damp-blockage and pain of the loins, spine and knees need medical assessment. Seeds eaten as food are not a dose recommendation. [USER: verify]
