# Chinese Hawthorn

## *Crataegus pinnatifida* Bunge | Family: Rosaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ, FMT

**Energetic Classification:** Sour and sweet, slightly warm — descending, dispersing, stagnation-breaking; enters Spleen, Stomach, Liver

**Training Phase:** Foundational — a digestive corrective taken with or after food, not a systemic agent

---

### Origin & Tradition

Two hawthorns, one genus, two entirely different doctrines. That is this entry.

**Traditional Chinese Medicine (山楂, *Shān Zhā*).** The dried fruit of *Crataegus pinnatifida* Bunge, or of its large-fruited variety *C. pinnatifida* var. *major* N.E.Br., is *Shan Zha* — one of the canonical *medicine-and-food homology* (药食同源) herbs, meaning it is legally and culturally both a drug and a foodstuff. Its classical function is **digestive**, and specifically it "disperses food accumulation" (消食化积, *xiāo shí huà jī*). The retrieved reviews state the indication with unusual precision: it has been used "for more than 2000 years to treat indigestion, **stagnation of meat**, hyperlipidemia, blood stasis, heart tingling, sores" (Zhang et al., 2022, *J Pharm Pharmacol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jpp/rgac050)), and traditionally "to treat indigestion, cardiodynia, thoracalgia, hernia, postpartum blood stagnation, and hemafecia" (Li et al., 2022, *J Ethnopharmacol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2022.115819)).

**On the attestation date — an honest correction to the tag.** This entry carries the SBJ tag, and it should be qualified. The retrieved ethnopharmacological review states that *C. pinnatifida*'s "usage was **first described in 'Xinxiu Ben Cao'**" (Li et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2022.115819)) — the *Newly Revised Materia Medica*, a **Tang-dynasty (7th century CE)** state pharmacopoeia, not the Han-era *Shennong Bencao Jing* (c. 200 CE). The parallel review's "more than 2000 years" (Zhang et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jpp/rgac050)) is a round rhetorical figure that does not agree with its sister review's specific first-attestation. **Where two reviews conflict, the specific text-name beats the round number.** Shan Zha's medicinal attestation should be treated as Tang, c. 659 CE, roughly 1,350 years — not Han, not 2,000. **The digestive doctrine is specific, and the specificity is the point.** *Shan Zha* is not a general tonic. In classical practice it is the fruit you reach for when the accumulation is of **meat and fat** — the after-effect of a heavy, greasy, protein-dense meal. Other food-stagnation herbs in the Chinese pharmacy are keyed to other substrates (*Shen Qu* to fermented grain-stagnation, *Mai Ya* to starch), and *Shan Zha* holds the meat-and-grease slot. This is not folkloric vagueness. It is a substrate-specific indication, and it is unusual.

**Folk medicine and food (FMT).** In everyday Chinese use *Shan Zha* is barely medicinal at all. It is *bīng táng hú lu* — hawthorn fruits on a skewer in hard candy, sold on winter streets — and it is *shān zhā piàn*, the pressed sour hawthorn discs eaten as a children's snack and after rich meals, and it is a component of digestive syrups and pastilles. It is one of the very few substances in this codex where the folk delivery vehicle is a confection sold to children.

**Korean and Japanese use.** *C. pinnatifida* is used across the East Asian sphere; one retrieved clinical study is a Korean cosmetic application (see Scientific Correlation).

#### **THE TWO HAWTHORNS — a doctrinal divergence within one genus**

**European hawthorn (*Crataegus monogyna*, *C. laevigata*) is the cardiac herb.** In the European tradition it is a heart drug: cardiotonic, used for angina and cardiac insufficiency, associated with leaf-and-flower preparations, and carried into modern European phytotherapy with a genuine (if contested) cardiological literature and a well-known monograph tradition.

**Chinese hawthorn (*C. pinnatifida*) is the digestive.** Its classical indication is food stagnation. Its delivery form is a snack eaten after dinner. Its organ affinity is Spleen and Stomach before Heart. Its part used is the **fruit**, not the leaf-and-flower.

Same genus. Same family. Two traditions that looked at closely related trees and derived doctrines that barely overlap. This is one of the codex's cleanest demonstrations that traditional indications are not read off chemistry alone — they are shaped by which organ was used, which preparation was normal, and what the local diet needed a remedy *for*. A culture eating heavy meat and needing a digestive found a digestive. A culture with a cardiac-herb tradition and an interest in the flowering tops found a cardiac herb.

**Modern research is blurring the two, and this must be named.** The retrieved *C. pinnatifida* literature is now overwhelmingly cardiovascular: hypolipidemic, antimyocardial, anti-ischemia, antithrombotic, anti-atherosclerotic (Zhang et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jpp/rgac050)); "cardiovascular protection, and lipid reduction" (Li et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2022.115819)); an antihypertensive study in spontaneously hypertensive rats opening with the claim that hawthorn fruits have "beneficial therapeutic effects on hyperlipidemia, hypertension, and coronary heart disease" (Chi et al., 2024, *Food Funct*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/d3fo02641h)); an atherosclerosis study explicitly framed by the observation that "the widely distributed hawthorn is used to prevent and cure heart disease not only in China but also in the United States and several European countries" (Zhang et al., 2013, *J Ethnopharmacol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2013.04.053)).

Read that last framing closely. It reaches for *European* cardiac usage to justify studying the *Chinese* species — then studies the Chinese species' *fruit*. **The European cardiac reputation is doing argumentative work that the Chinese tradition never asked it to do.** The result is a literature in which *C. pinnatifida*'s classical digestive doctrine is quietly subordinated to a cardiovascular research programme it did not originate. The lipid-lowering work is real (below). But "Chinese hawthorn is a heart herb with 2,000 years of use" is a **composite claim assembled from two traditions** and is not true of either one on its own. This codex does not make it.

#### **Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

*Shan Zha* is classed **sour** (酸) and slightly warm. The sourness is not incidental — in the five-flavour system sour is astringing and Liver-entering, but Shan Zha's sourness is doing something more concrete than doctrine required it to. The fruit is loaded with **organic acids**, listed among its constituent classes alongside flavonoids, phenylpropanoids, terpenoids, saccharides and essential oils (Zhang et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jpp/rgac050)). A sour, acid-rich fruit taken **after a heavy meat meal** is, in plain terms, an acid load delivered to a stomach that is working on protein — and gastric protein digestion is acid-dependent. The tradition tasted sourness, filed it under a flavour category, and deployed it precisely where an acid would plausibly help.

The doctrinal verb is **消 (*xiāo*) — to disperse, dissolve, melt away** — and **化積 (*huà jī*) — to transform accumulation**. Applied to meat and grease, this is a claim about *dissolving fat*. And the chemistry does something adjacent: Shan Zha fruit extract raised hepatic **PPARα** expression and PPARα-regulated β-oxidation enzymes in high-fat-fed mice, lowering blood lipids — with the paper noting the effect appeared **in liver before adipose tissue** (Niu et al., 2011, *Horm Metab Res*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0031-1283147)). PPARα upregulation driving fatty-acid β-oxidation is, mechanistically, *burning fat*. A doctrine that says "this melts greasy accumulation" and a mechanism that says "this upregulates hepatic fatty-acid oxidation" are not the same statement — but they are pointed at the same phenomenon, and the tradition got there by watching what happened after dinner.

**Where the doctrine must be deflated.** Three places.

*"Blood stasis" and "postpartum blood stagnation."* These indications sit in the classical list (Li et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2022.115819)) and have **no supporting evidence in this retrieval**. The antithrombotic activity claimed in review is not the same thing and does not validate them.

*The 2,000 years.* Addressed above. It is 1,350, and the round number is doing rhetorical work.

*"Medicine-food homology means it's safe."* This inference appears throughout the literature — the anxiolytic paper notes Shan Zha "is sold as a nutritional supplement; thus, its transition to clinical trials can be easier" (Nitzan et al., 2022, *Transl Psychiatry*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-022-01970-6)). Food status is a regulatory and cultural fact, not a pharmacological one. A sour fruit eaten as candy has a real contraindication profile (below).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Shan Zha's place in Kenetica is narrow, honest, and useful: **the after-meal corrective.** A practitioner eating heavily — high protein, high fat, the diet that accompanies serious physical training — has a specific problem the classical indication addresses directly. Taken with or after the meal, as the sour disc or the decoction, it is the tradition's answer to exactly that load.

Expect nothing else. There is no state-shift, no perceptual effect, no acute experience. The lipid work is chronic and preclinical. The mood work (below) is a mouse result. **Do not deploy this as a cardiac agent** — that is the European tree's doctrine and it does not transfer through the Latin binomial.

**As a cultivator's tree, it is one of the more forgiving in this volume.** *C. pinnatifida* is a hardy deciduous Rosaceae tree, distributed across "North China, Europe, and North America" (Li et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2022.115819)), cold-tolerant, and grown at commercial scale in Shandong, Hebei and Liaoning. The practical notes: **plant the variety you actually want** — *var. major* is the large-fruited cultivated form and is the one that produces the fruit worth processing; the type species fruits smaller. Propagate named cultivars by **grafting onto seedling rootstock**; seed is slow and requires long stratification, and seedlings do not come true. As a Rosaceae pome relative it shares the family's disease pressure — **fireblight and rust are the concerns**, and hawthorn is a rust alternate host, which matters if junipers or cedars are nearby. Harvest fruit in autumn at full colour; the medicinal article is the **dried** fruit, sliced and sun- or air-dried. **The fruit, not the leaf and flower** — if you harvest this tree the way European practice harvests hawthorn, you have made a different medicine belonging to a different tradition.

### Scientific Correlation

Graded honestly. Almost all of this is rodent work; the one human study is not about digestion or the heart.

**Lipid-lowering — MODERATE preclinical, and mechanistically the strongest result in the file.** Shan Zha fruit extract at 250 mg/kg orally for 7 days improved hyperlipidaemia in high-fat-diet-fed C57BL/6J mice, raising hepatic **PPARα** and PPARα-regulated β-oxidation enzyme expression, with adipose tissue unaffected — the first report that Shan Zha acts on liver before adipose (Niu et al., 2011, *Horm Metab Res*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0031-1283147)). PPARα is a real, validated lipid-lowering target (it is the fibrate target). This is a coherent mechanism for the doctrine of "dispersing greasy accumulation". **It is a 7-day mouse study.**

**Anti-atherosclerosis — MODERATE preclinical.** The sugar-free aqueous extract of *C. pinnatifida* var. *major* fruit, over 12 weeks in high-fat-diet/vitamin-D3/ovalbumin atherosclerotic rats, reduced TC, TG and LDL-C, raised HDL-C, decreased CRP, IL-1β, IL-8 and IL-18, inhibited arterial pathological change and reduced intima-media thickness, benchmarked against simvastatin; main components chlorogenic acid, procyanidin B2, (−)-epicatechin, rutin, isoquercitrin (Zhang et al., 2013, *J Ethnopharmacol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2013.04.053)). Well-conducted, well-characterised — and rats. Note this study reports AECP "increased levels of ET and TXB2 **but increased** 6-keto-PGF1α" — the retrieved abstract is internally inconsistent (an increase in endothelin and thromboxane would be an adverse direction), and **no directional claim on those three markers is made here.** [USER: verify against full text before citing this specific result.]

**Antihypertensive — WEAK-MODERATE preclinical.** Hawthorn fruit extract and hyperoside over 7 weeks significantly reduced blood pressure in spontaneously hypertensive rats and improved aortic vascular injury, with serum metabolomics implicating primary bile acid biosynthesis and altered FXR, TGR5, ET-1, NOS3, Akt1, TNF-α, Ptgs2, ACE2 and Kdr expression (Chi et al., 2024, *Food Funct*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/d3fo02641h)). The authors conclude hawthorn "has the potential to become a functional food or an alternative therapy for hypertension" — **potential, in rats.** And note: this is the cardiovascular research programme borrowing the European doctrine, applied to the Chinese species' fruit.

**Gut / colitis — MODERATE preclinical, and genuinely well-designed.** *C. pinnatifida* polysaccharide (CPP), given prophylactically, reduced DAI score, colon shortening and weight loss in DSS colitis mice, cut inflammation and oxidative stress, raised Claudin-1, ZO-1 and Occludin and mucin secretion, and enriched *Lactobacillus*. **Crucially, the effect was microbiota-dependent** — abolished by antibiotic depletion and transferable by faecal microbiota transplant — acting via microbiota-driven upregulation of ASS1 and intestinal arginine biosynthesis (Wei et al., 2025, *Phytomedicine*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phymed.2025.156794)). The FMT and depletion arms make this stronger than a typical extract study. It is also **colitis, not food stagnation** — it does not validate the classical digestive indication, it validates a different gut activity.

**Digestive / food-stagnation (the classical indication) — ASSERTED IN REVIEW, NOT DEMONSTRATED HERE.** Both reviews assert digestive promotion (Li et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2022.115819); Zhang et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jpp/rgac050)), and the lipid paper opens by calling Shan Zha "a traditional Chinese medicine used to increase digestion" (Niu et al., 2011, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0031-1283147)). **But no study testing gastric emptying, protein or fat digestion, digestive enzyme activity, or post-prandial symptoms was retrieved this run.** This is a striking gap: **the flagship 1,350-year indication of this fruit is the one thing the retrieved literature does not test.** The research has followed the European cardiac doctrine instead of the Chinese digestive one. That is the entry's central finding and it should be stated as such.

**Anxiolytic / antidepressant — WEAK, animal, and off-doctrine.** Shan Zha alone produced anxiolytic and antidepressant-like effects in stressed mice comparable to escitalopram, via 5-HT1A activation and raised hippocampal and PFC BDNF, **without** altering serotonin transporter levels and without blocking SERT in Xenopus oocytes (Nitzan et al., 2022, *Transl Psychiatry*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-022-01970-6)). Mechanistically interesting; entirely absent from the classical indication list; mouse behavioural models are weak proxies for human mood. **Do not use this fruit as an antidepressant.**

**Human clinical evidence — ESSENTIALLY ABSENT, and the one human study is about skin.** The only human study retrieved is a Korean cosmetic trial of a *Panax ginseng* + *C. pinnatifida* **mixture** on dermal fibroblasts and healthy skin, reporting improved procollagen type I, reduced MMP-1, and improved roughness measures (Hwang et al., 2016, *J Ginseng Res*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jgr.2016.01.001)). **This is a two-herb combination — the hawthorn contribution cannot be isolated — it is a topical cosmetic endpoint, and it says nothing about digestion, lipids, or the heart.** It is included here to be honest about what the human evidence base actually contains, which is: this.

**Net grade: WEAK-MODERATE.** A real PPARα mechanism, a well-designed microbiota-dependent colitis result, and a coherent doctrine — undermined by the complete absence of human trials, the complete absence of testing for its own flagship indication, and a research literature that has been captured by a doctrine belonging to a different species.

*According to PubMed, retrieved this run. Both major reviews conclude the same way: "More evidence-based scientific studies are required to verify the traditional uses" and "the possible mechanism of action [is] unclear" (Li et al., 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2022.115819)).*

### Contraindications

- **GI acidity and reflux — the primary caution.** This is a sour, organic-acid-rich fruit whose entire doctrine is built on that sourness. **Anyone with GERD, reflux, active gastritis, or peptic ulcer should avoid it**, and anyone taking it on an empty stomach should expect trouble. The classical instruction — with or after food — is also the safety instruction.
- **Dental erosion.** Sour fruit plus the sugar loading of *shan zha pian* and *bing tang hu lu* is a straightforward enamel risk, particularly given that the folk form is a children's confection.
- **Sugar load in the folk forms.** *Bing tang hu lu* is candy. *Shan zha pian* is sweetened. Anyone managing glycaemia should treat these as confectionery, not medicine.
- **Lipid-lowering and antihypertensive drug interaction — theoretical but plausible.** Shan Zha raises hepatic PPARα (Niu et al., 2011, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0031-1283147)) — the fibrate target — and lowered blood pressure in SHR rats (Chi et al., 2024, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/d3fo02641h)). Anyone on fibrates, statins, or antihypertensives should not be self-managing with medicinal-dose hawthorn extract without supervision. **Note that this caution follows from the preclinical mechanism, not from any human interaction study — none was retrieved.**
- **Pregnancy — avoid medicinal doses.** The classical indication list includes "postpartum blood stagnation" and stasis-moving actions; qi-moving, stasis-dispersing herbs are conventionally cautioned in pregnancy in Chinese practice. No safety data was retrieved. [USER: verify against a named source.]
- **Do not substitute for cardiac care.** The cardiovascular literature on this species is rodent-only in this retrieval. **This fruit is not a treatment for heart disease, and the European hawthorn's cardiac reputation does not transfer to it.**
- **Do not use leaf and flower as if they were the fruit.** Different organ, different tradition, different constituent profile.
- **Rosaceae seeds.** As with other Rosaceae, the seed is not the medicinal article; the dried sliced fruit is. [USER: verify whether *C. pinnatifida* seed carries cyanogenic glycoside content as some Rosaceae seeds do — not retrieved this run; do not assert either way.]
