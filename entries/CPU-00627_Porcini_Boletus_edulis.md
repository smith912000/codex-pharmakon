# Porcini — Cep, Penny Bun, King Bolete, Niu Gan Jun (牛肝菌)
## *Boletus edulis* Bull. | Family: Boletaceae
**Tradition Tags:** CM (Avicenna), DMM (Dioscorides), SBJ (Shennong lineage)
**Energetic Classification:** Cold and moist (Galenic 2nd degree) — an earthy, phlegmatic food-medicine
**Training Phase:** Foundation (nourishing ballast; a food-grade tonic, not an active agent)

---

### Origin & Tradition

A structural honesty note precedes everything below: *Boletus edulis* is a **fungus**, not a plant. It is filed in the Plant-Based volume by culinary and pharmacopoeial convention — pre-modern materia medica placed the larger fungi (*fungi*, *mykes*) alongside herbs because they were gathered, dried, and dosed the same way. Read every "botanical" reference here as a reference to a mushroom.

**Dioscorides (De Materia Medica):** Dioscorides treated edible fungi (*mykes*) with characteristic caution, dividing them into the wholesome and the strangling. The wholesome kinds — of which the firm-fleshed boletes were the archetype — were classed as cold, heavy, and slow to digest, eaten as food rather than prescribed as remedy. His warning that fungi growing near rusted iron, rotten cloth, or serpents' holes turn poisonous is a genuine early statement of the substrate-uptake problem now measured as heavy-metal bioaccumulation (see Scientific Correlation).

**Avicenna (Qanun fi'l-Tibb):** The Unani tradition inherited the Greek humoral reading and refined it: fungi were cold and moist in the second degree, phlegm-forming, and prone to obstruction of the stomach unless corrected with warming, drying condiments — pepper, garlic, salt, vinegar. This is sound pharmacology dressed as humoral doctrine: the aromatics both aid digestion of a dense chitinous food and reduce the risk from spoilage.

**Shennong lineage (Chinese materia medica):** *Niu gan jun* — "ox-liver mushroom," named for its dense liver-like flesh — sits within the broad Chinese tradition of fungi as *bu* (tonifying) foods that supplement *qi* and strengthen the spleen-stomach. It is a food-tonic of the everyday kind rather than a canonical drug like *ling zhi*, valued for building substance in the depleted.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Every tradition reads the porcino as *ballast* — heavy, grounding, earth-natured. That symbolism tracks its real composition: a low-glycaemic, high-fibre, protein-and-mineral-dense food whose β-glucans and chitin genuinely slow digestion and feed the gut. The "cold and moist" label is not superstition but a felt description of a food that sits heavily and cools a fevered, depleted body by nourishing rather than stimulating.

### Active Components

- **β-glucans and α-glucans** — branched fungal cell-wall polysaccharides; the principal candidate immunomodulatory and prebiotic fraction.
- **Chitin** — structural aminopolysaccharide fibre (largely indigestible; contributes to satiety and stool bulk).
- **Ergothioneine and glutathione** — sulphur-containing cytoprotective antioxidants concentrated in edible fungi.
- **Phenolic acids** — hydroxybenzoic acids, notably gallic, 2,3-dihydroxybenzoic and gentisic acid; the main radical-scavenging fraction in water extracts.
- **Selenium, potassium, phosphorus, B-vitamins, and complete plant-source protein.**
- **Caveat:** as a saprotroph/mycorrhizal fungus it also concentrates soil cadmium and other heavy metals — a component to be minimised, not celebrated (see Contraindications).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Porcini is a **Foundation-phase food**, not an intervention. In Kenetica terms it supplies dense, cooling, low-stimulation nourishment to a system being rebuilt — the kind of substance eaten to hold ground rather than to move it. Its documented antioxidant and prebiotic fractions fit the Foundation objective of restoring baseline resilience: feeding the gut, supplying cytoprotective thiols, and adding satiating fibre without spiking blood glucose. It is placed as culinary medicine — dosed as food, at food quantities, framed honestly as nutrition with a modest bioactive edge rather than as a therapeutic mushroom extract.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- Puia et al. (2025), *Life (Basel)* — water extracts of *Boletus edulis* and other temperate forest mushrooms were dominated by hydroxybenzoic acids (gallic, 2,3-dihydroxybenzoic, gentisic), which correlated with measurable DPPH (45% inhibition) and ABTS (64% inhibition) antioxidant activity and showed dose-dependent antimicrobial action against standard bacterial strains. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/life15111653)
- Sotek et al. (2025), *Molecules* — profiled structural polysaccharides (β- and α-glucans, chitin), carotenoids and phenolic acids in wild boletes and correlated antioxidant capacity with phenolic content; critically, it also documented **cadmium and nickel bioaccumulation**, warning that regular or excessive consumption of wild specimens can exceed the tolerable weekly intake for these metals. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules30153277)

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical / compositional (in-vitro antioxidant and antimicrobial assays; food-composition analysis). No clinical trials support therapeutic claims for *B. edulis* itself. Traditional use is culinary-nutritional and well attested; bioactivity is real but modest and food-level. Claims beyond "nourishing food with antioxidant compounds" are not supported.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Sweet, neutral-to-cool; tonifies *qi*, strengthens spleen-stomach, a *bu* food-tonic (*niu gan jun*).
- **Ayurveda (by analogy — fungi are not classical dravya):** *rasa* madhura (sweet); *guna* guru (heavy), snigdha (unctuous); *virya* shita (cooling); *vipaka* madhura (sweet) — kapha-increasing, vata-pacifying.
- **Galenic / Unani:** Cold and moist, 2nd degree; phlegmatic; corrected with warming aromatics (Avicenna).

### Contraindications

- **Heavy-metal accumulation:** wild boletes concentrate cadmium, lead and nickel from soil; avoid specimens from roadsides, industrial or polluted ground, and do not consume large quantities habitually (Sotek et al. 2025).
- **Never consume raw:** boletes are indigestible and gastro-irritant uncooked.
- **Misidentification is the central hazard:** foraged fungi carry lethal look-alike risk; only reliably identified specimens should be eaten. When in doubt, discard.
- **Mushroom allergy / intolerance:** possible; introduce cautiously.
- **Gout / renal impairment:** as a purine- and mineral-rich food, moderate intake.
- **Not a substitute for medical treatment.** No verified drug-specific interactions, but framing must stay culinary.
