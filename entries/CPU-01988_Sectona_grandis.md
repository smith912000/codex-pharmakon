### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta translation only, in its Appendix 3 name list.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; it is now sourced to the appendix line(s) below.

**Sushruta (SS).** The line is `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 32814, which prints "Sectona  grandis." It stands between ",  Blyxa  Octandra." (line 32812) and `See  "Kutaja."` (line 32816). A search of every text on disk for the Latin name, spelled with S or with T, found no other place that prints it. The only other mention of teak in the Sushruta text read is a simile in the Nidana (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/nidana/SS_Nidana_ch16.txt`, line 198), where the tongue "becomes rough like a teak leaf"; that names a leaf as a comparison and not a drug.

In this e-text the Sanskrit-name column and the Latin-name column of the appendix have been separated into different blocks. The extract row (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, line 32814) records the line as "Appendix 3 Latin column; not paired to a Sanskrit name (OCR column split)". Which Sanskrit name belongs to it cannot be read from the e-text. [USER: verify the Sanskrit name against the printed page.] The appendix states no use, part used, preparation or dose. The translator's note at the end of the appendices (lines 33351-33354) says he drew on *Vaidyaka-Sabdasindhu*, Wilson's *Sanskrit and English Dictionary*, *Indian Plants and Drugs* and *Kavirsiji S'iksha*, so the Latin name is the translator's gloss from those works and not a word of the Samhita itself.
