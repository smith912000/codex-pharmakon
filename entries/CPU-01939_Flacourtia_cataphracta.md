### Origin & Tradition

**Named in the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) only through the translator's Appendix No. 3, "Names of drugs with Latin synonyms".** This is a single-text entry. The appendix is printed in two columns, Sanskrit names on the left and Bhishagratna's Latin or English glosses on the right, and the e-text `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` splits the two columns into separate runs of lines. In that file the Sanskrit name stands at line 32529 as "Prsbchinamalaka" (the OCR garbles diacritics) and the Latin gloss ". . .  Flacourtia  cataphracta." at line 32583. The extraction file `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` lists the Latin line as unpaired ("OCR column split"). The pairing here is ours, made by counting entries in the two columns from points where the pairs are unambiguous: Piyala to Buchanania Latifolia, Plaksha to Ficus Infectoria, then Prachinamalaka to "Flacourtia cataphracta", Prapundarika to Nymphaea Lotus and Prapunnada to Cassia Tora, a run that reads correctly from the foot of the column, where Pundarika falls on Nelumbium speciosum, Punarnava on Boerhavia diffusa and Puga on Areca Catechu. The count held at every checked anchor, but it remains a reconstruction from OCR and must be checked against the page scan. [USER: verify] 

**The name in the main text.** The Sanskrit name is used in the Samhita, and the line was opened and read. In the Sutrasthana chapter on the properties of foods, the Fruit Group (Phala-varga) begins: "Now we shall discourse (on the specific virtues) of fruits, such as the Dadima, Amalaka, Kapittha, Vadara, Kola, Karkandhu, Sauvira, Shimvitikaphala, Matulunga, Amra, Amrataka, Karamarda, Piyala, Lakucha, Bhavya, Paravata, Vetraphala, Prachinamalaka, Tintidika, Nipa, Koshamra, Amlika, Naranga, and Jamvira, etc." (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 985 to 991). The general statement follows: "(We may describe the general properties of fruits by saying that) they are acid in taste and heavy of digestion, heat-making in their potencies, (expectorant) of (Kapham), generate Pittam and subdue the Vayu." The lines that follow, read as far as the Matulunga, describe the Dadima, Amalaka, Karkandhu, Sauvira, Shimvitika, Kapittha and Matulunga one by one. No separate description of the Prachinamalaka was found in them, so its place in the chapter is as a member of the group.

**No other text.** A search of the other source texts on disk for *Flacourtia* found nothing.
