# Udder — ضرع (*ḍarʿ*)

## *The udder of a (milk) animal, taken as food; the Canon does not name the species (confidence high for the material)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. Source file: `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`.

**Locus (CM).** *Canon* Book II, entry *ḍarʿ* (ضرع), heading at Arabic text line 19460; entry to line 19464; the next heading is *ḍifdaʿ* (frog; line 19465).

**What the text says (CM).** Temperament: 'cold and dry, because of the great nerve (sinew) in it' (الطبع: بارد يابس بسبب العصب الكبير الذي فيه; lines 19461-19462). Nourishment: the nourishment of the udder filled with milk, when it is digested, is near that of flesh; 'the best of it is that in which there is milk', and with spices (*afāwīh*), for they hasten its descent. It is 'of the animals of very good flesh, good in humour, thick and strong' (lines 19462-19464). The Canon names no species, and it assigns no medicinal use.

### Active Components

General biology: the udder is mammary tissue, mostly connective tissue, fat and protein. Not from the Canon.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The entry is a food note, not a remedy. It is not a recommended practice.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation named is cooking with spices (*afāwīh*), which hasten its descent; no recipe and no dose is given. [USER: verify]
