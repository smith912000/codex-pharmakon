# Gleditsia pod — 皂荚 *zaojia* (Shennong bencao jing)

## *The long seed pod of a spiny tree, our identification (high confidence) as Gleditsia sinensis, the Chinese honey locust or soap-pod tree, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification; Gleditsia australis is also a candidate, and the text gives no description* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Shennong bencao jing (SBJ).** The pod is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The identification is ours. The source is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`); the entry is in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 皂荚 *zaojia*, same file, line 3623).

**The Shennong text proper** (line 3623): "Flavour pungent and salty, warm. It governs wind numbing-obstruction, dead flesh, evil qi, wind in the head with tearing; it frees the nine orifices and kills *jingwu* (精物). It grows in river valleys." (味辛、咸，温。主风痹、死肌、邪气，风头、泪出，利九窍，杀精物。生川谷。) The term *jingwu* is usually understood as uncanny or haunting beings, and not as "essence-things"; the same word also occurs in the dragon-bone entry of this text. I read it that way but the paragraph does not define it. [USER: verify]

**The Mingyi bielu layer** (line 3625): "Grows at Yongzhou and in Zou county in Lu. Those shaped like a pig's tooth are good. Gather in the ninth and tenth months; dry in the shade." (生壅州及鲁邹县。如猪牙者，良。九月、十月采，阴干。) The "pig's tooth" shape is the only description of the pod in the entry and it is a later layer.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 3627).** Sun quotes the *Shuowen* ("*jia* is the fruit of a plant", 荚，草实), the *Fan Zi Jiran* ("*zaojia* comes from the Sanfu; the best price is one cash a pod", 上价一枚一钱), and the *Guangzhi* ("*jiqizi*, the 'roosting-fowl seed', is *zaojia*", 鸡栖子，皂荚也; the *Yulan*), and says that *zao* is an abbreviated form of *cao* (皂，即草省文).

**Preface.** The lower class is "the left-hand envoy, mainly treating disease to correspond to earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken for long; for one who wishes to remove cold and heat evil qi, break up accumulations and gatherings, and cure illness, take from the lower classic" (line 2935).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, the pods of *Gleditsia* species are rich in triterpenoid saponins, compounds that foam in water and irritate mucous membranes, and the seeds hold a gum-like galactomannan. These are general statements of plant chemistry, not findings of the text.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records the pod for wind numbing-obstruction, dead flesh, wind in the head with tearing, opening the nine orifices, and killing *jingwu*. The Codex does not turn these into modern indications and gives no protocol. The saponin content means that taking the pod by mouth can irritate the gut (see Contraindications). It is a lower-class drug, in the class that is "mostly toxic".

### Preparation & Dosing

The only handling is the Mingyi harvest rule: gather in the ninth and tenth months, dry in the shade (line 3625). The Shennong paragraph gives no preparation and no dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Gleditsia pods have been used as a natural soap because of their saponins; that is a general statement. Whether they act on the conditions in the text has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong).** Two flavours and one nature are printed: pungent and salty (辛、咸), warm (温). Lower class by placement. No further quality is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry is in the lower classic, which the preface (line 2935) describes as mainly for treating disease, mostly toxic and not for long use. The paragraph has no *jiu fu* ("taken for long") clause and no life-nourishing wording; every claim is therapeutic. Under the tradition's own grading the drug is for a short course against a defined complaint and then stopped, and the grading itself is the warning.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing for a cultivator here. A lower-class drug is not for long taking, the text gives no dose, and the pod's saponins make casual internal use unwise. If it matters at all, it matters as a record of how the tradition ranked it.

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity.** The lower class is "mostly toxic". Saponin-rich pods can irritate the mouth, throat and gut and can cause vomiting or diarrhoea if swallowed in quantity. This is general knowledge and the text says nothing of it. Do not take the pod on this entry's authority. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, children, medicines.** The text says nothing; no use is supported. [USER: verify]
- **Identification.** *Gleditsia sinensis* and *G. australis* are both candidates. Other trees with similar pods exist; do not use field-collected material without expert identification.
