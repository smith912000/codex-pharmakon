# Bat's blood — "Bat's Blood" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus)
## *The blood of the animal Bryan calls "Bat"; Bryan prints no Egyptian word and no binomial, so the spine row's "Chiroptera sp." is the list-maker's gloss and is not read from the text* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter VII "Organic Remedies (animals)". "Bat" stands in Bryan's list of the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1404, after "Beetle" and before "Wasp"; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 129, class animal, "no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). The parts-of-animals list (lines 1405-1415) includes "Blood" but ties it to no animal. The spine row reads "Bat's blood, Chiroptera sp." with the note "Ebers; Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus" and a marker that the identification is uncertain (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 84). Wikipedia was not used.

**Where the blood is prescribed.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "bat" and "blood". Bat's blood occurs in two prescriptions, both in Chapter XVI "Diseases of the Eyes" (heading lines 2720-2721), in Bryan's account of ingrowing eyelashes (introduced at line 2858, where he says the remedies "must have proved a very severe drain on the live-stock of the country").

- **"TO DRIVE OUT TRICHIASIS", lines 2864-2879**: "Myrrh, Lizard's Blood, Bat's Blood. Tear out the Hairs and put thereon in order to make him well." Each ingredient is followed by a single stroke "I" in the text on disk (lines 2868, 2872, 2876); it may be a proportion mark, and the text read does not explain it. [USER: verify]
- **"ANOTHER", lines 2897-2911**: "Bat's Blood", "Rim-of-a-new-hennu-vessel", "Honey" (each followed by a stroke "I"), with the direction "Powder and place where the Hair has been pulled out." It follows the prescription "to prevent the hair growing into the eye after it has been pulled out" (heading line 2882), whose own ingredients are a mixture of the bloods of cow, ass, pig, dog and stag with incense and collyrium. The word "ANOTHER" ties this remedy to that purpose; the text read does not state the purpose again. Bryan's summary elsewhere prints "Chips-of-a-new-hennu-pot" (line 2734); what the vessel rim contributes is not explained.

**Related items, printed separately, not counted as the blood.** In Chapter XV "Diseases of the Skin" (heading line 2606), Bryan writes at line 2608: "If the Itch was confined to the Neck, a Chopped-up Bat applied to it as a poultice healed it 'at once.'" This is the whole animal, not its blood. Bryan's summary at that point gives no further detail.

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say which bat is meant, how the blood was obtained, in what amount it was used, or why it was chosen. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. The Ghalioungui 1987 glossary was not opened and has no bat row in `_sources/ebers.csv`.

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it, because the animal is not identified. In general terms, mammalian blood contains water, proteins and cells, and it clots and dries quickly. Nothing in the sources retrieved says that bat blood contains anything that acts on hair follicles. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show bat's blood in two lid preparations for ingrowing eyelashes, once with myrrh and lizard's blood, and once powdered with honey and a vessel rim. Bats are protected over much of their range, and bat blood is a recognised hazard for rabies and other infections. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only what Bryan reports: the hairs are torn out and the first mixture is "put thereon" (line 2878); the second is "powdered" and placed where the hair was pulled out (line 2911). No quantity is printed other than the unexplained strokes. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, here ingrowing eyelashes, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); no recitation is printed with either bat's-blood prescription in Bryan's summary, which is no proof the papyrus gives none. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed.

### Contraindications

- **Infection:** bats can carry rabies and other zoonotic viruses; handling bats or their blood is a hazard.
- **Eye safety:** animal blood near the eye risks infection; ingrowing lashes need medical care.
- **Conservation and law:** many bat species are protected and disturbing them is regulated in many countries; the species is not identified in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
