# Thymus vulgaris — Erpullos, Erpullos zogis (Dioscorides 3-46); Thumoxalme (5-24)

## *Thymus vulgaris* (the Dioscorides translators' suggested identification, citing Linnaeus; garden thyme, potherb thyme) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** This plant is named only by Dioscorides (DMM). The binomial is the suggestion printed on the SUGGESTED lines of the translation by Osbaldeston and Wood (2000), not Dioscorides' own: 3-46 reads "Thymus, Serpyllum romanum [Fuchs], Thymus vulgaris [Linnaeus] — Garden Thyme, Potherb Thyme"; 5-24 reads "Thymoxalme — Thyme Vinegar Thymus vulgaris — Common Thyme". **Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).**

*3-46 ERPULLOS, ERPULLOS ZOGIS* (heading at line 6921). "Herpyllum is the garden kind, similar to sampsuchum [3-47] in smell, and used for making wreaths for the head. It is so-called from its creeping, and because if any part of it touches the earth, there it roots. It has leaves and small branches similar to origanum, yet whiter. Trailed down from unmortared walls it becomes more abundant. The other is wild and is called zygis — not creeping but upright, sending out thin branches full of sprigs, surrounded with leaves similar to rue, yet they are narrow, longer, and harder. The flower is sharp to the taste, sweet to the smell, the root useless. It grows on rocks, being stronger and hotter than the garden kind and more suitable for medicinal use." Uses: "Taken in a drink it expels the menstrual flow and causes an urge to urinate. It helps griping, hernia, convulsions, inflammation of the liver and snakebites taken as a drink and applied. Boiled with vinegar (with rosaceum [1-53] mixed in there) and the head moistened with it, soothes headaches. It is especially good for lethargy and frenzy. Four teaspoonfuls of the juice (taken as a drink with vinegar) stop the vomiting of blood." Other names: *zygis sylvestris*, *polion*, the Egyptians *meruopyos*, the Romans *serpyllum*, others *cicer erraticum*.

*5-24 THUMOXALME* (heading at line 9889; the thyme vinegar). "The ancients used thymoxalme for those with a weak stomach (mixing three or four cups with warm water) as well as for arthritis and those inflated with wind. It extracts thick black fluids, and it is made as follows. Take an acetabulum [vinegar cruet] of thyme pounded into small pieces, an equal amount of salt, and a little each of polenta, rue [3-52, 3-53, 4-98] and pulegium [3-36]; throw them into a jar, pour on them three pints of water and a quarter pint of vinegar, wrap it in a linen cloth, and set it in the open air."

### Active Components

Thyme (genus *Thymus*, Lamiaceae) is generally known to contain an essential oil rich in phenolic monoterpenes such as thymol and carvacrol. The composition of the plants Dioscorides describes was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides records garden and wild thyme as drinks and applications for menstrual, urinary, liver, convulsive and head complaints, and a thyme vinegar for a weak stomach; these are his claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol here.

### Preparation & Dosing

*3-46:* drunk; boiled with vinegar and rosaceum on the head; "Four teaspoonfuls of the juice (taken as a drink with vinegar)" for blood-vomiting. *5-24:* the thyme vinegar is prepared as above, and "three or four cups" are mixed with warm water. Those are the quantities the text prints. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. 3-46 gives a discipline of attention: the garden kind roots where it touches the earth and is grown trailing from walls and used for head wreaths, the wild kind is upright, rock-growing, hotter, and better for medicine. That is horticultural and sensory knowledge, not a cultivation status. The binomial is a translators' gloss. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the source read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. As a kitchen herb it needs no cultivation framing.

### Contraindications

The medicinal uses recorded include menstrual-expelling uses; do not use medicinally in pregnancy. Concentrated thyme essential oil is an irritant and should not be taken internally. The traditional uses are not advice.
