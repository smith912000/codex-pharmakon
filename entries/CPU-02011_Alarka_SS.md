# Alarka — Alarka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation, 1907)

## *Alarka, a plant listed beside Arka in the Sushruta Samhita. Identification: Calotropis gigantea (R. Br.), the giant milkweed, by a positional pairing in the botanical appendix of Bhishagratna's Vol. III; confidence low, because the same appendix gives the same binomial for Arka, and the group-list row gives no identification. The same word also names a rabid dog in Kalpasthana ch. 6* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** If the identification holds, the plant belongs to a genus whose white latex is caustic and poisonous. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take this plant.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk. This is a single-text entry.** Spellings in quotations are normalised silently from the OCR where the damage is plain (for example Nandi for the printed Xandi); the line numbers cited give the text as printed. The spelling "Alarka" was searched in the Bhishagratna translation (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, `Vol2`, `Vol3`) and in the chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`. The Dioscorides, Ebers and Shennong texts were not searched for it, because no other name is known for it in the sources read.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The group-list locus is Sutrasthana ch. 38, the Arkadi group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15444; chapter file `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`). The text reads: "The drugs known as the Arka, Alarka, the two kinds of Karanja, Nagadanti, Mayuraka, Bhargi, Rasna, Indrapushpi, Kshudrashveta Mahashveta, Vrishchikali, Alavana and Tapasha-Vriksha, enter into the composition of the group known as the Arkadi Gana." The metrical text adds: "The group known as the Arkadi destroys Kaphani, fat, and the effects of poison. It acts as a vermifuge and a specific aseptic agent in the case of an ulcer and proves curative in diseases of the skin."

The name occurs in four more places. (1) Sutra ch. 39, the list of errhines (drugs for the nose): "Pippali, Vidanga, Apamarga, Shigru ... Karanja, Arka, Alarka, Lashuna ..." (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` line 52; Vol. I line 15971). The same passage says the roots are the part used for the drugs "commencing with Karavira and ending with Arka", so Alarka, which follows Arka, falls outside that stated root range and no part is named for it in the sources read. [USER: verify] (2) Sutra ch. 39, the Vata-soothing list, "Ashmabhedaka, Alarka, Arka, Shatavari ..." (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` line 90; Vol. I line 16009). (3) Sutra ch. 16, the ear-lobe chapter: an unguent of milk, fat and marrow of animals and birds with clarified butter and white mustard oil, boiled with "the decoction or Kvatha of Arka, Alarka, Vali, Ativali, Ananta, Apamarga, Ashvagandha ..." (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch16.txt` line 314; Vol. I line 8902). (4) Chikitsa ch. 9, the chapter on cutaneous affections (Kushtha), where the line occurs twice: "A decoction of Nimha (as printed in the OCR) or that of Arka, Alarka and Saptachchhada should be given him if there be any worms in the diseased locality" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch09.txt` lines 526 and 611; Vol. II line 16169). Chikitsa ch. 6 also lists "Chitraka, Alarka and Snuhi-milk" among the ingredients of an oil cooked and "used as an unguent", which "leads to the falling off of the polypus" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch06.txt` line 263; Vol. II line 14699).

**The dog.** In Kalpasthana ch. 6 (`kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch06.txt` lines 314 and 359; Vol. II lines 31424 and 31469) "Alarka" is printed as the name of a rabid dog: "the poison of a rabid dog (Alarka)", and a mantra to "the Yaksha, lord of Alarka, who art also the lord of all dogs". These two lines name the animal and are not evidence for the plant. They are recorded here because the word carries both senses in the translation.

**Identification.** The row's group-list note gives none. Bhishagratna's Vol. III Appendix 3 is a two-column list of Sanskrit names and Latin names, printed in order (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` lines 31167 for the name, 31219 for the Latin). Read by position, "Alarka" is paired with "Calotropis gigantia" (OCR; the standard spelling is Calotropis gigantea), and its neighbours pair consistently (Akshota with Juglans regia, Alavu with Lagenaria vulgaris). The same appendix pairs "Arka" with the same binomial (line 31273 onward), so it does not show how Sushruta tells the two apart. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent for this drug. In general terms, plants of the genus Calotropis (milkweed family) produce a white latex that contains cardiac glycosides of the cardenolide type and is a skin and eye irritant. Whether this applies to the drug as Sushruta used it was not checked. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Probably toxic.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Arkadi group: destroying Kapha and fat, countering the effects of poison, killing worms, cleansing ulcers and healing skin disease (Sutra ch. 38). As a nasal drug (Sutra ch. 39), a Vata-soothing drug, and an ingredient in an ear-lobe unguent (Sutra ch. 16), a worm decoction (Chikitsa ch. 9, skin disease) and a polypus oil (Chikitsa ch. 6).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used for this drug, no duration.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives preparations in which the name occurs: a decoction (Kvatha) with other drugs for an ear-lobe unguent (Sutra ch. 16); a decoction alone for worms in a wound (Chikitsa ch. 9); an oil cooked with it as an unguent (Chikitsa ch. 6). Chapter 38 closes with a rule for all the groups: "Only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group, or a similar number of drugs chosen from the different groups, or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety, or in combination with another, should be used according to the indications of any particular case, as the physician, in his discretion, would determine" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, near the end; Vol. I, ch. XXXVIII). It also says that the groups "will be more elaborately dealt with later on in the chapter on Therapeutics". This is a rule of method for the groups, not a dose for this drug. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Calotropis latex is irritant and cardiotoxic in general terms, and the genus has a long record of folk and veterinary use in South Asia. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Alarka in the passages read. The only qualities given are those of the whole Arkadi group: it "destroys Kaphani, fat, and the effects of poison", acts as a vermifuge, an aseptic agent for ulcers and is curative in skin diseases (Sutra ch. 38). The Vata-soothing list in Sutra ch. 39 places it among drugs said to restore deranged Vata to its normal state.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is attested in the Sushruta Samhita, in the therapeutic groups of Sutrasthana ch. 38 and the other passages cited above, and its role there is therapeutic: groups are said to subdue Kapha, Vata or Pitta, to act as vermifuge or aseptic agents, and so on. Chikitsa ch. 26 (the vajikarana chapter, "increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons") and chs. 27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) were searched under the spellings printed here (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` to `ch30.txt`, and ch. 31) and the name was not found in them. The drug therefore carries no rasayana or vajikarana status in the sources retrieved, and nothing is said of ojas for it. The register is (b): framed by the therapeutic gana, with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A group-listed, probably toxic drug used in unguents, errhines and decoctions for worms, ulcers and skin disease. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use. A cultivator should not use it.

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC, probably.** If the identification as Calotropis is right, the latex is caustic and poisonous. Do not ingest it, and keep it from the eyes and skin.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Rabid-dog bite.** A medical emergency; the word Alarka in Kalpa ch. 6 names the dog, and the chapter is not a recommendation of this plant.
- **Identity.** Positional OCR pairing only; the same binomial is given for Arka. [USER: verify]
