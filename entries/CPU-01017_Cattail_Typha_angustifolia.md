### Origin & Tradition

The narrow-leaved cattail is a cosmopolitan marsh plant of the Old World wetlands, and almost every literate riverine culture that met it turned its parts to medicine. The medicinally decisive organ is not the root or the leaf but the **golden male pollen** — a fine, water-repellent, sulphur-yellow dust shaken from the upper spike in early summer. In the Chinese materia medica this pollen is *Pu Huang* (蒲黄), and it carries the single most striking doctrinal signature in the wetland pharmacopoeia: **the same drug performs two opposite actions depending on how it is prepared.**

**Shennong / Chinese tradition (SBJ, CM).** *Pu Huang* is listed in the early canonical stratum of Chinese drug literature and is standard in the modern *Chinese Pharmacopoeia*. Its classical remit is the blood: it is said to **invigorate blood and dispel stasis** (*huo xue qu yu*) when used **raw** (*sheng*), and to **staunch bleeding** (*zhi xue*) when used **charred/carbonised** (*Pu Huang tan*, 蒲黄炭). The named indications are almost all blood-and-vessel complaints — *metrorrhagia*, *haematuria*, *haematemesis*, traumatic bruising, *dysmenorrhoea*, post-partum abdominal pain, and painful urinary dribbling (*stranguria*). The paired formula *Shixiao San* ("Sudden Smile Powder"), Pu Huang with *Wu Ling Zhi*, is the emblematic stasis-and-pain prescription. The doctrine is therefore explicitly **bidirectional and preparation-dependent**: raw pollen moves stuck blood; fire-charred pollen seals a leaking vessel.

**Dioscorides / Graeco-Roman tradition (DMM).** The Greek herbal tradition knew *Typha* (the reed-mace) chiefly for its downy spike and its cooling, wound-drying application — the crushed parts and the down applied to burns and weeping sores, exploiting the astringent, moisture-absorbing quality. This is the same "dry the wet lesion" logic that underlies the Chinese haemostatic reading, arrived at from the outside of the body rather than the inside.

**Avicenna / Unani tradition (CM).** The Persianate physicians placed the marsh reeds in the cooling-drying band and used the down and pollen as a desiccant and mild astringent for oozing lesions and haemorrhage — consistent with a *cold, dry* humoral placement that opposes hot, wet, flowing blood.

**Assyrian / Mesopotamian tradition (AT).** Cattail was an omnipresent marsh economic plant of southern Mesopotamia (matting, thatch, food from the rhizome), and the cuneiform medical corpus includes marsh-reed materials among wound and bleeding preparations. The precise Akkadian binomial and the exact indication should be treated as **[USER: verify]** rather than asserted; the safe, general claim is that the plant was in the Mesopotamian marsh pharmacopoeia and associated with poultice/haemostatic use.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** The cattail's medicine is a doctrine of **fire acting on a blood-moving dust**, and the chemistry maps onto it with unusual precision. The raw pollen is loaded with intact **flavonoid glycosides** — typhaneoside, isorhamnetin-3-*O*-neohesperidoside, and related quercetin/isorhamnetin glycosides — polar, water-mobilising molecules that are the traditional "blood-mover." When the herbalist chars the pollen (*tan*), fire does two chemical things at once: it **hydrolyses and degrades the flavonoid glycosides** (the *moving* fraction is destroyed) and it **concentrates carbonised, tannin-like astringent residue** (the *sealing* fraction is enriched). The Chinese doctrine — *raw to move, charred to stop* — is thus not mysticism but a **remembered pyrolysis experiment**: the same starting powder yields a blood-invigorator or a haemostatic depending on whether its glycosides survive the fire. The symbolic pairing of the yellow (solar, dispersing) raw pollen against the black (chthonic, fixing) char is a colour-coded memory of a real phase change in the drug. The stranguria/dysmenorrhoea indications — pain from stuck blood and stuck fluid in the lower burner — sit exactly where a mildly analgesic, smooth-muscle-relevant flavonoid fraction would be expected to act.

### Active Components

- **Flavonoid glycosides (raw-fraction signature):** typhaneoside (isorhamnetin-3-*O*-(2G-α-L-rhamnosyl)-rutinoside), isorhamnetin-3-*O*-neohesperidoside, isorhamnetin-3-*O*-rutinoside, and quercetin/kaempferol glycosides. These are the dominant polar constituents of the raw pollen.
- **Aglycones released on processing/charring:** isorhamnetin, quercetin.
- **Tannins and carbonised astringent residue:** enriched in *Pu Huang tan* after charring.
- **Sterols, fatty acids, polysaccharides, and trace volatile constituents** of the pollen matrix.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Filed in Phase III (Sealing & Circulation) precisely because it is the training-manual example of **preparation-as-pharmacology**. The Kenetica use-case is dual and must be declared at the point of preparation:

- **Raw pollen** — a gentle circulatory/anti-stasis and anti-nociceptive agent, aligned with the classical dysmenorrhoea, bruise, and stranguria indications.
- **Charred pollen (Pu Huang tan)** — an astringent haemostatic for surface and mucosal bleeding, aligned with metrorrhagia/haematuria use.

Never treat the two preparations as interchangeable; the whole point of the entry is that fire inverts the action.

### Scientific Correlation

The traditional "fire inverts the action" doctrine has a direct laboratory correlate. According to PubMed, a spectrum–activity study comparing *Typha angustifolia* pollen **before and after carbonisation** found that charring **significantly reduced the flavonoid-glycoside content**, and that the change in **thrombin activity** tracked chiefly with **quercetin and isorhamnetin**, concluding that the pollen flavonoids are the main factors in its coagulation-relevant activity (Chen et al., *Zhong Yao Cai*, 2012; PMID 23320351). This is close to a direct vindication of the raw-versus-charred doctrine: processing measurably shifts both the chemistry and the clotting-relevant behaviour.

The **anti-stasis / analgesic** (raw) direction is supported by effect-directed fractionation of Typhae Pollen, which identified **isorhamnetin-3-*O*-neohesperidin and typhaneoside** as the principal **anti-nociceptive** constituents — chemistry consistent with the classical dysmenorrhoea and injury-pain indications (Zeng et al., *Nat Prod Res*, 2018; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2018.1539979)). On the anti-inflammatory axis, the pollen total-flavone fraction and **typhaneoside** inhibit **NLRP3 inflammasome activation** in macrophages via AMPK-mediated lipid metabolism (Ren et al., *BMC Complement Med Ther*, 2025; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-025-05024-4)). Typhaneoside also modulates **presynaptic glutamate release** by suppressing voltage-dependent Ca²⁺ entry in cortical nerve terminals — a genuine but off-target neuropharmacology finding, not a classical indication (Chiu et al., *Chem Res Toxicol*, 2021; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.0c00446)). Typhaneoside further shows anti-leukaemic ferroptosis-inducing activity in vitro (Zhu et al., *Biochem Biophys Res Commun*, 2019; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2019.06.070)).

**Honest evidence grade — LOW to MODERATE.** The *mechanistic plausibility* is real and the raw-vs-charred chemistry is genuinely demonstrated in vitro. But essentially all of the above is **preclinical** (rodent, macrophage, synaptosome, tumour-cell). There is no robust randomised clinical trial establishing *Pu Huang* as a haemostatic or blood-mover in humans at defined doses. The doctrine should be graded as **chemically vindicated in direction, clinically unproven in magnitude.** Claims of a specific human haemostatic effect size would be fabrication.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian (EP).** The Ebers corpus is indication-organised — it records what was done for a bleeding wound or a weeping sore, not what a substance does to a person's *ka*, *ib* or vital constitution, and where it does act it binds *heka* (the operative recited power) to the drug as one intervention. For the narrow-leaved cattail this entry carries no Egyptian prescription at all: the plant's medicine is documented here from the Chinese, Greek, Persianate and Mesopotamian sides, and no Ebers passage, deity association or *heka* recitation for *Typha* pollen is attested in the retrieved sources — substance identity in that corpus being in any case a modern hypothesis. **(c) absent** — and the Egyptian tag is not borne out by any attestation in this entry.

**Assyrian (AT).** Cattail was an omnipresent marsh plant of southern Mesopotamia — matting, thatch, rhizome food — and marsh-reed materials sit among the wound and haemostatic preparations of the cuneiform corpus worked by the *asû*. But under the standing blocking warning that governs every Assyrian substance identification, Akkadian binomial and tablet reference, no secure species-level reading or cultivation status is claimed; illness here is classed by causal agency (divine hand, ghost, oath), and nothing in the retrieved sources assigns *Typha* a role in that scheme. **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The classical herbal knew the reed-mace chiefly for its down and its cooling, moisture-drying application — the crushed spike and down laid on burns and weeping sores, the astringent "dry the wet lesion" logic. Dioscorides carries no subtle-body system into which a cultivation status could be set; what he offers a cultivator instead is a discipline of attention — recognition of the true down, the season of the ripe spike, the sensory assay of the material. That is a genuine practice of provenance, not a vital-body doctrine. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The Persianate physicians placed the marsh reeds in the cooling-and-drying band and used the down and pollen as a desiccant and mild astringent against oozing lesions and haemorrhage — a cold, dry humoral placement opposing hot, wet, flowing blood. The tradition's strongest cultivation idea — the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture as a finite vital endowment, so that longevity practice is conservation and not acquisition — is a frame of the whole regimen, not a status the corpus assigns to this cooling desiccant, and no *ruh*-strengthening or gladdening (cardiac) role is claimed for it. **(b) framed:** used within the humoral frame, no cultivation status sourced.

**Chinese — Shennong (SBJ).** Here the tradition does assign a cultivation status. *Pu Huang* (蒲黄), the golden pollen, is entered in the *Shennong Bencaojing* (神農本草經) — the earliest *bencao* stratum — in the **upper grade (上品)**, the tier the classic reserves for non-toxic drugs taken long-term to nourish life (養命); the whole cattail (香蒲) likewise stands among the upper-grade plants. The received entry gives the drug as sweet and balanced (味甘平), governing cold-and-heat of the heart, abdomen and bladder, freeing the urine, staunching bleeding and dispersing stasis-blood. The fuller life-nourishing formula sometimes attached to it — 久服輕身益氣力延年, "taken long, it lightens the body (輕身), augments the qi and strength, and extends the years" — is treated by careful commentary (*Bencao jing shu*, 本草經疏) as a later gloss rather than securely original, so the cultivation weight rests on the grade itself, not on that sentence; *qing shen* 輕身 is left untranslated into any pharmacological effect, as it must be. Note too that the classical drug is the pollen of several *Typha* species (including *T. angustifolia*), so the grade attaches to *Pu Huang* the drug, not to the modern binomial. **(a) attested** — upper-grade (上品) in the *Shennong Bencaojing*, with the longevity wording flagged as a contested later addition.

**Practitioner's note.** Cattail is the rare wetland drug that carries an upper-grade standing in the Chinese scheme, yet its actual working is emphatically therapeutic and preparation-split: raw pollen to move stuck blood and ease stasis-pain, charred pollen (*Pu Huang tan*, 蒲黄炭) to seal a leaking vessel — the same dust inverted by fire. The "nourish life" honour is asserted by the grade, not by the blood-and-vessel indications, and the pregnancy caution on the raw blood-mover argues against casual daily use. Everywhere else — Greek, Persianate, Mesopotamian — it is a topical desiccant for burns and weeping sores, taken up for the defined lesion and then set down. The honest move is to respect the raw/charred distinction at the point of preparation and to treat the upper-grade label as classical honour, not a licence for indiscriminate long dosing.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy — caution/avoid.** The blood-invigorating (raw) action is traditionally contraindicated in pregnancy on uterine grounds; classical texts warn against blood-movers in the gravid state. Treat as a pregnancy-caution herb.
- Not for bleeding that is due to active heat requiring different management without practitioner guidance.
- Pollen is a recognised **aeroallergen**; sensitised individuals may react to the raw dust.
- Distinguish the two preparations at the point of use — administering the wrong one (mover vs sealer) inverts the intended effect.
