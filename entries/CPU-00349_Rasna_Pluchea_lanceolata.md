### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** *Rāsnā* is the great **anti-Vāta, anti-rheumatic** drug of the classical corpus — the herb reached for first in disorders of *Vāta*, and above all in *āmavāta* (the classical picture that maps closely onto rheumatoid and inflammatory arthritis) and *sandhivāta* (osteoarthritic joint pain). Suśruta and the later nighaṇṭus place it at the head of formulations for pain, swelling, stiffness and restricted movement; the celebrated compound *Rāsnā-saptaka*, *Rāsnā-eraṇḍādi* and *Rāsnādi kvātha* all take their name and their organising principle from it. Its reputation is specifically for the **cold, dry, obstructive, painful** joint conditions of aggravated Vāta, and for the sticky, immobilising quality of *āma* (undigested metabolic residue) lodged in the joints — hence *āmavātaghna*, "destroyer of āmavāta."

> ⚠️ **Source-plant controversy flag — "Rāsnā":** *Rāsnā* is one of the most notoriously **unstandardised drug names in Ayurveda**. The classical texts do not fix a single species, and across regions and centuries the name has been assigned to at least three quite different plants: *Pluchea lanceolata* (Asteraceae) — the accepted north-Indian market source and the one indexed here; *Alpinia galanga* (Zingiberaceae) — favoured in the south and in some classical readings; and *Vanda* / *Vanda tessellata* and other orchids in yet other traditions. This is a genuine, documented substitution problem: a formulation calling for "Rāsnā" may contain any of these depending on region and supplier. The Kenetica entry treats *Pluchea lanceolata* as the referenced botanical while explicitly flagging that historical and commercial "Rāsnā" is not a single entity. **Verify the botanical source of any product.**

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Herb of Loosened Joints and the Problem of the Named Drug:**
Rāsnā sits at a doctrinally rich intersection in Kenetica: it is at once the archetypal *Vāta-jña* (Vāta-knowing) remedy and the standing example of how a *name* in a living tradition can drift free of a *thing*. On the first count, its symbolism is the loosening of what has seized: Vāta disorders are, in the Ayurvedic imagination, disorders of the wind element — cold, mobile, erratic, drying, and paradoxically productive of stiffness when it lodges in a joint and dries the lubricating *śleṣaka kapha*. Rāsnā's heating, penetrating, Vāta-pacifying action is the warm hand laid on the cold, cramped joint — the restoration of motion (its very name is glossed by some as "that which enables movement," *rasana*). On the second count, Rāsnā is the Codex's cautionary emblem of the **gap between the word and the plant**. A tradition that transmits knowledge in Sanskrit verse over two millennia and across a subcontinent will inevitably see a single revered name pinned to whatever effective local plant fills its therapeutic slot. Rāsnā teaches the practitioner a discipline that the anti-fabrication principle of this Codex shares: *always chase the name back to the verified species*, because the reputation attaches to the word but the pharmacology attaches to the plant.

### Active Components

The chemistry of *Pluchea lanceolata* is dominated by **pentacyclic triterpenoids and sterols**, together with flavonoids and lactones. According to PubMed, the principal bioactive triterpene is **taraxasterol** (and its acetate derivative, taraxasterol acetate), alongside sterols, flavonoids and terpenoid lactones (Srivastava & Shanker, 2012, *Fitoterapia*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fitote.2012.07.008). These constituents are credited with modulating multiple inflammatory targets — the plant's phytochemistry is a good structural match for its reputation as an anti-inflammatory *Rasāyana*-class herb.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica, Rāsnā is filed as the **primary anti-Vāta / anti-rheumatic support agent** — the herb of the joint-and-channel phase. Its use-case is the cold-dry-painful-stiff joint syndrome: inflammatory and degenerative arthropathies, *āmavāta*, sciatica-type Vāta pain, and the accompanying loss of range and mobility. Its heating, Vāta-Kapha-reducing energetics fit conditions where cold and obstruction dominate; it is not a herb for hot, Pitta-inflamed acute flares used alone (there it is balanced within compounds). Emerging pharmacology (below) supports an anti-neuroinflammatory and cytokine-modulating action, extending its classical joint use toward a broader "inflammation-quieting" role. In the training-phase language of the Codex it belongs to the **restorative / support arc** rather than the drastic-clearing arc — a herb of easing and mobilising, taken over courses of weeks within a formulation.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- **Anti-neuroinflammatory action of the triterpene:** In LPS-stimulated C6 rat glial cells, the *Pluchea* triterpene **taraxasterol** significantly attenuated release of the pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α, IFN-γ and IL-6, with molecular-docking support for binding to TNF-α and NF-κB targets; the acetate derivative was less active (Srivastava et al., 2013, *Naunyn-Schmiedeberg's Arch. Pharmacol.*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00210-013-0924-6).
- **Phytochemical/pharmacological review:** A dedicated review characterises *Pluchea lanceolata* ("Rasana") as a *Rasāyana* herb valued for anti-inflammatory disease, its triterpenoids, sterols, flavonoids and lactones acting on several therapeutic targets (Srivastava & Shanker, 2012, *Fitoterapia*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fitote.2012.07.008).

**Evidence grade: MODERATE-WEAK.** There is a coherent mechanistic story (triterpene cytokine modulation) and a good phytochemical basis for the classical anti-inflammatory use, but the human clinical trial base specific to *P. lanceolata* is thin. Claims here are limited to what the in-vitro and review literature actually show; the traditional anti-arthritic reputation is supported mechanistically but not yet by robust controlled human trials of this species alone.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Tikta (bitter), Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Guru (heavy), Rūkṣa (dry) — some sources note Sara (mobilising)
- **Doṣa action:** Vāta-Kapha-hara (reduces Vāta and Kapha); Pitta-neutral to mildly increasing in excess
- **Karma (actions):** Śothahara (anti-inflammatory), Śūlaghna (analgesic/antispasmodic), Āmavātaghna (anti-rheumatic), Vātānulomana (regulates downward Vāta flow), Dīpana (mild digestive)
- **Primary indications (roga):** Āmavāta (rheumatoid-type arthritis), Sandhivāta (osteoarthritis), Vātavyādhi (Vāta disorders generally), Śotha (inflammatory swelling)

### Contraindications

- **Identity first:** confirm the product is genuinely *Pluchea lanceolata* and not an unspecified "Rāsnā" (*Alpinia galanga*, *Vanda*, etc.) — the substitution changes the pharmacology.
- Heating potency — use with care in high-Pitta states, hyperacidity, and acute hot inflammatory flares unless balanced in a cooling formulation.
- Pregnancy, lactation, paediatric use: `[USER: verify]` — insufficient safety data; defer to practitioner.
- No robust human trial safety profile for this species; avoid high-dose or prolonged self-administration.
