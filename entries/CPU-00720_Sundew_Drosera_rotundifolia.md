# Round-leaved Sundew — Rosolis — Herba Droserae

## *Drosera rotundifolia* L. | Family: Droseraceae

**Tradition Tags:** DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Cool and moist by habitat, warm and dispersing by action — a plant of the wettest ground used against the wettest complaint. Acrid; the fresh plant is a vesicant.

**Training Phase:** Restoration. A repair-phase remedy for the airway, not a state-altering substance. It belongs in the practitioner's convalescence kit, not the working session.

---

### Origin & Tradition

The name is a description of a fact that should not be true. *Ros solis* — "dew of the sun." On the open sphagnum bog at midday in full August sun, when every other surface has been dry for hours, the sundew's leaves are still beaded with glistening droplets. The dew that does not burn off. Each round leaf is fringed with red tentacles, each tentacle tipped with a clear viscous bead, and the whole rosette sits there sparkling in conditions where dew is physically impossible.

The droplets are of course mucilage, not water — a sticky glue that the plant secretes to catch insects, because it lives in bog peat so nitrogen-poor that it cannot make a living from the soil. It eats flies instead. Field work in a Bavarian silting-up bog confirmed across two years of measurement that captured prey is a genuine limiting factor on *D. rotundifolia*'s fitness, with benefits carried forward into the next season via the winter bud (Thum 1989, *Oecologia*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00377091); via PubMed). This is a plant that hunts to survive.

But the alchemists did not know that. They saw only the impossibility.

**Alchemical and Renaissance Europe (DMM).** *Ros solis* was read as condensed solar dew — a celestial water, caught and held by the plant against the sun's own power to dissipate it. A substance that defies the sun must contain something of the sun. Within the alchemical frame this made sundew a candidate *aqua vitae* material, and it entered the literature as an ingredient in cordials and restorative waters, credited with prolonging life and restoring vigour to the wasted. The liqueur **Rosolio / Rossoli** carries the name into the present, long after any sundew left the recipe.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.**

The doctrine here is a two-step inference and both steps are wrong. *Step one:* the dew persists in sun, therefore it is solar/celestial in nature. *Step two:* what defies solar dissipation will defy bodily dissipation — consumption, wasting, the drying-up of vitality. Hence sundew for phthisis, for the aged, for the failing.

Deflate it plainly. The dew persists because it is **hygroscopic polysaccharide mucilage with negligible vapour pressure**, secreted continuously by stalked glands. It is not water and it is not celestial. It is flypaper. The observation was acute — the plant genuinely does something anomalous, and the alchemists were right to notice — but the inference from *anomalous* to *solar* to *anti-wasting* has no purchase at any joint. Sundew does not treat consumption. Nothing in the retrieved literature suggests it treats wasting, longevity, or vitality in any sense.

And yet the plant *did* survive into the modern pharmacopoeia — for something else entirely, and by a different route. Which is the interesting part, and the reason the entry earns its place.

**European folk and clinical phytotherapy.** Set against the alchemy is a plain, durable, unmystical use: **sundew is a cough drug**. *Herba Droserae* — the dried flowering herb — is the traditional European antitussive and antispasmodic, reached for in **whooping cough (pertussis)**, spasmodic and irritable dry cough, bronchitis, and laryngitis. It is characteristically indicated not for productive congestion but for the barking, convulsive, paroxysmal cough that will not stop — the cough that has become a spasm. It persists in European herbal practice and in cough preparations to this day, and the ethnopharmacological record notes that several Northern Hemisphere *Drosera* species have been used in the therapy of respiratory tract infections as the traditional medicine **Droserae Herba** (Fukushima et al. 2009, *J Ethnopharmacol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2009.06.009); via PubMed).

This second doctrine has no cosmology behind it. It looks like straightforward accumulated empiricism: people gave it to children with whooping cough and the spasms eased. **It is the doctrine that survived, and it is the one that maps.** [USER: verify] the specific pharmacopoeial monograph status and jurisdictions before printing any claim that it is "still official" in a named pharmacopoeia — I have not confirmed a current monograph this run, only that Droserae Herba is a recognised traditional drug in the ethnopharmacological literature.

**Homeopathy.** *Drosera* is a well-known homeopathic remedy, again for spasmodic cough. Note this only as historical fact. ⚠️ One of the mechanistic studies below (Arruda-Silva et al.) tests a homeopathic 3× dilution and is authored from within that tradition; it is flagged accordingly in Scientific Correlation and should not be laundered into evidence for the herbal drug.

### Active Components

**This section requires a correction to the received story, and the correction is the most important thing in the entry.**

The standard account — repeated across the popular herbal literature, and in the brief for this entry — is that sundew's action is due to **naphthoquinones: plumbagin, droserone, hydroxydroserone, 7-methyljuglone**. These compounds are genuinely present in *Drosera*, and plumbagin has real, well-documented antimicrobial and cytotoxic activity. The doctrine-to-chemistry story writes itself: acrid vesicant naphthoquinones, antimicrobial, kills the pertussis organism.

**The retrieved experimental work says this is wrong.** Twice, independently:

- Testing an aqueous and an ethanolic extract for anti-inflammatory and spasmolytic action, Krenn et al. found both inhibited human neutrophil elastase (IC50 5 and 1 µg/mL respectively) — and stated explicitly that the **very low naphthoquinone concentrations in the extracts seem not to be responsible for the effect, as the pure compounds were not effective in the test system used**. They attributed activity instead to the flavonoids **hyperoside, quercetin and isoquercitrin**, present in considerable concentration (Krenn et al. 2004, *Arzneimittelforschung*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0031-1296991); via PubMed).
- Comparing *D. rotundifolia* and *D. madagascariensis* extracts in the HET-CAM assay, Paper et al. likewise concluded the **effect cannot be attributed to naphthoquinones, but might be due to flavonoids**, with ellagic acid implicated in the antiangiogenic component (Paper et al. 2005, *Phytother Res*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.1666); via PubMed).

So the actual actives, on current evidence:

- **Flavonoids — the load-bearing class.** **Quercetin**, **hyperoside**, **isoquercitrin**, and **2″-O-galloylhyperoside**. The last of these is not a footnote: quercetin, 2″-O-galloylhyperoside and hyperoside were the compounds to which antispasmodic effects on airway smooth muscle were specifically attributed, and quercetin plus 2″-O-galloylhyperoside were identified as the actives raising ciliary beat frequency (Hake et al. 2022, *Molecules*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27196622); via PubMed).
- **Ellagic acid** — present, notably in ethanolic extract; substantially *less* active than the flavonoids in the elastase assay, but implicated in antiangiogenic effect.
- **Naphthoquinones (plumbagin, droserone, 7-methyljuglone)** — ⚠️ present in the plant and pharmacologically real *as isolated compounds*, but **not demonstrated to carry the traditional antitussive action**, and shown inactive in the specific assay where they were directly tested. Their concentration in extracts is low. Do not build the doctrinal bridge on them.
- **Mucilage** — the flypaper. Not shown to be therapeutically active; it is the plant's hunting apparatus and the source of the myth.
- **Hydrolytic enzymes** — the digestive machinery: class I chitinase **DrChit** (Rajninec et al. 2020, *Int J Biol Macromol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2020.06.123); via PubMed) and a class V β-1,3-glucanase (Michalko et al. 2016, *Planta*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00425-016-2592-5); via PubMed). Of biological and biotech interest — DrChit expressed in tobacco suppressed *Trichoderma viride* growth (Ďurechová et al. 2019, *Mol Biotechnol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12033-019-00214-1)) — but **irrelevant to the medicinal use**. Proteins do not survive a decoction or a tincture. Mentioned here only so a reader does not confuse "carnivorous digestive enzymes" with "why it stops a cough."

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Sundew is an **airway-spasm remedy**, and the modern mechanistic work has given it an unusually specific and coherent profile — arguably a better one than the tradition knew it had.

The traditional indication is the paroxysmal, unproductive, spasmodic cough. Three retrieved mechanisms converge on precisely that presentation:

1. **It relaxes airway smooth muscle.** Ethanol-water extract and its aqueous fraction were antispasmodic against acetylcholine-induced contractions in mouse tracheal slices, and the aqueous fraction also abrogated contraction induced by 60 mM K⁺ (Hake et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27196622)). Earlier work in guinea-pig ileum found antispasmodic effect possibly via an allosteric site on the muscarinic M3 receptor (Krenn et al. 2004, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0031-1296991)).
2. **It speeds mucociliary clearance.** The extract and its aqueous fraction *increased* ciliary beat frequency in murine tracheal slices — via PDE1A and PDE4D inhibition, an effect mimicked by rolipram (Hake et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27196622)).
3. **It damps the inflammatory limb.** Elastase inhibition at low µg/mL (Krenn et al. 2004); suppression of inflammatory gene expression in activated human HMC-1 mast cells (Fukushima et al. 2009, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2009.06.009)).

Relax the spasm, clear the mucus faster, quiet the mast cell. That is a *rational antitussive*, and it is a genuinely satisfying doctrine↔chemistry map — but note what it maps to. **It maps to the plain folk cough use, not to the alchemy.** The empirical tradition, which had no theory at all, got it right. The theorised tradition, with its solar dew and its defiance of dissipation, got nothing.

Use case: convalescent or irritable airway; dry spasmodic cough post-infection; the cough that has outlived its cause. **Not** for the acute management of pertussis in a child — see Contraindications.

⚠️ **No human clinical dose is established.** All pharmacology retrieved is *ex vivo*, *in vitro*, or in cell lines. Traditional doses are traditional.

**Cultivator's note — and this is a hard stop, see Contraindications.** *D. rotundifolia* is a boreal bog specialist requiring live sphagnum, permanently saturated acidic peat, full sun, and mineral-free water; it forms an overwintering hibernaculum and its fitness depends on prey capture (Thum 1989, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00377091)). It is growable — carnivorous-plant horticulture does it routinely — but it is not *farmable at pharmacopoeial scale* in any casual sense, and this is exactly why the commercial supply problem below exists. Note also the genetics: Korean *D. rotundifolia* showed **extremely low within-population variation (He = 0.005) and very high among-population divergence (FST = 0.817)**, with the authors arguing explicitly for tailored conservation strategy (Chung et al. 2013, *Am J Bot*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3732/ajb.1200486); via PubMed). Populations are small, isolated, inbred, and not interchangeable. A collected population does not come back.

### Scientific Correlation

**Overall grade: the *use* is well-supported mechanistically and entirely unsupported clinically; the *doctrine* is refuted; the *received chemistry* is wrong.**

**Antispasmodic on airway smooth muscle — GRADE: GOOD EX VIVO, ZERO CLINICAL.** Mouse tracheal slices; effect against both acetylcholine-induced and K⁺-induced contraction; attributed to quercetin, 2″-O-galloylhyperoside and hyperoside (Hake et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27196622)). Corroborated in a second tissue and species by the guinea-pig ileum M3 allosteric finding (Krenn et al. 2004, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0031-1296991)). Two labs, two decades apart, two tissues, same direction. That is real.

**Increased ciliary beat frequency via PDE1A/PDE4D inhibition — GRADE: GOOD EX VIVO, MECHANISTICALLY SPECIFIC.** (Hake et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27196622)). Named enzymes, named compounds, positive control (rolipram) reproducing the effect. This is the highest-quality single result in the entry.

**Anti-inflammatory — GRADE: MODERATE IN VITRO.** Elastase inhibition at 1–5 µg/mL (Krenn et al. 2004); mast cell inflammatory gene suppression (Fukushima et al. 2009, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2009.06.009)); HET-CAM inhibition at 500 µg/pellet stronger than 50 µg hydrocortisone (Paper et al. 2005, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.1666)).

**Bronchial epithelial gene expression — GRADE: WEAK, AND FLAGGED.** A 3× (1000-fold) dilution altered gene expression in 16HBE cells at 3 h and 6 h, upregulating EGFR ligands, xenobiotic detoxification proteins and cytokines; authors suggest stimulation of self-repair (Arruda-Silva et al. 2021, *Sci Rep*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-81843-y); via PubMed). ⚠️ **Read this carefully.** It is a homeopathic-dilution study from a homeopathy research group, in one cell line, with no dose-response across the herbal range. It is not evidence for the herbal drug and it is not evidence for homeopathy. Reported for completeness, weighted at approximately zero.

**NULLS, REFUTATIONS AND ABSENCES:**

- **The naphthoquinone hypothesis is refuted, not merely unsupported.** Pure naphthoquinones were **not effective** where the extract was (Krenn et al. 2004, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0031-1296991)), and a second group independently concluded the effect **cannot be attributed to naphthoquinones** (Paper et al. 2005, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.1666)). Any source telling you sundew works *because of plumbagin* is repeating an untested inference that has since been tested and failed. Correct it wherever it appears in this book.
- **No human clinical trial of *D. rotundifolia* for cough, pertussis, bronchitis, or anything else was retrieved this run.** Not weak evidence — none. For a drug in continuous European use for centuries against a specific, common, easily-measured symptom, this absence is remarkable and should be stated loudly.
- **No support of any kind for the alchemical indication** — longevity, wasting, consumption, restoration of vitality. Nothing retrieved touches it.
- **Species specificity is real and cuts against substitution.** *D. rotundifolia* and *D. tokaiensis* suppressed mast cell activation; ***D. spatulata* did not*** — and *D. tokaiensis* was **more** effective than the traditional *D. rotundifolia*, leading the authors to propose it as a substitute source of Droserae Herba (Fukushima et al. 2009, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2009.06.009)). Likewise the aqueous *D. madagascariensis* extract was **only very weakly active** where *D. rotundifolia*'s was strong (Paper et al. 2005, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.1666)). ⚠️ **Congeners are not interchangeable**, and this is demonstrated, not assumed. Data on *D. madagascariensis*, *D. spatulata*, *D. tokaiensis* or *D. ramentacea* must never be imported into a *D. rotundifolia* claim.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

| Axis | Classification |
|---|---|
| Element / humoral | Solar-attributed in the alchemical reading — a claim about the plant's *cosmology*, not its action |
| Taste | Acrid, slightly bitter |
| Fresh-plant quality | Vesicant / irritant — the acrid principle is real to the touch |
| Habitat signature | Bog: cold, acid, saturated, nutrient-starved. The doctrine ignored the habitat entirely and read only the leaf. |
| Action (traditional) | Antitussive; antispasmodic; expectorant; the drug for convulsive cough |
| Action (alchemical) | Preserving; anti-dissipative; "holds what the sun would take" — **not supported** |
| Direction | Relaxing and clearing at the airway |

The energetic reading worth keeping: sundew is a plant that **holds moisture where moisture should not persist, and dissolves what it holds**. That is at least an honest description of a carnivore's mucilage and enzymes. The Kenetica gloss — relax the gripped airway, keep the surface wet and moving, dissolve the stuck — happens to align with the PDE/CBF/smooth-muscle data. Offered as a mnemonic, not as a mechanism.

### Contraindications

- **⚠️ WHOOPING COUGH IN A CHILD IS A MEDICAL EMERGENCY, NOT AN HERBAL INDICATION.** This is the single most important line in the entry. Pertussis kills infants. It is vaccine-preventable and antibiotic-treatable. The classical indication that gave sundew its entire reputation is the one context where using it as a primary treatment is indefensible. Sundew has **no human trial data for pertussis**. Adjunctive symptomatic use in an adult with a diagnosed, medically-managed post-viral cough is a different proposition from treating a coughing child with a bog plant.
- **⚠️ Plumbagin — irritancy and toxicity.** Naphthoquinones including plumbagin are cytotoxic and irritant; the fresh plant is a vesicant. Although the evidence above indicates naphthoquinones are *not* the therapeutic actives and are present at low concentration in extracts, "not the active" is not "not present." Avoid fresh plant contact; do not concentrate the naphthoquinone fraction; do not take isolated plumbagin. [USER: verify] any specific plumbagin toxicity threshold before printing a number — I retrieved none this run and will not invent one.
- **Pregnancy and lactation — avoid.** No data retrieved. A plant containing cytotoxic naphthoquinones with no reproductive safety profile is excluded by default.
- **Anticholinergic-adjacent caution.** The M3-allosteric and PDE-inhibitory findings imply potential interaction with anticholinergics, theophylline/methylxanthines, and PDE4 inhibitors (roflumilast). Mechanistic inference from *ex vivo* work, not a documented interaction — flagged as a hypothesis to check, not a known event.
- **⚠️ CONSERVATION — TREAT AS THE PRIMARY SOURCING CONTRAINDICATION.** *D. rotundifolia* is a protected species across much of its European range and its bog habitat is among the most degraded in Europe; peat extraction and drainage have removed it from large parts of its former distribution. Wild collection is illegal in many jurisdictions and ecologically indefensible everywhere. The population-genetic work makes the damage concrete: extremely low within-population diversity and very high among-population divergence mean **each bog holds a genetically distinct, inbred, non-replaceable population** — you cannot restock from elsewhere (Chung et al. 2013, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3732/ajb.1200486)). ⚠️ **Do not wild-harvest sundew. Ever.** [USER: verify] the exact legal protection status in your jurisdiction — I have not confirmed specific statutory listings this run.
- **⚠️ SUBSTITUTION IS THE RULE, NOT THE EXCEPTION — and it is now a proven pharmacological problem.** Because *D. rotundifolia* cannot be wild-collected at scale, commercial "Drosera" is routinely other species (*D. madagascariensis*, *D. ramentacea*, *D. peltata*) or cultivated material. This is precisely the substitution the literature warns about: *D. spatulata* failed where *D. rotundifolia* worked, and aqueous *D. madagascariensis* was near-inactive where *D. rotundifolia* was strong ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2009.06.009); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.1666)). **If your supplier cannot name the species, you do not know what the material does.** Demand the binomial and the cultivation provenance, or do not buy.
