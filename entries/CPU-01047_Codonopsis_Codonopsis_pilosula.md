# Codonopsis — 党参 (Dǎng Shēn) / [no established Arabic name] / [no Greek equivalent] / Dangshen

## *Codonopsis pilosula — "Poor Man's Ginseng," Bellflower Root, Bonnet Bellflower Root*

**Tradition Tags:** CM · SBJ · FMT

**Energetic Classification (TCM):** ◉ Qi / ⊕ Yuan

**Training Phase:** `BUILD` `RESET` `CYCLE-OFF`

---

### Origin & Tradition

Codonopsis pilosula is a twining perennial vine of the Campanulaceae family, native to the mountain forests of northern and central China, particularly Shanxi, Gansu, and Sichuan provinces. Its fleshy, carrot-like root has served as the workingman's equivalent of Panax ginseng for centuries — sharing many of ginseng's tonic properties at a fraction of the cost, and gentler in action, making it suitable for long-term, daily supplementation without the overstimulating edge associated with Panax. The name Dǎng Shēn refers to its historical cultivation in the Shangdang region of Shanxi province, once considered the finest source of the root. It is among the most widely used medicinal herbs in East Asia by volume, a testament to its deep integration into clinical and domestic practice.

**Canon of Medicine (Ibn Sina / CM):** While Codonopsis itself was not within Ibn Sina's materia medica — which drew primarily on Mediterranean, Persian, and Central Asian flora — the Qi-tonic class to which it belongs corresponds closely to the Galenic concept of "tonics of vital spirit." Later Islamic physicians working along the Silk Road trade routes encountered Chinese tonic herbs as trade commodities, and the functional category of a root that "strengthens the centre and augments the vital breath" is well-represented in Ibn Sina's framework for tonifying the stomach-spleen organ system and building vital capacity.

**Bencao Gangmu (Li Shizhen / SBJ):** Li Shizhen's 1596 encyclopaedia does not provide a dedicated entry for Codonopsis pilosula under that name — it was later systematically codified in the Qing dynasty Bencao Congxin (1757) by Wu Yiluo — but related Codonopsis species appear in the Bencao Gangmu under the classification of "roots that supplement the middle and augment Qi." Li Shizhen's framework of zhongyao (middle-warming) tonics forms the intellectual basis for how Codonopsis is understood: it supplements Spleen and Lung Qi, nourishes Blood, and promotes the generation of fluids (Jin Ye). It is the cornerstone of the Sì Jūn Zǐ Tāng ("Four Gentlemen Decoction") formula family in many modern TCM adaptations, where it substitutes for or complements Panax ginseng.

**Four Medical Tantras (Gyushi / FMT):** In Tibetan medicine, the Gyushi describes a class of roots termed "supplement-roots" (Tib. *rtsa sbyin pa*) that warm the digestive fire, build constitutional strength, and support the lung-breath complex. Codonopsis, known in Tibetan practice as *wang lag* or incorporated into compound formulas under generalized tonic root categories, is classified as sweet in taste, warming in potency, and directed at restoring depleted vital substance following illness, exertion, or malnutrition. The Tibetan tradition emphasizes its role in addressing "wind-disease of the channels" — a functional category overlapping with Qi deficiency — and its suitability for long-term convalescent support.

### Active Components

- **Codonopsis polysaccharides (CPS)** — heteropolysaccharides (primarily glucose, galactose, arabinose backbones); principal immunomodulatory agents; TLR4 receptor agonism driving macrophage activation and cytokine release; NK cell upregulation
- **Tangshenine and related alkaloids** — nitrogen-containing compounds with mild cholinomimetic and smooth-muscle relaxant properties; contribute to cognitive and gastrointestinal effects
- **Syringin (eleutheroside B)** — phenylpropanoid glycoside shared with Eleutherococcus senticosus; adaptogenic activity; mild adrenal modulation
- **Lobetyolin and lobetyol** — polyacetylene glycosides unique to Codonopsis; antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory activity; modulators of prostaglandin synthesis
- **Phytosterols (β-sitosterol, stigmasterol)** — membrane stabilisation; mild androgenic precursor activity; anti-inflammatory via COX pathway modulation
- **Fructo-oligosaccharides (FOS)** — prebiotic substrate supporting Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium species; secondary immune support via gut microbiome axis
- **Inulin** — soluble fibre fraction; supports Spleen Qi function correlate (intestinal motility, glycaemic buffering)
- **Amino acids (arginine, glutamine)** — high free amino acid content; directly support nitrogen balance and protein synthesis; arginine as nitric oxide precursor with vasodilatory relevance to training

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Immune system priming for training blocks** `BUILD` — Codonopsis polysaccharides drive TLR4-mediated macrophage activation, upregulating IL-2 and NK cell cytotoxic activity. Administered during high-volume training phases, this counteracts the transient immunosuppression associated with heavy loading, reducing upper respiratory illness incidence without the stimulant burden of full Panax ginseng protocols.

2. **Spleen-Qi support for nutrient assimilation** `BUILD` `CYCLE-OFF` — In TCM clinical practice and corroborated by its FOS and inulin content, Codonopsis improves digestive efficiency and nutrient absorption. For athletes in caloric surplus phases, this translates to improved substrate partitioning and reduced bloating from high-volume food intake. The traditional formula Sì Jūn Zǐ Tāng (Four Gentlemen: Codonopsis, Poria, Atractylodes, Licorice) is the foundational Qi-tonic prescription for this purpose.

3. **Insulin sensitivity and glucose metabolism** `BUILD` `REFINE` — Polysaccharide-driven AMPK activation improves skeletal muscle glucose uptake independent of insulin signalling intensity. This is relevant both in bulking phases (improved carbohydrate utilisation) and cutting phases (improved fasted training substrate availability). Moderate anti-diabetic effects have been confirmed in preclinical models.

4. **Cognitive maintenance under fatigue** `BUILD` `CYCLE-OFF` — Aqueous extracts demonstrate cholinomimetic activity, improving acetylcholine availability and protecting against scopolamine-induced memory impairment in animal models. During prolonged training camps or high-stress competition periods, cumulative Qi depletion in TCM terms maps onto CNS fatigue and working memory decline; Codonopsis addresses this more gently than stimulant-class adaptogens.

5. **Convalescent recovery and Qi rebuilding** `RESET` — Following illness, overtraining syndrome, or post-competition depletion, Codonopsis is the classical first-line tonic for rebuilding Qi from the ground up. Its gentler profile compared to Panax ginseng makes it appropriate for athletes who are fatigued, depleted, or recovering, where stimulating adaptogens may be contraindicated.

6. **Off-cycle tonic maintenance** `CYCLE-OFF` — The cumulative, gentle nature of Codonopsis's Qi-building action makes it ideal for sustained off-cycle use when more pharmacologically active tonics (Panax ginseng, Cistanche, Morinda) are being rested. It maintains baseline immune tone, digestive function, and energy production without stimulating the HPA axis.

### Preparation & Dosing

**Standard Decoction (Traditional TCM):**
Dried root slices 9–30 g in 500 ml water; simmer 30–45 minutes; reduce to 250 ml; consume warm, 1–2 × daily. This is the classical preparation; polysaccharides and alkaloids are efficiently extracted in hot water.

**Sì Jūn Zǐ Tāng Formula Base:**
Codonopsis 15 g + Poria cocos (Fu Ling) 9 g + Atractylodes macrocephala (Bai Zhu) 9 g + Glycyrrhiza (Zhi Gan Cao, honey-fried) 6 g — decoct together; the foundational Qi-tonic formula for digestive and constitutional support.

**Standardised Extract (commercial):**
10:1 extract, 500–1000 mg/day; polysaccharide content should be verified (minimum 40% by weight). Taken with food, morning or midday.

**Tincture (1:5 in 40% ethanol):**
4–6 ml, 2–3 × daily. Less traditional but practical for compound formula use.

**Powder (whole root, ground):**
3–6 g/day mixed into congee, warm broth, or protein preparations; the most economical and traditionally aligned method.

**Timing:** Morning and midday dosing is preferred in TCM (aligns with Yang-rising phases of the day); avoid late evening as mild energising Qi effects may interfere with sleep in sensitive individuals.

**Duration:** Codonopsis is considered a long-course tonic; clinical benefit accumulates over 4–12 weeks of consistent use. Short-term acute use is less rewarding than sustained supplementation.

### Scientific Correlation

- Li Z et al. (2016) *J Ethnopharmacol* 193:592–602 — Systematic review of Codonopsis pilosula immunomodulatory mechanisms; polysaccharide fractions (CPS) activate macrophages via TLR4 signalling pathway; demonstrate NK cell upregulation and T-lymphocyte proliferation; comprehensive survey of in vitro and in vivo immunological findings

- Cai ZP et al. (2019) *Phytomedicine* 57:343–352 — Codonopsis pilosula polysaccharides improved insulin sensitivity and glucose metabolism in high-fat diet mouse model; AMPK activation identified as primary mechanism; reduced fasting blood glucose and improved glucose tolerance test performance; anti-diabetic effects comparable to modest pharmaceutical intervention

- Wan L et al. (2014) *Pharmacol Biochem Behav* 121:155–162 — Codonopsis pilosula aqueous extract (200 and 400 mg/kg) significantly improved spatial memory and cognitive function in scopolamine-impaired mice in Morris water maze and passive avoidance tests; mechanism attributed to cholinergic pathway support including AChE inhibition and ChAT upregulation; dose-dependent effect confirmed

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Primary Tier:** ◉ Qi — Spleen and Lung Qi supplementation; middle burner tonification; generation of Qi from food essence

**TCM Channel:** Spleen (Pi), Lung (Fei)

**Energetic Action:** Supplements the middle, augments Qi, nourishes Blood, promotes the generation of body fluids (Jin Ye); gently lifts Spleen Qi without excessive warming; tonifies without stagnating

**Ayurvedic:** No classical Ayurvedic equivalent, though the functional class aligns with *Rasayana* (rejuvenating tonics); sweet (*madhura*) and astringent (*kashaya*) rasas; predominantly *Vata* and *Pitta* pacifying; neutral to mildly warming virya (*veerya*); supports *Ojas* (vital essence) and *Agni* (digestive fire); comparable in function to Shatavari (Asparagus racemosus) as a gentle nutritive tonic

### Contraindications

- **Excess Heat patterns (TCM):** Codonopsis is a warming tonic; it is contraindicated in conditions of excess Heat, high fever, or Yin deficiency with vigorous Fire — use may exacerbate heat signs, irritability, or hypertension in these constitutions
- **Early-stage external pathogen invasion:** Classical TCM caution — do not supplement Qi while a pathogen (cold, flu) is in its acute Exterior stage; this may "close the door on the robber" and drive the pathogen deeper; wait until acute phase resolves
- **Concurrent use with strong stimulants:** Codonopsis mildly potentiates adrenoceptor sensitivity; combining with high-dose caffeine or Ephedra may amplify cardiovascular stimulation in sensitive individuals
- **Pregnancy:** Insufficient safety data for high therapeutic doses; culinary amounts in food preparations are traditional and likely safe; medicinal dosing requires medical supervision
- **Drug interactions:** Possible additive effects with antidiabetic medications (metformin, sulfonylureas) due to AMPK activation and glucose-lowering activity; monitor blood glucose if combining
