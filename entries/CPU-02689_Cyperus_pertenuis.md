### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The plant is attested here only in Sushruta, through the Sanskrit-to-Latin plant-name appendix at the end of Bhishagratna's English translation, vol. III (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, the "Appendix" pages from about line 31860 to 32470). A search of the chapter files on disk and of the three volume texts for the name under its Sanskrit and Latin spellings found no passage that uses it as a drug (the Sushruta text uses the neighbouring name *Mustaka* in several passages, for example in Kalpa ch. 2, but none is tied to this Latin name). The row therefore rests on a name list, not on a passage with indications.

**How the appendix is laid out, and why this row is uncertain.** In the OCR text the Sanskrit names stand in one run of lines and the Latin names in another, so a Latin name is matched to its Sanskrit name only by position. Here the Latin line is 32420, which reads "Cy penis  pertinuis", and the nearest Sanskrit entry by position is at line 32360, which the OCR prints as "NsCgara-mustaka (Nagara-mustaka)". The offset between the two columns drifts by a few lines along the appendix (about 60 lines here), so the pairing is a position guess, confirmed only as far as neighbouring pairs match in the same stretch.
