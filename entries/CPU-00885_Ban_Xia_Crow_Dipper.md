# Ban Xia — Crow Dipper, Pinellia rhizome (半夏 Bàn Xià; Di Wen "Earth Texture", Shui Yu "Watery Jade")

## *Pinellia ternata* (spine botanical; Yang 1998 prints "Rhizoma Pinelliae Tematae", an OCR rendering of the pharmaceutical Latin, and names the genus) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM, SBJ, SS, FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Evidence summary.** Of the four tags, only the Shennong tag has a locus. The Avicenna, Sushruta and Tibetan tags have none that could be found, and the entry says so under each. The Latin species is printed only as a genus-level form in the extraction list (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv`, "species not resolved"). [USER: verify]

**Chinese — Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ).** In Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) the entry is at lines 3463 to 3468, on PDF page 93, under the heading **"Herbs: Inferior Class"** (class heading at line 3157; running header on this page at line 3452). It reads: "Ban Xia (Rhizoma Pinelliae Tematae) is acrid and balanced. It mainly treats cold damage, cold and heat, and hardness below the heart. It downbears the qi, [treats] swollen and sore throat, head dizziness, chest distention, cough and counterflow, and rumbling intestines, and stops sweating. Its other name is Di Wen (Earth Texture). Another name is Shui Yu (Watery Jade). It grows in rivers and valleys." The spine's English "Crow Dipper" is not in the text read, and `_sources/shennong.csv` line 273 gives the list name "Pinellia Rhizome (半夏 Bàn Xià)". Yang's footnote 214 (line 3486 onward) lists many later uses of the drug. It is the translator's commentary, not the classic, and is not relied on.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** No locus was found. `_sources/avicenna.csv` has no *Pinellia* row, and no Canon chapter text is on disk.

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** No locus was found. A search of the three Bhishagratna volumes under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` for *Pinellia*, *Typhonium* and *Arisaema* returned nothing. `Sources/SS_Sushruta/views/gaps.csv` line 3957 lists this row as "ss_attested_no_evidence: not in chapters extracted so far".

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** No locus was found. Neither Clark 1995 nor the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text names *Pinellia*, and `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` has no such row. The nearest aroid in Clark ch.20 is "dva.wa" (line 1056), glossed *Typhonium giganteum* and three *Arisaema* species, which Yang prints as other drugs (Bai Fu Zi, line 3754, and Hu Zhang, line 3499). It is not this plant.

### Active Components

Pinellia belongs to the arum family, whose raw tubers are characteristically acrid because of needle-shaped crystals of calcium oxalate. That fits Yang's "acrid" and the "toxic" label of the Inferior Class. Specific constituents were not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The Shennong entry assigns Ban Xia to the class of drugs that "treat disease", and it is not a recommended self-directed practice in the Codex. The raw tuber is an irritant, and the text gives no preparation.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ:** "acrid and balanced" (line 3463). The preface describes inferior-class drugs generally as ones that "mainly treat disease and correspond to earth", "usually toxic and cannot be taken for a long time" (lines 391 to 393).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The tag rests on a spine row, not on a list row or text. No attestation and no cultivation framing was found.

**Chinese — Shennong (SBJ).** *(a) Attested, in the lower grade.* The entry sits under the printed heading "Herbs: Inferior Class" (line 3157; page header line 3452). The preface (lines 391 to 395) says of that class: "There are 125 inferior class medicinals which are used as assistants and envoys. They mainly treat disease and correspond to earth. They are usually toxic and cannot be taken for a long time. If one intends to eliminate cold and heat and evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and cure disease, one should base [one's efforts] on the inferior class." This is the *san pin* lower grade (下品), the grade of drugs that attack disease for a defined course. It is not the grade of substances taken long-term to nourish life. The Ban Xia entry itself has no "protracted taking" line, unlike many superior-class entries. No *jing*, *qi* or *shen* action is assigned to it by the entry.

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* No locus was found, so there is no naming in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter and no cultivation status.

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(c) Absent.* No locus was found. The frame of the tradition is that disease arises from ignorance and the three poisons (Clark 1995, lines 440 to 441), and nothing places this plant within it.

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: taken, if at all, for a defined complaint and a defined short course, and then stopped. Nothing read supports long-term use, and the tradition's own grading is the warning. The Avicenna, Sushruta and Tibetan tags are unverified.

### Contraindications

The Shennong text labels the class "usually toxic". The raw tuber of arum-family plants burns the mouth and throat. Do not ingest unprocessed material, and do not self-treat cough, throat or stomach complaints with it. Seek a qualified practitioner, and treat it as unsafe in pregnancy unless a qualified practitioner directs otherwise. [USER: verify]
