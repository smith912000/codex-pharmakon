# Gazelle — "Gazelle", "Gazelle's Ears", "Gazelle's dung" (Bryan 1930, Ebers Papyrus; no Egyptian word printed in the list)
## *The gazelle, as Bryan's English identification names it; the list gives no Egyptian word and no binomial* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The only text on disk for this tradition is C. P. Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). In his chapter VII, "Organic Remedies", Bryan says a full list of the animal materia medica "would not be possible", because "the identity of a considerable number of the animals mentioned is still beyond us", and then lists those "whose identity is not in doubt, and who were called upon to sacrifice themselves in the cause of the Healing Art" (lines 1385-1392). The gazelle is in that list: "... Goose Duck Stag Gazelle Ram Goat" (line 1398). The extract row (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, row 111, "Bryan 1930, Ch. VII Organic Remedies (animals) (line 1398)") records the same and adds that Bryan's list gives "no Egyptian word or binomial". `_sources/ebers.csv` line 78 has "Gazelle dung", with the genus *Gazella* and the source "Ebers; Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus", identification not flagged uncertain; I do not rely on that row. The Egyptian word and the species are not stated in anything on disk, and identification is a modern hypothesis in this corpus, since the papyrus is organised by complaint, not by substance. Bryan's own list simply says "Gazelle".

Bryan then lists the parts the Egyptians took from their animals, from "Hair Feathers Horns" to "Excrement Urine" (about lines 1406-1421). He does not say which parts came from which animal, except in a few remarks; for the gazelle, three recipes in his text name it, and each uses a different part.

1. *The ears.* In the part of Bryan's book that his running head labels "Diseases of Ear, Nose, and Mouth" (page 109), a remedy follows a discharge: "When it is thick thereunder, MAKE FOR HIM THE REMEDY WHICH DRIES THE WOUNDS: / Head-of-the-amamu-animal Gazelle's Ears Tortoise-shell annek-plant / Very frequently this stops it completely" (about lines 3062-3068). The *amamu*-animal and the *annek*-plant are Bryan's untranslated Egyptian words, and I do not identify them. The preparation is not described beyond the ingredient list.
2. *The dung, for the hair.* In Bryan's discussion of hair remedies: "other preservatives and restoratives are frankly and positively revolting, such as a concoction of Writing-fluid, Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus, and Gazelle's dung" (lines 4030-4031). The sentence is Bryan's description, not a quoted recipe, and the complaint is hair restoration. Bryan reports that the book holds several hair remedies, and this is one.
3. *The dung, for the granary.* Under his domestic hints, Bryan prints "TO PREVENT THE RODENTS EATING DURRA IN THE GRANARY / Gazelle's dung Put on the fire in the Granary. Cover the wall and the floor with Mice-excrement and with their urine. That will prevent the durra being eaten" (about lines 4237-4241). This is not a medical use; it is pest control.

I searched Bryan's text for "gazelle" (any case). It occurs in the list (line 1398) and in these three places (lines 3066, 4030, 4239). Bryan's text is a summary with selected quotations, not a full translation, so the absence of other gazelle recipes in it proves little about the papyrus. [USER: verify]

No spell, no deity and no dose is reported by Bryan with any of the three recipes.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, gazelle dung is the digested remains of grass and leaves, with fibre, bacteria and the parasites of a wild herbivore; the ear is cartilage and skin, with hair. Nothing in the entry links any constituent to the uses given.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Ebers material, as Bryan prints it, uses the gazelle's ears in a remedy for a discharge in the ear region, its dung in a hair preparation, and its dung burnt in a granary against rodents. The Codex does not recommend the first two: an ear discharge needs examination, hair loss has treatable causes, and animal dung on the scalp or in an ear carries infection. The third is pest control with smoke, and it is not a human medicine. Several gazelle species are threatened. The entry is a record of what Bryan reports.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted. The ear remedy gives its ingredients (head of the *amamu*-animal, gazelle's ears, tortoise-shell, *annek*-plant) with no method; "Very frequently this stops it completely" is Bryan's reported claim. The hair concoction is named as writing-fluid, hippopotamus fat and gazelle's dung, with no method. The granary use is "Put on the fire". Bryan gives no quantity for any ingredient and no length of course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Dung from wild herbivores carries bacteria and parasites; it should not be applied to the scalp, to broken skin, or put into the ear. An ear discharge may signal an infection or a perforated eardrum and is assessed medically. Hair loss has many causes, some treatable. Smoke from burning dung is an irritant. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Egyptian (EP):** None. Bryan's list and the recipes quoted give no quality (hot, cold, moist, dry) for the gazelle or its parts. The Ebers corpus is organised by complaint and records what was done for a condition, not a classification of the substance.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. ### Contraindications

- **Ear discharge or pain:** needs examination; do not put animal material or dung into the ear.
- **Hair loss:** has treatable causes; do not apply dung to the scalp.
- **Infection and hygiene:** wild-animal dung and tissue carry bacteria and parasites; do not apply them to broken skin.
- **Smoke:** burning dung produces irritant smoke; do not burn it indoors.
- **Conservation and law:** several gazelle species are threatened or protected in law; the Codex does not recommend taking any animal for these uses.
