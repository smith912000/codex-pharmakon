### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not identified. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The first-pass check guessed "possibly Barleria sp."; that is a guess from the name's likeness to Kuruntaka and is not taken as an identification. A search of all the Sushruta text on disk (the chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and Bhishagratna's three volumes) for the name found only the group list below.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Chapter 38 of the *Sutrasthana* is the chapter "which deals with the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam)" and says that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (Vol. I lines 15343 to 15350; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3 to 9). The drug is placed there by group, and a group's virtue is stated for the group as a whole.

1. **Group list.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38, the A'ragvadha'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15368 to 15374; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 29). Bhishagratna prints: "The drugs known as Aragvadha, Madana, Gopaghonta, Kutaja, Patha, Kantaki, Patala, Murva, Indrayava, Saptaparna, Nimva, Kuruntaka, Dasi-kuruntaka, the two kinds of Karanja, Patola, Kiratatikta, Guduchi, Chitraka, Shringshta, and Sushavi form the group known as the Aragvadhadi." (Obvious OCR damage in the e-text, for example "Ximva" and "Kiratttikua" in the printed line, is repaired here; the name Dasi-kuruntaka is as printed.) It stands directly after "Kuruntaka".
2. **The group's virtue.** The metrical text that follows gives the virtue of the group as a whole: it "destroys the deranged Kaphani and the effects of poison and proves beneficial in cases of Meha (morbid discharges from the urethra), Kushtha, fever, vomiting and itching of the body and acts as a purifying (aseptic) agent in the case of an ulcer" (lines 15378 to 15385). This is the group's virtue, not a statement about Dasi-kuruntaka alone.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unidentified.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the A'ragvadha'di group: lowering deranged Kapha, countering the effects of poison, and helping in urethral discharges, skin disease (*Kushtha*), fever, vomiting, itching and ulcers (group-level).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no taste or potency for this drug on its own, and no identification.

The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no plant for any of the conditions named.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in a chapter 38 group list, which classifies drugs by the use of the whole group (*Dravya-Sangrahaniya*). Chapter 38 is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns this drug no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only, and only at the level of its group. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it. Nothing in the A'ragvadha'di group's virtue is a life-nourishing claim.

**Practitioner's note.** An unidentified name in a group list, next to a drug the spine reads as Barleria. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without an identification nothing can be gathered or used. A cultivator reads it as a name in a record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified; Kuruntaka is a separate row and is not assumed to be the same drug. Do not substitute any herb for it.
- **The conditions named.** Urethral discharges, skin disease, fever, vomiting and ulcers are named for the group. These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Group membership.** The drug is one of many in its group, and the group's virtues are not safety statements about any one member.
