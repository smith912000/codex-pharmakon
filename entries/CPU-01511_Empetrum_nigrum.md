# Empetrum nigrum — Empetron (Dioscorides 4-181)

## *Empetrum nigrum (the translators' suggestion on the SUGGESTED line; "Crowberry, Black-berried Heath")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant as a drug in its own right (see the Tibetan note below). The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) printed under the chapter heading, not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED line reads "Empetrum nigrum — Crowberry, Black-berried Heath", and the translation prints the label "POISONOUS" below it. The row has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-181. EMPETRON* at line 9663, SUGGESTED line at 9665, label at 9666, text at line 9667. The chapter stands in Book Four, "Other Herbs & Roots", close to its end. The translators' name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 759. The next chapter, 4-182 *KLEMATITIS* (line 9668), is a different plant.

The whole chapter reads: "Empetron grows in hilly maritime places, salty to the taste, but that nearer to the earth is more bitter. Given in broth or honey water it purges phlegm, bile, and watery matter. It is also called phacoides."

So the chapter gives a habitat (hilly places by the sea), two tastes (salty, and more bitter in the part nearer the earth) and one use (a purge of phlegm, bile and watery matter, given in broth or honey water). It prints one other name, *phacoides*. It gives no description of leaf, flower or fruit. So the identification rests almost wholly on the habitat, the tastes and the name. The label "POISONOUS" is printed in the translation under the SUGGESTED line, and the chapter's own text states no danger. Whether the label is Dioscorides' or the translators' is not stated. **Tibetan note, not a tag.** The Tibetan record in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` prints "Empetrum niger" at line 1043 as the third binomial of a Tibetan drug, *shug.pa.tsher.chan*, whose first two binomials are species of juniper (*Sabina recurva*, *Juniperus recurva*). The drug is described there as "of three types" with a "small, thorny trunk". The text there says the leaf is recommended in disorders of the lower intestine and the fruit "is said to be supreme in (curing) fever in the limbs, in drying lymph accumulations and as an essence-extraction ingredient". That claim attaches to the Tibetan drug and is not carried over to Dioscorides' plant. The identification with *Empetrum* is therefore secondary, and this entry is not tagged FMT. It is flagged for review in the report.

### Active Components

The constituents of the plant Dioscorides describes were not read. *Empetrum nigrum* is a low evergreen shrub of cold and coastal ground bearing black berries. Its family placement has varied between authors and was not checked here. Nothing is stated about its composition, or about what would make a plant purge, taste salty or be labelled poisonous. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The one use Dioscorides gives is a purge, and the translation labels the plant poisonous. The Codex gives no protocol for purging, and a plant labelled poisonous whose text gives no dose is not offered as a remedy or a cultivation aid.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text says the plant is "given in broth or honey water". It gives no quantity, frequency or duration, and does not say which part is used beyond the contrast of the part nearer the earth. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints no hot, cold, moist or dry quality. It prints two tastes: "salty to the taste", and, for the part nearer the earth, "more bitter".

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The only thing the chapter gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a short sensory assay: the place where the plant grows, a salty taste, and a bitter taste that changes along the plant. It assigns the plant no status. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a purge, labelled poisonous in the translation, with no dose. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The translation labels the plant "POISONOUS" and the text describes it as a purge of phlegm, bile and watery matter, which is a strong effect. The identification is only the translators' suggestion, so nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of this entry. No safety information for pregnancy was read.
