# Cotoneaster manna (shir-khisht) — شيرخشك (shīrkhishk)

## *A manna: a dew-like exudate that the Canon says falls on the khilāf tree and on tragacanth at Herat; the label "Cotoneaster manna" is the modern identification* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Avicenna (CM).** This substance is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. It is read in the Arabic (`Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`, Book II, entry *shīrkhishk*, heading at Arabic text line 18211). The entry is two sentences long.

Definition: "it is a dew (*ṭall*) that falls on the *khilāf* tree and on tragacanth (*kathīrāʾ*) at Herat" (هو طل يقع على شجر الخلاف والكثيراء بهراة). Action (*al-khawāṣṣ*): "cleansing" (جال). Excretory organs (*aʿḍāʾ al-nafḍ*): "it is near *tarunjabīn* in its purging and its actions, indeed stronger than it" (هو قريب من الترنجبين في إسهاله وأفعاله بل أقوى منه). That is all the entry says. *Tarunjabīn* (the manna of the camel-thorn) is a separate drug and is not described in this entry.

The same word appears once elsewhere in the text (line 7362), in a list of gentle purgatives, "preserved violet, tamarind, *shīrkhishk* and *tarunjabīn*", offered as things that help nature incline a humour to purging (read at lines 7359 to 7362; the surrounding chapter is not otherwise used here).

**Identity doubt.** "Cotoneaster manna" is the modern identification of *shīr-khisht*, a Persian trade name for a manna. The *Canon* does not name *Cotoneaster*. It names the *khilāf* (a willow-type tree) and tragacanth (an *Astragalus* gum) as the trees on which the dew falls, and it places the drug at Herat. The text therefore does not confirm the modern botanical source; it confirms only a dew-like exudate with a purgative action. ### Active Components

Manna exudates in general are rich in sugars and sugar alcohols, which draw water into the bowel and so act as mild osmotic laxatives. That is a general fact about manna-type drugs, stated without a source specific to this one. No composition is asserted for *shīr-khisht* itself.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. In the entry Avicenna gives it one use: purging, in the same manner as *tarunjabīn* but stronger. He does not say which humour it draws, for what illness, or in what vehicle. It is not offered here as a recommended practice.

### Scientific Correlation

Sugar-rich manna exudates act as laxatives by osmosis; this is a general, well-established fact about such materials. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Avicenna (CM).** No temperament degree is given in the entry. The only quality printed is the action "cleansing" (*jālin*) and the comparison with *tarunjabīn* (stronger purging).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The entry is a definition and a comparison of strength with another purgative. No cultivation framing is attested for it, and the text says nothing of innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruḥ* in connection with it.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to cultivate here. It is a purgative recorded by comparison with a better-known purgative, and purging is the opposite of the conservation that the Unani cultivation idea values.

### Contraindications

Historical record only. The one action reported is purging, and the text calls it stronger than *tarunjabīn*, so heavy or repeated use would be expected to cause diarrhoea and fluid loss. No safety statement is given in the entry. Do not treat any use here as medical advice.
