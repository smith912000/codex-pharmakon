# Bull's suet (also leopard, lion) — STEAR TAUREION, PARDALEION KAI LEONTEION (Dioscorides 2-90), "Bulls' Suet etc."
## *Kidney fat of the bull, and the fat of the leopard (or panther) and lion, prepared by washing and boiling in wine; the translator prints "SUGGESTED: Bulls' Suet etc."* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

This entry is the one Dioscorides chapter that carries bull's, leopard's and lion's fat in its heading. Cattle fat taken as a material, with the Egyptian (Ebers) fat prescriptions and Dioscorides' ox and cow suet (2-89), is in `III_40_Ox_suet.md`, which also quotes 2-90 and 2-94; the lion as an animal is in `III_39_Lion.md`. This entry stays with the 2-90 chapter and does not repeat the Ebers material.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 2-90 *STEAR TAUREION, PARDALEION KAI LEONTEION* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading line 5315, text line 5317; `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` row "2-90 (line 5315)"; Osbaldeston and Wood 2000). The heading's three adjectives are bull's, leopard's and lion's. The text itself names only the bull in its first sentence, and then, at the end, "panthers', lions', wild boars', camels', horses', and other similar fats", prepared "in the same way". Whether the translator's "panthers" is the heading's *pardaleion* (leopard) is the edition's rendering and I have not checked it against the Greek. [USER: verify] The chapter is a method of preparing, with no uses in it. It reads:

"Bulls' suet must be prepared as follows. Take new fat from the kidneys, wash it with running water from the river, and having pulled off the skin put the fat into a new clay jar, sprinkle a little salt on it and melt it. Then strain it out into clear water. When it begins to congeal rub it diligently with the hands again, pouring out and pouring on water until it is thoroughly washed. Then place it into the jar again and boil it with the same amount of sweet-smelling wine. When it has boiled twice, remove the jar from the fire and let the fat remain in there all night. If there is any bad smell left in it the day after, place it into another ceramic jar, pour sweet-smelling wine on it again and repeat (as has been formerly described) until it discards all rank smell. It is also melted without salt to be used in some sores for which salt is not indicated. Prepared like this it is not very white."

Uses are in the general chapter 2-94 *STEATON DUNAMIS* (line 5342): "All fat is warming, softening and purifying; yet that of bulls, cows and calves is somewhat astringent. The lions' is similar to these and they say it is an antidote to defend against those who intend treachery." The leopard's fat is not given a use of its own in the lines read. Neighbouring chapters: 2-89 ox or cow suet prepared in sea water with Etruscan wax (line 5309); 2-91 perfuming the fat of calves, bulls and deer (line 5318); 2-93 keeping goose, poultry and calf fat from putrefying (line 5339). The bull's blood in a different chapter (2-97) is in `III_49_Blood.md`.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, suet is the hard fat from around the kidneys, almost wholly triglycerides rich in saturated fatty acids, with cholesterol and traces of fat-soluble vitamins. Washing and boiling, as the chapter describes, removes odour and water-soluble material.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Greek chapter tells how to render and de-odorise the fat. The only uses are in the general fat chapter: fat is warming, softening and purifying, bull's astringent, lion's, "they say", an antidote "to defend against those who intend treachery" (the text does not say how). Beef fat is a food in many cuisines. Lion and leopard are protected species, and no part of either belongs in a medicine. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparation is the method quoted above: new kidney fat, washed in running river water, skinned, melted with a little salt, strained into clear water, washed by hand until clean, boiled twice with an equal amount of sweet-smelling wine, left overnight, and repeated until it "discards all rank smell"; salt is omitted for sores that salt would hurt. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Rendered fat is an occlusive base for ointments, and washing and boiling reduce odour and some contaminants without making a raw tissue sterile. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM):** Dioscorides 2-94 (line 5342): all fat "warming, softening and purifying"; that of bulls, cows and calves "somewhat astringent"; the lions' "similar to these". No degree or humour is printed, and none is printed for the leopard.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. What the chapter offers is a discipline of attention in preparation: take the fat from near the kidneys while new, wash it in running water, wash it by hand until the water is clean, boil it in sweet-smelling wine, and repeat "until it discards all rank smell", with salt left out where a sore forbids it. The test is smell, with whiteness noted ("not very white" when salt is left out). 2-94 gives the fat's qualities as therapeutic ones. No chapter read assigns a cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing is sourced that makes suet a substance for cultivation. What the chapter offers a practitioner is a lesson in preparation, in judging by the nose, and in knowing when a salted fat will hurt. A cultivator who wants beef fat eats it as food and takes no practice from this entry. The lion's fat "antidote" is a report ("they say"), not a use to attempt. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** raw or poorly rendered animal fat on an open wound can introduce bacteria and spores.
- **Conservation and law:** lions and leopards are protected species, and international trade in their parts is restricted; do not obtain either fat. [USER: verify]
- **Prion disease:** bovine brain and spinal tissue can carry the agent of bovine spongiform encephalopathy; use only regulated beef fat.
- **Diet:** concentrated saturated fat; people with raised lipids or cardiovascular disease should treat it as any dietary fat.
- **Ethics and law:** cattle are sacred or protected in some communities and jurisdictions.
