# Saccharum munja — Munja (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Saccharum munja, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Saccharum  Munja.", line 32327) against the Sanskrit name Munja (OCR "Munja", line 32265). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Munja with the spellings Munja, Maunji, Moonja and Munji, for OCR-garbled letters, and by fuzzy matching of every word against Munja, Munji and Maunji. The Sanskrit name Munja alone is not printed as a drug in any chapter. One passage prints "Munja grass", and three other names share the stem Munja- without being tied to it by the text.

*Sharira ch.5 (anatomy, Sarira-sankhya-vyakaranam).* `SS_Sharira_ch05.txt` lines 223 to 236, under "Mode of dissection": the body chosen for dissection should have its excrement removed from the entrails and be left to decompose in the water of "a solitary and still pool", and "securely placed in a cage (so that it may not be eaten away by fish nor drift away), after having covered it entirely with the outer sheaths of Munja grass, Kusha grass, hemp or with rope etc. After seven days the body would be thoroughly decomposed, when the observer should slowly scrape off the decomposed skin etc. with a whisk made of grass-roots, hair, Kusa blade or with a strip of split bamboo". The grass is a covering material, in a list with Kusha, hemp and rope. The passage assigns it no property and no medicinal use. This is the only place in the chapters where the English "Munja grass" appears.

*Names that share the stem, not tied to Munja grass.* `SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` lines 111 to 121, in the Shleshma-samshamana group of the chapter on cleansing and soothing drugs: "The drugs known as Kaleyaka, Aguru, Tilaparni, Kushtha, Haridra, Shitashiva, Shatapushpa, Sarala, Rasna, Prakiryya, Udakiryya, Ingudi, Sumanah, Kakadani, Langalaki, Hastikarna, Munjataka, Lamajjaka, etc." are restorers of the deranged *Shleshma* to its natural state. `SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt` lines 122 to 125, in the treatment of Kaphaja Visarpa: "Drugs, such as Kalanusarya, Aguru, Chocha (cardamom), Gunja, Rasna, Vacha, Shita-shiva, Indra-parni, Palindi, Munjata and Mahi-Kadamba (applied similarly) are also efficacious in the present type." The names Munjataka and Munjata differ from Munja by a suffix, the text does not say that they are one plant, and the Codex does not claim it. [USER: verify]

*Munjavan, a Soma species.* `SS_Chikitsa_ch29.txt` lines 20 to 27 (the rasayana chapter on the "restorative and constructive agents which arrest innate morbific tendencies") says that "the one and the same divine Soma plant may be classified into twenty-four species", of which the second is "Munjavan". Lines 321 to 323 describe it: "The Munjavan puts forth leaves like those of a garlic". Lines 346 to 350 say of a locality "to the north bank beyond the Vitasta (river)" that "The Munjavan and the Amsumin species may also be likewise found in the same locality". The name is built on the word munja, but the text gives it as a species of the Soma plant and describes its leaf as that of a garlic; it nowhere says that it is Munja grass, and the Codex does not claim it. The chapter is a rasayana chapter, and it names Munjavan only. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Saccharum* is a genus of tall perennial grasses (Poaceae), and the leaf-sheaths and stems of several species are fibrous and used for cordage and mats. That is a statement about the genus and not a finding of the Sushruta text. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the name the chapters print, the plant (if it is this one) is a material and not a drug: the outer sheaths of Munja grass are a covering for a body being prepared for dissection (Sharira 5). The text gives no medicinal use under this name. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only use the chapters give is as a covering: "the outer sheaths of Munja grass" around the body in the dissection preparation (Sharira 5, lines 233 to 235). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The only passage that names the grass (Sharira 5) names it as a covering material in an anatomical procedure, in a list with Kusha grass, hemp and rope. That is a craft and anatomical context and not a cultivation status. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name Munja grass; the rasayana chapter Chikitsa 29 names Munjavan, which the text gives as a species of the Soma plant with a garlic-like leaf and does not identify with the grass, so it is not read as this plant's attestation. The text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for Munja. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with Munja grass: it is a material for wrapping a body. The similar-sounding Soma species Munjavan is a different thing in the text. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of a Latin name that the chapters do not give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this plant. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. The one use named, handling a decomposing body, carries the hazards of infection and of the law on human remains, which are general facts and not findings of the text.
