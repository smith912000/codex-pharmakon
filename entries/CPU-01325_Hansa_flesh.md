# Hansa flesh — Hansa, flesh of the swimming-bird (*Plava*) group, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the bird Bhishagratna prints as "Hansa"; the Vol. 3 glossary reads "A goose or duck" by column order, and the chapter gives no gloss; no binomial is printed* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Hansa is described in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 250 lists "Hansa (swan/goose)", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1"). The "swan/goose" is that list's gloss; the chapter prints no gloss. The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The marsh-dwellers (*Anupa*) fall into five groups (lines 659-669), and the second is the *Plavas*, "divers or swimmers". The Hansa heads the birds of that group: "Birds such as the Hansa, Sarasa, Kraimcha [Krauncha], Chakravaka, Kurura (belong also to the Prasaha group) Kadamva, Karandava, Jivan Jivaka, Vaka, Valaka ... Madgu, Utkrosha ... Megharava and Shvetacharana etc. belong to the Plava family. These birds are found to move about in large flocks" (lines 732-745; names omitted where the OCR is damaged; the OCR prints "Kraimcha", read here as Krauncha).

The group text reads: "The flesh of any one of this family is cooling, demulcent, and spermatopoietic and destroys the deranged Vayu. It proves beneficial in cases of haemoptysis, is sweet in taste and of digestion, and is possessed of laxative and diuretic properties" (lines 747-751). Of the Hansa by name: "The flesh of the Hansa is heavy (of digestion), heat-making, sweet and demulcent. It tends to improve the voice and complexion, and imparts strength to the system. It is spermatopoietic, tissue-building and tonic, and proves curative in nervous diseases (Vata-Vikara)" (lines 752-756). The group is called cooling and the Hansa heat-making; the text does not comment, and both are recorded as printed.

Two other places print "swan" in Bhishagratna's English without the Sanskrit, so they are not read as the Hansa here. In *Kalpasthana* ch.1, in the signs by which poisoned food is detected, "A swan cackles violently" (lines 178-179). In *Sutrasthana* ch.29, among the sights of good omen a physician may meet on his way to a patient, the list includes "a horse, a swan, a peacock, a bird of the Chasha species" (line 186). *Hansapadi* in ch.38 (line 17) is a plant name in a drug list, and a different thing.

**Identity.** Ch.46 does not say what bird the Hansa is. The glossary of Bhishagratna's Vol. 3 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, name at line 30561) gives, in a separate OCR column, "A goose or duck"; the pairing is by column order. The spine list says "swan/goose". The bird is not fixed here. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Poultry and waterfowl flesh is mostly water and muscle protein, with fat that is concentrated in the skin and varies by species, and supplies iron and B vitamins. No analysis of the bird Sushruta calls Hansa was read, and its species is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced record is one dietary description in a food chapter: a swimming bird's flesh, with stated qualities and one indication. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no use for voice, complexion, strength or any disease. The bird is not identified with confidence; swans and wild geese are protected by wildlife law in many countries; and wild waterfowl are a recognised reservoir of avian influenza viruses. A reader who eats no meat has nothing to adopt.

### Preparation & Dosing

None is given for the Hansa. The ch.46 lines state qualities and indications only. Ch.46 elsewhere describes ways of preparing meat in general, but none is attributed to the Hansa. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Wild waterfowl can carry avian influenza viruses and enteric bacteria such as *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*, and thorough cooking with hygienic handling is the standard precaution. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 747-756). For the *Plava* group: cooling; demulcent; spermatopoietic; destroys deranged *Vayu*; beneficial in haemoptysis; sweet in taste and digestion; laxative and diuretic. For the Hansa by name: heavy of digestion; heat-making; sweet; demulcent; improves voice and complexion; imparts strength; spermatopoietic, tissue-building and tonic; curative in nervous diseases (*Vata-Vikara*). The group's "cooling" and the Hansa's "heat-making" stand together in the text as translated. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Hansa is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food, not about this flesh. The text does call the flesh "spermatopoietic, tissue-building and tonic" and says it "imparts strength", which are properties listed among foods and not a placement in a *vajikarana* or *rasayana* chapter; no line read puts the Hansa in either. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta the Hansa is a food with stated qualities, and the sources read give a cultivator no regimen, season or course for it. The text's strength and tonic words describe the food; they do not make it a rejuvenative, and the bird is neither identified nor easy to obtain lawfully.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** wild waterfowl can carry avian influenza viruses and enteric bacteria; do not handle sick or dead wild birds, and cook any flesh through.
- **Identity:** swan, goose and duck are all offered by different notes; the text does not choose. [USER: verify]
- **Legal and conservation status:** swans, geese and other waterfowl are protected in many countries, with seasons and permits; status for any candidate species was not checked. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food or medicine is a moral question for many traditions; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
