# Crayfish / freshwater crayfish skull — "Skull-of-the-Crayfish" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus; no Egyptian word is printed)
## *The "skull" of the creature Bryan calls "Crayfish"; Bryan prints no Egyptian word and no binomial, so the spine's "Astacidae / Parastacidae sp." is the list-maker's gloss and is not read from the text* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*. The spine row reads "Crayfish / freshwater crayfish skull", "Astacidae / Parastacidae sp.", note "Ebers; Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus; id-uncertain" (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 92). Wikipedia was not used. "Crayfish" does not stand in Bryan's list of the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" in Chapter VII (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1404); that list has "Crab" and no crayfish, and `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` has no crayfish row. The animal therefore enters the project only through the prescription below.

**Where the skull is prescribed.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "cray" and "skull". The crayfish occurs once.

- **Pain in the head, lines 2100-2108**, in Chapter XI "Minor Medicine" (heading lines 2090-2091). Bryan notes that the headache remedies were "not to be taken internally but rubbed in externally" (line 2093). The prescription (line 2100 heading, 2102 ingredients, 2108 direction): "REMEDY TO DRIVE OUT THE PAIN IN THE HEAD / Inner-of-Onions Fruit-of-the-am-tree Natron setseft-seeds Bone-of-the-Sword-fish, cooked Redfish, cooked Skull-of-the-Crayfish, cooked Honey abra-ointment ... Smear the Head therewith for four days." The skull is "cooked", one of eight or nine ingredients, and the printed text carries no quantities.

**Related items, printed separately, not counted as the crayfish.** "Crab" is a head word in the list (line 1404), and Bryan quotes a "Bile-of-Many-Crabs" among hair remedies (line 4055); these are different items. "Skull-of-the-Shadfish, cooked in Oil" (line 2344) is a fish, not a crayfish.

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say what the "skull" of a crayfish is (the carapace or head shell is a modern guess), how it was cooked, or how much entered the compound. The crayfish of the Astacidae and Parastacidae are not, in general knowledge, Nile animals (a general statement, not read from Bryan); whether Bryan's word translates an Egyptian name for a crustacean such as a crab, prawn or other animal is not stated. His reasoning was not found. The identity is a modern hypothesis. The 1987 translation and the Egyptian word could change any reading above. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it. In general terms, crustacean shell is made of chitin with calcium carbonate. Nothing in the sources retrieved says that any component acts on a headache. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The one use the sources retrieved show is a cooked crayfish skull as one ingredient of an ointment smeared on the head. It is not supported as a treatment. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only what Bryan prints: "Skull-of-the-Crayfish, cooked", combined with the other ingredients and smeared on the head for four days (lines 2102-2108). No quantity is printed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Allergy:** shellfish allergy is common and can be severe.
- **Hygiene and infection:** animal material and the compound's other ingredients on the skin risk irritation or infection; the compound includes natron.
- **Conservation and law:** some freshwater crayfish are protected, and others are invasive and restricted; which animal was meant is not known from the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
