# Horsefly — 蜚虻 *Fei Meng* (Shennong bencao jing)
## *A biting fly of the horsefly family, Tabanus bivittatus Matsumura (复带虻) in the standard modern identification; medium confidence, because the classic's text gives no description of the insect and the name covers biting flies in general* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The pages of Yang's 1998 translation that carry the insect drugs are not readable on disk, so there is no second witness. The identification is ours. (木虻, "wood horsefly", is a separate entry of the classic, listed just before this one in the middle classic (Sun ed. line 2777), and is not treated here.)

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the middle (中) classic, reads 味苦，微寒 ("bitter, slightly cold"). It 主逐瘀血 ("mainly drives out static blood"), "breaks hard blood accumulations" (破下血积), treats 坚痞症瘕 ("hard lumps, concretions and conglomerations") and 寒热 ("cold and heat"), and 通利血脉及九窍 ("frees the blood vessels and the nine orifices"). The habitat is 生川谷 ("grows in river valleys"). There is no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 蜚虻 *fei meng*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2789.)

**Wu Pu.** No Wu Pu paragraph is printed for this entry.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰：生江夏。五月取。腹有血者，良: "grows in Jiangxia; taken in the fifth month; those with blood in the belly are good." (Line 2791.) This is the only guidance on gathering or quality.

**Sun's note (案).** None is printed for this entry.

**Identity.** The text gives only the name and uses. 蜚虻 names a biting fly. The species *Tabanus bivittatus* is the standard modern identification and is not in the sources. The Mingyi layer's "blood in the belly" suggests, in our reading, a female fly that has fed on blood, since in horseflies only the females bite; the text does not say this. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, an insect's body is mostly protein, chitin and fat. The salivary secretions of horseflies contain anticoagulant substances, but whether any survive drying was not retrieved for this entry, and none is asserted here. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records a bitter, slightly cold insect for static blood, blood accumulations and hard lumps in the abdomen. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends none. Hard abdominal masses and abnormal bleeding need medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives only the harvest: taken in the fifth month, preferring those with blood in the belly (Mingyi layer). It gives no preparation, route, quantity or course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Horseflies (family Tabanidae) are biting flies; the females feed on blood and their bites are painful and can transmit infections mechanically. No medical property of the dried insect is established in the general knowledge used here. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong text proper).** 味苦，微寒: bitter; slightly cold. No toxicity statement is printed.
- Class: middle (中). No organ affinity is printed. No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 蜚虻 in the middle grade (中品); its preface (Sun ed. line 1677) says middle drugs "mainly nourish the nature to correspond with humanity", that some are toxic and some not, and that one should weigh them before use. The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise; it lists static blood and hard lumps. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 蜚虻 *fei meng*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2789.)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A middle-grade insect-drug for static blood. SHORT entry: the sources give little.

### Contraindications

- **Bleeding.** A drug said to drive out blood accumulations is not for anyone who is bleeding, pregnant, or taking anticoagulants; this is a precaution drawn from the stated action, not from a statement in the text. [USER: verify]
- **Abdominal masses and abnormal bleeding.** Need medical assessment.
- **Hygiene.** Wild insects are unstandardised and may be contaminated.
- **Identification.** The name covers more than one biting fly.
