# Seawater wine — Oinos thalassikos (οἶνος θαλασσικός, "sea wine"), Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 5-27

## *Grape wine made with the addition of seawater, in one of three ways Dioscorides describes. The text names grapes and raisins but no species; "Vitis vinifera" is our inference, of low confidence at species level and of high confidence only that the base is a grape wine. The translator's line reads "SUGGESTED: Wine made with Seawater".* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 9910, chapter 5-27 "OINOS THALASSIKOS", in the section the translation heads "WINES" (Book Five).

**Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter reads in full: "Wines are made with seawater in various ways. Some add seawater after the grapes are gathered; others first place them in the sun and tread them, mixing in seawater; others make raisins from the grapes and steep them with seawater in casks. This wine becomes sweet. Those that are made with a harder taste are good against the signs of approaching acute fevers (if there is nothing better at hand), for bringing down the intestines, and for rotten spitters, hard bowels, and anyone affected in the head by Aminaean wines. Yet these are bad for the stomach and cause wind."

Three methods are therefore given: (1) seawater added after the grapes are gathered; (2) grapes first dried in the sun and trodden, with seawater mixed in; (3) raisins steeped with seawater in casks. The text sets the sweet result against "those that are made with a harder taste," without saying which method yields which. The text itself says only that "this wine becomes sweet," and does not say to which of the three it refers. [USER: verify.]

The ingredient seawater has its own chapter, 5-19 *Udor thalassion* (line 9863), which says seawater "is hot, sharp, and bad for the stomach. It troubles the bowels, expelling phlegm." That chapter is covered by the existing seawater entry (II_44, CPU-01304) and is cited here only as context, not as a statement about the wine.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the wine contains ethanol and the sugars, acids and phenolic compounds of grape, plus the dissolved salts of seawater, chiefly sodium chloride. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The harder-tasting wines: against signs of approaching acute fevers "if there is nothing better at hand"; for bringing down the intestines; for "rotten spitters" (the translation's phrase, left as printed); for hard bowels; for anyone affected in the head by Aminaean wines.
- **What the text says against it.** It is "bad for the stomach and cause[s] wind."
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no duration, no ratio of seawater to wine.

The Codex gives no protocol. Drinking seawater-adulterated wine carries a salt load, and the record is of historical interest only.

### Preparation & Dosing

The three methods above are the only preparation given. No quantity of seawater, grape, raisin or must, no length of steeping, and no dose to be drunk is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.] **Do not use any figure from this entry.**

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Seawater is a salt solution about 3.5 per cent by weight of dissolved salts, mostly sodium chloride, and drinking it in quantity is harmful. Wine is a fermented grape product. Adding seawater to wine or must is a recorded ancient practice, but this entry draws no conclusion from outside the text. The classical indications have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** No quality (warming, cooling, astringent) is printed for the wine. The only qualities in the chapter are the taste terms "sweet" and "harder", and the two statements "bad for the stomach" and "cause wind." No tradition-specific classification is imported.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and no cultivation status is attested for this wine. What the chapter offers is a record of craft variation: three ways of making one product, and a plain statement of its faults.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here supports a cultivation use. It is a record of an ancient adulteration-by-design, written down with its own warning. A cultivator can take from it that the author lists the harm ("bad for the stomach and cause wind") next to the use.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

The bodily claims in this entry fall on the bowels, the stomach, the head and the fever state. For the harder-tasting wines the text gives use against the signs of approaching acute fevers, if nothing better is at hand; for bringing down the intestines; for "rotten spitters", a phrase the translation keeps as printed and the entry does not explain; for hard bowels; and for anyone affected in the head by Aminaean wines. Against these, the text says the wine is bad for the stomach and causes wind. Dioscorides names no humour, dosha or qi channel and prints no warm, cool, moist or dry quality for the wine. The chapter does not say which of its three making methods gives the harder wine, so no organ is attached to any method. The Codex records these as historical indications only. Seawater adds a salt load, so anyone restricting salt or with heart, kidney or blood-pressure disease should avoid it, and acute fevers and head complaints need medical care.

### Contraindications

- **Alcohol.** The usual cautions for alcohol apply (pregnancy, liver disease, medication, driving, dependency).
- **Salt load.** Seawater added to a drink raises its sodium content; avoid in anyone told to restrict salt, with heart, kidney or blood-pressure conditions. The text itself says the wine is bad for the stomach.
- **Fevers.** "Acute fevers" are medical emergencies; this entry is no guide to treating them.
