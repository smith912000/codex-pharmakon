### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** Bryan's Chapter V (Mineral Remedies) list (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` lines 1155-1161; `extract/names.csv` row "Copper", line 1155) gives the forms "Copper, Copper-coal, Copper-rust, Copper-shavings, Copper-vitriol" and "Copper-verdigris", and "Verdigris" separately (line 1210). Bryan's running prose then shows them in recipes, paraphrased and not in the original wording: verdigris ground with bread, made into "three Pills" and swallowed with sweet beer (line 1685); a poultice of "Mint-of-the-Mountains dissolved in Hippopotamus Oil along with Copper and Lead-vitriol" for a stubborn headache (line 2112); a plaster in which "Copper-splinters" are rubbed in, "to use on the fifth day" (lines 2268-2269); "Chips-of-Verdigris" with collyrium, cow-fat and honey "to make the flesh grow" (lines 2399-2405); "Copperrust" with honey, milk, olive oil and collyrium in an enema (line 2636); verdigris in several eye remedies (lines 2735, 2747, 2779, 2823); "Copper Coal" in a pill for a growth (lines 3826-3828); and "Copper-shavings" and "Verdigris" in a poultice for a neck tumour (line 3858). One passage is a spoken formula: "Come, Verdigris! Come, Verdigris! Come, Thou Fresh One! Come, Efflux from the Eye of the god Horus!" **Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) has no entry for copper metal. Two copper materials are printed. Shi Dan (Cuprus Sulphate), in "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (line 1098; `names.csv` row): "sour and a little cold", it "mainly brightens the eyes"; Yang's note 52 says "This medicinal is toxic and erosive. **Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Tamra* is in the *Trapvadi* group of *Sutrasthana* ch.38 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 440): "Trapu, Sisa, Tamra, Rajata, Krishna-Lauha, Suvarna and Lohamala". The metrical text (lines 443-448) calls the group "a good vermifuge" with "the virtue of neutralising the effects of chemical poison originated through incompatible combinations", ranging over "jaundice, chlorosis, Meha ... Hridroga (heart disease), thirst and maladies incidental to the effects of poison". In the metal group of ch.46 (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 2128-2130): "Copper has a sweet and astringent taste, and acts as a liquefacient and corrosive agent. It is laxative and cooling in its potency." *Tuttha* (Bhishagratna: sulphate of copper; `names.csv`, Vol. III Appendix 3 line 33170) is in the *Ushakadi* group with *Tutthaka* (ch.38 lines 276-277). Elsewhere: "powders of dead iron and dead copper, Tuttha (Sulphate of copper), lead, bell-metal, Rasanjana" are pasted with honey into sticks used as an *anjana* (collyrium) on the eye (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch12.txt` lines 69-72); copper is named among the ingredients of an eye compound with "Anjana (black Antimony), Tuttha (Sulphate of copper), and copper" (`SS_Uttara_ch11.txt` lines 40-42; the sentence runs past the line cited and its stated use was not read), and in a powder with iron and *Srotonjana* stuffed in a ram's horn (`SS_Uttara_ch15.txt` lines 144-146); a superficial tumour is "covered with thin leaves of zinc, copper, lead, or of iron" before cauterisation (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt` lines 411-413); the iron-preparation method of *Chikitsasthana* ch.10 is "the mode of medically preparing all kinds of Loha (zinc, copper, lead and gold)" (`SS_Chikitsa_ch10.txt` lines 207-208); and the juice of "a Soma plant belonging to any other species" is to be taken "in a copper or an earthen pot" (`SS_Chikitsa_ch29.txt` lines 264-266), where copper is a vessel and not a drug.

### Active Components

Copper is the element Cu, a reddish metal and an essential trace element in small amounts. Exposed to acetic acid (vinegar) vapour it forms green-blue basic copper acetates, which is verdigris; exposed to air and moisture it forms oxides and carbonates. Copper sulphate (the blue crystalline salt, *tuttha* in the Sanskrit gloss above) is water-soluble. Soluble copper salts are irritant to the gut and toxic in quantity. The traditional "burnt" or "ash" preparations are not standardised and their composition is not given in the lines read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources use copper and its salts as external astringents and eye applications, as emetics and corrosives, as vessels, and in one case as a spoken substance; none supports taking copper for health. Copper salts and verdigris are toxic, preparations are unstandardised, and the Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation. Ritual and spoken uses (the Egyptian verdigris formula) are recorded as ritual.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only preparations the texts give. DMM 5-87: burnt brass from ships' nails with sulphur and salt, repeated three times, washed "like cadmia, the water changed four times a day until no froth stands on top"; decoction taken with honey water to induce vomiting. DMM 5-91: verdigris scraped from brass held over vinegar for ten days. DMM 5-88: "forty grains" of flowers of brass "extracts thick fluids" (the only quantity read). SS: powdered "dead" copper in collyrium sticks pasted with honey (Uttara ch.12); thin copper leaves as a covering before cautery (Chikitsa ch.18). EP (Bryan, paraphrase): verdigris in three pills with bread and sweet beer, no amounts given in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved for SS or EP. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Copper compounds are astringent and antimicrobial, which is consistent with their use on ulcers and eyes, and copper sulphate was long used as an emetic; both effects come with irritation and toxicity. Verdigris and copper sulphate are poisonous if swallowed. No clinical studies were retrieved for the traditional uses. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(b) Framed, thinly.* The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. The one frame the copper material sits inside is *heka*: in Bryan's rendering a formula is spoken over verdigris (lines 2829-2839), so spell and drug are components of one intervention and the recitation is half the prescription. No cultivation status for copper or verdigris is attested in the lines read.

**Assyrian (AT).** *(c) Absent, no locus.* Nothing was read. The blocking warning is in force. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What these chapters offer a cultivator is the discipline of attention: where the best is made (Memphis, then Cyprus, 5-87), how verdigris is produced, how it is adulterated with pumice, marble or "calcanthum", and how each fraud is told (by wetting the thumb, by crushing between the teeth, by fire, 5-91). Two chapters carry warnings in their headings (5-92 "RISK OF BLOOD POISONING"; 5-104 "TOXIC").

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent, and the attestation rests on a spine row.* No Avicenna text or list row was found. Innate heat and radical moisture cannot be tied to copper without a source and are not tied to it here. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* Copper metal has no entry. The one copper compound printed, Shi Dan (Cuprus Sulphate), stands in "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (the heading is at line 1097), and the preface describes the middle class as "mainly nurture personality" with drugs that "may or may not be toxic", to be weighed before use (lines 385-388). Shi Dan's own line says "Taking it after it is sublimated may prevent senility, while protrated taking may increase longevity and make one an immortal" (lines 1102-1104, the OCR's spelling kept), but this is the line for the sulphate, and it is not extended to copper. Xi Tong Jing Bi sits among the inferior-class pages (line 1350). Yang's note to the preface says "it was principally the Daoists who created the early materia medica literature", concerned with "the achievement of 'immortality'" through "elixirs" (lines 375-378); the longevity language on Shi Dan belongs to that context.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* *Tamra* is claimed by *dravyaguna* (the *Trapvadi* group and the ch.46 metal group) and not by *rasayana*. The *rasayana* chapters searched (*Chikitsasthana* ch.27-30) name copper only as a vessel for another plant's juice (ch.29 lines 264-266). The one longevity sentence near copper is in the *Kushtha* chapter, ch.10, and attaches to the iron compound (a *Tula* of it "makes the patient live for one hundred years"), with the method then said to serve "all kinds of Loha (zinc, copper, lead and gold)" (lines 207-208); that is not a *rasayana* chapter and is not read as assigning copper a status. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim for copper is printed in the lines read.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing here for a cultivator to take. Copper is, in these sources, an astringent and eye applications material, a toxin to be handled with care, a vessel, and in Egypt a substance spoken over. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Copper salts, verdigris and "burnt" copper preparations are poisonous if swallowed (vomiting, gut and liver injury); the Dioscorides headings themselves warn ("RISK OF BLOOD POISONING", "TOXIC"). Traditional ash and *bhasma* preparations are unstandardised and may carry other toxic metals. Sale and use of such preparations is regulated in many places. Spoken and ritual uses are ritual, not therapy.
