### Origin & Tradition

Fischer's spurge is a perennial *Euphorbia* of Northeast Asia (Manchuria, northern China, Siberia) whose thick root is the Chinese materia medica drug **Lang Du** (狼毒) — "wolf's poison," a name shared historically with a few drastic toxic roots. Like all spurges it bears the family's **caustic, irritant latex** and the characteristic toxic diterpene esters. It is one of the frankly poisonous, drastic drugs of the Chinese pharmacy, used only in small, carefully handled (often processed) amounts and within compound formulae.

In Chinese Medicine Lang Du is a **drastic agent to disperse cold-phlegm, kill parasites, and break up stubborn masses and accumulations** — applied to hard nodules, chronic skin lesions, parasitic conditions, and as a powerful external agent. Its action is harsh and its toxicity real; the tradition treats it as a specialist drug requiring processing to reduce its acridity. In modern times *E. fischeriana* has attracted significant research attention because its diterpenoids (including ent-abietane and jolkinolide-type compounds) show **antitumour/cytotoxic activity** — placing this "wolf's poison" among the *Euphorbia* species of contemporary anticancer interest.

The plant exemplifies the caustic-spurge register (with European spurge, this volume) in its specifically Chinese, mass-dispersing/antiparasitic application.
