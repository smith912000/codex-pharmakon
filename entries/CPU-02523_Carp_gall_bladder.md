# Carp gall bladder — Li Yu Dan 鲤鱼胆 (Shennong bencao jing)

## *The gall bladder of the common carp, Cyprinus carpio L.; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence for the fish (the Sun Xingyan note's own glosses give "red carp" among the colours of li), though the text names the organ and not the species* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The only text read for this entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. A search of the Dioscorides, Clark, Men-Tsee-Khang, Sushruta, Ebers, Assyrian and Avicenna texts on disk for "carp" found no use of the carp as a drug; the Sushruta translation prints a fish name beginning "Bohita" with the genus *Cyprinus* beside it in its fish lists (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, line 30880), which is a glossed fish name and not an entry for this organ and is not tagged here.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 鲤鱼胆, "carp gall", in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 鲤鱼胆 *li yu dan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1529). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，寒 ("bitter, cold"); 主目热赤痛青盲 ("governs heat, red and painful eyes, and blue-blindness"), 明目 ("brightens the eyes"); 久服，强悍、益志气 ("taken long: strong and tough, benefits will and qi"); habitat 生池泽 ("grows in pools and marshes"). The transcription prints the last two characters of the clause twice (志气志气), a copying fault, and the Codex reads the clause once. "Blue-blindness" (青盲) is the classic's term for blindness with a normal-looking eye; the Codex does not map it to a modern diagnosis.

The Mingyi bielu layer adds: 生九江，采无时, "grows at Jiujiang; gathered at no fixed time" (line 1531).

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 1533) is entirely about the fish. It quotes the Shuowen (鲤, with a second name whose character is missing in the transcription), the Erya (鲤 followed by a character missing), the commentator Sheren, and Guo Pu, who glosses li as "today's red carp" (今赤鲤鱼) and glosses the second name as "a big fish like the xun (sturgeon)". Sun says Guo Pu was wrong to treat them as two (据此，知郭璞别为二，非矣). The note ends with the Gujin zhu: the people of Yanzhou call the red carp "red ji", the blue carp "blue horse", the black carp "dark colt", the white carp "white qi" and the yellow carp "yellow zhi". These are statements about the fish and not the gall.

The paragraph is followed by the subtotal 上虫、鱼。上品一十种, "upper insects and fish: upper grade, ten kinds" (line 1535), which confirms that the compiler counts it in the upper grade.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, fish bile contains bile salts; carp and other cyprinid fishes are known to carry a sulfated bile alcohol (cyprinol sulfate) as the main bile component, which is toxic to the kidney and liver in people. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, cold gall for heat, redness and pain in the eye and for blue-blindness, with "brightening the eyes" as its action and strength and benefit to "will and qi" when taken long. The Codex does not recommend taking or applying carp bile in any form: see Contraindications.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph names the organ and no preparation, route or dose; the Mingyi layer says only that it may be gathered "at no fixed time". The classic's general preface says medicines differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste (line 4121), and gives no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Eating the raw gall bladder of carp (including the grass carp and other cyprinid fishes) has been reported as a cause of acute poisoning with kidney and liver injury. This is a documented hazard of the practice and not a quirk of one text. No clinical studies of benefit were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味苦，寒, "bitter, cold" (line 1529). No other quality is printed for this organ in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The paragraph for 鲤鱼胆 reads 味苦，寒 and sits in the upper classic (上经), whose preface (line 43) says upper medicines "nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic, and taken much or long do not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body and benefit qi, not age and extend the years, base it on the upper classic" (主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经). On the grading rule used for this series, placement in the upper classic is itself the ground for (a). The gall's own 久服 clause reads 久服，强悍、益志气, "taken long: strong and tough, benefits will and qi". That is a long-use clause, but it does not promise lightening of the body, freedom from ageing or extension of years, so the grade rests on the placement and not on the clause; the Codex does not reach for a stronger reading. The Mingyi bielu adds only the place and the timing. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 鲤鱼胆 *li yu dan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1529)

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing here for a cultivator to do. The classic's wording for this organ is a long-use clause that does not carry a life-nourishing promise, and the practice it describes is hazardous in the light of what is known today. The honest use of the entry is as a historical record. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Do not eat or apply carp gall.** Ingestion of raw carp gall bladder is a recognised cause of acute kidney and liver injury and can be fatal; the traditional "eye" use is not a reason to try it.
- **Eye disease:** redness, pain or loss of vision needs medical assessment.
- **Hygiene:** raw fish organs carry parasites and bacteria.
- **Ethics and sourcing:** the Codex does not recommend harming any animal for these uses.
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given.
