# Honey, Pauttika — Pauttikam (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's "Puttikas" bees)
## *A kind of honey that Sushruta's translator defines as honey from the hives of large, yellow bees; the bee is not named to species in the text, and any Apis binomial is ours and low confidence; the substance is a distinct kind of honey from Sushruta's list of eight* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** Historical record only. Sushruta says honey of every kind becomes "positively injurious" after contact with heat or fire, and that undigested honey "is as fatal as any poison" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 1183-1203 and 1221-1228). Nothing here is a recommendation.

**Single-text entry.** Only the *Sushruta Samhita* is attested for this kind of honey on the project's spine (CPU-02384), and no other text on disk was found to name it. The row began on 13 October 2026 as an unverified placeholder from `_nonplant/nonplant_master.csv` (curated 8 October, confidence clear); this entry replaces it. The general honey entry (CPU-00446, `III_13_Honey.md`) covers honey across eight traditions; this entry treats only the single kind that Sushruta names (*Pauttikam*).

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The honey group opens (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 1076-1092): "Honey is sweet, and leaves an astringent after-taste. It is parchifying, cold, stomachic, cosmetic, tonic, light, softening, palatable, liquefacient (Lekhanan), and fermenting (Sandhanam)", and it "pacifies the deranged Pittam and Kapham" (Bhishagratna's English). A metrical text then says that "Eight different kinds of honey are commonly used", naming Pauttikam, Bhramaram, Kshaudram, Makshikam, Chhatram, Arghyam, Auddalakam and Dalam (lines 1093-1102). A translator's footnote (lines 1120-1142) defines each kind by its bee or its source. 

**The passage on Pauttikam.** The name appears in the list of eight kinds (line 1094, "the Pauttikam") and is defined in the translator's footnote (lines 1120-1121): "The kind of honey obtained from hives of large, yellow bees is called the Pauttikam." The body text (lines 1103-1110) calls the bees "the Puttikas" (the spelling printed in Bhishagratna) and says: "the honey obtained from the hives of bees, known as the Puttikas, is hot and parchifying owing to their habit of sucking the juice or sap of flowers and plants without eliminating therefrom other foreign or poisonous matter, that might have become naturally or accidentally mixed with it. This kind of honey is intoxicating and acid in its re-action, and tends to aggravate the Vayu, blood and Pittam. It acts as a liquefacient or discutient agent." It is therefore the first of the eight in the order of the text and the only one described mainly by what it aggravates.

**Identity.** The footnote's "large, yellow bees" is the only description of the animal. The text gives no Latin name, and the project's `check` guess of "Apis sp." is a genus-level hypothesis only. The general honey entry (`III_13_Honey.md`, lines 96-103) assigns Apis species to the Sushruta kinds; those assignments were not found in the lines read here and are not adopted. Confidence in the identification of the substance as honey is high; confidence in the bee is none. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Pauttika honey is a bee product like other honey. The Sushruta text names no constituent. Honey in general is mainly the sugars fructose and glucose with water, small amounts of other sugars, organic acids, enzymes and traces of pollen and minerals; its composition depends on the flowers visited and on the bee. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text does not say.** It gives no dose, no duration and no preparation for this kind of honey.

The Codex does not translate Sushruta's categories (*Vayu*, *Pittam*, *Kapham*, *Meha*) into modern diagnoses. Honey is a food; this particular kind cannot be sourced or identified in a way that would let anyone choose it. The entry offers no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The passage on this kind gives no preparation and no dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] The honey group as a whole carries general rules: honey is not to be heated or boiled, and not used "in a hot country, or during the hot season of the year, or in a heated state of the body" (lines 1183-1203); rain-water also makes honey injurious, except Arghyam honey (lines 1203-1207); honey for emetic use may be given with a hot substance if it is ejected at once (lines 1208-1214). Honey also "partakes of the virtues of the drugs or substances with which it is so used" (*Yoga-Vahika*, lines 1176-1178). These are rules for honey as a group, not for this kind alone.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Honey is a concentrated sugar solution with antimicrobial properties from low water activity, acidity and hydrogen peroxide produced by an enzyme, and it is used as a topical dressing in modern wound care. Honey can carry spores of *Clostridium botulinum* and must not be given to infants under one year. Honey from some plants (for example certain rhododendrons) can contain grayanotoxins and cause poisoning. Sushruta's remark that honey is made "poisonous" by the bees' foraging has no precise modern equivalent. The Ayurvedic distinctions between kinds of honey have not been validated here. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The honey group stands in *Sutrasthana* ch.45, the chapter on the properties of liquids, and not in a chapter on *rasayana* or *vajikarana*. The group's opening lines call honey "tonic" and "aphrodisiac", and the line on fresh honey calls it "constructive and aphrodisiac" (lines 1076-1092 and 1165-1167), but these are properties of honey in general, not a placement of (*Pauttikam*) in a rejuvenation or vitality discipline. Nothing was read that names this kind in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* is made. The sattvic, rajasic and tamasic food lists are Samkhya and later categories and are not Sushrutan.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. The only property the text gives for this kind is that it is heating and aggravating, so on the tradition's own account it is the kind to avoid in a *Pitta* or hot condition. A cultivator has no use for it.

### Contraindications

- **Heat.** Sushruta forbids honey that has been heated, boiled or used in hot weather or a hot body (lines 1183-1203). Treat that as the tradition's own warning.
- **Undigested honey** is described as "as fatal as any poison" (lines 1221-1228).
- **Infants.** Never give honey to a child under one year (infant botulism); this is modern general knowledge, not from the text.
- **Diabetes and sugar restriction.** Honey is sugar.
- **Allergy** to bee products.
- **Identity.** The kind of bee cannot be identified from the text. [USER: verify]
