# Hordeum murinum — Phoinix (Dioscorides 4-43); "Wall Barley"

## *Hordeum murinum, the only binomial on the translators' SUGGESTED line for chapter 4-43, glossed "Wall Barley"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk names this plant under this name. The entry rests on one chapter of one text. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed under the chapter heading, and not Dioscorides' own identification. The Codex does not state the identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-43. PHOINIX* at line 8384, SUGGESTED line at 8385, text beginning at line 8386. A page break (page numbers 584 and 585, and a plate caption for *Equisetum* after Fuchs) falls inside the chapter, and the text resumes at line 8398 ("on newly-mortared roofs."). The chapter stands in Book Four, "Other Herbs & Roots", before 4-44 *IDAIA RHIZA*, a different chapter. The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 623.

The whole chapter reads: "Phoenix has leaves similar to barley only shorter and narrower, with an ear [of seed] similar to lolium [2-116, 4-140]; branches around the root six fingers in length, and the ears seven or eight. It grows in fields and on newly-mortared roofs. A decoction (taken as a drink in hard wine) it is able to stop discharges of the intestines, the excessive discharges of blood from the womb, and excessive urine. Some say that it is a blood-stauncher, bound in red wool and hanged about one. It is also called rhus, anchinops, phoenicopteron, rhus stachyos, or ostheles. The Romans say palolucupinum, the Egyptians, athnon." The square brackets are the translators'.

So the chapter gives a description for recognition (leaves like barley but shorter and narrower; an ear like *lolium*; branches six fingers long around the root; seven or eight ears), a habitat (fields and newly mortared roofs), one preparation (a decoction in hard wine) and three claims for it (it stops discharges of the intestines, excessive bleeding from the womb and excessive urine). It then records a saying, introduced by "Some say", that the plant is a blood-stauncher when bound in red wool and hung about the person. Dioscorides reports the saying and does not adopt it in his own voice. Recording is not endorsing. The five Greek names and the Roman and Egyptian names are printed as quoted. The Egyptian name is a name recorded by Dioscorides and not an Egyptian text.

**Overlap.** The same Greek heading *PHOINIX* stands on a different chapter, 1-148 (line 4602), whose translators' gloss is the date palm (*Phoenix dactylifera*), and the name index lists it separately (names.csv line 150). The two chapters share only the Greek word. Nothing is merged, and the date-palm chapter is not read as this plant. The wall-barley binomial has no other entry in the entries folder (chapter-level and name search). [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The text prints no constituent and no analysis of the plant Dioscorides describes was read. It is a general fact that *Hordeum* belongs to the grass family (Poaceae). Nothing is stated here about the composition of the translators' wall barley. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records a wine decoction against intestinal discharges, excessive uterine bleeding and excessive urine, and a reported saying about a bound amulet. These are Dioscorides' claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol for bleeding, and an amulet is not a treatment for it. Excessive bleeding from the womb is a condition for medical care.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives one preparation: "A decoction (taken as a drink in hard wine)". It prints no quantity, frequency or duration, and it does not say which part is boiled. The amulet is "bound in red wool and hanged about one". No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] It is a general, well-established fact that heavy uterine bleeding has many causes and needs medical assessment, and that a bound plant does not stop bleeding.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** chapter 4-43 prints no hot, cold, moist or dry quality and no taste. The only qualities printed are physical (the leaves "shorter and narrower" than barley) and the strength of the wine ("hard wine").

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a description for recognition (leaves against barley, an ear against *lolium*, the lengths of the branches and the number of ears) and a habitat that is unusual enough to be a field mark: fields and newly mortared roofs. The chapter also records, as something "some say", an amuletic use, bound in red wool. The spec notes that he sometimes records such uses while distancing himself, and here the report is attributed to others. The chapter assigns the plant no cultivation status. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The transferable point is the habit of noting where a plant grows as part of recognising it. The amulet is reported, not recommended. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

Chapter 4-43 prints no warning. The identification is only the translators' suggestion, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Heavy bleeding, intestinal discharges and excessive urine need medical care and must not be left to a drink or an amulet. The preparation is in hard wine, which is not suitable for everyone. The text gives no dose, and no safety information for pregnancy was read, though the text itself uses the plant for bleeding from the womb. [USER: verify]
