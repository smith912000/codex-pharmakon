# Musk deer — gla.rtsi (musk; Men-Tsee-Khang "La tsi"), and the musk deer as a game animal, rGyud-bzhi
## *The musk deer and its product, musk; Clark's text gives "musk" and a bracketed statement that it "is obtained from the testicles of the musk deer"; the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary names the deer "Moschus chrysogaster"; the spine's source list gives two other binomials* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Musk is entered in the Explanatory Tantra's materia medica (ch.20) in Clark 1995: "gla.rtsi: Musk: [has twenty names. It is obtained from the testicles of the musk deer. The granular type is superior, whilst the inferior type is non-granular.] It cures poisoning, worms, disorders of the kidneys and liver, septic conditions [and infectious disorders (including infectious fevers,) disorders of the eyes and channels, diseases caused by nagas and spirits, and urinary retention]" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, line 887; `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` row 48). The bracketed parts are Clark's commentary on the root verse, not the verse itself. Musk is then placed in groups. It is in the bitter-taste group (Clark line 768: "neem, Indian gentian, Aconitum balfouri, Picrorhiza kurroa, bitter cucumber, Conessi (Kurchi), musk, (animal) bile ..."); in the class of medicines "which neutralize poisons", first among them (line 1212); in "the category of anthelmintics", first (line 1221); and, among the supplementary additives for combined disorders, "musk and Indian Bdellium resin for infections and spirit disorders" (line 1383).

The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation transliterates the same medicine "La tsi" (Wylie gla.rtsi, as in Clark; the identification of "La tsi" with Clark's musk rests on the sound and on matching indications, and no line read states it). Its Explanatory Tantra says: "La tsi is an antidote against poison and it cures disorders caused by ein and cures disorder of the kidney, liver and infectious diseases" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, line 3384; "ein" is printed as shown and is probably an OCR error or a transliteration the lines do not explain). It stands in the bitter-taste group (line 3213), first in the group "that cure poisoning" (lines 3603-3605), among medicines for a disorder printed "cm disorders" in the OCR (line 3638), and among the additions made for "infection and disorders caused by evil spirits" (line 4276). The book's index gives "Latsi 187, 201, 220, 222, 267" (line 7415).

As an animal the musk deer appears in the meat classification: "The deer, musk deer, gazelle, argali, rabbit and antelope are the group of herbivorous wild animals" (Men-Tsee-Khang line 2907; index "musk deer 7, 161", line 7447); Clark's version places "musk deer" among the game animals (line 672). The text of the opening description of the medicine city names "elephants, bears, musk deer" among animals "bearing excellent medicines" (Clark line 111; Men-Tsee-Khang line 786). The glossary of animals reads "Musk Deer", paired in order with "Moschus chrysogaster" in an OCR-scrambled table (Men-Tsee-Khang lines 6997 and 7001). The dry-land game meat is, per Clark's chapter, cool, light and coarse in power (line 673). A paraphrase in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/FMT_Original_Tibetan.md` (lines 309-320, labelled "Chapter 25") gives musk a warm nature, "emergency revivification" and a "Shen-emergency opener" role; that file is a Codex paraphrase, its chapter number does not match Clark's chapter 20, the properties are not in the Clark or Men-Tsee-Khang lines read (Clark places musk in the bitter group, line 768), and they are not used here.

**Identity.** The deer is the musk deer; the species is not settled across the Codex's own sources. The Men-Tsee-Khang glossary gives *Moschus chrysogaster*, while `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` lines 94-95 list "Musk (Forest Musk Deer), Moschus berezovskii" and "Musk (Siberian Musk Deer), Moschus moschiferus". The statement that musk comes from the "testicles" is Clark's bracketed gloss as printed; musk is the secretion of a gland of the male deer, so the word is repeated here as printed and not endorsed. ### Active Components

Natural musk is a waxy secretion of the male musk deer's musk gland, containing the macrocyclic ketone muscone as its odorant, with steroids, fatty acids and proteins. Its odour has made it a perfume material; most commercial musk now is synthetic. Composition varies by source, and no analysis specific to this entry was read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources record musk as a medicine for poisoning, worms, kidney and liver complaints, infections and "spirit" disorders, and they treat the musk deer as an animal of the medicine forest. Wild musk deer are endangered and are protected by national and international law, and obtaining natural musk means killing the animal or taking its gland. The Codex does not recommend natural musk for any purpose and gives no protocol. Synthetic musk compounds are a separate matter and are not discussed in the sources.

### Preparation & Dosing

Clark's entry describes the grade of the product: "The granular type is superior, whilst the inferior type is non-granular" (line 887). The tradition's recommended supplementary use is as an additive for infections and spirit disorders in compound medicines (Clark line 1383; Men-Tsee-Khang line 4276). No preparation or dose is given for musk in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Muscone is a recognised odorant; musk has been used in traditional medicines in several regions, but wild musk deer are threatened by hunting for the gland. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Clark places musk in the group of medicines "having bitter taste" (line 768); the Men-Tsee-Khang text places La tsi in the bitter-taste group (line 3213). No potency (cool or warm) is printed for musk in the lines read. Its stated actions: neutralises poison; anthelmintic; for disorders of the kidneys and liver, infections, and spirit-caused disorders (Clark lines 887, 1212, 1221, 1383). The meat of the musk deer as game falls in the herbivorous wild animal group (Men-Tsee-Khang line 2907), whose meat is cool and light (Men-Tsee-Khang line 2923 and Clark line 673 for dry-land animals). No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Musk is a substance of the Explanatory Tantra's materia medica, with a stated taste group and stated actions, but none of the lines read connects it with *bcud len* (essence extraction) or with the roots the tradition gives the three humors (attachment, hatred and obscuration; Clark line 51). The "spirit" disorders that musk is said to cure are named as disorders in the materia medica and are treated as clinical categories there; the lines read give no practice role. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition, and none is asserted here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. Musk is a poison-antidote and anthelmintic in the tradition's materia medica, and wild musk deer are endangered. The honest reading is a historical one: the tradition valued the product, and the modern consequence is to leave it alone.

### Contraindications

- **Conservation and law:** musk deer are endangered, listed under CITES, and protected in the countries where they live. Trade in natural musk is restricted or illegal. [USER: verify]
- **Poisoning and quality:** the text itself treats musk as a medicine against poison, and nothing in it makes natural musk safe to take; no safe amount is given.
- **Identity:** the species and the product's source tissue vary across the Codex's sources and the glossary table is scrambled; do not rely on any one of them. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy and other contexts:** no statement is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** natural musk requires killing or capturing the animal; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
