### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan (rGyud-bzhi / FMT).** *Notopterygium incisum* is a high-altitude umbellifer of the Tibetan plateau and the Gansu–Qinghai–Sichuan alpine belt; its rhizome and root enter the Tibetan and Sino-Tibetan formulary as a warming, dispersing anti-*bi* (obstruction) agent used against wind-cold-damp painful conditions — the aches, stiffness and cold-damp joint pain characteristic of a cold, wet, high-altitude environment. Its growth in exactly those regions of high altitude (Gansu, Tibet) is part of its traditional identity as a plateau medicine.

**Chinese Medicine (Qiāng Huó 羌活).** In the Chinese tradition the root and rhizome are **Qiāng Huó**, acrid, bitter and warm, associated with the Kidney and Urinary Bladder channels. It is a principal **wind-cold-damp-dispersing / exterior-releasing** herb: it releases the exterior and disperses cold, expels wind-damp, and unblocks painful obstruction (*bi* syndrome) — classically directed to the **upper body, the neck, shoulders and the occipital / upper-back region** and to the *tai-yang* channel. It is the herb reached for in wind-cold exterior patterns with body aches, headache (especially occipital), and stiff, painful neck and shoulders, and in wind-cold-damp joint pain. It is frequently paired with Du Huo (which is directed to the lower body) to cover the whole body in *bi* syndrome.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the plateau wind-breaker.** The signature of Qiang Huo is a plant that thrives in cold, wind-scoured, high places and carries that same dispersing, penetrating quality into the body: an acrid-warm, aromatic root that "opens the exterior," drives out lodged wind, cold and damp, and unblocks the channels of the upper body where cold wind bites first — the nape, the shoulders, the occiput. The doctrinal reading of a "wind-mover for the upper body" maps onto real chemistry: the root is rich in coumarins (notably notopterol and isoimperatorin) and a volatile oil, and the coumarin notopterol has demonstrable anti-inflammatory activity on the NF-κB pathway, giving a plausible mechanistic basis for the traditional anti-*bi*, pain-relieving use.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Positioned as a **`[RESET]`-phase decongestant / anti-obstruction herb** — for stiffness, cold-damp aching and blocked upper-body channels, not for performance:

1. **Wind-cold-damp obstruction (core use).** Relieving stiff, aching, cold-damp joint and muscle pain, especially of the neck, shoulders and upper back — the classic *bi*-syndrome and occipital-headache indication.
2. **Exterior-releasing / diaphoretic.** In wind-cold exterior patterns with body aches and chills.
3. **Upper-body directional pair.** Traditionally combined with Du Huo (lower body) to address whole-body wind-damp pain.

No ergogenic claim: this is a dispersing, pain-relieving anti-obstruction herb. Its notopterol-based anti-inflammatory actions are preclinical and are framed as such.

### Scientific Correlation

Modern evidence is **preclinical / mechanistic** (cell and animal models and review-level ethnopharmacology); there are no efficacy-establishing human clinical trials. According to PubMed:

- **Ethnopharmacology / phytochemistry review.** Azietaku et al. (2017, *J Ethnopharmacol*) systematically reviewed *N. incisum*, documenting its traditional use for headache, rheumatoid arthritis, cold and as a diaphoretic, and cataloguing its coumarin constituents (notopterol, isoimperatorin, bergapten and others) with reported analgesic, anti-inflammatory, anti-cancer and antioxidant effects — while explicitly noting the need for more pharmacokinetic and toxicological study. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2017.03.022)
- **Notopterol — anti-inflammatory mechanism.** Zhou et al. (2023, *J Immunol*) showed notopterol inhibits the NF-κB pathway and activates PI3K/Akt/Nrf2 signalling in periodontal tissue, suppressing inflammatory mediators (IL-1β, IL-8, IL-32) in LPS-stimulated human gingival fibroblasts and limiting osteoclast-driven bone loss in vivo. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4049/jimmunol.2200727)
- **Notopterol — hypothesised broader anti-inflammatory role.** Nazari-Khanamiri & Ghasemnejad-Berenji (2022, *J Basic Clin Physiol Pharmacol*) is a **hypothesis letter** proposing notopterol's cytokine-suppressing (JAK/STAT) actions could be relevant in COVID-19 — noted here only as a hypothesis, not evidence of efficacy. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/jbcpp-2022-0028)

Evidence grade: **preclinical.** The anti-inflammatory mechanism is well characterised in laboratory models and is consistent with the traditional anti-*bi* / anti-arthritic use, but clinical efficacy in humans is not established.

### Contraindications

- **Yin deficiency / blood deficiency with heat (CM).** An acrid-warm, drying, dispersing herb is inappropriate where there is heat or dryness from deficiency; it can aggravate.
- **Excessive dosing.** Its acrid-dispersing, aromatic nature can cause nausea or GI upset; keep within the traditional range.
- **Furanocoumarin phototoxicity.** The root contains furanocoumarins (bergapten etc.); phototoxic/photosensitising and potential drug-interaction (CYP) considerations apply in principle, especially with concentrated extracts. [USER: verify before any topical/photosensitising claim.]
- **Pregnancy.** Treat as caution — a strong dispersing herb; use only under qualified guidance. [USER: verify]
- **Isolated notopterol.** An experimental compound with no established human safety profile; do not extrapolate laboratory data into therapeutic claims.
