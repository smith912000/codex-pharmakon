### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under the Sanskrit name Somaraji.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Bhishagratna's English translation (1907 to 1916), read in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch05.txt`, in the footnote file `SS_Kalpa_ch06.txt` and in the volume files `Bhishagratna_Vol{2,3}_djvu.txt`. **How the identification was checked.** The glossary "APPENDIX" (Vol. III, leaf 79) prints the Sanskrit names, then the identifications. The name block opens with "Soma-ra'ji" (line 33023), followed by "Snuhi", "Srotonjana", "Sthala-padma", "Sthulaila", and so on. The identification block (line 33049 region) begins "Vernonia Anthelnnntica.", then "Euphorbia Neriifolia.", "Antimony.", "Hibiscus Mutabilis.", "Amomum Subulatum." The rest of the leaf serves as a control: the next names *Snuhi*, *Srotonjana*, *Sthala-padma* and *Sthulaila* are followed by *Euphorbia neriifolia*, "Antimony", *Hibiscus mutabilis* and *Amomum subulatum* in the printed order, and a later identification line reads "See 'Snuhi'" in the slot of the following name. The columns line up in order, so *Soma-raji* is paired with *Vernonia anthelmintica*. The match depends on the order of OCR'd columns and not on a printed pairing. **Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The name is attested in the places that were read.

1. *Antidote for snake-bite (Kalpa ch.5, `SS_Kalpa_ch05.txt` lines 506 to 509; Vol. II line 31090 area).* The *Ekasara Agada* is listed: "The following drugs viz., Somaraji seeds and Somaraji flowers, Katabhi, Sindhuvara, Choraka, Varuna, Kushtha, Sarpa-gandha, Saptala, Punarnava, flowers of Shirisha, Aragvada and of Arka, Shyama, Ambashtha, Vidanga, Antra, Ashmantaka, black earth and Kuravaka comprise the Ekasara Agada. These should be applied singly or in combination of two or three to destroy the effects of poison" (verse 31). This is the only place read that names parts of Somaraji, the seeds and the flowers, and it names them as part of a compound of antidote drugs.

It is a commentators' reading of a phrase and is noted here because it shows Somaraji named a second time in the translator's apparatus. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Against poison, as part of the *Ekasara Agada*, in the chapter on snake-bite (Kalpa ch.5).
- **What the text does not say.** It names no disease other than the poisoning for which the whole compound is given, gives no dose, and says nothing of its taste or action.

The Codex does not translate "poison" or the antidote into a modern treatment. Snake-bite is a medical emergency. The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Vernonia anthelmintica* is an Asian herb of the daisy family, and its seeds have a long use in regional folk practice as a purgative or worm remedy, which is where the Latin epithet comes from (anthelmintic, "against worms"). That use is not in the Sushruta passages read, and the Latin name is the translator's. Antivenom, not herbal application, is the established treatment for snake-bite. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]
