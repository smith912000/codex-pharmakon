# Pimpinella involucrata — Ajamoda; Agnika (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Pimpinella involucrata, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Pimpinella  Involucrata.", line 31212) against the Sanskrit name Ajamoda (OCR "AjamodsC", line 31155). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print Ajamoda (OCR "Ajamodd") in some twenty compounds and four times gloss the name Agnika as Ajamoda; they give no Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 358 gives the name as "AjamodsC", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31155; id line 31212)" and the Latin as "Pimpinella  Involucrata.", with the note "Appendix 3 two-column OCR paired by position (block of 31)": that is, the script paired it because the page's two columns hold the same number of entries. The OCR garbles the final letters of this page's names (for example "AhigandbsL", "Ajagandh££"), so "AjamodsC" is read as Ajamoda. The Latin appears once in the appendix. The line 32195 "Pimpenella Anisum, Anisi" is another species, set against Madhurika.

**The pairing, checked by counting.** The first page of the appendix (lines 31126 to 31181) prints thirty-one Sanskrit names, with Amalaka and Amalaki bracketed as one, and thirty-one Latin entries (lines 31184 to 31235). Ajamoda is the seventeenth Sanskrit name (Abhaya, Abhraka, Adhaki, Aditya-vaktra, Ardraka, the entry OCR-printed "Agairadhama", Agasti, Agastya, Agni, Agnika, Agni-mantha, Aguru, Ahiphena, Ahigandha, Airavata with "(Narenga)", Ajagandha, **Ajamoda**), and "Pimpinella Involucrata." is the seventeenth Latin entry (Terminalia Chebula, Talc, "See Tuvara", the Cleome line, Zingiber, Soot, Sesbania grandiflora, Agati, "See Chitraka", "See Ajamodd", the Premna line, Aquilaria agallocha, Papaver Somniferum, Aristolochia Indica, Citrus Aurantium, Ocymum gratissimum, Pimpinella Involucrata). The neighbours agree: Ajagandha stands against "Ocymum gratissimum" (line 31210), Aja-s'ringi against "Gymnema Sylvestre" (31214), Akasa-valli against "Cassayta filiformis" (31215), Akhu-parni against "Salvinia cucullata" (31216), and Amla-vetasa, the last name of the page, against "Ruxem vesicarius" (31235). The same page is counted in the entries on *Ocimum gratissimum* (I_1407), *Cleome viscosa* (I_1381) and *Andropogon acicularis* (I_1390), which reach the same alignment for other names. [USER: verify]

**A second appendix name, Agnika.** The tenth Sanskrit name, Agnika (OCR "AgniksC", line 31141), stands against the tenth Latin entry, which is not a Latin name but the cross-reference "See Ajamodd." (line 31199); the ninth, Agni, stands against "See Chitraka." (31198). The appendix therefore sends Agnika to Ajamoda by position. The chapters agree in four places and differ in one (see below). Agnika is not a second Latin pairing and is not in the batch key. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Ajamoda and its OCR variants ("Ajamodd", "Ajamodd^", "Aj'amodd", "Ajmnodd", "Afamodd"), also by fuzzy matching of every word, and for Agnika. The hits are in the following chapters. The Ajamoda of every passage is named only in a list of drugs; no passage assigns it a taste, a potency or an action by itself.

*Sutra ch.38 (classification of drugs by therapeutic property).* `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 148 to 160. The Pippalyadi group is "Pippali, Pippali mulam, Chavya, Chitraka, Shringavera, Maricha, Hasti-Pippali, Harenuka, Ela, Ajamoda, Indrayava, Patha, Jiraka, Sarshapa, Maha-Nimva-Phala, Hingu, Bhargi, Madhurasa, Ativisha, Vacha, Vidanga and Katurohini." The group "acts as a good appetiser and is an absorbant of intestinal mucous and unassimilated lymph chyle" and its range "includes catarrh, deranged Kapham and Vatam, non-relish for food, abdominal glands, colic and gastralgia." The properties are the group's.

*Chikitsa ch.5 (Maha-Vata-Vyadhi).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt` lines 678 to 690, the Hingvadi-vati: a compound of asafoetida, Trikatu, Vacha, Ajamoda, Dhanya, Ajagandha and about twenty other drugs, "treated many times with the expressed juice of Matulunga", made into boluses "each weighing an Aksha (two Tolas)", one taken each morning "in all diseases of the deranged Vayu".

*Chikitsa ch.7 (urinary calculus).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch07.txt` lines 98 to 103: "the bones of the Krauncha, camel and ass, as well as the drugs known as S'vadamshtra, Talamula, Ajamoda, Kadamba roots and Nagara pounded together and administered through the vehicle of wine (Sura) or hot water, leads to the disintegration of Sarkara gravel."

*Chikitsa ch.8 (fistula-in-ano).* Lines 109 to 112: after fomentation, "Kushtha, salts (the five officinal kinds of salts), Vacha, Hingu and Ajamoda taken in equal parts and mixed with (an adequate quantity of) clarified butter, grape-wine, Kanjika, Sura or Sauviraka" as a potion.

*Chikitsa ch.14 (Udara).* Lines 148 to 150: "the oil of the Nikumbha with Saindhava-salt and powdered Ajamoda dissolved in it".

*Chikitsa ch.37 (Anuvasana and Uttara vasti).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch37.txt` three passages: lines 66 to 76, the Third Taila, a paste of some thirty drugs including Ajamoda "cooked with the proper quantity of milk and oil", "highly efficacious in the disorders of the deranged Vayu"; lines 194 to 205, the Ninth Sneha, a paste "weighing one seer in all" with the decoction of Valli and Kantaka, cooked in sesamum or castor oil "weighing four seers", for disorders of the bodily Kapha; and lines 222 to 230, an oil of a long list of drugs (it begins on the previous page) with Ajamoda, "speedily" curative in enlargement of the spleen, constipation, *Vata-Rakta* and *Gulma*.

*Kalpa ch.2 (vegetable and mineral poisons).* `SS_Kalpa_ch02.txt` lines 321 to 324: a gruel "prepared with the decoctions of Koshataki (Ghosha), Agnika (Ajamoda), Patha, Suryavalli, Amrita ..." drunk cold between stages of poisoning. *Kalpa ch.5 (snake-bite).* Lines 385 to 390, the Ajita-gada: "powdered Vidanga, Patha, Triphala, Ajamoda, Hingu, Chakra (Tagara), Trikatu, the five kinds of officinal salt and Chitraka, pasted with honey, should be kept for a fortnight inside a cow's horn covered with a lid of the same material."

*Uttara ch.9 (ophthalmia due to deranged Vayu).* `SS_Uttara_ch09.txt` lines 105 to 108: "Ghrita duly cooked with (the decoction of) Pattura, Agnika (Ajamoda) and Artagala and with milk".

*Uttara ch.24 (catarrh).* Lines 236 to 240: "Gargles prepared with the decoction of Musta, Tejovati, Patha, Katphala, Katuka, Vacha, Sarshapa (mustard), Pippali-mula, Pippali, Saindhava, Agnika (Ajamoda), "Ttittha" (so printed in the OCR), Karanja-seeds, Salt and Bhadra-daru should be prescribed."

*Uttara ch.36 (Naigamesha).* Lines 24 to 28: "Siddhartaka (white mustard), Vacha, Hingu, Kushtha, parched rice, Bhallataka and Ajamoda should be used in fumigating the body of the child."

*Uttara ch.40 (diarrhoea).* Lines 397 to 401: "A draught of milk, honey and clarified butter (in equal parts) mixed with sugar, Ajamoda, Katvanga and Yashti-madhu (as an after-throw) would relieve the griping (Sula) in the intestines"; and lines 810 to 815, for Pravahika: "Patha, Ajamoda, Kutaja-seeds, Shunthi and Magadhika taken in equal parts, pasted together and dissolved in tepid water or milk duly boiled with the testicles of a goat and mixed with clarified butter".

*Uttara ch.41 (phthisis).* Lines 304 to 310, the Eladi-mantha: a Prastha of clarified butter "cooked with the decoction of Ela, Ajamoda, Amalaka, Abhaya, Aksha, Gayatri ..." with some twenty drugs.

*Uttara ch.42 (Gulma).* Lines 270 to 281: an alkali made of ashes with the urines of five animals is cooked "with one Pala weight of each of the following pulverised drugs, viz. Kushtha, Saindhava, Yashti-madhu, Nagara, Vidanga and Ajamoda and with ten Pala weight of Samudra-salt thereto added" and taken as a lambative. Here the text gives a quantity for Ajamoda as part of a compound. At lines 315 to 318 the same chapter names Agnika with a different gloss, "Agnika (Chitraka)", in a powder with Patha, Nikumbha, Rajani, Trikatu and Triphala.

*Uttara ch.43 (heart disease).* Lines 71 to 75: "a pulverised compound consisting of Pippali, Ela, Vacha, Hingu, Yava-kshara, Saindhava, Sauvarchala, Shunthi and Ajamoda through the medium of the juice of (acid) fruits, fermented rice-boilings ... curd, wine, Asava or with any kind of Sneha".

*Uttara ch.44 (jaundice).* Lines 230 to 238: "the pulverised compound of Vidanga, Musta, Triphala, Ajamoda, Parushaka, Vyosha and Chitraka" cooked with ghee, honey, sugar and treacle in the decoction of the Shala-saradi group to a lambative, for Pandu with oedema and for the violent types of Kamala.

*Uttara ch.52 (cough).* Lines 299 to 312, the Kalyanaka Guda: "Powdered Granthika (Pippali-roots), Chavya, Jiraka, Vyosha, Gaja-pippali, Havusha, Ajamoda, Vidanga, Saindhava, Triphala, Yamani, Patha, Chitraka and Dhanya, each weighing a Pichu weight (two Tolas)" are added to a cooked mixture of Amalaka juice, treacle, Trivrit and oil, and "the patient should take an Aksha (two Tola) weight of this preparation", for Grahani, cough, asthma, hoarseness of voice and phthisis.

*Uttara ch.53 (hoarseness of voice).* Lines 78 to 82: "Ghrita prepared from goat's milk and duly cooked with Yava-Kshara and Ajamoda (OCR Afamodd) or with Chitraka and Amalaka, or with Deva-daru and Agni (Chitraka) should be taken with honey."

*Uttara ch.55 (retention of natural urges, Udavarta).* Lines 308 to 311 name Agnika, unglossed, in a compound with Ikshvaku seeds, Madana, Vishalya, Ativisha, Vacha, Kushtha and Kinva; lines 321 to 326: "A Varti made of Niktimbha (Danti), Kampilla, Shyama (Trivrit), Ikshaku, Agnika, (Ajamoda), Krita-Vedhana (Koshataki), Magadhi and salt pounded together, made into a paste with the addition of cow's urine, dried and cut into a proportionate size, should be inserted into the rectum."

*The name Agnika elsewhere.* `SS_Kalpa_ch04.txt` line 209 names Agnika among the families of the Mandali species of snakes, which is an animal. The chapters gloss Agnika as Ajamoda in Kalpa 2, Uttara 9, Uttara 24 and Uttara 55 and as Chitraka in Uttara 42, so the name is not single. [USER: verify]

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Ajamoda, Agnika and the variants found nothing.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Pimpinella* is a genus of the carrot family (Apiaceae), many of whose species carry aromatic oils in their fruits; that is a statement about the genus and not a finding of the Sushruta text. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the name the chapters print, the plant (if it is this one) is one drug among many in compounds for a wide run of complaints: deranged *Vayu*, urinary gravel, fistula, dropsy, catarrh, diarrhoea, *Gulma*, phthisis, cough, jaundice, hoarseness and the retention of natural urges, as a fumigant for a child possessed by Naigamesha, and in antidotes for poisoning and snake-bite. The text gives it no action by itself. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the chapters give for Ajamoda are all compounds: boluses (Chik 5), a potion in equal parts with ghee or wine (Chik 8), a powder in oil (Chik 14), medicated oils for enema (Chik 37), a gruel (Kalpa 2), an antidote paste in a cow's horn (Kalpa 5), a ghee (Uttara 9, 53), a gargle (Uttara 24), a fumigation (Uttara 36), pastes and powders (Uttara 40, 43, 44), a ghee decoction (Uttara 41), an alkali lambative (Uttara 42), a guda (Uttara 52) and a suppository (Uttara 55). Quantities appear only within compounds: "one Pala weight" of each of six drugs, Ajamoda among them, in the alkali of Uttara 42; "a Pichu weight (two Tolas)" each of fourteen powders, Ajamoda among them, in the Kalyanaka Guda, of which "the patient should take an Aksha (two Tola) weight" (Uttara 52); and boluses "each weighing an Aksha (two Tolas)" in the Hingvadi-vati (Chik 5). No dose is given for Ajamoda by itself in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** the chapters print no *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* for Ajamoda. The one quality in the text is the property of the Pippalyadi group (Sutra 38), to which it belongs: "a good appetiser", an absorbent of "intestinal mucous and unassimilated lymph chyle", for "catarrh, deranged Kapham and Vatam, non-relish for food, abdominal glands, colic and gastralgia".

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Ajamoda in some twenty passages across nineteen chapters, always inside a group or a compound and always for a complaint. It is a member of the Pippalyadi group, which the text sets against catarrh, loss of appetite and colic. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The plant therefore carries no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status in the text as retrieved.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with Ajamoda: it is a minor ingredient in many compounds, and it is never named alone. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of a Latin name that the chapters do not give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for Ajamoda. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Several of the compounds are strong (an alkali, a fumigation, a poison antidote, enema oils) and the text meant them for a physician's use. Dropsy, jaundice, persistent diarrhoea and poisoning need medical care. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
