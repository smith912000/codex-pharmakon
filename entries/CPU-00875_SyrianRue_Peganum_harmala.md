### Origin & Tradition

Syrian rue is among the most ritually charged and pharmacologically dangerous plants in the entire Codex, and the entry is written accordingly: rich on history and doctrine, deliberately silent on any method of use. A hardy perennial of the arid steppe from the Mediterranean and North Africa through the Middle East, Iran, and Central Asia into northern India, *Peganum harmala* is unrelated to culinary rue (*Ruta graveolens*) despite the shared common name. Its seeds are the source of the β-carboline "harmala alkaloids" that give the plant both its psychoactive/medicinal reputation and its toxicity.

**Ancient Near-Eastern and Persian ritual lineage (EP).** Across the Iranian plateau and the wider Near East, the seeds — *espand* / *esfand* — have been burned as a protective, purifying incense since antiquity, a practice that persists in living Persian, Anatolian, and Central Asian custom to ward off the evil eye and cleanse a space or a newborn. This apotropaic smoke-use is the plant's oldest and most continuous cultural role, and some scholars have proposed *P. harmala* as a candidate for the ancient sacred plant *haoma/soma* — a proposal the Codex records as a **contested, unproven hypothesis**, not a fact.

**Graeco-Roman / Dioscoridean lineage (DMM).** The classical writers describe a "wild rue" (*pēganon agrion*) with strong medicinal and emmenagogue properties. As with several ancient plant identities, the exact botanical referent is not certain, so the Codex attributes to this lineage the general category of a potent wild-rue drug rather than a species-specific *P. harmala* prescription.

**Unani-Tibb lineage (CM-analogue).** In the Graeco-Arabic medical system harmal (*ḥarmal*) is a hot-and-dry, third-degree drug: used historically as an emmenagogue, anthelmintic, for joint and nerve pains, and as a strong "opening" and resolvent agent. The tradition itself flags it as a powerful, cautiously handled drug — consistent with its modern toxicological profile.

**Folk Persian / Anatolian / North-African lineage.** Beyond incense, folk use spans anthelmintic, emmenagogue/abortifacient, analgesic, and red-dye applications (the seeds yield a red colourant historically used for carpets and the fez). The abortifacient use is precisely the use that has produced documented poisonings and is why the plant is dangerous in the domestic sphere.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Guardian Smoke and the Forbidden Threshold:** Syrian rue's symbolism is double-edged in a way no other plant in this volume quite matches. On one face it is the great **protector** — the smoke that guards the threshold, cleanses the eye's malice, shields the vulnerable. On the other it is the **forbidden opener** — the seed whose alkaloids unlock the monoamine system, disinhibit the brain's chemical brakes, and (in the ethnobotanical record of ayahuasca-analogue combinations) render orally active substances that the body would otherwise neutralise. This is the plant's true doctrinal signature in Kenetica: *the drug that removes a safeguard*. An MAOI does not add a new action so much as it disables a protective mechanism — the enzyme that dismantles monoamines and dietary amines — and in doing so makes both the sacred and the lethal newly possible. That is the plant's entire moral geometry: guardian and hazard are the same act seen from two sides. The Codex therefore files it under the symbolism of the *ambivalent guardian at the gate* — a substance to be honoured historically and understood pharmacologically, and precisely for that reason not to be casually opened. Its smoke protects; its seed, ingested, removes the body's own protection. The entry is deliberately a warning cast as a symbol.

### Active Components

- **Harmine** — principal β-carboline; reversible, competitive MAO-A inhibitor; also studied for antitumour and (with attendant neurotoxicity) other activities.
- **Harmaline** — closely related β-carboline; potent MAO-A inhibitor; tremorigenic/neuroactive.
- **Harmalol, harmol** — additional β-carbolines.
- **Tetrahydroharmine** — reduced β-carboline, also MAOI-active.
- **Quinazoline alkaloids (vasicine, vasicinone)** — uterotonic constituents contributing to the abortifacient action.
- The seeds are the richest source: harmine and harmaline reported at roughly 4–6% (w/w) of dry seed in analytical work — an extremely high alkaloid load.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**None assigned.** In the Kenetica framework Syrian rue is catalogued as a *documented-but-not-deployed* substance: a plant of profound historical, ritual, and pharmacological interest whose toxicity and interaction profile exclude it from any practice protocol. Its practical significance to a practitioner is precisely the opposite of a use-case — it is the standing example of why MAOI-class plants demand extreme caution, why dietary and drug interactions can be lethal, and why traditional reverence does not equal safety. It is presented here to be *understood and avoided*, not employed.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the pharmacology of *Peganum harmala* is well characterised and confirms both its psychoactive mechanism and its hazards.

- Seed and root extracts were shown to be potent, reversible, competitive inhibitors of human **MAO-A** (seed extract IC₅₀ ≈ 27 µg/L), with inhibition attributable to harmaline and harmine; harmine and harmaline accumulated in dry seeds at ~4.3% and ~5.6% w/w respectively (Herraiz et al., 2010, *Food and Chemical Toxicology*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fct.2009.12.019)). This is the mechanistic basis for both the plant's antidepressant-like reputation and its dangerous interaction profile.
- Studies of harmine and its derivatives report antitumour activity in mouse tumour models and cytotoxicity/apoptosis in HepG2 cells, but also document **marked acute neurotoxicity** (tremor, twitch, jumping) — underscoring the narrow line between activity and harm (Chen et al., 2005, *International Journal of Cancer*; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ijc.20703)).

**Evidence grade: MODERATE mechanistic / HIGH hazard.** The MAOI mechanism and high alkaloid load are firmly established; toxicity and neurotoxicity are well documented. The take-home is not efficacy but danger: a genuine, potent pharmacology that translates directly into serious risk. Science attributed to PubMed.

### Contraindications

⚠️ **Extensive — this is a hazardous plant.**

- **MAOI drug/food interactions:** as a potent MAO-A inhibitor, ingestion risks **hypertensive crisis** with tyramine-containing foods (aged cheese, cured meats, fermented products) and **serotonin syndrome** with SSRIs/SNRIs, tramadol, triptans, other serotonergic drugs, and other MAOIs. Potentially fatal.
- **Potentiation of other psychoactives:** disables first-pass metabolism of numerous substances (the ayahuasca-analogue mechanism); unpredictable, dangerous potentiation.
- **Pregnancy:** **absolutely contraindicated** — uterotonic/abortifacient (vasicine/vasicinone); implicated in poisonings from attempted abortion.
- **Overdose toxicity:** documented human poisoning — neurotoxicity (tremor, ataxia, seizures), hallucinations, vomiting, bradycardia/cardiovascular effects, hepatic/renal involvement; fatalities reported.
- **Neurological / psychiatric conditions:** avoid entirely.
- **General:** not to be self-administered under any circumstance covered by this Codex; documentary entry only.
