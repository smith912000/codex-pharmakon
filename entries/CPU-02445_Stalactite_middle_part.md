# Stalactite, middle part — 孔公孽 *Kong Gong Nie* ("Stalactitum", Yang 1998; also 通石 *Tong Shi*)

## *Stalactite or flowstone, calcium carbonate CaCO3, the identification we give to 孔公孽; of medium confidence: the Mingyi layer calls it the "root of Yin Nie", of a blue-yellow colour, and Yang's note, citing Li Shizhen, places it as the "body" of a stalactite between the nipple (Shi Zhong Ru) and the root (Yin Nie). The text gives no description beyond colour, and the two sources disagree on which part it is* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** *Kong gong nie* is a monograph of the Divine Farmer's Classic. It has no chapter in Sushruta, Dioscorides, Ebers, the Assyrian texts or the rGyud-bzhi on disk. The Codex already has the entry II_126 (Stalactite tip, *Shi Zhong Ru*), which concerns the stalactite's nipple; this entry is the separate monograph of the "middle part" from Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the middle (中) classic, reads 味辛，温 ("acrid, warm"). It governs 伤食不化 ("food damage, food not transformed"), 邪结气 ("evil bound qi"), 恶创 ("malign sores"), and a phrase printed as 疽 琅邪 in which a character is lost in the transcription, so that its meaning cannot be read from Sun's text; Yang 1998 renders the run as "malign sores, flat abscesses, and hemorrhoids and fistulas" (lines 1115-1116 and, after the page break, 1128-1130). It also "frees the nine orifices" (利九窍) and "brings down milk" (下乳汁, i.e. promotes lactation). The habitat is 生山谷 ("grows in mountain valleys"). **There is no 久服 ("taken long") clause.** A bracket in Sun's text says that the Yulan (*Taiping yulan*) quotes the paragraph with the added words "one name is Tong Shi" (一名通石), and that the Daguan edition prints those words in black (the later, non-Shennong layer). (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 孔公孽 *kong gong nie*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1769)

**Wu Pu layer (3rd century).** 《吴普》曰 records three views of the flavour: "Shennong: acrid; Qibo: salty; Bian Que: sour, non-toxic", and the colour 色青黄 ("blue-yellow") (line 1771). The three physicians' names are the layer's own device for citing earlier opinions; the Codex does not attach any further authority to them.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 adds the alias 通石 ("through-stone"), says **殷孽根也** ("the root of Yin Nie") and again 青黄色 ("blue-yellow"), and gives the place 生梁山 ("grows at Mount Liang") (line 1773).

**Position among its neighbours.** The next monograph in Sun's text is 殷孽 *Yin Nie* (line 1777 onward), whose paragraph reads 味辛，温, treats rotting wounds, stale blood, diarrhoea and dysentery, hot and cold spells, "mouse fistulas", and bound qi, and gives the alias 一名姜石 ("ginger stone"). Sun's bracket there says "this should be one item with Kong Gong Nie" (按：此当与孔公孽为一条). Its Mingyi layer says 钟乳根也 ("the root of the stalactite"). So the Mingyi calls Yin Nie the root of the stalactite and calls Kong Gong Nie the root of Yin Nie, and Sun suspects the two are one item. In the preface's list of middle-class drugs both appear side by side (line 1677).

**Yang 1998 (second witness).** Yang prints "Kong Gong Nie (Stalactitum)" under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (line 1115), and his note 53 says the drug "is obtained from the root of a stalactite", then reports that Li Shizhen (Ming dynasty) explains the stalactite as like a breast: "The nipple is Shi Zhong Ru, the body is Kong Gong Nie, and the root is Yin Nie" (lines 1120-1125). Yang's note 52, printed immediately before, says a drug of this group "is toxic and erosive" and is "only used to treat tooth decay, nasal polyps, sores, and flat abscesses"; the note does not make plain to which of the neighbouring drugs it belongs, so it is not applied here. **Identity.** The sources agree that the item is a stalactitic stone from the same family as the stalactite *Shi Zhong Ru*, and the standard modern identification is the thick middle part of a stalactite or flowstone, which is calcium carbonate. The texts disagree on the position: the Mingyi layer (earlier, from the Chinese side) says "root of Yin Nie", while Li Shizhen (a later reading, via Yang) puts it between the nipple and the root. The Codex notes the disagreement and does not settle it. Confidence in "calcium carbonate speleothem" is medium. The colour "blue-yellow" is the only description, and a pure white stalactite would not fit it; the colour may reflect copper or iron staining, which is a modern guess and not in the text. ### Active Components

Stalactites and flowstones are calcite (calcium carbonate, CaCO3) deposited from dripping water in limestone caves, often coloured by traces of iron or other metals. Calcium carbonate is a well-known antacid and calcium source; this is general chemistry. The texts read give no composition. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records the stone for food that does not transform, bound qi, malign sores, abscesses, "opening the nine orifices", and promoting milk. The Codex records these as the claims of the classic. Cave formations are protected in many countries and breaking them off destroys a feature that takes thousands of years to grow; the Codex does not recommend gathering any. Where "stalactite" is sold as medicine, its identity is not assured.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph gives no preparation, dose or course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved, and the Codex does not supply one. [USER: verify]

The later appendix to Sun's edition (headed 附：诸药制使, line 4193 ff.) records the compatibility line 孔公孽 木兰为使；恶细辛 ("*Kong gong nie* has *mulan* as its envoy; dislikes *xixin*", line 4237). It is a later layer, not the Shennong text, and it gives no weights.

### Scientific Correlation

Calcium carbonate neutralises stomach acid and is the main component of limestone, chalk and eggshell; this is well established. Nothing read supports a lactation effect, an effect on sores, or an effect on "opening the orifices". Calcium carbonate in large or prolonged doses can cause constipation and, with other calcium or vitamin D sources, raised blood calcium; this is general and applies to the pure compound, not to an unidentified cave stone. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong text proper).** 味辛，温: acrid; warm. The Wu Pu layer gives the alternative flavours "salty" (Qibo) and "sour" (Bian Que) and "non-toxic" (无毒). No organ or channel affinity is printed. The Shennong paragraph itself prints no toxicity word.
- No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph stands in the middle classic. The preface says middle drugs are "ministers" that "nourish the nature (养性) to correspond with humans", may be non-toxic or toxic and are to be weighed as suits, and are for those who want to "check illness and restore the lean" (line 1677). The paragraph itself has a flavour, a nature and a list of indications, and **no 久服 clause**: nothing promises a lighter body, longer life or contact with the spirit-light. So the stone has the middle class's frame and nothing more; it carries a therapeutic status and no cultivation status. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 孔公孽 *kong gong nie*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 1677 and 1769)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The monograph is a short list of complaints for a cave stone whose identity and place in the stalactite are disputed between layers of the text. If one is drawn to the entry, the useful lesson is that the Chinese materia medica sorted a single formation into nipple, body and root and gave each its own monograph. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The stone cannot be reliably identified from the text; do not take an unidentified cave stone as medicine.
- **Conservation.** Do not break stalactites or flowstone from caves; in many places it is illegal.
- **Dust.** Grinding any mineral produces dust that irritates the lungs.
- **Kidney stones, raised blood calcium, hypercalcaemia.** Avoid any calcium carbonate preparation. This is a precaution drawn from general knowledge of the compound, not from the Shennong text. [USER: verify]
- **Lactation and sores.** The classic's uses are historical claims. A breast problem or an abscess needs medical care.
