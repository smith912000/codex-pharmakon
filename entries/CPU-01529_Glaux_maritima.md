### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk names this plant. The entry rests on one chapter of one text, and the chapter is very short. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed under the chapter heading, and not Dioscorides' own identification. The Codex does not state the identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-141. GLAUX* at line 9266, SUGGESTED line at 9268, text at line 9269. The chapter stands in Book Four, "Other Herbs & Roots", before 4-142 *POLUGALON* and after 4-140 *BROMOS*, both different chapters. The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 719.

The whole chapter reads: "Glaux is similar in its leaves to cytisus [4-113] or lentils. Those from beneath are paler, and those from above green, and it sends out five or six thin shoots from the earth, twenty centimetres long, from the root. The flowers are similar to leucoioi [3-138] but smaller, of a purple colour. It grows by the sea. These (boiled with barley meal, salt, and oil) are sipped to restore milk that has stopped flowing [breastfeeding]."

The chapter prints no further names. It compares the leaves to *cytisus*, the plant of chapter 4-113 (*KUTISOS*, heading at line 9043), or to lentils, and the flowers to *leucoioi* (chapter 3-138 *LEUKOION*, heading at line 7743). Those chapters were not read for this entry. The word "These" in the last sentence is not tied by the text to a named part of the plant. The translators' bracket "[breastfeeding]" is theirs.
