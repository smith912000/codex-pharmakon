# Vaccaria seed — Wang Bu Liu Xing 王不留行 (Shennong bencao jing); "the king does not stay (the traveller)"

## *Vaccaria segetalis (Neck.) Garcke (= V. hispanica), cowherb or cow soapwort, an annual of the pink family whose round seeds are the drug; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence at species level, although the classic describes no plant and Yang 1998 prints the Latin as "Semen Vacarriae Segetalis"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; the other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), is read as a second witness and attributed to Yang. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder under a garbled pharmaceutical-Latin name, "Semen Vacarriae Segetalis [SBJ]", taken from Yang's English; this entry replaces it. A search of the other texts on disk for *Vaccaria*, and for the Chinese and pinyin names, found nothing.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 王不留行 in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 王不留行 *wang bu liu xing*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1051, 味 paragraph; name line 1049). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，平 ("bitter, balanced"); 主金创 ("governs incised wounds"), 止血逐痛 ("stops bleeding, drives out pain"), 出刺 ("brings out thorns"), 除风痹内寒 ("removes wind impediment and internal cold"); 久服，轻身、耐老、增寿 ("taken long: lightens the body, bears old age, increases longevity"), with the note that the *Yulan* reads 能老 for 耐老; and 生山谷 ("grows in mountains and valleys"). The paragraph gives no other alias.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 1053) gives a variant name, 王不流行 ("the king does not flow"), and the flavours "Shennong: bitter, balanced; Qi Bo, Lei Gong: sweet" (神农：苦，平；岐伯、雷公：甘); it adds "gathered in the third and eighth months" (三月、八月采, citing the *Yulan*). No Mingyi bielu layer is printed for this entry.

Sun's note (案, line 1055) cites Zheng Qiao, a later author the text on disk does not date: 王不留行，曰禁宫花，曰剪金花，叶似花，实作房 ("*wang bu liu xing* is called 'forbidden-palace flower' and 'cut-gold flower'; the leaf is like the flower; the fruit makes a chamber [房]"). This is the only description of the plant in the sources read. "The fruit makes a chamber" fits a capsule, which matches the identification but does not prove it. The note is the commentator's and not Shennong text.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 2053): "Wang Bu Liu Xing (Semen Vacarriae Segetalis) is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats incised wounds, stops bleeding, relieves pain, removes thorns, and eliminates wind impediment and internal cold. Protracted taking may make the body light, slow aging, and increase longevity. It grows in mountains and valleys." This agrees with Sun's text. Yang's footnote 121 (line 2069) is modern commentary and not part of the classic: "This medicinal is able to move the blood and treat wind toxins. Therefore, it is an important medicinal for the purpose of freeing the flow of the menses, promoting lactation, and hastening delivery." In both editions it stands in the upper class. A search of the drug-relations table (from line 4200) found no entry for it.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, balanced drug for incised wounds, for stopping bleeding and driving out pain, for bringing out thorns, for wind impediment and internal cold, and, when taken long, for lightening the body, bearing old age and adding to longevity. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. The menses, lactation and delivery uses appear only in Yang's modern footnote and are not the classic's words. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose, and does not recommend long taking on the strength of the classic's clause.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. Wu Pu gives only the gathering months, the third and the eighth (line 1053). The preface (line 4121) says that drugs differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Vaccaria hispanica* is an annual weed of cereal fields, widely naturalised, and its seed is used in current Chinese pharmacy, where it is also classed as a drug cautioned in pregnancy. [USER: verify] The classic's claims (lightening the body, bearing old age, increasing longevity) have no modern pharmacological equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Sun 1799).** Flavour and nature 味苦，平, "bitter, balanced" (line 1051). Wu Pu records a disagreement: "Shennong: bitter, balanced; Qi Bo, Lei Gong: sweet" (line 1053), which the Codex does not settle.
- **SBJ (Yang 1998).** "Bitter and balanced" (line 2053).
- No organ or channel assignment is given in the classic, and none is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The paragraph for 王不留行 carries a 久服 clause that names the terms the classic uses for life-nourishing status: 久服，轻身、耐老、增寿, "taken long: lightens the body, bears old age, increases longevity". The entry stands in the upper classic, whose preface (line 43) reads 上药一百二十种，为君，主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经: the upper drugs "nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic; many doses or long taking do not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body and benefit the qi, not age and extend the years, base it on the upper classic". This records the classic's wording and not a claim that the plant does these things. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 王不留行 *wang bu liu xing*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1051)

**Practitioner's note.** The classic gives this seed an upper-grade, long-use promise alongside a wound-and-bleeding profile, and then gives no part, preparation, dose or course. That is a record of wording, not a practice. Nothing here supports taking it long as a regimen; the later menses-and-lactation use in Yang's footnote is a different matter and is not a cultivation use.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy.** The only sources that speak to the menses and delivery are Yang's footnote and current Chinese pharmacy, where the seed is cautioned in pregnancy; avoid it in pregnancy. This is a precaution from general knowledge and not from the classic. [USER: verify]
- **Nursing, children.** No safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** Other plants are sold under the same Chinese name; the species is our identification, of high confidence. [USER: verify]
- **Bleeding and wounds.** The wound and bleeding uses are historical record only; wounds and bleeding need medical care.
- **Quantities.** No dose or duration is given; "taken long" is not a basis for a regimen.
