# Cockscomb seed — 青葙子 *qing xiang zi*, with its herb (草蒿 *cao hao*, 萋蒿 *qi hao*) and seed name 草决明 *cao jue ming*

## *Celosia argentea* L., silver cockscomb, the weedy amaranth-family annual of roadsides; the identification is the modern standard one for 青葙 and is of high confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** A search of the other traditions' texts in `Sources/` for *Celosia argentea* found nothing. The Codex's *Celosia cristata* entry (I_1250, the Tibetan texts, cockscomb) is a related and sometimes merged taxon, which some floras treat as the cultivated crested form of *C. argentea* (a general fact, not from the sources read); the two entries are not merged here and rest on different texts. [USER: verify] The entry rests on one tradition, in Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction and Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 translation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from Yang under the garbled name *Helba Celosiae Argenteae*; this entry replaces it.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), the Shennong text proper.** The entry sits in the lower classic (下经). Sun's 味 paragraph reads: "味苦，微寒。主邪气，皮肤中热，风瘙身痒，杀三虫，子：名草决明，疗唇口青。一名草蒿，一名萋蒿。生平谷。" ("Bitter, slightly cold. It governs evil qi, heat within the skin, wind-itching of the body; it kills the three worms. The seed: named 'herb that decides brightness' [草决明]; it treats blue-green lips and mouth. Other names: *cao hao*, *qi hao*. It grows in level valleys."). The paragraph has two parts: the herb, for skin heat and itch and worms, and the seed, with its own name and one indication, blue-green lips. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 青葙子 *qingxiangzi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3287)

**Second witness, Yang 1998 (attributed to Yang).** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, lines 3539-3545) reads: "bitter and slightly cold. It mainly treats evil qi, heat within the skin, and generalized wind itching. It kills the three [kinds of] worms. Its seed (*Semen Celosiae Argenteae*) is called Cao Jue Ming (Bright [Eye] Deciding Weed) and treats green-blue lips. Its other name is Cao Hao (Herbaceous Tall Stalk). Another name is Lou Hao (Crawling Tall Stalk). It grows in plains and valleys." This agrees with Sun. Yang's note 218 (line 3562) is commentary of a later period: "Currently, this medicinal is used mainly for eye troubles which are not mentioned at all in the text. It is believed to be able to settle the liver and brighten the eyes as well as cure clear-eye blindness and red screen." The Shennong text itself mentions no eye indication, only the seed name 草决明.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later additions, quoted by Sun).** Line 3289: "生道旁，三月三日采茎、叶，阴干；五月六日采子" ("It grows by the roadside; the stem and leaf are gathered on the third day of the third month and dried in the shade; the seed is gathered on the sixth day of the fifth month"). The parts are the stem and leaf (the herb) and the seed.

**Sun's own note (案).** Line 3291: "《魏略》云：初平中有青牛先生，常服青葙子。葙，当作箱字。" ("The *Weilüe* says: in the Chuping era there was a Master Green Ox who regularly took *qingxiangzi*. [The character] 葙 should be written 箱."). The anecdote is quoted by Sun from a later text. It is not a Shennong statement.

**Name overlap.** The Shennong text gives this plant the alias 草蒿, which is also the heading of a separate Shennong entry, 草蒿 (line 3161), whose own alias is 青蒿 ("bitter, cold… for itch, scabs, malign sores, kills worms… brightens the eyes"). It is a different Shennong drug (line 3161 onward: "味苦，寒…杀虫…明目", "bitter, cold… kills worms… brightens the eyes") and is not covered here. The name 草决明 also recalls the separate 决明子 entry (line 833). The Codex notes the shared names and does not resolve them. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The Chinese texts give no plant description here beyond the roadside habitat. The modern identification, *Celosia argentea*, rests on the standard identification of 青葙子 in current Chinese pharmacy. Confidence: high.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. As a general fact, *Celosia argentea* belongs to the amaranth family (Amaranthaceae); the seeds are small, shiny and black, and they contain fatty oil. This is a general statement and not drawn from the Codex's sources. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Shennong text lists a bitter, slightly cold herb for heat in the skin, wind itching and worms, and the seed for blue-green lips. These are the text's terms; the Codex does not translate "the three worms" or "evil qi" into modern diagnoses and gives no protocol. Itching skin and discoloured lips need medical assessment. The later eye use (Yang's note 218) is commentary and not in the Shennong paragraph.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text proper gives no preparation. The Mingyi layer gives the harvest dates: stem and leaf on the third day of the third month, shade-dried; seed on the sixth day of the fifth month (line 3289). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

*Celosia argentea* seed is used in current Chinese pharmacy, which is a general fact and not a finding. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ.** The Shennong text prints "味苦，微寒" (bitter, slightly cold). The Mingyi layer adds no flavour or temperature, and there is no Wu Pu layer for this entry. The text proper prints no toxicity word, though the entry stands in the lower classic, whose preface says "many are toxic" (line 2935).

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic (下经), whose preface (line 2935) says lower drugs "treat disease to correspond with the earth; many are toxic and cannot be taken long; for those who wish to remove cold and heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings and cure illness, take the lower classic". The entry has no 久服 clause. Sun's note records that a man named Master Green Ox "regularly took" the seed (*Weilüe*), a later anecdote of long use; it is not a Shennong claim and does not change the grade. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 青葙子 *qingxiangzi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3287)

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug for skin heat and itch in the text's own frame, to be used for a defined complaint and then set aside. The anecdote of regular use belongs to a later writer and rests on a name, not a regimen. Nothing in the entry supports a long-term practice, and no dose is given.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug beyond the class statement that lower drugs are mostly toxic and not for long use. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of this entry. Skin complaints, itching and discoloured lips need medical assessment. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
