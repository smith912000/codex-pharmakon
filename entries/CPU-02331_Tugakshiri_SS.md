# Tugakshiri — Tugakshiri (Sushruta Samhita); Appendix 3 prints Tvak-kshiri

## *Unresolved: a drug named Tugakshiri in the Kakolyadi group and in a burn plaster of the Sushruta Samhita; the nearest entry in Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 is Tvak-kshiri, glossed "Curcuma Angustifolia", but that match is by spelling and by column order, and is ours; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The name is printed *Tugakshiri* in Bhishagratna's translation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder with the locus "SS Sutra 38, Ka'kolya'di group (Vol. I line 15602)"; this entry replaces it. A search of all the chapter files and the three Bhishagratna volumes found the name twice in the *Sutrasthana*: ch. 38 and ch. 12.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The Kakolyadi group (Sutra ch. 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 250 to 272; Vol. I line 15602).* "The Ka'kolya'di Group: — The drugs known as Kakoli-Kshira-Kakoli, Jivaka, Rishabhaka, Mudgaparni, Mashaparni, Meda, Mahameda, Chhinnaruha, Karkata-Shringi, Tugakshiri, Padmaka, Prapaundarika, Riddhi, Vriddhi, Mridvika, Jivanti and Madhuka, combinedly form the group known as the Kakolyadi Gana." The metrical text: "The group of medicinal drugs under discussion subdues the action of the deranged Pittam, blood and Vayu. It increases the quantity of milk in the breast (galactagogue) and favours the accumulation of phlegm (Kapham) in the body. It is a restorative and an elixir and is endued with the therapeutic virtue of augmenting the virile potency of a man." These are properties of the group of named drugs and are not assigned to Tugakshiri singly. The phrases "restorative and an elixir" and "augmenting the virile potency" are the translator's English, and their Sanskrit was not available to check.

*A burn plaster (Sutra ch. 12, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch12.txt` lines 217 to 223).* "A plaster composed of Tugakshiri, Plaksha, Chandana, Gairika, and Amritam (Guduchi), pasted together with clarified butter, should be applied over a burn of the Samyag-Dagdha type, or the flesh of domestic or aquatic or amphibious animals should be pasted and plastered over the affected part." This is the only prescription in which Tugakshiri stands alone with a stated purpose. The plaster is a cooling application for a burn, in a chapter that lists cold applications and ghee unguents.

*Appendix 3 (Vol. III, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` lines 33145 to 33170).* The spelling *Tugakshiri* is not listed. The OCR prints, in one column, "Tvak-kshiri, Tri-kantaka, Triputi, Trivrit ..." and, in the other column, "Curcuma Angustifolia. See Gokshura. Lathyrus Sativus. Ipomea Turpethum ...", so that in column order *Tvak-kshiri* pairs with "Curcuma Angustifolia". Whether *Tvak-kshiri* is the same word as *Tugakshiri* was not settled by any text opened; the resemblance is in the spelling only, and the column pairing is ours. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a group member: Pitta, blood and Vayu, milk in the breast, and the stated restorative and virile qualities (ch. 38). In ch. 12: an ingredient in a plaster for a burn.
- **What the text does not say.** Everything about the plant itself.

The Codex gives no protocol. A burn is a medical matter and the plaster is not a treatment.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Tugakshiri alone. Its group, the Kakolyadi, is described as calming Pitta, blood and Vayu, as a galactagogue and as favouring the accumulation of Kapha.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Tugakshiri is named in the Sushruta Samhita as a member of a therapeutic group (*gana*) (the Kakolyadi group, Sutra ch. 38) and in a burn plaster (ch. 12). A *gana* is a grouping by therapeutic action. The Kakolyadi metrical text does call the group, in the translator's words, "a restorative and an elixir" that augments virile potency, which is group-level language close to *rasayana* and *vajikarana*; but it is said of the whole group in a classification chapter, not of Tugakshiri, and not in a chapter on *rasayana* or *vajikarana*. The chapters of the *Chikitsasthana* on *rasayana* and *vajikarana* (ch. 26 to 31 on disk) were searched for the name and it does not occur in them. Nothing links it to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The status is unattested; only a therapeutic one is recorded.

**Practitioner's note.** A name in a group that the text calls restorative, and in a burn plaster. The group wording is not the drug's, and a cultivator has nothing to use from it until it is identified.
