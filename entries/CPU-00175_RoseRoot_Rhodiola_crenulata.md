### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan medicine (FMT lineage):** *Rhodiola crenulata* is the Rhodiola of the high Tibetan plateau, growing on rocky, oxygen-poor ground at extreme altitude. In the Tibetan and Himalayan tradition its thick root and rhizome are used to support stamina, breathing, and recovery under the specific stress of high-altitude living — fatigue, breathlessness, and the weakness that follows exertion in thin air. It is important to distinguish it from *R. rosea* (rose root / golden root), the European–Siberian species that dominates the Western "adaptogen" literature: they share the salidroside/rosavin chemical family but are different species with different profiles, and much of the Western clinical Rhodiola data is on *R. rosea*, not *R. crenulata*.

**Later Chinese incorporation:** *R. crenulata* is the Rhodiola most used in modern Chinese practice as *Hóng Jǐng Tiān*, chiefly for altitude adaptation, fatigue, and lung/heart support — an integration of the Tibetan high-altitude use into the broader Chinese pharmacopoeia.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Root That Breathes Thin Air:** Rhodiola crenulata is defined by where it survives: the altitude where oxygen is scarce and every metabolic act costs more. The doctrinal reading in Kenetica is *efficiency under scarcity* — a plant whose signature is doing more with less breath. This maps unusually well onto the retrieved pharmacology, where its active salidroside acts on mitochondrial energy metabolism and the blood-brain barrier under hypoxic stress (see Scientific Correlation). Here the symbol and the mechanism genuinely converge: the plant of thin air is, in the lab, a plant that protects energy metabolism under low oxygen.

### Active Components

- **Salidroside (rhodioloside)** — the principal characterised active; a phenylethanoid glycoside. The dominant compound behind the studied anti-hypoxic and energy-metabolism effects.
- **Tyrosol** — the aglycone of salidroside; antioxidant phenol.
- **Flavonoids and phenolic acids** — antioxidant constituents.
- Note: *R. crenulata* is generally lower in **rosavins** than *R. rosea*; do not transfer rosavin-based claims across species.
- Documented preclinical actions: mitigation of hypoxic brain injury, support of mitochondrial homeostasis and ATP production, blood-brain-barrier protection, and antioxidant activity.

### Scientific Correlation

Evidence grade: **preclinical** (animal + in-vitro) for the specific species *R. crenulata*. According to PubMed:

- **Anti-hypoxic brain protection (mouse + cell model).** *Rhodiola crenulata* extract and its active **salidroside** alleviated hypobaric-hypoxia-induced brain injury in mice — reducing neuronal apoptosis, maintaining blood-brain-barrier integrity (preserving tight-junction proteins Claudin-1, ZO-1, occludin), and improving mitochondrial morphology and energy metabolism. Mechanistically the protection was linked to enhanced TCA-cycle flux and activation of the AMPK/Sirt1 signalling pathway with MCU-mediated mitochondrial homeostasis (Hou et al., 2024, *Phytomedicine*). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phymed.2024.155529)

This animal/cell evidence supports the traditional altitude/energy use mechanistically but is not a human clinical demonstration. Human adaptogen trials predominantly concern *R. rosea* and should not be cited as if they were *R. crenulata*.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Sweet, bitter; cool to neutral. Channels: Lung, Heart. Actions: tonifies Qi, invigorates blood, moistens/opens the Lung, and supports breath and stamina at altitude.
- **Tibetan (rGyud-bzhi sphere):** high-altitude strength and breath tonic; cooling, restorative.
- **Dantian correspondence:** Middle/Upper — breath and cardiac energy support.

### Contraindications

- **Over-stimulation:** as an activating tonic, may cause restlessness or disturbed sleep in sensitive individuals if taken late in the day.
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** insufficient safety data; avoid without practitioner supervision.
- **Interactions:** theoretical additive effects with stimulants; possible interactions with agents affecting blood pressure or CNS. No well-characterised human interaction dataset for *R. crenulata*.
- **Species substitution:** commercial "Rhodiola" is often *R. rosea*; verify species if the Tibetan profile is intended.
