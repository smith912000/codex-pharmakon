The one Ebers text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). Its chapter VII list of animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (lines 1385-1404) names lion, hippopotamus, crocodile, ox, serpent, tortoise, lizard, mole, vulture, ass, hog, scorpion, crab, ostrich, locust, tarantula, beetle, bat, wasp, fly, tapeworm and others, but no millipede, centipede or similar many-legged creature. I searched Bryan's text for "millipede", "centipede", "myriapod" and "multipede" and found nothing. Bryan's book is a summary with selected quotations, not a full translation, and he says that the identity of a considerable number of the animals in the papyrus "is still beyond us" (lines 1383-1386), so the absence proves little. What the row records is that a modern translator identified one Egyptian animal name as a millipede, with doubt flagged by the project's own list. The papyrus is organised by complaint, not by substance, so the identity of any animal named in it is a modern hypothesis.

Dioscorides discusses millipedes in his *De Materia Medica* (Book 2, the "living creatures" section), but this entry is not tagged DMM and that chapter is not drawn on here.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, millipedes (class Diplopoda) defend themselves with secretions from glands along the body; depending on the species these include benzoquinones, hydrogen cyanide and other irritant compounds, which can irritate or stain skin and eyes. Their bodies are largely chitinous exoskeleton.
