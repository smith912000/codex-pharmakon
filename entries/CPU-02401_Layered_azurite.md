# Layered azurite — 曾青 *Ceng Qing* ("Azuritum", Yang 1998)

## *Azurite, the deep-blue basic copper carbonate Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2, the identification we give to 曾青; of high confidence: Yang's translation reads "Azuritum", the Mingyi and Sun's commentators call it a blue stone and "the essence of copper", and Yang's note says Ceng Qing is the ore "found in stratified layers". This is a copper compound: historical record only, not to be taken* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** *Ceng Qing* is a monograph of the Divine Farmer's Classic. It has no chapter in Sushruta, Dioscorides, Ebers, the Assyrian texts or the rGyud-bzhi on disk. **Toxic substance: historical record only.** Azurite is a copper compound, and nothing in this entry is a recommendation. The Codex already has a combined entry on the blue-stone family, II_118 (Azure stone hollow, *Kong Qing*), which names Ceng Qing as one of its varieties from Yang 1998; this entry is the separate monograph of Ceng Qing from Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction and does not repeat that entry's other stones.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the upper (上) classic, reads 味酸，小寒 ("sour, slightly cold"). It "governs" (主) 目痛，止泪 ("eye pain; stops tearing"), 出风痹 ("brings out wind impediment"), 利关节 ("frees the joints"), 通九窍 ("opens the nine orifices"), and 破症坚积聚 ("breaks hard concretions, accumulations and gatherings"). Then comes the life-nourishing clause: **久服轻身能化金、铜** ("taken long: lightens the body, [and] can transform gold [and] copper"). The habitat is 生山谷 ("grows in mountain valleys"). (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 曾青 *ceng qing*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 169)

The last clause is ambiguous in the classical Chinese. It can be read as saying the stone itself can be turned into gold and copper, or that the substance can transmute other things into them. Yang 1998 renders it "It is able to transform into gold and copper" (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` lines 914-918), so Yang reads it as the stone changing. The Codex records the clause and does not decide its sense. It is an alchemical claim of the classic and nothing more.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 gives the places 生蜀中及越 ("grows in Shu and Yue"; one character after 越 is lost in the transcription) and "gathered at no fixed time" (采无时) (line 171).

**Sun's note (案).** Sun collects the early uses of the name (line 173). The *Guanzi* (chapter *Kuiduo*) names "the *ceng qing* of Qin Ming mountain". The *Xunzi* says the southern sea has *ceng qing*. A commentator on the *Xunzi* (the surname is partly lost in the transcription: 杨 and a lost character) glosses it **铜之精**, "the essence of copper". *Fan Zi Jiran* says *ceng* comes from Hongnong and Yuzhang, that *bai qing* (white blue) comes from Xintu, and that the blue-coloured kind is the good one. The *Huainanzi* (chapter *Dixing*) says "blue heaven, after eight hundred years, produces *qing ceng*", and Gao You's gloss says *ceng qing* is a "blue stone" (青石也). Sun does not himself state a chemical identity.

**Yang 1998 (second witness).** Yang lists it under "Jades and Stones: Superior Class", as *Ceng Qing* "(Azuritum)" (line 914), and his note 37 explains that this entry and the next three (*Kong Qing*, *Bian Qing*, *Bai Qing*) are "derived from the same mineral source", an ore found in different shapes: "Kong Qing is a round shape with a hollow center. Ceng Qing is found in stratified layers. Bian Qing is found in short cylinders. Bai Qing is the same substance as Bian Qing but is white" (lines 927-931). That is where "layered" in the entry's name comes from. In Yang's text the "light body" clause reads "may make the body light and prevent senility"; the clause 不老 ("not aging") does not appear in Sun's 曾青 paragraph as read, so the Codex follows Sun's wording.

**Identity.** The classical descriptions agree on a blue stone that is the "essence of copper" and can be turned to copper, which fits a copper ore. Azurite is the standard modern identification. Confidence is high at the level of "blue copper ore, azurite"; the text does not separate it from malachite (green) or from the other *qing* stones except by shape and colour. No contradiction was found between the text and the identification.

### Active Components

Azurite is a basic copper(II) carbonate, Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2, a deep-blue mineral found in the weathered zone of copper deposits, and was used as a copper ore and as a blue pigment. Copper salts taken by mouth are irritant and, in quantity, poisonous. This is general chemistry and toxicology. The texts read give no composition. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice, and not to be taken. The classic records *ceng qing* for painful, watering eyes, wind impediment, stiff joints, and for "breaking" accumulations, and adds a long-use clause promising a lighter body and a gold-and-copper transformation. The Codex records these as the classic's claims. A copper mineral swallowed over a long period is a way to be poisoned, and the "taken long" clause is the most dangerous sentence in the paragraph: a toxic mineral taken for a long time is the opposite of "nourishing life". The classic's preface says that the upper class is non-toxic (无毒) and may be taken in quantity and for long; for this mineral that claim is contradicted by modern toxicology, and the Codex follows toxicology.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph gives no preparation, dose or course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved, and the Codex does not supply one. [USER: verify]

The later appendix to Sun's edition, headed 附：诸药制使 ("appendix: the preparing and employing of the various drugs"; its first line is a remark by Tang Shenwei, line 4193, and it is not a Shennong layer) records the stone's compatibilities: 曾青 畏菟丝子 ("*ceng qing* fears dodder", line 4205); talc 恶曾青 ("dislikes *ceng qing*", line 4221); *huang shi zhi* (yellow stone fat) "uses *ceng qing* as its envoy" (line 4229); *tianmendong* "fears *ceng qing*" (line 4275); and *xixin* has "*ceng qing* and *dong gen* as envoys" (line 4301). They show that the stone was mixed into compounds. They give no weights. A separate passage quoted from the *Baopuzi* (line 4149), citing a "*Shennong sijing*", says that the five *zhi*, cinnabar, jade slips, *ceng qing*, realgar, orpiment, mica and *Taiyi yu yuliang* (太乙禹余粮) "may each be taken singly, and all make a person fly and live long." That is a quotation in the *Baopuzi* (a fourth-century work of Ge Hong) of an unidentified older text, a later and different layer, and the Codex does not treat it as the Shennong text.

### Scientific Correlation

Azurite is a copper ore and pigment. Copper is an essential trace element, but copper salts are irritant to the stomach and, in larger amounts, damage the liver and kidneys; this is well established. Whether the stone relieves eye pain or joint stiffness, or whether any substance "transforms" into gold, has no support in modern science: nuclear changes, not chemistry, make one element another. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

- No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: upper class, with a "taken long" clause.* The paragraph stands in the upper classic. The preface says upper drugs are "ruler" drugs that "nourish life (养命) to correspond with heaven", are non-toxic, may be taken in quantity and for long without harming the person, and are for those who wish to "lighten the body and benefit the qi, not age and extend the years" (line 43). The paragraph itself carries the life-nourishing clause **久服轻身** ("taken long, lightens the body"), and *qing shen* (轻身, "lighten the body") is a classical effect term with no pharmacological equivalent; it is not weight loss. It adds a clause on transforming gold and copper, which belongs to the alchemical side of the classic. The register is (a) as a statement of what the classic assigns; it is not a statement that the mineral does this. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 曾青 *ceng qing*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 43 and 169)

**Practitioner's note.** The Chinese scheme classes this stone with the drugs for long-term life-nourishing use, and the same scheme's compound claim about turning into gold shows that this class mixes ordinary medicine with alchemy. The cultivator's practical use of this entry is as a warning about how the grades were made: "upper" in the classic does not mean safe. Nothing here supports taking the stone. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Do not ingest.** Copper carbonate is a poison; swallowing it can cause vomiting, pain, and liver and kidney injury.
- **Dust and grinding.** Powdered copper minerals irritate the eyes, nose and lungs. The classic's use for the eyes is not a reason to put the powder in them.
- **Long use.** The "taken long" clause is not supported; cumulative copper exposure is harmful.
- **Children, pregnancy, liver or kidney disease.** Avoid any copper preparation. This is a precaution drawn from general toxicology, not from the Shennong text. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** Blue and green copper stones are not reliably distinguished in the text; treat any "azurite" sold as medicine as a copper compound of unknown purity.
