### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** Varuṇa is *the* classical urinary-tract drug of Ayurveda — the great **lithotriptic bark** named at the head of its own therapeutic group. Suśruta enumerates the **Varuṇādi gaṇa**, the group of drugs "led by Varuṇa," indicated for *medoroga* (disorders of fat/lipid tissue), *kapha* obstructions, internal abscesses (*antar-vidradhi*), *gulma* (abdominal masses), and above all *aśmarī* — urinary calculus, the stone. In the surgical tradition, where lithotomy (cutting for the stone) was a signature and dangerous operation, Varuṇa was the medical counterweight: the bark that could dissolve, shrink and help expel the stone, or at least prevent its recurrence, without the knife. The *Varuṇādi gaṇa* is one of the named drug-groups (*gaṇa*) that structure the Suśruta pharmacopoeia, and Varuṇa's position as its head signals its rank. Its actions are read as *bhedana* (breaking obstruction), *lekhana* (scraping away accumulation) and *mūtrala* (promoting urine flow) — the exact triad a stone-dissolving agent requires.

**Wider tradition:** Beyond calculus, the bark decoction has been used for urinary-tract infection, benign prostatic obstruction and difficult micturition, for palpable abdominal masses, and as a general srotoshodhana (channel-clearing) agent. It is a tree of riverbanks and sacred groves — its very name, Varuṇa, is that of the Vedic god of the cosmic waters.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — Varuṇa, Lord of Waters, and the Dissolution of the Stone:**
No plant name in the materia medica is more deliberately chosen. *Varuṇa* is the ancient Vedic deity of the waters, the cosmic ocean and the moral order (*ṛta*) — the god who binds with his nooses and who governs the flowing and the fixed. To name the stone-dissolving bark after the Lord of Waters is to state a whole doctrine in a word: disease of the urinary tract is, in this frame, a failure of *flow* — the living waters of the body congealing, hardening, crystallising into the stone that dams the channel. Varuṇa the plant is invoked to do what Varuṇa the god does: restore the sovereignty of water over the intransigent solid, loosen the noose, dissolve the fixed back into the flowing. In Kenetica this makes Varuṇa the archetype of the **dissolution phase** — the pharmacological counterpart to every teaching about not letting the vital current stagnate and harden. The stone is what happens when flow stops; the bark of the water-god is the remedy that teaches the body to flow again. Symbolically it belongs beside every "solve" in the alchemical *solve et coagula* — Varuṇa is the *solvent*.

### Active Components

The bark of *Crataeva nurvala* contains the triterpene **lupeol** as its most-studied active, together with related triterpenoids (β-sitosterol, lupeol acetate), flavonoids, tannins, saponins and plant sterols. Lupeol is widely credited in the pharmacological literature with the anti-lithic (anti-stone), anti-inflammatory and membrane-stabilising actions that underlie the bark's traditional use — though the classical action is best understood as that of the whole bark decoction (*kaṣāya*) rather than an isolated molecule.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica, Varuṇa is filed as the **flagship urinary / lithotriptic agent** — the head of the "dissolution" arc. Its use-case is the full spectrum of *aśmarī*: prevention and management of renal and urinary-tract calculi, reduction of stone-forming metabolic drive, symptomatic relief of stone-associated pain, and clearing of Kapha-type obstructions in the urinary and pelvic channels. Its heating, scraping, diuretic energetics fit the cold, sticky, obstructive pathology of stone formation. As the named head of the **Varuṇādi gaṇa** it also carries a broader "de-obstruction" role — fatty accumulation (medoroga), masses (gulma), and channel congestion. In training-phase terms it is a **support / clearing agent** taken over courses, and the best-evidenced of the five plants in this batch.

### Scientific Correlation

Varuṇa has, uniquely in this batch, **human clinical evidence**. According to PubMed:

- **Randomised controlled trial:** In a prospective, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (77 patients), an Ayurvedic formulation of *Crataeva nurvala* (varuna) plus banana stem ("Herbmed") produced a statistically significant reduction in calculus size versus placebo for stones 5–10 mm (≈33% reduction in the active arm vs a 5% increase with placebo, *p* = 0.017) and significantly lower pain scores, supporting a role in dissolving/passing upper-urinary-tract calculi (Patankar et al., 2008, *J. Altern. Complement. Med.*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1089/acm.2008.0189).
- **Experimental urolithiasis (mechanism):** In rats, oral *Crataeva nurvala* bark decoction reduced the oxalate-synthesising liver enzyme glycolate oxidase, lowered renal deposition of stone-forming constituents, and partially reversed the abnormal urinary crystalloid excretion of calculogenic animals — indicating a regulatory action on endogenous oxalate synthesis (Varalakshmi et al., 1990, *J. Ethnopharmacol.*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-8741(90)90082-5).
- **Ayurvedic experimental confirmation:** A surgical stone-implant rat model reported the Varuṇa decoction effective in the management of experimental urolithiasis (Agarwal et al., 2010, *Ayu*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-8520.77161).

**Evidence grade: MODERATE (best-supported in this batch).** One small randomised placebo-controlled human trial (of a *combination* product, so species-specific attribution is imperfect) plus consistent mechanistic animal data. The authors themselves called for a larger phase-III trial with longer follow-up. The traditional lithotriptic reputation is meaningfully — if not yet definitively — supported.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Tikta (bitter), Kaṣāya (astringent), Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry), Tīkṣṇa (penetrating)
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha-Vāta-hara (reduces Kapha and Vāta); Pitta-neutral to mildly increasing
- **Karma (actions):** Aśmarīghna / Aśmaribhedana (breaks/dissolves calculi), Mūtrala (diuretic), Bhedana (de-obstructing), Lekhana (scraping), Medohara (reduces fat accumulation), Dīpana-Pācana (digestive)
- **Gaṇa membership:** Head of the **Varuṇādi gaṇa** (Suśruta) — the drug-group led by Varuṇa for kapha-medo obstruction, gulma, abscess and calculus
- **Primary indications (roga):** Aśmarī (urolithiasis), Mūtrakṛcchra (dysuria), Medoroga, Gulma, Antarvidradhi (internal abscess)

### Contraindications

- Not a substitute for urological assessment of obstructing stones, infection, or impaired renal function — seek conventional care for large/obstructing calculi and acute symptoms.
- Heating potency: use cautiously in high-Pitta states and hyperacidity.

- Diuretic action — consider interaction/electrolyte effects in patients on diuretics or with renal disease.
