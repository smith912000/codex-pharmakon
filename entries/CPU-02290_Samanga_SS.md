# Samanga — Samanga, also printed Samang^ and Samangd (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *A drug named in the Priyangvadi and Amvashtha'di groups and in many recipes of the Sushruta Samhita; the translator gives one parenthetical gloss, once, in the OCR "Bardha-krdnti". Identification: unresolved. No Latin binomial is printed against it in the sources read. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not identified. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. A second placeholder, "Samang [SS]" (CPU-02289), was retired into this row on 30 October as the same substance; its attestation was not added then. It is the OCR form "Samang^" in the Priyangvadi list below, so it is covered here. The first-pass check guessed "unresolved (unknown)"; that stands. A search of all the Sushruta text on disk (the chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and Bhishagratna's three volumes) for *Samang* found the places below.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Chapter 38 of the *Sutrasthana* is the chapter "which deals with the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam)" and says that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (Vol. I lines 15343 to 15350; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3 to 9). A group's virtue is stated for the group as a whole.

1. **Priyangvadi group.** (`text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 320 to 324). "The group of medicinal drugs known as the Priyangvadi Gana consists of Priyangu, Samang^, Dhataki, Naga-pushpa, Chandana, Kuchandana, Mocharasa, Rasanjana, Kumbhika, Srotohnjana, Padma-keshara, Jojanvalli, and Dirghamula."
2. **Amvashtha'di group.** (Vol. I lines 15668 to 15676; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 326 to 335). "Drugs known as Amvashtha, Dhataki flowers, Samanga, Katvanga, Madhuka, Vilva-peshika, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Palasha, Nandi-Vriksha and Padma keshara, enter into the composition of the group known as the Amvashthadi Gana."
3. **The virtue of the two groups.** The metrical text that follows is said of "the two medicinal recipes or groups": they "prove beneficial in a case of deranged Pitta, favour the healing of ulcers, bring about the adhesion of fractured bones and prove curative in cases of dysentery where the stools are found to consist of lumps of thick and matured mucous (Pakvatisara)" (Vol. I lines 15677 to 15683). This is said of the pair of groups, not of Samanga alone.
4. **In recipes.** The name recurs in a wide range of compound preparations, and in none of them does the text say what Samanga does on its own. The places found are listed with what the translation says of the whole recipe:
   - *Sutrasthana* ch.16, Vol. I line 9005 (`text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch16.txt` line 417): in a plaster, or an oil, for the *Avamanthaka* type of ear-lobe disease, with Prapaundarikam, Madhukam and Dhavam.
   - *Sutrasthana* ch.36, Vol. I line 15082 (`text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch36.txt` line 170): a paste (*Kalka*) beginning "Samanga, Soma, Sarala wood, Soma-Valka ...", in the passage on the *Ropana-varti* (healing plug) and granulation of ulcers.
   - *Chikitsasthana* ch.2, line 433: an oil boiled with Samanga, Haridra, Padma, Trivrit, Tuttha, Vidanga, Katuka, Pathya, Guduchi and Karanja, which "acts as a good healing (Ropana) agent"; and line 487: an oil cooked with Samanga, Rajani, Padma and other drugs.
   - *Chikitsasthana* ch.8, line 256: an oil cooked with Pippali, Yashti-madhu, Lodhra and others, in which the translator prints "Samanga (Bardha-krdnti)"; "Bardha-krdnti" is the OCR of a parenthetical gloss, whose meaning is not explained in the lines read. [USER: verify against the printed page]
   - *Chikitsasthana* ch.16, line 90: clarified butter cooked with Kshira-Shukla, Prithak-parni, Samanga, Rodhra, Chandana and the leaves and bark of the Nyagrodhadi group, for the cavity of a *Pittaja* ulcer.
   - *Uttaratantra* ch.21, lines 261 and 277: a note naming Samanga among "the barks of Tinduka, Abhaya, Lodira, Samanga and of Amalaka" under the heading *Pancha-kashaya* as Dallana reads it, and a powder of the barks of Tinduka, Abhaya, Rodhra, Samanga and Amalaka mixed with honey and the expressed juice of Kapittha, used "similarly" to the preceding remedies (the passage is not read further here).
   - *Uttaratantra* ch.39, line 1062: Draksha, Paundarika, Utpala, parched barley, Ushira, Samanga and Kashmari fruit steeped, for relief from fever and burning.
   - *Uttaratantra* ch.40, lines 355, 374 and 622: Samanga with Dhataki flowers, Manjishtha, Lodhra and Musta, and in a decoction of Padma, Samanga, Yashti-madhu, dried Vilva and Jambu fruits with rice washings and honey, in the treatment of chronic (*Pakva*) *Atisara*; and Samanga with sugar, Utpala, Lodhra, Yashti-madhu and Tila with she-goat's milk.
   - *Uttaratantra* ch.61, line 153: a ghrita cooked with Samanga among many drugs and cow's urine, called *Siddharthaka Ghrita*.
   - *Kalpasthana* ch.5, line 453: Samanga among the drugs of an antidote whose title is printed *Mandali-Vishahara Agada* (the passage is not read further here).
   - *Sharirasthana* ch.10, line 768: a linctus of Samanga, Dhataki flowers, Navamalika, Gairika, resin and Rasanjana for excessive bleeding, "mixed with honey".

The most recurrent company of the name is astringent and wound-healing: Dhataki flowers, Lodhra, Rodhra, Yashti-madhu, Padma. The text does not say that Samanga is the cause of the effect. The Codex draws no property from the company it keeps.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unidentified.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Priyangvadi and Amvashtha'di groups: easing deranged Pitta, healing ulcers, knitting broken bones and stopping a mucous dysentery (the two groups together). In recipes: wound healing, ear-lobe disorders, fever with burning, chronic dysentery, bleeding and an antidote, each as one ingredient among several.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no taste or potency for this drug on its own, and no identification.

The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no plant for any of the conditions named.

### Preparation & Dosing

The recipes above are given in the translation as drug combinations (oils, a ghrita, plasters, powders with honey, a decoction). Their quantities, where printed, belong to the recipes and are not carried over to this row, because they are not doses of Samanga. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for this drug in the lines found. The only qualities given are those of whole groups: the Priyangvadi and Amvashtha'di groups are beneficial in deranged Pitta and in ulcers (Vol. I lines 15677 to 15683). No such classification is imported for Samanga itself.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in chapter 38 group lists (a classification of drugs by the use of the whole group) and in many therapeutic recipes. None of the passages found is in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter: a search of *Chikitsasthana* ch.26 (*vajikarana*) and ch.27 to 30 (*rasayana*) for *Samang* found nothing. The text therefore assigns this drug no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it.

**Practitioner's note.** An unidentified name that recurs in wound, ulcer and dysentery recipes. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without an identification nothing can be gathered or used. A cultivator reads it as a name in a record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified, and the translator's only gloss is unclear. Do not substitute any herb for it.
- **The conditions named.** Ulcers, dysentery, bleeding, fever, ear disease and poisoning are named in the recipes. These are medical conditions, some of them emergencies; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Group membership and recipes.** The drug is one of many in its groups and recipes, and their virtues are not safety statements about any one member. Some recipes name copper sulphate (*Tuttha*) or other strong materials, which are not part of this entry.
