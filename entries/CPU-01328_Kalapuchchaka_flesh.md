# Kalapuchchaka flesh — Kalapuchchaka, flesh of a shore-dweller (*Kulachara*) of the marsh (*Anupa*) group, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the animal Bhishagratna prints as "Kalapuchchaka", with no gloss in the chapter; his Vol. 3 glossary reads "A black-tailed bird" by column order; no binomial is printed* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Kalapuchchaka is named once in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, line 676, where the OCR prints "K^lapuchchaka"; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 248 lists "Kalapuchchaka", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", flagged identity-uncertain). The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The marsh-dwellers (*Anupa*) "may be divided into five groups", of which the first is the *Kulacharas*, "frequenting the shores of pools and lakes" (lines 659-669). The Kalapuchchaka is in that first group, in the same sentence as the Gokarna: "the elephant, the Gavaya, buffalo, Ruru ... Chamara, Srimara ... Rohita (red deer), boar, rhinoceros, Gokarna, Kalapuchchaka, together with the Nynku (a species of antlered deer) and the wild cow, etc., frequent the cool shores of swamps and lakes, and are accordingly included within the group of Kulacharas (shore-dwellers)" (lines 671-680; the translator glosses Ruru, Srimara and Nynku but not Kalapuchchaka).

The text gives qualities for the group, not for this animal by name. "The flesh of an animal of this group is spermatopoietic and destroys the deranged Vayu and Kapham. It is sweet in taste and digestion, cooling, tonic, demulcent and diuretic, and increases the quantity of Kapham" (lines 682-686). The sentences that follow describe the elephant, Gavaya, buffalo, Ruru, Chamara, Srimara, boar, rhinoceros and Gokarna one by one (lines 688-730); there is none for the Kalapuchchaka. Searches of every Sushruta chapter file for the spelling returned only line 676, so the text on disk has no second passage, no preparation, no cautionary use and no omen for it under this name.

**Identity.** Ch.46 does not say what animal the Kalapuchchaka is. The glossary of Bhishagratna's Vol. 3 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, name at line 30613) gives, in a separate OCR column, "A black-tailed bird"; the pairing is by column order. That sits oddly with a group the chapter fills with large mammals and which it calls "an animal of this group". *Uttaratantra* ch.39 (fever) lists "Kala-puchchha" among meats for a fever patient, "Ena, Prishata, Sarabha, Kala-puchchha, Kuranga, Mriga-matrika (different kinds of deer) or Sasa (hare)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt`, lines 904-906). The spelling differs and the text does not say it is the same animal, so it is not merged here. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Animal flesh is mostly water and muscle protein, with fat that varies by species, and supplies iron, zinc and B vitamins. No analysis of the animal Sushruta calls Kalapuchchaka was read, and its species is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced record is the animal's name in a list of shore-dwelling animals, with the qualities the text gives that group. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no use. The animal cannot be named with confidence, so no one could follow the text even if they wished to; wild animals are protected by wildlife law in many countries. A reader who eats no meat has nothing to adopt.

### Preparation & Dosing

None is given for the Kalapuchchaka. Ch.46 elsewhere describes ways of preparing meat in general, but none is attributed to it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Wild game can carry zoonotic infections and parasites, and thorough cooking with hygienic handling is the standard precaution. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 for the *Kulachara* group generally (lines 682-686): sweet in taste and in digestion; cooling; tonic; demulcent; diuretic; spermatopoietic; destroys deranged *Vayu* and *Kapham*; increases *Kapham*. The text gives no quality for the Kalapuchchaka by name, and none is assigned here beyond the group's. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Kalapuchchaka is named only in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food, not about this flesh, and the animal has no sentence of its own. The group text calls the flesh "spermatopoietic" and "tonic", which are properties listed among foods and not a placement in a *vajikarana* or *rasayana* chapter; no line read puts the Kalapuchchaka in either. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta the Kalapuchchaka is a name in a list, with only its group's qualities attached, and the animal cannot be identified. The honest answer is nothing.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** wild game can carry bacteria and parasites; cook through.
- **Identity:** the text does not identify the animal, and the glossary gloss "A black-tailed bird" does not fit the group it is listed in; no species should be substituted on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Legal and conservation status:** wild animals are protected in many countries; status for any candidate species was not checked. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food or medicine is a moral question for many traditions; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
