# Statice thouini — Leimonion (Dioscorides 4-16); "Wintergreen" or "Sea Lavender" on the translators' lines

## *Statice thouini, the name of the plate caption printed under the heading of 4-16; the SUGGESTED lines of the chapter name Limonium and Pyrola ("Wintergreen") and, as "other usage", Statice limonium, Limonium vulgare and Statice maritima ("Sea Lavender, Wild Marsh Beet")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk lists the plant under this name (the name indexes in `Sources/*/extract/names.csv` were searched). The entry rests on one chapter of one text. The binomial is not Dioscorides' identification, and it is not even on the translators' SUGGESTED line. *Statice thouini* is the caption of a plate ("Statice thouini after FAGUET — 1892", line 8152) printed between the heading and the SUGGESTED lines, and the name index (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 596) took it from there. The translators' own SUGGESTED lines (8154 to 8156) give something else. The Codex does not state the identity as certain, and it cannot say that the plant of the chapter is the plant of the plate. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-16. LEIMONION* at line 8150, the plate caption at line 8152, SUGGESTED lines at 8154 to 8156, text at line 8157. The chapter stands in Book Four, "Other Herbs & Roots", after *4-15b. SAXIPHRAGON* (page number 559 is printed inside that chapter) and before *4-17. LAGOPOUS* (line 8159). The SUGGESTED lines read: "Limonium, Pyrola [Fuchs], Pyrola rotundifolia major [Bauhin], Pyrola rotundifolia [Linnaeus] — Wintergreen" and, as "[other usage]", "Statice limonium, Limonium vulgare, Statice maritima — Sea Lavender, Wild Marsh Beet". These are two different groups of plants, and the translators print both.

The whole chapter reads: "Limonium has ten or more leaves similar to beet yet thinner and smaller, and a thin upright stalk equal (as it were) to the lily, full of red seed, astringent to the taste. An acetabulum [vinegar cruet] of the seed (pounded into small pieces and taken in a drink with wine) is able to help dysentery and abdominal cavities, and stop women's excessive bloody menstrual discharges. It grows in fields. It is also called neuroides, potamogeton, lonchitis, or rapronium; the Mysians call it mendruta, the Syrians, meuda, also, lycosemphyllon, helleborosemata, or scyllion; the Magi call it cor lupi, the Romans, veratrum nigrum, some, tintinabulum terrae, the Gauls, iumbarum, and the Dacians, dacina."

The description is for recognition: ten or more leaves like beet but thinner and smaller, a thin upright stalk "equal (as it were) to the lily", and the stalk "full of red seed". The seed is "astringent to the taste". The one preparation is the seed, pounded small and drunk in wine, and the three claims are for dysentery and the "abdominal cavities" and against excessive menstrual bleeding. The habitat is fields. The chapter then prints a long list of other names, among them peoples' names (Mysians, Syrians, Gauls, Dacians), the Roman *veratrum nigrum* and the "Magi" name *cor lupi*. These are names recorded by Dioscorides and not texts of those peoples.

**Index.** The translators' indexes disagree about the page: "Statice thouini 558" (line 11890), "Limonium 559" (line 11752), and "Limonium vulgare 560" and "LEIMONION 560" (lines 16319 and 12197). The chapter itself comes after the page number 559 printed in 4-15b, which fits 560. The "558" is not checked further. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for the chapter number, the Greek heading and the names *Statice*, *Limonium* and *Sea Lavender* found no entry on 4-16. I_1168 (*Potamogeton natans*, chapter 4-101) quotes this chapter's name list because *potamogeton* is also printed there, and states that it is a different plant. The genus *Pyrola* appears in I_884 only in a Tibetan row and is unrelated to this chapter. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The text prints no constituent. No analysis of the plant Dioscorides describes was read. It is a general fact that *Statice* and *Limonium* (sea lavenders) belong to the leadwort family (Plumbaginaceae), and that *Pyrola* (wintergreens) is not related to them, so a composition cannot be given for "the" plant of this chapter. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records a seed drunk in wine for dysentery, abdominal complaints and excessive menstrual bleeding. These are Dioscorides' claims and not Codex protocols. Because the plant cannot be identified with confidence, and because dysentery and heavy menstrual bleeding are conditions for medical care, the Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives one preparation with a quantity: "An acetabulum [vinegar cruet] of the seed (pounded into small pieces and taken in a drink with wine)". The volume of an acetabulum is not stated in the chapter, and the bracketed gloss "vinegar cruet" is the translators'. No frequency or duration is given. No modern dose is offered here. ### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] It is a general, well-established fact that dysentery and heavy menstrual bleeding have many causes and need medical assessment, and that astringency as a taste does not show that a seed treats either.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** chapter 4-16 prints one quality, that the seed is "astringent to the taste". It prints no hot, cold, moist or dry quality.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a description for recognition (the number and size of the leaves against beet, the stalk against the lily, the red seed), a taste check (astringent), a habitat (fields) and a long record of the names under which different peoples called the plant. That record also shows how unstable the identification is: the same chapter is read by the translators as two unrelated plants. The chapter assigns the plant no cultivation status. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The transferable point is the caution about naming: a plate caption, a wintergreen and a sea lavender are all attached to one Greek chapter. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

Chapter 4-16 prints no warning. The identification is not settled, and the translators' two groups of suggestions are unrelated plants, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Dysentery and heavy menstrual bleeding need medical care and must not be left to a seed in wine. The text gives no safety information for pregnancy. [USER: verify]
