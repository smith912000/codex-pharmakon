# Svarjika alkali — Svarjika-kshara (as printed in Bhishagratna's Sushruta Samhita)
## *An alkali (kshara) of the Sushruta group of alkalis, printed "Svarjikakshara" in Sutrasthana ch.46 and "Svarjika" in the Chikitsasthana; the translator glosses its companion Javakshara as carbonate of potash and Tankana as borax but gives no gloss for this one, so its identity is not fixed in the sources retrieved* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The locus given for this entry is `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, line 2089 (Sutrasthana, chapter 46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink", line 4; the same text is in `Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`). The chapter opens by linking food to bodily strength, complexion and the "albuminous (Ojas) principle" (lines 10-12), and then runs through groups of substances: the salts (line 2025), the alkalis (line 2087) and the metals (line 2121).

The passage reads (lines 2087-2100): "The group of Alkalis.—The different varieties of alkali (Kshara) such as the Javakshara (Carbonate of potash), the Svarjikakshara, the Pakima and the Tankana cure abdominal glands, piles, mesenteric diarrhoea, and gravel and stone in the bladder. All these varieties of alkali should be regarded as possessed of digestant or stomachic properties. Their abuse is known to have brought about cases of haemoptysis. Of these, the varieties known as the Javakshara and Svarjikakshara are inflammatory as fire, and they tend to reduce Kapham, remove Vibandha (suppression of stool etc.), piles, and abdominal glands and prove curative in cases of enlarged spleen. They are anti-spermatopoietic." The text then goes on to Usharakshara, Pakima and Tankana, with properties of their own (lines 2107-2118). So Svarjikakshara has two kinds of statement. As one of the four alkalis named in the opening sentence it shares in the group's cures (abdominal glands, piles, mesenteric diarrhoea, gravel and stone in the bladder), in its "digestant or stomachic" character and in the warning about abuse. Together with Javakshara alone, it is "inflammatory as fire", reduces *kapha*, removes *vibandha*, and is "anti-spermatopoietic". The properties printed for the other alkalis (Usharakshara "heat-making" and "detrimental to the strength of the body"; Pakima a reducer of obesity and diuretic; Tankana drying and sharp) are not carried over to it.

A search of the Sushruta chapter files for the name turns up the substance in use in recipes, written "Svarjika" in some chapters and "Sarjika" in others. With the spelling "Svarjika": the oil of *Jyotishka* seed "mixed with milk, Svarjikā and Asafoetida", among remedies in the chapter on dropsy (*udara*; Chikitsa ch.14, lines 157-161); a plaster of "Svarjikā, sulphate of copper, sulphate of iron, Vidanga, Agara-dhuma, Chitraka, Katuka, Sudhā, turmeric and Saindhava" pounded with the urine or bile of a cow, and another of "Svarjikā, Kushtha, sulphate of copper, Kutaja, chitraka, Vidanga, Maricha and Manah-sila" (Chikitsa ch.9, lines 201-203, 219-220, the chapter on skin diseases, *kushtha*); a pulverised compound of "Svarjikā, Tuttha, Kāsisa, Shaileya, Rasānjana and Manahsilā" that "arrests the extension of an ulcer" (Chikitsa ch.19, lines 308-311); the "alkalies (Kshara) of oyster-shells, Svarjika, and of Yava" plastered on a non-suppurated *aja-gallika*, with a footnote mark after the word (Chikitsa ch.20, lines 16-17, the chapter on minor ailments); "Svarjikā and Tila, Tejohvā, Danti and Chitraka roots" in a purifying remedy for a malignant ulcer due to aggravated *kapha* (Chikitsa ch.2, lines 543-548, on recent wounds); and "Svarjikā-kshāra" among the many ingredients of the *Hingvadi-vati*, a pill of asafoetida, Trikatu and salts treated with the expressed juice of *Matulunga* and taken, one a morning on an empty stomach, in diseases of deranged *vayu* (Chikitsa ch.5, lines 678-690). With the spelling "Sarjikā" or "Sarjikā-kshāra": a lick with honey (Uttara ch.50, line 117), compounds for *gulma* (Uttara ch.42, lines 285-291, with a footnote quoting Dallana on how the compounds divide), a ghrita (Uttara ch.42, line 229), a burnt ash taken with tepid water (Uttara ch.42, line 639), an oil (Uttara ch.39, line 1397), and a paste with honey on an overgrown gum (Chikitsa ch.22, line 162). The sources retrieved do not say that "Sarjikā-kshāra" and "Svarjikakshara" are one substance, though the spelling suggests it. [USER: verify]

No other tradition is tagged for this entry. The Sushruta saline group in Sutrasthana ch.42 (line 321) names Saindhava, Sauvarchala, Vida, Fakya, Romaka, Samudraka, Paktrima, Yavakshara, Ushara and Suvarchika, and not Svarjika.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent of Svarjika. In general terms, the alkalis of Ayurveda (*kshara*) are alkaline salts, usually obtained by leaching the ash of burnt plants or from mineral deposits, and the translator glosses two of its companions as carbonate of potash (Javakshara) and borax (Tankana). No such gloss is given for Svarjika, so what it was chemically cannot be said from the sources. Alkaline salts in general are caustic or irritant to tissue in strong solution, and carbonates and borates differ in their toxicity. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved say that Sushruta listed Svarjikakshara among the alkalis for abdominal glands, piles, mesenteric diarrhoea and stone, as "inflammatory as fire", as a reducer of *kapha*, and as "anti-spermatopoietic", and that its ash or salt was an ingredient in plasters, pills and licks. The substance cannot be identified from the sources, alkalis are caustic and can cause serious harm when swallowed, and no part of the sources supports a modern recommendation. The text itself warns that abuse of the alkalis has led to haemoptysis.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give Svarjika as an ingredient in the compounds above, and give one measure for one of them: the *Hingvadi-vati* is made into boluses each "weighing an Aksha (two Tolas)", one taken each morning on an empty stomach (Chikitsa ch.5, lines 684-688); that dose belongs to the compound and not to the alkali alone, whose own weight in it is not given in these lines. In chapter 46 no preparation is given, only the properties. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Alkaline salts such as carbonates and borates are irritants to the gut and to tissue, and some (borates) are toxic when swallowed in quantity; strong alkali burns skin and mucous membranes. Salts of this kind have been used as antacids and laxatives in small amounts, but there is no evidence that an alkali treats piles, stones or an enlarged spleen. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** the group statement gives all the alkalis "digestant or stomachic properties" (lines 2092-2094). For Svarjikakshara with Javakshara the text prints "inflammatory as fire" (lines 2096-2097) and actions (reduces *kapha*, removes *vibandha*, piles, abdominal glands; curative in enlarged spleen; "anti-spermatopoietic"). It prints no *rasa* (taste), no *virya* (potency) in the technical sense and no *vipaka* (post-digestive taste) for Svarjikakshara; the "heat-making in its potency" of line 2107 belongs to Usharakshara and is not transferred. No other tradition is tagged.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Svarjikakshara is named in the food-and-drink chapter among the salts, alkalis and metals that Sushruta lists for the qualities they impart to food and body (chapter 46, lines 10-19, 2087-2100). That chapter links food in general to the *ojas* principle (lines 10-12), but it does not link Svarjikakshara to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*, and the passage is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* passage. Of the alkalis, the text says this one and Javakshara are "anti-spermatopoietic", which is a statement about an effect on the semen-producing tissue and the opposite of a generative-vitality (*vajikarana*) claim; it is a property listed in a group, not a prohibition. No *shodhana* (purification) of the alkali is described in the lines read. The text's own caution, that "their abuse is known to have brought about cases of haemoptysis", is the opposite of a cultivation claim. The *sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas* categories are Samkhya and are not in the passage.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. Svarjikakshara is an alkali used in Sushruta's therapeutics for the bowels, the abdomen, the skin and wounds, and a strong alkali is not food. The honest use for a reader is to know that the text records it, that its identity is not glossed, and that the same passage warns against abuse and calls it anti-spermatopoietic. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Caustic and irritant:** alkalis burn the mouth, throat and gut when swallowed in strength; do not swallow an unidentified alkaline salt.
- **Abuse:** the text itself reports haemoptysis from abuse of the alkalis (lines 2094-2095).
- **Fertility and semen:** the text calls Svarjikakshara and Javakshara "anti-spermatopoietic"; no sourced reading of this beyond the word.
- **Open wounds and ulcers:** the plasters in the text put an alkali on ulcers, wounds and the gums; strong alkali damages tissue and is not a substitute for care.
- **Piles, stone, bleeding, enlarged spleen:** these need clinical diagnosis; bleeding, severe pain or retention of urine is an emergency.
- **Identification:** "Svarjika" is Bhishagratna's rendering, with no gloss; the "Sarjika" of other chapters may or may not be the same substance.
