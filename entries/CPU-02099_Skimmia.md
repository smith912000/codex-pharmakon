# Skimmia — Yin Yu 茵芋 (Shennong bencao jing); alias Bei Gong 卑共 (Wu Pu), Wan Cao 莞草 (Mingyi)

## *Skimmia reevesiana (Fortune) Fortune, a low evergreen shrub of the citrus family whose leaf is the drug yin yu; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence at species level, since the classic describes no plant, S. japonica is also used under the name, and Yang 1998 prints "Folium Skimmiae Japonicae"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** This is a lower-class drug that the Wu Pu layer describes as "slightly warm, toxic" (微温，有毒). This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take this plant.

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; the other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), is read as a second witness and attributed to Yang. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Folium Skimmiae Japonicae [SBJ]", taken from Yang's English; this entry replaces it. A search of the other texts on disk for *Skimmia* and for the Chinese and pinyin names found nothing for this drug.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 茵芋 in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 茵芋 *yin yu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3339, 味 paragraph; name line 3337). Sun's paragraph is damaged in this e-text. It reads: 味苦，温 ("bitter, warm"); 主五脏邪气 ("governs evil qi in the five viscera"), 心腹寒热 ("cold and heat in the heart and abdomen"), 羸瘦如疟状，发作有时 ("emaciation resembling malaria, with attacks at set times"), 诸关节风 ("wind in the various joints"), and then it breaks off at 风 and runs straight into the Wu Pu layer. The end of the indications, any alias, and the habitat are lost in this file. There is no 久服 clause in what survives.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, same line 3339) gives: 茵芋，一名卑共。微温，有毒。状如莽草而细软 ("*yin yu*, one name *Bei gong*. Slightly warm, toxic. In form like *mang cao* but fine and soft"; citing the *Yulan*). The comparison with *mang cao* is the only description of the plant in the sources read. The Mingyi bielu layer (line 3341) adds the aliases 莞草 and 卑共, the habitat 生太山 ("grows on Mount Tai") and a harvest: 三月三日采叶，阴干 ("the leaf is gathered on the third day of the third month and dried in the shade"). This is the only place that names the part used, the leaf. No Sun note (案) is printed for this entry.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 3418): "Yin Yu (Folium Skimmiae Japonicae) is bitter and warm. It mainly treats evil qi in the five viscera, cold and heat of the heart and abdomen with languor and emaciation, [cold and heat] which attack at regular intervals like malaria, and wind dampness impediment pain in various joints. It grows in rivers and valleys." (The OCR of Yang's page is damaged at "regular".) Yang therefore supplies the end of the indications (wind-damp impediment pain in the joints) and the habitat that Sun's e-text has lost. Yang's footnote 210 (line 3436) is modern commentary and not part of the classic: "Nowadays, this medicinal is seldom used. In olden times, it was prescribed as a ruling medicinal for treating wind epilepsy. This is either a kind of epilepsy with diverse signs or an epilepsy whose main manifestations are tremor of the extremities, head shaking, and clenched jaw." The "wind epilepsy" use is Yang's footnote and is not in the classic's paragraph.

**Appendix.** A search of the drug-relations table (from line 4200) found no entry for 茵芋.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Skimmia* belongs to the citrus family (Rutaceae), whose members characteristically carry aromatic oils and alkaloids; the genus is grown as an ornamental and its leaves and berries are generally regarded as poisonous if eaten. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Toxic.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Evil qi in the five viscera, cold and heat in the heart and abdomen, emaciation with attacks like malaria, and (in Yang's complete text) wind-damp pain in the joints.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation beyond drying the leaf, no duration.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest: the leaf, gathered on the third day of the third month and dried in the shade (line 3341). The preface contains a general rule of method for poisonous drugs (line 4125) that begins with a very small amount and stops when the illness goes. It is the classic's rule of method for toxic drugs in general and is not a dose for this plant. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Skimmia* species are small evergreen shrubs of the Himalaya and East Asia grown in gardens, and the citrus family to which they belong is known for aromatic oils and alkaloids. The Wu Pu layer's "toxic" agrees in kind with the usual view of the genus as poisonous. [USER: verify] The classic's malaria-like attacks and wind-damp impediment have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic, whose preface (line 2935) reads 下药，一百二十五种，为左使。主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经: the lower drugs are "the left envoys, mainly treating disease to correspond with earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to remove cold and heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and heal illness". The paragraph, so far as it survives, has no 久服 clause and no promise of lightening the body or of not ageing. The register is therefore (b): framed by the lower class, whose own preface is the warning, and with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade, toxic leaf for malaria-like attacks, joint wind and wasting, now "seldom used" by Yang's own account. The tradition's own grading is the warning, and nothing in the record supports any cultivation use. A cultivator should not use it.

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC.** The Wu Pu layer marks it toxic, and the genus is generally regarded as poisonous. Do not ingest it. Do not gather it.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Given the toxicity, avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Malaria-like fevers, wasting, epilepsy.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Identity.** Two *Skimmia* species and other plants are sold under the name; the species is our identification, of medium confidence. [USER: verify]
- **Damaged text.** The Shennong paragraph is cut off in Sun's e-text; the full indications are taken from Yang.
