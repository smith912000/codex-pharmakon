# Amvashth — Amvashtha (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation, 1907)

## *Amvashtha, a plant drug that heads a group in Sutrasthana ch. 38. Identification: Stephania hernandifolia (Willd.) Walp. (Menispermaceae) by a positional pairing of "Ambashtha" in the botanical appendix of Bhishagratna's Vol. III; confidence low to medium, since the appendix spelling differs from the group-list spelling and the pairing is by position in an OCR column* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk. This is a single-text entry.** Spellings in quotations are normalised silently from the OCR where the damage is plain (for example Nandi for the printed Xandi); the line numbers cited give the text as printed. "Amvashtha" was searched in all three volumes of Bhishagratna (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, `Vol2`, `Vol3`) and in the chapter files. The group-list spelling "Amvashth^" (the last letter is OCR damage) occurs only in Sutrasthana ch. 38.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The locus is Sutrasthana ch. 38, the Amvashthadi group (`Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15667 to 15675; chapter file `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 326): "Drugs known as Amvashtha, Dhataki flowers, Samanga, Katvanga, Madhuka, Vilva-peshika, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Palasha, Nandi-Vriksha and Padma-keshara, enter into the composition of the group known as the Amvashthadi Gana." (Names as printed; the OCR prints "Xandi" where "Nandi" is meant.) The group takes its name from this drug, which stands first in the list.

The metrical text that follows is shared with the group listed just before it, the Priyangvadi group (line 15660): "The two medicinal recipes or groups prove beneficial in a case of deranged Pitta, favour the healing of ulcers, bring about the adhesion of fractured bones and prove curative in cases of dysentery where the stools are found to consist of lumps of thick and matured mucous (Pakvatisara)." No separate sentence is printed for the Amvashthadi group alone. The text names no part used and no preparation for Amvashtha.

**Identification.** Bhishagratna's Vol. III Appendix 3 prints Sanskrit names in a left column and Latin names in a right column. Read by position, "Ambashtha" (OCR "AmbasbtbsC", `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 31177) is paired with "Stephania hernandifolia" (Latin column, line 31228, directly after "Nelumbium speciosum" at line 31227). The neighbouring names pair consistently (Ambhoja with the lotus, Amalaka with Phyllanthus emblica, Ambu with Pavonia odorata), which supports the positional reading. The spelling differs, and the appendix gives no statement that it is the Sutra 38 drug. [USER: verify]

No occurrence of Amvashtha was found in Chikitsa ch. 26 to 31 (vajikarana and rasayana).

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent for this drug. In general terms, the genus Stephania (moonseed family) is known for isoquinoline alkaloids. Whether this applies to the drug as Sushruta used it was not checked. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Amvashthadi group, with the Priyangvadi group: deranged Pitta, healing of ulcers, knitting of fractured bones, and dysentery with thick mucous stools (Sutra ch. 38).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no preparation.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this drug.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given for Amvashtha alone. Chapter 38 closes with a rule for all the groups: "Only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group, or a similar number of drugs chosen from the different groups, or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety, or in combination with another, should be used according to the indications of any particular case, as the physician, in his discretion, would determine" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, near the end; Vol. I, ch. XXXVIII). It also says that the groups "will be more elaborately dealt with later on in the chapter on Therapeutics". This is a rule of method for the groups, not a dose for this drug. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. If the identification holds, the plant is a climbing herb of the moonseed family, a family known for alkaloid-bearing plants. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Amvashtha. The only qualities given are those of the paired groups (Sutra ch. 38, as quoted above): beneficial in deranged Pitta, favouring the healing of ulcers, uniting fractured bones, and curative in Pakvatisara.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is attested in the Sushruta Samhita, in the therapeutic groups of Sutrasthana ch. 38 and the other passages cited above, and its role there is therapeutic: groups are said to subdue Kapha, Vata or Pitta, to act as vermifuge or aseptic agents, and so on. Chikitsa ch. 26 (the vajikarana chapter, "increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons") and chs. 27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) were searched under the spellings printed here (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` to `ch30.txt`, and ch. 31) and the name was not found in them. The drug therefore carries no rasayana or vajikarana status in the sources retrieved, and nothing is said of ojas for it. The register is (b): framed by the therapeutic gana, with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A group-heading drug for Pitta, ulcers, fractures and dysentery, with an uncertain identification. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use.

### Contraindications

- **Identity uncertain.** Do not substitute any Stephania or other climber on the strength of this entry.
- **Fractures, ulcers, dysentery.** Medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
