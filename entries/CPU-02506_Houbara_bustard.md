# Houbara bustard — لحم الحبارى (laḥm al-ḥubārā), the flesh of the houbara

## *The flesh of the houbara bustard,* Chlamydotis *(Arabic* ḥubārā*; the species is the modern identification)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** TBN

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Prophetic medicine (TBN).** The houbara is named only by Ibn Qayyim's *al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī* among the texts on disk. This is a short entry because the text is short. It is read in the Arabic (`Sources/TBN_Prophetic/text/IbnQayyim_TibbNabawi_OpenITI_Shamela0023649.txt`, letter *lām*, entry *laḥm al-ḥubārā*, line 6661, two sentences).

Hadith: "In the *Sunan*, from the hadith of Burayh ibn ʿUmar ibn Safīna, from his father, from his grandfather, may God be pleased with him, who said: 'I ate with the Messenger of God the flesh of the houbara'" (في «السنن» ... أكلت مع رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم لحم حبارى). Ibn Qayyim does not name which of the *Sunan* collections it is in and gives no verdict on the chain in this passage. I did not find it in the Bukhari or Muslim files on disk and have not traced it further. [USER: verify the collection and the grading of this report.]

Medical judgement: "it is hot and dry, hard to digest, beneficial for people of exercise and fatigue" (وهو حار يابس، عسر الانهضام، نافع لأصحاب الرياضة والتعب). That is the whole of his statement. The Prophetic report establishes only that the Prophet is reported to have eaten it with a companion; Ibn Qayyim attaches no further virtue to it.

**Identity.** Arabic *ḥubārā* is the houbara bustard; the genus *Chlamydotis* (Asian and African houbara) is the modern identification, and the text gives no species. The identification of the name with the bird is secure; the species is not. [USER: verify if a species is wanted.]

### Active Components

The flesh of a bird: protein and fat; nothing specific is stated in the text, and none is asserted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The text records that it was eaten and that it suits those who toil. It is not offered as a recommended practice, because the bird is protected or regulated in much of its range (see Contraindications).

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Ibn Qayyim (TBN).** Hot and dry (no degree given); hard to digest (*ʿasir al-inhiḍām*).

### Cultivation Register

**Prophetic medicine (TBN).** *(b) Framed.* The frame is *sunna* as report: a companion's account that he ate houbara with the Prophet. That is a report about food in the Prophet's company, not a claim of a life-lengthening or cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to cultivate beyond ordinary food. The cultivation question here is restraint: the text records a meal, and the bird is now a conservation concern.

### Contraindications

Historical record only. The text itself notes the flesh is hard to digest. The houbara is threatened (the IUCN lists the African and Asian houbaras as Vulnerable) and its hunting is regulated or banned in many countries; do not take this entry as a reason to hunt or eat it.
