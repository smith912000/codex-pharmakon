# Yeast (barm, lees) — Yeast / Yeast-of-Wine / Yeast-of-Beer / Yeast-of-Sweet-Beer (Bryan's renderings)
## *Yeast: the fermenting ferment of wine and beer, in Bryan's English forms; the Egyptian words are not printed in the text on disk* | *(Volume IV — Fungal/Algal, shelved in Volume I)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 translation of the Ebers papyrus. In the list of remedies the translator's introduction groups under the heading "MINERAL REMEDIES" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` lines 1134-1221), the entry reads "Yeast Yeast-of-Wine Yeast-of-Beer Yeast-of-Sweet-Beer Yeast-of-Bitter-Beer / Yeast-of-Beer-that-is-Fermenting / Yeast-of-Beer-that-has-Fermented" (lines 1213-1215), with a further "Yeast-of-Beer-that-has-been-whipped-up Yeast-of-the-Opium-drink" at line 1221. The same forms are recorded in `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` (row at line 41, "Bryan 1930, Ch. V Mineral Remedies (line 1213)"). That row classes the item "mineral", which is the list's heading and not a judgement that yeast is a mineral: the list also holds soot, wax and writing-fluid. In the Codex it is shelved with fungi because yeast is a fungus; Bryan does not say so, and the papyrus knows only the substance. Because the Ebers material is organised by complaint, the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis; here it is Bryan's English.

Bryan's own comment on the vehicles of the papyrus names yeast in passing: "Thus Beer, a favourite vehicle, was prescribed as Plain Beer, Sweet Beer, Bitter Beer, Cold Beer, Warmed Beer, Flat Beer, Yeast-of-Beer, Froth-of-Beer, Beer-which-has-been-Brewed-from-many-Ingredients" (line 1131). In the prescriptions read, yeast is a medium in which another substance is rubbed, crushed or drunk, or an ingredient of a compound. The occurrences are:

- **Internal.** For roundworm, "Inner-of-the-Fruit-of-the-Caster-oil-tree Yeast Water... Keep moist, strain, and take for a day" (lines 1958-1960). For a displaced womb, "The-Film-of-Dampness-which-is-found-on-the-Wood-of-Ships (mould) / Rub in Yeast-of-Fermented-Beer and let her drink it" (lines 2564-2567).
- **Suppository.** A "Peg (suppository) for the Man or Woman" among remedies "to heal the diseased anus" includes "Yeast-of-Wine" (lines 2036-2052).
- **Burns.** In the series headed "AGAINST BURNS" (line 2247), the remedy for the second day: "Goat's dung. Burn, crush, rub in Yeast-that-is-fermenting, and put thereto" (lines 2254-2255).
- **Wound dressing.** "Human excrement. Crush in Yeast-of-Sweet-Beer, sefet-oil, and Honey, and apply as a Poultice" (lines 2323-2325).
- **To the phallus.** "Wood-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus. Mix in Yeast-of-the-mesta-drink, and anoint the Phallus therewith" (lines 2431-2432).
- **Suppleness.** In the passage on making things supple, a salve of "Flesh-of-the-Shadfish and Honey beaten up in Yeast-of-Sweet-Beer" (line 2182); another with "Yeast-of-the-Opium-drink, Goat's Fat, Lettuce, Onions, Beans, and White Oil" (lines 2186-2188); and, for making the *met* supple, a liniment of "Bullock's fat, Yeast-of-Wine, Garlic, Saltpetre-from-the-South, Poppy-berries, and Oil-of-Myrrh" with the scribe's note "Do not let it dry" (line 3170).
- **Itch and scabs.** A poultice of "Elderberries, Linseed, Wormwood, Natron, and various native plants, rubbed in Semen-of-a-Man, Yeast-of-Wine, and Juice-of-the-Wild-Date" (line 2633); for "Scabs in every Limb", a poultice of "Clay-from-the-Wall, Wheaten Flour, and Fat-of-the-deher-animal, strained into Yeast-of-Sweet-Beer" (line 2644).
- **Back and neck remedies.** A "Back-remedy" prescription begins "Make for him a pot-yeast and then make him the following remedy" (line 3748), and its remedy is crushed and cooked in "Yeast-of-Sweet-Beer" and smeared on for four days (lines 3760-3762). For a growth in the neck, the compound lists "Garlic Palm-juice ... Sea-salt Yeast Bean-meal ... Honey" (line 3788). Bryan's rendering "pot-yeast" is unexplained in the passage, and what it was is not shown. [USER: verify]

Note that some of these prescriptions (human excrement, semen, dung) are recorded as the papyrus gives them and are not endorsed here.

### Active Components

Stated generally. Yeast is a single-celled fungus that ferments sugars to ethanol and carbon dioxide. Its cells contain protein, B-group vitamins and cell-wall polysaccharides (glucans and mannans). Brewer's yeast and wine lees are also rich in the dead cells and sediment of fermentation. "Yeast-of-Beer-that-is-Fermenting" and "that-has-Fermented" (line 1214-1215) are stages of the same fermentation, and the active material in a living ferment would include live cells and the alcohol and acids produced. No figures were read in the sources retrieved.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. In the passages read, yeast is a carrier or ferment in remedies for a defined complaint, and most of those remedies are external. The papyrus gives no account of what yeast does to the person. Many of the compound prescriptions it appears in contain faecal or other animal materials, which are not a modern practice. No modern protocol is supplied.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the text gives are those above: yeast as a medium for rubbing or crushing another substance (burn, wound, scab, womb remedies); as an ingredient of a salve (supple-making, with fat and honey); as a suppository ingredient; and, for the roundworm remedy, "Keep moist, strain, and take for a day" (lines 1958-1960). The "Yeast-of-Beer-that-has-been-whipped-up" form (line 1221) is named without a recipe. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*). Yeast appears as a vehicle or ingredient in dressings, salves, suppositories and a few drinks (loci above). Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); the prescriptions quoted here are Bryan's summaries and carry no recitation in the lines read, which does not prove that the papyrus gives none. No deity is attributed. The identity of the substance is Bryan's English, a modern hypothesis.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator. Yeast in the Ebers is a working ingredient in remedies for specific complaints, often alongside materials that are not acceptable now. For a cultivator the honest use of this entry is as history. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Infection and hygiene:** the papyrus uses yeast in dressings for wounds and burns together with excrement and other animal matter; applying fermenting or faecal material to broken skin risks serious infection.
- **Alcohol and fermentation:** fermenting beer yeast and lees contain alcohol and are not suitable for children, in pregnancy, or for people avoiding alcohol.
- **Fungal infection:** people who are immunocompromised can develop invasive infections from yeasts.
- **Allergy:** yeast allergy and sensitivity to brewer's yeast occur.
- **Ethics and legality:** this entry gives no endorsement to any of the animal-derived or human-derived components named in the prescriptions it quotes.
