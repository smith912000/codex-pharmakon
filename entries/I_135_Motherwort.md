# I_135 — Leonurus cardiaca | Motherwort / Lion's Tail / Throwwort / Heart Herb

**Volume I: Plant-Based Substances**
**Entry:** I_135
**Convergence Score:** 4/9 traditions
**Energetic Tier:** ◉ Qi (cardiac/uterine) | ☿ Shen (anxiolytic/nervine)
**Safety Class:** Moderate caution; uterotonic (avoid in pregnancy); photosensitizing potential; large doses emetic; generally safe at therapeutic doses

---

## Section 1 — Nomenclature

| Register | Name |
|----------|------|
| Scientific | *Leonurus cardiaca* L. (Lamiaceae) |
| DMM (Greek) | Not a major DMM entry; Dioscorides mentions related plants; *Leonurus* identification in classical sources is contested |
| CM (Arabic/Persian) | Ufrasiyā / Jirjīr; some texts include *Leonurus*-like plants under uterine nervine category |
| SBJ | **Yì mǔ cǎo (益母草 — "benefiting-mother herb")** — one of the most important gynecological herbs in TCM; *Leonurus japonicus* is the Asian species; classical Shennong Bencao Jing and Bencao Gangmu (both); major uterine tonic and cardiovascular herb |
| FMT | Possibly identified in Tibetan obstetric and cardiac formulas under shug-pa or sha-wa related herb categories |
| European folk | Motherwort (mother = uterine; wort = herb); Lion's Tail (for the lion-shaped flower whorls = Leonurus); Throwwort (throws out retained matter); Heart Herb (for cardiac applications) |
| German folk | Herzgespann ("heart-clasp" — the clasping whorled leaves around the stem, and "heart-grip" for cardiac palpitations); Löwenschwanz (Lion's tail) |
| Russian folk | Пустырник (Pustyrnik) — "wasteland herb" (grows in disturbed habitats); Russia has longest clinical trial tradition for motherwort; used as cardiovascular sedative throughout Soviet-era medicine |

**Botanical identity:** Erect perennial to 1.2m; square stems; deeply lobed leaves (3-lobed, then 5-lobed toward base — the "lion's tail" shape); pink-purple flowers in dense axillary whorls with spiny bracts; sharp spines on calyx. Grows in disturbed, waste ground, near human settlement — another cosmopolitan medicinal weed. *Leonurus japonicus* (Yi mu cao) is the Asian species; pharmacologically similar but with some alkaloid profile differences.

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**Score: 4/9** (SBJ confirmed; CM · FMT · European folk tradition)

The 4/9 convergence conceals an important pharmacological fact: SBJ's *yì mǔ cǎo* tradition is enormous — one of the most important gynecological herbs in the entire Chinese pharmacopoeia, appearing in hundreds of classical formulas. The European tradition is smaller but clinically documented (Commission E approval). The convergence is real and the pharmacological overlap (uterotonic + cardiac sedative) between TCM and European traditions is independently derived.

**The uterine-cardiac duality** is the defining pharmacological convergence: motherwort is simultaneously a uterine stimulant (emmenagogue, aids postpartum involution) AND a cardiac sedative (reduces tachycardia, calms palpitations). These applications exist independently in both Chinese and European medicine, suggesting the dual pharmacology is real enough to be detected empirically in both traditions without cross-cultural communication about these specific applications.

**Stachydrine** (the principal alkaloid, shared with *Stachys* and *Marrubium*) is uterotonic. **Leonurine** (Asian species-specific) is potently uterotonic. **Iridoid fraction** contributes cardiac sedation. The plant pharmacologically IS what both traditions say it is.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Alkaloids:**
- **Stachydrine** (proline betaine) — principal alkaloid in *L. cardiaca*; uterotonic; anti-platelet; cardiovascular (positive inotropic + negative chronotropic — increases contractility, decreases rate); also anti-arrhythmic potential
- **Leonurine** (4-guanidino-butyl syringate) — more abundant in Asian *L. japonicus*; potently uterotonic (dose-dependent oxytocic); also cardioprotective in ischemia models

**Iridoids:**
- **Leonuride** — antiarrhythmic; cardiac sedative (negative chronotropic)
- Ajugoside, ajugol — anti-inflammatory

**Diterpenes:**
- Leocardin, marrubenol — labdane diterpenes (similar class to marrubiin in horehound I_129)

**Flavonoids:**
- **Rutin** (also in rue I_133 — capillary-protective)
- Quercetin, kaempferol, hyperoside, luteolin — anti-inflammatory; antioxidant

**Phenolic acids:**
- Caffeic acid, chlorogenic acid, rosmarinic acid — antioxidant; anti-inflammatory

**Tannins:**
- Condensed tannins — astringent; mild antimicrobial

**Volatile oil (0.05%):**
- Minor; β-caryophyllene, germacrene — anti-inflammatory

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**Uterotonic / Emmenagogue:**
- **Stachydrine:** stimulates uterine smooth muscle via oxytocin receptor sensitization and direct smooth muscle contractility; explains emmenagogue and postpartum involution applications
- **Leonurine (Asian species):** even more potent uterotonic; dose-dependent; at high doses mimics oxytocin; at low doses antispasmodic (smooth muscle regulation, not simple stimulation)
- Combined alkaloid: reliable emmenagogue effect in amenorrhea and dysmenorrhea

**Cardiac sedative / Antiarrhythmic:**
- **Leonuride:** negative chronotropic (slows heart rate); L-type calcium channel blockade → reduced automaticity → fewer ectopic beats; anti-tachycardia
- **Stachydrine:** positive inotropic + negative chronotropic — increases contractile force while reducing rate; pattern similar to class III antiarrhythmic drugs in mild form
- **Mechanism summary:** decreases resting heart rate; reduces anxiety-driven tachycardia; calms functional palpitations (heart pounding without structural pathology)
- **Clinical implication:** motherwort is indicated for palpitations secondary to anxiety (nervine-cardiac dual action), not for structural arrhythmias requiring medical management

**Anxiolytic / Nervine:**
- Iridoid fraction: GABA-A receptor positive modulation (mild benzodiazepine-like mechanism; much weaker)
- Rutin: anti-anxiety in animal models
- Synergy: cardiac-sedative (heart stops pounding) + mild anxiolytic (anxiety reduced) → combined effect greater than either alone
- Traditional "motherwort calms the heart and the spirit" is pharmacologically coherent

**Anti-platelet / Cardiovascular:**
- Stachydrine: inhibits platelet aggregation (ADP-induced) → anti-thrombotic; reduces cardiovascular risk in traditional Chinese cardiovascular formulas

**Diuretic:**
- Leonurine + alkaloid fraction: mild diuretic (reduces edema); explains post-partum edema application

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Commission E (Germany): APPROVED.**
- Indication: cardiac disorders associated with nervous disorders; nervous excitability and associated heart disorders; also in ancillary treatment of hyperthyroid states (to reduce heart rate)

**WHO Monographs on Selected Medicinal Plants (Vol. 2, 2002):** Positively reviewed; confirms cardiac and uterine indications.

**Russian clinical tradition (most extensive):**
- USSR-era Soviet medicine used motherwort tincture (Pustyrnika) as a standard cardiovascular sedative; clinical observations from Soviet hospitals documented its efficacy for nervous tachycardia
- Pustyrnika extract tincture remains a standard OTC product in Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus
- Russian clinical studies: multiple small trials demonstrating reduction in heart rate, anxiety, blood pressure in patients with functional cardiovascular complaints

**Chinese clinical research:**
- Yì mǔ cǎo extensive clinical use for dysmenorrhea, postpartum uterine involution, irregular menstruation
- Compound formulas (Chinese medicine RCTs) — motherwort component consistently associated with gynecological outcomes but not isolated in single-agent RCTs

**Limitations:** No large-scale, placebo-controlled RCT for European motherwort in English-language literature. Evidence base is traditional use + mechanism + small clinical studies (primarily Soviet-era Russian).

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The name duality — maternal and cardiac:**
*Leonurus cardiaca* contains two clinical commitments in its name: *leonurus* (lion's tail — morphological) and *cardiaca* (heart herb — therapeutic). The species epithet is unusual in being a direct therapeutic designation rather than a place name, honorific, or morphological description. This makes *L. cardiaca* one of the few plants whose scientific name doubles as a therapeutic prescription: "the heart herb."

**TCM: Yì mǔ cǎo — "benefit-mother herb":**
The Chinese name is even more explicit: every character is a therapeutic declaration. Yì = benefit/assist; mǔ = mother; cǎo = herb. "The herb that benefits mothers." In TCM, this encompasses the full obstetric-gynecological tradition: assists labor, promotes postpartum involution, regulates menstruation, treats "blood stagnation" (dysmenorrhea, amenorrhea with clotting). The herb's status in TCM gynecology is comparable to mugwort (*A. vulgaris*, I_031) — a foundational obstetric-gynecological substance.

**Chinese Classical Formula — Yì Mǔ Cǎo Gāo (益母草膏):**
A concentrated paste preparation of motherwort, used for dysmenorrhea and gynecological blood stagnation. This formula has been continuously produced since at least the Song Dynasty (960–1279 CE) and is still commercially manufactured today. One of the longest continuously-produced Chinese herbal patent medicines.

**German Herzgespann:**
The German folk name captures the therapeutic experience: "Herzgespann" = the sensation of the heart being clasped, gripped, or contracted — the palpitation/cardiac anxiety symptom that motherwort was specifically used to treat. The name is not morphological but symptomatic: it describes the patient's complaint that the herb resolves. A pharmacological description encoded in folk nomenclature.

**Medieval European use — the "throwwort" tradition:**
The English folk name "Throwwort" (herb that throws out) refers to the emmenagogue action: the plant "throws out" retained menstrual blood, retained placenta, retained toxins. This expulsive-therapeutic concept runs through many European uterine herbs (birthwort I_124, artemisia, rue) and represents a consistent theoretical framework for uterine pharmacology.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

**Primary modern applications:**

1. **Palpitations / Functional tachycardia (primary):** 2–4g dried aerial parts as infusion, 3× daily; or tincture 1:5 in 25%, 2–4mL 3× daily; effective for anxiety-driven tachycardia, benign palpitations, sympathetic nervous system overactivation; NOT for structural heart disease
2. **Dysmenorrhea / Menstrual regulation:** same dose protocol; begin 3–5 days before expected menstruation; continue through first 2 days; reduces uterine cramping and promotes regular flow; most useful for amenorrhea and irregular cycles with accompanying pelvic congestion
3. **Menopausal complaints (nervousness + cardiovascular symptoms):** combine with sage (*Salvia officinalis*) and black cohosh (*Actaea racemosa*) for menopausal syndrome; motherwort addresses the cardiac palpitation component of hot flashes
4. **Anxiety with cardiac component:** combines well with valerian (I_090) and passionflower (*Passiflora incarnata*) for sustained anxiolytic effect with specific cardiac sedation

**Dose (European tradition):**
- Infusion: 1.5–4g dried herb per cup; steep 10 min; 3× daily
- Tincture (1:5 in 25%): 2–4mL 3× daily
- Commission E dose: 4.5g daily divided (infusion) or 1–2mL tincture 3× daily

**Dose (TCM tradition — *L. japonicus*):**
- Decoction: 10–30g dried herb per day in formula
- Patent medicine Yì mǔ cǎo gāo: as directed

**Contraindications:**
- **Pregnancy: ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION.** Stachydrine and leonurine are potently uterotonic; reliable abortifacient at medicinal doses in animal models; classical literature explicitly warns against use in pregnancy; this is non-negotiable.
- **Structural cardiac conditions:** Not appropriate as self-treatment for atrial fibrillation, heart block, or any structural arrhythmia — requires medical management
- **Photosensitization:** Contact sensitization documented; avoid direct sun exposure on skin contact with plant
- **Anticoagulant medications:** Stachydrine anti-platelet effect may potentiate warfarin; monitor INR
