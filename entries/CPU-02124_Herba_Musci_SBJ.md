# Herba Musci — Yuan Yi (Yang 1998), 垣衣 *yuan yi* "wall garment"; aliases Xi Xie 昔邪 "Past Evil", Wu Jiu 乌韭 "Black Leek", Yuan Ying, Tian Jiu, Shu Jiu

## *A moss-like growth on old walls and roofs; Yang 1998 prints "Herba Musci" and his footnote 185 says it is "a group of species of moss growing over old walls and the roofs of old houses"; no species can be named from the sources, and the plant is of low confidence at any level finer than "wall moss"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry: named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The row was filed from Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 English translation, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, "Herbs: Middle Class, PDF p.82 (line 3075)". The principal text for the Chinese is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; Yang is read as a second witness and is attributed to Yang.

**The two editions do not agree, and this entry records both.** Sun's text has no monograph headed 垣衣. Its only mention in the monographs is in the note to the lower-classic entry 乌韭 (line 3505), which cites the *Guang ya*: 昔邪，乌韭也，在屋，曰昔邪；在墙，曰垣衣 ("*xi xie* is *wu jiu*; on a house it is called *xi xie*, on a wall *yuan yi*", line 3509). Yang prints under "Yuan Yi" the aliases Xi Xie and Wu Jiu, so his paragraph corresponds to Sun's 乌韭 by name, and yet its indications and class differ. The identity of the two is therefore by shared alias, supported by the *Guang ya* note. 垣衣 also appears in Sun's drug-relations table (lines 4275 and 4329) as an "envoy" for 天门冬 and for 天名精. **Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), Sun 1799.** The entry 乌韭 (lower classic, 卷三 下经) reads, at line 3507, 味甘，寒。主皮肤往来寒热，利小肠膀胱气。生山谷石上 ("sweet, cold; governs alternating cold and heat in the skin, disinhibits the qi of the small intestine and bladder. Grows on rocks in mountain valleys"). The note at line 3509 also quotes the *Xishan jing* that 萆荔 resembles 乌韭, and the *Tang ben* note that it is the same as 石衣, called 石苔 and 石发. There is no 久服 clause.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), Yang 1998.** Yang (line 3075): "Yuan Yi (Herba Musci) is sour. It is nontoxic, treating mainly jaundice, heart vexation, cough and counterflow, blood and qi, and fulminant heat in the stomach and intestines. It stops incised wounds. Protracted taking may supplement the center, boost the qi, promote growth of the muscles, and render the facial complexion good. Its other name is Xi Xie (Past Evil). Another name is Wu Jiu (Black Leek). It is also called Yuan Ying (Ying on the Wall), Tian Jiu (Celestial Leek), and Shu Jiu (Mouse Leek). It grows on the north side of old walls or over the roofs of old houses." Yang's footnote 184 explains *biao* (an earlier line) and footnote 185 gives the "moss on old walls" gloss. Footnote 186 (line 3087) is his reading of "stops incised wounds": that the drug heals incised wounds, though "some scholars interpret the words *jin chuang nei sai* as a single term meaning lockjaw".

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Yang: jaundice, heart vexation, cough and counterflow, fulminant stomach and intestinal heat, incised wounds. Sun's 乌韭: alternating cold and heat in the skin, and the qi of the small intestine and bladder.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no duration.

The Codex does not translate these terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and recommends no use.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given in either edition. Yang gives the habitat (north side of old walls, roofs); neither edition gives a harvest time, and neither gives a dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Mosses are bryophytes, and many species grow on masonry and roofs. The classic's "heart vexation" and "fulminant heat" have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Sun 1799, 乌韭).** 味甘，寒, "sweet, cold" (line 3507).
- **SBJ (Yang 1998).** "Sour. It is nontoxic" (line 3075). The two flavours differ.
- No organ or channel assignment is given in the classic, and none is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The grade differs between the editions. Yang prints the drug among the middle-class herbs (p.82), and its paragraph has a 久服 clause ("Protracted taking may supplement the center, boost the qi, promote growth of the muscles, and render the facial complexion good"), but those promises are not 轻身, 不老, 延年 or 通神, and do not meet the project's rule for (a). In Sun's text the corresponding monograph 乌韭 is in the lower classic, whose preface (line 2935) says the lower drugs are "mostly toxic, not to be taken long", and its paragraph has no 久服 clause; Sun's grade decides, and it is lower. The register is therefore (b): framed by the class, with no cultivation status attested in either edition.

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug in Sun's reconstruction and a middle-grade one in Yang's, for jaundice, heat and wounds. Nothing in the record supports a cultivation use, and the plant cannot be named. A cultivator has nothing to do with this entry beyond reading it.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** "Wall moss" covers many species, and the correspondence between Yang's and Sun's paragraphs is by alias. Do not gather or use moss on the strength of this entry. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Jaundice, cough.** Medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Hygiene.** Moss from old walls and roofs is outdoor, unclean material; the sources say nothing on its cleaning.
