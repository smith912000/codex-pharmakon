# Katurohini (Kutki) — Kaṭukā, Kaṭurohiṇī, Kaṭvī, Tiktā, Kutki, Kaur

## *Picrorhiza kurroa* Royle ex Benth. | Family: Plantaginaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SS, FMT
**Tags (previous line, retained for reference):** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā) · Ayurvedic classical materia medica · Himalayan alpine ethnomedicine

**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa (taste): Tikta (bitter), Kaṭu (pungent) · Vīrya (potency): Śīta (cooling) · Vipāka (post-digestive): Kaṭu (pungent) · Guṇa (qualities): Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry) · Doṣa action: Kapha-Pitta-śāmaka (pacifies Kapha and Pitta); may aggravate Vāta in excess.

**Training Phase:** Purification & clearing phases — the great bitter used where heat, congestion, and sluggish elimination must be broken before deeper work. A phase-opener, not a tonic.

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** Kaṭurohiṇī / Kaṭukā appears in the classical Ayurvedic corpus as one of the pre-eminent *tikta* (bitter) drugs. It is a recurring member of bitter, febrifuge and cholagogue formulations, prescribed where Pitta and Kapha obstruct the channels — in *jvara* (fever), *kāmalā* (jaundice / liver disorder), *kuṣṭha* (skin disorders), *prameha* (urinary/metabolic disorders), and *kṛmi* (parasitic conditions). Its reputation as a *dīpana-pācana* bitter — one that kindles digestive fire while scraping away *āma* (metabolic residue) — places it among the drugs used to reset a congested constitution before restorative therapy.

**Classical Ayurvedic lineage:** Later nighaṇṭus (materia medica lexicons) list Kaṭukā under the *tikta skandha* and repeatedly pair it with the liver and the bile. The drug is frequently described as *bhedana* (breaking, deobstruent) and *rocana* (appetite-restoring). In the classical logic, an intensely bitter, light, dry, cooling substance is the natural corrective for the "hot-and-heavy" pathology of jaundice: it drains excess Pitta downward and scrapes Kapha from the channels. This is why Katurohini sits at the centre of hepatobiliary formulae across the Ayurvedic canon.

**Himalayan ethnobotany:** *Picrorhiza kurroa* is an endemic perennial herb of the alpine and sub-alpine Himalaya, growing at roughly 3,000–4,500 m above sea level from the western to eastern ranges. The bitter rhizome and root are the medicinal parts. Its restriction to high, cold, exposed habitat and its slow growth have made it both prized and vulnerable — see Contraindications for the conservation flag.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Cold Bitter that Governs the Gate of Bile:**
Katurohini embodies a specific symbolic role: the bitter cold that presides over the liver, the organ Ayurveda reads as the seat of Pitta and the forge of bile. In the doctrinal imagination, bitterness is the taste of dispassion and clarity — the flavour that no craving reaches for, and precisely for that reason the flavour that clears. Where sweet builds and salt binds, bitter *undoes*: it dissolves accretion, opens obstructed channels, and returns the system to a legible baseline. Katurohini is the archetypal instrument of this undoing. Its coldness is not the coldness of depletion but of quenching — the dousing of an over-hot inner fire that has begun to scorch the tissues (the "yellowing" of jaundice being read as Pitta overflowing its banks). In the training frame of Kenetica, Katurohini is filed as a *gate-keeper of the clearing phase*: it is taken not to add but to subtract, to strip the residue that blocks perception and elimination alike, so that whatever restorative practice follows lands on clean ground. Its symbol is the alpine root that survives in thin cold air by concentrating bitterness to the point of medicine — endurance distilled into a corrective.

### Active Components

The pharmacologically defining constituents of *Picrorhiza kurroa* are its **iridoid glycosides**, chiefly **picroside I** and **picroside II**, together historically referred to under the collective name **kutkin** (kutkoside + picroside complex). According to PubMed, tissue-culture work has quantified picroside I and picroside II as the principal secondary metabolites of the rhizome (Ganeshkumar et al., 2017) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/pr.pr_89_17). Additional characterised constituents include further **iridoid glycosides**, **phenylethanoid glycosides**, **androsin**, and simple **aromatics** such as 4-hydroxy-3-methoxyacetophenone, isolated from methanol extracts of the rhizome (Sakamoto et al., 2023) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1248/bpb.b23-00167). The intense bitterness that names the plant (*Picrorhiza* = "bitter root") tracks these glycosides.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Within Kenetica, Katurohini is deployed as the **hepatobiliary clearing bitter** — the substance reached for when the working hypothesis is that a congested, over-heated liver-and-gut axis is the bottleneck. Traditional and preliminary experimental uses converge on:

- **Hepatoprotective / liver-clearing action** — the classical *kāmalā* (jaundice) indication, echoed by experimental hepatoprotection data below.
- **Bitter digestive priming** (*dīpana-pācana*) — restoring appetite and digestive fire while scraping *āma*.
- **Skin and Pitta-heat conditions** — classical *kuṣṭha* use, consistent with a cooling deobstruent bitter.
- **Deobstruent / cholagogue role** in formulae aimed at moving stagnant bile.

Use-case framing: a **short-arc corrective**, not a daily tonic. It belongs to the opening of a protocol, given for a defined window to clear the ground.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- A methanol extract of *P. kurroa* rhizome showed **hepatoprotective effects** against D-galactosamine/lipopolysaccharide-induced liver injury in mice; **picroside II, androsin, and 4-hydroxy-3-methoxyacetophenone** were active hepatoprotective isolates (50–100 mg/kg p.o.), reducing hepatocyte cytotoxicity and TNF-α sensitivity (Sakamoto et al., *Biol Pharm Bull*, 2023) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1248/bpb.b23-00167).
- Tissue-culture work confirmed **picroside I and picroside II** as the plant's principal hepatoprotective metabolites and characterised their production (Ganeshkumar et al., *Pharmacognosy Res*, 2017) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/pr.pr_89_17).

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical (in vivo animal and in vitro / phytochemical). The hepatoprotective signal for picrosides is mechanistically characterised in animal and cell models; robust human clinical trial evidence is thinner and not established here. Treat the liver claim as biologically plausible and traditionally corroborated, but **not clinically proven in humans** on the basis of these sources.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Tikta (bitter), Kaṭu (pungent) — the bitterness is the operative property.
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling) — quenches excess Pitta heat.
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** Kaṭu (pungent) — sustains the deobstruent, drying action after digestion.
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry) — scraping, non-cloying, anti-Kapha.
- **Doṣa action:** *Kapha-Pitta-śāmaka* — pacifies Kapha and Pitta; being light, dry, and cold it can aggravate Vāta if overused or given to a depleted, dry constitution.
- **Karma (actions):** *Dīpana* (kindles digestion), *pācana* (digests āma), *bhedana* (deobstruent/purgative-leaning), *rocana* (restores appetite), *yakṛt-uttejaka* (liver-stimulant, in the classical reading).

### Cultivation Register

**Avicenna — Unani (CM).** Under the Unani tag Kutki would read as an intensely bitter, cold-dry deobstruent apt for a hot, bilious (*ṣafrāwī*) liver — the kind of cooling bitter a physician reaches for to drain excess heat. The retrieved sources, however, carry no actual Unani material for this species. Unani's governing cultivation idea — the conservation of innate heat (*ḥarārat gharīziyya*) and radical moisture (*ruṭūbat aṣliyya*) as a finite endowment spent over a lifetime — is a longevity frame of the whole regimen, and a cold, drying, subtractive bitter would if anything be a corrective within that regimen, not a substance the tradition grades for building or preserving vitality. No *mufarrih* or *ruh*-strengthening role and no *Canon* attribution is recorded or claimed. Cultivation status: **(c) absent.**

**Ayurveda — Suśruta (SS).** Kaṭurohiṇī stands among the pre-eminent *tikta* drugs of the classical corpus, worked as *dīpana–pācana*, *bhedana* and *yakṛt-uttejaka* to scrape *āma*, drain Pitta and open obstructed hepatobiliary channels in *jvara*, *kāmalā*, *kuṣṭha* and *prameha*. This is an explicitly *subtractive* drug — one that undoes accretion — and no *rasāyana* or *vājīkaraṇa* assignment for *P. kurroa* is traceable to Caraka, Suśruta or Meulenbeld in the sources retrieved; no *ojas*-building action is claimed for it, and the tradition itself files it as a phase-opener taken before restorative work, not as a rejuvenative. Its standing is therefore therapeutic and deobstruent, not a cultivation grade. **(b) framed:** claimed and heavily used, but carrying no sourced rejuvenative status. (*Note: the sattva/rajas/tamas triad is Sāṃkhya and is not invoked here.*)

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Within Sowa Rigpa a bitter cooling root of this kind belongs to the fever- and bile- (*mkhris pa*-) clearing repertoire, but in the Four Tantras' scheme medicine is only the third of four treatment branches and does not reach the root of the three *nyes pa*, which trace to the three poisons and are at root a spiritual condition. No *bcud len* (essence-extraction) role is attributed to this species in the retrieved sources, and Tibetan medicine holds no formal category of "substances for subtle-body practice" under which to grade it. Cultivation status: **(c) absent.**

**Practitioner's note.** Kutki is the clearest possible case of a substance a cultivator takes *to subtract, not to add*. It opens the clearing phase — draining a hot, congested liver-and-gut axis so that whatever restorative practice follows lands on clean ground — and is then stopped; it is bitter, cold, drying and unsuited to a depleted constitution, and none of the three tagged traditions grades it as a longevity or vitality substance. The honest cultivational role here is preparatory: strip the residue that blocks elimination and perception, then move on.

### Contraindications

- **Conservation flag — endangered/threatened species.** *Picrorhiza kurroa* is a slow-growing, over-harvested Himalayan endemic under serious wild-collection pressure. Prefer cultivated, tissue-cultured, or certified-sustainable sources; avoid wild-harvested material. `[USER: verify current CITES/IUCN status]`
- Its bitter, light, dry, cold profile can **aggravate Vāta** and is unsuited to depleted, emaciated, or very cold-dry constitutions without balancing.
- Not a daily long-term tonic; intended for defined corrective windows.
