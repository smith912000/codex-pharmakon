# Rhododendron flower — 羊踯躅 *yang zhi zhu*, "the sheep that stumbles" (also 玉支 *yu zhi*)

> **TOXICITY CAUTION. This plant is poisonous.** The flowers and leaves of *Rhododendron molle* contain toxins that can cause severe, sometimes fatal, poisoning, and the text itself records that animals die from eating it. This entry is a historical record of what one early Chinese text says. It is not a recommendation, not a preparation guide, and not a basis for any use. Nothing here should be gathered, prepared or taken.

## *Rhododendron molle* (Blume) G. Don, the yellow-flowered Chinese azalea (Yang prints the older name *Rhododendron sinense*); the identification is the modern standard one for 羊踯躅 and is of high confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** A search of the other traditions' texts in `Sources/` for *Rhododendron molle* and *R. sinense* found nothing; the Codex's other rhododendron entries (I_1345, I_1346) rest on the Tibetan texts and are other species. The entry rests on one tradition, in Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction and Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 translation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified genus-level placeholder, *Rhododendron* sp., from Yang's line; this entry replaces it.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), the Shennong text proper.** The entry sits in the lower classic (下经). Sun's 味 paragraph reads: "味辛，温。主贼风在皮肤中，淫淫痛，温疟，恶毒，诸痹。生川谷。" ("Acrid, warm. It governs thief-wind within the skin, with a creeping [淫淫] pain; warm malaria [温疟]; malign poison [恶毒]; the various impediments. It grows in rivers and valleys."). The paragraph does not say that the plant is toxic; that statement is in the next layer. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 羊踯躅 *yangzhizhu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3383)

**Second witness, Yang 1998 (attributed to Yang).** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, lines 3387-3388) reads: "Yang Zhi Zhu (*Flos Rhododendri Sinensis*) is acrid, warm, and toxic. It mainly treats bandit wind within the skin causing continual dull pain…" and the text is cut by a footnote. Yang writes "toxic" in the paragraph where Sun prints no such word; the difference is reported, not resolved. Yang's note 208 beside it (line 3389 onward) belongs to the preceding drug (Tian Xiong).

**Wu Pu layer (3rd century, quoted by Sun).** Line 3385: "羊踯躅花，神农、雷公：辛，有毒。生淮南。治贼风、恶毒，诸邪气" ("The flower of *yang zhi zhu*; Shennong and Lei Gong: acrid, toxic. It grows in Huainan. It treats thief-wind, malign poison, and the various evil qi."). The explicit "toxic" is attributed there to Shennong and Lei Gong, which the Shennong paragraph itself does not print.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later additions, quoted by Sun).** Line 3387: "一名玉支，生太行山及淮南山。三月采花，阴干" ("Another name: Jade Branch [玉支]; it grows on Mount Taihang and in the mountains of Huainan; the flower is gathered in the third month and dried in the shade."). The part named is the flower.

**Sun's own note (案).** Line 3389: "《广雅》云：羊踯躅，英光也。《古今注》云：羊踯躅花，黄羊食之，则死；羊见之，则踯躅分散，故名羊踯躅。陶弘景云：花苗似鹿葱" ("The *Guangya* says: *yang zhi zhu* is *ying guang*. The *Gujin zhu* says: of the *yang zhi zhu* flower, if a yellow sheep eats it, it dies; sheep, seeing it, stumble and scatter, hence the name. Tao Hongjing says: the flower and seedling resemble 鹿葱."). The name therefore encodes the poison: the plant that makes sheep stagger. The comparison with 鹿葱 (a lily-like plant) is the only description of the plant's appearance.

**Identity.** The text read gives no flower colour (the "黄羊" of the *Gujin zhu* is an animal, "yellow sheep", not a flower colour), and the Codex does not infer one. The modern identification, *Rhododendron molle*, is the standard one for the drug name in current Chinese botany and pharmacy (a general fact). Yang's older name, *R. sinense*, is the same plant under a synonym. Confidence: high. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. As a general fact, *Rhododendron molle* contains grayanane diterpenoids (grayanotoxins and the related rhodojaponins), which act on voltage-gated sodium channels and slow the heart; poisoning from the flowers and from honey made from them is documented. This is a general statement and not drawn from the Codex's sources. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The Codex has no use case for this plant. The Shennong text lists an acrid, warm drug for pain within the skin, warm malaria and impediments, and the next layer names it toxic. The Codex does not translate "thief-wind" or "malign poison" into modern diagnoses. There is no protocol, and the plant must not be used.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text proper gives no preparation. The Mingyi layer says the flower is gathered in the third month and dried in the shade (line 3387). No dose is given in the sources retrieved, and none is supplied. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Poisoning by *Rhododendron* flowers, leaves and honey is documented in clinical toxicology, with salivation, vomiting, slowed heart rate, low blood pressure and, in severe cases, collapse; this is a general fact. No clinical studies of therapeutic use were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ.** The Shennong text prints "味辛，温" (acrid, warm). Wu Pu attributes "acrid, toxic" to Shennong and Lei Gong (line 3385). The Mingyi layer adds no flavour or temperature.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic (下经), whose preface (line 2935) says lower drugs "treat disease to correspond with the earth; many are toxic and cannot be taken long; for those who wish to remove cold and heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings and cure illness, take the lower classic". The entry has no 久服 clause. The class is the one the preface bars from long use, and the name itself records a plant that kills sheep; the grade is a warning and not a status. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 羊踯躅 *yangzhizhu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3383)

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this plant. It is a lower-grade drug in the text's own scheme, named for the animals it staggers and kills, and the toxicity statement is in the tradition's own record. The honest practice is to read the entry as a record and keep away from the plant.

### Contraindications

**Poisonous. Do not use.** Wu Pu records "toxic" (line 3385); the *Gujin zhu* quoted by Sun records that a sheep dies from eating the flower (line 3389). Flowers, leaves and honey from the flowers are all documented sources of poisoning. Poisoning is a medical emergency: contact emergency services at once. The plant must not be used in pregnancy or by children. Livestock and pets are at risk from the shrub itself.
