# Lark — Korudallos, as printed in the English edition of Dioscorides (Alauda arvensis — Skylark, Crested Lark)
## *The lark, a small crested songbird; the translator's identification is Alauda arvensis, skylark or crested lark* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek Tradition (DMM).** The chapter is Dioscorides, *De Materia Medica*, Book 2, chapter 59, headed "2-59. KORUDALLOS" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 5170; row in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 249). The identification line printed under the heading is "Alauda arvensis — Skylark, Crested Lark" (line 5171). That is the translator's identification and not Dioscorides' own.

The whole chapter is two sentences: "The lark is only a little bird having on the top of its head a tuft standing up similar to that of the peacock. This bird eaten roasted is good for those troubled with colic." (line 5172).

So the text gives one description (a small bird with a crest like a peacock's) and one use (eaten roasted, for colic). It gives no season, no region and no part other than the whole bird. It gives no dose and no preparation beyond "roasted".

The neighbouring chapters show how Dioscorides treats birds in this section. The swallow (2-60) is dissected "at the increase of the moon" for stones in the young birds' bellies, which are tied to the arm or neck for epilepsy, and the swallow is also eaten, or its ashes are rubbed on with honey, for sharpness of sight and for tonsillitis (lines 5173-5181). The lark chapter, by contrast, has no such ritual or amuletic element. The preceding chapter, 2-58, on the *phinis* (translator: osprey), says that given in drink "little by little" it is "said to expel stones from urine" (line 5169). These are cited only as context. They are not lark uses.

### Active Components

A roasted lark is a very small portion of poultry-like meat: protein, fat and minerals. The text names no active compound, and none is asserted here.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The Greek text records roast lark as a remedy for colic. The Codex does not recommend it. Larks, like other wild songbirds, are protected in many countries (see Contraindications), and the Codex gives no protocol. Colic, meaning severe cramping abdominal pain, can mark an emergency (obstruction, appendicitis, kidney stones) and needs a clinician.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter gives a description and one use, and it states no cultivation status. What the text does demonstrate is recognition by sight: the crest is the identifying mark it chooses (line 5172). That is a small instance of the attention to the material's appearance that the Greek text shows, and it is not a cultivation claim.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. There is no cultivation role for the lark in this text.

### Contraindications

Wild songbirds are legally protected in many countries, and taking or eating them can be an offence. Wild-caught birds can carry pathogens, and undercooked wild bird meat can cause food-borne illness. Colic with fever, vomiting, a rigid abdomen or blood in the stool is an emergency. This entry gives no medical advice.
