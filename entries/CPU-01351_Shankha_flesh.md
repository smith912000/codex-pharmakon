# Shankha flesh — Shankha (also printed S'amkha), flesh of a conchiferous (Koshastha) animal, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the animal Bhishagratna prints as "Shankha" in the Koshastha (conchiferous) group of Sutrasthana ch.46; his Vol. 3 glossary reads "A conch-shell"; the spine list glosses it "conch"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Shankha is named in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 270 lists "Shankha (conch)", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1"). The chapter divides the animals of marshy places (*Anupa*) into five groups, the third being the *Koshastha* (conchiferous aquatic animals such as molluscs) (lines 659-668). The group is defined: "Animals such as, the Shankha, Shankhanakha (a species of small bivalve molluscs), Shukti, Shambuka and Bhalluka, etc., belong to the Koshastha (conchiferous) group" (lines 758-762). The next group, the *Padinas*, is listed as "the tortoise, alligator, crab, black crab, porpoise, etc." (lines 763-765).

The properties are given for the two groups together: "The flesh of animals of the Shankha and Kurma orders is sweet in taste and digestion, cooling in its potency, demulcent, and beneficial to stool and the Pittam. It destroys the deranged Vayu and produces Kapham. Of these, the species of black crab is strength-giving and heat-making in its potency, and tends to destroy the deranged Vayu. The white species is laxative and diuretic in its effect, and tends to bring about an adhesion of fractured bones (or produces fermentation). It destroys the Vayu and Pittam" (lines 775-785; the parentheses are Bhishagratna's). The passage does not define the "Kurma order"; the *Padina* list just before it names the tortoise and the black crab (lines 763-765), but the lines read do not say to which group the "white" species belongs. Nothing is said of the Shankha by name beyond the group sentence.

The after-drink: "The Asava (wine of Shringataka or Kasheruka should be taken after having eaten the flesh of an animal of the Kulechara (frequenting the shores of lakes etc.) species. The same wines should be regarded as proper after-potions after a meal consisting of molluscs (Koshastha), or of the flesh of an animal of the Padi (lizard) family" (lines 2854-2863).

Other places. The conch-shell, as a material and not as food, appears in other recipes, for example an eye-salve (*Anjana*) of Talisa, Ela, Gairika, Ushira and Samkha (conch-shell), "pasted with breast-milk" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch10.txt` lines 60-63). Those are separate materials and are not treated here.

**Identity.** The Vol. 3 glossary reads "S'amkha — A conch-shell", and the entries beside it read "S'ambuka — A small conch-shell; a snail" and "S'mkha-nakha — A small shell" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` lines 30913-30950; an OCR table with entries and glosses in separate columns, matched by order). The text of ch.46 calls the group "conchiferous" and "molluscs". Which species of shell-bearing animal is meant is not stated in the lines read. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Mollusc flesh is muscle and visceral tissue: protein, with low fat, and minerals such as zinc and iron. Marine shellfish can concentrate toxins from algae and bacteria such as *Vibrio* from warm water, and are a common food allergen. No analysis specific to this entry was read, and the species is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text records the flesh as a food of the conchiferous group, with stated actions on *Vayu*, *Pittam* and *Kapham*. Nothing in the sources supports eating or sourcing it as a medicine. Shellfish are a common allergen and a recognised source of foodborne illness, and the conch shell in other recipes is a different material. The Codex does not recommend the flesh for any complaint and gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation or dose is given for the Shankha. The only related instruction is the after-drink: wine of Shringataka or Kasheruka after a meal of molluscs (ch.46 lines 2854-2863). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Shellfish must be cooked through and kept cold before cooking; filter-feeding molluscs can carry norovirus, *Vibrio* and algal toxins. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 for the "Shankha and Kurma orders" together (lines 775-785): sweet in taste and in digestion; cooling in potency; demulcent; beneficial to stool and *Pittam*; destroys deranged *Vayu*; produces *Kapham*. The black crab, which the text puts in the same passage, is strength-giving and heat-making. No property is printed for the Shankha alone. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Shankha is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and the *ojas* principle (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food in general, not about this flesh. The group sentence lists actions on the *doshas* and none beyond that; no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter read names the Shankha as food. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta this is one of the shell-bearing animals eaten as food, with properties stated for the group and none for the animal itself. A person who does not eat shellfish has nothing to adopt.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** the species is unspecified in the lines read; do not substitute a species on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Allergy:** shellfish are a major food allergen; avoid in anyone with a shellfish allergy.
- **Food safety:** raw or undercooked molluscs can carry *Vibrio*, norovirus and algal toxins; cook through.
- **The text's own cautions:** the group "produces Kapham" (ch.46 line 779).
- **Legal and conservation status:** harvest of some large marine snails is regulated in many places. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food is a moral question for many traditions, and meat is restricted in some; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
