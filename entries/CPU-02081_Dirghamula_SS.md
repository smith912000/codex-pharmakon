**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Chapter 38 of the *Sutrasthana* is the chapter "which deals with the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam)" and says that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (Vol. I lines 15343 to 15350; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3 to 9). The drug is placed there by group, and a group's virtue is stated for the group as a whole.

1. **Group list.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38, the Priyangvadi group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15661 to 15665; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 324). Bhishagratna prints: "The group of medicinal drugs known as the Priyangvadi Gana consists of Priyangu, Samanga, Dhataki, Naga-pushpa, Chandana, Kuchandana, Mocharasa, Rasanjana, Kumbhika, Srotohnjana, Padma-keshara, Jojanvalli, and Dirghamula." It is the thirteenth and last name. (The heading is printed "Priyangvardi" in one place in the e-text; the list itself says Priyangvadi.)
2. **The group's virtue.** Bhishagratna gives a single metrical text for the Priyangvadi group and the Amvashthadi group that follows it: "The two medicinal recipes or groups prove beneficial in a case of deranged Pitta, favour the healing of ulcers, bring about the adhesion of fractured bones and prove curative in cases of dysentery where the stools are found to consist of lumps of thick and matured mucous (Pakvatisara)" (lines 15678 to 15684). This is a virtue of the two groups together and not of Dirghamula alone.

The name reads as a Sanskrit compound; *mula* is "root" (Bhishagratna glosses Dasha-Mulam as "the ten roots", line 15838), and *dirgha* is "long" in ordinary Sanskrit, so the name may mean "long-root". That is a reading of the name and not a statement of the text, and it does not identify a plant. ### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in a chapter 38 group list, which classifies drugs by the use of the whole group (*Dravya-Sangrahaniya*). Chapter 38 is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns this drug no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only, and only at the level of its group. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it. The groups' virtues are wound-healing and dysentery virtues, not life-nourishing ones.

**Practitioner's note.** An unidentified name at the end of a group list. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without an identification nothing can be gathered or used. A cultivator reads it as a name in a record.
