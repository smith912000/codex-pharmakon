# Stachys arvensis — Sideritis etera, "the other sideritis" (Dioscorides 4-34)

## *Stachys arvensis [Mabberley], "Stagger-weed", the only name on the translators' SUGGESTED line for 4-34 ("Stachys arvensis [Mabberley] — Stagger-weed")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED line (line 8299) prints the one name with the single bracketed attribution [Mabberley]. It was added from that line (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is *4-34. SIDERITIS ETERA* ("the other sideritis"), heading at line 8298, SUGGESTED line at 8299, text at line 8300. It stands in Book Four ("Other Herbs & Roots"), after *4-33. SIDERITIS* (heading at line 8291, with its own SUGGESTED name, *Stachys recta*) and before *4-35. SIDERITIS TRITE* (heading at line 8301, SUGGESTED *Stachys sylvatica*, "Hedge Woundwort"). The Codex's own name extraction lists it (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, row 614); the translators' general index prints "Stachys arvensis 576" (line 17697).

The whole chapter reads: "The other sideritis has slender branches of two feet, and leaves on long stems (similar to those of fern) with many in-cuts towards the top on either side, and from the upper wings long thin shoots with a rough head on the top, round like a sphere, in which is the seed — similar to beet but rounder and harder. This (with the leaves) is good for wounds."

The chapter gives a description only: slender branches of two feet; leaves on long stems, "similar to those of fern", with many in-cuts towards the top on either side; long thin shoots from the upper "wings" with a rough head on the top, "round like a sphere", in which is the seed, "similar to beet but rounder and harder". Its one use is that "This (with the leaves)" is "good for wounds". The text does not say which "this" is, whether the seed head alone or the seed head, and the parenthesis "with the leaves" suggests the head and leaves together; the Codex does not decide. It gives no habitat, no quality, no preparation, no quantity and no other names.

**The three sideritis chapters.** The text numbers its sideritis by position: 4-33 *SIDERITIS*, 4-34 *SIDERITIS ETERA* ("the other") and 4-35 *SIDERITIS TRITE* ("the third", line 8301). 4-34 identifies itself as the second only through the word "other", and gives no name of its own. The three chapters are three plants in Dioscorides' own division. The first (4-33) is the subject of the entry I_1200 (*Stachys recta*) in this batch, and the third (4-35) was not read beyond its heading and SUGGESTED line for this entry and has no entry of its own that was found.

**Overlap.** A chapter-level search of the entries folder for 4-34 found no file. The only file that mentions the neighbouring 4-35 is I_977 (*Achillea ageratum*, chapter 4-36), where the number appears as a neighbour. The spine's betony entries I_033 (*Betonica officinalis*) and I_648 (*Stachys officinalis*) were not read for this entry; the Codex's name extraction places betony at 4-1 and 5-54 (names.csv rows 583 and 827), not at 4-34. No entry on 4-34 as its own plant was found.

### Active Components

The chapter prints none. It is a general fact that *Stachys* (the woundworts and betonies) is a genus of the mint family (Lamiaceae). The composition of the plant Dioscorides describes was not read, and the chapter prints neither a taste nor a smell. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides reports a seed head, with the leaves, as good for wounds. This is the text's report in one short sentence, the identification is a single modern suggestion, and the Codex gives no protocol. A wound that needs closing or cleaning needs medical care.

### Preparation & Dosing

4-34 gives no preparation: "This (with the leaves) is good for wounds" says that something is used, not how. It gives no quantity, frequency or duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter records no amuletic use. The part of the discipline of attention that is present is the whole of the chapter: it is a description and nothing else, meant to let a reader tell this sideritis from the other two by the fern-like leaf with its many in-cuts, the sphere-shaped rough head and the seed like beet but rounder and harder. The plant is given no status beyond that of a wound herb. The identification is a single modern suggestion. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The text is a description for telling three similar plants apart, and that is the practice it supports. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The chapter states no caution. The identity of the plant rests on one modern suggestion, and three different plants share the name *sideritis* in the text, so no wild plant should be gathered or applied on the strength of this entry. Wounds, especially deep, dirty or bleeding ones, need medical assessment. None of the traditional uses above is advice.
