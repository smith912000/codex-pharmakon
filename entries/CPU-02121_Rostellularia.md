# Rostellularia — Jue Chuang 爵床 (Shennong bencao jing); Wu Pu alias Jue Qing 爵卿

## *Rostellularia procumbens (L.) Nees (now usually Justicia procumbens L.), a small sprawling herb of the acanthus family, whose whole herb is the Chinese drug jue chuang; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence for the modern drug, though the classic describes no plant and a later note ties the name to another one, 香苏* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** The principal text read is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Its other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), also has the drug, as "Jiao Chuang (Herba Justiciae Procumbensis)" (line 3039; "Jiao" is Yang's rendering of the same character, usually read *jue*), and is read here as a second witness, attributed to Yang. The row began as an unverified placeholder under the pharmaceutical-Latin name "Herba Justiciae Procumbensis [SBJ]". No other text on disk names the plant.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 爵床 in the middle classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 爵床 *jue chuang*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2329). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味咸，寒 ("salty, cold"); 主腰脊痛 ("governs pain in the lumbar spine"), 不得着床 ("so that one cannot lie on the bed"), 俯仰艰难 ("with difficulty bending forward and back"), 除热 ("removes heat"), 可作浴汤 ("can be made into a bath-decoction"); habitat 生川谷及田野 ("grows in river valleys and in fields"). There is no 久服 clause and no alias in the paragraph itself.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 2331) is a single alias: 爵床，一名爵卿 ("also called jue qing"; the *Yulan*). The Mingyi bielu layer (line 2333) is a single habitat: 生汉中, "grows in Hanzhong". Neither gives a flavour, a harvest month or a description.

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 2335) is one line: 别本注云：今人名为香苏 ("another edition's note says: people today call it xiang su"). Xiang su is not explained there. The next entry in the classic, 假苏 (line 2339 onward), is a different drug; the Codex does not equate the two and does not know which plant "xiang su" names in that note. [USER: verify]

Yang 1998 (line 3039) renders the paragraph as "Jiao Chuang (Herba Justiciae Procumbensis) is salty and cold. It mainly treats pain in the lumbar spine which is unable to touch the bed and where there is difficulty bending forward and back. It eliminates heat and can be used to make bathwater." No footnote on this entry was found in his text. Both editions place it in the middle class (Yang's nearest class heading above reads "Herbs: Middle Class", line 3020).

The appendix of drug relations, later than the main text, names no relation for this drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a salty, cold herb for lumbar pain so severe that one cannot lie on the bed, for removing heat, and as a bath-decoction. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose. Severe back pain, especially with fever, weakness or loss of bladder control, needs medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the sources read is the bath-decoction: the herb "can be made into" one (可作浴汤, line 2329). No quantity, volume, temperature, duration or frequency is given. The preface (line 4121) says that drugs differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Justicia procumbens* (the accepted modern name for *Rostellularia procumbens*) is a low annual herb of East Asia and India, and the whole herb is used in Chinese folk and clinical medicine. The classic's claim for lumbar pain is not translated into a modern pharmacological claim. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味咸，寒, "salty, cold" (line 2329). There is no flavour in the Wu Pu layer, so no disagreement is recorded.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The paragraph for 爵床 stands in the middle classic, whose preface says the middle medicines "nourish the nature, correspond to man; non-toxic or toxic, weigh what is fitting; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the lean" (主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经; line 1677). The paragraph has no 久服 clause, so no promise of lightening the body or extending the years attaches to it; it is a flavour, nature and indication list, with an external bath use. The register is (b). (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 爵床 *jue chuang*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2329)

**Practitioner's note.** The classic gives this herb a back-pain and heat indication and a bath use and no long-use promise. A cultivator has nothing to do with it beyond the record. The entry is no licence for any use.

### Contraindications

- **Back pain:** pain that prevents lying down, or that comes with fever, weakness, numbness or loss of bladder or bowel control, needs urgent medical assessment; do not treat it with this entry.
- **Identity:** the classic describes no plant, and a later note ties the name to "xiang su"; products sold under either name should not be assumed to be this herb.
- **Pregnancy, nursing, children:** no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Quantities:** no dose, volume or duration is given.
