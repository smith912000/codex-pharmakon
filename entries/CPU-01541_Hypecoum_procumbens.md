### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk names this plant under this name. The entry rests on one chapter of one text, and the chapter is a single sentence with a second name in a bracket. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed under the chapter heading, and not Dioscorides' own identification. The Codex does not state the identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-68. UPEKOON* at line 8615, SUGGESTED line at 8616, text at line 8617. The chapter stands in Book Four, "Other Herbs & Roots", after 4-67 *MEKON APHRODES* (the frothy poppy, covered by the existing entry I_1100) and before 4-69 *UOSKUAMOS* (henbane), both different chapters. The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 648.

The whole chapter reads: "Hypecoon (also called hypopheon) grows among wheat and fields. It has a leaf similar to rue, with little branches, and it has uses similar to those of the juice of poppy."

So the chapter gives a habitat (among wheat and in fields), a short description (a leaf like rue, with little branches), a second name (*hypopheon*) and one statement of use, by reference: its uses are "similar to those of the juice of poppy". The chapter names no condition, no part, no preparation and no quantity. What the uses of poppy juice are is stated in other chapters of the same text, which were not read for this entry, so nothing is stated here about them. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A chapter-level search of the entries folder for 4-68 and for *Hypecoum*, *Hypecoon* and *Upekoon* found no existing entry on this chapter. The only hit for 4-68 is the entry I_1100, which names it as the next chapter. The Tibetan materia medica in Clark 1995 (line 1055, *par.pa.ta*) prints a different species, *Hypecoum leptocarpum* Hook f. et Thoms, among the names for common fumitory. That is a different species in a different tradition, and no Tibetan tag is added here. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The text prints no constituent. It is a general fact that *Hypecoum* is a genus of small annual herbs in the poppy family (Papaveraceae), and that many members of that family contain alkaloids.
