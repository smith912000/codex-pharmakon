# Bhustrina [SS] — Bhustrina (Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch. 38 Surasa'di group; ch. 46 spices and herbs); printed "Bhu-trina" in Bhishagratna's plant index

## *Probably a fragrant lemon-grass type, Andropogon (Cymbopogon) schoenanthus, as Bhishagratna's plant index (Vol. III, Appendix No. 3) glosses the matching name "Bhu-trina"; the identification is ours, from the index spelling, and is of low confidence: the Sutra text prints "Bhustrina" with no equivalent, and the index spelling differs by one letter* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry, and the text is Bhishagratna's English translation, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` (and the chapter files `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`), with the plant index in Vol. III. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it.

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Sutra ch. 38, the Surasa'di group* (Vol. I, lines 15459-15465). The group is given as: "The drugs known as Surasa, white Surasa, Fainjjhaka [sic], Arjaka, **Bhustrina**, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika form the group known as the Surasadi Gana." The metrical texts (lines 15466-15470) read: "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough."

*Sutra ch. 46, the spices and herbs* (Vol. I, line 21080; chapter file `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 1420). In the chapter on the properties of foods and drinks, the list "The spices and herbs include the Pippali, Maricha, Shringavera, Ardraka, Hingu, Jiraka, Kustumvuru, Jamviraka, Sumukha, Surasa, Arjaka, **Bhustrina**, Sugandhaka, Kasamarddaka, Kalamala, Kutheraka, Kshavaka, Ksharapushpa, Shigru, Madhu-Shigru, ..." (lines 21078-21085) is followed (Vol. I, lines 21158-21162; chapter file line 1498) by statements on their properties: "The herbs known as the Surasa, Arjaka and Bhustrina destroy the Kapham, are light of digestion, and tend to produce a state of parchedness in the organism. They are demulcent, generate the Pittam, and are heat-making in their potency and pungent in taste and digestion." The words "demulcent" and "parchedness" are as the translator prints them; the pairing is odd and the Sanskrit is not checked here. [USER: verify]

*The index.* Bhishagratna's plant index (Vol. III, Appendix No. 3) has the name "Bhu-trina" at line 31423 and, against it in the second column, "Andropogon Schoenanthus" (line 31483). The columns are separated by the OCR and the alignment is ours, supported by the neighbouring pairs, in which "Bhu-Nimba" is paired with "Swertia Cherata" and "Bhurjapatra" with "Betula Bhojapattra". That "Bhu-trina" is the same word as the "Bhustrina" of the Sutra is our reading from the spelling. [USER: verify.]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, aromatic grasses of the genus *Cymbopogon* (lemon-grass and its relatives) contain volatile oils rich in citral-type and related aromatic compounds, which give the lemony scent. Whether that applies to the plant meant here depends on the unconfirmed identification. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** It stands in the Surasa'di group (a vermifuge and aseptic agent; Kapha, catarrh, loss of appetite, asthma, cough), and in chapter 46 it is a spice or herb that destroys Kapham, is light of digestion, and is heat-making and pungent.
- **What the text does not say.** The part used, any preparation of its own, or what it is apart from the name.

The Codex does not translate the Sanskrit terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text on disk gives no preparation of Bhustrina as such. It is named in a list of spices and herbs for food and in a drug group, with no weight. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Aromatic grasses of this type are used as flavourings and for scent, and their oils are aromatic and mildly antimicrobial in the laboratory; none of this establishes that the classic's plant is one of them, and none is evidence for the classic's claims. The classic's *Kapham* has no modern equivalent and is not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Sutra ch. 46).** With Surasa and Arjaka: "destroy the Kapham, are light of digestion", "generate the Pittam", "heat-making in their potency and pungent in taste and digestion" (lines 21158-21162). Taste and post-digestive taste are printed as pungent and potency as heat-making, all as a statement about the three herbs together. The Sanskrit terms for these are not printed in the English lines read and none is supplied here.
- **SS (Sutra ch. 38).** The group action given above.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The herb stands in a therapeutic group (chapter 38) and in the chapter on foods and drinks, among the spices and herbs (chapter 46), where its properties are given together with Surasa and Arjaka. Neither place is a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no passage read names it in either discipline or relates it to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. Its status in the tradition is therapeutic and dietetic only, and the cultivation status is unattested. The popular *sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas* food lists come from Samkhya and later material and not from Sushruta, and none is applied here.

**Practitioner's note.** A heating, Kapha-reducing herb in a list of kitchen spices, whose botanical identity is not settled. It is not a rejuvenative in Sushruta, and the text gives a cultivator nothing to take beyond the classification of the spice by its effect on Kapha and Pitta. A reader should not use a lemon-grass product on the strength of this entry.

### Contraindications

- **Heating (the text's word).** The herb is said to generate Pittam and to be heat-making; where Pitta is already aroused the text itself implies caution. This is a reading of the lines above and not a statement in them. [USER: verify]
- **Identity unknown.** Do not substitute any plant on the strength of this entry.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Asthma, cough, worms.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
