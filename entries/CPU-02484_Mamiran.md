# Mamiran — ماميران (*māmīrān*, as printed in the Arabic)

## *Mamiran: a dark, knotty root-wood described in the Canon, identified in modern drug-trade usage as the rhizome of Coptis teeta Wall. (identification modern, moderate confidence)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Mamiran is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. The locus is *Canon* Book II, entry *māmīrān* (ماميران), Arabic text line 15347 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; the entry runs to line 15356. It is a short entry with no quotation of Dioscorides or Galen.

**Identification.** The English name is ours, from the Arabic name and the short description, and is the modern one: *māmīrān* is generally given in later materia medica as *Coptis teeta*, a Himalayan goldthread. The Canon's description is thin: "wood like twisted knots inclining to black, with a little curvature, and it is sharper than the dyers' roots" (خشب كعقد مائلة إلى السواد فيها انعطاف قليل وهو أحد من عروق الصباغين; lines 15348-15349). Nothing in it fixes the species, so the identification is moderate, not certain. The text does not contradict it, but it does not confirm it either.

**What the text says (CM).** Temperament: "hot and dry at the end of the second degree" (حار يابس في آخر الثانية; line 15349). Action: "a cleanser and purifier" (جال منق). Cosmetic: it "polishes away whiteness of the nails" (يجلو بياض الأظفار; line 15350). Head: its juice "draws thick moisture from the head and purifies the superfluities of the brain, and its root is useful for toothache" (lines 15350-15352). Eye: it clears the whiteness in the eye and sharpens sight when used as a kohl, and clears thick moisture, "especially its juice" (lines 15354-15355). Digestive organs: "its root is useful for jaundice" (أصله نافع من اليرقان; line 15355). Excretory: it helps colic and "has in it a promotion of urine" (ينفع من المغص وفيه إدرار; lines 15355-15356).

### Active Components

*Coptis* rhizomes are in general known to contain isoquinoline alkaloids, berberine among them. The Canon names no constituent, and the drug of the Canon is not shown to be a *Coptis* by anything in the text. [USER: verify the identification before relying on the chemistry.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The entry is a list of actions (cleansing, drawing moisture from the head, eye and tooth uses, jaundice, colic, urine) and is reported here as Avicenna's. It is not a recommended practice, and no modern protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

The entry gives the part used for different purposes (the juice for the head and eye, the root for teeth and jaundice) and one form: as a kohl for the eye. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Hot and dry at the end of the second degree (*Canon* Book II, entry *māmīrān*, line 15349). No other quality is given. No energetic classification is printed for any other tradition because no other text on disk names the drug under this name.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The entry assigns mamiran no cultivation status. It is a list of local and evacuant actions. The doctrine of innate heat and radical moisture, which the Codex's register spec offers as the frame for this tradition, is not used in this entry and the text does not bring it to bear on the drug.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to cultivate here. It is an ordinary drug of the Canon, used for a named complaint (teeth, eye, jaundice, colic) and not for the long-term maintenance of the person. The honest use for the reader is to know what the text says and to treat the identification as unproven.

### Contraindications

No contraindication is given in the text. General caution: the identification is uncertain, so no safety statement about the actual material of the Canon can be made. Do not use a root of unknown species. *Coptis* species of the Himalaya are over-collected and the wild plants are protected in several countries; legal status varies. [USER: verify]
