# Triphala — Triphala (Sushruta Samhita)

## *The compound "group of medicinal fruits" of Haritaki, Amalaka and Vibhitaka, three myrobalan fruits: Terminalia chebula, Phyllanthus emblica and Terminalia bellirica; the composition is stated by the text itself (Sutra ch. 38 and ch. 44), the binomials are the translator's and the Codex's own, and confidence in the composition is high* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder with the locus "SS Sutra 38, Parushaka'di group (Vol. I line 15654)", and the first-pass check thought it a misreading; it is not. Triphala is a real compound drug that the text defines itself. This entry replaces the placeholder. The three fruits have their own entries (CPU-00095 Haritaki, CPU-00098 / CPU-00728 Amla, CPU-00369 Vibhitaka); this entry records the compound, which is a different object.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The composition, stated twice.* In Sutra ch. 44, the chapter on fruits (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch44.txt` lines 486 to 492): "The group of medicinal fruits known as the Triphala consists of Haritaki, Amalakam and Vibhitakam, which are collectively marked by an acid-astringent taste with a shade of bitter and sweet. Powdered Triphala regularly taken with clarified butter of a three quarter part of its own weight acts as a regular panacea and is endued with a rejuvenating virtue." In Sutra ch. 38 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 403 to 410): "The Triphalar Group: — The drugs known Haritaki, Amlaki and Vibhitaka, constitute the group known as the Triphaladi Gana." The metrical text: "The present group destroys the action of the deranged Vayu, Kapham and Pittam and proves curative in Meha, and in diseases of the skin (Kushtham). It is a good appetiser, improves the eyesight and proves beneficial in chronic intermittent fever (Vishama-jvara)." The row's locus is the third place where the word occurs in this chapter:

*The Parushakadi group (Sutra ch. 38, lines 310 to 319).* "The Parushaka'di Group: — The group known as the Parushakadi Gana consists of Parushaka, Draksha, Katphala, Dadima, Rajadana, Kataka-phala, Shaka-phala and Triphala." The metrical text: "It subdues the deranged Vayu, allays thirst, acts as a cordial, increases one's relish for food and cures the diseased or abnormal components of urine or its defects." Triphala here is listed as a single member of a larger group, which is the form of the entry on the spine row. The Mushkakadi group ends in the same word (lines 133 to 140): "Mushkaka, Palasha, Dhava, Chitraka, Madana, Shinshapa, Vajra-Vriksha and Triphala", described as "destroying fat and removing the defects of semen", with Meha, piles, jaundice, chlorosis, gravels and urinary calculi yielding to it.

*Other places the compound is used (Sutrasthana; line numbers in the chapter files).* Ch. 44: a powder taken "with a decoction of Guduchi, Arishta or Triphala" in diseases of deranged Kapham (line 42); "one part of Triphala, one of Yavakshara, one of Krishna (Pippali), and one of Vidanga, pounded together and used as an electuary with the addition of honey and clarified butter, or made into bolases (Gutika) with treacle", for enlarged spleen, Gulma caused by Kapham and Vayu, chlorosis and abdominal dropsy, and "one of the most harmless purgative compounds (of our pharmacopoeia)" (lines 205 to 216); a purgative with Trivrit, Katvi, Musta and others "administered through the vehicle of clarified butter, essence of meat, or water" (lines 220 to 224); castor oil taken with a decoction of Triphalas (line 510); and a compound of Trivrit, Triphala pulp, Vidanga, Pippali and Yavakshara licked with honey and ghee, which "does not entail any strict regimen of diet and conduct" (lines 571 to 590). Ch. 36 uses it in pulverised compounds and as a "condensed extract" for healing an ulcer (lines 186 to 204). Ch. 5, line 146, names it among the "astringent decoction (of Nimba, Triphala, etc.)" for washing a wound. In *Kalpasthana* ch. 5 it is a powder in the Rishabhagada (see the Surasi entry, I_1780). In *Chikitsasthana* ch. 28 (the elixir chapter, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch28.txt` line 151) the three fruits, written singly and not as "Triphala", are cooked with Brahmi juice, Vidanga, Vacha, Trivrit and ghee in the Brahmi Ghrita.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the three fruits are the tannin-rich fruits of the myrobalan group: the two *Terminalia* fruits are rich in hydrolysable tannins, and the *Phyllanthus emblica* fruit is a rich dietary source of vitamin C and tannins. This is stated of the three plants in general and is not a figure from any text opened. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended protocol.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The chapter 44 passage gives a regular panacea with a rejuvenating virtue when taken as powder with ghee; the group passages give Vayu, Kapha and Pitta, Meha, skin diseases, appetite, eyesight and chronic intermittent fever; the compounds give purgation, spleen, Gulma, chlorosis and dropsy, and wound-washing.
- **What the text does not say.** A dose for the plain powder, a duration, or the season. The ghee is given as a ratio only.

Triphala is a familiar compound in present-day Ayurvedic practice; the Codex records what Sushruta says and gives no schedule.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives a ratio, not an amount: "Powdered Triphala regularly taken with clarified butter of a three quarter part of its own weight" (Sutra ch. 44, lines 489 to 492). The compounds in the lines above give parts ("one part of Triphala, one of Yavakshara, one of Krishna, and one of Vidanga") and, in the last compound, "three Shana weights (one tola and a half)" for the first drug, Trivrit, with the Triphala figure cut by a page break in the file. Those figures belong to the compounds and are not a dose for Triphala. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The three fruits are astringent, tannin-rich fruits, and amla is a well-known source of vitamin C. Triphala is widely used as a mild laxative in traditional practice and has been studied in the laboratory and in some trials, but no study was opened for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** For the compound, ch. 44 prints a taste: "acid-astringent ... with a shade of bitter and sweet" (lines 487 to 489). For the individual fruits the same chapter prints: Amalakam "cooling, and refrigerent ... subdues Pittam and Kapham and is antifat"; Vibhitakam "cooling; it subdues Pittam and Kapham" (lines 482 to 486). The Triphaladi group (ch. 38) subdues Vayu, Kapha and Pitta. No virya or vipaka is printed for the compound.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(a) Attested, limited.* Sutra ch. 44 (lines 489 to 492) says that powdered Triphala taken regularly with ghee in a stated proportion "acts as a regular panacea and is endued with a rejuvenating virtue" (Bhishagratna's English; the Sanskrit term was not available to check and the gloss is the translator's). That is a status assigned to the compound itself, and the only claim of this kind in the lines read. The limits matter. The *rasayana* chapters of the *Chikitsasthana* (ch. 26 to 31 on disk) do not name "Triphala"; ch. 28 names the three fruits separately inside the Brahmi Ghrita, a compound whose action is on the memory and intellect. The Parushakadi, Mushkakadi and Triphaladi group lists are groups by action, not by cultivation status. No passage read links Triphala with *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*, and none gives a duration of use. The status is therefore a single "rejuvenating virtue" in ch. 44 and nothing larger.

**Practitioner's note.** A fruit compound that Sushruta calls a panacea with a rejuvenating virtue when the powder is taken regularly with ghee, and which is also an ordinary purgative and wound-wash. A cultivator can treat it as a gentle, long-used digestive and cleansing medicine, but nothing in the text gives a dose or a course. The claim is the translator's English for a single sentence and should be read as that.

### Contraindications

- **Purgative effect.** The text uses the compound in purgatives (ch. 44); it can loosen the bowels. Avoid with diarrhoea or dehydration. General caution.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Medicines.** Tannin-rich fruits can reduce the absorption of some drugs taken at the same time; ask a pharmacist. General caution.
- **Medical conditions named in the text** (spleen, dropsy, chronic fever, eye disease, diabetes-like discharge) need proper medical care; the entry gives no protocol.
- **Identity.** The species of the three fruits rest on the translator's glosses. [USER: verify]
