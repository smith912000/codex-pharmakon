# Vrikshadani — Vrikshadani (also printed Brikshadani, Vriksliadani; Bhishagratna's Sushruta Samhita)

## *Vrikshadani, a plant of the Viratarva'di group of Sutrasthana ch.38 whose leaves the text discusses; the translator prints no gloss, no botanical name is given in any source read, and the identification is unresolved (confidence low)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Vrikshadani [SS]", taken from an extraction of Bhishagratna's translation; this entry replaces it. The locus given was "SS Sutra 38, Viratarva'di group (Vol. I line 15400)".

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Vrikshadani is printed in the Sutrasthana, chapter 38 (locus: Viratarva'di group, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 60; the same text is in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, line 15400). Chapter 38 is "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3-6), and says that "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (lines 8-10). The name is given by the translator without a gloss, a botanical name or a description.

In the Viratarva'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 57-64 (the list), lines 65-72 (the metrical text)), Vrikshadani is one of the drugs listed. The group's metrical text reads: "The group subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and ... proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles." (the sentence runs across a page break after "Vata and") That is a property of the group as a whole, not of Vrikshadani alone.

**Sutrasthana ch.46 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 1598-1599).** In the account of leaves the text says: "The leaves of the Vrikshadani plant subdue the Vayu." The same chapter prints the name in a list of leaves (line 1585, "Vriksliadani" in the OCR) that are "astringent, sweet and bitter" and curative in haemoptysis; that list is of the leaves of several plants and the property is not given to this one alone.

**Chikitsasthana ch.7 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch07.txt` lines 24-28).** Printed "Vrikshddani" in the Vataja Asmari ghee, among Pashanabheda, Vasuka, Vasira and others. **Uttarasthana ch.40 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch40.txt` line 233).** Printed "Brikshddani" in the twelfth Amatisara recipe with Virataru, the two kinds of Vrihati and the two kinds of Saha.

The chapter closes (lines 554-566) by saying that the groups "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", and that plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, Ghritas or potions are to be prepared "according to the exigencies of each individual case". That is a general rule of the chapter and is not a recipe for this drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The text says that the leaves subdue Vayu (ch.46); puts the plant in the Viratarva'di group (Vata disorders; stone, gravel and urinary troubles); and uses it in a ghee for Vataja Asmari and in an Amatisara recipe.
- What the text does not say: what Vrikshadani is as a plant, how its leaf is taken, or any dose.

The Codex does not translate the text's terms (Vayu, Pitta, Kapham, Gulma, Shosha, Asmari and the rest) into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend a plant it cannot identify.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Vrikshadani stands in the dravya-group classification of Sutrasthana ch.38, a classification by therapeutic action. The Viratarva'di group is therapeutic, not rasayana or vajikarana. A search of the chapter files on disk for the spellings printed here found no mention of Vrikshadani in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters, and no text read calls it an elixir or a drug of vitality. The register is therefore (b): framed by its therapeutic group, with no cultivation status attested. The Codex does not assign *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* effects to it, because no source read does.

**Practitioner's note.** Vrikshadani is a name in a list. The tradition's text gives the drug a place among therapeutic groups and little more, and nothing in the record supports a cultivation use. A cultivator can learn from it only how Sushruta organises drugs into groups by action, and that some names in that classification are no longer certainly identified. Nobody should try to find a plant to match the name.
