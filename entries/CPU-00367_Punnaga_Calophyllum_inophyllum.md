# Punnaga — पुन्नाग (Punnāga), Nāgakesara (regional), Tuṅga, Kesara, Sultāna-campā; oil: Tamanu / Beauty-leaf oil

## *Calophyllum inophyllum* L. | Family: Calophyllaceae (formerly Clusiaceae / Guttiferae)

**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā) — beauty-leaf / tamanu oil; skin and wound applications; Pitta–Kapha, varṇya (complexion), vraṇaropaṇa (wound-healing)

**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa — kaṣāya (astringent), tikta (bitter), madhura (sweet) · Vīrya — śīta (cooling) · Vipāka — kaṭu (pungent) · Doṣa — pacifies Pitta and Kapha

**Training Phase:** Tissue-Repair & Skin-Integrity · Cooling-Soothing · Recovery / Restorative phases

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā:** Punnāga is a classical fragrant tree of the coastal and southern flora, valued both for its flowers (used, like other *kesara/nāgakesara*-type fragrant dravyas, in cooling and complexion preparations) and, above all, for the thick greenish oil pressed from its nuts — the substance known pan-tropically as **tamanu** or **beauty-leaf oil**. Suśruta and the classical tradition employ Punnāga among cooling, astringent, complexion-improving (*varṇya*) and skin-and-wound (*vraṇya / kuṣṭhaghna*) agents. The oil is a topical mainstay for *vraṇa* (wounds and ulcers), *kuṣṭha* (skin diseases broadly), *dāha* (burning), and cosmetic skin care.

**Ethnobotanical breadth:** Across Polynesia, Southeast Asia, and the Indian coast, tamanu oil is one of the most storied traditional skin remedies — applied to wounds, scars, burns, eczema-type eruptions, insect bites, and dry or damaged skin, and used to soothe and beautify. This cross-cultural convergence on the *same* topical skin-and-wound use is one of the strongest ethnomedical signatures in the plant-oil literature.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Sealing Balm of the Surface:**
Punnāga's tamanu oil is Kenetica's archetype of the **restorative surface-sealant** — the substance that mends the boundary between self and world. The skin is the frontier organ: the meeting-place of inner and outer, the first tissue wounded and the last to be perfected. Where the astringents *close* the vessel and the coolants *quell* the fire, Punnāga's oil does something subtler — it *rebuilds and re-anoints the frontier itself*, knitting broken tissue, calming the inflamed margin, and restoring the smooth, integral surface.

Its symbolic register is **completion and beautification**: the oil that turns a healed wound back into whole, unblemished skin. Fragrant, cooling, unctuous, it belongs to the *varṇya* lineage whose deeper meaning is that healing is not finished when bleeding stops — it is finished when the surface is *whole and radiant* again. In the training arc Punnāga governs the **tissue-repair and restorative phase**: the patient, unctuous, final work of sealing, soothing, and returning the boundary to integrity after the active battle is over.

### Active Components

Tamanu oil is chemically distinctive for its **calophyllolide** (a 4-substituted dipyranocoumarin / neoflavonoid) and related **coumarins/neoflavonoids** (inophyllums, calophyllic acid, calanolide-type compounds in the wider plant), together with a **fatty-acid rich lipid fraction** (oleic and linoleic acids predominating) and a **resinous unsaponifiable fraction** carrying the coumarins, sterols, and antioxidants. Calophyllolide is the constituent most directly linked in the primary literature to the oil's anti-inflammatory and wound-healing action; the fatty-acid matrix supports the emollient, barrier-restoring character.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

- **Wound-healing (vraṇaropaṇa):** the flagship use — topical acceleration of wound closure and repair.
- **Anti-inflammatory skin-soothing:** calming inflamed, reactive, or dermatitis-type skin.
- **Emollient / barrier restoration (varṇya):** restoring smooth, whole, hydrated skin; scars, dryness.
- **Antimicrobial / antioxidant topical support:** for at-risk or damaged skin.
- **Cooling of surface heat (dāha):** burns and burning eruptions.

Phase role: the **restorative / tissue-repair** agent — cooling, unctuous, boundary-sealing.

### Scientific Correlation

*According to PubMed:*

- **Nguyen et al., 2017, *PLoS ONE* — calophyllolide anti-inflammatory and wound-healing activity.** Calophyllolide isolated from *Calophyllum inophyllum* promoted wound closure in a mouse model without body-weight loss, reduced fibrosis, lowered myeloperoxidase (MPO) activity, down-regulated pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α) while up-regulating anti-inflammatory IL-10, and skewed macrophages toward the pro-healing M2 phenotype — a direct mechanistic correlate to the traditional topical wound use. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0185674)
- **Pribowo et al., 2021, *Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine* — tamanu oil for atopic dermatitis (review).** Reviews in vitro and in vivo studies attributing anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antimicrobial, analgesic, and wound-healing properties to tamanu oil and evaluates its potential in atopic dermatitis, while noting the need for further clinical validation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/6332867)

Evidence grade: **preclinical (in vivo rodent wound model with defined molecular mechanism) plus a peer-reviewed review.** The calophyllolide wound-healing mechanism is well characterised at the animal/cellular level; there is no cited large human clinical trial here, so human-efficacy claims and percentages must not be asserted. Traditional topical use is strongly convergent cross-culturally and mechanistically plausible.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa:** kaṣāya (astringent), tikta (bitter), madhura (mild sweet)
- **Guṇa:** snigdha (unctuous — the oil), guru (heavy), sometimes rūkṣa for the astringent flower/bark fraction
- **Vīrya:** śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka:** kaṭu (pungent)
- **Doṣa action:** Pitta-śāmaka and Kapha-śāmaka; the unctuous oil is also Vāta-soothing topically
- **Karma:** vraṇaropaṇa (wound-healing), varṇya (complexion-improving), dāhapraśamana (heat-relieving), kuṣṭhaghna (skin-disease-allaying), vedanāsthāpana (topical analgesic/soothing), kaṇḍūghna (anti-itch)
- **Srotas / dhātu:** acts on tvak (skin), rakta, and māṃsa; a surface/frontier remedy for the outer sheaths
- **Note:** predominantly a *bāhya* (external) dravya in Kenetica's use

### Contraindications

- **Nut allergy / sensitisation:** patch-test before use; tamanu oil can sensitise; avoid on those with known tree-nut sensitivities. [USER: verify]
- **Open deep/infected wounds:** occlusive oils on the wrong wound type may trap infection — use with clinical judgement, not as a substitute for proper wound care.
- **Internal use:** not recommended — the seed/oil carries bioactive coumarins and potential toxicity; this entry treats Punnāga as an external remedy.
- **Pregnancy / lactation:** topical use is traditional, but concentrated internal use is not advised; insufficient verified human safety data. [USER: verify]
- **No validated human clinical dosing** — evidence base is preclinical and ethnomedical.
