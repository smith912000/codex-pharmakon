### Botanical Identity
Perennial 20–80cm; pinnate leaves with 12–18 pairs of leaflets; pale yellow flowers; root is the drug material — long, thick, yellow taproot, harvested at 4–7 years (older roots have higher astragaloside IV and polysaccharide content). Inner Mongolia and Shanxi Province produce the highest quality commercial *huang qi*. The botanical and pharmacological profile is elaborated in I_01; this entry provides supplementary traditional context and the specific Mongolian steppe tradition.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

*(Complementary to I_01 — focusing on distinct applications)*

### 2.1 Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ)

*Huang qi* — listed as the first *superior herb* in the first rank of the Shennong classification system:
> *"Sweet; slightly warm. Tonifies the Exterior and consolidates the Wei (defensive Qi). Tonifies the middle burner and Qi, promotes urination, detumescence, discharges pus, promotes healing of sores."*

The **Wei Qi consolidation** application distinguishes *huang qi* from all other tonics: it specifically strengthens the defensive surface of the body — the boundary between self and pathogen. This is the immunological immune-surface function expressed in classical Chinese medicine.

### 2.2 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā on astragalus types:
> *"Of warm and moist nature. Strengthens the body, promotes flow of the vital spirit (*ruh*). Useful in weakness and debility of the vital faculties."*

### 2.3 Four Medical Tantras (FMT)

The Tibetan *dkar po chig thub* ("single white accomplisher") — an epithet indicating a substance capable of treating many conditions alone, without need for formula combination. *Huang qi* in Tibetan medicine is used for lung weakness, constitutional debility, and wound healing.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action (Supplementary to I_01)

### 5.1 Telomere Extension / Anti-Ageing (Unique Focus)

**Astragaloside IV / Cycloastragenol**: Activate human telomerase reverse transcriptase (hTERT) → elongate telomeres in immune cells, skin cells, liver cells. This is the only well-characterised natural compound mechanism for measurable telomere elongation in vivo. Clinical product TA-65® (cycloastragenol) is the commercial realisation.

The *huang qi* longevity application (*rasāyana*-equivalent in TCM) is the most mechanistically supported longevity claim in the entire Codex: telomere length is a measured biomarker of biological ageing; its extension is a direct anti-ageing intervention by definition.

### 5.2 Wei Qi and Mucosal Immunity

**APS (polysaccharides)**: Specific APS fractions activate secretory IgA production on mucosal surfaces (gut, respiratory tract, urogenital) — the front-line defensive barrier of Wei Qi. This is the pharmacological mechanism for the classical "consolidates the Exterior/Wei" action.

## Section 6 — Distinct Clinical Evidence (from I_01)

**Telomere length (TA-65 — cycloastragenol)**: Harley (2011, *Rejuvenation Research*): TA-65 (astragaloside IV-derived) significantly increased mean telomere length in CD8 T-cells and reduced senescent T-cell percentage in elderly subjects at 12 months. The first human RCT demonstrating measurable telomere elongation from a natural product.

**Diabetic nephropathy**: Zhang (2009): Astragalus injection significantly improved proteinuria and creatinine clearance in diabetic nephropathy patients — specific renoprotective application not in I_01.

**Cancer adjunctive (platinum-resistant)**: Expanded meta-analysis (Cochrane-level systematic review, Qi 2010): 34 RCTs of *huang qi*-based Chinese herbal medicine as adjunctive to chemotherapy — significant improvement in 1-year survival and tumour response rates for NSCLC.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The Yellow Elder**: The Chinese name *huáng qí* — "yellow elder/leader" — names astragalus as the chief among the superior herbs. In the Shennong classification (superior, middle, inferior herbs), *huang qi* is the first-ranked superior herb. The systematic herbalism of the Shennong tradition places this plant at the apex — the tonic most suitable for prolonged, sustained use to build constitution without injury.

**Mongolian steppe ecology**: The highest quality *huang qi* comes from the Inner Mongolian grassland — one of the harshest seasonal environments, with temperature ranges from −40°C to +40°C and exposure to extreme UV, wind, and drought. The root's adaptation mechanisms (producing astragalosides for cellular protection) are the same mechanisms that confer human cellular protection. The plant's pharmacological resilience mechanisms and the human body's required resilience mechanisms have overlapping chemistry.

### Energetic / Contraindication Notes

**TCM**: Sweet; slightly warm. Supreme Wei Qi tonic. Caution: avoid during acute infections or "excess" patterns — strengthening the Exterior while an exterior pathogen is present may trap the pathogen (classical principle: do not tonify during acute invasion).

**Contraindications**: Autoimmune disease — immunostimulation; transplant medications — antagonise immunosuppression. TA-65/cycloastragenol: telomerase activation theoretically possible concern in cancer-prone individuals (telomerase is upregulated in cancer cells); no clinical evidence of cancer promotion confirmed in trials to date.
