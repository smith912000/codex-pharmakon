# white Surasa — "white Surasa" (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907); Sanskrit form not printed

## *A drug named second in the Surasadi group of Sushruta chapter 38, beside plain "Surasa". The translator prints it as the English-and-Sanskrit hybrid "white Surasa". No Latin name, no description and no part used is printed. Identification: unresolved; a white-flowered or pale variety of the Surasa of the same group is the plain reading of the name, and the species of Surasa is itself not given. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The name is a bare entry in a drug list. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should gather or take a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this printed form.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38. A first pass judged it a duplicate of CPU-01842 (*Ocimum album*), and an audit disagreed. This entry replaces the placeholder and does not merge it with that row, for the reason given in point 4 below.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

1. **Group list, Surasadi.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15460 to 15466; `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 119 to 126). Bhishagratna prints: "The Surasa'di Group: The drugs known as Surasa, white Surasa, Fainjjhaka, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika form the group known as the Surasadi Gana." The name is the second item, directly after "Surasa", and it is the only place where it is printed.
2. **The group's virtue.** The metrical text (ch.38 lines 127 to 131) says: "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough." This is the virtue of the group as a whole, and no line is given to "white Surasa" alone.
3. **The plain Surasa elsewhere.** Surasa (without "white") is named in other places, which bear on the neighbour and not on this drug: a spice and herb list in the chapter on food and drink (*Sutrasthana* ch.46, `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, the list that includes "Sumukha, Surasa, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka"), where "The Surasa generates the Pittam, relieves the colic and pain at the sides" and where "the Surasa, Arjaka and Bhustrina destroy the Kapham, are light of digestion, and tend to produce a state of parchedness in the organism" (Vol. I lines 21078 to 21082 and 21150 to 21160); and in a fever plaster, "leaves of Surasa, Arjaka and Shigru pasted together with water" against shivering (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 8836). None of these lines says "white Surasa", and nothing licenses reading them onto this drug.
4. **Why it is not merged with *Ocimum album*.** Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 (Vol. III, "Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms") pairs the Latin "Ocimum Album" with the Sanskrit name *Arjaka*, by position in two OCR columns, as the entry on that row (`I_1406_Ocimum_album.md`) records. The appendix was searched for *Surasa* and for "white Surasa"; no row was found for either. The only link between *Ocimum album* and this drug is therefore that *Arjaka* and the white Surasa both stand in the same Surasadi list, which is not an identification. The word "album" (Latin "white") and the English "white" in the printed name are a coincidence of language and are not evidence.
