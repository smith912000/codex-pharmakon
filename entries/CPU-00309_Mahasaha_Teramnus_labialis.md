# Mahasaha — Sanskrit: Mahāsahā; Blue Wiss, Rabbit Vine, Horse Gram relative

## *Teramnus labialis* (L.f.) Spreng. | Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae, subfam. Faboideae)

**Tradition Tags:** Suśruta Saṃhitā (identified with Mahāsahā, paired with Sahā/Alpasahā; balya / vātahara context); Ayurvedic nighaṇṭus (Mahāsahā–Sahā pair, *māṣaparṇī* substitute discussions); Indian folk & tribal medicine (tonic, galactagogue, food legume); pan-tropical fodder/food legume

**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Predominantly Vāta-pacifying and balya; *madhura-vipāka*, *śīta*-to-mild *vīrya*, *guru-snigdha* — a nourishing, strength-giving legume-drug

**Training Phase:** Consolidation · Recovery / Restoration · Rejuvenation (as nourishing tonic)

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta & the Sahā pair:** In classical dravyaguṇa *Mahāsahā* ("the greatly enduring/powerful one") is paired with *Sahā* (also *Alpasahā*), a twin of "enduring" drugs. A well-supported botanical identification of Mahāsahā is *Teramnus labialis*, a slender twining legume; the pair belongs to the nourishing, Vāta-pacifying (*vātahara*), strength-giving (*balya*) group and is discussed in the context of the *māṣaparṇī*/*mudgaparṇī*-type leguminous tonics used to rebuild tissue, support strength and reproductive vitality, and calm Vāta. The name *sahā* — "able to bear, to endure, to overcome" — marks the drug as one that confers resilience and tolerance of strain, an apt classical reading for a tonic legume. (As with several classical names, the botanical assignment of Mahāsahā/Sahā is debated among commentators; *T. labialis* is a principal candidate.)

**Folk, dietary & regional use:** *Teramnus labialis* is used as a **food legume in India** and across the tropics as fodder and a soil-improving cover crop; its seeds are edible and its herb enters folk practice as a general tonic, galactagogue, and restorative in debility and nervous/rheumatic complaints — folk uses consonant with the classical *balya*/*vātahara* placement.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Enduring Legume:** Mahāsahā's doctrine is carried in its name. *Sahā* is the Sanskrit root of endurance — the same word that names *sahiṣṇutā*, the capacity to bear — and *Mahā-sahā* is its intensified form: the drug that makes the body *able to withstand*. This places Mahāsahā within Ayurveda's leguminous-tonic logic, where sweet, cool, unctuous pulse-drugs (the *parṇī* group — *māṣaparṇī*, *mudgaparṇī*) are given to build the flesh and nerve tissue that Vāta, when aggravated, wastes and dries. The pairing with Sahā/Alpasahā ("lesser enduring one") repeats the Ayurvedic couple-motif seen in the Aṣṭavarga: a greater and lesser twin of a single virtue, prescribed together to complete an action. Symbolically Mahāsahā is the plant of resilience — the twining vine that persists, feeds the soil, feeds the body, and lends the trained or convalescent frame the *saha*, the sheer bearing-power, to endure load without breaking.

### Active Components
Seeds are notably rich in **flavonols — chiefly quercetin** (with kaempferol and isorhamnetin), contain **seed oil** with a favourable unsaturated profile (high **linoleic, α-linolenic and oleic acids**, low saturated fat), plus proteins and carbohydrates typical of a food legume. According to PubMed, the seed flavonol and fatty-acid profile is characterised by Morris et al. (2014), *J Diet Suppl* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3109/19390211.2014.937048).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica
Mahāsahā serves Kenetica as a **nourishing, strengthening, Vāta-pacifying (balya–vātahara) legume-tonic** for Consolidation, Recovery, and Rejuvenation: appropriate where training or convalescence has produced Vāta-type depletion, dryness, fatigue, or reduced endurance, and where a mild, food-grade nutritive tonic (rather than a potent single-molecule agent) is wanted. Its documented lactogenic activity and antioxidant flavonol content align with its restorative, tissue-supporting role; being a food legume, it sits at the gentle, nourishment-first end of the materia medica.

### Scientific Correlation
According to PubMed:
- **Lactogenic activity** — Sahoo et al. (2016), *Indian J Pharmacol*, report that a methanolic fruit extract of *T. labialis* dose-dependently increased milk yield, pup and dam weight, mammary glycogen/protein, and serum prolactin and cortisol in nursing rats — corroborating the traditional galactagogue use [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0253-7613.194856).
- **Nutraceutical seed chemistry** — Morris et al. (2014), *J Diet Suppl*, quantify high seed quercetin and a favourable unsaturated fatty-acid profile across accessions, underpinning its antioxidant/nutraceutical potential as a food legume [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3109/19390211.2014.937048).

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical/nutritional. One rodent galactagogue study and seed-composition analyses; no human clinical trials. Classical *balya*/*vātahara* use is traditional, with lactogenic and antioxidant/nutritional data as partial mechanistic support. Botanical identity of "Mahāsahā" is itself subject to classical debate.

### Traditional Energetic Classification
- **Rasa (taste):** Madhura (sweet); some sources add Tikta/Kaṣāya
- **Guṇa (quality):** Guru (heavy), Snigdha (unctuous)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling) — with mild reading in some sources
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Madhura (sweet)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** *Sahatva* — conferring endurance/bearing-power (balya), the sense encoded in the name
- **Doṣa action:** Pacifies Vāta (sweet-unctuous-nourishing) and Pitta; heavy quality can increase Kapha and burden weak digestion
- **Karma (actions):** Balya (strengthening), vātahara (Vāta-pacifying), bṛṃhaṇa (nourishing), stanya (galactagogue), rasāyana (restorative, mild), vṛṣya (reproductive-supportive, per pair-context)
- **Gaṇa / group:** Classically paired as *Mahāsahā* with *Sahā/Alpasahā*; associated with the *parṇī* (leguminous tonic) group

### Contraindications
Heavy, unctuous, nourishing — may aggravate Kapha, weak digestion (*mandāgni*), and *āma* states if over-used. As a legume, ordinary pulse cautions apply (gas/bloating in weak agni; standard legume anti-nutrient handling — cook seeds). Safety in pregnancy at therapeutic (non-food) doses not established `[USER: verify]`. Human clinical safety data absent. Confirm botanical identity given the classical ambiguity around Mahāsahā/Sahā.
