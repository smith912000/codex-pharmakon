### Origin & Tradition

Galangal is one of the great aromatic rhizomes of the Old World — a cousin of ginger, cardamom and turmeric in the family Zingiberaceae, and like them a plant that crossed easily between the kitchen and the dispensary because warmth, pungency and digestion were never separate categories in the traditions that used it. *Alpinia galanga*, the **greater galangal**, is the larger, paler, sharper-tasting of the galangals; the **lesser galangal** (*Alpinia officinarum*) is its smaller, more medicinally concentrated relative, and the two are often discussed together. In the cookery of Thailand, Indonesia and much of Southeast Asia greater galangal (*kha*, *laos*) is a foundational aromatic; in the materia medica of several traditions it is a warming carminative and stomachic.

In **medieval Europe**, "galingale" was a prized and expensive spice-drug of the trade routes, reaching the West from the East and appearing in the cookery and the herbals of the period. It belonged to the family of hot, dry aromatics — alongside ginger, cardamom and cubeb — used to "comfort the stomach," sweeten the breath, dispel wind and warm a cold constitution. Hildegard of Bingen, among others, wrote of galangal in characteristically warm terms as a heart- and stomach-strengthening remedy, and it held a steady place in the Western aromatic pharmacy for centuries before fading as the New World spices arrived.

In the **Unani** tradition the rhizome is **Khulanjan**, a hot-and-dry drug used as a stomachic, carminative and aphrodisiac, to strengthen a cold and weak digestion, to relieve flatulence and colic, and to sweeten the breath — a profile almost identical to the Western reading and clearly drawn from the same Greco-Arabic humoral framework. In **Ayurveda** it appears as **Kulanjana**, a *kapha*- and *vata*-reducing aromatic used for the throat, voice, cough and weak digestion.

In **Chinese Medicine** the closely allied lesser galangal is the classic **Gao Liang Jiang** (高良薑), a warming herb that "dispels cold and relieves pain" in the stomach and middle burner — used for cold epigastric pain, vomiting and hiccup from cold. Greater galangal (sometimes distinguished as **Da Gao Liang Jiang** and its seed as Hong Dou Kou / Cao Dou Kou in related usage) shares the warming, stomach-settling character. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Greater galangal is a **[GENERAL]** warming digestive aromatic — a culinary-grade spice with a long stomachic reputation. Applications below are traditional/culinary.

1. **Stomachic and carminative.** The central use across traditions: warming a cold, weak, windy digestion — relieving flatulence, bloating, colic and the heavy, sluggish feeling of a chilled stomach. Taken as spice, infusion or in compound formulae.
2. **Anti-nausea / settle the stomach.** Like its relative ginger, galangal is used to calm nausea, vomiting and hiccup attributed to cold in the middle burner (the explicit Chinese-Medicine indication for lesser galangal).
3. **Warming, breath-sweetening aromatic.** Chewed or infused to sweeten the breath and warm the constitution — the medieval and Unani role.
4. **Throat, voice and cough (Ayurveda/folk).** Kulanjana used for hoarseness, cough and throat complaints of a cold/kapha character.
5. **Culinary keystone (Southeast Asia).** As *kha*/*laos*, the defining aromatic of curries, tom-yum-type soups and pastes — the everyday "use case" that keeps the plant in continuous traditional circulation.

### Contraindications

- **Heat patterns / high pitta.** As a hot, drying aromatic, galangal can aggravate heat conditions, acid reflux, gastritis and yin-deficient heat; use sparingly where there is internal heat.
- **High-dose / concentrated extracts.** Pungent rhizomes can irritate the gastric mucosa in excess; culinary amounts are well tolerated, concentrated extracts less characterised.
- **Pregnancy.** Culinary use is traditional and generally regarded as safe; medicinal/concentrated doses in pregnancy should follow professional advice. [USER: verify before stating a firm pregnancy position.]
- **Allergy / Zingiberaceae sensitivity.** Cross-reactivity with ginger/cardamom family possible in sensitive individuals.
