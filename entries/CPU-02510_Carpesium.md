# Carpesium — 天名精 *Tianmingjing* (Shennong bencao jing), with the aliases 麦句姜, 虾蟆蓝, 豕首
## *Carpesium abrotanoides L., the carpesium (Asteraceae), the modern identification of the Chinese name 天名精; our identification, of high confidence at species level, made from the name and the standard modern reading, not from any description in the text* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on one text, Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction of the classic (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`); the other Shennong text on disk, Yang 1998, is an English rendering of the same classic. The identification is ours. The text prints no description of the plant, so it neither confirms nor contradicts the species name; it gives only names, a habitat and a month of gathering.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the first (upper) classic, reads 味甘，寒 ("sweet, cold"). It "governs" (主) 瘀血、血瘕欲死、下血: "stagnant blood", "blood conglomerations to the point of death" and "bleeding downward". It also "stops bleeding" (止血) and "frees the urine" (利小便). Its long-use clause reads 久服轻身、耐老, "taken long: lighten the body, withstand ageing". It gives three names, 一名麦句姜, 一名虾蟆蓝, 一名豕首 ("wheat-sentence ginger", "toad-orchid/indigo", "pig's head"), and the habitat 生川泽, "grows in streams and marshes". (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 天名精 *tianmingjing*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 825)

**The Mingyi bielu layer (later additions, not the Shennong text).** 《名医》曰 adds the aliases 天门精, 玉门精, 彘颅, 蟾蜍兰 and 觐, the habitat 生平原 ("grows on level plains") and the time of gathering, 五月采 ("gathered in the fifth month"). The paragraph names no part of the plant. There is no Wu Pu (《吴普》) quotation for this entry on the page.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案), 1799.** Sun gathers older glosses on the name 豕首 "pig's head": Shuowen and Erya are cited for it, then Guo Pu, who says that in Jiangdong (the lower Yangzi region) it is called by a name the transcription has lost, and Tao Hongjing, who says that "this is what people today call" a name likewise lost in the transcription. The 《唐本》 (Tang Materia Medica) is cited for 鹿活草 ("deer-reviving herb"); the 别录 for 天蔓菁 ("heaven turnip"); and 南文 for 地松 ("earth pine"). He ends with Zhang Yuxi's remark that Chen Cangqi set up a separate entry, 地菘, and that later people should not keep his mistake. The note is partly illegible in the transcription (several characters are blank), so it cannot be read in full, and nothing here is built on the missing characters.

**Identity.** The note gives our confidence as high. Carpesium abrotanoides is the species now sold under 天名精; the dried fruit of the plant is sold in Chinese pharmacy under a separate name (鹤虱, *heshi*). ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Shennong text records a sweet, cold drug for stagnant blood and bleeding, to stop bleeding and free the urine, with a promise for long use. The Codex gives no protocol. The text's terms (blood conglomerations, "bleeding downward") are not modern diagnoses, and bleeding of any kind needs medical assessment. The long-use promise is quoted as the classic's claim for the upper grade and is not endorsed as a practice.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The classic files 天名精 in the upper grade (上品); its preface says upper-grade drugs nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic, and may be taken for a long time. Its own 久服 clause reads 久服轻身、耐老 ("taken long: lighten the body, withstand ageing"), so the long-use promise is in the paragraph itself, which is what makes the register (a). *Qing shen* ("lighten the body") is the classic's term for an effect with no pharmacological equivalent; it is not weight loss. The Mingyi bielu, not the Shennong text, gives the habitat 平原 and the month 五月. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 天名精 *tianmingjing*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 825)

**Practitioner's note.** The classic promises a long-use effect for this herb, and it is the upper grade's frame that makes the promise a cultivation claim. The Codex cannot turn that into a practice: no part, preparation, dose or course is given, and the plant's identity rests on the later name. The honest use of the entry is as a record of what the classic says. Nothing here is a recommendation to take the plant.

### Contraindications

- **Identification.** The plant is matched to the Chinese name by later convention; do not gather or use any plant on the strength of this entry.
- **Bleeding.** The text concerns bleeding and blood stasis; these need medical assessment.
- **Pregnancy and safety.** The text gives no statement on pregnancy or safety; the Codex has none to add. [USER: verify]
- **Long use.** The upper-grade promise is the classic's own; no long-term safety data were retrieved.
