# Mriga-matrika flesh — Mriga-matrika (also printed Mriga-mdtrikd), venison of the high-ground (Janghala) group, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of an animal Bhishagratna prints as "Mriga-matrika" among the venison species, glossing the Uttaratantra ch.39 list "(different kinds of deer)"; the spine list flags the identity as uncertain; no binomial is printed* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Mriga-matrika is named in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 153 lists "Mriga-matrika", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", flagged id_uncertain). The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The high-ground (*Jangala*) animals fall into eight sub-groups, of which "the Janghala and the Viscira are the most important" (lines 403-410). The Mriga-matrika is last in the list of the first, the *Janghala*, translated "large-kneed group": "the Ena (black deer), the Harina (red deer), the Rishya (blue deer), the Kuranga (antelope), the Karala, the Kritamala, the Sharabha, the Shvadanstra, the Prishata, the Chitrila (Spotted deer), the Charushka, the Mriga-matrika, etc." (lines 411-426).

The group text: "These species of venison have a sweet and astringent taste, are light, keen, pleasant (palatable), laxative, and diuretic in their effect. They subdue the Vayu and the Pittam" (lines 426-431). The metrical text then takes the species singly, and for this one says: "The venison of the Mriga-matrika species is cooling and proves curative in cases of haemoptysis, Sannipata diseases (due to the concerted action of the three deranged humours), consumption, dyspnoea, cough, and hiccough and creates a relish for food" (lines 444-448). The same passage defines the Ena as the black deer, the Harina as the red, and the Kuranga as those "neither red nor black" (lines 440-443).

Other places. *Sutrasthana* ch.20 (*Hitahitiya*, on wholesome and unwholesome regimen) includes "the Mriga, the Mriga-matrika" among the flesh of "Articles or substances which may be safely included within the food stuffs of all human beings" (`SS_Sutra_ch20.txt`, lines 43-55). *Uttaratantra* ch.39 (fever) gives the meat of "Lava, Kapinjala, Ena, Prishata, Sarabha, Kala-puchchha, Kuranga, Mriga-matrika (different kinds of deer) or Sasa (hare)" as diet for a fever patient accustomed to animal food (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt`, lines 905-908). The Vol. 3 index of the translation carries "Mriga-matrika" (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, line 22444). No Dioscorides, Tibetan or other text is attached to this entry; the tag is SS only.

**Identity.** The translator gives no English name for the Mriga-matrika in ch.46 beyond placing it among the venison species; in *Uttaratantra* ch.39 he glosses the whole list "(different kinds of deer)". His Vol. 3 glossary has a line "Mriga-maitrika A doe" (line 30801), a different spelling that may or may not be the same name. No binomial is printed, and the spine list flags the identity as uncertain. No species is proposed here. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Venison is mostly water and muscle protein with little fat, and supplies iron, zinc and B vitamins. No analysis specific to this entry was read, and the animal is not identified. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The animal cannot be identified from the sources, so no one can follow this text to a particular species. Wild deer and antelope are subject to hunting, conservation and food-safety law, and some species are protected. As a medicine, the sourced record is a dietary one: the text states qualities and indications for haemoptysis, consumption and cough, and the Codex does not recommend the flesh as a therapy for any of them. It gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation or dose is given for the Mriga-matrika in ch.46 or elsewhere in the passages read. The text names it as an ingredient of a meat diet for fever (*Uttaratantra* ch.39) and as a flesh safe for all people (*Sutrasthana* ch.20), without method. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 426-448). For the *Janghala* group: sweet and astringent in taste; light; keen; palatable; laxative; diuretic; subdues *Vayu* and *Pittam*. For the Mriga-matrika: cooling; curative in haemoptysis, *Sannipata* diseases, consumption, dyspnoea, cough and hiccough; creates a relish for food. No post-digestive taste (*vipaka*) is printed for it by name. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Mriga-matrika is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food in general, not about this flesh. The text calls it cooling and gives indications, but no "spermatopoietic" or tonic claim, and no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter read names it. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta this is a venison with stated qualities, given to the sick and counted safe for all people, and the animal cannot be identified. A person who eats meat may read the classification as the tradition's own description of the food; a person who does not has nothing to adopt.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** the animal is not identified, and "doe" in the glossary rests on a variant spelling; do not substitute a species on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Food safety:** wild game can carry parasites and zoonoses; cook through and keep raw and cooked foods apart.
- **Legal and conservation status:** many deer and antelope are protected or regulated, and hunting and trade can be unlawful. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food is a moral question for many traditions, and meat is restricted in some; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
