# black Sariva [SS] — black Sariva, Asita-sariva (Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch. 38, Vidari-gandhadi group); Syama-lata in the commentators' gloss

## *Ichnocarpus frutescens (L.) W.T.Aiton, a twining shrub whose roots are the "Sariva" type of drug, reached through a two-step gloss: Dallana (as Bhishagratna relays him) reads Asita-sariva, "black Sariva", as Syama-lata, and Bhishagratna's plant index pairs "Syama-lata / Sariva" with Ichnocarpus frutescens. The identification is the translator's and the commentator's, at low-medium confidence: the Sutra text prints "black Sariva" with no equivalent, and the index pairing is for the double name, not for the black kind alone* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry, and the text is Bhishagratna's English translation, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` (Vol. I), with notes and the plant index in Vols. II and III. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it.

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Sutra ch. 38, the Vidari-gandhadi group* (Vol. I, lines 15346-15359). The chapter is the classification of drugs "according to their therapeutical properties" in thirty-seven groups. The first group is given as: "The drugs known as Vidarigandha, Vidari, Sahadeva, Vishvadeva, Shvadanstra, Prithakparni, Shatavari, Sariva, **black Sariva**, Jivaka, Rishavaka, Mahasaha, Kshudra-Saha, Vrihati, Kantakari, Punarnava, Eranda, Hansapadi, Vrishchikah, and Rishavi, form the group known as the Vidari-gandhadi." Plain "Sariva" and "black Sariva" are therefore two members of the same group, side by side (lines 15356-15357). The metrical text (lines 15360-15364) reads: "The present group of drugs subdues the action of the deranged Vayu and Pittam and proves beneficial in phthisis, Gulma, aching of the limbs, Urdha Shvasa and cough." No property is given for black Sariva alone; the action is the group's.

*Sariva elsewhere.* Plain "Sariva" heads another group, the Sarivadi (Vol. I, lines 15626-15632: "Sariva, Madhuka, Chandana, Kuchandana, Padmaka, Kashmari phala, Madhuka-pushpa and Ushira ... allays thirst and proves curative in a case of haemoptysis"), and "the two kinds of Sariva" are named in a medicated ghee of Uttara-tantra, Chap. XXXIX (Vol. III, line 8618). The Sarivadi group does not name the black kind.

*The commentators' gloss (the reason for the identification).* Three notes in Bhishagratna bear on the second kind of Sariva.
- Vol. III, line 2886 (a note to the eye treatment of *Praklinna-vartma*, Uttara-tantra Chap. XII, where the text has "Utpala-sariva"): in the variant reading the translator says it means "in this case 'Syama-lata' by the term 'Asita-sariva'. Others again mean 'Rasanjana' by the term 'Asita'. — Dallana." *Asita* is "black"; so Dallana is reported as reading Asita-sariva as Syama-lata, and others as taking "Asita" to mean Rasanjana (a different drug).
- Vol. II, lines 18405-18407 (note to a Chikitsa recipe): "Chakrapani Datta in his compilation ... reads both the kinds of sariva, i.e., Anantamula and Syama-lata."
- Vol. III, plant index (Appendix No. 3): "Anantamula" is paired with *Hemidesmus Indicus* (line 31317), and further on in the same index (names at lines 32946-32947, equivalent at line 33005) the braced pair "Syama-lata / Sariva" is paired with *Ichnocarpus Frutescens*. The OCR separates names from equivalents, and the alignment is ours, supported by the many neighbouring pairs in the same block (for instance "Sigru" with *Moringa*, "Simsapa" with *Dalbergia Sissoo*). [USER: verify against the printed page.]

So the chain is: black Sariva = Syama-lata (Dallana, via Bhishagratna) = *Ichnocarpus frutescens* (Bhishagratna's index); the other, plain Sariva = Anantamula = *Hemidesmus indicus* (Chakrapani, via Bhishagratna). The index pairs "Sariva" with the Ichnocarpus entry as well, so which of the two plants the Sutra's bare "Sariva" means is left open by the sources read. The name is not "Krishna-sariva" anywhere in the text on disk; that alias in the first-pass note is not found in the three volumes (a search for "Krishna-sariva" and "Krishna sariva" returned nothing).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** It is a member of the Vidari-gandhadi group, whose action (soothing Vayu and Pittam; phthisis, Gulma, aching limbs, Urdha Shvasa, cough) is stated for the group.
- **What the text does not say.** Which part is used, any action of black Sariva alone, any preparation of its own.

The Codex does not translate the Sanskrit disease names into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text on disk gives no preparation of black Sariva as such; it is named in a group list. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The roots of the Sariva type are used in Indian practice as a cooling, scented drug, and two different plants (a *Hemidesmus* and an *Ichnocarpus*) have been traded under the name, which is why the identification of any single "Sariva" in an old text is held loosely. The classic's *Vayu*, *Pittam*, *Gulma* and *Urdha Shvasa* have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** The Vidari-gandhadi group's action is given above. No taste (*rasa*), potency (*virya*) or post-digestive taste (*vipaka*) is printed for black Sariva in the chapters read. None is imported from other texts or from the plain Sariva.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Black Sariva sits in a therapeutic group in Sutra chapter 38, classified by its action on the humours and on disease. No *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter was found that names it, and no relation to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* is stated. Its status in the tradition is therapeutic only, and the cultivation status is unattested. The popular *sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas* lists come from Samkhya and later material and not from Sushruta, and none is applied here.

**Practitioner's note.** A member of a Vayu-and-Pittam-soothing group, one of two Sariva kinds, and a plant the commentators identify by a chain of glosses. There is nothing here for a cultivator to take or do. A reader should not use any Sariva product on the strength of this entry.

### Contraindications

- **Identity is open.** Two different plants are connected with Sariva in the sources read; do not substitute one for the other.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Phthisis, cough, breathlessness, Gulma.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
