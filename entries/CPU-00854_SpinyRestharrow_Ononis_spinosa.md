# Spiny Restharrow — Restharrow, Cammock, Ononidis Radix, ʿirq al-anjudān (root of the plough-stopper)

## *Ononis spinosa* L. | Family: Fabaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, CM, DMM
**Energetic Classification:** Warm and dry in the second degree (Galenic); opening and attenuating, deobstruent to the urinary passages
**Training Phase:** Clearing Phase — the flushing and de-silting work that precedes deeper tonification

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Assyrian (AT):** The cuneiform materia medica of the Nineveh tablets records a family of thorny leguminous roots gathered for "stones in the water passage" and for the swelling of the legs that would not go down. Spiny restharrow, a tenacious field weed whose roots were stubborn enough to stop a plough — the origin of both "restharrow" and *Ononis* (from Greek *onos*, ass, the only beast said to relish it) — belongs to this class of hedgerow diuretics harvested at the field margins the Mesopotamian herb-gatherers worked.

**Dioscorides (DMM):** In *De Materia Medica* Dioscorides describes *ononis* (anonis) as a shrub whose root, boiled in wine and honey-water, provokes urine, breaks "the stone," and clears gravel from the bladder. The bark of the root taken in oxymel is his specific for urinary retention. This is the founding written statement of the plant's aquaretic reputation and the source from which the medieval and Unani physicians inherited it.

**Avicenna's Successors (SS):** Working from Dioscorides through the Arabic translation tradition, the Unani physicians classed the root as *mudirr al-bawl* (a promoter of urine) and *mufattit al-hasa* (a breaker of gravel), warm and dry, an opener of obstructions of the kidney and bladder. It entered the compound diuretic and lithontriptic formulas of the Persian and Andalusian pharmacopoeias in this role, and remained a European official drug (*Radix Ononidis*) into the modern herbal pharmacopoeias for irrigation therapy of the lower urinary tract.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** The plant that halts the plough became, by inversion, the plant that unhalts the body's own channels — the deobstruent whose signature is resistance overcome. The doctrine of "flushing" (irrigation therapy) is not fanciful: modern work rationalises the tradition through two mechanisms that sit neatly under the old "opening" language. The root's isoflavonoids inhibit human hyaluronidase-1, an enzyme in the homeostasis of the renal extracellular matrix, and its aqueous extract strips uropathogenic *E. coli* of its grip on the bladder wall — a molecular literalisation of "clearing the passage."

### Active Components

- **Isoflavones and isoflavanones:** formononetin, calycosin, pseudobaptigenin, and the dihydroisoflavones onogenin and sativanone, present largely as glucosides, glucoside-malonates and acetates. **Ononin** (formononetin-7-O-glucoside) is the classical marker glycoside.
- **Pterocarpans:** medicarpin and maackiain (phytoalexin-type isoflavonoid derivatives).
- **Norneolignan:** clitorienolactone B (first reported in the genus from this species).
- **Triterpene saponins, tartaric-acid derivatives, licoagrosides**, and a small essential-oil fraction historically cited for the mild diuresis.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Restharrow root is the Clearing-Phase aquaretic — used to raise urine volume and mechanically flush the lower urinary tract ("irrigation therapy") in minor complaints, gravel, and the sluggish, water-logged tissue state the older texts called dropsy of the legs. In the Kenetica frame it is a de-silting agent: it opens and rinses the excretory channel before any restorative or mineralising work is layered on top. It is adjuvant, not curative — a mover of water and a disruptor of bacterial adhesion, not an antibiotic or a stone-dissolver in any strong sense.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the traditional diuretic and urinary-tract indications now have partial mechanistic support, though no controlled clinical efficacy trials establish the aquaretic effect in humans.

- **Anti-adhesive activity against uropathogenic *E. coli*.** Deipenbrock, Sendker & Hensel (*Planta Medica*, 2020) showed that an aqueous root extract, characterised by isoflavones, isoflavanones, pterocarpans, licoagrosides and saponins, significantly and concentration-dependently (125–1000 µg/mL) inhibited adhesion of uropathogenic *E. coli* UTI89 to human T24 bladder cells and reduced their internalisation, without affecting bacterial proliferation or host-cell viability — rationalising the traditional use in urinary-tract complaints. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-1089-8645)
- **Hyaluronidase-1 inhibition as a diuresis mechanism.** Addotey et al. (*Fitoterapia*, 2018) isolated four isoflavonoids (onogenin, sativanone, medicarpin, calycosin-D) with human Hyal-1 inhibitory activity; sativanone was the most active (IC₅₀ ≈ 1501 µM), out-performing the glycyrrhizinic-acid control, offering a candidate molecular basis for the root's diuretic reputation via renal extracellular-matrix modulation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fitote.2018.08.013)
- **Phytochemical spine.** Gampe et al. (*J Pharm Biomed Anal*, 2016) resolved the full isoflavonoid profile by HPLC-QTOF-MS/MS and NMR, identifying 34 compounds (formononetin, calycosin, pseudobaptigenin, maackiain, medicarpin, onogenin, sativanone and their glucosides), correcting older mis-assignments (no biochanin A, genistein or daidzein confirmed). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpba.2016.01.058)

**Evidence grade: Traditional with emerging mechanistic support.** In-vitro anti-adhesive and enzyme-inhibition data plus a well-characterised phytochemistry; human clinical efficacy for diuresis or stone-clearance is not established. Claims should stay at the "flushing/adjuvant" level the European monographs use.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic / Unani:** Warm and dry (second degree); *mufattih* (deobstruent), *mudirr al-bawl* (diuretic), *mufattit al-hasa* (lithontriptic). Opens obstructions of kidney and bladder.
- **TCM analogue (by function, not classical listing):** would sit among the "drain dampness, promote urination" (*li shui shen shi*) agents — cool-to-neutral draining of water accumulation in the lower burner. *Ononis* is not a classical Chinese materia medica plant; this is a functional cross-reference only.
- **Ayurvedic analogue (by function):** *mūtrala* (diuretic) action; provisional *rasa* tikta-kashaya (bitter–astringent), *vīrya* ushna (heating), *vipāka* katu (pungent) by analogy to its warm-dry Galenic profile — not an attested classical Ayurvedic dravya.

### Contraindications

- **Oedema from cardiac or renal insufficiency — do NOT self-treat.** Irrigation/flushing therapy with any aquaretic is explicitly contraindicated where oedema results from impaired heart or kidney function; increasing fluid load in these states is dangerous and requires medical management. Restharrow is for minor, otherwise-healthy urinary complaints only.
- **Do not use where fluid intake must be restricted**, as the therapy depends on high fluid throughput.
- Fabaceae/legume sensitivity — avoid on known allergy.
- Pregnancy and lactation: insufficient safety data; avoid.
- If fever, flank pain, haematuria, or symptoms persisting beyond a few days occur, stop and seek medical assessment — these are not for self-directed flushing.
