# Pterospermum suberifolium — Muchukunda (printed "Muchukundu" in the Appendix of Bhishagratna's Sushruta Samhita)

## *Pterospermum suberifolium (printed in the Appendix as "Peterospermum Suberfolium"), a tree of the mallow family (Malvaceae, formerly Sterculiaceae); the identification is Bhishagratna's own, taken from his Appendix glossary by position in two separate columns, and is of medium confidence because the OCR has split the Sanskrit and Latin columns and the Latin spelling is garbled* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 8 November 2026 as an unverified placeholder from a residual extraction (key "Peterospermum Suberfolium [SS]"); this entry replaces it. A search of the Sushruta chapter files on disk for the Sanskrit name found one use of the drug in the text, and the Appendix gives the only Latin name.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The Latin name is printed in the Appendix glossary (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, "APPENDIX", line 32322: "Peterospermum Suberfolium."). The e-text has set the Sanskrit names in one column (lines about 32225 to 32255) and the Latin equivalents in another (lines about 32285 to 32335), so the pairing has to be made by position. The Sanskrit column runs "...Mesba-S'ringi, Methi, Methik^, Mishi (Madhurik^), Muchukundu" (lines 32250 to 32255), and the Latin column runs "...Gymnema Sylvestre. Trigonella foenum graecum. Foeniculum vulgari Anisi. Peterospermum Suberfolium. Phaseolus Mungo..." (lines about 32317 to 32323). The fourth name in that run, *Muchukundu*, therefore falls against *Pterospermum suberifolium*. The pairing of the earlier names in the same run (Methi and Methika with fenugreek, Mishi with fennel) is consistent with it. The name is given as *Muchukundu* in the Appendix and as *Muchukunda* in the chapter text.

The one place the drug is used in the chapter files is Chikitsa-sthana, ch. XVIII, "the medical treatment of Glandular Swellings (Granthi), Scurvy (Apachi), Tumour (Arvuda) and Goitre (Gala-ganda)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt`, lines 97 to 99). In the treatment of the *Pittaja* type of *Granthi*, after leeches, milk-and-water affusion, cold decoctions and plasters of bark, the text reads: "As an alternative, hot plasters compounded of the roots of the Trina-s'unya (Ketaki), or Muchukunda mixed with sugar, should be constantly applied to the affected part. The Granthi should be opened when fully suppurated, and the pus let out." The part used (the text says "the roots" of the pair, and the grammar leaves open whether it applies to *Muchukunda* as well) is not certain. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** One external use: a hot plaster of the root, mixed with sugar, applied constantly to a *Pittaja Granthi* (a glandular swelling of the *pitta* type), as an alternative to the first line of treatment.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no quantity of sugar, no duration, no internal use, and no rasa, virya or vipaka for this drug in the passages read.

The Codex does not translate *Granthi* into a modern diagnosis, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation given is the hot plaster (*pralepa*) of *Muchukunda* mixed with sugar, applied "constantly" (Chikitsa ch. XVIII, as quoted above). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(c) Absent.* The drug appears once, as a plaster for a glandular swelling, in the treatment chapter of Chikitsa-sthana. It was not found in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no passage read assigns it a rejuvenating or vitalising status. ### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The Latin name rests on Bhishagratna's Appendix glossary and on column position in a damaged e-text; the Sanskrit name may also have been applied to other trees. Do not gather or use any plant on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Toxicity.** None is stated in the passages read, and none is ruled out. [USER: verify]
- **Swellings.** A glandular swelling, suppuration or goitre is a medical condition for proper care; the text's instruction to open a suppurated swelling is a surgical act and not for self-treatment.
