### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the name index (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`: "KEPAIA, 3-168 (line 7938), UNKNOWN"); this entry replaces it. A search of the Dioscorides text for *kepaia*, *cepea* and *Cepea* found the name at 3-168 (line 7938) and the index entry "Cepea 536", and also in the heading "2-144. MALACHE AGRIA, MALACHE KEPAIA" (line 5752), which is the garden mallow and a different chapter. *Kepaia* there is the adjective "of the garden" in the Greek name for cultivated mallow and is not this plant. **Dioscorides (DMM).** Book 3, chapter 168, `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 7938 to 7940, within the Book 3 section headed "Roots of Akanthoda or Prickly Plants". The translator's gloss line is the single word "UNKNOWN". The whole chapter reads: "Cepea is a succulent similar to portulaca [4-168], yet it has darker leaves, and a thin root. A decoction of the leaves (taken as a drink with wine) helps slow painful urination and those who have a scabbed bladder, and helps most taken as a drink with a decoction of the roots of that asparagus called myacanthus." That is the full description: a succulent like purslane (the cross-reference is to chapter 4-168), darker in leaf, thin in root. No habitat, season, flower, height or alias is printed. The chapter gives no list of other names.

### Active Components

The text names no constituent and the plant is not identified, so no composition can be stated. In general terms, purslane-like succulents hold mucilage and organic acids; that is a statement about the comparison plant's family type and not about this plant. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** A decoction of the leaves, drunk with wine, helps "slow painful urination" and "those who have a scabbed bladder" (3-168, line 7940).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no duration, no instruction on gathering.

The Codex gives no protocol for an unidentified plant and does not translate "scabbed bladder" into a modern diagnosis.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation is a decoction of the leaves, taken as a drink with wine, best (says the text) when taken with a decoction of the roots of "that asparagus called myacanthus" (3-168). The roots of the plant itself are not said to be used. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
