# Bergenia stracheyi — Gadur, li ga dur (rGyud-bzhi), as paired by column position in the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary

## *Bergenia stracheyi, as the binomial printed in the scientific-name run of the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary; the Tibetan name "Gadur" (li ga dur) beside it is paired by column position only, and Clark 1995 prints other binomials for ga.dur, among them Bergenia crassifolia* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** **Named only by the Tibetan texts, and under this binomial only by the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. Both Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The extraction note says: "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, row 276). The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (Explanatory Tantra, glossary).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. The Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients prints its columns in separate blocks. In the block that follows the letter "G" the phonetic names are at lines 5274 to 5278: "Gabur", "Gadra", "Gadur" (line 5276), "Gakya", "Gangga chhung", "Gokya", "Goje", "Gongthokpa", "Go nyoe" and "Gudue" (ten). The Tibetan transliterations are on one line (5280): "ga bur", "ga bra", "li ga dur", "sga skya", "gang ga chung", "sgog skya", "go bye", "gong thog pa", "go snyod" and "rgu drs" (ten again). The scientific names are at lines 5282 to 5287: "Cinnamomum camphora", "Rubus ellipticus", "Bergenia stracheyi", "Hedychium spicatum" (all on line 5282), then "Gentiana umula", "Allium sativum", "Senecarpus sp. Erysimum melicentaea", "Carum carvi" and "Senecio scandens" (ten, counting the two names of line 5285 as two). The English names (lines 5289 to 5297) include "Camphor", "Raspberry", "Wild ginger", "Garlic" and "Caraway", with a dash for the others. "Bergenia stracheyi" is the third binomial, and "Gadur" is the third phonetic name and "li ga dur" the third transliteration. The glossary does not print them on one row, and the Codex does not state that it pairs them. The pairing is by column position only. Neighbouring positions agree with the names: "Gabur" is first against "Cinnamomum camphora" and "Camphor", and "Gudue" (*rgu drs*) is last against "Senecio scandens", which is the pairing of the Senecio entry I_1189. The third English place is a dash, so the glossary prints no English name for this drug. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text.* A search of the Men-Tsee-Khang text for "Gadur", "Ga dur" and "Bergenia" returned only the glossary lines above. The running text of chapter 20 does name "Ga dra" (a different glossary name, the second in the block), and the Codex does not read it as this drug. No action is therefore printed in the Men-Tsee-Khang text for "Gadur". [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, the same Tibetan name with other binomials (line 977).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, in the stretch of the materia medica that precedes the heading "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The text file prints headings for sections (4) tree medicines (line 871) and (5) resins (line 879), but none for section (6), plants, which the list of eight sections at line 802 names. The entry reads: "(li.)ga.dur: Meadow Cranesbill: Geranium pratense: Erodium stephanium: Bergenia crassifolia: Rhodiola wallichiana: [The male type has seven names and the female type has six. It has balanced hot and cold power, hot and sweet tastes, cures lung disorders and reduces swellings. It has thick leaves similar to rice shoots, and the root resembles a withered gland. The flower has a pink interior and resembles a bird's beak. The common variety grows in Tibet and has pink petioles. All types] cure infectious fevers, lung fevers and channel fevers."

Clark's preface (line 82) says that "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". Inside the brackets (the commentary layer) are the number of names, the hot and cold power, the tastes, the lung and swelling indications, the description and the words "All types". Outside the brackets (the root-text layer, as far as Clark's brackets mark it) are the Tibetan name, the English and Latin names, which are Clark's own identifications and not the root text's, and the clause "cure infectious fevers, lung fevers and channel fevers". Clark prints *Bergenia crassifolia*, and not *Bergenia stracheyi*, as the *Bergenia* of this line, and it is the third of the four Latin names the line prints (*Geranium pratense*, *Erodium stephanium*, *Bergenia crassifolia*, *Rhodiola wallichiana*), after the English name "Meadow Cranesbill". The reading of "Gadur" as the name *ga.dur* rests on the spelling ("li ga dur", "(li.)ga.dur").

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Bergenia stracheyi* and *stracheyi* found no entry on this binomial. I_1189 (*Senecio scandens*) names "*Bergenia stracheyi*" in its description of the same glossary block (the block that "also holds *Cinnamomum camphora*, *Rubus ellipticus*, *Bergenia stracheyi* and *Hedychium spicatum*"). I_884 (Elephant Ears, *Badan*) is the entry on Clark's *(li.)ga.dur* line (977), the same Tibetan name, and itself mentions the glossary pairing at line 5282 as by order. I_308 (*Bergenia purpurascens*) is an FMT entry on a different binomial of the genus. I_1242 (*Bergenia ciliata*, Clark's *brag.lcham*) is the sibling row in this batch. None is an entry on this binomial. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Bergenia* is a genus of the saxifrage family (Saxifragaceae) and that the genus is generally known to contain the compound bergenin along with tannins. Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained, which may not be this species. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan text that carries the name *ga.dur* records a drug that "cure[s] infectious fevers, lung fevers and channel fevers" (Clark, line 977). The link from that line to *Bergenia stracheyi* rests on a column position and a spelling. These are the text's claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Clark's line 977 prints "balanced hot and cold power" and "hot and sweet tastes", both inside the square brackets and so in the commentary layer, and only for the Clark drug *ga.dur*. The Men-Tsee-Khang text prints no quality for "Gadur". For this binomial no quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The only text that describes what *ga.dur* does is Clark's line 977, which classes it by what it cures: infectious fevers, lung fevers and channel fevers. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this name places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice. Its framing is therapeutic only, and the link from this binomial to the line rests on a column position.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The Tibetan name behind the binomial is a fever drug in Clark, and the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary links the binomial to the name only by column position. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. The identity rests on a column position and the species is not certain, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Infectious and lung fevers need medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
