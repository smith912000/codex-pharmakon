### Origin & Tradition

Lovage is a tall, robust umbellifer with a powerful celery-and-yeast aroma, long cultivated across Europe and the Near East as a pot-herb and medicine. Its classical name connects it to *Liguria* (the Genoese coast) via the old name *ligusticum*, and it has been a garden staple since at least Roman times — Charlemagne's capitulary and the medieval monastic physic gardens (Walahfrid Strabo's *Hortulus*, the plan of St Gall) record it among the essential medicinal herbs. The folk name "love parsley" and its reputation as a love-charm and aphrodisiac are part of its lore, while the modern "Maggi plant" nickname reflects how closely its savoury aroma resembles that famous stock seasoning.

Medicinally lovage is a classic **warming carminative and diuretic** of the aromatic-umbellifer type, kin in action to angelica, celery and fennel. Its dominant uses across the tradition are digestive and urinary: warming the cold, windy, sluggish stomach, dispelling flatulence and colic, stimulating appetite and digestion; and promoting urine, easing fluid retention and flushing the urinary passages (the root the stronger diuretic form). It also served as an **emmenagogue** and as an expectorant and "warming" remedy for cold catarrhal chest conditions, and the aromatic root and seed were chewed as a breath-sweetener and digestive.

The whole plant is aromatic and edible — leaf, stalk (sometimes blanched like celery), seed and root — and its therapeutic identity is unusually unified: a warming, drying, aromatic mover of digestive wind and urinary fluid, gentle enough for everyday use yet carrying the umbellifer family's standard pregnancy caution through its emmenagogue action and its furanocoumarin content.

3. **Antispasmodic.** For spasmodic digestive and menstrual complaints — the phthalide smooth-muscle action.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM / Galenic humoral degree:** Hot and Dry (commonly hot in the third degree) — a strongly warming, drying, attenuating aromatic; carminative and diuretic.
- **Unani mapping:** hot-dry; carminative (kasir-e-riyah), diuretic (mudirr-e-baul), emmenagogue, deobstruent.
- **Kenetica Qi/Shen tier:** ◉ **Qi** — on the warming, qi-and-wind-moving, fluid-opening axis; an aromatic-umbellifer carminative-diuretic kin to angelica, celery and Chuan Xiong (via shared ligustilide).

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy — caution/avoid medicinal doses.** Emmenagogue reputation and furanocoumarins; culinary amounts are food but medicinal preparations are traditionally avoided in pregnancy. [USER: verify.]
- **Kidney disease.** Avoid the diuretic "irrigation" use in impaired kidney function or where fluid balance is a concern (a standard caution for aquaretic herbs).
- **Phototoxicity.** Furanocoumarins can cause photodermatitis with skin contact + sun.
- **Allergy.** Possible in those sensitive to Apiaceae (celery/carrot family).
