# Lemnian earth — Lemnia Ge (LEMNIA GE), Dioscorides 5-113

## *A clay-like earth dug at a cavern on the island of Lemnos, mixed with goats' blood, made into tablets and sealed with the figure of a goat; the translator's gloss is simply "Lemnian Earth". The material is identified by its name and place with high confidence, but the chapter gives no mineral description, so "clay" is our general term and not Dioscorides' word* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** Dioscorides recommends this earth as an antidote to "deadly poisons" and as a means to make one "vomit up poisons". This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and it is not an antidote to anything.

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it. A search of `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` for "Lemnian" and "Lemnos" found this chapter and the index entry and nothing further.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-113, *Lemnia Ge* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10412; name-index row in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` at row 886, translator gloss "Lemnian Earth", class guess mineral; list row in `_sources/dioscorides.csv` at line 839, "Lemnia Ge", mineral; the book index lists "Lemnian Earth 800"). The whole chapter was read. It reads (lines 10414 and 10418):

"Lemnian earth is brought from the Island Lemnos out of a certain hollow cavern in a marshy place. It is gathered from there and mixed with goats' blood, and the men there make it into tablets, seal it with the figure of a goat and call it the seal of a goat. Taken as a drink with wine it is a very powerful antidote against deadly poisons, and taken beforehand it forces one to vomit up poisons. It is good against strikes and bites from venomous or poisonous beasts. It is mixed with antidotes. Some use it for purifications, and it is also good for dysentery."

The chapter thus gives: a place of origin (a hollow cavern in a marshy place on the island of Lemnos); the way it was handled there (gathered, mixed with goats' blood, formed into tablets, and sealed with the figure of a goat, so that it was known as "the seal of a goat"); and a list of uses: a drink with wine as an antidote against deadly poisons; taken beforehand, it makes one vomit poisons up; a remedy for strikes and bites from venomous or poisonous beasts; a component of antidote mixtures; used by some "for purifications"; and good for dysentery.

**The seal.** The seal is the one part of the chapter that is a mark of provenance: the tablets carry the figure of a goat, and the text says the men of Lemnos call them "the seal of a goat". The chapter does not say what the seal guaranteed, who applied it or whether the stamp was an assay of quality. [USER: verify]

**"For purifications."** The text says only "Some use it for purifications" (line 10418). It does not say what is purified, by what means or in what rite, and the Codex does not supply that.

**What the chapter does not give.** No colour, texture, taste or test of quality; no washing or burning; no season; no adulterant; no dose.

**Identity.** The translator's gloss is "Lemnian Earth", with no mineral name. The place and the tableting identify the material as the sealed earth of Lemnos by name. Our general description, "clay", is a plain-language guess from "earth" tablets and is not in the chapter. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The chapter names no constituent. In general terms, an earth taken from a cavern is a natural clay or similar earthy mineral, probably a hydrated aluminium silicate with iron and other elements in small amounts; this is a general statement about earths and is not drawn from Dioscorides. The goats' blood added at Lemnos is an animal product and, once dried into the tablets, contributes protein and iron; again, this is general knowledge and not in the chapter.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** Dioscorides records Lemnian earth, taken in wine, as a very powerful antidote to deadly poisons, and, taken beforehand, as a means to force the vomiting of poisons. It was also a remedy for venomous bites and for dysentery, an ingredient in antidotes, and used by some for purifications. These claims should not be relied upon: no earth is an antidote to poison, and a person who has swallowed a poison or has been bitten needs emergency medical care. Swallowing an earth of unknown composition, or inducing vomiting, can be harmful. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives a preparation as made at Lemnos: the earth is gathered from the cavern, mixed with goats' blood and made into tablets that are sealed with the figure of a goat (line 10414). It is taken "as a drink with wine" for poisons and "taken beforehand" to make one vomit them up (lines 10414 and 10418). It is also "mixed with antidotes". No quantity of earth, wine or blood is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any quantity from this entry.** [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] As general facts: clays can adsorb some substances in the gut, which is the basis of their historical reputation against poisons, but this does not make them reliable antidotes, and clays can themselves interfere with the absorption of medicines; swallowing earth carries a risk of infection and of contamination with metals or parasites; and inducing vomiting after a poisoning is no longer advised in general. The chapter's claims against bites and dysentery have no established modern equivalent. These points are not drawn from the chapter.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM).** The chapter prints no quality of the earth: no warming, cooling, drying, moistening or astringent quality is stated. It prints only actions (antidote, emetic when taken beforehand, good for dysentery).

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What the herbal offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention (provenance, season of gathering, sensory assay, adulteration detection), and this chapter shows the provenance part more fully than most in the book: a named island, a named kind of site (a hollow cavern in a marshy place), a local method and a stamp on the tablet. The chapter also records a ritual use ("Some use it for purifications") without describing or endorsing it; recording is not endorsing. The chapter carries no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation beyond noting how the herbal ties a material to a named place and a local seal, and that it records a purification use without detail. The earth is not an antidote and should not be tried as one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Poisoning, bites, dysentery:** these are medical emergencies or conditions needing care; do not rely on an earth. Do not induce vomiting after a poisoning unless a poison-control service advises it.
- **Swallowing earth:** risk of infection, parasites, metal contamination and of blocking absorption of other medicines.
- **Animal blood:** the Lemnos preparation includes goats' blood, an animal product.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children:** no statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity:** the chapter does not describe the earth; do not assume any commercial "Lemnian earth" matches it.
