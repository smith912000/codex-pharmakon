# Scrophularia incisa — gyer.shing.pa, Yhershingpa (rGyud-bzhi, Clark 1995 line 1066; Men-Tsee-Khang glossary and running text by name)

## *Scrophularia incisa, the first of three binomials Clark 1995 prints on line 1066 for gyer.shing.pa, followed by Scrophularia ningpoensis Hemsl and Tournefortia sibirica; no English name is printed there, and the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary sets Scrophularia decomposita ("Figwort") against "Yhershingpa" and prints no Scrophularia incisa* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi, read in two modern editions.** The editions are Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, both in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. They are two translations of one tradition's text and are not two independent traditions. A search of the Sushruta, Ebers and Assyrian text folders on disk for *Scrophularia* found no hit. The Shennong text (Yang 1998) prints the genus once, in the name Xuan Shen "(Radix Scrophulariae Ningpoensis)" at line 2726 (bitter and slightly cold, "nontoxic", with Yang's note 160 at line 2744 as his modern commentary). That is Clark's **second** binomial, and the Shennong entry is a Chinese drug in another tradition; the Codex does not read it as the Tibetan *gyer.shing.pa*. The Dioscorides text prints *Scrophularia aquatica* and *Scrophularia maior* in a translator's SUGGESTED line for the Greek drug *galiopsis* (line 8890), which are European species and are not read as this plant. The row was added from a printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**One Tibetan drug, two rows.** This entry and **I_1355** (*Scrophularia decomposita*, CPU-01791) are written from the same two passages, because the two editions give two different species for one Tibetan name. This entry takes Clark's first binomial and I_1355 takes the Men-Tsee-Khang binomial. Neither is merged into the other, and the Codex does not choose between the species.

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (line 165) the row is keyed by the **Tibetan name** *gyer.shing.pa*, which is the first name Clark prints, and not by a binomial. Its locus is "Clark 1995 (line 1066)", its translator identification reads "Scrophularia incisa; Scrophularia ningpoensis Hemsl; Tournefortia sibirica", and its note reads "more than one Latin name given; key uses the first". The spine binomial *Scrophularia incisa* is therefore the **first** of Clark's three Latin names. *Scrophularia ningpoensis* and *Tournefortia sibirica* have no row of their own. The Men-Tsee-Khang row for the neighbouring binomial is line 392 (*Scrophularia decomposita*, glossary line 6044), and it is the key of I_1355.

**Identity doubt.** Clark prints two figworts and a *Tournefortia*, which belongs to a different plant family, for one Tibetan name, and the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary prints a third figwort against the same name by column position. The two editions therefore agree on the Tibetan name and on the genus, or on two of Clark's three genera, and differ on the species, and the Codex does not choose. Clark's preface says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance" (line 83) and that "the data given in some of these is occasionally suspect" (line 68). Neither the Clark line nor the glossary prints an author for *Scrophularia incisa*. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1066).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 ("The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines", line 792), within "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (heading at line 997). The entry follows the entry that ends "Both Codonopsis and Leucas] cure chest fevers and common cold" (line 1065) and is followed by *me.tog.ser.chhen* (line 1067). It reads:

"gyer.shing.pa: Scrophularia incisa: Scrophularia ningpoensis Hemsl: Tournefortia sibirica: [The superior type is dense and black, whereas the inferior type grows in damp areas and has a red petiole, yellow flower and seed similar to Zanthoxylum (gyer.ma).] It cures [all types of] pox-fever."

Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries. The two types and their description are therefore the commentary layer, and so is "all types of". Outside the brackets, as Clark's marks stand, are the Tibetan name, the three Latin names, which are Clark's identifications, and "It cures ... pox-fever". The seed "similar to Zanthoxylum (gyer.ma)" ties the drug to a neighbouring Tibetan name, *gyer.ma*, which is a different drug. Clark's name index lists *gyer.shing.pa* (line 1752) and the Latin index lists *Scrophularia incisa* (line 1644) and *Tournefortia sibirica* (line 1652).

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, the same Tibetan name (read through the name, not the binomial).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. In the Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, "Y" block, the phonetic names are "Yakyima" (line 6036), "Yherma" (6037) and, on line 6038, "Yhershingpa Yugu shing Yumo dejin Yungwa". The transliterations on line 6042 begin "gyer shing pa" after "gya' kyi ma" (6040) and "gyer ma" (6041). Counted in order, "Yhershingpa" is the third phonetic name and "gyer shing pa" the third transliteration, and the third scientific name on line 6044 ("Chrysoplenium camosum Zanthoxylum armatum Scrophularia decomposita") is *Scrophularia decomposita*, with "Figwort" among the English names (line 6051). The count is checked on "Yherma" against *Zanthoxylum armatum* and on "Yungwa" against *Curcuma longa* and "Turmeric". The glossary does not print the pairing in one row, so it is by position only, and the full count is set out in I_1355. In the running text, chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225; colophon at line 3583), within the run headed "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (line 3428), line 3448 reads: "Yhershingpa cures hot disorder associated with small pox." The printed index lists "Yhershingpa 209" (line 7567). "Hot disorder" and "small pox" are the translators' words, kept as printed.

The two editions agree in kind: Clark's drug "cures ... pox-fever" and the Men-Tsee-Khang *Yhershingpa* "cures hot disorder associated with small pox". The Codex reads *gyer.shing.pa* and *Yhershingpa* as one drug from the spelling and that agreement; neither text says so. The Codex's own list of rGyud-bzhi names (`_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv`, line 289) carries "Yhershingpa" with no botanical name and the identity flagged uncertain. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Scrophularia incisa*, *Scrophularia decomposita*, *gyer.shing.pa*, *Yhershingpa* and *Tournefortia* found no entry on this drug under its own name. **I_1355** (*Scrophularia decomposita*) is the sibling written from the same two passages. **I_1326** (*Papaver nudicaule*, *me.tog.ser.chhen*, tag FMT) quotes the next Clark entry (line 1067) and the sentence on Men-Tsee-Khang line 3448, so it prints the *Yhershingpa* sentence as context. **I_973** (*Zanthoxylum armatum*, tags SS, FMT) quotes Clark's line 1066 for the seed "similar to Zanthoxylum". **I_641** (*Scrophularia ningpoensis*, Xuan Shen, tags CM, DMM, SBJ) is the entry on Clark's second binomial in other traditions, and **I_1185** (*Scrophularia aquatica*, tag DMM) and **I_715** (Common Figwort, tags AT, DMM, CM) are entries on European figworts. None is merged. Whether I_641 should gain an FMT tag on the strength of Clark's second binomial is a question of identity, not of attestation, and is not decided here.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Scrophularia* is a genus of the figwort family (Scrophulariaceae) whose species commonly carry iridoid glycosides, and that *Tournefortia*, Clark's third genus, is a different genus of woody plants and herbs in another family; these are statements about the genera and not about this plant. The lines read give no composition for the Tibetan drug, and nothing is stated here about what it contains. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a drug that "cures [all types of] pox-fever" (Clark, line 1066) and "cures hot disorder associated with small pox" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3448). These are the texts' claims, attached to this binomial only through Clark's first Latin name, and they are not Codex protocols. Pox, fever and rash need medical care, and the Codex gives no protocol and no dose for them. The Chinese Xuan Shen, whose Latin name is Clark's second, is a drug of another tradition with its own indications (line 2726 of the Shennong text), and none of them is carried over to this entry.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica gives this drug by what it does: it cures pox-fever, and the Men-Tsee-Khang text says the same in its own words. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. A search of both Tibetan texts for the drug's names (*gyer.shing.pa*, *Yhershingpa*, *Scrophularia*) found it only in the lines cited above, the glossary and the indexes. It is not in the rejuvenation chapter (Men-Tsee-Khang chapter 23, colophon at line 3950), and the herb entries of Clark's chapter 20 that mention essence extraction (lines 971, 1043, 1078, 1103, 1121 and 1144) do not include it. Its framing is therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a pox-fever drug in one short line of each edition, with no preparation and no dose, and its Latin name is the first of three that Clark prints. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. Pox and fever need medical care; the identity is open among at least three species, no preparation or dose is given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry.
