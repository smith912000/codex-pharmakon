# Animal fat / tallow — pihu / shamnu (the project's Assyrian list's transliterations)
## *Rendered animal fat, "rendered fat (bovine / sheep suet)" in the project list's own words; no text on disk prints an identification, so the animal is the list's gloss and is not read from a tablet* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** AT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Assyrian (AT).** The attestation rests on a list row and nothing more. The project's list `_sources/assyrian.csv` has one row, line 63: "Animal fat / tallow (pihu / shamnu)", chemical column "Rendered fat (bovine / sheep suet)", class animal, source reference "Assyrian tablets; BM tablet K.4120 (suet of kidney of black animal); africame", not flagged id-uncertain. The "africame" reference is a popular website and was not used. The same K.4120 reference sits on a separate list row for lapis lazuli (line 54: "BM tablet K.4120 cited in search results for eye ointment (lapis + suet)"), where it is described as coming from "search results".

The Assyrian text on disk (Thompson 1923, `Sources/AT_Assyrian/text/Thompson_1923_Assyrian_Medical_Texts.txt`) is OCR of the cuneiform plates of 660 tablets. Its "Index to Tablets" (from line 44) is a table of British Museum numbers and plate references, and line 322 holds a row "4120 .. 12, 4"; it appears to give a plate and entry for K. 4120, but the key to the columns was not verified and the plate itself is unreadable cuneiform OCR. No content of tablet K.4120 was read, so the list's parenthesis "suet of kidney of black animal" is unverified here. A search of the text for "fat", "suet", "tallow" and "kidney" returned no usable line, and `Sources/AT_Assyrian/extract/README.md` says "nothing extractable". The transliterated names *pihu* and *shamnu* are the list's; the same list gives *shamnu* as the name of sesame oil (line 62), so the word may not mean tallow at all. The blocking warning is in force: no tablet number is relied on, and no deity or demon is given. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the list names it. In general terms, tallow and suet are rendered animal fat, mainly triglycerides of saturated and monounsaturated fatty acids, with small amounts of cholesterol. This is a general statement, not a reading of any tablet.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene:** animal fat that is unrendered, old or contaminated can carry bacteria and should not be put on broken skin or the eye.
- **Diet and ethics:** the material is of animal origin; some people avoid it for religious, ethical or dietary reasons.
- **Identification:** the animal, the part and the meaning of the Assyrian words are not established by the sources retrieved.
