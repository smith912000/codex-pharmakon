# Ashvattha (Pipal / Bodhi Tree) — Aśvattha, Bodhi-vṛkṣa, Pippala, Caladala, Peepal

## *Ficus religiosa* L. | Family: Moraceae

**Tradition Tags:** Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS) — member of the **Panchavalkala** (five astringent barks) and the **Kṣīrī-vṛkṣa** (milk-trees); Nyagrodhādi gaṇa; Caraka Saṃhitā; classical nighaṇṭus
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Kaṣāya (astringent) rasa · Śīta (cooling) vīrya · Kaṭu (pungent) vipāka · pacifies Pitta and Kapha (Kapha-Pitta-hara)
**Training Phase:** Containment · Cooling · Wound-consolidation · Contemplative grounding

### Origin & Tradition

No plant in the Indian world carries more sacred weight than the *Aśvattha* — the pipal, whose very species epithet, *religiosa*, records the awe it commanded in the botanists who named it. It is the **Bodhi tree**, under which the Buddha attained awakening; it is the *Aśvattha* of the *Bhagavad Gītā*'s inverted cosmic tree; it is a **Kṣīrī-vṛkṣa** milk-tree and one of the **Panchavalkala** astringent barks. Its heart-shaped leaves on their slender petioles tremble in the faintest breeze — the *caladala*, "moving-leaf" — a restlessness the tradition read as the tree's perpetual inner life.

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (surgical-Ayurvedic tradition):** Ashvattha bark is one of the five **Panchavalkala** barks used together as the classic astringent wound-wash and healing decoction (*vraṇaśodhana/vraṇaropaṇa*) for ulcers, mucosal lesions, and inflamed surfaces. Like its sibling figs it is *saṃgrāhī* (astringent/absorbent), cooling, and staunching — arresting discharge, cooling Pitta, and consolidating tissue. Its bark, latex, leaf, and fruit are used across the cooling-astringent groupings.

**Caraka Saṃhitā and internal medicine:** Ashvattha serves *raktapitta* (bleeding), *atisāra/pravāhikā* (diarrhoea, dysentery), *prameha* (the "sweet-urine"/urinary group), *pradara* (menorrhagia), skin disorders, and burning Pitta conditions. It carries a reproductive-tonic reputation in some contexts and is used for gum and dental health.

**Folk and pan-Indian use:** Bark decoction for diabetes, diarrhoea, and skin; leaf and latex topically; the tree venerated and never casually felled. According to PubMed, a major review records ethnomedical use "throughout South Asia, where it has been used for about 50 types of disorders," spanning the central nervous, endocrine, gastrointestinal, reproductive, and respiratory systems and infectious disease (Singh, Singh & Goel, 2011, *J Ethnopharmacol*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2011.01.046).

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Tree of Awakening:**
The pipal is arguably the single most sacred tree of both Hinduism and Buddhism. In the Vedic-Hindu stream it is *Aśvattha*, dwelt in by Viṣṇu (and, in the *Gītā*, identified with the cosmic world-tree — "roots above, branches below" — whose felling with the axe of non-attachment leads to liberation); its trunk is circumambulated, threaded with sacred cords, and never desecrated. In Buddhism it is the **Bodhi tree** — the very species under which Siddhārtha Gautama sat and became the Buddha at Bodh Gaya — making *Ficus religiosa* the axis-tree of enlightenment, and its descendants (the Anurādhapura Bodhi in Sri Lanka among them) objects of living pilgrimage for over two millennia. The tree that shelters awakening, that trembles perpetually as if breathing, that outlives generations, is the perfect Kenetica emblem for *containment that grounds contemplation*: an astringent, cooling, stabilising drug whose symbolic charge is stillness, rootedness, and clarified attention. Among the astringent tetrad, Ashvattha carries the *contemplative-grounding* accent — the containing tree under which the mind settles. It is the sacred inverse counterpart to the sweet, outpouring, life-giving Jīvantī: where she enlivens and refills, Ashvattha stills, cools, and holds.

### Active Components

- **Phytosterols & amino acids:** β-sitosterol and related sterols; free amino acids
- **Furanocoumarins:** bergapten, psoralen-type compounds (characteristic of the species)
- **Phenolic / flavonoid constituents:** phenolic components and flavonoids
- **Tannins:** condensed tannins underlying bark astringency
- **Hydrocarbons, aliphatic alcohols, and volatile components** reported across bark, leaf, and latex

Per the review literature, phytochemical work "led to the isolation of phytosterols, amino acids, furanocoumarins, phenolic components, hydrocarbons, aliphatic alcohols, volatile components and few other classes of secondary metabolites from F. religiosa" (Singh, Singh & Goel, 2011, *J Ethnopharmacol*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2011.01.046).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Ashvattha is filed as a **cooling containment and contemplative-grounding agent** within the Ficus tetrad — the fig-drug for *staunching, cooling, and settling*. Its astringent-cooling-absorbent profile serves discharging, over-heated, and dysregulated states, with a symbolic-functional accent on grounding and clarity.

Primary Kenetica use cases:
- **Wound-consolidation:** as a **Panchavalkala** component for cleansing and healing ulcers and mucosal surfaces.
- **Arrest of discharge / bleeding:** diarrhoea, dysentery, bleeding disorders (*saṃgrāhī* / *stambhana*).
- **Metabolic containment:** traditional and preclinically-noted antidiabetic action for prameha-type disorders.
- **Pitta-cooling and skin support:** burning states, inflammatory heat, dermal complaints.
- **Contemplative-grounding accent:** the tree's cognitive-enhancer / nervine reputation (traditionally noted) — used symbolically in Kenetica for stillness and settled attention.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, a comprehensive review of *Ficus religiosa* collates its botany, traditional uses, phytochemistry, and pharmacology, reporting that "fresh plant materials, crude extracts and isolated components ... showed a wide spectrum of in vitro and in vivo pharmacological activities like, antidiabetic, cognitive enhancer, wound healing, anticonvulsant, anti-inflammatory, analgesic, antimicrobial, antiviral, hypolipidemic, antioxidant, immunomodulatory, antiasthmatic ... antitumor, antiulcer" and more (Singh, Singh & Goel, 2011, *J Ethnopharmacol* 134(3):565–583) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2011.01.046). Crucially, the same review cautions that many studies "employed uncharacterized crude extracts. Thus, it is difficult to reproduce the results and pinpoint the bioactive metabolite," calling for phytochemical standardisation and bioactivity-guided identification.

**Evidence grade — MODERATE / PRECLINICAL.** The activity spectrum is broad and repeatedly reported, but the review's own verdict is that much of it rests on uncharacterised crude extracts with reproducibility limits and few isolated-compound confirmations. Present the wound-healing, antidiabetic, and neurocognitive claims as **traditionally-grounded and preclinically-reported, not clinically proven.** Do not invent effect sizes, trial data, or the specific "50 disorders" as efficacy claims — that figure describes *traditional use*, not proven action.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṣāya (astringent), with Madhura (sweet) note
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Guru (heavy), Rūkṣa (dry) — astringent-drying
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha-Pitta-hara; may aggravate Vāta if used excessively dry
- **Karma (actions):** Saṃgrāhī (astringent/absorbent), Stambhana (arresting), Vraṇaropaṇa (wound-healing), Grāhī (bowel-binding), Raktastambhaka (haemostatic), Prameha-hara (antidiabetic tendency), Dāhapraśamana (relieves burning), Varṇya (complexion-improving), Medhya tendency (traditionally noted cognitive support)
- **Gaṇa membership:** **Panchavalkala** (five astringent barks) and **Kṣīrī-vṛkṣa** (milk-trees); allied to the Nyagrodhādi gaṇa
- **Prabhāva:** Its sacred status and traditionally-reported broad-spectrum action, including the contemplative/nervine accent

### Contraindications

- **Vāta aggravation / constipation:** dry, astringent, binding — can worsen Vāta-dryness and constipation; pair with unctuous adjuvants.
- **Furanocoumarin content:** bergapten/psoralen-type compounds are photosensitising — caution with topical use plus sun exposure, and with photosensitising drug interactions `[USER: verify]`.
- **Not a substitute for glycaemic or neurological medical management:** antidiabetic and cognitive uses are traditional/preclinical — do not present as proven therapy.
