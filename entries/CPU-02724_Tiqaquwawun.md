**Avicenna, Canon (CM), quoting Dioscorides.** The entry is *ṭīqāqūwāwūn* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text line 13725, to line 13732). It opens: قال ديسقوريدوس (13726), "Dioscorides said". The plant: ورق شبيه بورق عنب الثعلب البستاني وله شعب كثيرة زهره أسود صغير كثير وبزره يشبه بالجاورس في غلف شبيه بالخرنوب الشامي في شكله (13727-13728): "a leaf like the leaf of the garden *ʿinab al-thaʿlab* (literally 'fox-grape', the name of a nightshade-type plant; our reading, [USER: verify]), with many branches; its flower black, small and abundant; its seed resembles millet (*jāwars*) in husks resembling the Syrian carob in shape." Roots: "three or four, about a span long, white, sweet-smelling, warming" (13728-13729).

Use: the next words are corrupt in this text (وأكثرها ينبت هذا النبات إذا أخذ منه مقدار من وينفع في ست قوطوليات من شراب حلو يوما وليلة, 13729-13731): "... when a quantity of it is taken ... in six *qūṭūlīyāt* (cotylae) of sweet wine for a day and a night". The quantity of the plant is missing; the six cotylae are the wine. After that: "and the drinking of that cleanses the womb" (شرب ذلك نقى الرحم) and "if made as a stuffing and drunk, it brings on milk, as it is said" (أدر اللبن فيما يقال, 13731-13732). The sentence between is not intelligible and is not translated. [USER: verify against another copy of the Canon or Dioscorides].
