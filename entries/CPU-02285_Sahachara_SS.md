# Sahachara — Sahachara, "the two kinds of Sahachara" (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *A drug of the Viratarva'di group, printed "the two kinds of Sahachara", so two kinds under one name; the translator gives no gloss in the chapter text. Identification: unresolved at species level. The only Latin line the sources read offer is positional, from the Vol. III appendix columns, and reads "Barlaria Cristata" (Barleria cristata). Basis: a column count, not a stated pairing. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not securely identified. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The first-pass check on 30 October guessed "unresolved (Barleria candidate)"; that guess is kept, with the basis given below. A search of all the Sushruta text on disk (the chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and Bhishagratna's three volumes) for *Sahachar* found three places: the Viratarva'di group list, the Vata-soothing list in chapter 39, and the Vol. III appendix of names.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Chapter 38 of the *Sutrasthana* is the chapter "which deals with the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam)" and says that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (Vol. I lines 15343 to 15350; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3 to 9). The drug is placed there by group, and a group's virtue is stated for the group as a whole.

1. **Group list, chapter 38.** The Viratarva'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15398 to 15405; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 57 to 64). Bhishagratna prints: "The drugs known as Virataru, the two kinds of Sahachara, Darbha, Vrikshadani, Gundra, Nala, Kusha, Kasha, Ashmabhedaka, Agnimantha, Morata Vasuka, Vasira, Bhalluka, Kuruntaka, Indivara, Kapotavanka, and Shadanstra enter into the composition of the group known as the Viratarvadi." (The OCR prints "I\'ala" for Nala and "Sh^adanstra" for the last name.) "The two kinds" is the translator's wording; the text read does not name the two kinds or say how they differ.
2. **The group's virtue.** The metrical text reads: "The group subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles" (lines 15407 to 15411, across a page break). This is the group's virtue, not a statement about Sahachara alone.
3. **The Vata-soothing list, chapter 39.** In the *Samshamaniya* drugs (those that "soothe or pacify the deranged humours"), the Vata-Samshamana-Varga names "Bhadradaru, Kustha, Haridra, Varuna, Meshshringi, Vala, Ativala, Artagala, Kachhura, Sallaki, Kuverakshi, Virataru, Sahachara, Agnimantha, Vatsadani, Eranda ..." and adds that these, with the Vidarigandhadi group and the first two Panchamula groups, "are possessed of the general virtue of soothing (restoring to its normal state) the deranged Vayu" (Vol. I lines 16004 to 16016; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` lines 86 to 97). Again the virtue is the list's, not the drug's.
4. **The appendix.** Bhishagratna's Vol. III, Appendix No. 3 prints Sahachara in a left-hand column of Sanskrit names (line 32673, between "Rodhra" and "Sahadeva") and the Latin synonyms in a separate right-hand column. The right-hand lines are not tied to their names in the OCR. Counting down both columns, the Latin line standing against Sahachara is "Barlaria Cristata" (line 32722); the lines on either side are "See Lodhra" (32718, against Rodhra) and "See Bala" (against Sahadeva). The pairing is positional, made by counting, and the OCR does not state it. Entry I_1391 (*Barleria prionitis*) reports that entry I_371 (*Kuruntaka*, *Barleria cristata*) names *Sahacara* as *Barleria prionitis* in its own text, which the appendix lines do not support; I did not re-read I_371, and the Codex does not reconcile the two here. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Identity unresolved.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Viratarva'di group: subduing disorders of deranged Vata and curing stone, gravel and urinary troubles (group-level). As a member of the Vata-soothing list in chapter 39: restoring deranged Vayu to its normal state (list-level).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no taste or potency for this drug on its own, no account of the two kinds, and no identification beyond a positional appendix line.

The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no plant for any of the conditions named.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in two lists, in a classification of drugs by the use of the whole group (chapter 38, *Dravya-Sangrahaniya*) and in a list of drugs that soothe a deranged humour (chapter 39). Neither is a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns this drug no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only, and only at the level of its group. A search of the Sushruta chapter files found the name nowhere in the *Chikitsasthana* (the rejuvenation and vitality chapters are among them). The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it.

**Practitioner's note.** An under-described name in two lists, with two kinds the text does not tell apart and an identification that rests on a column count. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without a secure identification nothing can be gathered or used. A cultivator reads it as a name in a record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is not securely identified, and the translator's "two kinds" is unexplained. Do not substitute any herb for it, and do not rely on the appendix's positional Latin line.
- **The conditions named.** Urinary obstruction, stone and gravel are named for the group. These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Group membership.** The drug is one of many in its lists, and a group's virtues are not safety statements about any one member.
