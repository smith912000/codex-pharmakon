# Field mushroom (Agaricus) — Mushrooms, "Udbhida group" / Chhatrika (Appendix 3, OCR "Ohhatriksb")
## *Mushrooms generally; Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 pairs the Sanskrit name with "Agaricus Campestris", the field mushroom, by a pairing made from two OCR columns* | *(Volume IV — Fungal/Algal, shelved in Volume I)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurvedic Tradition (SS).** Sushruta treats mushrooms as food, in the chapter on food and drink. In Bhishagratna's translation, *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (*Annapana-Vidhi*), the passage headed "The Udbhida Group" reads: "Mushrooms are generally found to grow on stacks of straw (Palala), or are seen vegetating on the stems of bamboo (Venu) or sugar-cane (Ikshu), or as sprouting up from beneath the surface of the ground (Udbhida), or growing on a heap of decomposed cow-dung (Karisha)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1847-1853). The text then gives the properties of each kind by the substrate it grows on (lines 1853-1873; see Traditional Energetic Classification). The grouping is by habitat. No text read separates one species of mushroom from another by name.

**Identity.** The entry's tag rests on one row of `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` (line 457): "Ohhatriksb", Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31522; id line 31553), identified as "Agaricus Campestris." The notes field says this pairing was "proposed by a Sonnet agent from the two OCR columns" and checked by script. In `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` the Appendix 3 name column and the Latin column are printed separately (names at line 31522, the Latin "Agaricus Campestris." at line 31553), so the pairing is an inference from column order, not a printed equation. The OCR form "Ohhatriksb" is damaged and may stand for *Chhatrika*. Whether it is the same word as the *Chhatra* of the rasayana list (below) was not established. [USER: verify]

**Other places the word or the shape occurs.** (1) *Nidanasthana* ch.2: polyps of the vaginal passage "may assume the shape of a mushroom, or an umbrella" (`nidana/SS_Nidana_ch02.txt` line 206), a simile for a growth, not a use of the mushroom. (2) *Sutrasthana* ch.19: "Drugs such as Chhatra, Atichhatra, Languli, Jatila, Bramhacharini, Lakshmi, Guha, Atiguha, Shata-viryaya, Sahasra-viryaya and white mustard seeds should be placed on the head of the patient" (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch19.txt` line 190), in the management of the patient with rites and fumigation. (3) *Chikitsasthana* ch.30, the chapter on tonic remedies "which remove mental and physical distress" (*Nivritta-santapiya-Rasayana*, lines 3-4), lists *Chhatra* and *Ati-chhatra* among the "all-healing drugs" (line 29, "S'veta-kapoti, Krishna-kapoti, Gonasi, Varahi, Kanya, Chhatra, Ati-chchatra, Karenu, Aja, Chakraka ..."; the descriptions run to about line 220), and describes them: "The Chhatra and the Ati-chchhatra are bulbous in their origin and are found to be attached to a plant of the Sveta-kapoti species. Both of them are possessed of the virtue of arresting death and decay and act as prophylactic against the Rakshasas (malignant spirits)" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch30.txt` lines 145-149). Bhishagratna describes these as bulbous plants, and the passage does not call them mushrooms; they are therefore not treated here as the field mushroom. (4) *Sutrasthana* ch.45 mentions honey "known as the Chhatram" and a "Chhatra species" (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 1118, 1133); that is a honey term and is unrelated.

### Active Components

Stated generally. A mushroom is the fruiting body of a fungus. Edible field-type mushrooms are mostly water, with protein, chitin-bearing cell walls (dietary fibre), B vitamins and some minerals; fungi contain ergosterol, which can form vitamin D2 on exposure to light. Fungi also include poisonous species, and visual identification of a wild mushroom is a recognised source of fatal error. No figures were read in the sources retrieved and none are given here.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Sushruta's mushrooms are an item of diet described by humoral effect, not a drug with an indication. No modern protocol is supplied. Wild mushroom collecting carries a real risk of misidentification, and the tradition's own grouping by substrate (straw, cane, bamboo, dung, soil) does not help a modern reader tell an edible species from a poisonous one.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurvedic (SS).** Sushruta's ch.46 gives, for each kind (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 1853-1873), only the qualities Bhishagratna prints:

- Those on stacks of decomposed straw (*Palala*): "sweet in taste and digestion and tend to produce a state of dryness in the organism. They subdue the three deranged humours of the body."
- Those on sugar-cane stems (*Ikshuja*): "a sweet pungent taste. They leave an astringent after-taste in the mouth and are cooling in their potency."
- Those on decomposed cow-dung (*Karisha*): "properties similar to those of the preceding class. They aggravate the Vayu, are heat-making in their potency, and have an astringent taste." The wording "similar to the preceding class" sits oddly beside "heat-making", since the preceding class was called cooling. This is the translation as printed. [USER: verify]
- Those on bamboo stems (*Venuja*): "an astringent taste and tend to enrage or aggravate the bodily Vayu."
- Those that grow on the ground (*Bhumija*): "heavy of digestion and do not inordinately generate the Vayu, their tastes varying according to the soil they grow on."

No single *rasa*, *virya* and *vipaka* is given for mushrooms as a whole, and none is given for the field mushroom in particular.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status for mushrooms as read.* Sushruta's frame for ch.46 is food and drink (*annapana*), judged by taste, potency and effect on *vayu*, *pitta* and *kapha*. Mushrooms appear there as food by substrate, with mixed effects (the straw kind subdues the three humours; the cane, dung and bamboo kinds vary). Nothing in that passage places mushrooms in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for them. A word *Chhatra* and an *Ati-chhatra* do appear in the *rasayana* chapter *Chikitsasthana* ch.30, with a claim of "arresting death and decay", but Bhishagratna describes them as bulbous plants attached to another plant, and no link to the Appendix 3 "Agaricus Campestris" row is shown. The cultivation claim is therefore not transferred to the mushroom. The sattvic, rajasic and tamasic food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here supports taking a mushroom as a cultivation substance. Sushruta describes mushrooms as an ordinary, variable food and the single rasayana-chapter hit is of uncertain identity. The practical caution is the modern one: do not eat wild mushrooms on a textual identification. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Poisoning:** many wild fungi are poisonous or deadly; field mushrooms have toxic look-alikes. Never eat a wild mushroom on the strength of this entry.
- **Allergy and digestion:** some people react to mushrooms; Sushruta himself calls the ground-growing kind "heavy of digestion" (ch.46).
- **Hygiene:** mushrooms from dung or decomposed straw (the substrates Sushruta names) carry microbial risk when taken wild.
- **Tradition's own cautions:** the bamboo and dung kinds "aggravate the Vayu" (ch.46).
- **Identity:** the Sanskrit-to-Latin pairing is uncertain (see Origin & Tradition).
