# Grasshopper (tettix) — Tettiges (as printed in the Osbaldeston and Wood Dioscorides, "Grasshoppers")
## *Grasshoppers; the translators gloss the chapter "Acridiiae, Locustidae — Grasshoppers"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The grasshopper is chapter 2-56 of *De Materia Medica*, headed "TETTIGES", in the Osbaldeston and Wood English (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 5161; the entry is also in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`). Under the heading the translators print "Acridiiae, Locustidae — Grasshoppers" (line 5162). That line is the editors' gloss; Dioscorides' own word is *tettix* (plural *tettiges*), and the text gives the animal no further description. The whole chapter is one sentence (line 5163): "Grasshoppers if they are eaten roasted help disorders of the bladder."

The chapter is immediately followed by 2-57 *Akrides*, locusts (line 5164-5166), a separate substance with a different use: "Locusts (smoked and inhaled) help difficulty in urinating, especially in women. Their flesh is useless." That chapter adds that a wingless kind called *asiracos* or *onos*, dried and "taken in a drink with wine", is "a great help to those bitten by scorpions", and that "the Africans who inhabit Leptis feed on these abundantly [food]". It is mentioned here only because the two chapters sit together and the second says of locust flesh the opposite of what the first says of grasshopper flesh: the locust's "flesh is useless", the grasshopper is "eaten roasted". The file gives no explanation of the difference. [USER: verify]

The chapter sits in Book Two, which Dioscorides introduces (line 4904) as the book of "living creatures", honey, milk and animal fat, followed by cereals and pot herbs. This entry reads only the grasshopper chapter and its neighbour on locusts.

### Active Components

Grasshoppers are insects of the order Orthoptera. Insect bodies are mostly water, protein and chitin (the structural polysaccharide of the exoskeleton) with fat and minerals; roasting drives off water and denatures the protein. No composition specific to this entry was read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced content is one sentence: roasted grasshoppers eaten "help disorders of the bladder". The Codex gives no protocol. Bladder complaints (pain on passing urine, blood in the urine, retention) can signal infection, stones or other conditions that need diagnosis, and no source read supports the claim. In some regions grasshoppers and locusts are eaten as food, and that culinary use is lawful where the insects are safe to eat, but it is not what Dioscorides describes.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation given is that they are "eaten roasted" (line 5163). No number, quantity, duration or frequency is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] Route: oral, as a food.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Insects are an edible protein source in many cultures, and allergy to them (cross-reacting with shellfish and dust-mite allergens) is documented. Grasshoppers collected from sprayed fields can carry pesticide residues. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is supplied here. What he can offer a cultivator is a discipline of attention (provenance, season, sensory assay, detection of adulteration), and this one-sentence chapter has none of it: no species, source, season or quality sign. The only practical detail is that the insects are eaten roasted. There is no cultivation status and no amuletic use.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this. It is a one-line folk remedy for the bladder recorded by a first-century physician, and the sensible use of the entry is as a record, together with the reminder to see a clinician for bladder symptoms.

### Contraindications

- **Bladder symptoms:** need assessment; infection or stones can worsen if untreated.
- **Allergy:** insect allergy is real, and people with shellfish or dust-mite allergy may react.
- **Contamination:** wild-caught insects may carry pesticide residues or parasites; they should be cooked.
- **Legal and conservation status:** common grasshoppers are not generally protected; collecting rules in reserves vary. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** some diets and faiths restrict insects as food; this entry takes no position beyond noting it.
