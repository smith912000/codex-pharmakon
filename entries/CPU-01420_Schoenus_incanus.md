# Schoenus incanus — Schoinos (Dioscorides 1-16, 4-52)

## *Schoenus incanus* (the Dioscorides translators' suggested identification; bog rush, with rushes of the genus *Juncus*) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** This plant is named only by Dioscorides (DMM). The binomial is the suggestion printed on the SUGGESTED lines of the translation by Osbaldeston and Wood (2000), not Dioscorides' own. 1-16 offers *Schoenus incanus* ("Bog Rush"), *Juncus conglomeratus*, *J. effusus* ("Rushes, Sweet Rushes") and *J. arabicus*; 4-52 offers *S. incanus*, *S. ferrugineous*, *S. mucronatus*, *J. conglomeratus*, *J. effusus*, *J. arabicus* and *J. acutus*. The two chapters describe different things (a fragrant imported aromatic in Book One, a native rush in Book Four) under one Greek name, *schoinos*. The binomial is therefore a loose gloss and the identity is doubtful. [USER: verify] Source: `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).**

*1-16 SCHOINOS* (Book One: Aromatics; heading at line 3685; "see schoenus 4-52"). The text, headed in the translation as *Juncus odoratus*: "grows in Libya and Arabia and in that part of Arabia called Nabataea, and this is the best. The Arabic is next, but that from Libya is useless." The assay: "Choose that which is new, red, and full of flowers; which when cut or cleft inclines to a purple colour, is thin, smells sweet like a rose when it is rubbed between the hands, and bites the tongue with considerable burning." The flower, the reeds and the root are used. Action: "It is diuretic, bringing down the menstrual flow, and dissolving gaseousness. It causes catarrh in the head. It is mildly astringent. It has a breaking, digesting and opening strength." The flowers drunk "are good for bloody vomiting and a painful stomach, as well as the lungs, liver and kidneys. It is mixed with antidotes, but the root is more astringent and therefore is given for a squeamish stomach. One teaspoonful is good for dropsy and convulsions, and is given for some days with the same amount of pepper. A decoction of it as a hip bath is most convenient for inflammation around the vulva." Other names: Babylonian [juncus], *teuchitis*.

*4-52 SCHOINOS* (Book Four: Other Herbs & Roots; heading at line 8454; "see 1-16"). "Two types of shoenus are found, the one of which is called the smooth juncus, the other the sharp juncus, pointed on the top, and of this again there are two types for one is barren, and the other has a round, black seed — but the reeds of this are thicker and more fleshy. There is a third type — much more fleshy and rougher than the first two — which is called holoschoenos." Use: "The seed of any of them (dried and taken in a drink with diluted wine) stops discharges of the intestines, and excessive bloody discharges, and induces urine. It is also good for headaches, and the tender leaves near the root (applied) are good for harvest spider bites. The Ethiopian juncus has seed that will cause sleep. We must beware of too much of it in liquid medicines for it encourages sleep excessively." Other names: *juncus laevis*, *oxypternos*, *supercilium solis*; the Romans *juncus marinus* or *juncus manualis*; the Africans *chudua*.

### Active Components

Rushes and sedges are generally monocot plants of wet ground. No constituent information was read for the plants Dioscorides describes, and the identification is doubtful. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides records an aromatic diuretic and emmenagogue (1-16) and an astringent seed for bowel and bleeding complaints (4-52), as his claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

*1-16:* the flower, reeds and root; "One teaspoonful is good for dropsy and convulsions, and is given for some days with the same amount of pepper"; a hip-bath decoction. *4-52:* the dried seed "taken in a drink with diluted wine", with the warning against too much of the Ethiopian kind in liquid medicines. The teaspoonful in 1-16 is the only quantity printed. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** 1-16 prints "mildly astringent", with "a breaking, digesting and opening strength"; the root is more astringent. The best sample "bites the tongue with considerable burning". 4-52 prints the seed as stopping discharges and inducing urine. No degree system is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. 1-16 is a good example of the discipline of attention the spec describes: provenance (Nabataea best, Libya useless), freshness, colour, the colour of a cut section, the rose-like smell when rubbed, and the burning on the tongue. 4-52 distinguishes smooth, sharp and fleshy kinds. No cultivation status is assigned. The binomial is a translators' gloss across two chapters that describe different plants. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the source read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The sensory assay in 1-16 is the lasting element; the plant itself is not recommended.

### Contraindications

The identity is doubtful and no safe preparation can be stated. Dioscorides himself warns of too much Ethiopian rush seed for sleep (4-52) and that the 1-16 aromatic "causes catarrh in the head". It is recorded as bringing down the menstrual flow; do not use in pregnancy. The traditional uses are not advice.
