**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Two kinds of locus were read.

*Appendix 3 identification.* Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3, "Names of drugs (with Latin synonyms)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`), prints the name *Tuvari* at line 33172 and, in the Latin column of the same page, "Cajanus Indica." at line 33198. `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` (line 635) records this pairing as made by position from the two OCR columns, checked by script. On the page I read, the name column runs Tulasi, Tumburu, Tuttha, Tuvari, Tuvarika, Tvak and the Latin column runs "Ocimum Sanctum", "Zanthoxylum Alatum", "Sulphate of Copper", "Cajanus Indica", then "Cinnamon bark", so the order is consistent, but the pairing remains an OCR inference. In the same appendix the name *Adhaki* (line 31130) sits in a block whose Latin column reads, for that position, "See Tuvara." (line 31188), which points *Adhaki* to a head-word *Tuvara*. No separate head-word *Tuvara* was found in the name column read, so that cross-reference is only probable. [USER: verify]

*Adhaki in the text.* *Adhaki* is named as one of the edible pulses in the Sutrasthana: in the pulse list of ch.20 (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch20.txt`, lines 56 to 60: "the Mudga, the Vana-Mudga, the Makushtha, the Kalaya, the Masura, the Mangalya, the Chanaka, the Harenu, the … Adhaki and the Satina"), in the list of dried pot-herbs and pulses of ch.21 (`SS_Sutra_ch21.txt`, line 268) and in ch.46 (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 190 to 220). Of the properties, ch.46 says: "The species Adhaki subdues the Pittam and Kapham, and does not excessively agitate the Vayu in the organism." The closing verses of the same chapter (line 2158) count "the pulses such as the Mudga, the Adhakia, and the Masura" among the best of the grains of the Paddy Group. These passages are about food, and none states that *Adhaki* is the pigeon pea. Separately, the drug *Tuvaraka* (ch.45 line 1001; Uttara ch.16 line 50, where the translator glosses it "Lodhra") is a different substance and is not read here as this plant.

### Active Components

Pigeon pea seed is a pulse. As a legume seed it is generally understood to be rich in protein and starch, with fibre. No constituent is given in the sources read, and the composition of this plant is not asserted from them. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The one sourced statement, Sushruta's on *Adhaki* as a food pulse (ch.46), concerns diet, and the identification with this plant is unconfirmed. The Codex gives no protocol for pigeon pea.

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* *Adhaki* appears in the food chapters, in the pulse list of ch.20 and in ch.46's account of the pulses and cereals, where it is described by its effect on the doshas and ranked among the best of the Paddy Group. It is not named in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters, and nothing read ties it to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*. The identity of *Adhaki* and *Tuvari* with the pigeon pea rests on Bhishagratna's appendix as paired from OCR columns. **Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. If the Sushruta identification holds, the pulse is an ordinary article of diet with a stated effect on the doshas, and the Chinese and Unani tags are unsupported inheritances from an index.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Pulses are ordinarily eaten cooked, and the Codex does not recommend any therapeutic use of this plant from the passages read. Anyone with a legume allergy should avoid it, and ordinary medical care should be sought for any illness.
