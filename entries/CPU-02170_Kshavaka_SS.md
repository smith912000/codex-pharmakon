**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** Single-text entry. The name is printed *Kshavaka* in Bhishagratna's translation (Vol. I; also *Kshavakam* in the metrical text of ch. 46). The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder with the locus Sutra 38, Vol. I line 15462; this entry replaces it. No Latin name is printed, so the identification is unresolved.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The gana (Sutra ch. 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 122; Bhishagratna Vol. I line 15462).* "The Surasa'di Group:— The drugs known as Surasa, white Surasa, Fainjjhaka, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika form the group known as the Surasadi Gana." The metrical text (lines 126 to 129): "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough."

*The potherbs (Sutra ch. 46, `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1415 to 1424).* Kshavaka stands in the list of "spices and herbs" (Pippali, Maricha, Shringavera and so on), after Kutheraka and before Ksharapushpa and Shigru. The metrical text that follows (line 1427 on) says "Potherbs have a pungent taste, are relishing, and heat-making in their potency, and subdue the Vayu and Kapham. They are variously used for seasoning food." That is said of the class.

*The sprouts (Sutra ch. 46, lines 1830 to 1840).* "The Kshavaka, the Kulechara, and the tender sprouts of Vansha, etc., generate the deranged Kapham, and tend to increase the discharge of stool and urine." The metrical text adds: "The Kshavakam helps the germination of worms in the intestines. It is slimy and sweet in taste, and tends to increase the secretions of the internal organs. It generates the Vayu and does not inordinately increase the Pittam and Kapham in the body." The same passage therefore gives Kshavaka a different character from the Surasadi group (which subdues Kapham), a difference the text does not explain.

*A recipe (Uttara-tantra ch. 40, `SS_Uttara_ch40.txt` lines 816 to 817).* In the treatment of Pravahika: "Clarified butter mixed with oil and duly cooked with S'unthi and Kshavaka would give instantaneous relief, if licked." The recipe gives no amounts in the lines read.

**What the sources do not give.** No Latin name and no description. Both the gana and the potherb list suggest a pungent herb, and the sprout passage suggests a young shoot. The text does not say which part is meant. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As part of the Surasadi group: worms, catarrh, loss of appetite, asthma and cough (group action). As food: a seasoning herb, or a sprout that increases Kapham and the discharge of stool and urine. In a medicated ghee with dry ginger (S'unthi) for Pravahika (Uttara-tantra ch. 40).
- **What the text does not say.** What the plant is, which part is used, or any rasayana use.
- The Codex gives no protocol.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** Sutra ch. 38: the Surasadi group subdues Kapham (lines 126 to 129). Sutra ch. 46: as a sprout, "slimy and sweet in taste", it "generates the Vayu" and "does not inordinately increase the Pittam and Kapham" (lines 1835 to 1839), and as a herb it falls under the pungent, heat-making potherbs (lines 1427 to 1429). The two passages do not agree about Kapham, and the Codex does not reconcile them. Each is given as printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Kshavaka is named in the Sushruta Samhita as a member of a therapeutic group (gana) and in the other places listed under Origin & Tradition. The gana is a grouping by therapeutic action. It is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status, and the text read assigns Kshavaka neither. A search of the whole chapter set on disk (sutra, chikitsa, kalpa, uttara, nidana, sharira) for the name found no occurrence in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters. It is also listed as a potherb and a sprout (ch. 46), and in one ghee recipe (Uttara-tantra ch. 40). Nothing links it to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The status is unattested, and only a therapeutic one is recorded.

**Practitioner's note.** A seasoning herb or sprout and an ingredient in a dysentery ghee. The tradition records no more, and an unidentified plant gives a cultivator nothing to act on.
