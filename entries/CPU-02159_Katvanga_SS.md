### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk (Bhishagratna's 1907 English translation).** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from a name list (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, which records "group list (ch.38); no identification given here"). This entry replaces it. The text was opened at the line given and the neighbouring lines read. Searches of the other texts on disk (Dioscorides, Shennong, rGyud-bzhi, Ebers) were not made under a Sanskrit spelling, so absence elsewhere is not claimed.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Katvanga is printed in the Amvashtha'di group, Sutrasthana chapter 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15665 to 15668 (the name is on line 15668). The translator's sentence reads: "Drugs known as Amvashtha, Dhataki flowers, Samanga, Katvanga, Madhuka, Vilva-peshika, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Palasha, Nandi-Vriksha and Padma keshara, enter into the composition of the group known as the Amvashthadi Gana." The metrical text (lines 15676 to 15682) reads: "The two medicinal recipes or groups prove beneficial in a case of deranged Pitta, favour the healing of ulcers, bring about the adhesion of fractured bones and prove curative in cases of dysentery where the stools are found to consist of lumps of thick and matured mucous (Pakvatisara.)" The translation says "the two" because the Priyangvadi group stands immediately before.

**Two further appearances.** (1) In the Uttaratantra, chapter 40, on diarrhoea (*Atisara*): "A draught of milk, honey and clarified butter (in equal parts) mixed with sugar, Ajamoda, Katvanga and Yashti-madhu (as an after-throw) would relieve the griping (Sula) in the intestines" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch40.txt` line 399). (2) In the Uttaratantra, chapter 12, on eye disease, "sugar, Yashti-madhu, Katvanga, Mastu, Amla, honey, Saindhava, Vija-puraka, Kola and Dadimba of acid taste and other acid fruits" are used as an eye-drop or wash (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch12.txt` lines 116 to 118). The bracketed "(in equal parts)" in the first passage is the translator's measure for the milk, honey and ghee, not for Katvanga.

**Identification.** The text gives none. Katvanga is listed in Sanskrit lexica as a name of the tree Shyonaka (*Oroxylum indicum*), which the spine holds as CPU-00863; that equation is not stated in the Bhishagratna text read, and the sweet and astringent uses above are not tested against it. ### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is named inside a therapeutic drug group (*gana*) of Sutrasthana chapter 38, the Amvashtha'di group. The group's metrical text speaks of curing or subduing disorders. It does not call the group, or any member, a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) drug, and it does not mention *ojas*. The sources retrieved therefore attest a therapeutic framing only, and no cultivation status. The *sattva/rajas/tamas* lists of later popular literature are not from Sushruta and are not applied. Its recipe uses (a diarrhoea after-drink and an eye wash) are also therapeutic.

**Practitioner's note.** A name in a group against *Pitta* disorders, ulcers and dysentery, and in two recipes. Nothing in the record supports a cultivation use, and with the plant unidentified there is nothing to do with it. A cultivator does nothing with this entry.
