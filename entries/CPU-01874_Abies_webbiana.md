# Abies webbiana — Talisha, Talisa-patra (Sushruta Samhita, as transliterated by Bhishagratna)

## *Abies webbiana (Wall. ex D.Don) Lindl., the Himalayan silver fir, whose leaf is the drug known in Sanskrit as talisa or talisa-patra; the identification is Bhishagratna's, printed in his Appendix as "Abies Webbiana" against the name "Ta'lisa / Ta'lisa'-patra", and is of high confidence at species level on that basis, but the Sushruta text itself describes no plant, so the identification rests on the Appendix alone* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, under the Sanskrit name Talisa.** This is a single-text entry. The row was added on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the Appendix to Bhishagratna's Volume III; this entry replaces it. The attestation was confirmed on 30 October by opening the Appendix (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, lines 33078-33092) and then searching the chapter files for the name.

**Sushruta Samhita, Appendix (SS).** The Appendix is a two-column glossary that the scan has split into a column of Sanskrit names and a column of identifications. At line 33084 the names read "Ta'lisa" and "Ta'lisa'-patra" (joined by a bracket), and at line 33086 the identification beside them reads "Abies Webbiana," (Vol. III, Appendix, `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`). The name beneath it, "Tama'la", is paired with "Zanthochymus Pictorius", and the next, "Ta'mbula", with "Peper Beltle", so the pairing runs name by name down the page. The pairing is read from a damaged scan. [USER: verify against the printed page.]

**Sushruta Samhita, main text (SS).** The name occurs in the chapters as follows (spelling as printed by Bhishagratna, who uses both Talisha and Talis'a):

- *Sutrasthana* ch.39, the chapter on cleansing and soothing drugs, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` line 53: Talisha stands in the list of errhines (drugs for the nose): "Pippali, Vidanga, Apamarga, Shigru, Siddharthaka, Shirisha, Maricha, Karavira, Vimvi, Girikarnika, Kinihi, Vacha, Jyotishmati, Karanja, Arka, Alarka, Lashuna, Ativisha, Shringavera, Talisha, Tamala, Surasa, Arjaka..." The same chapter (lines 62-63) says which part of each plant is used: "the leaves of those commencing with Talisha and ending with the Arjaka therein". On this passage the leaf is the part of Talisha used as an errhine.
- *Uttaratantra* ch.17, `uttara/SS_Uttara_ch17.txt` line 76: "Rasanjana, Rasa (Juice), honey, Talis'a-patra and Svarna-gairika should be pasted together with the Rasa of cow-dung and applied (in the manner of an Anjana)" in day-blindness. Ch.10 (line 61) has a compound of Talisa, Ela, Gairika, Usira and conch-shell pasted with breast-milk and applied as an eye-salve, with a variant reading "Kasis'a" noted by the translator.
- *Uttaratantra* ch.51, asthma, line 139: a ghrita named for it, "Talis'adi-Ghrita", cooked with Hingu, Talis'a, Tamalaki, Ugra, Jivanti, Kushtha, Saindhava and others, "proves curative in all forms of asthma".
- *Uttaratantra* ch.39 (fever), line 1293, and ch.62, line 130: Talis'a-patra among the drugs of the Kalyanaka and Maha-kalyana ghrita, the latter said to cure "Gulma, cough, fever, asthma, phthisis and insanity" (ch.62, lines 130-135).
- *Kalpasthana* ch.5 (snake-bite), line 409, in the Rishabhagada; ch.7, lines 64 and 108, in the Kalyanaka Ghrita and a second compound. *Chikitsasthana* ch.2 (fresh wounds), line 437: an oil with Talis'a, Padmaka, Mansi, Harenu, Aguru, Chandana and others "acts as a good healing remedy in cases of traumatic ulcers"; ch.5, line 205, a plaster.
- *Uttaratantra* ch.32 (Putana), line 18: ghrita cooked with Talis'a, Kushtha, Khadira and Chandana.

In every one of these places Talisa is one ingredient in a longer list. The text never describes the plant, its habitat, or its taste on its own account in the passages read. A search of `Sources/SBJ_Shennong`, `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides` and the other texts on disk for the name found nothing that can be tied to this plant under the tradition's own name. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The leaf is an errhine in ch.39. It is an eye-salve ingredient in ch.10 and ch.17, a wound-oil ingredient in Chikitsa ch.2, and an ingredient of several ghritas given for asthma, fever, insanity and in children's diseases.
- **What the text does not say.** It does not describe the plant, and it names no dose and no duration for Talisa on its own.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

Every preparation found is a compound: a paste applied as an eye-salve (Uttara ch.17, line 76), a ghrita cooked with the other drugs named above (ch.51, line 139; ch.39, line 1293; ch.62, line 130), an oil (Chikitsa ch.2, line 437), a plaster (Chikitsa ch.5, line 205), and a leaf in the errhine list (Sutra ch.39, lines 53-63). No quantity of Talisa is given in the passages read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Abies webbiana* is a conifer of the western Himalaya, and its needles are aromatic. Whether the plant has the effects the classic lists is not established here. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* The name appears only in ingredient lists for errhines, eye-salves, ghritas, an oil and a plaster. The one chapter read on tonic remedies, Chikitsa ch.30 (the *Nivritta-santapiya Rasayana* chapter, `chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch30.txt`), does not name Talisa, and no vajikarana or rasayana status is attested for it in the sources retrieved. No ojas, tejas or prana claim is made here. The Kalyanaka ghritas that contain it are given for fever, asthma, insanity and similar disease, which is the therapeutic frame, not a cultivation one.

**Practitioner's note.** A leaf used as a nasal drug, an eye-salve ingredient and a minor ingredient in compound ghritas for disease. The record does not support any cultivation use, and a cultivator has nothing to do with it from this text.

### Contraindications

- **Nasal and eye use.** The classic's errhine and eye-salve uses are medical procedures. Do not apply any preparation of this plant to the nose or eyes on the strength of this entry.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources read. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The species is the Appendix's identification, taken through a damaged scan. Other conifers and unrelated plants have been sold under the same Sanskrit name in later practice. [USER: verify]
- **Conservation.** *Abies webbiana* is a Himalayan conifer and its legal status where gathered has not been checked. [USER: verify]
