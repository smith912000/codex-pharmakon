# Gentiana robusta — kyi.lche, Kyiche (rGyud-bzhi, Men-Tsee-Khang glossary)

## *Gentiana robusta, as the scientific name the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints (line 5405) in the position that column order gives to "Kyiche"; Clark 1995 prints Gentiana straminea Maxim for the same Tibetan name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT), and under this binomial only by the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Sushruta texts on disk (including the three Bhishagratna volumes) for the genus name *Gentiana* found Dioscorides 3-3 GENTIANE (line 6541, glosses *Gentiana lutea* and Yellow Gentian) and Yang 1998's Long Dan (line 1936) and Qin Jiao (line 2494). These are other species and are not attributed to this row. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary, line 5405.* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. In the "K" block the phonetic names include "Kyerpa Kyiche Kyurura" (line 5401), with the transliterations "skyer pa kyiIce skyu ru ra" (line 5403, where "kyiIce" is an OCR form of the Tibetan *skyi lche*). The scientific names at line 5405 read "Berberis aristata Gentiana robusta Emblica officinalis". Three names stand against three scientific names: "Kyerpa" against *Berberis aristata* (the English column has "Indian barberry"), "Kyiche" against *Gentiana robusta*, and "Kyurura" against *Emblica officinalis* (English "Emblic myrobalan", line 5418). The first and last pairings are consistent with the names as printed, which supports the middle one. The English cell for "Kyiche" cannot be read with certainty because the English column is out of step in this block. The pairing is by position and was not printed in one row. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text.* "Kyiche" is named in four places. In chapter 19, the list of the bitter-tasting group (line 3213) reads "Nimpa, Tikta, Bongnga karpo, Honglen, Sergyi metok, Dhungmo nyung, La tsi, Tripa, Kyerpa, Bhashaka, Drak shhun, Kyiche, Rekon and other similar substances are the group of medicinal substances having a bitter taste." In chapter 20, line 3428: "Kyiche cures hot disorders of the vessel organs and tripa." In chapter 21, lines 3596 to 3597: "Kyiche and Kyerpa are the group of medicines that cure tripa disorders." The index lists "Kyiche 187, 206, 220" (line 7412).

*Clark 1995, the same Tibetan name (line 1003).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20. The entry reads: "kyi.lche: Gentiana straminea Maxim: Swertia petiolata (white type): [has fourteen names, for example, sha.lang, etc. It has long, smooth, thick green leaves and white flowers similar to Gentiana algida (spang.rgyan). The stalk is red, and the seeds are so dark as to resemble iron filings. The taste is bitter with coarse power. The black type (Gentiana dahurica) grows in shady places and has thinner leaves than the white type. It has a blue-brown flower and petiole, reduces swellings and cures scrofula.14 Both types] cure vessel organ fever and bile fever." Clark's chapter 21 (line 1209) lists "Gentiana straminea" in "the class of medicines which cure bile disorders". Clark does not print *Gentiana robusta*. The two Tibetan texts agree on the Tibetan name, the genus and the bitter taste, and differ on the species; the Codex does not choose.

**Overlap.** I_316 (*Gentiana straminea*, the first of Clark's two names for *kyi.lche*) is the sibling entry on the same Tibetan drug; I_1257 (*Corydalis edulis*) and I_1260 (*Corydalis nepalense*) name "Kyiche" in neighbouring Men-Tsee-Khang lists, and I_315 (*Gentiana crassicaulis*) cites the drug in passing. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Gentiana* is a genus of the gentian family (Gentianaceae) and that bitter secoiridoid glycosides, of which gentiopicroside is an example, are characteristic of the family; both Tibetan texts record a bitter taste for the drug (the Men-Tsee-Khang bitter group, line 3213; Clark, line 1003). Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained or which part was used.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a bitter drug that "cures hot disorders of the vessel organs and tripa" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3428) and that "cure[s] vessel organ fever and bile fever" (Clark, line 1003). These are the texts' claims, attached to this binomial through the glossary's column position, and they are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no part used, no quantity, no frequency and no duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] Clark's commentary describes leaves, flowers, stalk and seed and does not say which part is taken.

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Tibetan terms ("hot disorders of the vessel organs", "tripa", "bile fever") are not modern diagnoses or measured outcomes. The identity is open between two species the Tibetan texts print, so no study of one would bear directly on the other. The bitterness of gentians is a general, well-established property of the genus.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** The Men-Tsee-Khang text places "Kyiche" in the group of medicinal substances having a bitter taste (line 3213). Clark's commentary layer for the same Tibetan name (line 1003) prints "The taste is bitter with coarse power". No post-digestive taste is printed. The actions printed are the curing of hot disorders of the vessel organs and *tripa*, and of *tripa* disorders in the paired group (lines 3428 and 3596 to 3597).

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Men-Tsee-Khang text gives this drug by its taste and what it does: it is a bitter drug that cures hot disorders of the vessel organs and *tripa*, and Clark's entry says the same in kind. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this name places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the sentences cited above were read in full and none mentions one. Its framing is therapeutic only, and the link from this binomial to that framing rests on the glossary's column position, with Clark naming a different species for the same Tibetan name.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a bitter drug for *tripa* and vessel-organ disorders in the Tibetan texts, with no preparation and no dose, and its species is open between two. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. The species is open, no preparation or dose is given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Gentians are a large genus and some alpine species are protected in places; no conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
