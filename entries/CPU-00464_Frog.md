# Frog (in oil) — "Frog", "A Frog" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus; no Egyptian word is printed)
## *A frog, as Bryan renders the animal; Bryan prints no Egyptian word and no binomial, so the spine's "Anura sp." is the list-maker's gloss and is not read from the text* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*. "Frog" stands in Bryan's list of the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" in Chapter VII "Organic Remedies" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1404, between "Cat" and "Tadpole"; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 118, class animal, "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). The spine row reads "Frog (in oil)", "Anura sp.", note "Ebers; Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus; id-uncertain" (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 89). A second list row, line 117, reads "Frog", source "Ebers Papyrus; Ghalioungui 1987 trans., Glossary I (Medicaments)". Wikipedia was not used, and the Ghalioungui glossary was not opened.

**Where the frog is prescribed.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "frog" and "toad". "Frog" occurs in two lines only: the list (line 1404) and one prescription.

- **Burns, lines 2226-2231**, in Chapter XII "Minor Surgery" (heading lines 2208-2209). Bryan introduces the topic: "Next, Burns. . .. Burns are troublesome lesions at the best of times and much needless suffering is saved if one can have at hand a quick and easy remedy to apply. The Ancients recognized this, although we might not be able to see any particular merit in the preparations they employed" (line 2226). The prescription, as printed (lines 2228-2230): "TO PREVENT BURN WOUNDS / A Frog / Warm in Oil and rub therewith." The next line (2231) is a second remedy in the same series: "ANOTHER The Head-of-the-Electric-Eel Warm in Oil and apply to the part of the body."

**Related items, printed separately, not counted as the frog.** "Tadpole" is its own head word in the list (line 1404), and Bryan quotes "Dried-Tadpoles-from-the-Canal, crushed in Oil" among hair remedies (line 4055); that is a different item and is not read as a frog.

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say what kind of oil was used, whether the animal was whole or in part, living or dead, fresh or dried, how many were used, or how often the rub was repeated. The word "to prevent" in Bryan's heading is his rendering of the Egyptian title; the Egyptian was not seen. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. The 1987 translation and the Egyptian words for these items could change any reading above. ### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it. In general terms, amphibian skin carries mucus and, in some species, skin secretions of varying toxicity, and the body is mostly water, protein and fat. Nothing in the sources retrieved says which species was meant or that any component acts on a burn. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The one use the sources retrieved show is a frog warmed in oil and rubbed on a burn. Putting an animal body, or fat warmed with it, on burnt skin is an infection risk, and some amphibian skin secretions are toxic. Burns need medical care. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution, and the one use found is an external application for burns. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); no recitation is printed with this prescription in Bryan's summary, which is no proof the papyrus gives none. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** an animal body on a burn risks infection; burns need medical care.
- **Toxicity:** some amphibian skin secretions are poisonous, and the species meant is not known from the sources retrieved.
- **Conservation and law:** many amphibians are protected or declining in parts of their range, and collection may be regulated; the status was not retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** the material is a killed animal.
