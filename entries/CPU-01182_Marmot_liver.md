# Marmot liver — hphyi.wa, as printed in Clark's translation of the rGyud-bzhi (Marmota bobak Müller; Marmota himalayana Hodgson)
## *The liver of the marmot, a burrowing rodent of the high plateau; named in the Tibetan materia medica of animal origin* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan Tradition (FMT).** The text is Barry Clark's 1995 translation of the rGyud-bzhi, file `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`. Under "(8) Medicines of Animal Origin" (line 1147), in the "Meat" group, the line reads: "hphyi.wa: Marmot: Marmota bobak Muller: Marmota himalayana Hodgson: [has seven names]. Its liver unites cracks [and fractures] in bones." (line 1170). The preceding item on the same line is the otter: "Its liver cures urinary retention", and the one after it is goat liver, "supremely beneficial in eye diseases [and as an anthelmintic]". The project list `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` has no row for this item that I found. The binomials are Clark's printed identifications. [USER: verify]

The brackets in the printed text mark added matter. Clark says in his preface (line 80) that he "interpolated liberally from the Blue Aquamarine commentary", so the phrase "[has seven names]" is commentary-derived, and the main line "Its liver unites cracks ... in bones" is the part to take as the root-text statement. I did not read a statement from Clark that explains his bracket convention in so many words, so this reading is an inference. [USER: verify]

Marmot appears elsewhere in the same translation, as flesh and not as liver:

1. Diet for wind disorders (chapter "5. Methods of Treatment", line 174): "meat of horse, donkey and marmot, one-year-old (dried) meat, human flesh, ..."
2. The classification of meats (line 672): marmot is listed among "(h) Burrowing animals", together with porcupine, frog, snake, Tibetan badger, lizard, gecko and scorpion.
3. Direct therapeutic techniques (chapter "30. Direct Therapeutic Techniques", line 1411): the wind treatment lists "the flesh of marmot, horse, donkey and human".

These loci concern the flesh. The liver has the one statement above. I do not carry the flesh statements over to the liver.

Clark observes (line 57): "Nowadays only very few animal products are used, although many are documented in the traditional texts." The marmot liver entry is a record of what the text documents.

### Active Components

Liver is a metabolic organ: protein, fat, glycogen, iron and vitamin stores (including vitamin A in many mammals). No figures are given and the text names no active compound. As a general fact, marmots are a recognised natural reservoir of plague (*Yersinia pestis*) in parts of Central Asia, and they can carry other zoonotic infections.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The Tibetan text says marmot liver "unites cracks [and fractures] in bones". The Codex does not recommend using it for fractures or anything else, and it provides no protocol. A fractured bone needs imaging, reduction where needed and fixation; no food can replace that. Marmot hunting and handling is dangerous (see Contraindications) and is regulated in places. The text is retained for the record only.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Tibetan: the line prints an action only ("unites cracks [and fractures] in bones"), with no taste, power or post-digestive taste for the liver. At line 673 Clark says that all meats in the sections listed at line 672 are sweet-tasting with a sweet post-digestive taste. Marmot is in section (h) of that list, and the statement is about "meats", so I mention it as the general rule for the flesh of this animal, and I do not treat it as a property printed for the liver. ### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan (FMT).** *(b) Framed.* The marmot liver sits in the animal-origin materia medica (line 1170) with a therapeutic action only. No cultivation status, no essence-extraction (*bcud len*) role and no rejuvenation role is printed for it in the lines read. The flesh of the marmot appears in the diet for wind disorders (lines 174 and 1411), which is again a therapeutic frame and not a cultivation one. Its status in the text is therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. A cultivator does not use marmot liver. The text records it against bone fracture, and the Codex keeps the record without carrying it into practice.

### Contraindications

Marmots can carry plague, tularaemia, brucellosis and other zoonoses; skinning, handling or eating wild marmot meat or offal has caused outbreaks of plague in Central Asia and the Himalayan region. Local health authorities advise against it. The animals are protected or regulated in some jurisdictions. Do not treat a suspected fracture with food or folk remedies; seek medical care. This entry gives no medical advice.
