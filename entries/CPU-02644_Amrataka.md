### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this Sanskrit name.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Bhishagratna's English translation (1907 to 1916), read in the chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and in the volume files `Bhishagratna_Vol{1,2,3}_djvu.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk for *Spondias* and for the Sanskrit name found nothing outside the Sushruta Samhita, apart from an unrelated rGyud-bzhi row for *Spondias axillaris* (`CPU-01797`), which is a different species under a Tibetan name and is not folded in here. The mango (*Amra*, `CPU-00386`, *Mangifera indica*) is a separate row, and the Samhita lists *Amra* and *Amrataka* as two fruits side by side, so the two are not merged.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The substance is attested in four places that were read.

1. *Fruit group (Sutra ch.46, `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 990; Vol. I line 20650).* In the *Phala-varga* ("Fruit Group", introduced as "Now we shall discourse (on the specific virtues) of fruits"), the list of fruits runs: "Dadima, Amalaka, Kapittha, Vadara, Kola, Karkandhu, Sauvira, Shimvitikaphala, Matulunga, Amra, Amrataka, Karamarda, Piyala, Lakucha, Bhavya, Paravata, Vetraphala, Prachinamalaka, Tintidika, Nipa, Koshamra, Amlika, Naranga, and Jamvira, etc." The general rule that follows is for the whole group: fruits "are acid in taste and heavy of digestion, heat-making in their potencies, (expectorant) of (Kapham), generate Pittam and subdue the Vayu" (same chapter, Vol. I line 20728 onward).
2. *Its own sentence (Sutra ch.46, `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 1077; Vol. I line 20737).* "The Amrataka (Hog-apple) is spermatopoietic, is surcharged with a kind of oily matter and tends to increase the Kapham in the system." The sentence follows the account of ripe mango (*Amra*), which the text calls pleasant, cosmetic, relishing and tonic, sweet and heavy, a tissue-builder, tending "to increase the formation of semen" and subduing Vayu and Pittam. The brackets "(Hog-apple)" are Bhishagratna's gloss.
3. *Pregnancy sign (Sharira ch.3, `SS_Sharira_ch03.txt` line 367; Vol. II line 7647).* In the signs of male and female conception the text says a pregnant woman who "dreams of having received lotus flowers (red and white), Utpala, Kumuda, Amrataka, or flowers of such masculine denomination in her sleep" may be expected to bear a male child. Here the name is a flower of masculine name, with no medicinal use.
4. *A treatment (Chikitsa ch.21, `SS_Chikitsa_ch21.txt` line 31; footnote at Vol. II line 20297).* In the chapter on sores of the penis, a healing application is "the oil, cooked with Triphala, Lodhra, Tindika and Amrataka". A footnote (line 37 to 39) reports that Shivadasa, in his commentary on the *Chakradatta*, quotes the couplet "but there he reads ... A'mra should be used in place of Amrataka". This is a commentarial variant noted by the translator and not part of the Samhita's verse.

**Appendix identification (Vol. III).** The glossary "APPENDIX" prints Sanskrit names in one block and their identifications in a second block on the next leaf. The name block lists "Amliksa", "Amra", "Amra'taka" (line 31247), "Amritak" in order; the identification block (line 31314 region) lists, in the same order, "Tamarindus Indica. Tamarind." "Mangifera Indica. Mango." "Sapondias Mangifera." "See Guduchi." The positions match, and the neighbouring pairs (*Amra* with the mango, *Amritaka* with "See Guduchi") confirm the alignment. The misprinted "Sapondias" is OCR or typesetting for *Spondias*.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As food: a fruit that is "spermatopoietic", oily, and increases Kapham (Sutra ch.46). In treatment: one ingredient of a medicated oil for sores of the penis (Chikitsa ch.21).
- **What the text does not say.** It gives no dose, no part-by-part account of tree, bark or leaf, and no cultivation or rasayana use. The mention among dream-flowers in Sharira ch.3 is an omen and not a drug use.

The Codex does not translate *Kapham* or "spermatopoietic" into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend the fruit or oil.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the passages read is the oil cooked with Triphala, Lodhra, Tindika and Amrataka for sores (Chikitsa ch.21, line 31). The text gives no quantity for Amrataka in that line. The commentary's substitution of *Amra* is recorded as a variant only. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Spondias* species are tropical fruit trees whose fruit is eaten and used in cooking, and the genus is a member of the Anacardiaceae (the family that also contains the mango and the cashew), some members of which can provoke contact allergy in sensitive people. The classic's "spermatopoietic" and "increases Kapham" are the tradition's own terms and have no modern equivalent; they are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Sutra ch.46).** The group rule gives the fruits as acid, heavy, heat-making in potency, expectorant of Kapham, generating Pittam and subduing Vayu. Amrataka's own sentence adds "spermatopoietic", oily, and "tends to increase the Kapham" (line 1077). The text does not print a separate rasa, virya and vipaka for Amrataka, and none is imported here. Whether the group rule applies to every fruit in the list without exception is not stated, and the same passage singles out *Dadima* as an exception. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The fruit stands in the *Phala-varga*, the fruit group of the dietetic chapter, and its own sentence calls it "spermatopoietic". That is a dietetic quality in a food chapter. It is not placed in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no text read names it as one of those formal disciplines, so no *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status is attested. The popular lists that sort foods into sattvic and rajasic categories belong to the Samkhya and later literature and not to Sushruta, and none is applied here.

**Practitioner's note.** A fruit of the fruit group, listed as food and as an ingredient in one oil, with no cultivation status. A cultivator has nothing to do with it beyond what any dietetic fruit offers, and the text's own sentence is a reason for care, since it says the fruit increases Kapham.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Per the Sushruta Samhita, Amrataka (the translator's Hog-apple, a fruit) acts on the reproductive system and on the humours. Its own sentence calls it spermatopoietic, oily, and one that tends to increase Kapham. In the fruit group, the classic's general rule applies: fruits are acid and heavy in digestion, heat-making in potency, expectorant of Kapham, generating Pittam and subduing Vayu. Modern terms can help orient the reader: the reproductive tract (semen formation) and the digestive tract (where the fruit is taken in and digested) are the systems the text touches. The only treatment mapped is an oil cooked with Triphala, Lodhra, Tindika and Amrataka for sores of the penis, so the genital skin is the tissue it names. The dream-flower mention in Sharira ch.3 is an omen and maps to no organ. Kapham, Pittam and Vayu are the tradition's own humoral terms, and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. No constituent, dose or pregnancy guidance is stated.

### Contraindications

- **General.** The text records that the fruit increases Kapham (line 1077). No other warning is printed for it.
- **Allergy.** Members of the cashew family can provoke allergy in sensitive people. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, illness.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
