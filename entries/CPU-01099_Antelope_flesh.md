# Antelope (kuranga) flesh — Kuranga (Bhishagratna: "antelope"), venison of the Janghala group
## *The meat of the animal Bhishagratna renders "Kuranga (antelope)"; no binomial is printed* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The English is Bhishagratna's 1907 translation of the *Sushruta Samhita*. The animal is named in the "meat group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 146, "Kuranga (antelope)", "Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1"). The chapter opens the group by saying that the flesh of animals "which are aquatic in their habits (Jaleshaya), or frequent marshy lands (Anupa), or dwell in villages (Gramya), or are carnivorous ..., or are possessed of unbifurcated hoofs (Ekashapha), or dwell on high ground (Jangala), is generally used as food. Of these each succeeding kind is superior to the one immediately preceding it in the order of enumeration" (lines 385-394), so the high-ground kind named last is ranked first. The high-ground kind is divided into eight sub-species, of which "the Janghala and the Viscira are the most important", and the Janghala list reads: "the Ena (black deer), the Harina (red deer), the Rishya (blue deer), the Kuranga (antelope), the Karala, the Kritamala, the Sharabha, the Shvadanstra, the Prishata, the Chitrila (Spotted deer), the Charushka, the Mriga-matrika, etc." (lines 403-427). The group statement follows at the top of the next page: "These species of venison have a sweet and astringent taste, are light, keen, pleasant (palatable), laxative, and diuretic in their effect. They subdue the Vayu and the Pittam." The English gives no statement for the Kuranga alone. It does describe the colour of the animals at line 443: deer "which are black are called Ena, while those which are red are called Harina. Those, that are neither red nor black, are designated as Kuranga."

The medicinal use that admitted the entry (`_nonplant/meats.csv`, row "antelope (kuranga) flesh", Janghala group, locus *Uttarasthana* ch.39 line 904) is the fever diet. Chapter 39 is "the (symptoms and) medical treatment of Fever (Jwara-Pratishedha)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt` lines 8-9). Under "Meat-Diet", Bhishagratna renders (lines 904-907): "The meat of Lava, Kapinjala, Ena, Prishata, S'arabha, Kala-puchchha, Kuranga, Mriga-matrika (different kinds of deer) or S'asa (hare) may be prescribed as diet for a fever-patient accustomed to the use of animal food." The text goes on to say that "several authorities" do not recommend the flesh of certain heavier birds in fever "owing to its heaviness (as regards digestion), as well as to its heat-making potency"; the author subscribes "with a certain limitation", and allows those meats "in moderate quantity and at proper time provided the fever is marked by a preponderance of the deranged bodily Vayu". The Kuranga is not among the meats so cautioned. The same chapter also lists, as the fever-patient's prohibitions, among other things, "sexual intercourse" and "any cold articles".

Two further passages put the Kuranga in lists of recommended foods. In ch.46 (line 2162), after the salt group, the text says that "the flesh of the Lava, Tittiri, Saranga, Kuranga, Ena, Kapinjala, Mayura (peacock), Varmi and Kurma (tortoise) should be regarded as the best of those of all other animals respectively belonging to their own species." In *Sutrasthana* ch.20, on salutary and non-salutary regimen (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch20.txt` lines 40-52), the Kuranga's flesh is among "articles or substances which may be safely included within the food stuffs of all human beings", beside red *Shali* rice, the *Shastika*, the flesh of the Ena, Harina, Mriga and others, and the pulses.

Which animal Bhishagratna meant by "antelope" for *Kuranga* is not stated beyond that word; the text itself defines it by colour (neither red nor black). [USER: verify]

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, the flesh of deer and antelope is lean muscle: mostly water and protein, with little fat, iron, zinc and B vitamins. Wild game can carry parasites and, in some regions, chronic wasting disease (deer) or brucellosis.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources record antelope venison as a food that is light, laxative and diuretic and that subdues *Vayu* and *Pittam* (ch.46), as a meat that may be given to a fever patient accustomed to animal food (ch.39), and as one of the foods safe for all (ch.20). It is food. Antelope are wild animals, often protected, and Kenetica gives no protocol and recommends no one hunt or buy one for this.

### Preparation & Dosing

The passages read give no preparation for the Kuranga alone beyond "the meat" or "the flesh", and the fever diet gives no amount for it. The "moderate quantity and at proper time" clause in the same passage (lines 910-917) applies to the heavier birds (Sarasa, Krouncha, peacock, cock, Tittira), not to the deer. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Lean game meat is a source of complete protein and iron. Undercooked wild game can transmit bacterial and parasitic infection. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** by the group statement of ch.46 (the Janghala list, which names the Kuranga): taste sweet and astringent; light, keen, pleasant; laxative and diuretic; subdues *Vayu* and *Pittam*. The English prints no separate *virya* or *vipaka* for the group or the Kuranga in the passage read, and none for the Kuranga alone. The statement belongs to the group, not specifically to this animal.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The Kuranga's flesh is attested in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 as one of the Janghala venisons (lines 403-427), among the flesh "best of those of all other animals respectively belonging to their own species" (line 2162), as a food safe for all human beings (ch.20, line 52), and as a fever diet (*Uttarasthana* ch.39, line 904). Those are food and regimen statements. It is not placed in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter in any passage read, and the English prints no claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The popular *sattvic*/*rajasic*/*tamasic* food lists belong to *Samkhya*-era and later material, not to Sushruta, and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes antelope flesh a cultivation substance. Sushruta ranks the high-ground venisons first among the meats, calls the Kuranga's flesh safe for all, and allows it to a fever patient used to meat. That is dietary and clinical advice, not practice. Antelope are wild and often protected, so a cultivator takes ordinary food from this entry, or nothing. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Contraindications

- **Conservation and law:** many antelope species are protected, and killing, possessing or trading in them is restricted or illegal in many places; the Sanskrit animal's modern identity is not settled. [USER: verify]
- **Hygiene and infection:** wild-killed meat carries parasitic and bacterial risk and must be cooked through; handling carcasses carries zoonotic risk.
- **Fever (Sushruta):** the fever diet is for a patient "accustomed to the use of animal food"; the text is cautious about heavy meats in fever.
- **Ethics and diet:** many people abstain from meat; the Codex recommends no animal food.
- **Pregnancy:** ordinary food hygiene applies; nothing is stated in the sources.
