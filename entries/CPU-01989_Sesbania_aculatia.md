# Sesbania aculatia — Jayanti (Sushruta, Bhishagratna's Appendix 3, as aligned by position)

## *A leguminous plant of the genus Sesbania (dhaincha / sesban type); the printed binomial "Sesbania aculatia" is a misprint of S. aculeata (Willd.) Pers., and the same Appendix line adds "S. Aegyptiaca"; the Sanskrit name Jayanti is assigned by position in a damaged two-column list, so the identification is ours: genus high confidence, species low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The only attestation is the Sushruta Samhita in Bhishagratna's English translation. A search of the other texts on disk for *Sesbania* found no row for this name (other Sesbania entries of the Codex are separate: *Sesbania grandiflora*, CPU-01408, and *Sesban*, CPU-02488, from Avicenna).

**Sushruta (SS).** The name occurs once, in "Appendix No. 3. Names of Drugs (with Latin synonyms)" at the end of Bhishagratna's Vol. III (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, line 31863). The appendix is printed as two columns, Sanskrit names first and the Latin synonyms after them, and the OCR has separated the columns. The line reads ".  Sesbania  aculatia  ;  S.  .Egyptiaca." The Sanskrit name is not on the same line. Counting down both lists for the J section (Sanskrit names from *Jambira* at line 31791 to *Jayanti* at line 31812; Latin synonyms from *Citrus medica* at line 31841 to this line) pairs the last Latin line with *Jayanti*. The pairing is supported by the lines above it: *Jiraka* falls against "Cuminum Cyminum" and *Jayapala* against "Croton tiglium" (printed "tigliura"). One Latin line between them, "Pentaptera tomentosa", falls against *Jivaka*, which is doubtful. The pairing is therefore ours, not the printed text's. [USER: verify against the printed page.]

Elsewhere in the translation the word occurs once in a recipe: Uttara Tantra ch. 35, the chapter on the disorder of children called Mukha-mandika, gives a decoction of "Kapittha, Vilva, Tarkari (Jayanti), Vamsa-lochana ... and Kuverakshi" for sprinkling the possessed child (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch35.txt` line 10). The translator's gloss equates Tarkari with Jayanti. No part, no action and no quality are given for Jayanti itself. Jayanti does not appear in the thirty-seven drug groups of Sutra ch. 38.

### Active Components

No source on disk names a constituent. In general terms, *Sesbania* species belong to the pea family (Fabaceae); they form root nodules with nitrogen-fixing bacteria, and some species are grown as green manure or for fibre. **Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Sushruta text on disk gives the name in a list and in one gloss in a recipe for sprinkling a child. It does not say what the plant does. The Codex gives no protocol, and does not infer one from the genus.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Sesbania* is a genus of legumes with soil-enriching roots; the names *S. aculeata* and *S. aegyptiaca* are, in current botanical usage, treated as synonyms of other species, so the printed names may point to one plant or two.
