# Electric ray — Narke (ΝΑΡΚΗ), "Sea torpedo" (Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 2-17)
## *The electric ray: the Osbaldeston English prints "SUGGESTED: Torpedinidae — Electric Ray" under the Greek heading NARKE, and calls it the "sea torpedo" in the chapter* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The text on disk is the 2000 English edition of Dioscorides' *De Materia Medica* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`). The chapter is 2-17, headed "NARKE" (line 4982; row 207 of `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, "2-17 (line 4982)"). The line under the heading, "SUGGESTED: Torpedinidae — Electric Ray" (line 4983), is the editors' identification, not Dioscorides' own words; the text itself says "sea torpedo". The chapter sits in Book Two, which the author's preface describes as dealing with "living creatures and of honey and of milk and of animal fat"; it is in the run of sea creatures, after the sea scolopendra (2-16, line 4979) and before the viper (2-18, line 4985).

The whole of 2-17 reads (line 4984):

"The sea torpedo applied to sores of long endurance around the head lessens the fierceness of the suffering. The same applied lifts up a perineum that has either overturned or else fallen down."

So the chapter gives two uses, both external and both "applied": to long-standing sores around the head, to lessen "the fierceness of the suffering"; and to a perineum "overturned or else fallen down", which the edition does not gloss; what condition is meant is not stated. [USER: verify] The chapter does not say what part of the animal is used, whether it is alive or dead, or how it is applied, and it states no mechanism. It does not mention the animal's electric shock, though the edition's identification makes the shock the animal's well-known character. Whether Dioscorides knew of it in this chapter cannot be said from the text.

The chapter gives no description of the animal, no place or season of capture, and no assay or sign of adulteration, which Dioscorides gives for many plant and mineral materials. The English uses the plain voice for both uses; it carries none of the hedging words ("said to", "they say") that he uses elsewhere for remedies he does not vouch for.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, an electric ray is a cartilaginous fish with paired electric organs on either side of the head, made of stacked modified muscle cells that can discharge a strong electric shock; its body is mostly water and protein, with a fatty, oil-rich liver. Nothing in the chapter ties any of this to the remedies given.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records the sea torpedo as an application for long-standing sores around the head and for a displaced perineum. The Codex does not recommend either. Long-standing sores on the head need examination (they may be infection, ulcer or skin cancer), a displaced perineum needs medical care, and a freshly caught electric ray can deliver a painful shock. The animal is also protected in parts of its range. The entry is a record of what the chapter says.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation is the one quoted: the sea torpedo "applied". The chapter gives no part, no method, no quantity and no length of course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Electric rays (order Torpediniformes) produce electric discharges from specialised organs, used in hunting and defence. This chapter says nothing of the shock, and no use of it is attributed to Dioscorides here. A displaced pelvic-floor structure or prolapse is today a matter for medical assessment; a fish laid on it is no treatment and carries an infection risk. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM):** None printed for this chapter. The English of 2-17 gives no quality (warming, cooling, drying, astringent) for the sea torpedo. Other chapters of the same book print qualities for other materials, for example "All fat is warming, softening and purifying" (line 5344), but that statement is about fat and is not carried to the ray.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is supplied here. The chapter is two short external remedies. It assigns the sea torpedo no cultivation status, and no passage read connects it to *pneuma* or to any practice. Unlike many of his plant and mineral chapters it offers not even a discipline of attention (no provenance, season, assay or adulteration), so the entry has nothing to say under that heading either.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The chapter is a pair of remedies for a chronic sore and a displaced part, and the Codex does not recommend them. The honest use of the entry is as a record. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Sores that do not heal:** need examination; do not apply animal tissue.
- **Perineal displacement or prolapse:** needs medical care.
- **Electric shock:** a live electric ray can discharge a painful shock; do not handle one.
- **Infection and hygiene:** fresh marine animal tissue carries bacteria; do not apply it to broken skin.
- **Conservation:** many ray species are threatened and some are protected by law; the Codex does not recommend taking any animal for these uses.
