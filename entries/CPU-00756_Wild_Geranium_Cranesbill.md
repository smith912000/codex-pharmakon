# Wild Geranium / Cranesbill — Geranion, Geranion eteron (Dioscorides 3-131), "Crane's Bill" (translator's gloss)

## *Geranium maculatum* (spine botanical; the translator's gloss for Dioscorides 3-131 lists *G. robertianum*, *G. sanguineum*, *G. pratense* and *G. fuscum*, and not *G. maculatum*) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity warning.** The spine botanical, *Geranium maculatum*, is a name that Dioscorides and the other texts on disk do not use. The only real locus is Dioscorides 3-131, and the translator's identifications there are other species. The English word "Cranesbill" carries the match. A finished entry already exists for the same Dioscorides chapter under *G. robertianum*, I_814 "Herb Robert" (CPU-00757). The two rows overlap. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 3-131, "GERANION, GERANION ETERON" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, text line 7700). The translator's gloss reads "Geranium tertium, Herba Roberti, Robertiana [Fuchs], Geranium robertianum [Linnaeus] — Herb Robert, Adder's Tongue, Fox Geranium Geranium sextum [Fuchs], Geranium sanguinem [Linnaeus], Geranium praetense, Geranium fuscum — Crane's Bill". The chapter says: "Geranium has a jagged leaf similar to anemone but longer; a root somewhat round, sweet when eaten. A teaspoonful of a decoction (taken as a drink in wine) dissolves swellings of the vulva. It has slender little downy stalks two feet long; leaves like mallow; and on the tops of the wings certain abnormal growths looking upward (like the heads of cranes with the beaks, or the teeth of dogs), but there is no use for it in medicine." Other names printed are pelonitis, trica, geranogeron, echinaster, iesce, alterum geranium, oxyphyllon, mertryx, myrrhis cardamomum, origanum, hierobryncas, pulmonia, cicotria, herba gruina and ienk. No other chapter with *Geranium* in the translator's lists was found (the only hits are the 3-131 lines and the index at line 15789). *G. maculatum* is not named by the translator, and the page index and gloss give the plant "Crane's Bill" generally.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** No locus was found. `_sources/avicenna.csv` has no *Geranium* or cranesbill row, and no Canon chapter text is on disk.

**Nahua — Badianus (BM).** No locus was found. `_sources/badianus.csv` has no *Geranium* or cranesbill row (208 rows searched), and no Badianus chapter text is on disk (`Sources/BM_Badianus/extract/README.md`). *Geranium maculatum* is a North American species, and the spine row gives no reason from the texts read to attach it to the Badianus. ### Active Components

Geranium roots are generally known to be rich in tannins, which is consistent with astringent use. This is a general statement for the genus, and the content of *G. maculatum* in particular was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides' use (a wine decoction for swellings of the vulva) is recorded as his, with his own remark that the "abnormal growths" have no medicinal use. The Codex does not recommend it and gives no protocol for any *Geranium*.

### Preparation & Dosing

Dioscorides gives "a teaspoonful of a decoction (taken as a drink in wine)" (3-131, line 7700). That is the only quantity in the sources read, and it belongs to the plant of that chapter. No other dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent* as to cultivation status. Dioscorides carries no subtle-body system. What he offers a cultivator is attention: he describes the leaf (like anemone, longer), the round, sweet root, the downy two-foot stalks and the crane-beak growths, and he says plainly that part of the plant has "no use in medicine". The Romans' and Africans' names are recorded, and recording is not endorsing.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The tag rests on a MASTER_INDEX row, not on a list row or a text read. No cultivation framing, and no attestation of any kind, was found.

**Nahua — Badianus (BM).** *(c) Absent.* No attestation was found. The Badianus manuscript is itself naturalistic and does not contain the *tonalli*, *teyolia* and *ihiyotl* model, which is reconstructed from later ethnohistory (López Austin), so even a real Badianus entry would not carry a cultivation framing on its own account.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing to cultivate here in the Codex's sense. The one Greek account is a short, descriptive and therapeutic one for a plant that is probably not the spine's species, and the Unani and Nahua tags are unsupported.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Do not treat the traditional use as advice, and in particular do not use any plant on the vulva or in pregnancy on the strength of this entry. Seek ordinary medical care for those conditions.
