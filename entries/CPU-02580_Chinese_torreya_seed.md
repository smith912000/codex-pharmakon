# Chinese torreya seed — 彼子 *bizi*, "Bi Zi" (the character is read as 柀; later 榧 *fei*); also 熊子 *xiongzi*

## *The seed of a tree "like the fir (杉) with seed like the areca nut", taken in modern works to be Torreya grandis Fort. ex Lindl., the Chinese torreya; the identification is ours, of medium confidence, and rests on a Song commentator's description and on the later standard reading, because the classic's own name is 彼子 and the character 榧 is not printed anywhere in the file; Tao Hongjing, in the fifth-to-sixth century, said the drug was no longer recognised* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

*Bi zi* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The spine had no row for it before this one (CPU-02580). The readable pages of Yang's English translation (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) did not carry the entry (a search for its name and for *Torreya* found nothing), so Sun's text is the only witness read.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 彼子 in the lower classic (卷三 下经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 4103, 味 paragraph; name line 4101). The paragraph reads: 味甘，温。主腹中邪气，去三虫、蛇螫、蛊毒、鬼注、伏尸。生山谷（旧在《唐本》退中）。In English: "Flavour sweet, warm. Governs evil qi in the abdomen; removes the 'three worms' (三虫), snake stings (蛇螫), *gu* poison (蛊毒), *guizhu* (鬼注, 'ghost infusion') and *fushi* (伏尸, 'hidden corpse'). It grows in mountains and valleys." The parenthesis, in Sun's hand, says that the entry was formerly placed in the *Tang ben*'s section of retired drugs (退中), that is, it had been dropped from the main body of the Tang edition and Sun restores it to the classic. The terms 三虫, 鬼注 and 伏尸 are the classic's own; they are not defined in the sources read here and are not rewritten into modern disease names. There is no alias in the paragraph, no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement.

*Wu Pu layer.* None.

**Mingyi bielu layer** (《名医》, later additions; line 4105). "Grows at Yongchang" (生永昌). Nothing more.

**Sun Xingyan's note** (案, line 4107). Tao Hongjing: "the formula-makers (方家) have never used this; ancients and moderns, physicians and drug dealers, no longer know the seed (子不复识). Another name is 熊子, and I do not know what form it has." Zhang Yuxi (掌禹锡, Song): "the tree is like the fir (杉); the seed is like the areca nut (槟榔)." The *Bencao jing*'s insect section has 彼子, and Zhi's note says "the character 彼 should take the wood radical (柀)". The *Erya*: "彼, another name [lost glyph]". Sun's paragraph thus gives the identification through the character: 彼 is a miswriting for 柀, a tree. The file does not print the later character 榧 for this entry; the identification of 柀 with the torreya is the later standard reading and is not made in Sun's note.

**Identity.** The Codex's name, *Torreya grandis*, rests on Zhang Yuxi's description ("tree like the fir, seed like the areca nut") and the later standard reading of 柀 as 榧. Tao Hongjing's statement that the drug was already unknown to the physicians of his time means the classic's own plant is not securely attested, and the Codex states that doubt. The Codex keeps the proposed name, and the later name 榧 *fei* is given in the title line as the Codex's gloss, not the classic's. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

No analytical source was retrieved for this plant, and no constituent is asserted. As a general fact, *Torreya* is a genus of conifers, and the seeds of *T. grandis* are oil-rich and are roasted and eaten as a nut in China. That is a statement about the tree and its seed and not a finding of the classic. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic records a sweet, warm seed against evil qi of the abdomen, the "three worms", snake stings, *gu* poison, *guizhu* and *fushi*. The Codex gives no protocol and does not turn these terms into modern diagnoses. A snake sting is a medical emergency; this entry is not a treatment for it. Poisoning, severe abdominal illness and suspected parasite infection need medical assessment.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic (下经), which the preface describes as "to treat disease, in correspondence with earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to remove cold-and-heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and cure illness" (line 2935). Its 味 paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 彼子 *bizi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 4103). Its status is therapeutic only. The Mingyi and 案 layers add none, and Tao Hongjing's remark that the formula-makers never used the drug is a note of disuse, not a status.

**Practitioner's note.** There is no cultivation practice here. This is a lower-class record of a seed against worms, stings and poisons, whose plant its own early commentator no longer knew, with no dose. Read it as history.

### Contraindications

- The classic prints no toxicity statement for this entry, but the preface calls lower-class drugs mostly toxic and not for long taking (line 2935). Treat the grade as the caution.
- **Identity.** Tao Hongjing could not identify the drug, and the later reading is a Song-period description. Do not gather seeds of a conifer or other tree for use on this entry.
- **Stings and poisoning.** Snake stings, *gu* poison and suspected poisoning are emergencies and need medical care.
