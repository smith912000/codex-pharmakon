### Botanical Identity
Low shrub 30–90cm; pinnate leaves; yellow flowers; flattened pods; native to arid northeastern Africa (Sudan, Upper Egypt, Somalia); also Indian senna (*S. alexandrina* cultivated in Tinnevelly, Tamil Nadu — "Tinnevelly Senna" = highest quality commercial grade). The leaf and the dried pod are both used — pods are slower-acting with gentler onset; leaves more rapid and potent. One of the oldest traded medicines in the world — Alexandria's role as the primary spice and drug trading port of antiquity made "Alexandrian senna" the trade designation that entered all pharmacopoeias.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā on *sanā*:
> *"Cold and dry in the second degree. Gently purges melancholic humour and bile from the body without harming the vital organs. The most suitable of the purgative herbs — purges without weakening. The pods are gentler than the leaves. Combined with ginger it reduces the griping. The best purgative for the elderly and the weak."*

The Islamic medicine evaluation of senna as the superior laxative — "purges without weakening" — reflects 1,000 years of clinical pharmacological comparison with other purgatives (colocynth, aloe, castor oil). Senna's dose-dependency and mechanism allowed more precise control.

### 2.2 Ebers Papyrus (EP)

Senna or senna-type plants (Upper Egyptian origin) in Egyptian medical texts for constipation and "clearing the bowel."

### 2.3 Assyrian Medical Tablets (AT)

Purgative plant preparations in Babylonian texts for constipation and "obstructed belly."

### 2.4 Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ)

*Fān xiè yè*:
> *"Bitter, sweet; cold. Enters Large Intestine, Stomach. Clears heat, promotes bowel movement, purges accumulated food stagnation. Quick-acting; used for constipation from heat accumulation, early-stage infectious dysentery."*

### 2.5 Sushruta Samhita (SS)

*Mārkandī* (*Cassia angustifolia*) — Indian senna used in Ayurvedic purgation (*virechana*) protocols as a *rechana* (purgative) herb.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Laxative — Two-Phase Mechanism (Primary)

**Sennosides A+B**: Classic two-phase mechanism that explains the 6–12 hour latency:

**Phase 1 (0–6h)**: Sennosides A+B reach the large intestine (unabsorbed by small intestine); colonic bacteria hydrolyse the glycoside bond → release sennidins (anthraquinone aglycones) → stimulate colonic sensory nerve endings → reflexly increase peristaltic motility.

**Phase 2 (6–12h)**: Rhein (reduced sennoside metabolite) inhibits Na+/K+ ATPase in colonic mucosa → blocks water/electrolyte absorption → increases luminal water content → softens stool.

This two-phase mechanism (motility stimulation + secretion) produces a predictable, dose-dependent 6–12 hour time-to-effect — making senna one of the most pharmacologically reliable and precisely dosed laxatives known. The Islamic "gentlest of the purgatives" assessment reflects this controllability.

### 5.2 Anti-Cancer (Emerging — Anthraquinone Metabolites)

**Aloe-emodin, rhein**: At concentrations above laxative threshold → induce apoptosis in hepatocellular, colorectal, and lung cancer cells via intrinsic apoptotic pathway (cytochrome c release, caspase-3 activation). This is a pharmacological property of the anthraquinone metabolites, not exploited in traditional laxative use, but scientifically documented.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Constipation**: Multiple Cochrane-quality reviews: senna is the most evidence-based stimulant laxative for constipation in adults. Equivalent or superior to bisacodyl (pharmaceutical stimulant laxative) in multiple head-to-head RCTs.

**Bowel preparation (colonoscopy)**: Senna-based preparations widely used for colonoscopy bowel prep; comparable to polyethylene glycol in multiple RCTs with better patient compliance (volume).

**Opioid-induced constipation**: Senna standard of care for opioid-induced constipation in palliative medicine — recommended in UK NHS and WHO palliative care guidelines.

**Hepatic encephalopathy**: Lactulose + senna combination standard in some guidelines — reduces nitrogen-producing colonic bacteria load.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The trade drug that named a city's pharmacology**: The name "Alexandrian senna" — *sanā makkī* in Arabic, *fān xiè yè* in Chinese — reflects that this plant reached global pharmacopoeias through a specific trade geography. Alexandria, founded by Alexander the Great in 331 BCE, became the world's primary pharmaceutical trade hub — Alexandrian senna entered every pharmacopoeia not because Alexandria grew it (it came from Upper Egypt/Sudan) but because it was traded through Alexandria. A plant can become historically important not only through pharmacology but through geography — where it was traded determined which traditions recorded it. The "Alexandrian" designation is the oldest surviving commercial pharmaceutical trademark.

**Virechana — the precision purge**: Ayurvedic *pañcakarma* (the five classical detoxification procedures) includes *virechana* (therapeutic purgation) as one of the five primary interventions. The precise control of senna (dose-dependent, time-predictable effect) made it the drug of choice for *virechana* — a protocol requiring exact timing (patient must purge within a specific window of a prescribed preparation sequence). The pharmacological reliability of senna's sennoside mechanism is exactly what controlled purgation therapy requires.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Standardised tablet (sennoside content) | 12–36mg sennosides at bedtime | Constipation — next morning effect |
| Dried leaf tea | 1–2g per cup (short steep) | Constipation; traditional |
| Pods (gentler) | 3–6 pods steeped in hot water | Elderly; slow transit; gentler action |
| Combined with psyllium | — | Enhanced bulk + motility combination |
| Virechana protocol (Ayurvedic) | Under practitioner supervision | Therapeutic purgation in *pañcakarma* |

**Anti-griping**: Combine with ginger (as Ibn Sīnā specified) or fennel seed — reduces colonic spasm and griping pain from sennoside motility stimulation.

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Bitter, sweet; cold. Channels: Large Intestine (大腸), Stomach (胃). Clears heat, promotes bowel movement, purges accumulation.

**Ayurvedic**: Bitter; cooling. Reduces Pitta ↓↓, reduces Kapha ↓. Primary *Virechaka* (purgative), *Anulomana* (normalises downward movement of Vata).

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Long-term use (>2 weeks)**: Electrolyte imbalance (hypokalaemia); laxative dependence; melanosis coli (brown discoloration of colonic mucosa — benign but indicates overuse); enteric neuropathy with very prolonged use
- **Pregnancy**: Stimulant laxative activity; may cause uterine contractions — avoid; use osmotic laxatives (lactulose, polyethylene glycol) instead
- **Inflammatory bowel disease (active flare)**: Contraindicated during active Crohn's/UC flare — mucosal irritation
- **Intestinal obstruction**: Absolute contraindication — risk of perforation
- **Appendicitis / acute abdomen**: Contraindicated
- Short-term constipation use: well-evidenced and safe
