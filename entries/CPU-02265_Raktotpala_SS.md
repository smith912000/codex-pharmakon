# Raktotpala — Raktotpala (Sushruta Samhita)

## *A water-lily named Raktotpala, the second name in the Utpaladi gana; the text prints no Latin name and no description. The name is Sanskrit for a "red" utpala (our gloss); Nymphaea rubra Roxb. ex Andrews, the red water-lily, is a candidate at low to medium confidence, from the name alone and not from any text read.* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry, and the text is Bhishagratna's English translation (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/`). The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the group list (Vol. I line 15717). A first-pass check suggested *Nymphaea rubra* "(verify)". The names file (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, line 290) says "group list (ch.38); no identification given here", and Bhishagratna's Latin appendix (Vol. III Appendix 3) prints the Sanskrit name "Raktotpala" at line 32655 with no Latin name paired to it in the extraction's pairs file (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/appendix3_pairs.json`). The Latin name is therefore ours.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The gana (Sutra ch. 38, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 375-378; Vol. I line 15717).* "The Utpala'di Group:— The drugs known as Utpala, Raktotpala, Kumuda Saugondhika, Kuvalaya, Pundarika and Madhuka constitute the group known as the Utpaladi Gana." The metrical text (lines 380-386): "This group is possessed of the therapeutic virtue of allaying thirst and corrects the deranged Pittam and the vitiated blood. It assuages the burning sensation of the body and proves curative in cases of vomiting, in Hridroga (Angina pectoris) in syncope, in haemoptysis and in cases of poisoning as well." The action is the group's, not Raktotpala's alone.

*The group as leaves (Sutra ch. 46, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1600-1603).* In the account of leaves: "The leaves of trees or plants belonging to the Kshira-Vriksha or to the Utpala group are cooling in their potency, astringent in their taste and action, and prove beneficial in dysentery (intestinal haemorrhage) and haemoptysis." This is the only passage that speaks of the Utpala group's members as a class beyond the gana line; it does not name Raktotpala.

*Where Raktotpala is not named.* The flower passage (Sutra ch. 46, lines 1795-1805) names Padma, Kumuda, Kuvalayam and Utpalam, and does not name Raktotpala. A search of the Sutra, Chikitsa, Kalpa, Uttara, Nidana and Sharira chapter files found Raktotpala only at the gana line. The word "Raktaluka" (line 1932 of ch. 46), a different item in a list of tubers and water plants, is not Raktotpala and is not treated here.

**Overlap.** The sister names have their own entries or rows: Utpala (`I_479_Utpala_Nymphaea_stellata.md`), Kumuda (`I_913_Kumuda.md`), Kuvalaya (`I_1710_Kuvalaya_SS.md`). Raktotpala is kept apart because the text names it apart. Whether it is a colour form of Utpala or another species is not stated. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent, and the identity is unresolved, so no composition is stated. In general terms, water-lily flowers contain flavonoids and pigments; the red colour of a flower is due to such pigments. No analysis was read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Utpaladi group: thirst, deranged Pitta, vitiated blood, burning of the body, vomiting, heart disease, fainting, haemoptysis and poisoning. As a class, the leaves of the group are cooling and astringent and are good for dysentery and haemoptysis.
- **What the text does not say.** The part of Raktotpala used, its colour, any single-drug recipe, or any dose.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this plant.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Water-lilies are aquatic plants of the genus *Nymphaea*, and several species are cultivated as ornamentals and eaten as food plants in South Asia. Red-flowered water-lilies are known in the genus. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Raktotpala itself. The group (Utpaladi) is said to relieve thirst and burning and to correct Pitta and the blood (Sutra ch. 38). The leaves of the group are "cooling in their potency, astringent in their taste and action" (Sutra ch. 46, line 1601).
- No dosha assignment beyond the group's is given, and none is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda (SS).** *(c) Absent.* Raktotpala is not named in a rasayana or vajikarana chapter in the passages read. It appears only as a member of a Pitta-and-blood drug group. No ojas, tejas or prana status is attested for it.

**Practitioner's note.** A red water-lily named once in a group of cooling drugs for thirst, burning and Pitta. The sources give it no cultivation role, and a cultivator has nothing to do with it beyond knowing it is there.
