# Ibex fat — no passage in Bryan's text; the nearest printed item is "Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat"
## *The fat of the ibex as the spine row names it; the word "ibex" does not occur in Bryan's English text on disk, and the fat Bryan prints in the hair pomade is "Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The spine row reads "Ibex fat, Capra nubiana" with the note "Ebers (baldness pomade); history.com" and a marker that the identification is uncertain (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 75). A separate list row, "Ibex", cites the Ghalioungui 1987 translation, Glossary I (line 126); that glossary was not opened. The website was not opened and is not used as authority.

**What Bryan's text says.** Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`) was searched for "ibex"; it has no match. "Ibex" is not in Bryan's list of identified animals either (Chapter VII, lines 1390-1404), and `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` has no row for it. The baldness pomade the spine row cites is in Chapter XXII "The Hair" (heading line 3993), lines 4023-4029: "ANOTHER FOR THE GROWTH OF THE HAIR ON A HEAD WHICH IS BECOMING BALD. Fat-of-the-Lion, Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus, Fat-of-the-Crocodile, Fat-of-the-Cat, Fat-of-the-Serpent, Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat. Make into one and rub the head of the Bald One therewith." The sixth fat is the "Egyptian-Goat". The same words occur in a burn remedy that Bryan describes at line 2273: "a mixture containing amongst other ingredients, Stone-from-the-Shore, Crocodile-incense, Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat, Onions, and Wax". "Goat" is in Bryan's animal list (line 1398), and he prints "Goat's Fat" and "Goat's dung" elsewhere (lines 2182, 2188, 2253), as separate items.

**Identity.** Whether the "Egyptian-Goat" of Bryan's English is an ibex is not said in the sources retrieved. The spine row's "Capra nubiana" and its ibex reading come from the website cited or from the list-maker, not from Bryan. That reading is not adopted here. [USER: verify]

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No passage for this substance under the name "ibex" was found, and no cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); no recitation is printed with the pomade, though Bryan summarises, and that is no proof the papyrus gives none. ### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** animal fat on the scalp or broken skin carries infection risk.
- **Conservation and law:** wild ibex are protected in many places and trade in their products is controlled; the status of any species cannot be given from the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** the pomade needs the killing of six animals; it should not be reproduced.
