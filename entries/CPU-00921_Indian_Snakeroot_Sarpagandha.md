**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

*Kalpa ch.5 (snake-bite).* `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch05.txt`, lines 505 to 510, names the Ekasara Agada (quoted in the file's OCR spelling): "Ekasara Agada: — The following drugs viz., Somardji seeds and Somardji flowers, Katdbhi, Sindhuvdra, Choraka, Varuna, Kushtha, Sarpa-gandhd, Saptald, Punarnavd, flowers of S'irisha, Aragvada and of Arka, S'ydmd, Amhashthd, Vidanga, Antra, As'mantaka, black earth and Kuravaka comprise the Ekasara Agada. These should be applied singly or in combination of two or three to destroy the effects of poison." The chapter's closing line says it "deals with the medical treatment of snake-bites". The name stands at line 507.

*Kalpa ch.6 (rat-poison).* `SS_Kalpa_ch06.txt`, lines 175 to 181, lists Sarpa-gandha among the drugs pasted as a Kalka for a ghrita: "with Trivrit, Tila, Am[rita] (Guduchi), Chakra, Sarpa-gandhd, (black) earth (of an ant-hill) and the barks of Kapittha and Dadima as Kalka. The whole should be duly cooked over a gentle fire. The Ghrita thus prepared would destroy the poison of the five kinds of rats viz., Aruna, etc."

*Appendix 3 (Bhishagratna Vol. III).* `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` prints the Sanskrit name "Sarpagandha" (OCR "SarpagandhsC") at line 32782. Its Latin column prints "Ophioxylon Serpentinum." at line 32859. The name column runs Sarala, Sarala', Sara-pumkha, Sariva, Sarjikshara, Sarja, Sarpagandha, Sarpakshi, Sarshapa, and the Latin column runs Pinus longifolia, "See Trivrit", Tephrosia purpurea, Hemidesmus indicus, Carbonate of Soda, Shorea robusta, Ophioxylon serpentinum, Ophiorrhiza mungos, Brassica campestris. The nine names and nine identifications pair one to one by position, with Sarpagandha against Ophioxylon serpentinum and the next name Sarpakshi against Ophiorrhiza mungos. ### Active Components

The roots of *Rauwolfia serpentina* are generally known to contain indole alkaloids, among them reserpine, ajmaline and related compounds. No composition is given in any source read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Sushruta places *Sarpa-gandha* in a snake-bite antidote group and in a rat-poison ghrita, and these are recorded as Sushruta's and not as a Codex protocol. The root is a potent pharmacological agent, the plant is a threatened one in the wild, and the Codex gives no protocol for it.

### Preparation & Dosing

Kalpa ch.6 (lines 175 to 181) gives a ghrita cooked with a Kalka that includes the plant. No quantity for the plant is given in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

Reserpine, isolated from *Rauwolfia* roots, was used in the twentieth century as an antihypertensive and as a sedative, and it can cause low blood pressure, slow heart rate and depression. This is a general statement about the compound. No clinical study was opened for this entry. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. In Sushruta it is an antidote ingredient, and in general knowledge a pharmacologically potent root. The abstentive answer is the correct one: do not use it, and treat the Unani tag as unsupported.

### Contraindications

Root preparations of *Rauwolfia* act on blood pressure, heart rate and mood, and reserpine is associated with depression. They are not self-administration substances, and none of the traditional uses above is advice. Avoid in pregnancy and with depression, and consult a physician about any interaction with blood-pressure or psychiatric medicines. *R. serpentina* is generally reported as threatened by over-collection, and wild collection should be avoided.
