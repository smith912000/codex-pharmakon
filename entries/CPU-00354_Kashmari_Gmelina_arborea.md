# Kashmari — Kāśmarī / Gambhārī / Gambhari / Sriparni / Bhadraparni (Sanskrit); Gamhar, Gumhar (Hindi); Beechwood, White Teak, Gmelina (English); Gumadi teku (Telugu)
## *Gmelina arborea* Roxb. ex Sm. | Family: Lamiaceae (formerly placed in Verbenaceae)
**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā — member of the Bṛhat Pañcamūla / greater Daśamūla), CS (Caraka), AH (Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya)
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Tridoṣaghna with a lean toward Vāta–Pitta pacification; heating (uṣṇa vīrya), sweet post-digestive resolution (madhura vipāka) — nourishing rather than depleting
**Training Phase:** Grounding / Restorative phase; Consolidation phase (rasāyana support); adjunct in the Recovery arc

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda (Suśruta, Caraka, Vāgbhaṭa):** Kāśmarī is one of the most structurally important trees in the Ayurvedic pharmacopoeia because of its membership in the **Daśamūla** — the "ten roots" that form the backbone of anti-vāta and anti-inflammatory formulation. Specifically, *Gmelina arborea* is one of the five great-tree roots (Bṛhat Pañcamūla) alongside Bilva (*Aegle marmelos*), Agnimantha (*Premna*), Śyonāka (*Oroxylum indicum*), and Pāṭalā (*Stereospermum*). Suśruta places Gambhārī among the sweet, cooling-yet-nourishing dravyas useful in fevers with burning, in emaciation (kārśya), and in disorders where Vāta and Pitta are jointly aggravated. The heartwood, root, fruit, leaf and flower are each assigned distinct actions in the classical texts: the root as stomachic and galactagogue, the fruit as an aphrodisiac and hair tonic, the flowers for skin conditions. According to PubMed, a review documents this whole-plant usage across the Indian Systems of Medicine, describing the plant as astringent, bitter, digestive, cardiotonic, diuretic, laxative and a pulmonary/nervine tonic (Warrier et al., 2020, *J Ethnopharmacol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2020.113593)).

**Folk & regional practice (South and South-East Asia):** Beyond the śāstric texts, Gambhārī is a workhorse of village medicine across India, Sri Lanka, Myanmar and Indonesia. The mucilaginous fruit pulp is eaten to relieve constipation and thirst; decoctions of the bark and root are given for fevers and to promote lactation in nursing mothers; leaf paste is applied to ulcers and to encourage wound closure. Its timber value (it is a fast-growing plantation tree, "white teak") means the medicinal parts are often a by-product of forestry, keeping it widely available.

**Unani:** Absorbed into the Unani materia medica of the subcontinent, where it is treated as a cooling, moistening (murattib) tonic to the heart and a corrective for hot temperamental imbalances — consistent with its Ayurvedic reading as a Pitta-soother.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Root That Anchors the Ten:** Within Kenetica, Kāśmarī carries the doctrinal weight of *rootedness*. It is not a spectacular single-action herb; it is a **structural** herb — one of the ten roots from which the great restorative decoction (Daśamūla kvātha) is built. Its symbolism is that of the load-bearing pillar rather than the ornament: the tree that grows fast and tall yet whose value to the physician lies underground. In the training vocabulary this maps onto the discipline of *foundation before flourish* — the practitioner who consolidates the base (nervous-system tone, digestive fire, tissue nourishment) before reaching for peak states. The sweet vipāka signals that Kāśmarī *builds back*: it belongs to the grounding and recovery movements, the exhale after exertion, the herb you return to rather than the one you spike on.

### Active Components

According to PubMed, sixty-nine phytochemicals have been isolated across the plant, including lignans (arboreol, isoarboreol, arborone, gmelanone, gummadiol), acylated iridoid glycosides, phenylpropanoid glycosides (verbascoside, martynoside), flavones and flavone glycosides (luteolin, apigenin), coumarins (umbelliferone), tyrosol, the alkaloid premnazole, and balanophonin (Warrier et al., 2020, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2020.113593)). Separately, a phytochemical study of the stem reported eight previously undescribed **prenylated coumarins** alongside known compounds, of which N-methylflindersine and artanin showed neurite-outgrowth activity (Kubo et al., 2023, *Phytochemistry*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2023.113721)).

- **Lignans** (arboreol, gmelinol, gummadiol) — heartwood; associated with antioxidant/antihepatotoxic reports
- **Iridoid & phenylpropanoid glycosides** (verbascoside, martynoside) — anti-inflammatory, antioxidant scaffolds
- **Flavonoids** (luteolin, apigenin, isoarboreol) — antioxidant, anti-nociceptive
- **Prenylated coumarins** (N-methylflindersine, artanin) — neurotrophic in PC12 cells
- **Premnazole** (alkaloid) — reported anti-inflammatory constituent

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Kāśmarī is deployed as a **grounding, tissue-nourishing base agent** rather than a stimulant. Its Kenetica role is threefold:

1. **Daśamūla anchor** — as the primary vehicle when a practitioner needs the classical anti-Vāta, anti-inflammatory restorative decoction. This is the "come-down" and recovery formula: post-exertion aches, nervous depletion, the fraying that follows sustained intensity.
2. **Pitta-fever soother** — the sweet, refrigerant fruit and flower for states of burning, thirst and restlessness where the system is over-heated.
3. **Neurotrophic-adjacent support** — the preliminary neurite-outgrowth findings (preclinical only) make it a candidate for the Consolidation phase, where the goal is nervous-system repair and re-wiring rather than acute activation. This claim is graded **preclinical** and must not be presented as an established human benefit.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the evidence base is **largely preclinical and traditional**. A comprehensive review reports antioxidant, anti-diabetic, anti-inflammatory, antiulcer, analgesic, anti-nociceptive, anticancer, wound-healing and antidiarrhoeal activities across in-vitro and animal studies, while explicitly noting that the pharmacology of many isolated compounds is "yet to be understood" (Warrier et al., 2020, *J Ethnopharmacol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2020.113593)). A separate phytochemical investigation found prenylated coumarins from the stem with neurite-outgrowth-promoting activity in NGF-mediated PC12 cells — a cell-culture model, not a clinical outcome (Kubo et al., 2023, *Phytochemistry*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2023.113721)).

**Evidence grade:** Traditional use is extensive and well-documented; controlled human clinical trials are essentially absent. Bioactivity claims are in-vitro/animal-level. Neurotrophic and anticancer signals are early preclinical and should not be represented as therapeutic in humans.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Tikta (bitter), Kaṣāya (astringent), Madhura (sweet) — with the fruit adding Amla (sour); the flower described as sweet-bitter-astringent-acrid
- **Guṇa (quality):** Guru (heavy), Snigdha (unctuous) — nourishing, grounding
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Madhura (sweet) — anabolic, tissue-building
- **Prabhāva (special effect):** Its signature is as a Daśamūla constituent — a synergistic anti-Vāta restorative whose action exceeds the sum of its listed tastes
- **Doṣa action:** Tridoṣaghna, with primary Vāta-Pitta śāmaka (pacifying); its heaviness and sweetness make it nourishing rather than reducing
- **Karma (actions):** Dīpana (digestive kindling), Grāhī/Stambhana (checking flux — antidiarrhoeal), Balya (strengthening), Stanyajanana (galactagogue), Vraṇaropaṇa (wound-healing), Jvaraghna (antipyretic), Rasāyana (rejuvenative, in support role)
- **Gaṇa membership (Suśruta):** Bṛhat Pañcamūla → Daśamūla

### Contraindications

- No serious human toxicity is documented, but the absence of clinical safety data means **pregnancy** (beyond traditional galactagogue folk use), **lactation** and **paediatric** use should follow qualified Ayurvedic supervision, not self-administration.
- Its heavy, unctuous, heating profile may be unsuitable for high-Kapha, high-āma (undigested-toxin) states without appropriate correctives.
- Sourcing/identity: because the tree is grown primarily for timber, ensure medicinal material is correctly identified and not treated with forestry chemicals.
- Not a substitute for conventional care in fever, infection, or ulcer disease.
