# Ushaka (alkaline earth) — Ushaka, the head of the Ushakadi group (as printed in Bhishagratna's Sushruta Samhita)
## *"Ushaka (alkaline earth)", the translator's gloss in the Ushakadi list of Sutrasthana ch.38; no other description of the substance is printed there, and its relation to the Ushara and Ushakshara of ch.42 and ch.46 is not stated* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The locus given for this entry is `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, "Sutra 38, Ushaka'di group (Vol. I line 15616)", which points to the Sutrasthana, chapter 38, in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, line 274 (the same text is in `Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, line 15616). Chapter 38 is "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (lines 3-6), and begins: "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (lines 8-9). The passage reads (lines 274-278): "The Ushaka'di Group:—The medicinal drugs and substances known as Ushaka (alkaline earth) Saindhava salt, Shilajatu, the two kinds of Kasisa, Hingu and Tutthaka enter into the composition of the group known as the Ushakadi Gana." The OCR of the names list prints "Ushaka (alkaline earth)" and "Saindhava salt" without a comma between them; they are two substances in the group, and the extract row that reads them as one name is a run-together of the two. The group is named after its first member, and the English adds no description of Ushaka beyond "alkaline earth".

The metrical text that follows (lines 280-283) states what the group does: "It destroys kapham (mucous), absorbs the fat of the body and proves curative in cases of stone or gravel in the bladder (urinary calculi), dysuria and abdominal glands (Gulma)." That is a property of the group, not of Ushaka alone. The chapter's closing instruction (lines 558-563) says the groups "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", and that "only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group, or a similar number of drugs chosen from the different groups, or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety, or in combination with another, should be used according to the indications of any particular case, as the physician, in his discretion, would determine." So the text does not say how much of each member to use, nor that Ushaka must be present.

The group is used by name in the treatment chapters. In the Chikitsasthana: the Kalka (paste) of the Ushakadi drugs with ghee and a decoction of other drugs "speedily brings about the disintegration of Asmari (urinary calculi, etc.)" (ch.7, lines 25-31); the Ushakadi group again, with the Varunadi group, Guggulu, Kushtha and others, in a medicated preparation "leads to the speedy disintegration and expulsion of the Asmari" (ch.7, lines 66-71); and the Ushakadi drugs "thrown in as an after-throw" to an alkali boiled with other alkalis, with Trikatu, which "proves curative in cases of stone, Gulma, and gravel" (ch.7, lines 111-116). A decoction of the Varunadi group "saturated with the powders (Kalka) of those of the Ushakadi group" is a potion for an unsuppurated internal abscess (*vidradhi*; ch.16, lines 150-158). In the Sharirasthana, a decoction of the Viratarvadi group "mixed with a powdered compound of the Ushakadi Gana" is given for *makkalla*, the postpartum complaint described, after a delivery, with lochia obstructed by local *vayu*, nodules, piercing pain about the navel and bladder and retention of urine (ch.10, lines 355-371). In the Uttaratantra, oils and gruels cooked with the Surasadi, Ushakadi, Mustadi and Varunadi groups are prescribed in *kaphaja mutra-krichchhra* (dysuria; ch.59, lines 144-147). In each case the group is named, not the member.

Three other spellings in the same translation are near the name. In Sutrasthana ch.46 (line 2082), among the salts, a salt "quarried from a salt mine situated at the foot of a hill (Shailamulaja), or from a sandy (Valukelam) or alkaline soil (Ushakshara)" is said to have a pungent taste and to help disintegrate *kapha*, and is "also called pungent (Katu) salt". In ch.42 (line 321) the saline group names "Ushara and Suvarchika" along with the salts. In ch.46 (line 2107) the alkali "Usharakshara" is heat-making in potency and detrimental to the strength of the body (see the entry on *Ushara alkali*). The sources retrieved do not say whether Ushaka, Ushara and the *ushara* of "alkaline soil" are one substance, two, or three spellings of one; the translator glosses only the first as "alkaline earth". ### Active Components

No text read names a constituent of Ushaka. In general terms, "alkaline earth" in the translator's phrase may refer to an earth or soil that effloresces alkaline salts, such as sodium carbonate, sodium sulphate or sodium chloride, but the sources do not say which, and the modern term "alkaline earth" in chemistry (calcium, magnesium and their relatives) is a different meaning. Alkaline salts in general are irritant to tissue in strong solution. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved say that Sushruta placed Ushaka first in a group of seven drugs and substances (with rock salt, *shilajatu*, two kinds of *kasisa*, asafoetida and *tutthaka*) used against bladder stone, gravel, dysuria, abdominal glands and excess *kapha*, and that the group was an ingredient in internal and external preparations. The substance cannot be identified from the sources, the toxicity of the group's other members is not discussed in the lines read, and no part of the sources supports a modern recommendation. ### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give the group in the forms above: as a powder or Kalka (paste) added to a decoction or ghee (Chikitsa ch.7, ch.16; Sharira ch.10), as an "after-throw" into an alkali (Chikitsa ch.7, lines 111-116), and as an addition to oils and gruels (Uttara ch.59). Ushaka is not given alone. No weight is given for the Ushakadi powder in the lines read, and the chapter's general rule is the physician's discretion. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Alkaline salts and some mineral sulphates are irritants to the gut, and some are toxic in quantity; the diuretic and laxative effects of salts depend on the salt. Kidney and bladder stones need diagnosis and often imaging, and no mixture of salts is a reliable way to dissolve a stone. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** for the group, the metrical text prints actions: it destroys *kapha*, absorbs fat, cures stone, gravel, dysuria and *gulma* (lines 280-283). No *rasa* (taste), *virya* (potency) or *vipaka* (post-digestive taste) is printed for Ushaka itself. The pungent taste and *kapha*-disintegrating action printed for the salt from "alkaline soil (Ushakshara)" in ch.46 (line 2082), and the heat-making potency of Usharakshara, are not transferred to Ushaka. No other tradition is tagged.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Ushaka is named in the classification of drugs by therapeutic group (chapter 38), a chapter whose frame is the humours (*kapha*, *pitta*, *vata*) and the choice of drugs for a case, not the nourishing of life. The group is used for stone, dysuria, abscess and a postpartum complaint; none of the passages read is a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) passage, and none mentions *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. No *shodhana* (purification) of Ushaka is described in the lines read. The *sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas* categories are Samkhya and are not in the passage.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. Ushaka is a substance in a therapeutic list for stone and urinary complaints, and the text does not describe it by itself. The honest use for a reader is to know that the text records the group, that its first member is only glossed "alkaline earth", and that the chapter leaves the choice and amount to the physician. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Bladder stone, dysuria, retention of urine:** these need clinical diagnosis; severe pain, blood in the urine or inability to pass urine is an emergency.
- **Unidentified mineral or alkaline earth:** do not swallow an unidentified salt or earth; alkaline and sulphate salts can irritate or poison.
- **Postpartum complaints:** the text uses the group after childbirth; any postpartum pain or retention of urine needs a clinician.
- **Internal abscess:** an abscess needs diagnosis and drainage or antibiotics; a powder in a potion is not a substitute.
- **Identification:** "Ushaka" is Bhishagratna's rendering with a one-phrase gloss; its relation to "Ushara" and to the "Ushakshara" soil is not stated.
