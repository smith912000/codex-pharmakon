# Nandi-Vriksha — Nandi-Vriksha (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907; printed "Xandi-Vriksha" in one OCR line)

## *A tree named in two groups of Sushruta chapter 38, the Amvashthadi and the Nyagrodhadi. The translator's glossary glosses the shorter name "Nandi" as "Ficus Cingalensis" and a bracket in chapter 34 of the Uttaratantra glosses it "Vata" (the banyan). Identification: a fig tree (Ficus), species not settled. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is identified only to the genus level, and that rests on a shorter name. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The first-pass guess was "unresolved (Nandi-vriksha)". That stands at species level; the readings below narrow it to a fig tree only if "Nandi" and "Nandi-vriksha" are the same name, which the text does not say.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

1. **Group list, Nyagrodhadi.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15685 to 15692; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 344 to 351). The group opens "Nyagrodha, Audumvara, Ashvattha, Plaksha, Madhuka, Kapitana..." and ends "...Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Bhallataka, Palasha, and Nandi-Vriksha, combinedly form the group known as the Nyagodhradi Gana." Its first four names are those of fig trees (this is the reader's observation; no gloss is printed in these lines).
2. **The Nyagrodhadi group's virtue.** The metrical text gives it for the group as a whole: "beneficial in cases of ulcer, cures all disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones and all sorts of secretions of the body in addition to its astringent properties (Sangrahi) and proves curative in a case of haemoptysis. It is an anti-fat and assuages the burning sensation of the body" (Vol. I lines 15693 to 15702). This is the group's virtue, not a statement about Nandi-Vriksha alone.
3. **Group list, Amvashthadi.** The same ch.38 (Vol. I lines 15667 to 15675; `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 326 to 334) lists "Amvashtha, Dhataki flowers, Samanga, Katvanga, Madhuka, Vilva-peshika, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Palasha, Nandi-Vriksha and Padma keshara". The OCR of the text file prints the name as "Xandi-Vriksha" here. The metrical text covers this group and the Priyangvadi group together: the two "prove beneficial in a case of deranged Pitta, favour the healing of ulcers, bring about the adhesion of fractured bones and prove curative in cases of dysentery where the stools are found to consist of lumps of thick and matured mucous (Pakvatisara)".
4. **The shorter name.** Bhishagratna's glossary in Vol. III (Appendix 3, line 32376, with the identification column printing "Ficus Cingalensis" against "Nandi"; the column alignment is read from the OCR) and the bracket "Nandi (Vata)" in *Uttaratantra* ch.34 (`text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch34.txt` line 11) agree that *Nandi* is a fig tree. *Vata* is the ordinary Sanskrit name of the banyan, but the translator's two glosses name different figs. Whether the compound *Nandi-Vriksha* ("Nandi tree") is the same tree is an inference, not a statement of the text. [USER: verify against the printed pages]

### Active Components

The tree is not identified to species, so no specific composition can be stated. In general terms, fig trees (*Ficus*) exude a milky latex and their barks are generally tannin-bearing; this is a statement about the genus, not about this drug. The sources read name no constituent. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unresolved identity.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Nyagrodhadi and Amvashthadi groups: ulcers, uterine and vaginal disorders, the knitting of fractured bones, haemoptysis, burning, deranged Pitta and mucous dysentery (group level).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no taste or potency for this drug alone, and no firm identification.

The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no plant for any of the conditions named.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given for this drug. The groups are classifications of drugs by shared virtue; the text read here (ch.38) does not say how any member is prepared. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any recipe from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Nandi-Vriksha in the lines found. The Nyagrodhadi group is described as astringent (*Sangrahi*) as a whole (Vol. I lines 15696 to 15698); that belongs to the group and is not imported for this drug.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in two chapter 38 group lists, which classify drugs by the use of the whole group (*Dravya-Sangrahaniya*). Chapter 38 is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns this drug no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only, and only at the level of its groups. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it.

**Practitioner's note.** A name in two group lists, with a fig tree the likeliest but unproven meaning. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without a firm identification nothing can be gathered or used.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The tree is not identified to species. Do not substitute any fig for it.
- **The conditions named.** Ulcers, uterine disorders, fractures, haemoptysis and dysentery are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Group membership.** The drug is one of many in each group, and the groups' virtues are not safety statements about any one member.
