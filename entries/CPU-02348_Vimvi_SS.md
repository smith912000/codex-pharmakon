# Vimvi — Vimvi (as printed in Bhishagratna's Sushruta Samhita)

## *Vimvi, a fruit-bearing plant of the Varuna'di group of Sutrasthana ch.38; the translator prints no gloss, and the candidate Coccinia grandis (L.) Voigt, the ivy gourd (Sanskrit bimba), is ours, from the name alone, and is of low confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Vimvi [SS]", taken from an extraction of Bhishagratna's translation; this entry replaces it. The locus given was "SS Sutra 38, Varuna'di group (Vol. I line 15388)".

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Vimvi is printed in the Sutrasthana, chapter 38 (locus: Varuna'di group, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 47; the same text is in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, line 15388). Chapter 38 is "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3-6), and says that "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (lines 8-10). The name is given by the translator without a gloss, a botanical name or a description.

In the Varuna'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 44-51 (the list), lines 52-55 (the metrical text)), Vimvi is one of the drugs listed. The group's metrical text reads: "The group is possessed of the efficacy of reducing the deranged Kapham and fat and proves efficacious in cases of cephalaegia, Gulma and internal abscesses." That is a property of the group as a whole, not of Vimvi alone.

**Sutrasthana ch.39 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt`).** Vimvi is in the list of emetic drugs: "Vimvi, Vacha, Mrigervaru and Chitra, etc. are possessed of emetic properties" (lines 13-14), and in the list of errhines (drugs for the nose): "Pippali, Vidanga, Apamarga, Shigru, Siddharthaka, Shirisha, Maricha, Karavira, Vimvi, Girikarnika, Kinihi, ..." (lines 48-51).

**Sutrasthana ch.46 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`).** The chapter, on foods and drinks, treats Vimvi as a fruit. Line 1126: "The fruit of such trees as the Ashvatha, the Plaksha, the Audumvura, etc. ... Jamva, Rajadana, Todana, Tinduka, Vakula, ... Pushkara, Varti, Villa and Vimvi, etc. are cooling, and astringent in their effect. They subdue the Pittam and Kapham, tend to produce a condition of parchedness in the system, and have a sweet and astringent taste." Lines 1185-1188: "The Vimvi fruit, as well as that known as the Ashva karna, is galactagoguic. They destroy the Pittam and Kapham and prove beneficial in cases of thirst, burning sensation of the skin, fever, haemoptysis, cough, asthma and consumption." Line 1585 prints a different name, "Vimvitika", in a list of leaves that are astringent, sweet and bitter and curative in haemoptysis; whether it is the same plant is not stated and it is not folded in here. The chapter closes (lines 554-566) by saying that the groups "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", and that plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, Ghritas or potions are to be prepared "according to the exigencies of each individual case". That is a general rule of the chapter and is not a recipe for this drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The text gives the fruit as cooling and astringent with a sweet and astringent taste, subduing Pitta and Kapham, and galactagogue (ch.46); lists the plant as an emetic and an errhine (ch.39); and places it in the Varuna'di group.
- What the text does not say: the botanical identity, which parts are emetic, or any dose. The Codex does not give a plant name on the strength of the sound of a Sanskrit word.

The Codex does not translate the text's terms (Vayu, Pitta, Kapham, Gulma, Shosha, Asmari and the rest) into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend a plant it cannot identify.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (ch.46, line 1126).** Cooling; astringent in effect; sweet and astringent in taste; subduing Pitta and Kapham (as part of a list of fruits). Line 1185: galactagogue; helps thirst, burning of the skin, fever, haemoptysis, cough, asthma, consumption.
- No vipaka or virya terms are printed for it in the lines read.
- No other tradition names the drug, and no classification is imported from any.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Vimvi stands in the dravya-group classification of Sutrasthana ch.38, a classification by therapeutic action. The Varuna'di group is a therapeutic group, not a rasayana or vajikarana group, and the food chapter (ch.46) does not assign the fruit a cultivation status. A search of the chapter files on disk for the spellings printed here found no mention of Vimvi in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters, and no text read calls it an elixir or a drug of vitality. The register is therefore (b): framed by its therapeutic group, with no cultivation status attested. The Codex does not assign *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* effects to it, because no source read does.

**Practitioner's note.** Vimvi is a name in a list. The tradition's text gives the drug a place among therapeutic groups and little more, and nothing in the record supports a cultivation use. A cultivator can learn from it only how Sushruta organises drugs into groups by action, and that some names in that classification are no longer certainly identified. Nobody should try to find a plant to match the name.
