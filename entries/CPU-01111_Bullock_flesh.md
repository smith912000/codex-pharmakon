# Bullock flesh — Beef (Bhishagratna), the flesh of "cows, bullocks" in the Gramya (domestic animal) group
## *The meat of cattle, which Bhishagratna renders "Beef" and lists as "cows, bullocks"; no Sanskrit word and no binomial is printed in the lines read* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The English is Bhishagratna's translation of the *Sushruta Samhita*. The cattle are named in the meat group of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 4-6). The chapter ranks the kinds of flesh by habitat, "each succeeding kind" superior to the one before (lines 385-394), and the domestic (*Gramya*) kind, named third, ranks below the high-ground kind named last. The domestic group is introduced (lines 614-617): "Animals such as horses, mules, cows, bullocks, asses, camels, goats, sheep, and Medapuchhas (fat tailed or Turkish sheep) etc., belong to the group of domestic animals (Gramyas)."

The group statement (lines 618-633) reads: "The flesh of domestic animals is possessed of constructive, tonic and appetising properties, is sweet in taste and digestion. It destroys the deranged Vayu and produces the Kapham and Pittam." The chapter then gives a line for the goat and sheep and then the line that admitted this entry (`_nonplant/meats.csv`, row "bullock flesh", locus line 643 with the note that the text lists cows and bullocks at line 616 and gives one beef statement; lines 643-646): "Beef is holy and refrigerant, proves curative in dyspnoea, catarrh, cough, chronic fever and in cases of a morbid craving for food (Atyagni), and destroys the deranged Vayu." The word "holy" is the translator's English; the Sanskrit word is not printed in the passage, and what it renders is not stated. [USER: verify] The statement says "beef" without separating bullock from cow. The entry's name follows the text's "bullocks" at line 616, and the line is taken to cover the flesh of the cattle listed there.

A general statement for animals living near villages follows (lines 653-657): "the flesh of an animal living in a village or near a reservoir of water, excessively increases that secretion", where "that secretion" is the secretion of the internal organs; the text says the flesh of a high-ground animal "is said to increase" it (lines 650-651). The statement is about habitat, not about cattle by name.

Other places where beef is named, which I opened:

- *Eye disease.* In ophthalmia from vitiated blood, the thin watery matter in the eye in a case of *Ajaksi* is drained by puncture, "after which the incidental ulcer should be filled with powdered beef mixed with clarified butter" (*Uttarasthana* ch.12, line 232).
- *Collyrium.* Collyriums for the *Pitta-vidagdha* and *Sleshma-vidagdha* kinds of vision are prepared "with Gairika, Saindhava, Pippali, and the charred remains (Masi) of cow's teeth, or with beef" (*Uttarasthana* ch.17, line 35).
- *Insanity.* "Fumigation with burnt putrid beef or dog's flesh should be constantly employed" (*Uttarasthana* ch.62, line 99).
- *Pregnancy.* A mother's longing for beef during gestation "indicates the birth of a strong and vigorous child capable of sustaining any amount of fatigue and physical pain" (*Sharirasthana* ch.3, "pregnancy", line 232). This is a sign read in the mother's wish, not a use of the meat.

The other cattle products in the Codex are in `III_17_Cattle.md` (Ebers and Tibetan beef, hide, bile, urine and the rest) and `III_40_Ox_suet.md`.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, beef is skeletal muscle: complete protein, iron (largely as haem), zinc, vitamin B12 and other B vitamins, with a variable fat content.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text records beef as a food: constructive and tonic as one of the domestic meats, "refrigerant", and curative in breathlessness, catarrh, cough, chronic fever and a morbid craving for food. It also records powdered beef as an eye dressing and burnt putrid beef as a fumigant in insanity. Beef is an ordinary food in many cuisines and needs no protocol from this Codex; the Codex does not recommend the eye or fumigation uses, which are unsafe by modern standards.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the text gives are the ones above: the flesh as food; powdered beef mixed with clarified butter to fill an eye ulcer after puncture (Uttara ch.12); beef as an ingredient of a collyrium (Uttara ch.17); burnt putrid beef as a fumigation (Uttara ch.62). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Beef is a source of complete protein, iron and vitamin B12. Undercooked beef can transmit bacteria such as *Salmonella* and *E. coli*, and parasites. Powdered or putrid meat placed in an eye or inhaled as smoke is a route to infection or irritation. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** ch.46 lines 618-633 (domestic group): constructive, tonic, appetising; sweet in taste and in digestion; destroys deranged *Vayu*; produces *Kapham* and *Pittam*. Line 643: beef "holy and refrigerant", curative in dyspnoea, catarrh, cough, chronic fever and *Atyagni*, and destroying deranged *Vayu*. The English prints no separate *virya* or *vipaka* for beef. The group statement belongs to the group; the beef line is specific.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Beef is attested in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 as one of the domestic meats and by its own line, and in the eye, collyrium, fumigation and pregnancy passages above. Those are food and drug statements. It is not placed in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter in any passage read, and the English prints no claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The popular *sattvic*/*rajasic*/*tamasic* food lists belong to *Samkhya*-era and later material, not to Sushruta, and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes beef a cultivation substance. Sushruta classes it as a tonic, cooling, wind-subduing domestic meat and gives it a short list of uses. That is dietary advice, not practice. A cultivator who eats beef eats it as food; one whose community holds cattle sacred takes nothing from it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** raw, undercooked or putrid meat carries bacterial and parasitic risk; powdered meat in an eye, or fumigation with putrid meat, is unsafe.
- **Prion disease:** bovine brain and spinal tissue can carry the agent of bovine spongiform encephalopathy; the Codex does not recommend raw or unregulated bovine tissue.
- **Allergy:** some people react to red meat (alpha-gal sensitisation after tick bites).
- **Diet:** people with cardiovascular disease or on a lipid-lowering regimen should treat beef as any dietary fat and protein source.
- **Ethics and law:** cattle are sacred or protected in some communities and jurisdictions; slaughter and the trade in cattle products are restricted there.
- **Pregnancy:** ordinary food hygiene applies.
