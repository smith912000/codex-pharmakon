# Serpent / snake fat — "Fat-of-the-Serpent" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus)
## *The fat of an animal Bryan calls "Serpent"; no Egyptian word, species or binomial is given in the sources retrieved, so the spine row's "unspecified snake sp." is only the plain English* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter VII "Organic Remedies (animals)". "Serpent" stands in Bryan's list of the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1393, between "Porcupine" and "Tortoise"; lines 1385-1388 give the list's introduction). The name-index file `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` was searched and has no row for the fat. Bryan's list of animal parts (lines 1405-1415) includes "Fat" and "Tallow" but ties neither to any animal. The spine row reads "Serpent / snake fat, unspecified snake sp." with the note "Ebers (baldness pomade); history.com" and a later marker that the identification is unresolved (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 73). The website was not opened and is not used as authority.

**The one prescription for the fat.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "serpent", "snake", "cobra" and "viper". The fat occurs once, at line 4027, in Chapter XXII "The Hair" (heading line 3993): "ANOTHER FOR THE GROWTH OF THE HAIR ON A HEAD WHICH IS BECOMING BALD. Fat-of-the-Lion, Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus, Fat-of-the-Crocodile, Fat-of-the-Cat, Fat-of-the-Serpent, Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat. Make into one and rub the head of the Bald One therewith." (lines 4023-4029). The serpent fat is the fifth of six fats in one compound, and the six are not set apart from each other in what Bryan prints. The same compound is the one given in the entries for lion, hippopotamus, crocodile and cat fat.

**Other places the serpent appears, none of them a drug.** Bryan's survey of the contents of the papyrus lists, among the "handy hints", one "to prevent the serpent creeping out of his hole" (line 1039). In Chapter XXIV "Domestic Hints" he describes three devices against a serpent creeping out of its hole: dried ant-fish set at the entrance, natron put into the hole, and a garlic-ball inserted in it, each followed by the Scribe's assurance that it will not creep out (lines 4225-4232). In a description of a tumour he says that it "winds itself like snakes" (line 3950); that is a simile for the swelling and not a remedy. None of these uses a part of the serpent.

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say what kind of snake, how its fat was obtained or rendered, in what quantity it entered the compound, or why it was included. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. No Egyptian word for the fat is printed in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it, because the animal is not identified. In general terms, animal fat is mainly triglycerides, and rendered fat is a solid or semi-solid ointment base. Nothing in the sources retrieved says that snake fat contains any component that acts on hair. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show serpent fat as one of six fats in a single hair pomade, with no quantity and no preparation beyond making them "into one". The snake is unidentified; many snakes are protected, and snake material can carry infection. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

Only what Bryan reports: the six fats are made "into one" and rubbed on the head (line 4029). No quantity is printed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution, and a hair pomade is an external remedy for an external complaint. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*). The one spoken text in the hair chapter, "TO CHARM AWAY ALOPECIA O Shining One, Thou who hoverest above! [...] To be spoken over Iron Red-lead Onions Alabaster Honey" (lines 4069-4072), is attached to a different mixture and not to the fats. Bryan prints no recitation with the six-fat pomade, though he summarises, and that is no proof the papyrus gives none. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The record is one fat among six in a hair pomade, from an unidentified snake, with no quantity and no cultivation role. A cultivator has nothing to do with it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** animal fat on the scalp or on broken skin carries infection risk; snakes can carry bacteria.
- **Unidentified animal:** the species is unknown, and some snakes are venomous; an unidentified snake should not be handled for this purpose.
- **Conservation and law:** many snake species are protected and trade in them is controlled in many countries; the status of any particular species cannot be given from the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** the pomade needs the killing of six animals; it should not be reproduced.
