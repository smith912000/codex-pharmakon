# Qalqaṭār — colcothar (قلقطار)

## *Qalqatar, the Arabic form of the Greek name for the reddish vitriol-derived substance colcothar; the Canon quotes Galen that "chalcitis turns into qalqatar", which fits the modern reading as an iron-sulfate (vitriol) product but does not itself give a chemistry; confidence medium; not the same heading as qalqand* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** This is a caustic mineral. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should apply or swallow it.

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. Book II, entry *qalqaṭār*, Arabic text from line 17554 of `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk was not run beyond the spine check. 

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** What it is: "Galen said: chalcitis (*qalqadīs*) may turn into *qalqaṭār*" (قال جالينوس إن قلقديس قد يستحيل قلقطارا). Temperament: "hot and dry in the third". Actions and properties: "in it is strong burning (*iḥrāq*), and astriction of flows of blood, and drying; the burnt kind is more drying and less stinging; and with the much astringency in it there is much heat." Swellings and pustules: "benefits erysipelas (*al-namla*, *al-ḥumra*) when smeared with coriander water; it is sprinkled on the malignant and creeping ulcer (*al-khabītha*, *al-sāʿiya*); it burns away excess flesh and makes an eschar." Head: benefits nosebleed and swellings of the gums and of the parotid region. Eye: "enters into eye salves for clearing and for thinning the humour of the lids". Excretory organs: "cuts bleeding of blood from the womb." Poisons (line before the heading): "it repels the harm of mushrooms" belongs to the entry just before and is not this one.

**Overlap.** The spine already has *Chalcitis* (CPU-00802) and *Green vitriol* (CPU-00737), both tagged Avicenna, and *Black alunite* (CPU-01369). This entry is the Canon's own *qalqaṭār* heading and is kept separate. Whether it is distinct from a *qalqand* heading was not checked. [USER: verify]

**The next entry.** Lines from *qanābarī* onward (a plant, with 'vitiligo' and 'liver and spleen' uses) belong to a different heading that the file does not mark; they are not used here.

### Active Components

Not given in the text. In general, colcothar is the red residue of strongly heating iron sulfate (vitriol) and is chiefly iron oxide with some residual sulfate; this is a general chemical fact, not from the text. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Corrosive.** The text records a caustic, drying mineral for skin ulcers, gum swelling, nosebleed, eye salves and womb bleeding. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

Smeared with coriander water on erysipelas; sprinkled on ulcers; used in eye salves (text above). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General fact only: iron and copper vitriol products are caustic and astringent. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Avicenna: hot and dry in the third degree; burning, astringent, drying; the burnt kind drier and less stinging.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No cultivation framing is attested. **(c) Absent.** The *Canon* entry is organised by organ and complaint and says nothing here about innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruh*. 

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to practise. It is a caustic mineral of the surgeon and eye-salve maker, and burns tissue.

### Contraindications

- Caustic: burns tissue; do not apply to eyes, wounds or gums.
- Vitriol products may be toxic if swallowed.
- Identity: medium confidence; the Canon gives no chemistry.
