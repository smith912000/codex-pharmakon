# chu.gang — chu.gang / "Bamboo concretion" (Clark 1995, Explanatory Tantra ch.20); Chugang (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011)

## *Bamboo concretion, the hard whitish or grey siliceous deposit found inside the joints of some bamboos (the material sold as tabasheer); Clark's text also speaks of a "mineral type" that is white like salsoda. Identification: from Clark's English gloss, which is plain; medium confidence for the substance, low for the botanical source of the bamboo. The row is filed in Volume II because the text allows a mineral type; Clark places the entry among the resins* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the rGyud-bzhi (Four Tantras) in Clark's 1995 translation, chapter 20, and again, as "Chugang", in the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder with the locus "Clark 1995 (line 881)"; this entry is written from that line and from the further lines below. A search of the other texts on disk found nothing for the name or for the bamboo concretion.

**Rig.pa tradition, the Four Tantras (FMT).** Clark's chapter 20 sets out the materia medica in sections. This entry sits in section (5) "Resins" (`Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 879), and the entry reads at line 881:

> "chu.gang: Bamboo Concretion: [has four names. The real bamboo concretion is found inside the bamboo, and is yellowish-grey with indeterminate taste. The mineral type is white, like salsoda, and is of two types according to quality. Good quality concretion is white and tasteless. It] cures all [types of] lung diseases and removes wound fever."

In Clark's convention (introduction, line 82) the words in square brackets are taken from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries, and the unbracketed words are the root text. So the root text itself says only that it cures lung diseases and removes wound fever; the four names, the two kinds (the "real" one from inside bamboo, and a "mineral type") and the descriptions come from the commentaries. The existence of a mineral type is what places the row in Volume II. No further characterisation of the mineral type is given in the lines read. [USER: verify]

The substance recurs in other passages of the chapter, as follows.

- **Taste.** The Explanatory Tantra's taste lists put bamboo concretion in the sweet group: "The group of medicines having sweet taste includes liquorice, raisins, saffron, bamboo concretion, cassia pods, ..." (Clark line 765; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3204, where the sweet group opens with "Shingngar, Gundrum, Gurkum, Chugang").
- **Power.** For bile medicines, the "blunt" power is exemplified by "bamboo concretion" (Clark line 181), and bile disorders are pacified by powders of "camphor, white sandalwood, saffron or bamboo concretion as principal ingredients" (line 187).
- **Fever.** The "standard cures for fevers" include "Camphor, White Sandalwood, Bile of elephant and so forth, Bamboo concretion, Saffron and Blue Lily" (line 1208; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3591).
- **Lungs.** The "medicines for lung disorders" begin with "Bamboo concretion, Liquorice, Raisin, Hippophae rhamnoides, Costus root ..." (line 1213; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3611). As a supplementary ingredient in compounds, "bamboo concretion for the lungs" (Clark line 1383; Men-Tsee-Khang line 4276, "Chugang for the lungs").
- **Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 as second witness.** "Chugang cures all the disorders of the lungs and hot disorder of the wounds" (line 3382), which agrees with Clark's root text.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the concretion that forms in bamboo joints (tabasheer) is largely silica, a hard, porous, glassy deposit, and is not a resin in the chemical sense. The text's own placement among the "Resins" is an old classification and not a statement of chemistry. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Lung diseases, wound fever, hot disorders in general, and bile disorders (as a powder, with camphor, sandalwood and saffron).
- **What the text does not say.** No amount, no preparation in the lines read, and no single-drug use beyond the listed one.

The Codex does not translate "lung diseases" or "wound fever" into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend taking a bamboo concretion as a medicine.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text names powder as the form (line 187: "powders ... of camphor, white sandalwood, saffron or bamboo concretion as principal ingredients" for bile disorders). No quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Tabasheer is a silica-rich deposit; it is not known to be a lung medicine in modern practice, and the Tibetan indications are not translated into modern ones here. Silica dust is a hazard to the lungs when inhaled in quantity, which is a reason not to treat any fine mineral powder as harmless. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT.** Taste: sweet (Clark line 765; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3204), though the commentary at line 881 calls the real concretion "indeterminate" in taste and the good mineral type "tasteless". Power: "blunt", as the example given for bile medicines (line 181; camphor is the example of the "cool" power in the same sentence). It is used as a standard fever medicine (line 1208). The two statements on taste are not reconciled in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan (FMT).** *(b) Framed.* The substance is attested in the Explanatory Tantra's materia medica and in its lists of fever, lung and bile remedies, but no cultivation status is recorded for it. Medicine in this tradition is the third of four treatment branches: it supports practice and does not reach the root, which is the three poisons of attachment, aversion and delusion. It is not described as an ingredient of *bcud len* (essence-extraction) compounds in the lines read. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in the sources retrieved, and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** A cooling, sweet-tasting siliceous deposit named for lung disease, wound fever and bile disorders. Nothing here supports cultivation use. A cultivator reads it as a plain therapeutic substance of the resin section.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** Material sold as bamboo concretion may be a different siliceous or mineral preparation. Do not use on the strength of this entry.
- **Fine powders.** Do not inhale mineral powders.
- **Lung disease and wound fever.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
