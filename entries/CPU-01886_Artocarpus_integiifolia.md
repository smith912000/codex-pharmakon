# Artocarpus integiifolia — Panasa (Sushruta Samhita; "Artocarpus Integiifolia" as scanned in Bhishagratna's Appendix, "Artocarpus Integrifolia" in his introduction)

## *Artocarpus heterophyllus Lam., the jackfruit tree, whose large fruit is the panasa of Sanskrit; "Artocarpus integiifolia" is a scan error for the old name Artocarpus integrifolia, which Bhishagratna prints in his introduction and Appendix and which is a synonym of A. heterophyllus; the identification is Bhishagratna's, and is of medium confidence at species level because the Sushruta text itself names only the fruit and gives no description of the tree* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, under the Sanskrit name Panasa.** This is a single-text entry. The row was added on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the Appendix to Bhishagratna's Volume III, with the scan's misspelling of the binomial; this entry replaces it. The attestation was confirmed on 30 October by opening the Appendix and reading every chapter where the name occurs.

**Sushruta Samhita, Appendix (SS).** In the Appendix to Volume III (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`) the names column of the "P" page reads, among others, "Palindi" (line 32479), "Panasa" (line 32480), "Patribhadra", "Patrijata", "Parkati", "Parna" and "Parpata", and the identifications column reads "See Trivrit.", "Artocarpus Integiifolia.", "Erythrina Indica.", "Ficus Infectoria." (line 32551 is the second of these). The first two names and the first two identifications line up (Palindi is "See Trivrit", a cross-reference, and Panasa is "Artocarpus Integiifolia"), and the editor's introduction independently prints "Panasa (Artocarpus Integrifolia)". The pairing is read from a damaged scan. [USER: verify against the printed page.] A later line on the same page reads "See Panasa." (line 32570) against another name. That is a cross-reference, not a second identification.

**Sushruta Samhita, main text (SS).** The tree's fruit is named in the chapters read as follows.

- *Sutrasthana* ch.46, on food and drink (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 1191-1213), in the fruit passages: "The fruits such as, the Tala, Narikela, Panasa and Maucha, etc. are sweet in taste and of digestion, and subdue the Vayu and Pittam. They are cooling in their potencies, and act as constructive tonics. Of these the fruit of a Tala is sweet in taste and heavy of digestion... The Panasam is sweet and astringent in taste, demulcent and heavy of digestion." This is the one passage in the text that states the properties of Panasa itself.
- *Chikitsasthana* ch.31 (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch31.txt`, line 65): "The expressed oils of the fruits of Tala, Narikela, Panasa, Mocha, Piyala, Vilva, Madhuka, Shleshmataka and of Amrataka are used in diseases due to the deranged Vayu acting in concert with the deranged Pitta."
- *Nidanasthana* ch.2, on haemorrhoids (`nidana/SS_Nidana_ch02.txt`, line 118): the piles of the Kapha type "resemble the teats of a cow or the stones of the Karira, or of a Panasa fruit". Here the fruit is used as a simile for shape and hardness.
- *Kalpasthana* ch.4 (`kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch04.txt`, lines 207-208): "Panasa" and "Maha-panasa" appear in the list of Mandali snakes. This is the same word used for snakes and is not the tree.
- Bhishagratna's introduction, Vol. I (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, lines 1141-1142; also `sutra/SS_Vol1_frontmatter.txt`), summarising the surgeon's training: "extraction of solid bodies on Panasa (Artocarpus Integrifolia) and such like fruit". The line is the editor's summary, and the grep did not find the name Panasa in the text of Sutra ch.9 itself. [USER: verify]

A search of the other texts on disk (Dioscorides, Shennong and the others) found nothing for this plant.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The fruit is a food in the sweet, cooling fruit group that subdues Vayu and Pittam (Sutra ch.46); its expressed oil is listed for Vayu with Pitta (Chikitsa ch.31).
- **What the text does not say.** It gives no dose, no preparation of the fruit as a drug, and no description of the tree.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend a use of this plant on the strength of the text.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparation named is the expressed oil of the fruit in the list at Chikitsa ch.31, line 65; the fruit itself is eaten as food in ch.46. No quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The jackfruit is a tropical tree of South Asia and its fruit is a staple food in many regions; the latex can irritate the skin and mouth in some people. [USER: verify] The classic's uses are not assessed here. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The text places Panasa in the fruit group, and says of that group that its members "act as constructive tonics". That is a statement about a food group, not a rasayana or vajikarana status for this fruit. The tonic-remedy chapter read (Chikitsa ch.30) does not name Panasa. The frame is therefore the ordinary Sushrutan dietetics of food and drink in Sutra ch.46, and no cultivation status for Panasa is attested. No ojas, tejas or prana claim is made.

**Practitioner's note.** A sweet fruit that the text counts among the cooling, nourishing foods, and whose oil is named in a list for Vayu with Pitta. It is food in the text's own terms, and the record supports nothing more for a cultivator than the eating of the fruit in season.

### Contraindications

- **Latex and allergy.** The tree's sticky latex and the fruit can irritate or sensitise some people; this is general knowledge, not the text. [USER: verify]
- **Diabetes and fruit sugars.** Ripe jackfruit is a sweet fruit; no statement in the text. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources read. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The species is the editor's identification, and the scan's spelling of the binomial is wrong. [USER: verify]
