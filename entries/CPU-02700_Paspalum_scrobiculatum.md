# Paspalum scrobiculatum — Kodrava (Sushruta; vol. III appendix and chapter texts)

## *Paspalum scrobiculatum, the millet grown as kodo millet, read from the Latin line in the appendix and matched by position to the Sanskrit key "Kodrava"; the Latin spelling is clear; confidence high for the species-level pairing, because the chapter texts put Kodrava among the cultivated coarse grains* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The plant is attested here only in Sushruta, through the Sanskrit-to-Latin plant-name appendix at the end of Bhishagratna's English translation, vol. III (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, the "Appendix" pages from about line 31860 to 32470). A search of the chapter files on disk for the Sanskrit name *Kodrava* found it in several Sushruta passages (set out below), so unlike its neighbours in this batch the name is attested in the running text and not only in the appendix. The link from *Kodrava* to the Latin name rests on the appendix pairing.

**How the appendix is laid out, and why this row is uncertain.** In the OCR text the Sanskrit names stand in one run of lines and the Latin names in another, so a Latin name is matched to its Sanskrit name only by position. Here the Latin line is 32045, which reads "Paspalum  Scrobiculatum", and the nearest Sanskrit entry by position is at line 31972, which the OCR prints as "Eodrava (Kodrava)". The offset between the two columns drifts by a few lines along the appendix (about 60 lines here), so the pairing is a position guess, confirmed only as far as neighbouring pairs match in the same stretch. **Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The pairing here is the best supported of the batch, since the chapters of the text name *Kodrava* as a grain. In the food-and-regimen chapter, Sutra ch. 20 (line 49), it appears in a list of articles that may be taken as food, after the *Nivara* and before the *Uddalaka*, *Shyamaka*, *Godhuma* and *Venu*. In Sutra ch. 46, in the genus of *Kudhanya* (coarse grains), the list of species includes *Kodrava* among others, and the text says (line 180) that "the species known as the Kodrava, Nivara, Shyamaka and Shantanu have a sweet and astringent taste and prove curative in Sheeta pitta (urticaria)", while the genus as a whole is said (lines 168-179) to tend to produce retention of the urine and to enrage the bodily *Vayu* and *Pittam*. In Uttara ch. 64 (line 171) *Kodrava* rice is among articles to be prescribed after the cold, in the spring. In Kalpa ch. 8 (line 446) "Kodrava straw mixed with clarified butter" is used with *Kusumbha* flower and the two kinds of *Rajani* in a fumigation against the poison of a venomous insect. In Chikitsa ch. 17 (Chap. XVII of the translation, vol. II text, lines 18803-18811) "the seeds of the Dhustura, Madana and Kodrava, Kosataki, Shuka-nasa, Mriga-bhojani" and others "should be pounded together and applied to a sinus (Nadi) after having washed it with a decoction of Lakshd", and the oil cooked with cow's urine and these drugs is said to heal a sinus "in seven nights". [USER: verify each chapter line against the printed page.] The appendix gives the Latin name and nothing else; the properties below come from the chapter texts under the Sanskrit name. The translator's Latin is nineteenth-century and the OCR is rough, so the spelling in the text is a witness to a name, not a modern botanical determination.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says.** *Kodrava* is a cultivated coarse grain of the *Kudhanya* genus, listed among foods (Sutra ch. 20, line 49; Sutra ch. 46), prescribed with other light grains after the cold season in the spring (Uttara ch. 64, line 171), curative in *Sheeta pitta* (urticaria) according to Sutra ch. 46 (line 180), its straw is burnt in a fumigation against insect poison (Kalpa ch. 8, line 446), and its seeds are one of several drugs pounded for a sinus (Chikitsa ch. 17).
- **What the Codex does.** It records these passages. It gives no protocol and does not transfer any of them to a modern use. The urticaria statement is a report of the text's claim and not advice.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text prints a food use (the grain eaten as rice-like food, Uttara ch. 64, line 171), a fumigation of the straw "mixed with clarified butter" with *Kusumbha* flower and the two kinds of *Rajani* (Kalpa ch. 8, line 446), and a sinus remedy in which the seeds are pounded with *Dhustura*, *Madana* and other drugs, applied after washing the sinus with a *Laksha* decoction, or used in oil cooked with cow's urine (Chikitsa ch. 17, vol. II text, lines 18803-18811). The companions *Dhustura* and *Madana* are not harmless plants, so the remedy's activity cannot be assigned to *Kodrava*. No quantity or timing is printed for any of these in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.] **Do not use any dose from this entry.**

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Grains of this species are eaten as a food; in warm wet seasons the grain can be infected with a mould, and eating grain so infected has been reported to cause poisoning with nausea, dizziness and tremor. The entry cannot say whether Sushruta's remarks touch this. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** Sutra ch. 46 (line 180): the species *Kodrava*, *Nivara*, *Shyamaka* and *Shantanu* "have a sweet and astringent taste". The *Kudhanya* genus as a whole is said (lines 168-179) to be heating, parching, pungent of digestion, to subdue the *Kapham*, and to produce retention of the urine and enrage the bodily *Vayu* and *Pittam*; whether each clause applies to *Kodrava* in particular is not separated by the lines read. ### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* The passages are dietary and toxicological. It is a food grain and a fumigation straw in the passages read, none of them a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no Sushrutan source retrieved assigns it a place in either division. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is attested for it, and none is made. The popular sattvic or rajasic food lists trace to the Gita and later material and not to Sushruta, so none is applied.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to practise. A coarse grain eaten as food in the season the text names; the cultivator's act is dietary, within the text's own caution that the genus tends to produce retention of urine, and nothing here supports a cultivation use beyond that.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The Latin pairing rests on the appendix's position and on the grain context of the chapters. Do not gather or use a plant on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Text's own caution.** The *Kudhanya* genus is said to tend to retention of the urine and to enrage *Vayu* and *Pittam* (Sutra ch. 46). Mouldy grain should not be eaten. [USER: verify]
- **Look-alikes.** Several species of the same genus or family look alike and are used under different names; the entry cannot tell them apart.
