# Shilajatu — "Shilajatu" / "Silajatu" / "S'ildjatu" (as Bhishagratna prints the word in the *Sushruta Samhita*); "Shad-Yoni" (the translator's name for it in Chikitsa ch.13)
## *A gelatinous substance that the Sushruta text says is secreted from the sides of mountains heated by the sun in the months Jyaishtha and Ashadha; the text attributes its character to six metals. The translation gives no plant, animal or modern mineral identification* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The spine row cites *Sushruta Samhita* Sutrasthana ch.38; the name-index row reads "mineral pitch / humic exudate", which is the Codex's own label and not Bhishagratna's wording. The text on disk was searched for the word under all the spellings the OCR gives (Shilajatu, Silajatu, S'ildjatu, S'ilajatu, S'ild-jatu) in every chapter file of the Sutra, Chikitsa, Uttara, Kalpa, Nidana and Sharira folders. It occurs in the following places, and in no others.

- **Sutrasthana ch.38, the Ushaka'di group** (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 274-283; Bhishagratna Vol. I line 15617). Chapter 38 opens (lines 8-10): "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana) which are as follows". The group reads: "The medicinal drugs and substances known as Ushaka (alkaline earth) Saindhava salt, Shilajatu, the two kinds of Kasisa, Hingu and Tutthaka enter into the composition of the group known as the Ushakadi Gana." The metrical text that follows is about the group, not about Shilajatu alone: "It destroys kapham mucous), absorbs the fat of the body and proves curative in cases of stone or gravel in the bladder (urinary calculi), dysuria and abdominal glands (Gulma)." The "restorative and an elixir ... virile potency" text printed just above it (lines 265-272) belongs to the preceding Kakolyadi group and is not read as applying to Shilajatu.
- **Sutrasthana ch.15** (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch15.txt` lines 617-624), in the passage on obstruction of the channels by deposits of fat: "medicated compositions, consisting of such drugs and substances as Shilajatu, Guggulu, Go-Mutram, Triphala, Loharaja, Rasanjanam, Madhu, Yava, Mudga, Koradusha, Shyamaka and Uddalaka which are anti-fat in their properties ... should be prescribed."
- **Chikitsasthana ch.13** (treatment of diabetes, *Madhu-Meha*; `chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch13.txt` lines 16-106; Bhishagratna Vol. II lines 17277-17333): the one passage that describes the substance itself, set out below.
- **Scattered uses as a drug in a compound**, listed under Preparation & Dosing: Chikitsa ch.5 (line 744), ch.7 (line 55), ch.9 (line 95 and footnote 120), ch.16 (line 170); Uttara ch.12 (line 68), ch.41 (line 349), ch.44 (line 249), ch.46 (line 142 and the footnote at the head of `uttara/SS_Uttara_ch47.txt`).

**What Chikitsa ch.13 says it is (lines 16-36).** Under the heading "Shilajatu, its origin and properties" Bhishagratna prints: "A kind of gelatinous substance is secreted from the sides of the mountains when they have become heated by the rays of the sun in the months of Jyaishtha and Ashadha. This substance is what is known as the Silajatu and it cures all distempers of the body." The next sentence is: "The presence of the six kinds of metal, such as tin, lead, copper, silver, gold and black-iron, in their essential form in the substance (Silajatu), may be detected by their respective smell and hence it is known to the people by the name of Shad-Yoni (lit. — having six different origins). The taste of this shellac-coloured substance has the same taste (Rasa) and potency (Virya) as the metal to whose essence it owes its origin." The text adds that "as tin, lead and iron, etc., are progressively more and more efficacious, so the different varieties of Sildjatu, originated from the essence of tin, lead, iron, etc., are progressively more efficacious in their application." The text on disk names six metals and says their "essential form" is in the substance; it does not describe six separate products beyond that, so no separate variety entries are written here.

**Which kind is best (lines 42-49).** "Of these what looks black and glossy, is heavy and devoid of sandy particles, as well as what smells like the urine of a cow, should be considered as the best."

**What is not said.** The sources read do not give a botanical or geological identification, a place of collection beyond "the sides of the mountains", or the mode of collection. Whether the Sushruta substance is the same material as the dark resinous exudate sold today under similar names cannot be settled from these chapters. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The text names no constituents beyond the six metals it says are present "in their essential form" and detectable by smell (Chikitsa ch.13 lines 24-28). The Codex does not take that statement as a statement of modern chemistry. In general terms, dark mountain exudates of this kind are mixtures of plant-derived organic matter that has decayed under mineral conditions, with variable mineral content, and the composition varies with source. Raw material of this sort can carry heavy metals, fungal and bacterial contamination. This is a general statement about the class of material, not a reading of Sushruta.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Sushruta treats Shilajatu as a drug of broad use: it is named in a group credited with destroying *kapha* and relieving urinary stone, it is among anti-fat compounds, and it is the subject of a full passage in the diabetes chapter that credits it with curing "all distempers of the body", with removing urinary gravel and stone, and with prolonging life. The same chapter makes its use conditional on preparation, prior emetics and purgatives, a meal of boiled rice with the soup of Jangala-group animal flesh after the dose, and the avoidance of pigeon flesh and Kulattha pulse. The purity of material sold under this name today is not controlled by that tradition, and the text's unqualified longevity claim cannot be carried into a recommendation. No modern protocol is supplied.

### Preparation & Dosing

**The main preparation (Chikitsa ch.13 lines 49-59).** "This best kind of Sildjatu should be infused with the decoction of the drugs of the S'ala-sarddi group after the manner of Bhavana saturation (for ten, twenty or thirty days). Then after cleansing the body (by the application of emetics and purgatives), it should be taken every morning (by the patient in adequate doses), well pasted with Sarodaka. He should further be made to take a meal of boiled rice mixed with the soup of the flesh of animals of the Jangala group after the medicine had been fully digested." A footnote (lines 79-82) says the editor reads *Sarodaka* as a decoction of the S'ala-sarddi drugs, and that Dallana's reading ("Pancha-sarodaka") is "quite unintelligible".

**Quantity (lines 61-69).** The only quantity in the passage is a long-course total: "A Tula measure of this hill-begotten panacea (Silajatu), when gradually taken, (in adequate doses) tends to improve the strength and complexion of the body, cures an attack of Madhu-Meha and enables the user to witness a hundred summers on earth, free from disease and decay. Each Tula weight of this medicine, taken successively, adds a century to the duration of human life, while ten Tula measures extend it to a thousand years." The text does not state a single dose ("in adequate doses"), and it does not state what a *Tula* is in modern units in these lines. The conversion is not made here. No single dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

**Conduct and diet (lines 69-72).** "The regimen of diet and conduct during the period of its use should be identical with that described in connection with the use of the Bhallaitaka compounds." That regimen is in a different chapter and was not read for this entry. [USER: verify] At lines 103-106 the text adds that a person using Shilajatu and Makshika "should refrain from taking pigeon-flesh and Kulattha pulse (during his life-time)". It also says Shilajatu "should be treated (soaked and dried) with appropriate medicinal drugs by stirring it up with the same" (lines 89-91).

**Conditions the passage names (lines 72-77).** "Cases of Meha, Kushtha, epilepsy (Apasmara), insanity, elephantiasis, poison-begotten distempers, phthisis, aedema, haemorrhoids, Gulma (internal tumours), jaundice and chronic fever, prove readily amenable to the curative efficacy of Silajatu." Lines 87-91: "It acts as a potent solvent in cases of long-standing Sarkari (gravel) in the bladder as well as of stone."

**Other uses as a drug in a compound.** No quantity is printed in the lines read for any of these.
- Chikitsa ch.5 (Maha-Vata-Vyadhi), line 744: "a potion, consisting of Guggulu or S'ildjatu dissolved in cow's urine", said to subdue aggravated Vayu surcharged with fat and Kapha, and to be curative in "heart-disease, an aversion to food, Gulma and internal abscesses" (lines 745-749).
- Chikitsa ch.7 (urinary calculus), line 55: S'ildjatu, Madhuka flower and Indivara seeds as the paste (*kalka*) with a decoction of many drugs, to break down Pittaja calculi.
- Chikitsa ch.9 (Kushtha), line 95 and the footnote at lines 119-122: "special medicinal remedies prepared from Bhallataka, Silajatu, Guggulu, Aguru, Tuvaraka, Khadira, and Asana" in the later forms of the disease; the footnote says preparations of Shilajatu are described "in the treatment of Prameha-pidaka".
- Chikitsa ch.16 (abscesses), line 170: "Purified S'ildjatu, Guggulu, S'unthi, or Devadaru, dissolved in the decoction of the drugs antidotal to the aggravated Doshas".
- Uttara ch.12 (eye), line 68: one of the ingredients of a collyrium rod (*varti*) of sandalwood, powdered metals and other drugs pasted with honey.
- Uttara ch.41 (phthisis), line 349: garlic (Rasona), "Ndga-vald", "MdgadJdkd" (OCR as printed) or S'ildjatu "with milk, in the prescribed manner".
- Uttara ch.44 (Kamala, jaundice), line 249: "Srotanjana and S'ildjatu with cow's urine in a case of Kumbha-Kamala".
- Uttara ch.46 (fainting), line 142: "S'ildjatu mixed with Tri-phala, Chitraka, S'unthi, etc. should be employed in combination with sugar and continued for a month". A footnote at the head of `uttara/SS_Uttara_ch47.txt` (line 1) says Dallana wants it "prepared with the said drugs after the manner of Bhavana saturation and then be applied with sugar".

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Chikitsa ch.13 prints qualities for the substance (lines 29-32 and 38-42): that it has "the same taste (Rasa) and potency (Virya) as the metal to whose essence it owes its origin"; and that "All kinds of Silajatu have a bitter and pungent taste with an astringent after-taste (Anu-rasa), are laxative, pungent in their digestionary reaction, heat-making in their potency and possessed of absorbing and purifying (Chhedana) properties." The text is Bhishagratna's English; the Sanskrit terms in brackets are his. Chapters 45 and 46 of the Sutrasthana (the dravya chapters) were searched for the word and do not list it.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; the rasayana status is not attested in a rasayana chapter.* Shilajatu is not named in any of the rasayana chapters searched (Chikitsa ch.27, 28, 29 and 30; searched under all OCR spellings of the word), and Sutra ch.38 puts it in a therapeutic group (Ushaka'di) whose printed actions are against *kapha*, fat, stone and *gulma*. It is therefore not graded (a). The one passage that comes near a cultivation claim is in the diabetes chapter, Chikitsa ch.13 (lines 61-69): the text says the substance improves "the strength and complexion of the body", enables the user to see "a hundred summers on earth, free from disease and decay", and that each *Tula* "adds a century to the duration of human life." That is a longevity claim made inside a chapter on *Madhu-Meha*; the passage does not use the words *rasayana*, *vajikarana* or *ojas*, so none of them is attributed to the substance here. The passage also fixes conduct for the user (the regimen of the Bhallataka compounds, avoidance of pigeon flesh and Kulattha pulse), which belongs to the tradition's own discipline of diet and conduct; it is stated as printed, not as a cultivation practice the Codex recommends. Whether Dallana or other commentary places Shilajatu among the *rasayana* drugs was not read. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation. The tradition's text ties the substance to a prepared, supervised course after emetics and purgatives, in a patient already treated for a named disease, and its figures for longevity are not a basis for use. Purity of what is sold today is not guaranteed by that tradition. No single dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Identity and purity:** the substance as the text describes it is a mountain exudate, and what is sold today may be adulterated or contaminated with heavy metals or microbes; the text itself says the metals are present in it.
- **Diet and conduct in the text:** the passage forbids pigeon flesh and Kulattha pulse for the person using it, and requires prior emetics and purgatives. These are the tradition's own conditions and are not medical advice.
- **Chronic illness:** the diabetes, stone and epilepsy claims are traditional; they do not replace modern care for any of these conditions.
- **Pregnancy, children and other drugs:** the sources retrieved say nothing on these. [USER: verify]
