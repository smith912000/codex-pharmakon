# Honey, Kshaudra — Kshaudram (Sushruta Samhita)
## *A kind of honey that Sushruta's translator defines as honey from the hives of small, tawny brown bees; no species is named in the text, and an Apis binomial would be ours; the first pass called this a duplicate of general honey (CPU-00446) and the audit disagreed, since Sushruta lists it as a separate kind of honey* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** Historical record only. Sushruta says honey of every kind becomes "positively injurious" after contact with heat or fire, and that undigested honey "is as fatal as any poison" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 1183-1203 and 1221-1228). Nothing here is a recommendation.

**Single-text entry.** Only the *Sushruta Samhita* is attested for this kind of honey on the project's spine (CPU-02382), and no other text on disk was found to name it. The row began on 13 October 2026 as an unverified placeholder from `_nonplant/nonplant_master.csv` (curated 8 October, confidence clear); this entry replaces it. The general honey entry (CPU-00446, `III_13_Honey.md`) covers honey across eight traditions; this entry treats only the single kind that Sushruta names (*Kshaudram*).

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The honey group opens (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 1076-1092): "Honey is sweet, and leaves an astringent after-taste. It is parchifying, cold, stomachic, cosmetic, tonic, light, softening, palatable, liquefacient (Lekhanan), and fermenting (Sandhanam)", and it "pacifies the deranged Pittam and Kapham" (Bhishagratna's English). A metrical text then says that "Eight different kinds of honey are commonly used", naming Pauttikam, Bhramaram, Kshaudram, Makshikam, Chhatram, Arghyam, Auddalakam and Dalam (lines 1093-1102). A translator's footnote (lines 1120-1142) defines each kind by its bee or its source. 

**The passage on Kshaudram.** The name is the third of the eight (line 1095, "Kshaudram") and is defined in the translator's footnote (lines 1126-1127): "The kind of honey obtained from hives of small, tawny brown bees is called the Kshaudram." The body text (line 1114) says only that "the one known as the Kshaudram is extremely cool, light and liquefacient." It is set beside Bhramaram, which "is extremely heavy owing to its extremely sweet taste and slimy character" (lines 1111-1114), and is the comparison point for Makshikam, which the text calls "lighter, dryer and more efficacious than the honey of the preceding class (Kshaudram)" (lines 1115-1117).

**Identity.** The footnote's "small, tawny brown bees" is the only description of the animal. The general honey entry (`III_13_Honey.md`, line 98) names *Apis florea* for Kshaudra; that assignment was not found in the lines read and is not adopted. Kshaudra honey is a Sushrutan kind, and it is held separate from CPU-00446 because the text assigns it properties of its own. [USER: verify the bee]

### Active Components

Kshaudra honey is a bee product like other honey. The Sushruta text names no constituent. Honey in general is mainly the sugars fructose and glucose with water, small amounts of other sugars, organic acids, enzymes and traces of pollen and minerals; its composition depends on the flowers visited and on the bee. ### Preparation & Dosing

The passage on this kind gives no preparation and no dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] The honey group as a whole carries general rules: honey is not to be heated or boiled, and not used "in a hot country, or during the hot season of the year, or in a heated state of the body" (lines 1183-1203); rain-water also makes honey injurious, except Arghyam honey (lines 1203-1207); honey for emetic use may be given with a hot substance if it is ejected at once (lines 1208-1214). Honey also "partakes of the virtues of the drugs or substances with which it is so used" (*Yoga-Vahika*, lines 1176-1178). These are rules for honey as a group, not for this kind alone.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Honey is a concentrated sugar solution with antimicrobial properties from low water activity, acidity and hydrogen peroxide produced by an enzyme, and it is used as a topical dressing in modern wound care. Honey can carry spores of *Clostridium botulinum* and must not be given to infants under one year. Honey from some plants (for example certain rhododendrons) can contain grayanotoxins and cause poisoning. Sushruta's remark that honey is made "poisonous" by the bees' foraging has no precise modern equivalent. The Ayurvedic distinctions between kinds of honey have not been validated here. ### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The honey group stands in *Sutrasthana* ch.45, the chapter on the properties of liquids, and not in a chapter on *rasayana* or *vajikarana*. The group's opening lines call honey "tonic" and "aphrodisiac", and the line on fresh honey calls it "constructive and aphrodisiac" (lines 1076-1092 and 1165-1167), but these are properties of honey in general, not a placement of (*Kshaudram*) in a rejuvenation or vitality discipline. Nothing was read that names this kind in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* is made. The sattvic, rajasic and tamasic food lists are Samkhya and later categories and are not Sushrutan.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. Sushruta describes this honey as cool and light and names no use. A cultivator would have no way to choose this kind over any other honey, and the text does not attach a cultivation purpose to it.

### Contraindications

- **Heat.** Sushruta forbids honey that has been heated, boiled or used in hot weather or a hot body (lines 1183-1203). Treat that as the tradition's own warning.
- **Undigested honey** is described as "as fatal as any poison" (lines 1221-1228).
- **Infants.** Never give honey to a child under one year (infant botulism); this is modern general knowledge, not from the text.
- **Diabetes and sugar restriction.** Honey is sugar.
- **Allergy** to bee products.
- **Identity.** The kind of bee cannot be identified from the text. [USER: verify]
