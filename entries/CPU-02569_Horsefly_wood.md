# Wood horsefly — 木虻 *Mu Meng* (also 魂常 *Hun Chang*)

## *A biting fly of the horsefly family (Tabanidae), probably Tabanus or Atylotus species, the identification we give to 木虻; of medium confidence at family level only: Sun's note quotes the Shuowen on 虻 as a "biting flying insect", and the Huainanzi, which links the word to a dragonfly-like creature, but the Shennong paragraph gives no description of the animal, and the classic has a second fly monograph, 蜚虻 (Fei Meng), that the sources do not clearly separate from this one* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** *Mu meng* is a monograph of the Divine Farmer's Classic. It has no chapter in Sushruta, Dioscorides, Ebers or the Assyrian texts. Yang 1998 does not carry this monograph in the readable part of its text: its animal pages are not readable on disk, and the only "Mu Meng" its readable pages print (line 3202) is an alias of a different herb, *Zi Shen* (Radix Salviae Chinensis), not the fly. The monograph therefore rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction alone.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the middle (中) classic, reads 味苦，平 ("bitter, balanced"). It governs 目赤痛 ("red, painful eyes"); a clause whose first character is lost in the transcription and which ends 伤泪出 (printed as "…wound, tears come out"; the clause concerns the eye, but the lost character prevents a sure reading) [USER: verify]; 瘀血血闭 ("stagnant blood, blocked blood"; 血闭 is commonly read as stopped menses, but the text does not say so); 寒热酸… (a run beginning "cold and heat, aching…" with a lost character); and 无子 ("without children", i.e. infertility). It gives the alias 一名魂常 (*hun chang*) and the habitat 生川泽 ("rivers and marshes"). **There is no 久服 ("taken long") clause.** (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 木虻 *mu meng*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2779)

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 gives the place 生汉中 ("Hanzhong") and the harvest 五月取 ("taken in the fifth month") (line 2781).

**Sun's note (案).** Sun cites three older glosses (line 2783). The *Shuowen* says 虻，啮人飞虫: "the *meng* is a flying insect that bites people". The *Guangya* has a variant character glossed as *meng*, which Sun calls "the abbreviated form" (此省文). The *Huainanzi* (chapter *Qisu*) says 水虿，为…芒 ("the water *chai* becomes a …*mang*"; one character is lost), and Gao You's commentary says it is 青蛉也 ("a blue-green *ling*", the old name of a dragonfly-type insect). Sun adds that the *Huainanzi* (the chapter is printed as 说文训, which looks like a slip for another chapter title) says **虻，散积血** ("the *meng* disperses accumulated blood"). That last line is the one early statement in Sun's note about what the insect does.

**Position among its neighbours.** The next monograph in Sun's text is 蜚虻 *Fei Meng* (line 2787 ff.): 味苦，微寒, which "drives out stagnant blood, breaks down accumulations of blood, hard lumps and masses, cold and heat, frees the blood vessels and the nine orifices", habitat 生川谷, with the Mingyi remark that "those with blood in the belly are good" (腹有血者，良) and a harvest in the fifth month, at Jiangxia. Both flies are listed in the middle class (line 1677). The sources do not separate them: both are blood-dispersing flies taken in the fifth month. The curator's choice to keep them as separate monographs is followed here, and the Fei Meng monograph is not written in this entry. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** *Meng* is the old general word for biting flies of the horsefly family. A description, a body part or a preparation is never given. The standard modern reading of 木虻 is therefore genus or family level only: Tabanidae, with *Tabanus* and *Atylotus* the usual genera named. The Huainanzi link to a dragonfly-like animal in Sun's note is not followed by modern materia medica and is mentioned only as it stands in the text. Confidence is medium, and the Codex does not claim a species.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms an insect's body is mostly protein, chitin and fat; the biting mouthparts and saliva of horseflies contain anticoagulant compounds that keep a bite wound bleeding. This is a well-established fact about the family and does not come from the texts read. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records the insect for red, painful eyes, for stagnant and blocked blood, for cold-and-heat spells, and for infertility. The Codex records these as the classic's claims. The animal is a blood-feeding fly, taken in the fifth month; the Codex does not recommend catching, drying or eating flies, and a blocked period, an eye condition or infertility needs medical care.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph gives no preparation, dose or course. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest (the fifth month). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Horseflies (Tabanidae) are blood-feeding flies whose females bite large mammals, and their saliva contains anticoagulant and anti-clotting factors; this is general zoology. The *Huainanzi* line "disperses accumulated blood" matches the idea of an anticoagulant only in a loose sense, and nothing read shows that dried flies have any such effect in the body. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong text proper).** 味苦，平: bitter; balanced. No organ or channel affinity is printed. The paragraph prints no toxicity word; the middle-class placement carries the preface's general statement that middle drugs may be non-toxic or toxic and are to be weighed accordingly.
- No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph stands in the middle classic. The preface says middle drugs are "ministers" that "nourish the nature (养性) to correspond with humans", may be non-toxic or toxic and are to be weighed as suits, and are for those who want to "check illness and restore the lean" (line 1677; the drug is named in that list). The paragraph is a flavour, a nature and a list of complaints and has **no 久服 clause**; nothing promises a lighter body or a longer life. It therefore carries the middle class's frame and a therapeutic status only. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 木虻 *mu meng*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 1677 and 2779)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The entry is a record of a blood-dispersing insect drug whose text is partly lost and whose identity does not go below the family. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy.** The text names the drug as dispersing accumulated blood and treating blocked blood. It should not be used in pregnancy or with anticoagulant drugs. This is a precaution drawn from the stated actions and not from a statement in the text. [USER: verify]
- **Bleeding.** Do not use it in bleeding disorders or before surgery.
- **Identity and hygiene.** Wild-caught flies carry pathogens; the Codex does not recommend gathering them.
- **Eye symptoms.** Painful red eyes need medical examination; do not apply insect material to them.
- **Animal welfare.** The Codex does not recommend killing animals for these uses.
