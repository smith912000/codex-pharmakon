### Origin & Tradition

> **IRRITANT CAUTION.** The chapter itself says that the creature "when touched ... breeds itching". Whatever animal it was, handling it is recorded as causing irritation. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation.

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk, under this Greek name.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is *De Materia Medica* 2-16, heading "SKOLOPENDRA" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 4979; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 206, "DMM, SKOLOPENDRA, 2-16 (line 4979), Myriapoda, Cheilopoda — Sea Centipedes, Millepedes, animal"; list row `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 197, which marks the class "plant" in error, since the row sits in Book II among the animals; the book index gives "Scolopendra 188", line 17456). The chapter was read in full (lines 4979 to 4981).

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The entire chapter is one sentence, line 4981: "The sea scolopendra boiled in oil and rubbed on removes hair [depilatory], but when touched it breeds itching." The bracket "depilatory" is the translator's gloss. The text names a preparation (boiled in oil) and a purpose (to remove hair) and the reaction on touch (itching), and nothing else: no description of the animal, no habitat, no part used, no measure.

**The neighbouring chapters, read for context.** Chapter 2-16 sits in the run of sea creatures at the start of Book II (the section is headed "LIVING CREATURES", line 4910):

- 2-14 SKORPIOS THALASSIOS, sea scorpion (translator: Scorpaenidae, scorpion fish, sculpin), "The gall of the sea scorpion is good for bathing eyes, white spots on the cornea, and excessive moisture in the eyes" (line 4975).
- 2-15 DRAKON THALASSIOS, sea dragon (translator: Trachinidae, weevers), "The sea dragon (opened and applied) is a cure for the hurt done by his prickles" (line 4978).
- 2-16 SKOLOPENDRA, this row.
- 2-17 NARKE, sea torpedo (translator: electric ray), applied to long-standing sores about the head (line 4982, heading; text 4984).
- 2-18 ECHIDNE, the viper (heading line 4985).

The sequence places the sea scolopendra with fish and other marine animals, not with the land centipede. That agrees with the text's word "sea" and is the only reason this row is kept apart from the Chinese centipede row (`CPU-00275`, wu gong), which is a land arthropod attested in several traditions. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Many marine invertebrates and fish produce irritant or venomous secretions, and handling unknown sea creatures can cause skin reactions. The text's "itching" on touch agrees in kind with that. Whether the oil removed hair is not a point this entry can check. Modern depilation does not use animal preparations. The translator's suggested groups (Myriapoda, Cheilopoda) are land arthropods, and the word *scolopendra* in later authors has been used for several unrelated creatures, so the animal cannot be named from the Greek alone.
