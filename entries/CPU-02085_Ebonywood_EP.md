# Ebonywood — as printed by Bryan 1930 (Ebers papyrus); forms Ebonywood, Shavings-of-Ebonywood

## *A dark wood called "Ebonywood" in Bryan's English, listed in his Chapter VI; Bryan prints no Egyptian word and no botanical name in the lines read. Identification: the black heartwood the Egyptians imported or traded as ebony, usually taken by modern writers to be African blackwood (Dalbergia melanoxylon) and not the Asian Diospyros ebenum; this is the audit's note and our reading, not a statement of Bryan's text. Confidence: medium that a black hardwood is meant, low as to species* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Egyptian Papyrus Ebers (EP).** Bryan's Chapter VI, "Plant Remedies", lists "Ebonywood" with the form "Shavings-of-Ebonywood" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1278; names.csv row). Two eye recipes were found by searching the text.

1. **Ebonywood with collyrium, for leucoma.** Bryan describes the oculists' attempts at leucoma: after lotions of cream and milk, and of collyrium, ink and water, "they ground some Ebonywood and Collyrium to powder and applied it. Next they dressed the eyes with a poultice of Powdered Granite", then a paste of collyrium and the bile of the *abdu*-fish, and finally magic with the brain of a tortoise in honey (line 2804).
2. **Shavings of ebony with soda, for the pupil.** "For the Drawing-together of the Pupil of the Eye, 'whatever that may mean', one bathed the eye frequently with a lotion made by crushing Shavings-from-Ebony-Wood with Soda-from-Upper-Egypt in Water" (line 2981). Bryan's phrase "whatever that may mean" is his own comment on the complaint's name.

No third use, no description of the wood and no statement of its origin was found in the lines read. The English on disk does not give the Egyptian word. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Two eye conditions: leucoma (a white growth or opacity in the eye, as Bryan renders it) and the "drawing-together of the pupil".
- **What the text does not say.** No quantity of ebony in either recipe and no description of how the shavings were prepared beyond grinding and crushing.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend putting wood powder, soda or any of the companion materials in the eye. The leucoma sequence includes granite powder and a fish-bile paste, and none is a safe eye treatment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations found are a powder of ebonywood and collyrium "applied" to the eyes, and a lotion of shavings crushed with soda in water for bathing the eye. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any recipe from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Ebony and blackwood are dense, fine-grained hardwoods valued in joinery and instrument making; fine wood dust is a known irritant to eyes and airways, and some hardwood dusts can sensitise the skin. Soda (sodium carbonate) is an alkali and irritates the eye. The Ebers terms (leucoma, drawing-together of the pupil) are not translated here into modern diagnoses. ### Contraindications

- **Do not use the recipes.** Wood dust and soda in the eye can cause injury. Do not apply any of these mixtures to the eye.
- **Eye disease.** Leucoma and other eye opacities are medical conditions needing proper care from an eye doctor.
