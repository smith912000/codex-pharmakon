# Crocodile fat — "Fat-of-the-Crocodile" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus); "Crocodile Oil" printed separately
## *The fat of the crocodile, an animal on Bryan's animal list; Bryan prints no Egyptian word, no species and no preparation of the fat, and the fat occurs once in the running text, as one of six fats in a hair pomade* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter VII "Organic Remedies (animals)". "Crocodile" stands third on Bryan's list of the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1392, after "Lion" and "Hippopotamus"; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 94, class animal, "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). Bryan's list of animal parts (lines 1405-1413) includes "Fat" and "Tallow" but does not tie either to an animal. The spine row ("Crocodile fat, Crocodylus niloticus", with "Ebers (baldness pomade); crocodile-oil.com citing Ebers") was not used as authority: the species is the list-maker's, and the website was not opened.

**The one prescription for the fat.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "crocodile". The fat occurs once, at line 4027, in Chapter XXII "The Hair" (heading line 3994): "ANOTHER FOR THE GROWTH OF THE HAIR ON A HEAD WHICH IS BECOMING BALD. Fat-of-the-Lion, Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus, Fat-of-the-Crocodile, Fat-of-the-Cat, Fat-of-the-Serpent, Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat. Make into one and rub the head of the Bald One therewith." (lines 4023-4029). The crocodile fat is the third of six fats in one compound, which the entries for the lion fat and the hippopotamus fat share. The word "ANOTHER" shows that it follows an earlier hair remedy.

**Other crocodile items in Bryan's text, printed separately and not counted as the fat.**
- **Crocodile Oil**, line 2181, in the liniment "to make the limbs supple" with natron, beans, honey, incense and sweet myrrh, "beaten up with Hippopotamus Oil, Crocodile Oil, Oil-of-the-Shadfish [...] and Oil-of-Two-Days". Bryan does not say whether "Oil" is fat rendered or another preparation.
- **Crocodile-earth**, lines 2112, 2824, 2844 and 4058: with the egg of an ostrich for headache; in a cataract poultice; with black knife-stone and honey for the eye; and "mixed with Honey which has been dissolved in Onion-water" for grey eyebrows. Bryan does not say what it is. It is a separate item and is not described here.
- **Crocodile-incense**, line 2273, among the ingredients of a burn remedy, with "Stone-from-the-Shore, [...] Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat, Onions, and Wax".
- **The crocodile's bite**, lines 2211-2213, in "Minor Surgery": "WHAT TO DO AGAINST THE BITE OF A CROCODILE If thou meetest a Crocodile's Bite, and thou findest his flesh equally fallen away on both sides, so thou coverest it with Raw Meat the first day." This is a remedy against a bite, not a use of the animal.
- **A spoken formula**, line 2978, in the eye chapter text that runs up to the heading "Diseases of the Ear, Nose and Mouth" (line 3027): after an injection into the ear, "repeat this Magic Formula: I HAVE BROUGHT THIS THING AND PUT IT IN ITS PLACE. THE CROCODILE IS WEAK AND POWERLESS. (Twice)." The remedy it follows is an ear injection of collyrium, red-lead and wild honey, not crocodile fat.

**Related items not used.** A Codex project file (`Sources/EP_Ebers/EP_Original_Hieroglyphic.md` line 371) prints a hair remedy that lists "fat of a hippopotamus; fat of a crocodile; fat of a serpent; fat of an ibex; fat of a wild goat" under a translator's label; it disagrees with Bryan's six fats and could not be checked, and it was not used. **What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say how crocodile fat was obtained or rendered, in what quantity it was used, or why it appears in the hair compound. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. The Ghalioungui 1987 glossary row for "Crocodile" in `_sources/ebers.csv` line 107 was not opened.

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it. In general terms, reptile fat is mainly triglycerides, rich in unsaturated fatty acids, and rendered fat is an ointment base. Nothing in the sources retrieved says that crocodile fat contains any component that acts on hair. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show crocodile fat once, as one of six fats rubbed on the head of a bald man. No quantity is legible. Crocodiles are protected over much of their range and the fat has no established use. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution, and a hair pomade is a remedy for an external complaint. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*). Bryan prints no recitation with the six-fat pomade, though he summarises, and that is no proof the papyrus gives none. The crocodile appears in a printed formula at line 2978 ("The Crocodile is weak and powerless"), but it belongs to an ear injection and is not transferred to the fat. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The record is one fat among six in a hair pomade, with no quantity and no cultivation role. A cultivator has nothing to do with it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** animal fat on the scalp or on broken skin carries infection risk; crocodilians can carry *Salmonella*.
- **Conservation and law:** crocodile species are protected in many countries and trade in their products is controlled; the present legal status of any species was not retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** crocodile products come from killing a wild or farmed reptile and are not recommended.
