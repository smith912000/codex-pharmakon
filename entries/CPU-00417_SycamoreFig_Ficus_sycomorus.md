# Sycamore Fig — Egyptian Fig, Mulberry Fig, *nehet* (Egyptian), Pharaoh's Fig

## *Ficus sycomorus* L. | Family: Moraceae

**Tradition Tags:** EP, AT, DMM, CM
**Energetic Classification:** Cool and moist in the first degree; the milky latex hot and cleansing (Galenic — a moistening flesh with a sharp, drawing sap)
**Training Phase:** Foundation

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ebers (Egyptian):** The sycamore fig is among the most sacred trees of the Nile. Its Egyptian name *nehet* attaches it to the goddess Hathor, "Lady of the Sycamore," and the tree appears in tomb imagery as the source of shade, sap, and nourishment for the deceased. The wood was used for coffins and funerary statuary; the sweet fruit was a common food; and the milky latex and leaves belong to the household pharmacopoeia of the Egyptian healers whose remedies survive in the medical papyri. The plant sits comfortably in the class of moistening, nourishing foods that also carry a cleansing, wound-drawing sap.

**Assyrian:** In the cuneiform materia medica of Mesopotamia, figs and fig-type trees recur among the poultice and plaster plants — soft fruits and mucilaginous saps applied to swellings and to soften indurated tissue. The sycamore, distributed across the Levant and the ancient Near East, belongs to this shared regional stock of drawing, ripening agents.

**Dioscorides (DMM):** In *De Materia Medica*, Dioscorides treats the sycamore (*sykomoros*) as distinct from the common fig, noting its watery sweetness, its role as a foodstuff, and the use of its incised latex to soften and disperse hard swellings and to help the bowels. The drawing, softening action of the sap is the through-line of the classical account.

**Avicenna (CM):** In the *Qanun*, figs and their kin are catalogued among the ripening, softening simples — moistening to the flesh, gently opening, and useful in plasters for hard tumours and for cleansing wounds. The latex is treated as the sharper, more actively drawing part of an otherwise mild and nourishing tree.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** The sycamore's doctrine is the doctrine of the threshold — a tree of shade and sap that stands between the living and the dead in Egyptian cosmology. Its pharmacology mirrors the symbolism: a moist, sweet flesh that nourishes and settles, paired with a white latex that draws, ripens, and cleanses. The two faces — nourishing food and cleansing sap — make it a foundation plant, one that steadies the body while gently opening what is congested. Modern work echoes the "settling" reputation: an extract of the stem bark reversed stress-induced behavioural and oxidative damage in rodents, a pharmacological rhyme with the tree's ancient role as a place of rest.

### Active Components

- **Flavonoids** — the dominant class identified in stem-bark extract by LC-MS; the principal antioxidant fraction.
- **Phenolic acids and tannins** — astringent, wound-drawing constituents consistent with the traditional plaster use.
- **Triterpenoids and phytosterols** — common Moraceae constituents contributing to anti-inflammatory activity.
- **Latex proteins and proteolytic enzymes (ficin-type)** — the "sharp" digesting action of the milky sap.
- **Furanocoumarins and simple sugars** in the fruit — the nourishing, mildly opening food fraction.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Sycamore fig is a **Foundation-phase** plant: it steadies before it moves. The nourishing fruit and the antioxidant bark fraction support the ground state — reducing the oxidative "noise" that accompanies chronic strain — while the drawing latex is reserved for topical use on congested, indurated, or slow-healing surface tissue. In the training arc it is used to settle a depleted or over-strained baseline before purification work begins, not to force a crisis. Internally it is treated as a gentle, restorative food-medicine; externally as a ripening, cleansing plaster.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- Foyet et al. (2017), *BMC Complementary and Alternative Medicine* — a hydromethanolic *Ficus sycomorus* stem-bark extract (100–200 mg/kg) reversed anxiety-, depression- and memory-related impairments induced by unpredictable chronic mild stress in rats, and raised total glutathione while lowering malondialdehyde in the temporal lobe, indicating a genuine antioxidant, anti-stress action; the major LC-MS constituent was a flavonoid. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-017-2012-9)
- Anosike, Babandi & Ezeanyika (2021), *Neotropical Entomology* — an active fraction of *F. sycomorus* additively potentiated the insecticide permethrin against permethrin-resistant *Anopheles coluzzii*, an effect linked to inhibition of glutathione-S-transferase detoxifying enzymes, supporting a phytochemically active, enzyme-modulating fraction. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13744-021-00858-2)

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical and traditional. Human clinical data are lacking; the antioxidant and neurobehavioural findings are rodent-level, and the topical/plaster uses rest on classical and ethnobotanical testimony rather than trials. Claims here are graded accordingly.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM (by analogy):** cooling, moistening, sweet; tonifying to fluids with a mild descending/draining quality in the latex. (Not a classical Bencao entry — mapped by property.)
- **Ayurveda (by analogy):** *rasa* madhura (sweet) with kashaya (astringent) in bark/latex; *guna* heavy, unctuous fruit vs. sharp latex; *virya* cooling; *vipaka* sweet. Broadly *pitta*- and *vata*-settling, mildly *kapha*-increasing in the fruit.
- **Galenic/Unani (as attested):** cool and moist flesh, first degree; latex hot, cleansing, ripening — a *muhallil* (dissolving/softening) and gently *mushil* (opening) agent.

### Contraindications

- Latex is a **local irritant** to broken skin, eyes, and mucous membranes — restrict to intact-margin surface use and spot-test.
- Moraceae latex can provoke **contact dermatitis and allergy** in sensitive individuals; those with **latex or fig (Ficus) sensitivity** should avoid.
- No established internal human dose; rodent doses must not be scaled directly to people. Avoid medicinal internal use in **pregnancy, lactation, and children** absent clinical data.
- Ripe fruit as food is well tolerated; the caution attaches to concentrated bark extracts and raw latex.
