# Ewe's Milk (Sushruta Samhita)

## *Milk of the ewe (the female domestic sheep, Ovis aries) as named in Sushruta Sutra ch.45; the text says "ewe", and the identification is of high confidence* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta Samhita only, among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The English text is Bhishagratna's translation, Sutra ch.45, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`. The Sushruta Samhita (Bhishagratna's English translation, as on disk) opens its milk group in Sutra ch.45 with the sentence "The milk of a cow, she-goat, she-camel, ewe, she-buffalo, mare, she-elephant, or of a woman, is what generally comes to the use of man" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` line 415). The group paragraph that follows is about milk as a whole, not about one animal: "heavy, sweet, slimy, cold, glossy, emollient, laxative and mild" (line 422), "rejuvenating and aphrodisiac" (line 453), "increases the duration of life, and acts as a vitaliser" (line 457) and, through kindred properties, "augments the quality of bodily albumen (Ojah)" (line 461).

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The ewe is the fourth animal in the list at line 415. The milk is described at lines 493-496: "The milk of a ewe is sweet, demulcent, heavy and proves aggravating in disorders of Pittam and Kapham. It forms a good diet in Kevalavata and in cough due to the deranged condition of the bodily Vayu." The curd of ewe's milk is described (line 597) as "aggravating in derangements of the Vayu and Kapham, as well as in cases of piles. It is sweet in taste and its chemical reaction increases the slimy secretions of the organs, and tends to derange the bodily humours." Her clarified butter is "light in digestion. It does not enrage Pittam, and proves beneficial in cases of rigour, phthisis (Shosha) and in diseases due to the action of the deranged Vayu and Kapham, as well as in those which affect the female organs of generation" (lines 819-829). Her urine is dealt with in the text at line 1735 and belongs to another entry. The same chapter gives the rules for the time of milching and for boiling milk (lines 524-543), which apply to ewe's milk as to the rest.

### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms sheep's milk is richer in fat and protein than cow's milk and is the milk used for several traditional cheeses; these are well-established general facts, not read in the source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** A "good diet" in *kevalavata* and in cough from deranged *Vayu*. The text also warns that it aggravates disorders of *Pittam* and *Kapham*.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no amount, no duration.

The Codex does not translate the Sanskrit disease terms into modern diagnoses and gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no dose. The milk is described fresh, as curd (line 597) and as clarified butter (line 819). The chapter's general rules: milk milched in the morning is heavy, evening milk cooling; unboiled milk is very heavy and boiling removes that fault; woman's milk is the exception (lines 524-543). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Sheep's milk is a common dairy food in several regions, and is a source of protein, fat and calcium. Allergy to cow's-milk protein often extends to sheep's and goat's milk. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (ch.45).** Rasa: sweet. Qualities printed: demulcent, heavy (lines 493-494). Effect on the *doshas*: aggravates *Pittam* and *Kapham*; good diet where *Vayu* is deranged (lines 494-496). No *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for the milk itself.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Ewe's milk is described in the dietetic chapter in terms of its effect on the *doshas*; it is not named in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter and no *ojas* claim is made for it. The group paragraph (lines 419-461) says milk as a whole is rejuvenating, increases the duration of life and augments *ojas* (rendered "bodily albumen (Ojah)" by the translator). Those words are printed for the group and are not repeated under this animal, so this entry does not carry them across: the status is framed, not attested for this milk alone. 

**Practitioner's note.** A heavy, sweet milk that the text values in *vata* conditions and warns against in *pitta* and *kapha* disorders. As with all the milks, the cultivation statements are in the group paragraph, not under the ewe.

### Contraindications

- **Dosha cautions.** The text itself says the milk aggravates *Pittam* and *Kapham* disorders (lines 493-494).
- **Hygiene.** Raw milk of any animal can carry infection.
- **Allergy.** Cross-reaction with cow's-milk allergy is a general statement, not from the source.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
