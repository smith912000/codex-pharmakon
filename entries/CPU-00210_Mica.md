# Mica — Yun Mu (雲母) "Muscovitum" (Yang 1998); other names Yun Hua, Yun Ying, Yun Ye, Yun Sha, Lin Shi
## *Mica in the Divine Farmer's Classic; Yang 1998 prints "Muscovitum" (muscovite, the pale potassium mica) for Yun Mu, with five other names printed* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yun Mu is printed in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) on PDF page 24, within the run that opens "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (heading at line 907 and again at line 985). The OCR of the page prints the first letter as "¥un Mu" (line 954); the entry is the one the list `_sources/shennong.csv` carries as 雲母 (line 8). The text (lines 954-961): "Yun Mu (Muscovitum) is sweet and balanced. It mainly treats dead muscles and skin in the body as well as wind stroke cold and heat [with dizziness and sickness] as if on board a cart or boat. It eliminates evil qi, quiets the five viscera, boosts the fetal essence [i.e., semen], brightens the eyes, makes the body light, and prolongs life. Its other name is Yun Hua (Cloud Flower). Yet another name is Yun Ying (Cloud Floret). It is also called Yun Ye (Cloud Fluid), Yun Sha (Cloud Sand), and Lin Shi (Fluorescent Stone). It is produced in mountains and valleys." Yang's footnote 40 (lines 975-976) says: "Dead muscles refer to a sensation of itching in the flesh like wriggling worms, i.e., formication." The bracketed words "[with dizziness and sickness]" and "[i.e., semen]" are the translator's. `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` has no row for Yun Mu (its mineral rows run from Yu Quan to Xiao Shi without it), so this entry rests on the text and the list row, and not on a names row. The identification "Muscovitum" is the translator's and no footnote doubts it. The spine's note is "high".

### Active Components

Muscovite is a potassium aluminium silicate, KAl2(AlSi3O10)(OH)2, of the mica group. It is a sheet silicate that splits into thin, flexible, transparent, pearly flakes, and it is chemically inert, insoluble in water and acids, and heat resistant. Other micas (biotite, phlogopite) are darker. The sources read give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records the stone for skin and muscle complaints, for dizziness of wind stroke, for the viscera, the eyes and semen, and for a light body and a long life. These are in part the aims of the Daoist elixir tradition, and the Codex does not endorse them. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation, and a ground silicate is not recommended for swallowing.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is printed in the monograph and no dose is given in the sources retrieved. The names ("Cloud Flower", "Cloud Floret", "Cloud Fluid", "Cloud Sand", "Fluorescent Stone") are names and not preparations. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Mica is an industrial mineral used as an electrical insulator, a filler and a pigment base, and as a pearlescent additive in cosmetics. Finely ground mica dust is a lung irritant. No medical benefit of swallowing mica is established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- SBJ: "sweet and balanced" (Yang line 954). Class: Superior (heading lines 907 and 985). "Balanced" is Yang's rendering for neither cold nor hot, neither warm nor cool (footnote, lines 849-855).

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: an upper-grade drug (上品).* Yun Mu stands under "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (Yang lines 907, 985). Yang's preface says the superior class medicinals "mainly nourish life and correspond to heaven. They are nontoxic and taking them in large amounts and for a long time will not harm people. If one intends to make one's body light, boost the qi, prevent aging, and prolong life, one should base [one's efforts] on the superior class" (lines 352-358). The monograph prints its own cultivation lines: it "quiets the five viscera", "boosts the fetal essence [i.e., semen]", "makes the body light, and prolongs life" (lines 954-960). Unlike the entries for Jade and Cinnabar it prints no phrase about protracted taking and no immortality line. The text uses "essence" for the fetal essence, glossed by the translator as semen, and no *jing* claim is made here beyond that printed gloss. The grade is a statement of the Classic as translated by Yang in 1998; the claim is not carried forward from later materia medica works. Yang's introduction (lines 312-328) warns that elixirs of this kind made some adepts ill.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: the Classic held it safe for long use. Mica is an inert silicate and the Classic's claims have no established support, so nothing here supports taking it. For a cultivator the entry is a record of how the Han-period elixir tradition ranked a shiny, layered stone among the "nourish life" drugs. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Powdered mica can irritate the lungs if inhaled, and powdered stone sold as a remedy may be a different mineral or may be contaminated. Mineral medicines are unstandardised and their sale is regulated in many places. Ethical sourcing of mica is a concern in some producing regions, and this has not been examined here. [USER: verify] The Classic's "prolongs life" is not a claim of fact.
