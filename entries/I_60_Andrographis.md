### Botanical Identity
Annual herb 30–110cm; erect square stem; dark green lanceolate leaves; small white-purple flowers; native to South Asia; widely cultivated in tropical Asia, China, and Latin America. One of the most bitter plants known — andrographolide (the primary active) has a taste threshold at 1:2.5 million dilution. The bitterness is pharmacologically instrumental: bitter receptor (TAS2R38) activation on immune cells directly triggers anti-infective responses, providing an additional immune mechanism beyond andrographolide's direct pharmacology.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Sushruta Samhita / Ayurveda (SS)

*Kālamegh* in classical Ayurveda:
> *"Bitter, light, dry. Reduces Pitta and Kapha. Pacifies fever, destroys parasites, clears liver heat, beneficial for skin diseases. The primary herb for fever of any kind, especially in epidemic fevers. The most effective bitter liver herb."*

The "epidemic fever" application reflects consistent historical use during outbreaks — dengue, malaria, cholera, influenza — across South and Southeast Asia.

### 2.2 Shennong Bencao Jing / TCM (SBJ)

*Chuān xīn lián*:
> *"Bitter; cold. Enters Heart, Lung, Large Intestine, Urinary Bladder. Clears heat and resolves toxins, cools the blood, reduces swelling. Primary use: respiratory tract infections (bronchitis, pneumonia), sore throat, diarrhoea (infective), urinary tract infections, snake bite."*

The Chinese application is specifically infectious — anti-infective at the Lung and Intestine level.

### 2.3 Four Medical Tantras / Tibetan (FMT)

*Tig ta* (Tibetan bitter, used interchangeably with andrographis and *Swertia chirayita*):
> *"Of the hot bitter medicines — clears fever from the blood and bile channels, purges heat from liver and lung, destroys parasites. Essential in fever management formulas."*

Tibetan medicine embeds andrographis in multi-herb formulas for fever — most famous is *Tig-ta* 25 (Tig-ta-nyer-lnga), a compound formula for respiratory fevers.

### 2.4 Assyrian Tablets (AT — minor)

Bitter anti-fever preparations consistent with andrographis-type herbs in Babylonian texts.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 NF-κB Inhibition / Anti-Inflammatory

**Andrographolide**: Covalently binds to the p50 subunit of NF-κB → prevents translocation to nucleus → suppresses transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, COX-2, iNOS). This is a direct, well-characterised molecular mechanism — the NF-κB interaction is confirmed by X-ray crystallography (Xia 2004).

Critically: andrographolide's NF-κB inhibition is **immunomodulatory, not immunosuppressive** — it reduces cytokine storm while preserving innate immune function. This explains its paradoxical ability to both fight infection and reduce excessive inflammatory response.

### 5.2 Anti-Viral

**Andrographolide**: Inhibits viral replication by multiple mechanisms:
- Interferes with viral protease activity (blocks maturation of viral particles)
- Induces type I interferon response (IFN-α, IFN-β) → antiviral state in uninfected cells
- Inhibits nuclear import of viral proteins (NF-κB nuclear transport pathway exploited by multiple viruses)

Active against: influenza A and B, RSV, HIV (in vitro), dengue, SARS-CoV-2 (confirmed in vitro; clinical trial data emerging).

### 5.3 Hepatoprotective

**Andrographolide**: Nrf2/ARE pathway activation → upregulates hepatic antioxidant enzymes (SOD, CAT, GPx) → reduces oxidative liver damage. Also direct hepatocyte protection against toxin-induced injury (CCl4 model confirms). The "liver clearing" in all traditions is validated.

### 5.4 Anti-Malarial

**Andrographolide + 14-deoxy derivatives**: Inhibit *Plasmodium falciparum* growth in erythrocytes (IC50 1–10µM range). Mechanism involves interference with haemoglobin polymerisation and disruption of parasitophorous vacuole membrane. Not yet antimalarial-grade as monotherapy but synergistic with artemisinin.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Upper respiratory tract infections**: Caceres et al. (1999, *Phytomedicine*): Andrographis (SHA-10 standardised extract, 1200mg/day × 4 days) significantly reduced URI symptoms (nasal secretion, throat symptoms, fatigue) vs placebo. Multiple subsequent RCTs confirm. Saxena et al. (2010) meta-analysis of 7 RCTs: andrographis significantly reduces URI symptom severity and duration.

**Influenza**: Poolsup et al. (2004) systematic review: Andrographis superior to placebo for reducing fever and sore throat in uncomplicated influenza.

**COVID-19 (emerging)**: Thai clinical trials (2021–2022): Andrographis extract reduced viral load and symptom severity in mild COVID-19. Mechanistic basis (NF-κB inhibition + antiviral) supported; data preliminary.

**Rheumatoid arthritis**: Burgos et al. (2009): HMPL-004 (andrographis extract) significantly reduced RA tender joints and disease activity vs placebo over 14 weeks.

**Inflammatory bowel disease**: Tang et al. (2011): HMPL-004 comparable to mesalazine for maintaining UC remission (53% vs 48% remission at 52 weeks) — first botanical non-inferior to standard therapy for IBD in a rigorous RCT.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The epidemic herb**: Andrographis has been the primary response herb to epidemic infectious disease across South and Southeast Asia for centuries. During cholera epidemics in India (18th–19th century), Ayurvedic physicians distributed *kālamegh* preparations to affected populations. During the 1919 influenza pandemic in India, andrographis was reportedly one of the most widely distributed herbs for symptom management. During the COVID-19 pandemic (2020–2022), Thai and Indian health authorities issued guidance on andrographis as adjunctive therapy — continuing a 2,000-year trajectory of epidemic response.

**The bitterest medicine**: Andrographolide's taste threshold at 1:2.5 million makes it among the most bitter compounds known — more bitter than quinine. In folk pharmacology across Asia, the extreme bitterness is understood as a marker of potency: "what is most bitter is most medicine." This is pharmacologically meaningful: bitter receptor (TAS2R) activation on macrophages and dendritic cells directly triggers innate immune signalling, creating an immune-activating effect independent of the andrographolide systemic pharmacology.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Standardised extract (SHA-10, 10% andrographolide) | 400mg 3× daily (acute) | URI; influenza; fever |
| Dried herb/tablet | 1–3g/day crude herb | Liver; digestive; chronic infections |
| Traditional decoction | 3–6g dried herb | Fever; anti-infective; liver |
| HMPL-004 (commercial extract) | Per RCT protocol | IBD; RA — clinical applications |

**Timing**: Best taken at onset of infection — prophylactic value less established than acute response.

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Bitter; cold. Channels: Lung (肺), Stomach (胃), Large Intestine (大腸), Urinary Bladder (膀胱). Clears heat, resolves toxins, cools blood.

**Ayurvedic**: Bitter; cooling. Reduces Pitta ↓↓, reduces Kapha ↓. *Jvarāghna* (anti-febrile), *Yakrit* (hepatic), *Krimighna* (anti-parasitic).

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Pregnancy**: Uterotonic and anti-fertility activity demonstrated in animal models — contraindicated
- **Autoimmune conditions**: Immunostimulatory effect may exacerbate autoimmunity — use with caution in lupus, MS, myasthenia gravis (though IBD RCT suggests bidirectional modulation)
- **Immunosuppressive medications (post-transplant)**: Immunostimulatory effect may interfere — contraindicated
- **Fertility (male)**: Andrographolide reduces sperm motility in vitro — counsel men trying to conceive
- Generally very safe for short-term acute use (up to 8 weeks); long-term safety less established
