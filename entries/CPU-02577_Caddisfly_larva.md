# Caddisfly larva — 石蚕 *shican*, "Shi Can" ("stone silkworm"); also 水虱 *shuishi*, 沙虱 *shashi*

## *A creature of ponds and marshes found attached to stones and "shaped like an old silkworm", taken in modern works to be the cased larva of a caddisfly (order Trichoptera, for example Eubasilissa); the identification is ours, of medium confidence, and is weakened by the classic's own aliases, which the commentaries attach to a different, harmful creature* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

*Shi can* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The spine had no row for it before this one (CPU-02577). The animal pages of Yang's English translation are not readable (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), so Sun's text is the only witness read.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 石蚕 in the lower classic (卷三 下经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3927, 味 paragraph; name line 3925). The paragraph reads: 味咸，寒。主五癃，破五淋，堕胎，内解结气，利水道，除热。一名水虱。生池泽。In English: "Flavour salty, cold. Governs the five *long* (五癃, five kinds of urinary blockage); breaks the five *lin* (五淋, five kinds of dribbling urination); causes the fetus to drop (堕胎); internally dissolves bound qi; frees the water passages; removes heat. Another name is Shui Shi ('water louse'). It grows in pools and marshes." There is no 久服 clause. The classic itself says the substance makes the fetus drop.

**Wu Pu layer** (《吴普》, third century; line 3929). "Also called *shashi* (沙虱, 'sand louse'). Shennong and Lei Gong: sour, non-toxic. It grows in Hanzhong. It treats the five *lin*, breaks bound [gathering] within, frees the water passages and removes heat" (神农、雷公：酸，无毒。生汉中。治五淋...利水道，除热). The citation to the *Yulan* is Sun's, and one phrase in the file ("破随内结气") is garbled. Wu Pu's "Shennong" gives "sour" where the classic as reconstructed has "salty", and Wu Pu calls the drug non-toxic. The two statements are not reconciled here.

**Mingyi bielu layer** (《名医》, later additions; line 3931). "Grows in Jiang-Han" (生江汉). No part or harvest is given.

**Sun Xingyan's note** (案, lines 3933-3935). The *Guangya*: 水虱 is [a lost glyph]. The *Huainan wanbi shu*: "the *shashi* is also called 蓬活 (*penghuo*) and 地脾 (*dipi*)." The *Yulan*, in its insect section, quotes Li Dangzhi: "[it is a] worm-like creature, shaped like an old silkworm; it lives attached to stones" (类虫，形如老蚕。生附石). The *Guangzhi*: "all are lice; the louse is red, larger than a nit. [It lives] in the water, enters the human skin and kills people. This appears to differ from Li's [description]" (在水中，入人皮中，杀人，与李似不同). Sun's last remark thus records that the creature described under the alias 沙虱 (a small red water or sand louse that enters the skin and kills) does not match Li Dangzhi's stone-attached silkworm-like creature. Sun leaves the difference open.

**Identity.** The Codex's name rests on Li Dangzhi's description, "shaped like an old silkworm, attached to stones", and on the habitat (pools and marshes), which a modern reader can match to the cased larva of a caddisfly (a general fact). The aliases 水虱 and 沙虱, however, belong in the *Guangzhi* to a quite different, red, skin-entering creature, and Sun records the mismatch. The commentaries on disk do not settle which creature the classic meant, and the Codex keeps the proposed name with the doubt stated. A later herb sold under the same name 石蚕 is a different, plant drug; that fact was not read in the sources on disk. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic records a salty, cold creature for urinary blockage and dribbling, for bound qi and for heat, and says that it makes the fetus drop. The Codex gives no protocol. Urinary retention needs prompt medical care. The classic's own statement that the drug causes the fetus to drop is a serious warning, not a use to be recommended. The creature's identity is uncertain, and the name is attached in the commentaries to a creature said to "kill people". This entry is not a basis for collecting or ingesting any water insect.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Divine Farmer's Classic).** 味咸，寒, "salty, cold" (line 3927).
- **Wu Pu layer.** Shennong and Lei Gong: sour, non-toxic (line 3929). The flavour differs from the classic's "salty".

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic (下经), which the preface describes as "to treat disease, in correspondence with earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long" (下药...主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服; line 2935). Its 味 paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 石蚕 *shican*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3927). Its status is therapeutic only. The Wu Pu, Mingyi and 案 layers add none.

**Practitioner's note.** There is no cultivation practice here. This is a lower-class record of a water creature, of uncertain identity, for urinary complaints, which the classic itself says causes the fetus to drop. Read it as history.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy.** The classic states that the drug makes the fetus drop (堕胎; line 3927). Do not use in pregnancy, or while pregnancy is possible.
- **Toxicity.** Wu Pu calls the drug non-toxic; the preface calls lower-class drugs mostly toxic and not for long taking (line 2935); the commentary on the alias 沙虱 says the creature it describes kills. The Codex has no source on the safety of the material and recommends none of it. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The name was attached to more than one creature. Do not gather water insects for use.
- **Urinary conditions.** Urinary retention or blockage needs prompt medical care.
