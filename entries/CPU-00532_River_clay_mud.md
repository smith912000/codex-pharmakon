### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the list names it. In general terms, river clay is a fine-grained sediment of clay minerals (such as illite and kaolinite) with silt, sand, organic matter and, in many places, microorganisms; its composition varies from river to river. This is a general statement, not a reading of any tablet.

### Scientific Correlation

Clay minerals adsorb water and some substances, which is the general basis of clay poultices and of clays used in medicine in other settings; this is not an Assyrian finding. Soil and river mud can carry tetanus spores and other bacteria. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Infection:** river mud can carry tetanus spores and other bacteria; it should not be put on a wound.
- **Contamination:** modern river sediment may hold heavy metals and industrial residues.
