# Adiantum pedatum — re.ral, Rerel, "a type of Fern" (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Adiantum pedatum L., the first of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for re.ral (with Adiantum aspidium), under the English "a type of Fern"; the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints a different binomial in the position of the same name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** **Named only by the Tibetan texts in this row.** No other text read for this entry names the plant under this name. Both loci are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance", and the row's binomial is the first of two on Clark's line. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1083).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The entry reads: "re.ral: a type of Fern: Adiantum pedatum L.: Adiantum aspidium: [has five names, for example rgyal.po.re.ral(lit. 'king fern') and ldum.bu.re.ral. It grows on the trunks of trees, and has a furry root similar to a monkey's tail, slightly sweet taste, cool power and the shape of the leaf is similar to a tongue of flame.]"

Clark's preface (line 82) says "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". The whole of line 1083 after the binomials is inside one pair of brackets, so it is the commentary layer: five names, habitat on tree trunks, a furry root, "slightly sweet taste, cool power" and a leaf shaped like a tongue of flame. Outside the brackets the line gives only the Tibetan name, the English "a type of Fern" and the two binomials. It prints no action.

The action stands two lines below, on the entry for the Tibetan tamarisk (line 1085, hom.bu): "Adiantum and Myricaria neutralize meat poison and compounded poison [and cure bile fevers. The external application of Myricaria is beneficial for sur.ya sores.16]". The words "Adiantum and Myricaria neutralize meat poison and compounded poison" are outside the brackets; the bile-fever clause is inside them, and it is not clear from the line whether it belongs to the fern, to the tamarisk or to both. Clark's name for the plant here is "Adiantum", the genus, and the Codex takes it to refer back to re.ral on that basis only. [USER: verify]

Line 1084, the next entry, reads: "ldum.bu.re.ral: Drynaria propinqua: Polystichum squarrosum: is said to be the 'king fern', whilst brags.spos (Lepisorus) is the 'queen fern' and brags.skya.ha.wo (Corallodiscus) is the 'minister fern']." It is printed with a closing bracket and no opening one. So line 1083 calls rgyal.po.re.ral ("king fern") and ldum.bu.re.ral two of re.ral's names, and line 1084 treats ldum.bu.re.ral as a drug of its own, with two other binomials. The text does not reconcile the two. Lines 1068 and 1069 give the "minister fern" and "queen fern" entries. Clark's compounding chapter, chapter 21 ("The Principles of Medicines: Compounding of Medicines", heading at line 1204), lists "Fern, Tibetan tamarisk" (line 1212) in the class that neutralizes poisons, without a binomial.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011.* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. Chapter 20, line 3456: "Rerel and Vombu cure meat poisoning and compounded poisoning." Line 3605 lists "Rerel, Vombu" in the group of medicines that cure poisoning. The index (line 7478) gives "Rerel 210,220". The Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients prints its columns in separate blocks. In the block at lines 5667 to 5683 there are six names in each column: the phonetic "Ranye Rechagpa Rekon Rerel Risho Ru-ta" (lines 5667 to 5668), the Tibetan "ra mnye, re lchag pa, re skon, re ral, ri sho, ru ta" (lines 5671 to 5674), the scientific "Polygonatum cirhifolium, Stellera chamaejasme, Corydalis nepalense, Polystichum squarros[um], Ligularia cymbulifera, Saussurea lappa" (line 5676) and the English "Solomon's seal ... Fern, Sheathing groundel, Costus" (lines 5678 to 5683). The names are not printed on one row. If the columns are read by position, the fourth name ("Rerel", "re ral") takes *Polystichum squarrosum* and the English "Fern", and not *Adiantum*; that binomial is the one Clark prints for ldum.bu.re.ral (line 1084). The Codex does not state the pairing. This is an identity doubt between Clark and the glossary. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Adiantum pedatum*, *re.ral*, *Rerel* and *Polystichum* found no entry on this drug. I_363 (*Adiantum lunulatum*, Hamsapadi, a Sushruta-attested maidenhair fern) is an entry on a different species of the same genus and carries no FMT tag. I_1019 (*Asplenium adiantum-nigrum*) and I_1020 (*Asplenium scolopendrium*) are Dioscorides entries whose binomial lists contain *Adiantum* names; neither is an entry on re.ral. None of them is an entry on re.ral.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Adiantum* (the maidenhair ferns) and *Polystichum* are genera of ferns, and that ferns reproduce by spores. Which species the Tibetan drug is, and what it contains, was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a fern of slightly sweet taste and cool power (Clark's commentary layer) that, with the Tibetan tamarisk, neutralizes meat poison and compounded poison (Clark line 1085; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3456). These are the texts' own claims and not Codex protocols. The botanical identity is unsettled, and the Codex gives no protocol for poisoning.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no part used (other than the "furry root" in Clark's description, which is a description and not a use), no quantity, frequency or duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica places the fern among the medicines that neutralize meat poison and compounded poison (line 1085) and in the class of poison-neutralisers (line 1212). The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this entry places re.ral in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice; the essence-extraction mentions in the herb section are for other plants (for example at lines 971 and 1121). The "king fern", "queen fern" and "minister fern" names (lines 1083, 1084) are names within the fern group and are not a cultivation grade. Its framing is therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is an antidote-class fern in the Tibetan texts, and nothing more can be said from them. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. The botanical identity is uncertain, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry, and suspected poisoning needs medical care and not a fern. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
