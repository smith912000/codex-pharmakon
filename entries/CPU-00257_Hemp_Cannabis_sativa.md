### Origin & Tradition

Cannabis is documented in seven of the eight ancient medical traditions (absent only from the Ebers Papyrus — though some researchers argue for identification in certain Egyptian botanical records). Its cultivation history traces to Central Asia (~10,000 BCE for fiber; medicinal use from ~4,000 BCE in China). The pharmacological identity of cannabis is now one of the most comprehensively researched in botanical medicine: over 140 identified cannabinoids, with CBD (cannabidiol) and THC (Δ9-tetrahydrocannabinol) as the primary bioactives operating through a dedicated mammalian endocannabinoid system (ECS) that modulates pain, inflammation, sleep, appetite, and anxiety. The discovery of the endocannabinoid system in the 1990s retroactively explained 6,000 years of empirical cross-traditional use — the plant evolved ligands that bind with extraordinary precision to receptors that mammals evolved independently. This entry covers the full botanical pharmacology; legal status varies by jurisdiction and is a patient/practitioner responsibility to verify.

**AT — Assyrian (Qunnabu in Nineveh tablets — cannabis as incense and medicine):** Cannabis (qunnabu — likely the linguistic origin of the English word "cannabis") appears in Assyrian medical texts as a medicine used by inhalation (incense/smoke), orally, and topically. Applications: pain relief; sedation; anti-anxiety; and as an incense for ritual/medicinal fumigation — one of the earliest documented cannabinoid delivery methods.

**DMM — De Materia Medica (Κάνναβις — Kannabis):** Dioscorides covers cannabis in Book III. He notes: the seed as digestive (the hemp seed oil/food application); juice from the green plant for earache; the "juice from the head" (the resin — THC-containing) causes "confusion of mind and strong hallucination" — the explicit psychoactive documentation. He recommends the seed oil for earache and skin preparations, showing the topical/enteral vs. inhaled distinction recognized in antiquity.

**CM — Canon of Medicine (قنب — Qannab):** Ibn Sina covers cannabis with characteristic medical rigor. He classifies it as hot and dry in the third degree. Applications: the seed (food-grade — high nutrition); the oil for earache; the resin preparation for pain and diarrhea; the smoking preparation for congestion. He explicitly documents the cognitive effects ("weakens reason and causes stupor") and warns against chronic use causing memory impairment — a remarkably prescient description of THC's amnestic effect and the risk of cannabis use disorder.

**SS — Sushruta Samhita / Charaka Samhita (Vijaya / Bhāṅg / Charas):** Cannabis has the most complex documentation in Ayurveda of any psychoactive plant. Three preparation forms are classically documented: Bhāṅg (the dried leaves and seeds — mildest; the traditional festival preparation of Holi); Ganja (dried flower tops — moderate THC); Charas (hashish/resin — most concentrated). Classification: Tikta rasa, Ushna virya, Vata-Kapha shamaka. Applications: Anandamaya (joy-giving — the literal ananda/bliss connection); Nidrajana (sleep-inducing); pain relief; anti-diarrheal; appetite stimulant; and — most specifically — the restriction that Vijaya should be used "only by those with strong constitutions, only in proper doses, and only under guidance of a physician." The Ayurvedic medical documentation is the most explicit in recognizing both therapeutic value and abuse potential.

**SBJ — Shennong Bencao Jing / Bencao Gangmu (大麻 — Dàmá — hemp; 大麻仁 — Dàmárén — hemp seed):** The Shennong Bencao Jing (the oldest Chinese pharmacopoeia, ~100 CE) documents hemp seed (*huǒ má rén* — 火麻仁) for constipation (the mild laxative seed oil effect) and as a Yin-nourishing food tonic. The psychoactive resin (*麻蕡 — má fén*) is documented separately with a specific warning: "If taken in excess, it makes people see demons." Li Shizhen in the Bencao Gangmu expands the hemp seed documentation as a Yin-tonic laxative and distinguishes clearly between the food-grade seed and the psychoactive preparations.

**FMT — Four Medical Tantras (Tibetan — documented as a potent mind-substance requiring control):** Cannabis appears in Tibetan medical texts as one of the powerful *dug* ("poison" that can be medicine at correct dose) substances. Applications include pain management and ritual/meditative contexts — consistent with the tantric tradition's use of specific plant substances in controlled sacred contexts.

**BM — Badianus Manuscript (Aztec):** While *Cannabis* is not native to the Americas, the Badianus Manuscript documents plants with convergent mind-altering and pain-relieving applications. The BM tag here reflects the cross-cultural convergence of psychoactive plant medicine rather than a direct *Cannabis* citation.

### Active Components

- **CBD (cannabidiol)** — non-psychoactive cannabinoid; antagonist/inverse agonist at CB1 receptor; TRPV1 agonist (capsaicin receptor pathway — anti-nociceptive); 5-HT1A agonist (anxiolytic); GPR55 antagonist (anti-seizure); anti-inflammatory via multiple pathways; neuroprotective; FDA-approved as Epidiolex for drug-resistant epilepsy; the primary therapeutic non-psychoactive cannabinoid
- **THC (Δ9-tetrahydrocannabinol)** — the primary psychoactive cannabinoid; CB1 partial agonist (CNS — psychoactive, analgesic, anti-emetic, appetite-stimulating) and CB2 partial agonist (peripheral — anti-inflammatory, immune modulation); FDA-approved as dronabinol/nabilone for chemotherapy nausea and AIDS wasting; analgesic (CB1-mediated); the compound responsible for both therapeutic potential and abuse/dependence risk
- **CBN (cannabinol)** — THC oxidation product; mild CB1 agonist; sedative (the compound increasing in aged cannabis); antibiotic activity against MRSA (Turner CE et al. 2008)
- **Terpenes (the "entourage effect" fraction):**
  - **Myrcene** — sedative, analgesic; β2 synergism with cannabinoids
  - **Limonene** — anxiolytic; antidepressant; 5-HT2A modulation
  - **Linalool** — GABA-A; anxiolytic (same as lavender/coriander)
  - **β-Caryophyllene** — selective CB2 agonist; anti-inflammatory (Gertsch J et al. 2008 *PNAS* — the dietary cannabinoid from black pepper also present in cannabis)
- **Hemp seed (non-psychoactive fraction):** 25–35% protein; complete amino acid profile; 30% fat (ALA + LA at near-ideal 1:3 omega-3:omega-6 ratio); significant zinc, magnesium; edestin and albumin proteins — the most digestible plant protein forms
- **Endocannabinoid system targets:** Anandamide (AEA) and 2-AG — the endogenous cannabinoids that cannabis-derived cannabinoids mimic; the ECS regulates pain, inflammation, appetite, sleep, stress, and immune function

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**1. Sleep support ([SLEEP] — CBD + low-dose THC)**
CBD at 25–150mg/day reduces sleep onset latency and increases deep sleep (NREM stage 3) without the next-day impairment of benzodiazepines. Low-dose THC (≤5mg) can reduce sleep onset but may suppress REM sleep at higher doses (the reason THC-dominant cannabis disrupts dream sleep). For practitioners in [SLEEP] phase with training-related insomnia (cortisol dysregulation, pain-mediated), CBD is first-line botanical; low-dose THC may be added for pain-mediated insomnia in jurisdictions where legal.

**2. Anti-inflammatory and pain management ([RESET])**
The CB2-mediated peripheral anti-inflammatory effect (CBD + β-caryophyllene) reduces exercise-induced inflammatory cytokines without the GI risk of NSAIDs. CBD's TRPV1 mechanism provides analgesic activity particularly effective for neuropathic and inflammatory pain. Professional athletic bodies' evolving CBD policies (WADA removed CBD from the prohibited list in 2018) reflect growing acceptance.

**3. Anti-anxiety and nervous system recovery ([CYCLE-OFF])**
CBD's 5-HT1A agonism provides anxiolytic activity with clinical RCT support (Bergamaschi MM et al. 2011 *Neuropsychopharmacology* — CBD reduced simulated public speaking anxiety vs. placebo). For practitioners in [CYCLE-OFF] managing overtraining-related anxiety and HPA axis dysregulation, CBD provides a non-addictive anxiolytic option.

**4. Hemp seed nutrition ([BUILD] — food-grade)**
Hemp seed provides a nutritionally exceptional complete protein (all essential amino acids) with an ideal omega-3:6 ratio. For practitioners requiring high-quality plant protein with anti-inflammatory fat profile, hemp seeds are one of the most nutritionally complete plant foods. No psychoactive activity — purely nutritive.

### Preparation & Dosing

**CBD oil/isolate (therapeutic — sleep/anxiety/anti-inflammatory):**
25–150mg CBD daily; oil format allows sublingual absorption (higher bioavailability than capsules — 13–35% vs. 6–15%); start low (25mg) and titrate; take 1 hour before sleep for sleep applications; morning for anxiety applications. Product quality: third-party tested, Certificate of Analysis confirming <0.3% THC (legal threshold in most jurisdictions).

**Hemp seed (food-grade nutrition):**
3–4 tablespoons (30–40g) hulled hemp hearts added to smoothie, yogurt, or salad daily. The food-dose for protein and omega-3 contribution. No THC or CBD at meaningful concentrations in properly processed hulled hemp seeds.

**Bhāṅg preparation (traditional Ayurvedic — low-THC leaves):**
Traditional Ayurvedic preparation using dried cannabis leaves (lowest THC content) in milk with spices (cardamom, saffron, rose) — the traditional Hindu festival/medicinal preparation. Lower potency than resin preparations. This is a traditional documentation — legal status varies.

### Scientific Correlation

- **Endocannabinoid system discovery:** Devane WA et al. (1992) *Science* 258:1946-1949: anandamide identified as the first endogenous cannabinoid ligand; the discovery establishing the ECS; provided the molecular basis for all traditional cannabis pharmacology
- **CBD epilepsy (FDA basis):** Devinsky O et al. (2017) *N Engl J Med* 376:2011-2020: CBD (Epidiolex) significantly reduced monthly convulsive seizure frequency vs. placebo in Dravet syndrome (RCT, n=120); the pivotal trial establishing CBD as a pharmaceutical
- **CBD anxiety:** Bergamaschi MM et al. (2011) *Neuropsychopharmacology* 36:1219-1226: CBD (600mg acute dose) significantly reduced anxiety in simulated public speaking test vs. placebo in social anxiety disorder patients; 5-HT1A mechanism
- **β-Caryophyllene CB2:** Gertsch J et al. (2008) *Proc Natl Acad Sci* 105:9099-9104: β-caryophyllene identified as a selective CB2 agonist; anti-inflammatory activity confirmed; the first dietary cannabinoid identified (present in cannabis, black pepper, cloves, hops)
- **MRSA (CBN):** Appendino G et al. (2008) *J Nat Prod* 71:1427-1430: five major cannabinoids (including CBN) demonstrated potent antibacterial activity against MRSA strains at concentrations below current antibiotics; membrane disruption mechanism

### Cultivation Register

*This entry concerns the **seed** — *ma zi* 麻子, *ma zi ren* / *huo ma ren* 麻子仁, the achene. Cultivation claims attaching to the flowering tops, the resin, or any psychoactive preparation belong to a different plant part and are **not** transferred here. Where a tradition's claim attaches to a different part, that is stated explicitly rather than quietly carried across.*

**Assyrian (AT).** Absent. The blocking warning on Assyrian substance identifications, deity and demon attributions and tablet references is in force, and nothing retrieved lifts it for hemp seed. No cultivation status is claimed.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Hemp seed (*shahdānaj*) is attested in the Unani materia medica, but **no substance-specific cultivation status is attested in the academic sources retrieved**. The tradition's cultivation frame is the doctrine of **innate heat and radical moisture** — a finite vital endowment whose depletion *is* ageing, so that longevity practice here is **conservation, not acquisition**. Whether hemp seed conserves or spends that endowment is not something the sources retrieved say, and it is not guessed at. Note also that the Unani narcotic *banj* is a separate drug entirely and nothing from it is imported.

**Nahua / Badianus (BM).** No cultivation framing is attested. The *tonalli* / *teyolia* / *ihiyotl* model is **not in the Badianus manuscript**, which is naturalistic; it is a reconstruction from later ethnohistory (López Austin), and that qualification is repeated every time. Hemp is an Old World plant and no Nahua vitality assignment for its seed survives in sources meeting this project's standard. The pre-Columbian hot/cold versus post-conquest Galenic question is left unresolved.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, so no cultivation status is available to assign — this is not a gap in the retrieval but a fact about the text. What the tradition offers a cultivator is a **discipline of attention**: knowing the seed's provenance, its season, its condition by sight, smell and taste, and whether the oil in it has turned. For an oil-bearing seed that goes rancid, that discipline is the whole of the practical question.

**Egyptian (EP).** Absent. The corpus is indication-organised — it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to a person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib* — and substance identification within it is a modern hypothesis. No Egyptian cultivation framing for hemp seed is attested, and *heka*, real where attested, is not attested here.

**Chinese / Shennong (SBJ).** This is where the greatest care is needed, because the tradition's famous cultivation language attaches to **a different part of the plant**. Brand and Zhao (*Frontiers in Pharmacology* 8:108, 2017) distinguish ***ma fen*** 麻蕡 — the immature female inflorescence or mature infructescence, i.e. the flowering tops — from ***huo ma ren* / *ma zi ren*** 麻子仁, the achenes. The *Shennong bencao jing* passage that cultivators reach for — excessive consumption causing one to see ghosts and run about frenetically, and prolonged consumption freeing the spirit light and lightening the body (通神明輕身) — is recorded of ***ma fen***, the flowering tops. **It is not a claim about the seed and is not applied to the seed here.** ⚠️ Note too that *qing shen* 輕身, "lighten the body," has **no pharmacological equivalent** and is never translated into one. The seed itself is recorded as a non-toxic food-grade substance and a moistening laxative, the basis of the classical Cannabis Seed Pill for constipation (Brand and Zhao 2017). **No *san pin* 三品 grade for the seed could be confirmed** in academic sources meeting this project's standard — the "upper grade" attribution circulates widely but was traceable only to commercial TCM sites, which are refused — so the seed carries **no attested cultivation status here, only a therapeutic and dietary one**.

*Dating the Daoist material.* The association of hemp with adepts and transcendence is real but **later than the Han classic and attached to the flower**: Brand and Zhao record that **Tao Hongjing, sixth century CE**, noted that adepts take cannabis *flower* with ginseng and thereby know of things that have not yet come — and that **Li Shizhen (*Bencao gangmu*, 1596) dismissed this as an overstatement**. So: sixth-century, about the flower, and disputed within the tradition itself by the sixteenth. None of it is evidence about *ma zi ren*.

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Vijaya* is attested in the Ayurvedic materia medica, but the classification recorded above — *Tama*-increasing at high dose, *Nidrājana*, *Ānandamaya* — describes the **psychoactive leaf and flower preparation, not the seed**, and is not transferred to it. For the seed specifically, **no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* membership is attested** in sources meeting this project's standard, and no *ojas* claim is made. (*Ojas* is recorded only in its own register.) ⚠️ The *sattva/rajas/tamas* triad is a **Samkhya** category used alongside *dravyaguna*, not a member of it, and the popular sattvic-food lists derive from the Gita and later material rather than from Sushruta.

**Tibetan (FMT).** No cultivation status is attested for hemp seed, and **no formal category of "substances for subtle-body practice" exists** in this tradition to place it in; none is invented. The system's own framing: the three *nyes pa* derive from the three poisons, so pathology is at root a spiritual condition, and medicine is the third of four treatment branches — it supports practice, it does not reach the root.

**Practitioner's note.** Hemp seed is a food and a bulk-forming, moistening laxative grain, and across eight traditions that is very nearly all it is. The cultivation reputation of *Cannabis* is real, historically documented, and belongs to the flower — it does not transfer to the seed by botanical adjacency, and a cultivator who eats *ma zi ren* expecting the *ma fen* literature has misread the register. What is actually available here is unglamorous and worth having: a nutritious oil-bearing seed, gentle on the bowel, safe as food, with no long-term status claimed for it by any tradition tagged in this entry. Buy it fresh, keep it cold, and expect nothing of it beyond nutrition and regularity.

### Contraindications

- **Psychosis / schizophrenia spectrum / family history** — THC is a known precipitant of psychotic episodes in genetically susceptible individuals (CB1 receptor modulation of dopamine in mesolimbic pathways); absolute contraindication for THC-containing preparations in those with personal or family history of psychotic disorder
- **Adolescent use** — ECS maturation continues to age 25; THC exposure during adolescent brain development associated with increased risk of psychiatric disorders (Caspi A et al. 2005 *Biol Psychiatry*); not appropriate for under-25 use except in clear medical necessity
- **Driving / machinery operation** — THC impairs psychomotor function, reaction time, and judgement; do not drive or operate heavy machinery with THC preparations; CBD does not impair driving at standard doses
- **Anticoagulants** — CBD inhibits CYP2C9 (warfarin metabolism) at doses above 300mg/day; significant INR elevation risk; monitor closely
- **WADA and sports testing** — WADA removed CBD from the Prohibited List in 2018; THC remains prohibited in-competition; hemp seed consumption does not produce positive tests; CBD isolate products with <0.3% THC do not produce THC positive tests at standard doses (but full-spectrum products can)
- **Dependence (THC)** — cannabis use disorder affects approximately 9% of users (lifetime); 17% of adolescent initiators; withdrawal syndrome (irritability, sleep disruption, appetite loss) is real but not life-threatening; not appropriate for those with substance use disorder history without specialist oversight
