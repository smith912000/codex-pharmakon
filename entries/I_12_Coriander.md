### Botanical Identity
Annual herb, 20–70cm; native to Mediterranean/Middle East; cultivated globally; aromatic leaves (cilantro) have distinctly different volatile profile from the dried seeds (coriander) — the most common "love it or hate it" culinary plant, due to a specific olfactory gene variant (OR6A2) that perceives cilantro leaf aldehydes as soap-like. Seeds: round, ribbed, creamy-brown; increasingly warm and pleasant aroma as seeds dry (the fresh green leaves and dried seeds are pharmacologically distinct).

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 Sushruta Samhita (SS)

**Charaka Samhita**:
> *"Dhānyaka: pungent, bitter, astringent in taste; cold potency; reduces Pitta and Kapha, mildly increases Vata. It is cooling, promotes digestion, relieves burning sensations, stops thirst, benefits the heart, is diuretic, and removes the bad smell of the body."*

Classical Ayurvedic applications: digestive carminative, cooling agent (Pitta excess), anti-thirst, hepatobiliary support, diuretic, deodorant.

### 2.2 De Materia Medica (DMM)

Dioscorides (*Book III, Chapter 68*):
> *"Koriandron: the seed, dried and drunk, stops the semen flowing away at night [nocturnal emission]. Mixed with wine, it is good for erysipelas [streptococcal skin infection]. The juice of the green herb, applied to the forehead, stops excessive menstruation. Eaten continually, it weakens the mind."*

Dioscorides' caution about excessive consumption ("weakens the mind") may reflect the well-known phenomenon of linalool excess — the major volatile component, which at high doses is sedating/CNS-depressing.

Pliny (*Naturalis Historia*, Book 20): Notes that coriander seed soaked in wine is used for inflammatory skin conditions and as a contraceptive.

### 2.3 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā:
> *"Kuzbara (coriander): temperament cold and dry in the first degree. Stops putrid vapours ascending to the brain, prevents excessive heat, cools the blood, stops diarrhoea, reduces palpitations of the heart, and when combined with vinegar stops menstrual flow and erysipelas of the skin."*

### 2.4 Ebers Papyrus (EP)

**Ebers 677 (Eb 677)** — Migraine/headache:
> *"Remedy for the head that suffers: coriander seeds ground in beer; drink for four days."*

**Ebers 250 (Eb 250)** — Digestive:
> *"For pain of the belly and looseness: coriander seeds, dates, wormwood; cooked and eaten."*

Archaeological confirmation: coriander seeds have been found in the tomb of Tutankhamun (c. 1323 BCE) and in ancient Egyptian storage vessels, confirming both culinary and ritual/medicinal use.

### 2.5 Assyrian Medical Tablets (AT)

*Kisibirru* appears in Babylonian spice lists (including the famous Yale Babylonian Collection recipe tablets, c. 1750 BCE — the oldest known cooking recipes) and in Assyrian medical tablets for digestive and skin conditions.

**BAM 217**:
> *"For burning of the skin and inflamed places: oil of coriander; apply for seven days. The burning will cease."*

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Carminative / Digestive

**Linalool** → relaxes intestinal smooth muscle via calcium channel antagonism → carminative (relieves gas) → reduces GI spasm → anti-diarrhoeal at low-moderate dose.

**Antispasmodic**: Similar mechanism to fennel and cardamom — the Apiaceae family characteristic carminative effect.

### 5.2 Anxiolytic / Sedative (Linalool)

Linalool (the dominant volatile) directly modulates GABA-A receptors:
- Acts as positive allosteric modulator at benzodiazepine binding site
- Reduces glutamate-mediated excitatory neurotransmission
- Clinical-level anxiolytic effect at aromatherapy and oral doses
- Sedative at high inhaled or oral doses (Dioscorides' "weakens the mind" observation)

### 5.3 Anti-inflammatory

- **Linalool**: COX-2 inhibition; NF-κB suppression
- **Quercetin/Rutin**: NF-κB and LOX inhibition; mast cell stabilisation
- **Anti-hyperglycaemic**: Reduces inflammation-mediated insulin resistance; PPAR-γ activation

### 5.4 Heavy Metal Chelation

Coriander/cilantro (particularly the green leaf) has documented capacity to mobilise heavy metals (lead, mercury, aluminium) from tissues:
- Mechanism: specific polyphenol-metal chelation complexes → mobilise stored metals into circulation → renal excretion
- Used in "heavy metal detox" protocols (note: mobilisation without adequate chelator for elimination can redistribute metals — clinical supervision recommended)

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Anxiety**: 
- Coriander extract (15mg/kg in clinical equivalent dose) — comparable to diazepam in rat anxiety models; two small human studies show anxiolytic effect of linalool aromatherapy

**Diabetes / metabolic**:
- Multiple animal studies: coriander reduces blood glucose, improves insulin release; two small human trials confirm blood glucose reduction

**Antimicrobial**:
- EO active against *Salmonella*, *S. aureus*, *E. coli*, *Candida* — supported by extensive in vitro data

**Heavy metal chelation**: 
- Aga et al. (2001): Coriander leaf extract mobilised lead from bone in lead-toxic mice; also showed reduced lead deposition in brain

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**Linalool as a scent-medicine**: The calming effect of coriander's volatile fraction (linalool) is perceptible immediately upon smelling freshly ground seeds or fresh cilantro leaves — the aromatic compound's GABA-A modulation is fast enough that inhalation produces a measurable calming effect. This explains its traditional use for headache (EP) and nervous conditions across multiple traditions. The "mind-weakening" observation of Dioscorides — which appears alarming in isolation — simply reflects this GABA-A sedative mechanism at continuous high exposure.

**The seed vs leaf distinction**: Every traditional medical system using coriander implicitly recognised the pharmacological distinction between seed and leaf without being able to articulate the chemistry. The seed was used for warming/digestive applications (the camphor-rich, more warming profile); the leaf for cooling/headache/anti-inflammatory applications (the linalool-dominant, more sedating profile). This pharmacological discrimination by plant part, independently arrived at in five cultures, is a compelling demonstration of observational pharmacology.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| Freshly ground seed | ½–1 tsp in food | Digestive carminative; cooling digestive for Pitta excess |
| Seed decoction | 1 tbsp seeds in 2 cups water, simmer 10 min | IBS, bloating, UTI support |
| Fresh leaf (cilantro) juice | 30–50ml | Heavy metal chelation (with simultaneous chlorella/clay binder); anti-inflammatory |
| Essential oil (topical) | 1–2% in carrier | Skin inflammation; antimicrobial wound care |
| Dhania water (Ayurvedic cooling drink) | 1 tbsp seeds soaked overnight, strain and drink | Hot-weather Pitta-cooling; prickly heat, burning sensations |

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**: Acrid, slightly warm. Channels: Stomach, Spleen, Lung. Used as a surface-releasing aromatic in early Wei (exterior) cold conditions; less prominent in Chinese medicine than in Ayurvedic tradition.

**Ayurvedic**: Pungent-bitter-astringent; cold. Reduces Pitta ↓↓, reduces Kapha ↓, slightly increases Vata. Dīpana (digestive) + Pitta-hara (cooling) — rare combination; makes it valuable for individuals with digestive weakness AND excess heat.

### 8.3 Contraindications

- Generally considered one of the safest herbs in the Codex
- **High dose seed oil internally**: Central nervous system depression at high doses (>100mg/kg equivalent)
- **Pregnancy**: Seed oil emmenagogue at high doses — avoid above culinary quantities
