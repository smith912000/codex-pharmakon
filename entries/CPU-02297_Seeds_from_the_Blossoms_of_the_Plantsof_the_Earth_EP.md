# Seeds-from-the-Blossoms-of-the-Plants-of-the-Earth — Bryan's English rendering of a Papyrus Ebers ingredient (printed "Plantsof" in the OCR)

## *A descriptive label, not a species: Bryan (1930) lists it among the "recognizable Plants" of the Papyrus Ebers and prints no Egyptian word and no binomial against it. Identification: unidentified; the label describes a part (seeds taken from blossoms) of plants in general. Basis: Bryan's list and one narrative passage. **Papyrus Ebers (EP).** The label appears in two places in Bryan's text (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`):

1. **Bryan's list of plants.** Bryan introduces the plant list with a remark that many substances "are completely hidden from us under the guise of meaningless words", and that "of the recognizable Plants, and of their preparations, there is a not inconsiderable showing as the following list will reveal" (line 1233). In the alphabetical list the label stands at line 1333, as its own head word between *Saffron* (with *Saffron-seeds* and *Blossom-of-the-Saffron*, line 1331) and *Spring-plant* (line 1335): "Seeds-from-the-Blossoms-of-the-Plantsof-the-Earth". The OCR has run "Plants" and "of" together. The row in `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` (row for this head word, "Bryan 1930, Ch. VI Plant Remedies (line 1333)") records Bryan's English rendering only. The extract's README notes that identifications are Bryan's English renderings only, with no Egyptian word and no binomial.
2. **A narrative passage.** In Bryan's account of the treatment of the "uha-disease" (under the running head "THE AAA DISEASE" first printed at line 3213), a run of salves is described for the "woodlike uha-disease" (lines 3292 to 3300). Bryan ends the run with this sentence: "Finally there remained a salve calculated to arouse feelings of homicide in every poetic breast; a salve which sought to combine Seeds-from-the-Blossoms-of-the-Earth and Fresh Cream with the Hoof-of-an-Ass!" (line 3300; the OCR prints "ofthe-Earth"). So the ingredient is named in a salve with fresh cream and an ass's hoof, and Bryan's tone about it is that of a sceptical commentator, not a translator's neutral rendering.

That is all the Ebers text on disk says. The label gives no plant, no part beyond "seeds from the blossoms", no habitat, no preparation, no dose and no indication other than the one salve above. Bryan's running head for the disease ("THE AAA DISEASE") and the Egyptian term he renders "uha" are his; this entry does not translate them into a modern diagnosis. The wording "of the Earth" is also seen in Bryan's "Oil-of-the-Earth (? Petroleum)" (line 1194, and line 2572 "Another Oil-of-the-Earth (? Petroleum)"); in the sources read nothing explains it, and no inference is drawn between the two labels. [USER: verify against the printed Bryan edition]
