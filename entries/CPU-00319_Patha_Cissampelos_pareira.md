### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta & Caraka — the great binder.** Pāṭhā is one of the most widely-cited single drugs of the classical corpus, and its signature is *saṅgrāhaṇa* — the astringent binding and consolidation of loose, over-flowing conditions. Caraka names Pāṭhā among his *saṅgrāhaṇīya* (bowel-binding / anti-diarrhoeal) drugs, and it is a pillar of the treatment of *atisāra* (diarrhoea) and *grahaṇī* (sprue/IBS-type malabsorption), where a bitter that both scrapes *āma* and firms the stool is exactly what is required. Suśruta ranks Ambaṣṭhā (a Pāṭhā-group root) among the *sandhānīya* and *vraṇaśodhana* drugs — those that promote union/healing and cleanse wounds — and Pāṭhā recurs in his fever, poison, and gynaecological formulae. In the great compound *Ṣaḍaṅgapānīya* and countless *jvarahara* decoctions Pāṭhā appears as the antipyretic-digestive corrective.

**Gynaecological reputation.** Pāṭhā holds a special place in classical prasūti-tantra (obstetrics): it is famed as a *garbhāśaya*-acting drug used to correct disordered uterine function, ease difficult labour and expel a retained placenta — the "uterine" reputation the modern literature echoes. This dual identity — bowel-binder and uterus-mover — is not a contradiction to the tradition but two faces of a single *tīkṣṇa*, channel-directing action.

**Curare kinship (the New World).** *Cissampelos pareira* is a genuine pan-tropical plant, and its Amazonian and African relatives in the Menispermaceae are the botanical source of tubocurarine-type arrow poisons (curare). The genus thus sits at the crossroads of medicine and toxin — the same bisbenzylisoquinoline alkaloid chemistry that paralyses a hunted animal, at low dose and different congener, relaxes smooth muscle and modulates the immune and allergic response.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Two-Handed Root:** Pāṭhā is an emblem of *directed force*. One hand binds (the loose bowel, the bleeding, the leaking channel); the other releases (the labouring uterus, the retained placenta, the obstructed flow). The very genus that yields curare — the arrow that stills a living body — yields in Pāṭhā a medicine that stills disordered flux. The doctrinal reading is that *stambhana* (arresting) and *anulomana* (releasing) are not opposites but the same mastery over movement applied in opposite directions; the healer, like the hunter, must know exactly where to aim. In Kenetica Pāṭhā is filed among the **flow-governors** — pharmaka whose gift is not adding or removing but *regulating* the rate and direction of a channel, and whose curare shadow is the standing reminder that a flow-governor mishandled becomes a flow-stopper.

### Active Components

- **Bisbenzylisoquinoline alkaloids** — hayatin, hayatinine, cissampareine, and (in the genus) warifteine / methylwarifteine, curine, tetrandrine-type congeners; the muscle-relaxant, anti-allergic, immunomodulatory and antimalarial core.
- **Aporphine alkaloids** — cissampeline (bebeerine), magnoflorine, and related.
- **Quaternary alkaloids** underlying the neuromuscular/curare-adjacent effects.
- Sterols, flavonoids, tannins (the astringent principle behind saṅgrāhī action), and essential-oil traces.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Patha is filed as the **flow-governor / consolidation pharmakon** of the Binding tier. Primary Kenetica role: to firm and consolidate loose, over-flowing states — diarrhoea, dysentery, malabsorptive *grahaṇī*, excessive bleeding — while simultaneously clearing the *āma* and heat that drive them (it binds *and* cleanses, not merely plugs). Secondary role: as a directed uterine and smooth-muscle modulator under expert supervision. Indicated in the loose, hot, leaking, kapha-pitta-with-āma picture. Because its alkaloid chemistry is curare-adjacent (smooth-muscle and, in the genus, neuromuscular activity), it is a *steered* agent — dosed low, aimed precisely, never scattered.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the pharmacology is documented largely at genus level with *C. pareira* as the most-studied species:

- Semwal DK, Semwal RB, Vermaak I, Viljoen A, 2014, *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* — comprehensive ethnobotanical/phytochemical/pharmacological review of *Cissampelos* (Menispermaceae). It documents, confirmed in vitro and/or in vivo across the genus (with *C. pareira* most explored): **antimalarial, antidiarrhoeal, anti-inflammatory, analgesic/antipyretic, bronchodilator, anti-allergic, immunomodulatory, hepatoprotective, antidiabetic, muscle-relaxant, antifertility and antivenom** activity, and identifies the bisbenzylisoquinoline alkaloids (warifteine, methylwarifteine, hayatin, hayatidin, berberine) as key actives — while also flagging the genus's curare/arrow-poison toxic use. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2014.06.054)

This review directly corroborates the three headline traditional uses (anti-diarrhoeal, antimalarial, uterine/smooth-muscle) and the alkaloid basis. **Evidence grade: MODERATE for breadth of preclinical signal, LOW for confirmatory human trials** — most activity is in vitro/in vivo; robust human RCTs are lacking. (Note: *C. pareira* has been investigated as an antimalarial candidate; readers should treat any clinical-efficacy claim as `[USER: verify]` against current trial data.) Attribution: PubMed.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Tikta (bitter), Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry), Tīkṣṇa (sharp)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** *Saṅgrāhī-prabhāva* — a defining astringent, flow-consolidating action on the bowel disproportionate to taste; plus a directional smooth-muscle/uterine effect that is the classical prabhāva of Ambaṣṭhā-Pāṭhā.
- **Doṣa action:** ↓ Kapha, ↓ Pitta (bitter, saṅgrāhī); can ↑ Vāta if overused (dry, binding). *Kapha-pitta-śāmaka.*
- **Karma (actions):** Saṅgrāhaṇa/Stambhana (binding, anti-diarrhoeal), Dīpana–Pācana, Āmapācana, Jvarahara (antipyretic), Krimighna, Viṣaghna (anti-poison), Vraṇaśodhana (wound-cleansing), Garbhāśaya-śodhana (uterine-corrective), Kaṇḍūghna (anti-pruritic).
- **Suśruta gaṇa membership:** Ambaṣṭhā (the Pāṭhā-group root) is enumerated in Suśruta's **Ambaṣṭhādi gaṇa** and the *sandhānīya/saṅgrāhaṇīya* groupings — its signature classificatory home as a binding, wound-uniting drug. Caraka lists Pāṭhā among *saṅgrāhaṇīya* and febrifuge drugs.

### Contraindications

Pregnancy — **contraindicated / use only under expert supervision** (uterine-acting, classical use to expel placenta and induce labour; antifertility activity documented in the genus). Neuromuscular caution: curare-kin alkaloid chemistry — avoid combining with neuromuscular blockers or in myasthenia/anaesthetic settings without medical oversight. High Vāta, constipation, dryness — the binding/drying action can aggravate. Not for the depleted or dehydrated in excess. Alkaloid overdose risk — respect the low therapeutic window; not a casual daily herb.
