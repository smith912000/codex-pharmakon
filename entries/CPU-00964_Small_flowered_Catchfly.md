The related campions in the text are these two.

*3-114 LUCHNIS STEPHANOMATIKE* (heading at line 7558). The translator's identification is "Lychnis — Campion, Lamp Flower, Maltese Cross", with *Lychnis coeli-rosa* ("Rose of Heaven") and *L. coronaria* ("Rose Campion, Mullein Pink"). The text (line 7563) reads: "Lychnis has a flower similar to a white violet but almost purple, interwoven into little crowns, the seed of which (taken in a drink with wine) helps those bitten by scorpions." The names given include athanates, aquilonium, vallarium, geranopodium, corymbion, taurion, sceptrum and maloion, and "the Magi call it the blood of a menstrual woman". Dioscorides records the Magi's name and does not say that he endorses it.

*3-115 LUCHNIS AGRIA* (heading at line 7564). The translator's identification is *Lychnis viscaria*, "Viscid Campion". The text (line 7567) reads: "Lychnis sylvestris is similar to the cultivated in all things. Two teaspoons of a decoction of the seed (taken as a drink) expels bilious matter through the intestines and helps those touched by scorpions. They say that when this herb is laid near scorpions they become numb and unable to hurt." The names given include tragonoton, atocion, hieracopodion and lampas.

These two chapters are the nearest Dioscorides text to the genus and are reported here for completeness. They are not attributed to *Silene gallica*. Both *L. coeli-rosa* and *L. viscaria* are generally treated by some botanists as *Silene* species (as *Silene coeli-rosa* and *Silene viscaria*), and that change of genus is not stated in the text read. [USER: verify] The small-flowered catchfly itself is a different, small-flowered annual.
