### Active Components

Droppings of a small rodent are dried faecal pellets: undigested plant fibre, bacterial and intestinal debris, and whatever the animal has eaten. They are a biological waste product and not a standardised drug. The sources read give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. No Tibetan text read says what was done with flying squirrel droppings, so nothing can be said of their use in this tradition. Faecal material carries infection risk (see Contraindications). The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation.

### Contraindications

Animal droppings can carry bacteria, parasites and viruses, and dried droppings raise infectious dust; they should not be swallowed or handled without hygiene precautions. Flying squirrels are wild animals, and the legal and conservation status of *Trogopterus xanthipes* has not been checked here.
