# Tadpole — "Tadpole", "Dried-Tadpoles-from-the-Canal" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus)

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** On the project's spine (CPU-02393) only the Ebers Papyrus is attested for the tadpole, through Cyril P. Bryan's 1930 English version. The row began on 13 October 2026 as an unverified placeholder from `_nonplant/nonplant_master.csv` (curated 8 October, confidence clear); this entry replaces it. The project already has an entry for the Ebers frog (`III_314_Frog.md`), which is a separate row; nothing here equates the two.

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The Ebers material reaches this Codex through Bryan's *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). In his Chapter VII, "Organic Remedies", Bryan says that a complete list of the animal materia medica "would not be possible" because the identity of many of the animals is "still beyond us", and then lists the animals "whose identity is not in doubt"; the second line of the printed list reads "Ass Hog Dog Cat Frog Tadpole Scorpion Eel ..." (line 1404; `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` row for "Tadpole", described there as "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list").

The only prescription found by name is in Bryan's discussion of the hair, under the remedies for grey hair. After the "Horn-of-a-Fawn, warmed in Oil", he writes: "For the 'Bile-of-Many-Crabs' conjures up a messy mass, and a smelly one too; and 'Dried-Tadpoles-from-the-Canal, crushed in Oil' does not sound much better. Both of course were applied to the outside of the head." (lines 4055-4056, on a page headed "THE HAIR"). The quoted words in the second quotation marks are Bryan's rendering of an Ebers ingredient line; the papyrus's own column and line are not given in what was read, and the Egyptian wording is not given. ### Active Components

Bryan names none. In general terms a tadpole is a small aquatic larva, mostly water and protein, and, in some species, the skin of tadpoles carries defensive chemicals. The specific tadpole is not identified, and no analysis was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** An external hair remedy: dried tadpoles from the canal, crushed in oil, applied to the outside of the head for grey hair (lines 4055-4056).
- **What the text does not say.** No quantity, no duration and no incantation is attached to it in the lines read.

The Codex gives no protocol. The remedy is a record of what the Ebers physicians tried for greying hair, and nothing supports it.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparation, as Bryan gives it, is: dried; from the canal; crushed in oil; applied to the head. No quantity is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Greying of hair results from loss of pigment cells in the follicle and is not reversed by applying oil or animal material. Wild amphibians can carry *Salmonella*, and the skin of some tadpoles and adult amphibians carries toxins. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene.** Wild animal material from a canal can carry infection; do not apply it to the skin.
- **Toxins.** Some amphibian larvae and adults carry skin toxins.
- **Conservation.** Amphibians are declining in many places and many species are protected; wild collection can be illegal.
- **Identity.** The species is unknown. [USER: verify]
