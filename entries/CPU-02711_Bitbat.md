# Bitbat — بطباط (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II; also *ʿaṣā al-rāʿī*, 'shepherd's staff')

**Avicenna, *Canon* of Medicine (CM).** (Canon Book II, entry *Baṭbāṭ*, Arabic text line 11494 and entry *ʿAṣā al-rāʿī*, line 16428–16436). At line 11494 the whole entry is: "it is the shepherd's staff, and we shall give the properties of the shepherd's staff in the chapter of the letter *ʿayn*". At line 16428 the entry *ʿaṣā al-rāʿī* reads: "it is the *baṭbāṭ*, male and female, its male the stronger". *Properties:* it has astringency but the watery part in it is large, and because it so strongly repels descending matters it is thought drying; it likewise prevents haemorrhage. *Swellings:* as a plaster for phlegmon, erysipelas and the creeping eruption called *namla*, it is very beneficial for swollen sores. *Wounds:* it heals fresh wounds very well. *Head:* its juice kills ear worms and dries ear sores. *Chest:* its water benefits spitting of blood. *Digestive:* as a cooling plaster for inflammation of the stomach. *Excretion:* it stops bleeding from the womb and heals sores of the intestines, and the entry then breaks off at "Dioscorides claimed that it …" (زعم ديسقوريدوس أنه يدر), so the Dioscorides claim is lost in this e-text.
