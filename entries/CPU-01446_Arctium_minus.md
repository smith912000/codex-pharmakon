# Arctium minus — Arktion (Dioscorides 4-106)

## *Arctium minus (the translators' suggestion; "Lesser Burdock")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) printed on the chapter's SUGGESTED line, not Dioscorides' own identification, and this row was added from that line (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) without being verified against POWO. [USER: verify] The existing entry I_514 (Burdock, *Arctium lappa*) overlaps it: Dioscorides separates a smaller burdock here (4-106) from a greater one in the next chapter (4-107, Arkeion, whose translators' line is *Arctium lappa* and related binomials with "see 4-106"). The two are two chapters of one author and are two rows on the spine. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading at line 8974.

*4-106. ARKTION.* "Arctium (which some call arcturum) has leaves similar to verbascum [4-104] but rougher and rounder; the tender root is sweet and white; and the soft stalk long like little cumin. The root and seed of this (soaked in wine) have the strength (held in the mouth) to soothe toothache. It is a poultice for burns and chilblains. It is also drunk in wine for sciatica and dysuria." The index prints ARKTION at p.656.

*Doubled passage in the preceding chapter, 4-105 AITHIOPIS (line 8973).* The Aethiopis chapter as printed carries, after its own description, the sentences "The root of this is called arcturum, and it has leaves similar to verbascum, only rougher and rounder. The root is tender, sweet and white; and the stalk is soft and long, similar to little cumin. The root and seed of this (boiled in wine) are held in the mouth to lessen toothache; and it is applied with hot cloths for burns and chilblains. It is taken as a drink in wine for sciatica and painful urination." This repeats the 4-106 text almost word for word, and appears to be a textual intrusion in the translation. It is noted and not attributed to 4-105's own plant. [USER: verify]

*4-107 ARKEION (line 8989).* The greater burdock: leaves like colocynthis, a large root white within and black without, a drink with pine kernels for spitters of blood and corrupt matter, and a smear for sores and old ulcers. It is reported only to mark the neighbouring chapter and belongs to the Burdock entry.

### Active Components

Plants of the daisy family (Asteraceae) to which burdock belongs are generally known for polysaccharides such as inulin in the root. The composition of the plant in this chapter was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records the root and seed held in the mouth for toothache, a poultice for burns and chilblains, and a drink in wine for sciatica and dysuria. These are the text's claims, not a Codex protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

4-106 gives the root and seed "soaked in wine" and held in the mouth; a poultice; and a drink in wine. No quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints "sweet" for the root. No other quality is printed for 4-106.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter offers a cultivator is the discipline of attention that the cultivation spec describes for this tradition: how to know the plant by its parts, where it grows, what it is called, and what is claimed for it. 4-106 describes the plant against verbascum (rougher, rounder leaves), the sweet white tender root and the long soft stalk, gives its uses, and notes that some call it arcturum. It assigns no status. The identification with *A. minus* is the translators', and the greater burdock is a separate chapter. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The chapter is a plain remedy note, and the useful part is the care in telling the lesser burdock from the greater.

### Contraindications

Burdock-type plants can cause contact dermatitis in some people, and the root is easily confused with the roots of poisonous plants in the wild. Do not forage from this entry. Do not use in pregnancy without advice. None of the traditional uses above is advice. [USER: verify]
