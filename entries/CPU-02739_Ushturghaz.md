# Ushturghaz — اشترغار (*ushtarghār*, as printed)

## *An unidentified drug likened to *anjudhan* (the Canon's asafoetida-type drug) but inferior to it; no modern identification is made here (confidence low)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk, so far as searched. Source file: `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`.

**Locus (CM).** *Canon* Book II, entry *ushturghār* (اشترغار), heading at Arabic text line 10315; entry to line 10319; it is followed by an unlabelled heading whose text begins 'it is barberry' (line 10320, not part of this entry).

**What it is (CM).** 'It is near to *anjudhan* in its nature, and inferior to it; and the right course is to use its vinegar' (الماهية: هو قريب من الأنجدان في طبعه وأردأ منه والأصوب استعمال خله; lines 10316-10317). **Identity doubt:** *anjudhan* is commonly the asafoetida drug, but this entry gives no plant or gum description, and the Codex has Hingu (*Ferula asafoetida*, I_406) only from Sushruta. The row's label 'asafoetida-like gum' is our reading of the comparison only. [USER: verify]

**What the text says (CM).** Temperament: hot and dry at the end of the third degree (line 10317). Digestive: its vinegar is good for the stomach, cleans and strengthens it and stimulates appetite; the substance itself (*jirm*) causes nausea by its sting and is slow to stay in the stomach and in digestion. Fevers: 'its special property is benefit in quartan fevers' (خاصته النفع في حميات الربع; lines 10317-10319).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(b) Framed.* The entry is a drug-by-drug list of qualities and uses inside the *Canon*'s materia medica. It assigns no cultivation status and says nothing of innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruh*. Its temperament is hot and dry in the third degree, and the text promotes no long-term use.

**Practitioner's note.** A gum-like or herb-like drug likened to *anjudhan*, with its vinegar preferred for the stomach and a special use in quartan fever. The identity is open and nothing supports a cultivation use.

### Contraindications

The text says the substance itself nauseates and stings. The drug is not identified; do not ingest. [USER: verify]
