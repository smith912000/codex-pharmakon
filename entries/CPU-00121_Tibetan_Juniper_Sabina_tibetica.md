### Origin & Tradition

Tibetan juniper is a juniper of the high Tibetan plateau and Himalaya (placed by some botanists in *Sabina*, by others within *Juniperus*), one of the most culturally central plants of the region. Its needled branches are the classic **sang** — the juniper incense burned in Tibetan and Himalayan Buddhist purification rituals, whose fragrant smoke is offered daily at monasteries, passes and households to purify space and as offering. The tree is sacred, and its ritual and medicinal lives are deeply intertwined.

Medicinally the Tibetan tradition (*rGyud-bzhi*) and Himalayan folk practice use juniper leaf, berry and wood in the juniper family's standard **warming, drying, antiseptic and diuretic** register, adapted to the plateau's disease patterns: as a **fumigant and antiseptic** (the purifying smoke also serving to disinfect and to treat respiratory and infectious conditions), for **kidney and urinary** complaints and "cold" disorders, for rheumatic and arthritic pain, and for skin conditions and wounds. The berries and leaves are decocted or used in compound Tibetan formulae; the smoke is both ritual and therapeutic.

As with all junipers the activity rests on the **monoterpene-rich essential oil** (α-pinene and relatives) and the resinous wood constituents, and the same family cautions apply — notably the renal-irritant and abortifacient reputation of concentrated juniper preparations.

Botanically, the Flora of China (efloras.org) treats the plant as *Juniperus tibetica* Komarov (Chinese name da guo yuan bai), with *Sabina tibetica* (Komarov) W. C. Cheng & L. K. Fu listed among its synonyms. It describes a tree to 30 m, rarely a shrub, bearing both scalelike and needlelike leaves (needlelike leaves usually on seedlings and young plants, in whorls of 3, 4–8 mm). The erect seed cones ripen brown, black or purplish black, are ovoid or subglobose, 0.9–1.6 × 0.7–1.3 cm and one-seeded, and the seeds, 7–11 × 6–8 mm, have deep resin pits. Its habitat is forests on mountain slopes or in valleys at 2700–4800 m, in southern Gansu, southern Qinghai, Sichuan, and eastern and southern Xizang. A tree-ring study across 30 sites on the southern Tibetan Plateau (Jia et al. 2022, *Annals of Botany* 130(1):85–95; doi:10.1093/aob/mcac065) found that its growth began in April and ended in October, and was limited by summer soil moisture at 24 of the 30 sites and by temperature at the other six.

- **Species-level essential-oil data.** Hu et al. 2023 (*Int J Mol Sci* 24(20):15203; doi:10.3390/ijms242015203), comparing the steam-distilled oils of six *Juniperus* species from the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau by GC-MS, report for *J. tibetica* a sabinene-rich oil (16.13% of the oil) with α-pinene at 6.93%, and class it with the hedycaryol chemotype group. ### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan medicine (FMT).** Sabina tibetica is not named in either translation of the rGyud-bzhi checked (Clark 1995; Men-Tsee-Khang 2011). The materia medica (Explanatory Tantra ch. 20) lists shug.pa.tsher.chan under other identifications, Sabina recurva and Juniperus recurva, and a juniper in the sour-taste group (Explanatory Tantra ch. 19, Clark tr. 1995); it says of the fruit of that drug that it is "an essence-extraction ingredient" (rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch. 20, Clark tr. 1995). That statement belongs to a differently identified species and is not transferred here; the identification of Tibetan juniper names with Linnaean species is modern and uncertain. No attestation for S. tibetica. Grade (c).

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing is attested for this species in the Tibetan texts. Any juniper use in the tradition is a physician's compounded medicine, and the sources give no home cultivation or self-directed use.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy — avoid.** Junipers carry an abortifacient/emmenagogue reputation; concentrated preparations contraindicated. [USER: verify.]
- **Kidney disease.** Juniper volatile oils can be renally irritant; avoid internal concentrated use in renal disease.
- **Essential oil — diluted/external.** Not for undiluted internal self-use.
- **Smoke sensitivity.** Fumigation smoke can irritate sensitive airways.
