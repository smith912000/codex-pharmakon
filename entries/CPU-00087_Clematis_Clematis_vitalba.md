### Origin & Tradition

*Clematis vitalba* is the rampant climbing vine of European hedgerows — "traveller's joy" for the way it festoons the wayside, and "old man's beard" for the silvery, feathery seed-heads that cloak it in autumn. It is a member of the buttercup family (Ranunculaceae), and like its kin (the buttercups, hellebores, anemones) it carries the family's characteristic acrid, blistering chemistry. Dioscorides records the *klematis* among the acrid plants, noting the biting quality of the leaves and their use to raise blisters and to clear the skin.

The plant's defining property is the **acrid, vesicant (blistering) sap** of the fresh leaf. This gave it two linked traditional uses. First, as a deliberate **counter-irritant and caustic**: the bruised fresh leaf applied to the skin to raise a blister over rheumatic or arthritic joints (the old logic of drawing deep pain to the surface), or to burn off warts and skin lesions. Second — and notoriously — as the **"beggar's herb":** mendicants were said to apply clematis (and other acrid Ranunculaceae) to the skin to raise ulcers and sores that would excite pity and alms, healing them once the begging was done. Internally the plant is too acrid and toxic for routine use, and was essentially avoided.

The acridity is real but **labile**: the active vesicant, **protoanemonin**, is released when the fresh plant is crushed and is unstable, dimerising on drying to the harmless **anemonin** — which is why dried clematis loses its blistering power and why only the fresh plant is irritant. This same chemistry runs through the whole Ranunculaceae and is the key to understanding the plant. (Quite separately, *Clematis* lends its name to a gentle **Bach flower remedy** for "dreaminess" and inattention — a distinct modality recorded here as cultural context, not a material-dose claim.)

### Preparation & Dosing

**No internal home dose is given. The fresh plant is acrid, vesicant and toxic; only cautious external counter-irritant use has a traditional place.**

- **Fresh bruised leaf (external, specialist):** applied briefly to raise a counter-irritant blister or to a wart, with surrounding skin protected; removed before excessive blistering; not for broken skin or the eyes. [USER: verify method; emphasise caution.]

### Contraindications

- **Internal use — avoid.** Acrid, toxic protoanemonin; severe mucosal/GI irritation; not for ingestion.
- **Eyes, broken or sensitive skin.** Vesicant sap causes painful blistering; protect surrounding skin; keep from eyes.
- **Children and livestock.** Toxic fresh plant.
- **Pregnancy — avoid.** Acrid/toxic plant; contraindicated. [USER: verify.]
- **Species confusion.** Do not equate acrid *C. vitalba* with prepared CM *C. chinensis* (Wei Ling Xian).
