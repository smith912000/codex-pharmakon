# Ostrich egg — "Ostrich Egg", "Egg-of-an-Ostrich", "Whipped-up Ostrich Egg" (Bryan's English renderings of the Ebers Papyrus; the text on disk also prints "Egeg-of-the-Ostrich" and "Ege-of-an-Ostrich", which are scanning errors for the same words)
## *The egg of the bird Bryan calls "Ostrich"; Bryan prints no Egyptian word and no binomial, so the spine row's "Struthio camelus" is the list-maker's gloss and is not read from the text* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter VII "Organic Remedies (animals)". "Ostrich" stands in Bryan's list of the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1404, after "Swordfish" and before "Locust"; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 125, class animal, "no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). The parts-of-animals list (lines 1405-1416) ends with "Eggs" (line 1416) but ties it to no animal. The spine row reads "Ostrich egg, Struthio camelus" with the note "Ebers; Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus" (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 88). Wikipedia was not used.

**Where the egg is prescribed.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "ostrich" and "egg". The egg occurs in the following places; the summary Bryan prints gives no quantity in any of them.

- **A face-wash for the ageing face, lines 4115-4126**, in Chapter XXIII "Cosmetics" (heading lines 4086-4087). Bryan introduces it as "reserved for those from whom the second and third, as well as the first bloom of youth had departed": "ANOTHER REMEDY. Bullock's Bile, Whipped-up Ostrich Egg, Oil, Dough, Refined Natron, Hautet-resin. Mix, make into a pap, mix in Fresh Milk, and wash the face therewith daily." His survey earlier in the book mentions the same compound as a "Face-cream of Bullock's Bile and Ostrich Egg, beaten up with Fresh Milk" (lines 1380-1381).
- **A headache poultice, line 2112**, in Chapter XI "Minor Medicine" (heading line 2090), the section on headache beginning at line 2100: "One consisted of Crocodile-earth beaten up with Egeg-of-the-Ostrich" (the text on disk misprints the word). Bryan sets it among external applications that were "not to be taken internally but rubbed in externally" (line 2093).
- **A stinking ulcer, lines 2362-2367**, in Chapter XII "Minor Surgery" (heading line 2209): "ANOTHER REMEDY TO DRIVE OUT A STINKING ULCER IN THE BODY OF A MAN OR WOMAN. Ege-of-an-Ostrich, Tortoise-shell, Thorns-of-the-am-tree. Warm and anoint therewith. But DON'T GET TIRED DOING IT!" Bryan remarks before it (line 2360) that it is puzzling why a "tag of advice" is attached only to this remedy.
- **A check on excessive granulation, line 2411**, in the same chapter: Bryan calls it "an unguent concocted of Ostrich-egg ground down with Tortoise-shell and Thorns-of-the-am-tree" and adds "its efficacy may be seriously doubted". The comment is his. The same three ingredients appear as the stinking-ulcer remedy just above, and the text read does not say whether the two are one prescription or two.
- **A stinking ulcer in the skin chapter, lines 2690-2694**, in Chapter XV "Diseases of the Skin" (heading line 2606): "'To drive out a Stinking Ulcer either in a Man or Woman' one rubbed the body with a ball made of Powdered Onions. A Paste was also provided: ANOTHER. Ostrich Egg, Tortoise-shell, Thorns-of-the-am-tree. Warm and anoint therewith. But DON'T GET TIRED DOING IT." A second ostrich-egg paste with the same ingredients and the same closing advice appears in this chapter.
- **Pterygium, lines 2846-2851**, in Chapter XVI "Diseases of the Eyes" (heading lines 2720-2721): "ANOTHER TO DRIVE OUT PTERYGIUM. Red-lead, Powdered-wood-from-Arabia, Iron-from-Apollonopolis-parva, Calamine, Egg-of-an-Ostrich, Saltpetre-from-Upper-Egypt, Sulphur, Honey. Make into one and apply to the Eyes."

**Related items, printed separately, not counted as the egg.** "Ostrich Oil whipped up with Bile-of-the-Black-abdu-fish" is a further headache application (line 2112). Other eggs ("Egg-of-the-Goose", line 2080; "Egg-of-a-Vulture", line 2844; "Egg-of-the-gabgu-bird", lines 1377 and 4056) are different items.

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say whether the shell, the contents or the whole egg was used, whether the egg was fresh or old, or how much entered any compound; only "Whipped-up" in the face-wash tells of a preparation. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. The Ghalioungui 1987 glossary was not opened and has no ostrich row in `_sources/ebers.csv`. The 1987 translation and the Egyptian words for these items could change any reading above. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it. In general terms, a bird's egg contains water, proteins, fats and some vitamins and minerals in the contents, and a hard shell made mainly of calcium carbonate. Nothing in the sources retrieved says which part was used or that any component acts on the complaints named. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show an ostrich egg in compounds for the face, the head, ulcers and the eye, with no quantity legible. Placing raw egg and other unsterile material on an ulcer or in the eye is an infection risk, and the eye compound carries red-lead, sulphur and saltpetre. The compounds it appears in are not to be reproduced. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only what Bryan reports: whipped egg mixed with bullock's bile, oil, dough, natron and resin and then milk, as a daily face-wash (lines 4115-4126); egg beaten up with Crocodile-earth (line 2112); egg with tortoise-shell and *am*-thorns, warmed and rubbed on (lines 2362-2367, 2694); egg ground down with the same two (line 2411); egg in a mixture with red-lead and others, applied to the eyes (lines 2846-2851). No quantity is printed in any of them. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution, and the uses found are a cosmetic wash, a headache poultice, ulcer and eye applications. Bryan's remark that the ageing face's wash was "reserved for those from whom the second and third, as well as the first bloom of youth had departed" is his own comment and is not a claim about vital essence. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); no recitation is printed with any ostrich-egg prescription in Bryan's summary, which is no proof the papyrus gives none. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The record is an egg ingredient in cosmetic, wound and eye compounds, with no quantity and no cultivation role. A cultivator has nothing to do with it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** raw egg and other animal material on an ulcer or in the eye risks infection; ulcers and eye disease need medical care.
- **Toxic co-ingredients:** the pterygium compound names red-lead, sulphur, saltpetre and calamine, and the headache poultice names crocodile-earth; red-lead is a lead compound and is poisonous.
- **Allergy:** egg is a common food allergen.
- **Conservation and law:** wild ostrich populations and their eggs are protected in parts of their range, and trade in eggs may be controlled; the present status was not retrieved. [USER: verify]
