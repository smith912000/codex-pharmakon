# I-344: Astragalus membranaceus (Astragalus / Huang Qi)

## 1. Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Primary name:** Astragalus (Western); Huang Qi (Chinese)  
**Botanical name:** *Astragalus membranaceus* (Fisch.) Bunge (syn. *A. membranaceus* var. *mongholicus* (Bunge) Hsiao; *A. propinquus* Schischkin)  
**Family:** Fabaceae, subfamily Papilionoideae  
**Synonyms:** Milk vetch root, tragacanth relative (genus), yellow vetch, Chinese milk vetch  
**Traditional names:** Huang Qi (Chinese 黄芪 — "yellow leader/energy"; the *qi* is energy/vital force); Hogi (Japanese 黄耆); Hwanggi (Korean 황기); Astragale membraneux (French); Tragant (German)

**Taxonomic note:** *Astragalus* is the LARGEST genus in the plant kingdom (~2,500 species); *A. membranaceus* is the medicinal species; *A. tragacantha* produces gum tragacanth (a food additive — different application). Complete distinction from toxic locoweeds (*A. mollissimus*, *A. lentiginosus*) which contain swainsonine — a toxic indolizidine alkaloid causing neurological disease in livestock.

**Part used:** Root (6-year-old plants preferred; younger roots less potent); sliced root used in soups and decoctions (the distinctive flat, yellow, fibrous slices recognizable in TCM markets)

## 3. Convergence Analysis

**Pattern:** Huang qi represents the TCM "superior herb" (shanphin) category — herbs taken long-term for health promotion, not primarily for acute disease. The *Shennong Bencao Jing*'s original three-tier classification placed huang qi in the top category ("superior" herbs for longevity, non-toxic, safe for continuous use) alongside ginseng, licorice, and wolfberry. This traditional framing predates but anticipates the modern concept of "adaptogen."

The limited cross-traditional convergence reflects geographic specificity — astragalus root pharmacology is primarily documented in the Chinese and Mongolian (Inner Mongolia is primary cultivation site) traditions. However, the pharmacological mechanisms identified — polysaccharide immunomodulation, astragaloside telomere effects, cycloastragenol anti-aging properties — have generated an international research effort that has imported astragalus into Western integrative medicine despite its absence from Western traditional pharmacopoeias.

**Cycloastragenol (TA-65):** The most commercially significant modern discovery from astragalus; cycloastragenol activates telomerase, extending telomeres in vitro and in animal models — the first natural compound shown to activate human telomerase enzyme. This has generated enormous commercial interest in anti-aging medicine and placed astragalus at the center of contemporary longevity research.

## 4. Phytochemical Composition

### Polysaccharides (primary immunomodulatory fraction)

| Compound | Content | Activity |
|----------|---------|----------|
| Astragalus polysaccharides (APS): APS-I, APS-II, APS-III | 5–15% root (dry) | PRIMARY immunomodulatory; β-glucan type; TLR4 agonist; macrophage activation; NK cell stimulation |
| Heteropolysaccharides | Variable | Additional immunostimulant |

**Mechanism:** APS activates TLR4 (toll-like receptor 4) on macrophages and dendritic cells → NF-κB activation → pro-inflammatory cytokine production (paradoxically beneficial in immunodeficiency states) → downstream T-cell, B-cell, NK cell activation; in autoimmune states, higher doses may shift toward regulatory T-cell induction

### Saponins (telomerase activating and adaptogenic fraction)

| Compound | Content | Activity |
|----------|---------|----------|
| Astragaloside IV | 0.02–0.08% | Major saponin; precursor to cycloastragenol; anti-aging in vivo |
| Cycloastragenol | Trace (metabolite/minor saponin) | TELOMERASE ACTIVATOR; hTERT upregulation; TA-65 commercial product |
| Astragaloside I, II, III | Minor | Cardioprotective; antiviral |
| Soyasaponin I | Minor | Immunomodulatory |

**TA-65:** Commercial product containing cycloastragenol at a standardized dose (5–50 mg/day); licensed from Geron Corporation (telomere biotechnology company); sold by TA Sciences; pricing reflects exclusivity

## 5. Mechanisms of Action

### Immunomodulation (APS — primary mechanism)

**Innate immunity activation:**
- APS → TLR4/TLR2 on macrophages → NF-κB → increased IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α (innate immune priming)
- NK cell activation: APS increases NK cell cytotoxicity in vitro and ex vivo in cancer patients
- Dendritic cell maturation: APS stimulates DC antigen presentation capacity

**Adaptive immunity:**
- T-lymphocyte proliferation enhancement (in lymphocyte cultures)
- CD4⁺ T helper cell shift toward Th1 (cellular immunity — beneficial for infections)
- B-cell antibody production support
- Regulatory T-cell (Treg) induction at higher concentrations: potentially relevant in autoimmune contexts

**Bidirectional (adaptogenic) immune modulation:**
- Low APS concentrations: pro-inflammatory (enhances inadequate immune response)
- Higher APS concentrations: anti-inflammatory (dampens excessive immune response)
- This bidirectional effect is the mechanistic basis of the "immunomodulator" rather than "immunostimulant" designation

### Telomerase Activation (cycloastragenol/astragaloside IV)

- Cycloastragenol → binds to and activates **hTERT** (human telomerase reverse transcriptase)
- hTERT is the catalytic subunit of telomerase — the enzyme that extends telomere repeats
- Cellular telomere length is correlated with aging (shorter = older biologically)
- In vitro: cycloastragenol extends telomeres in human T-lymphocytes and endothelial cells
- Harley 2011 (*Rejuvenation Research*): TA-65 (cycloastragenol) in older adults — peripheral blood immune cell telomere length increased vs. placebo; immune function improvements

**Critical scientific caveat:** The relationship between telomere extension and healthspan extension is NOT established in humans. Telomere length is an aging marker but not proven to be causally important vs. a biomarker. Telomerase activation carries a theoretical oncological risk (cancer cells are characterized by inappropriate telomerase activation). Long-term safety data in humans limited.

### Cardioprotective Mechanisms
- Astragaloside IV: reduces myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (animal models); mechanism: antioxidant + eNOS activation + Ca²⁺ channel modulation
- Calycosin: vasodilatory (eNOS); anti-platelet aggregation

## 6. Clinical Evidence

### Cancer Supportive Care — Level B

**Most robust evidence area:**
- McCulloch 2006 (*Journal of Clinical Oncology*: meta-analysis, 34 RCTs, n=2,815, Chinese patients): astragalus-based Chinese herbal formulas as adjunct to chemotherapy for non-small cell lung cancer; significantly improved 1-year survival (RR 0.67), tumor response, quality of life vs. chemotherapy alone
- Caveat: All trials Chinese; most use astragalus in combination formulas (not pure astragalus); independent Western replication lacking; methodological quality varies
- Mechanistic rationale: APS augments NK cell activity depleted by chemotherapy; may reduce chemotherapy-induced immunosuppression

### Immune Function (healthy adults) — Level B

- Cho 2007 (RCT, n=36): astragalus 6g/day × 8 weeks in healthy volunteers: significantly increased CD4/CD8 ratio, NK cell activity vs. placebo
- Multiple Chinese RCTs in recurrent respiratory infections (children): astragalus supplementation reduced upper respiratory infection frequency; consistent positive direction

### Cardiovascular — Level B (astragaloside IV — injectable form, China)

- IV astragaloside IV (approved injectable pharmaceutical in China — injectable cardiac drug): multiple Chinese RCTs for heart failure, myocardial infarction showing reduced infarct size, improved cardiac function
- Oral astragalus root: Level C for cardiovascular (less bioavailable; oral bioavailability of astragalosides low ~4%)

### Anti-aging / Telomere Extension — Level C

- Harley 2011 (n=117, age 53–87, randomized): TA-65 (cycloastragenol) vs. placebo; telomere length increase in immune cells; CMV-specific T-cell proportions; no standard hard endpoints (disease/death/function)
- Insufficient clinical evidence for anti-aging claims; biologically plausible but unproven

## 7. Phenomenological Record

### Superior Herb Status — 2,000 Years of Continuous TCM Use
Huang qi's placement in the *Shennong Bencao Jing*'s "superior herb" (shangphin) category means it has been used in Chinese medicine continuously for at least 1,800 years (text compiled ~200 CE) and likely 4,000+ years based on archaeological evidence. The superior herb classification implied: safe for long-term continuous use; tonifying/strengthening; suitable for health promotion in well individuals; able to "nourish life and delay aging." This pharmacobotanical tradition of identifying safe adaptogenic plants long predates the concept of "adaptogen" introduced by Nikolai Lazarev in 1947.

### Wei Qi and the Immune System
The TCM concept of "Wei Qi" (卫气 — Defensive/Protective Energy) maps remarkably well onto the modern immunological concept of innate immune surveillance. Wei Qi circulates on the body's surface, protecting against external "evils" (pathogenic factors). Huang qi is the premier herb for tonifying Wei Qi. The pharmacological translation: APS stimulates macrophage surveillance, NK cell activity, and innate immune priming — exactly the innate immune surveillance that the Wei Qi concept describes. This is one of the most compelling examples of pre-modern conceptual systems intuitively capturing what modern science later described mechanistically.

### Geron Corporation and the Telomere Revolution
The discovery of telomerase by Elizabeth Blackburn, Carol Greider, and Jack Szostak (Nobel Prize 2009) placed telomere biology at the center of aging research. Geron Corporation was founded to exploit telomerase biotechnology; when cycloastragenol was found to activate hTERT, Geron licensed the discovery to TA Sciences. TA Sciences markets TA-65 at prices ranging $600–$1200/month — the most expensive commercial nutraceutical derived from a traditional herb. The scientific legitimacy of the investment (telomere extension = meaningful human aging benefit) remains contested; the commercial exploitation of traditional knowledge (astragalus use for thousands of years before cycloastragenol identification) raises ethnobotanical equity questions.

## 8. Practical Application

### Immunomodulation and Cancer Supportive Care

**Standard TCM oral use:**
- Dried root slice decoction: 9–30 g/day in water (500 mL, simmer 30–60 min)
- Concentrated extract: 2–4 g/day equivalent
- Duration: 8–12 weeks; pause; reassess; chronic use well-tolerated

**Cancer supportive care (adjunct — NOT alternative to oncological treatment):**
- Discuss with oncologist before initiating (drug interactions possible)
- Standard Chinese formula context: often combined with Codonopsis (Dang Shen), Atractylodes (Bai Zhu), and other Qi tonics in the "Four Gentlemen Decoction" (Si Jun Zi Tang) base
- Not to be used as cancer treatment replacement under any circumstances

### TA-65 / Cycloastragenol (anti-aging use)

**Current evidence supports:** biologically plausible mechanism; limited but positive RCT evidence for telomere extension in immune cells
**NOT established:** meaningful longevity, healthspan, or disease prevention benefit in humans
**Theoretical concern:** telomerase activation in individuals with existing occult malignancy — not quantified but not dismissible

**If pursuing:** 5–25 mg cycloastragenol/day; standard TA-65 dose; screen for active malignancy before starting; review with physician

### Safety Profile

**Outstanding safety record:**
- Thousands of years of TCM use at 9–30 g/day doses
- No significant toxicity documented at standard doses
- LD50 extrapolation suggests very high safety margin
- Most commonly reported effects: mild GI upset at very high doses

**Specific cautions:**
- **Autoimmune disease (lupus, RA, MS):** CAUTION with immunostimulant doses; APS bidirectional effects but higher doses may tip toward Th1 pro-inflammatory in susceptible individuals
- **Organ transplant patients:** immunostimulation could increase rejection risk — AVOID high-dose APS
- **Pregnancy:** limited data; avoid therapeutic doses
- **Drug interactions:** potential immunosuppressant interaction (cyclosporin); monitor closely

### Drug Interactions
- **Immunosuppressants (cyclosporin, tacrolimus, mycophenolate):** APS immunostimulation may counteract — MONITOR; AVOID in transplant recipients
- **Cyclophosphamide:** possible protective effect against cyclophosphamide-induced myelosuppression (emerging evidence) — discuss with oncologist
- **Warfarin:** case reports of interaction; monitor INR

### Energetic Classification
**Primary:** ◉ Qi (functional) — THE defining Qi tonic in TCM; direct Qi supplementation at the Lung and Spleen level; immune surveillance restoration  
**Secondary:** ◈ Jing (structural) — cycloastragenol telomere extension; aging clock modulation; deep constitutional longevity support
