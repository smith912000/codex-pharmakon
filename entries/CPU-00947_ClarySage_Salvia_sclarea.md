### Origin & Tradition

*Salvia sclarea* — clary sage — is a tall, biennial Mediterranean sage with broad, wrinkled, resin-sticky leaves and towering pastel bracts. Its English name is a fossil of its central doctrine: *clary* is a worn-down form of *clear-eye*, and its old Latin epithets — *Oculus Christi* ("eye of Christ"), *clarus* ("clear") — all encode a single, mechanically literal use. The **mucilaginous seed** was placed into the eye, where its outer coat swelled into a slippery gel that trapped a grain of grit or a lodged particle and floated it out on tears. This is not chemistry; it is **physics** — a seed-coat hydrogel acting as a soft, self-lubricating extraction tool. Alongside this eye doctrine runs a second, aromatic tradition: the **essential oil of the flowering tops** (rich in linalyl acetate and the diterpene sclareol) used as a calmative and as a women's herb for menstrual and labour support.

Clary sage must be kept distinct from **common / garden sage, *Salvia officinalis*** — the culinary, thujone-bearing sage used for sore throats, memory, and drying secretions. The two are close relatives (genome studies place them as near neighbours in *Salvia*), but their chemistry and use diverge: *S. officinalis* is a drying, astringent, thujone-containing throat-and-cognition herb; *S. sclarea* is a linalyl-acetate/sclareol relaxant-and-eye herb, essentially thujone-poor. Where a text says only "sage," the eye-clearing and calmative-labour doctrines described here belong specifically to *S. sclarea*.

**AT (Assyrian medical tablets, ~7th century BCE) [USER: verify]:** Mesopotamian ophthalmic practice made heavy use of mucilaginous seeds and plant gums applied to the eye to remove specks and soothe inflammation, and of aromatic sages as fumigants and women's remedies. A secure species-level identification of *Salvia sclarea* in the tablets cannot be asserted, so this tradition is flagged for verification with no specific tablet cited.

**DMM (De Materia Medica, Dioscorides, ~77 CE):** Dioscorides describes *ὅρμινον* (horminon) — the clary/wild-sage group — and the wider *elelisphakon* sages as warming, drying, aromatic herbs; the clary type is noted for its **sticky, mucilaginous seed used to draw foreign bodies and films from the eye**, and the sages generally as emmenagogue and warming to the womb. This fixes both the ophthalmic and the gynaecological strands at the origin of the Western record.

**CM (Canon of Medicine, Ibn Sina, ~1025 CE):** In Unani medicine clary (a *qurrat al-'ayn* / "coolness of the eye" type) is a warm, dry aromatic sage; the mucilaginous seed is used ophthalmically to cleanse the eye of grit and films, and the herb is *muḥallil* (resolvent) and an emmenagogue/uterine aromatic. Avicenna's sages are warming womb-herbs and nervines — the same twin doctrine of **eye-clearing seed + calmative/gynaecological aromatic**.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** Clary sage splits cleanly into two doctrines with two entirely different physical bases, and honest mapping means treating them separately. (1) The **eye doctrine** is *mechanical, not chemical*: the seed coat is rich in polysaccharide **mucilage** that hydrates on contact with tear fluid, swelling into a soft gel that adheres to a foreign particle and lifts it clear. "Clear-eye" names a hydrogel physics — surface tension, swelling, adhesion — and the doctrine is literally, if crudely, correct; it is a soft-matter tool, not a drug, and it says nothing about pharmacology. (2) The **aromatic doctrine** *is* chemical: **linalyl acetate** and **linalool** are the calmative volatiles of the flower oil, and **sclareol** is its characteristic labdane diterpene. The symbolic "calming, womb-warming" reading maps onto genuine sedative-aromatic monoterpene esters. The one-word name "clary" thus hides two unrelated mechanisms — a swelling seed and a fragrant oil — and the Codex keeps them from contaminating each other.

### Active Components

- **Seed-coat mucilage** (polysaccharide hydrogel) — the physical basis of the "clear-eye" doctrine; swells on wetting to trap and float out ocular foreign bodies; a mechanical, not pharmacological, action
- **Linalyl acetate** — dominant monoterpene ester of the flower/leaf essential oil; the calmative, mildly sedative, characteristic aroma principle
- **Linalool** — co-dominant monoterpene alcohol; sedative-anxiolytic-associated aroma volatile; partner to linalyl acetate in capitate oil glands
- **Sclareol** — labdane diterpene concentrated in peltate glands (and calyx); characteristic of *S. sclarea*, industrially prized as an ambergris/sclareolide precursor; contributes to the oil's chemical signature
- **Sesquiterpenes** (germacrene D, β-caryophyllene) — leaf-gland volatiles; aromatic/anti-inflammatory minor actors
- **Low thujone** — unlike *S. officinalis*, clary is not a thujone-rich sage, part of what distinguishes the two

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- **Oil composition — the aromatic doctrine's chemistry.** Choi et al. (2025), *Sci Data* — chromosome-level genome of *Salvia sclarea*, describing its essential oil as valued for **sclareol, linalool, and linalyl acetate**, and placing *S. sclarea* in a **close evolutionary relationship with *S. officinalis*** (confirming they are distinct but neighbouring species). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-04347-0). Schmiderer et al. (2008), *Plant Biol* — single-gland analysis showed **capitate glands produce linalool + linalyl acetate + sclareol**, with linalool/linalyl acetate characteristic of the **flowers**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1438-8677.2008.00053.x). Hudaib et al. (2001), *Farmaco* — GC-MS/HPLC confirming **linalyl acetate and linalool as principal components** with sclareol among the diterpenoids. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0014-827x(01)01038-2). **Grade: STRONG for composition; the calmative aroma chemistry is firmly established.**

- **Calmative / sleep-quality signal (clinical, but blended).** Kao et al. (2017), *J Altern Complement Med* — RCT in 132 career women: a **blended essential oil containing *Salvia sclarea*** (1:1:1 with *Lavandula angustifolia* and *Origanum majorana*) significantly improved sleep quality and quality-of-life over 4 weeks, outperforming lavender alone on QOL. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1089/acm.2016.0403). **Grade: WEAK–MODERATE and INDIRECT** — real randomised human data for a *calming* effect, but clary sage was one component of a three-oil blend, so its individual contribution cannot be isolated. It supports the *plausibility* of the calmative doctrine, not a standalone claim.

- **The "clear-eye" seed doctrine (mucilage physics).** No retrieved PubMed trial evaluates the swelling-seed foreign-body technique. **Grade: MECHANISTICALLY PLAUSIBLE, CLINICALLY UNTESTED (here) — and deprecated on safety grounds.** The action is soft-matter hydrogel physics, not pharmacology; its historical logic is coherent, but placing plant seed in the eye is not endorsed given infection/abrasion risk and the availability of modern eye care.

- **Menstrual/labour uses.** Not independently verified in the sources retrieved this run beyond the general calmative signal above. **Grade: TRADITIONAL / UNVERIFIED HERE** — presented as an aromatherapy-framed tradition, not an evidenced treatment.

**Honest bottom line:** the aromatic-calmative doctrine rests on solidly characterised linalyl-acetate/linalool chemistry with supportive (but blended) human data; the eye doctrine is real *physics* rather than pharmacology and is documented but not practised; menstrual/labour claims remain traditional pending direct evidence.

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian (AT).** Mesopotamian ophthalmic practice used mucilaginous seeds and plant gums on the eye to lift specks and soothe inflammation, and aromatic sages as fumigants and women's remedies — but a secure species-level identification of *Salvia sclarea* in the tablets cannot be asserted. Under the standing blocking warning for Assyrian identifications and the tradition's classification of illness by causal agency, and with the plant's presence in the corpus unverified, no cultivation status is claimed; any attestation would be therapeutic and mechanical — a seed to draw grit from the eye, a warming womb-aromatic — not a status conferred on the person's vital constitution. **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *Horminon*, the clary/wild-sage type, is treated among the warming, drying, aromatic sages, noted specifically for its sticky mucilaginous seed used to draw foreign bodies and films from the eye, and for the sages' emmenagogue, womb-warming action. Dioscorides carries no subtle-body doctrine into which this could become a cultivation status; what he offers a cultivator is his discipline of attention — recognising the resin-sticky biennial sage, holding it distinct from the thujone-bearing common sage that shares the name, and understanding the seed as a soft-matter tool whose action is hydrogel physics, not pharmacology. That discrimination is the real practice. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** Clary, a *qurrat al-'ayn* ("coolness of the eye") type, is classed a warm, dry aromatic sage: the mucilaginous seed *jālī* (cleansing) to the eye, the herb *muḥallil* (resolvent), a nervine/calmative and an emmenagogue/uterine aromatic. The tradition's deepest longevity idea, the lifelong conservation of innate heat and radical moisture, frames the whole regimen rather than assigning this mild warm-dry aromatic a status; clary is directed at the eye, the nerves, and the womb, and while its calmative aromatic action is the nearest thing here to a *ruh*-gladdening role, no *mufarrih* status is recorded for it in the sources. Its standing is therapeutic and dietetic, not a formal cultivation status. **(b) framed:** attested therapeutically, no cultivation status sourced.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator's use of clary sage is narrow and route-restricted: the flower oil (linalyl acetate + linalool) inhaled or diffused as a calmative wind-down on high-stress RESET evenings, and — traditionally, aromatherapy-framed — for menstrual comfort. The oil is markedly sedating, is not for ingestion, and is cautioned against in pregnancy. The historic clear-eye seed technique is documented physics but not practised, given infection risk and modern eye care. None of this is a longevity tonic; it is targeted, occasional, symptomatic support. **(b) framed** overall, resting on the calmative aromatic strand rather than any conservational status.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy (until term):** avoid — traditional emmenagogue and uterine aromatic; do not use for calming or menstrual purposes while pregnant.
- **Before driving / operating machinery, or with alcohol/CNS depressants:** caution — the oil can be markedly sedating; additive sedation possible.
- **Internal use of the essential oil:** not advised in Kenetica — aromatherapy (inhalation) and properly diluted topical only.
- **"Clear-eye" seed application:** not recommended — infection and corneal-abrasion risk; use clinical eye irrigation/care for foreign bodies.
- **Known Lamiaceae/Salvia sensitivity:** possible contact irritation; patch-test topical dilutions.
- **Do not conflate with common sage (*S. officinalis*):** thujone content, drying action, and indications differ; the calmative/eye doctrines here are specific to *S. sclarea*.
