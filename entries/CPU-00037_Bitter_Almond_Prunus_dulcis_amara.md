### Origin & Tradition

The almond tree yields two kinds of kernel that are botanically the same species and visually nearly indistinguishable: the sweet almond, eaten freely as food, and the bitter almond, var. *amara*, which carries within its tissue a chemical apparatus for generating hydrogen cyanide. This duality — one tree, one fruit, two destinies, one nourishing and one lethal — has made the bitter almond an object of fascination, caution, and medicine since antiquity. The bitterness is not incidental; it is the sensory signature of amygdalin, the cyanogenic glycoside that distinguishes the bitter kernel from the sweet, and the same compound that, on hydrolysis, releases the volatile benzaldehyde responsible for "almond essence" and the hydrogen cyanide responsible for almond's reputation as a poison.

Dioscorides treats almonds in Book I of *De Materia Medica*, among the fruits and the trees that yield oils and edible kernels. He records the medical uses of the bitter almond specifically: taken in measured quantity it was held to provoke urine and menstruation, to relieve pain and headache, to act on the liver and spleen, to clear the chest and ease cough, and — applied externally — to clean the skin and remove blemishes. The expressed oil of almonds, sweet and bland, was a staple emollient and a base for other medicines. The ancients also recorded, in the form of cautionary anecdote, the toxic potential of the bitter kernel in quantity; the observation that a handful of bitter almonds could sicken or kill belongs to the empirical knowledge of the classical world even where the mechanism was wholly unknown.

Through the Galenic and medieval pharmacy the bitter almond persisted as a bittering and "opening" (deobstruent) agent, an ingredient of cough preparations, and — in the form of distilled bitter-almond water and almond-flavoured syrups — a flavouring whose pharmacological edge was understood but not quantified. The danger of these preparations was real: distilled bitter-almond water and the related "essential oil of bitter almonds" carried free hydrogen cyanide and benzaldehyde, and the history of pharmacy includes both their therapeutic use as sedative-antitussive flavourings and a catalogue of poisonings, accidental and deliberate, arising from them. The characteristic almond odour of cyanide became, in forensic lore, a sign to be recognized.

In the modern era the bitter almond's chemistry was clarified: amygdalin and its enzymatic partner, hydrolysed by β-glucosidase when the kernel is crushed and moistened, liberate glucose, benzaldehyde, and hydrogen cyanide in a stepwise reaction. This understanding closed the loop between the ancient empirical caution and the molecular reality, and it underlies the modern regulatory treatment of bitter almonds and their oil. It is also the basis of one of the more notorious episodes in alternative oncology — the promotion of amygdalin (and its semi-synthetic relative marketed as "laetrile" or "vitamin B17") as a cancer cure, a claim that has been repeatedly tested and found both ineffective and dangerous (see Scientific Correlation). The bitter almond therefore sits at a crossroads of food, flavour, folk medicine, poison, and pseudo-medicine — a single kernel that demands the [SPECIALIST ONLY] designation without qualification.

### Active Components

- **Amygdalin** (cyanogenic diglucoside; mandelonitrile-β-D-gentiobioside): the defining constituent of the bitter kernel; on enzymatic or acid hydrolysis it releases glucose, benzaldehyde, and hydrogen cyanide; the source of both the bitterness and the toxicity; concentration is markedly higher in bitter than in sweet kernels. [USER: verify any specific amygdalin percentage before printing; cite a confident range only.]
- **β-Glucosidase / emulsin** (hydrolytic enzyme system, including amygdalin hydrolase and prunasin hydrolase): the endogenous enzymes that, on crushing and wetting of the kernel, cleave amygdalin to liberate hydrogen cyanide — the reason intact dry kernels are far less acutely dangerous than crushed, moistened ones.
- **Hydrogen cyanide (HCN)** (hydrolysis product): the lethal agent; binds cytochrome c oxidase (complex IV of the mitochondrial electron transport chain), arresting cellular aerobic respiration and producing histotoxic hypoxia — cells cannot use oxygen even when it is abundant.
- **Benzaldehyde** (aromatic aldehyde; hydrolysis product): the volatile "almond essence" odour and flavour; the basis of natural bitter-almond flavouring (after cyanide removal).
- **Fixed almond oil** (triglyceride oil, oleic-acid rich): the bland expressed oil common to both sweet and bitter kernels; emollient, demulcent, a classic pharmaceutical and cosmetic base; essentially free of the cyanogenic risk, which resides in the meal/water fraction.
- **Prunasin** (monoglucoside intermediate): partial-hydrolysis intermediate between amygdalin and the cyanohydrin.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **[SPECIALIST ONLY] Antitussive / chest-clearing (historical DMM)** — Dioscorides and the later tradition used bitter almond preparations to ease cough and clear the chest; modern interpretation links this to the cyanogenic/benzaldehyde fraction acting as a centrally sedating antitussive at sub-toxic dose — a vanishingly narrow therapeutic window that places the use firmly under specialist control and largely supersedes it with safer agents.

2. **[SPECIALIST ONLY] Bitter/deobstruent and emmenagogue (historical DMM)** — The classical "opening" actions on liver and spleen, the provocation of urine and menses, and pain relief reflect the bitter principle and the systemic effect of low-dose cyanogenic exposure; archival, not recommended.

3. **Emollient almond oil (sweet-oil fraction — GENERAL within this otherwise specialist entry)** — The expressed fixed oil, which does not carry the cyanogenic hazard, is a safe and traditional emollient and demulcent base; this is the one component of the bitter almond complex that crosses into general use, and only as the de-toxified oil.

4. **Natural bitter-almond flavouring (benzaldehyde, cyanide-removed)** — After removal of hydrogen cyanide, the benzaldehyde fraction is the source of natural almond flavouring; documented here to separate the safe flavour use from the toxic kernel.

5. **[SPECIALIST ONLY] Amygdalin / "laetrile" — discredited cancer claim** — Listed expressly to document and refute: amygdalin has been promoted as an anticancer agent ("vitamin B17"); controlled evaluation shows no anticancer efficacy and a real risk of cyanide poisoning. This is included as a cautionary, not a therapeutic, application.

### Preparation & Dosing

**⚠ This is a [SPECIALIST ONLY] cyanogenic plant. Crushed, moistened bitter-almond kernels and bitter-almond water/oil can be lethal. No self-administration of the kernel, the distilled water, or amygdalin/laetrile.**

**Sweet almond fixed oil (the safe fraction):** Expressed almond oil used externally as an emollient or as a carrier/base, and internally in traditional pharmacy as a bland demulcent. This fixed oil is essentially free of the cyanogenic hazard. This is the only preparation here suitable for general use.

**Bitter-almond water / essential oil of bitter almonds (historical):** Carried free hydrogen cyanide and benzaldehyde; historically used in minute, standardized doses as a sedative flavouring antitussive. **Obsolete and dangerous; do not prepare or dose.** Any modern "essential oil of bitter almonds" intended for flavouring must be the cyanide-free (FFPA) grade.

**Amygdalin / laetrile:** No legitimate therapeutic dose. Promoted doses in the discredited cancer literature have caused cyanide poisoning, especially when combined with oral β-glucosidase sources. Do not administer.

**Acute toxicity note:** A relatively small number of crushed bitter almonds can deliver a clinically significant cyanide dose, and the lethal range for children is far smaller than for adults. ### Contraindications

- **Cyanide poisoning (lethal):** Crushed/moistened bitter-almond kernels, bitter-almond water, and amygdalin/laetrile can release hydrogen cyanide and cause rapid histotoxic hypoxia — headache, dizziness, confusion, breathlessness with paradoxically well-oxygenated blood, seizures, cardiovascular collapse, and death. The classic almond odour may or may not be detectable. This is the governing contraindication for all non-oil preparations.
- **Children — drastically reduced margin:** The cyanogenic dose that endangers a child is far below that for an adult; bitter almonds and their water/oil must be kept entirely away from children.
- **Amygdalin/laetrile use:** Contraindicated as therapy; no proven benefit and documented cyanide-poisoning deaths, with risk amplified by concurrent oral β-glucosidase sources (certain enzyme supplements, large amounts of certain raw foods).
- **Combination with β-glucosidase / acidic gut conditions:** Anything that accelerates hydrolysis of amygdalin increases cyanide liberation and danger.
- **Pregnancy:** Bitter-almond preparations (kernel/water) contraindicated — emmenagogue/uterine actions historically and cyanogenic toxicity; the bland sweet oil is the only acceptable derivative. [USER: verify.]
- **Tree-nut allergy:** Standard almond (sweet or bitter, kernel or oil) is contraindicated in tree-nut allergy.
- **Mislabelled "essential oil of bitter almonds":** Only the cyanide-free (FFPA) flavour grade is acceptable for flavouring; cyanide-bearing grades are hazardous and must not be ingested.
