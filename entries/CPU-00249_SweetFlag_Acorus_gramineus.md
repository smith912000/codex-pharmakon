### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese materia medica (SBJ lineage):** *Shí Chāng Pú* — the slender rhizome of grassleaf sweetflag, growing on wet rocks by streams — is a classic aromatic "opening" herb. Its traditional signature is the pungent, penetrating aroma held to "open the orifices" (the sensory and cognitive apertures — the "openings of the Heart"), disperse turbid damp-phlegm clouding the mind, and thereby restore clear perception, memory, hearing, and voice. Classical indications include phlegm-misting-the-mind patterns (dullness, poor concentration, muddled awareness, in severe cases seizure/loss of consciousness patterns), tinnitus and hearing dullness, and — as a spirit-settling herb — palpitations, insomnia, and forgetfulness. It is distinguished from the larger, wetland *Acorus calamus* (a related species with a heavier β-asarone load); *A. gramineus* is the smaller, rock-dwelling "stone" sweetflag preferred in Chinese practice.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Aroma That Opens the Gate:** Sweetflag's doctrine is olfactory: the herb works, in the classical imagination, *through its scent* — the acrid aromatic that pierces "turbid phlegm" and re-opens the shuttered senses. In Kenetica this is read as *clearing the input channels* — restoring signal fidelity when the mind is fogged, damped, congested. The honest anchor is that its actives are aromatic phenylpropanoids (the asarones) that genuinely cross the blood-brain barrier and act on the CNS (see Scientific Correlation) — but the same chemistry carries the herb's central safety problem, so the "opening" and the "toxicity" are one molecule.

### Active Components

- **β-asarone** — the principal aromatic phenylpropanoid; crosses the blood-brain barrier and drives much of the studied neuroactivity — **and is also the herb's chief toxicological liability** (see Contraindications).
- **α-asarone** — related phenylpropanoid; also bioactive.
- **Essential oil** — the aromatic fraction responsible for the pungent scent and orifice-opening action.
- **Other terpenoids and phenolics.**
- Documented preclinical actions: neuroprotection, promotion of synaptogenesis/spatial memory, and CNS activity — set against genotoxic/carcinogenic concerns for β-asarone.

### Scientific Correlation

Evidence grade: **preclinical** (animal/cell); no robust human clinical efficacy trials retrieved, and a significant β-asarone toxicity literature exists. According to PubMed:

- **β-asarone rescues memory & synaptogenesis (rat model).** β-asarone (an active of *Acorus*), given to lead-exposed developmental and adult rats, attenuated Pb-induced loss of dendritic spine density in hippocampal CA1/dentate gyrus, partially rescued spatial-memory deficits (Morris water maze), and up-regulated NR2B, Arc/Arg3.1 and Wnt7a — indicating neuroprotection via synaptogenesis pathways; β-asarone was confirmed to cross the blood-brain barrier (Yang et al., 2016, *PLoS One*). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0167401)
- **Cognitive/nootropic context (review).** Reviews of *Acorus*/β-asarone-containing preparations describe cognitive-enhancing, neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activity, while calling for the preclinical-to-clinical gap to be closed with proper trials (Aleem & Khan, 2023, *J Complement Integr Med*). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/jcim-2021-0447)

The neuroactivity is real in models, but so is the safety concern: the same β-asarone that drives the CNS effects is the compound flagged below. No claim of proven human efficacy or safety is made.

Attribution: findings from PubMed.

### Cultivation Register

**Shennong / classical Chinese (SBJ).** The *Shennong Bencaojing* carries an *Acorus* lemma in its **Superior Class** (上品), and it is one of the most explicitly cultivation-shaped entries in the whole upper grade: the drug "opens the heart portals, supplements the five viscera, frees the nine orifices, brightens the eyes and [sharpens] the hearing, and [helps] the articulation of the voice. Protracted taking may make the body light, improve memory, prevent confusion, and prolong life" (Yang Shou-zhong, trans., *The Divine Farmer's Materia Medica Classic*, Blue Poppy Press, 1998, Herbs: Superior Class, p. 21). Three upper-grade markers stand together there — 久服 *jiu fu* "protracted taking", 輕身 *qing shen* "make the body light", and 延年 "prolong life" — and 輕身 in particular must be left as the tradition's own effect-category, with **no pharmacological equivalent** substituted for it. The opening of the "heart portals" belongs to the *shen* 神 stratum of the materia medica, the one stratum the Chinese sources actually formalise (later *bencao* organise such drugs under *kaiqiao* 開竅, orifice-opening, and *anshen* 安神, spirit-quieting) — so the *yang sheng* reading here is unusually well founded. **But the grade does not transfer cleanly to this species.** The classic's lemma is plain *Chang Pu* 菖蒲, without the 石 "stone" qualifier that marks *Shí Chāng Pú* 石菖蒲, and its stated habitat in the same sentence is "pools and swamps" — the wetland station of the *A. calamus* complex, not the wet-rock streamside habit of *A. gramineus*. The species assignment *Rhizoma Acori Graminei* in the Blue Poppy edition is the translator's modern binomial identification; the Han text does not make it, and the *calamus*/*gramineus* differentiation is not drawn in this text at all. The upper-grade status is therefore securely attested for the classical *chang pu* lemma and **not** securely attested for *A. gramineus* specifically. Cultivation status: **(b) framed** — an upper-grade 上品 status is recorded at (a) strength for the *Bencaojing*'s *chang pu* lemma (Yang Shou-zhong, trans., 1998, p. 21), but the identification of that lemma with *A. gramineus* rather than the *calamus* complex is a modern editorial one and is not carried across here; see entries I_534 / I_047.

**rGyud-bzhi / Tibetan (FMT).** No Tibetan cultivation status is attested for *Acorus gramineus*, and on the sources retrieved no secure Tibetan attestation of this species is available at all. Wangyal et al.'s dataset of Sowa Rigpa materia medica — compiled from the *Four Medical Treatises*, the *Crystal Orb and Rosary*, and Gawé Dorjé's *Stainless Crystal Mirror of Materia Medica*, covering roughly 700 of the most commonly used substances — yields no *A. gramineus* identification (Wangyal, Tidwell, Dhondrup, Yungdrung, Dhondrup, He & Zhang, *Data in Brief* 33: 106498, 2020), and the entry itself carries no FMT body paragraph and no Tibetan indication to test. Any Tibetan engagement of the genus that a reader may have in mind belongs to a different *Acorus* and would need to be established for that plant on its own evidence — transferring it to *A. gramineus* is precisely the species-conflation this entry warns against in its own Contraindications, where the toxicological profile is stated to differ by β-asarone content. Two structural points also apply and are worth stating rather than leaving implied. The Four Tantras contain **no formal category of "substances for subtle-body practice"**, so there is no Tibetan slot corresponding to a *san pin* grade into which this rhizome could be placed even if it were attested. And *bcud len* (essence extraction) is a genuine practice located inside the Four Tantras (Gerke, "'Treating the Aged' and 'Maintaining Health': Locating *bcud len* Practices in the Four Medical Tantras') — but it may only be claimed for a species a source actually places there, and none does so for this one. The applicable Sowa Rigpa frame would be the three *nyes pa* (*rlung* / *mkhris pa* / *bad kan*), rooted in the three poisons, with medicine as the third of the four treatment branches; nothing attaches that frame to *A. gramineus*. Cultivation status: **(c) absent.**

**Practitioner's note.** This is the rare case where the tradition's cultivation language is strong and the cultivator's honest response is still restraint. The classical *chang pu* entry says almost exactly what a practitioner would want said — protracted taking, the body lightened, memory improved, confusion prevented, the nine orifices freed — and that language sits in the one register the Chinese sources genuinely formalise, the *shen* stratum. Two things nonetheless bar a simple long-term regimen. First, the grade is attached to a lemma, not to a binomial: the Han text says "pools and swamps", which is not where stone sweetflag grows, so a cultivator invoking the *Bencaojing*'s authority for *A. gramineus* is borrowing a status the text did not assign to this plant. Second, and decisively, 久服 is the exact instruction the material register forbids. β-asarone is genotoxic in animal studies, crosses the blood-brain barrier, and is regulated by exposure limit in several jurisdictions — so the very pattern of use the upper grade prescribes, protracted and cumulative, is the pattern that concentrates the risk. The two registers here point opposite ways, and neither adjudicates the other; what the cultivator does is take the tradition's *aim* (clarity of the input channels, the senses re-opened) and refuse its *duration*. In practice that means low dose, short course, in formula, with a defined stopping point — the exact opposite of an upper-grade regimen — and it means treating the aroma as a punctuation mark in practice rather than a daily foundation. On the Tibetan side there is nothing to do, because there is nothing attested to do it with.

### Contraindications

- **β-asarone toxicity — the defining caution.** β-asarone is a phenylpropanoid with documented **genotoxic and carcinogenic/hepatotoxic concerns** in animal studies; several jurisdictions regulate maximum β-asarone levels in foodstuffs. Prefer low-β-asarone *A. gramineus* over high-β-asarone *A. calamus*; use low dose, short courses, and avoid high-concentration essential-oil products and chronic use.
- **Pregnancy and lactation: avoid** — genotoxic/mutagenic concern for β-asarone makes this a firm contraindication.
- **Yin-deficiency-with-heat, excessive sweating, seminal emission:** the warm, drying, dispersing aromatic is inappropriate.
- **Interactions:** CNS-active; caution with sedatives and other centrally-acting drugs. Possible additive effects with agents affected by hepatic metabolism.
- **Species/identity:** confirm *A. gramineus* vs *A. calamus* — the toxicological profile differs by β-asarone content.
