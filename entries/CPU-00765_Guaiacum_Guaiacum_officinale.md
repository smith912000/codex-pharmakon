# Guaiacum — Lignum Vitae, Holy Wood (Palo Santo de Indias), Guaiac, Pockwood

## *Guaiacum officinale* L. | Family: Zygophyllaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, BM, DMM
**Energetic Classification:** Hot and dry (Galenic: hot in the second-to-third degree, dry in the second) — a resinous, warming, drying agent read as an attenuator of thick cold humours and a promoter of sweat.
**Training Phase:** Purgation / Mobilisation — a heating, diaphoretic, alterative substance deployed to move stagnation and "cook" a cold, damp, chronic condition, not a gentle daily tonic.

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Dioscorides / Graeco-Roman lineage (DMM):** *Guaiacum* is a genuinely post-classical addition to the European materia medica — a New World tree unknown to Dioscorides himself. It is important to state this honestly: the ancient Greek herbal has no entry for Lignum Vitae. What the tradition did was slot the imported wood and resin into the *existing Dioscoridean framework* of hot, dry, sweat-promoting woods and resins, treating it by analogy to sarsaparilla, china root and sassafras — the "sudorific woods." Its inclusion in European pharmacopoeias is a case study in how the classical system absorbed Columbian-exchange botanicals.

**Avicenna / Unani (CM):** Post-Columbian Unani and Arabic-influenced European practice received guaiacum through the same channel: classed as *hār-yābis* (hot and dry), an opener of obstructions, a resolver of cold phlegmatic swellings, and a diaphoretic. It entered the Unani repertoire as a foreign but readily assimilated hot-dry alterative.

**Badianus (Aztec/Nahua herbal, BM):** The *Badianus Manuscript* (Libellus de Medicinalibus Indorum Herbis, 1552) records the Indigenous Mesoamerican and Caribbean medicine into which guaiacum was native. The resinous heartwood ("holy wood," *lignum sanctum*) was a New World remedy for skin eruptions, ulcers and "the pox" long before it crossed the Atlantic — its European fame is downstream of Indigenous American use.

**Columbian-exchange history — stated honestly:** In the early 16th century guaiacum became Europe's celebrated remedy for **syphilis** ("the French disease"), promoted heavily (the Fugger banking house held a commercial interest in the trade) as a gentler alternative to mercury. The historical record is candid that its efficacy against syphilis was overstated; it worked partly as a heroic sweating cure and its reputation outran its results. It remains a real and instructive chapter in pharmaceutical history rather than a validated antitreponemal.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** The wood is called *Lignum Vitae* — "wood of life" — and *Holy Wood*, names earned by its extreme hardness, density (it sinks in water) and self-lubricating resin, which made it prized for ship bearings and mortars. The symbolic reading of an incorruptible, resin-sealed heartwood as a purifier of corrupted, "rotten" humours maps onto its real chemistry: the heartwood is saturated with guaiac resin whose lignans are genuine antioxidants and radical-scavengers — the guaiac test for peroxidase/blood exploits exactly this redox reactivity.

### Active Components

- **Guaiac resin (gum guaiac)** — up to ~20–25% of the heartwood; the medicinally active exudate.
- **Lignans** — dehydroguaiaretic acid, meso-dihydroguaiaretic acid, furoguaiacin (α-guaiaconic acid), furoguaiaoxidin, nectandrin B and related compounds; the redox-active and anti-inflammatory fraction.
- **Guaiaconic acid / α-guaiaconic acid** — the readily oxidised phenol responsible for the blue colour in the guaiac (guaiacum) test used in faecal occult blood screening.
- **Guaiacol** — a phenolic degradation product associated with the wood/resin.
- **Saponins, terpenoids and vanillin-type aromatics.**

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Guaiacum is filed as a **Mobilisation-phase alterative and diaphoretic.** Its traditional and plausible pharmacological actions are anti-inflammatory (lignan-mediated suppression of nitric-oxide and inflammatory signalling) and antioxidant, historically directed at chronic rheumatic and "cold-damp" joint conditions and at cutaneous eruptions. In the Kenetica frame it is a heating, sweat-promoting agent for stuck, chronic, damp-cold terrain — used in courses, not indefinitely, and respecting its GI-irritant resin.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, direct clinical evidence for therapeutic guaiacum is sparse; the modern literature is mechanistic and diagnostic rather than trial-based.

- Lignans purified from guaiac resin (dehydroguaiaretic acid, meso-dihydroguaiaretic acid, furoguaiacin and others) significantly suppressed nitric-oxide production in interleukin-1β-treated rat hepatocytes, supporting a real anti-inflammatory basis for the resin's traditional reputation (Nakano et al., *J Nat Med*, 2016; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11418-016-1048-3)).
- The best-characterised modern application is diagnostic: guaiac-based faecal occult blood testing exploits the oxidation chemistry of guaiaconic acid; a systematic review examined whether medication restrictions before guaiac FOBT are necessary and found little evidence supporting routine NSAID/anticoagulant withdrawal (Konrad & Katz, *Can Fam Physician*, 2012; PMID 22972722 — no DOI registered in PubMed).

**Evidence grade: traditional-to-preliminary.** There are no robust human efficacy trials for guaiacum as a therapeutic. The anti-inflammatory data are in-vitro/animal; the well-validated modern use is as a chemical reagent. Historical antisyphilitic claims are not supported and should not be repeated as fact.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic / Unani (Avicennian):** hot and dry (2nd–3rd degree); attenuant, resolvent, diaphoretic, opener of obstructions; corrector of cold phlegmatic and "corrupt" humours.
- **European early-modern:** a "sudorific wood" — sweat-driving alterative for chronic venereal and rheumatic disease.
- **Mesoamerican (Badianus/BM):** *palo santo / lignum sanctum* — a purifying heartwood for eruptions, ulcers and pox.
- (No classical TCM or Ayurvedic rasa/guna assignment — guaiacum is not attested in those traditions; stated to avoid fabrication.)

### Contraindications

- **⚠️ Conservation — CITES Appendix II.** *Guaiacum officinale* is listed on **CITES Appendix II**, and the genus *Guaiacum* is protected; the species is threatened by overexploitation for its dense timber and resin. Any sourcing must be legal, documented and sustainable — this is an ethical and legal contraindication to casual use, not merely a clinical one.
- **GI irritation.** Guaiac resin is a gastrointestinal irritant; it can cause nausea, gastric burning and diarrhoea, especially at higher doses or on an empty stomach. Contraindicated in active gastritis/peptic ulcer.
- **Contact dermatitis / allergy.** The resin is a recognised skin sensitiser; handling powdered resin can provoke allergic contact dermatitis.
- **Pregnancy/lactation.** As a heating diaphoretic alterative with irritant resin, avoid in pregnancy and lactation. [USER: verify.]
- **Diagnostic interference.** Because guaiaconic acid drives the FOBT reaction, ingestion of guaiac (and dietary peroxidase/redox-active substances) is relevant to occult-blood test interpretation.
