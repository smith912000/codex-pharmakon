# KUPHI — Kyphi, Cyphi (Dioscorides 1-24, KUPHI)

## *A compound Egyptian-style aromatic (cyphi), a wine-raisin-honey-resin perfume with cyprus (the translators' cross-reference 1-124), juniper, calamus, aspalathus, juncus odoratus and myrrh, as printed; not a single plant; identification as a recipe is firm, and the components are the translators' cross-references, unread here* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM) among the texts on disk.** Single-text entry. The chapter is *1-24. KUPHI* in Book One ("Aromatics"), `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 3758, with the translators' subtitle "Cyphi — a perfume" (line 3759) and the text at lines 3760 to 3765. The translators' index also prints the heading at page 28 (names.csv row for KUPHI, 1-24). The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, and a first-pass check proposed that it was a misreading; the audit disagreed, since Kyphi is a real chapter heading. It is a compound preparation, not a single plant. It has been shelved in Volume I only because the spine files it as a plant row. [USER: verify the shelving]

**Greek, Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter reads: "Cyphi is the composition of a perfume welcome to the Gods. The priests in Egypt use it abundantly. It is also mixed with antidotes and it is given to the asthmatic in drinks. There are many ways that the manufacture of it is carried out, including the following." The recipe given is: "Take one litre of cyprus [1-124], the same amount of ripe juniper berries, six kilos of stoned plump raisins of the sun, two and a half kilos of resin (cleaned again), a half kilo each of calamus aromaticus [1-2, 1-17, 1-114], aspalathus [1-19] and juncus odoratus [4-52, 1-16], twelve teaspoonfuls of myrrh [1-77, 1-73, 4-116], five litres of old wine and one kilo of honey." The method: "Having removed the stones from the raisins pound them and work them together with the wine and myrrh. Pound and sift the other things, mix them with these, and let them drink up the liquid for one day. Afterwards boil the honey until it comes to a glutinous consistency, mix the melted resin carefully with it, and then having pounded all the other things diligently together, put them into a clay jar." The bracketed numbers are the translators' cross-references to other Dioscoridean chapters.

Dioscorides says the Egyptian priests use it; he does not say for which god or what rite. The words "welcome to the Gods" are his. No deity is named and none is assigned here.

**What the sources do not give.** No Latin name for the whole, because it is a compound. No dose for the drink. The component identifications are those of the translators' cross-references (the plants of chapters 1-2, 1-17, 1-19, 1-77, 1-114, 1-124, 4-52 and others), and were not opened for this entry.

### Active Components

The chapter names its ingredients (cyprus, juniper berries, raisins, resin, calamus aromaticus, aspalathus, juncus odoratus, myrrh, wine, honey). In general terms, such a mixture is rich in aromatic resin and terpene compounds and in sugars from raisins and honey. No analysis of the compound was read. The identity of each component is for its own chapter. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** A perfume (burnt or used for scent), an ingredient mixed with antidotes, and a drink given to the asthmatic.
- **What the text does not say.** An amount for the drink, a duration, or the rite for which it is made.
- The Codex does not recommend the drink. A complex fermented resin and wine mixture of unknown strength is not a practice.

### Preparation & Dosing

The recipe above is Dioscorides' own and is a manufacturing recipe in his litres and kilos (as the translators render them), not a dose. For the drink given to the asthmatic, no amount is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] The recipe requires a clay jar and a stage of standing (the dry ingredients 'drink up the liquid for one day'), and then the honey and melted resin are added.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Incense and aromatic mixtures were used in antiquity as perfumes; their scent comes from volatile resin and plant compounds. The Egyptian setting of the chapter is the author's own statement. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek, Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides records that cyphi is "welcome to the Gods", that the Egyptian priests use it abundantly, that it is mixed in antidotes and given to the asthmatic in drinks. The Dioscoridean frame offers no subtle-body system, and none is imported here. What is attested is a provenance and a method: a recipe with its ingredients and an exact order of working. That is the discipline of attention (provenance, preparation, order) that the Codex reads in Dioscorides, and it is not a cultivation status. Recording the priests' use is not endorsing it. The status is therefore (b), a framed temple and perfume use, with no cultivation grade attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A recipe for a perfume and a drink, recorded as an Egyptian priests' practice. What a cultivator can take from it is the care of the recipe: the stoning and pounding, the standing, the honey boiled to glutinous, the melted resin. The Codex draws no spiritual claim from it that Dioscorides does not make, and Dioscorides makes none beyond the words 'welcome to the Gods'.

### Contraindications

- **Alcohol, honey and resin.** The drink is a wine-based preparation; it is unsuitable for children, in pregnancy, for anyone avoiding alcohol, and for anyone with allergy to resins or to the plants listed. [USER: verify]
- **Asthma.** The text gives it to the asthmatic. Asthma needs medical care; this entry is not advice.
- **Identity.** Some ingredients depend on the translators' identifications.
- **Smoke.** Burning aromatics indoors can irritate the airways, an obvious general caution.
