# Vasuka — Vasuka (as printed in Bhishagratna's Sushruta Samhita)

## *Vasuka, a drug named in two groups of Sutrasthana ch.38; the translator prints no gloss, no botanical name is given in any source read, and the identification is unresolved (confidence low)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Vasuka [SS]", taken from an extraction of Bhishagratna's translation; this entry replaces it. The locus given was "SS Sutra 38, Varuna'di group (Vol. I line 15388)".

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Vasuka is printed in the Sutrasthana, chapter 38 (locus: Varuna'di group, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 47; Viratarva'di group, line 61; the same text is in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, line 15388). Chapter 38 is "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3-6), and says that "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (lines 8-10). The name is given by the translator without a gloss, a botanical name or a description.

In the Varuna'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 44-51 (the list), lines 52-55 (the metrical text)), Vasuka is one of the drugs listed. The group's metrical text reads: "The group is possessed of the efficacy of reducing the deranged Kapham and fat and proves efficacious in cases of cephalaegia, Gulma and internal abscesses." That is a property of the group as a whole, not of Vasuka alone.

In the Viratarva'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 57-64 (the list), lines 65-72 (the metrical text)), Vasuka is one of the drugs listed. The group's metrical text reads: "The group subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and ... proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles." (the sentence runs across a page break after "Vata and") That is a property of the group as a whole, not of Vasuka alone.

**Other places the name appears.** In the Viratarva'di list the e-text prints "Agnimantha, Morata Vasuka, Vasira" (line 61); the Varuna'di list has Morata as a separate drug (line 46), so it is likely that "Morata" and "Vasuka" are two names run together, but this is not stated. [USER: verify] Sutrasthana ch.39 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt`, lines 85-92) lists Vasuka among the Vata-soothing drugs. Chikitsasthana ch.7 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch07.txt` lines 24-28) has it in the Vataja Asmari ghee, with Vasira and others. In Sutrasthana ch.46 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, line 2877) the text on drinks and after-drinks says: "The Asava of Shvadanstra or of Vasuka should be taken after Pippalis, etc." An Asava is a fermented infusion; the lines read give no recipe for it.

The chapter closes (lines 554-566) by saying that the groups "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", and that plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, Ghritas or potions are to be prepared "according to the exigencies of each individual case". That is a general rule of the chapter and is not a recipe for this drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The text uses it in two ganas (Varuna'di and Viratarva'di), in the Vata-soothing list of ch.39, in the Vataja Asmari ghee of ch.7, and as a wine (Asava) taken after pungent foods in ch.46.
- What the text does not say: what Vasuka is, which part is used, or any dose.

The Codex does not translate the text's terms (Vayu, Pitta, Kapham, Gulma, Shosha, Asmari and the rest) into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend a plant it cannot identify.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Vasuka stands in the dravya-group classification of Sutrasthana ch.38, a classification by therapeutic action. Both groups are therapeutic, not rasayana or vajikarana groups. A search of the chapter files on disk for the spellings printed here found no mention of Vasuka in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters, and no text read calls it an elixir or a drug of vitality. The register is therefore (b): framed by its therapeutic group, with no cultivation status attested. The Codex does not assign *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* effects to it, because no source read does.

**Practitioner's note.** Vasuka is a name in a list. The tradition's text gives the drug a place among therapeutic groups and little more, and nothing in the record supports a cultivation use. A cultivator can learn from it only how Sushruta organises drugs into groups by action, and that some names in that classification are no longer certainly identified. Nobody should try to find a plant to match the name.
