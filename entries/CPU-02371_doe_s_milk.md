# Doe's Milk (Sushruta Samhita, translator's footnote)

## *Milk of a female deer ("doe"); the English text says only "the milk of a doe" and does not name the species or the Sanskrit; "female deer" is the usual English sense of doe, and the species is not identified; the attestation is the translator's inference from the Sanskrit sentence, not an item printed in the classic's list* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta Samhita only, among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is a translator's footnote in Bhishagratna's English, Sutra ch.45, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` line 442: "From the construction of the present sentence in the original texts, we are warranted to include the milk of a doe, or of a she-mule, or of a cow-rhinoceros in the list, as they sometimes prove beneficial for external applications." The footnote hangs on the sentence at line 415 that lists the milks "which generally come to the use of man" (cow, she-goat, she-camel, ewe, she-buffalo, mare, she-elephant, woman). The doe is therefore not in the classic's list as the English prints it. The translator says the Sanskrit construction allows it to be added.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The Sushruta Samhita (Bhishagratna's English translation, as on disk) opens its milk group in Sutra ch.45 with the sentence "The milk of a cow, she-goat, she-camel, ewe, she-buffalo, mare, she-elephant, or of a woman, is what generally comes to the use of man" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` line 415). The group paragraph that follows is about milk as a whole, not about one animal: "heavy, sweet, slimy, cold, glossy, emollient, laxative and mild" (line 422), "rejuvenating and aphrodisiac" (line 453), "increases the duration of life, and acts as a vitaliser" (line 457) and, through kindred properties, "augments the quality of bodily albumen (Ojah)" (line 461). The footnote at line 442 adds the doe, the she-mule and the cow-rhinoceros to those milks and says only that they "sometimes prove beneficial for external applications". No taste, potency or disease is given for the doe's milk. This is the whole attestation. The weakness of the locus is the reason the row is marked probable and not clear. ### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms the milk of any mammal contains water, protein, fat, lactose and minerals, in proportions that differ by species; no figure for deer was read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Only that it "sometimes proves beneficial for external applications" (footnote, line 442).
- **What the text does not say.** The animal, the disease, the preparation, the dose.

Obtaining deer milk is neither practical nor advisable. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No quality of any kind is printed for the doe's milk. The group description of milk (heavy, sweet, slimy, cold; lines 422-423) is for the milks of the listed animals and is not assigned to it.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* The only locus is a translator's inference that the doe's milk may be added to the list of milks, with a remark on external use. The Sushrutan terms *rasayana*, *vajikarana* and *ojas* are not applied to it. The group paragraph (lines 419-461) says milk as a whole is rejuvenating, increases the duration of life and augments *ojas* (rendered "bodily albumen (Ojah)" by the translator). Those words are printed for the group and are not repeated under this animal, so this entry does not carry them across: the status is framed, not attested for this milk alone. 

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing here for a cultivator. The row records that the translator, reading the Sanskrit, allowed deer milk into the list of milks, and no more.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and zoonosis.** Wild-animal milk can carry infection; no safe handling is described in the source.
- **Conservation and law.** Deer species are protected in many places; taking milk from a wild doe is not advised.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The animal is unnamed beyond "doe"; do not assume a species.
