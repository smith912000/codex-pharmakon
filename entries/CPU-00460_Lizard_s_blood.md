# Lizard's blood — "Lizard's Blood" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus)
## *The blood of the animal Bryan calls "Lizard"; Bryan prints no Egyptian word and no binomial, so the spine row's "Lacertidae sp." is the list-maker's gloss and is not read from the text* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter VII "Organic Remedies (animals)". "Lizard" stands in Bryan's list of the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1394, after "Tortoise" and before "Mole"; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 102, class animal, "no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). The parts-of-animals list (lines 1405-1415) includes "Blood" but ties it to no animal. The spine row reads "Lizard's blood, Lacertidae sp." with the note "Ebers; Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus" and a marker that the identification is uncertain (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 83). Wikipedia was not used.

**Where the blood is prescribed.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "lizard" and "blood". The lizard's blood occurs in Chapter XVI "Diseases of the Eyes" (heading lines 2720-2721), in Bryan's account of ingrowing eyelashes. Bryan introduces it at line 2858: "The victims of ingrowing eyelashes must have been a veritable godsend to the rising ophthalmologist in Ancient Egypt. At the same time they must have proved a very severe drain on the live-stock of the country, as may be seen from a perusal of some of the remedies prescribed for that irritating affliction". Two prescriptions follow.

- **"TO DRIVE OUT TRICHIASIS", lines 2864-2879**: "Myrrh, Lizard's Blood, Bat's Blood. Tear out the Hairs and put thereon in order to make him well." Each of the three ingredients is followed by a single stroke "I" in the text on disk (lines 2868, 2872, 2876); it may be a proportion mark, and the text read does not explain it. [USER: verify]
- **"TO PREVENT THE HAIR GROWING INTO THE EYE AFTER IT HAS BEEN PULLED OUT", lines 2882-2894**: the ingredients listed are "Incense-ground-in-Lizard's-dung", "Cow's Blood, Ass's Blood, Pig's Blood, Dog's Blood, Stag's Blood", "Collyrium" and "Incense", with the direction "Crush, rub into one in the different kinds of Blood, and place on the part whence the Hair has been pulled out so that it may not grow again." Here the lizard's part is its dung, and the lizard's blood is not named in this prescription.

**Related items, printed separately, not counted as the blood.** "Lizard's dung" occurs in a remedy for pterygium (a poultice of lizard's dung, collyrium, soda from Upper Egypt and honey, line 2843) and in the incense above (line 2884). "Black Lizard", with vulva and phallus, is a hair-restorer (lines 1377 and 4031; Bryan calls it "vile" and says some of the other ingredients "cannot be deciphered"). In the domestic chapter a lizard put on the fire kills a scorpion, and a burnt scorpion kills a lizard (line 4224). None of these is the blood.

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say which lizard is meant, how the blood was obtained, in what amount it was used, or why it was chosen for trichiasis. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. The Ghalioungui 1987 glossary was not opened and has no lizard row in `_sources/ebers.csv`.

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it, because the animal is not identified. In general terms, vertebrate blood contains water, proteins and cells, and it clots and dries quickly. Nothing in the sources retrieved says that lizard blood contains anything that acts on hair follicles or the eyelid. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show lizard's blood once, as one of three ingredients (with myrrh and bat's blood) put on the eyelid after the ingrowing hairs had been pulled out. Putting animal blood on the eye region is an infection risk. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, here ingrowing eyelashes, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); no recitation is printed with the trichiasis prescription in Bryan's summary, which is no proof the papyrus gives none. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The record is a three-ingredient eyelid application for ingrowing lashes, with no species and no stated quantity. A cultivator has nothing to do with it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Eye safety:** animal blood near the eye risks infection; ingrowing lashes need medical care.
- **Hygiene and infection:** reptile blood can carry bacteria, including *Salmonella*.
- **Unidentified animal:** the lizard is not identified; some lizards are protected and a few are venomous.
- **Conservation:** many lizard species are protected; the status of any particular species cannot be given from the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
