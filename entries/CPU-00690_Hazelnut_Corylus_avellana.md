# Hazelnut — Hazelnut / Filbert / Cobnut (Latin: *nux pontica*, *nux Avellana*; Arabic: *bunduq*; Chinese: *zhēn zi* 榛子)

## *Corylus avellana* L. | Family: Betulaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT (Assyrian), CM (Avicenna), DMM (Dioscorides), SBJ (Shennong)
**Energetic Classification:** Warm and moist (first degree tending dry on roasting); nourishing, fattening, mildly astringent in the skin
**Training Phase:** Foundation

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Assyrian (AT):** Nuts belong to the oldest layer of Near-Eastern nutrition and medicine; the hazel, native to Anatolia and the Pontic-Caspian rim that bordered Assyria, supplied a storable, oil-dense food valued as a strengthening and warming aliment. Its Latin trade names — *nux pontica*, "Pontic nut" — record the fact that the classical Mediterranean imported hazelnuts from exactly this Anatolian/Black-Sea region, the same corridor the Assyrian world drew on.

**Dioscorides (DMM):** Dioscorides describes the *karyon pontikon* (Pontic nut / hazel) in *De Materia Medica*, noting it as nourishing but heavy on the stomach, and recording its use — burned and ground, mixed with fat or oil — as a topical for scalp complaints and, taken with honey and pepper, for chronic cough. The pairing of an oily nut with warming honey/pepper is a coherent Galenic prescription for a cold, moist chest condition.

**Avicenna (CM):** In the *Qanun*, the hazelnut (*bunduq*) is treated as a warming, nutritious nut that fattens the body and benefits the chest and cough, while being slow to digest; roasting was recommended to lighten it, and it was combined with honey for pectoral use. Unani practice descending from Avicenna continues to use *bunduq* as a nervine/brain tonic and restorative.

**Shennong (SBJ):** In the Chinese materia medica tradition rooted in the *Shennong Bencao Jing* lineage, hazelnut (*zhēn zi*) is classed as a sweet, neutral food-medicine that tonifies the spleen and stomach, boosts qi and strengthens the body — the standard reading for an energy-dense, sweet, oily seed.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Four traditions converge on one image: a warming, strengthening, *fattening* seed — the nut as concentrated nourishment. The pharmacology is exactly that: hazelnut kernel is ~60% lipid, dominated by monounsaturated oleic acid, with tocopherols, phytosterols and B-vitamins — a genuinely restorative fat. The traditional "hard to digest, roast it first" caution mirrors the real behaviour of a dense lipid load and the fact that roasting alters the skin's tannins and the kernel's texture. The astringent, medicinal quality the ancients located in the nut's covering corresponds to the concentrated phenolics of the hazelnut *skin* (pellicle) that modern chemistry singles out as the plant's richest antioxidant fraction.

### Active Components

- **Lipids (~60% of kernel)** — predominantly oleic acid (monounsaturated), with linoleic acid; the basis of hazelnut oil and its cardiometabolic profile.
- **Tocopherols** — high α-tocopherol (vitamin E); a major fat-soluble antioxidant.
- **Phytosterols** — β-sitosterol predominant.
- **Phenolics (concentrated in the skin/pellicle)** — gallic acid, caffeic acid, flavan-3-ols/proanthocyanidins; the principal antioxidant and antimicrobial fraction, and a valorised roasting by-product.
- **Taxanes (in leaf/cell culture, not the kernel)** — *Corylus avellana* callus and cell-suspension cultures produce paclitaxel (Taxol) and related taxanes, a notable and unexpected biosynthetic capacity outside the *Taxus* genus.
- **Protein, fibre, B-vitamins, minerals** — including manganese, copper and magnesium.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Hazelnut is a **Foundation-phase nutritive tonic** — a building, warming, energy-dense food-medicine for the base of the training arc. Its job is restoration and consolidation: healthy monounsaturated fat, vitamin E, sterols and minerals to underwrite tissue repair and steady energy, with the phenolic-rich skin supplying antioxidant cover. Traditionally a chest/cough and brain-tonic nut, in Kenetica it anchors the nourishing, grounding end of the spectrum — taken as whole roasted kernels, ground meal or cold-pressed oil. It is a food first and a remedy second, which is precisely its Foundation role.

### Scientific Correlation

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed:

- **Bottone et al. (2019), *Planta Medica*** — Review of *Corylus avellana* kernel and by-products: phenolics are the most abundant phytochemicals in both kernel and by-products (skin, shell, leaf), with documented antioxidant, antiproliferative and antimicrobial activity; taxane derivatives are noted in the leaves. DOI: [10.1055/a-0947-5725](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-0947-5725)
- **Bestoso et al. (2006), *BMC Biotechnology*** — In-vitro cell cultures from hazel stems, leaves and seeds produced Taxol, 10-deacetyltaxol and 10-deacetylbaccatin III at levels comparable to yew cultures, with antiproliferative activity on human tumour cells — evidence that *Corylus avellana* possesses the paclitaxel pathway once thought unique to *Taxus*. DOI: [10.1186/1472-6750-6-45](https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6750-6-45)
- **Salehi et al. (2020), *PLoS ONE*** — Fungal elicitors (cell extract plus culture filtrate of *Camarosporomyces flavigenus*) significantly boosted paclitaxel biosynthesis and secretion in *C. avellana* cell suspension culture, supporting hazel cell culture as an inexpensive elicited production system for the drug. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0236191](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0236191)

**Evidence grade: Moderate.** The nut's lipid/tocopherol/phenolic composition and antioxidant activity are well characterised; the paclitaxel-from-hazel work is robust and reproducible but concerns *cell culture/leaf tissue*, not the edible kernel — no clinical claim should be attached to eating hazelnuts on that basis. Traditional pectoral and tonic uses are well attested but not rigorously trialled.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic / Unani:** Warm and moist (1st degree), tending dry when roasted; nourishing, fattening, brain/nerve-restorative, pectoral; slow to digest.
- **TCM (Shennong lineage):** *zhēn zi* — sweet, neutral; tonifies spleen–stomach, boosts qi, strengthens the body.

### Contraindications

- **⚠ Tree-nut allergy (potentially fatal):** hazelnut is a major tree-nut allergen and a recognised cause of IgE-mediated reactions up to and including **anaphylaxis**. It also cross-reacts with birch pollen in oral allergy syndrome. Anyone with tree-nut allergy, or birch-pollen sensitisation with nut symptoms, must **strictly avoid** hazelnut in all forms (kernel, meal, oil, skin extract). Cross-contamination in shared processing is common — read labels.
- **Aflatoxin / mould:** poorly stored hazelnuts can carry aflatoxin-producing moulds; use fresh, dry, well-stored nuts.
- **Caloric density:** very energy-dense; portion accordingly in weight-management contexts.
- **Taxane note:** paclitaxel content is a feature of cell culture/leaf tissue, **not** the food kernel — do not treat culinary hazelnuts as a source of a chemotherapeutic agent.
