# Shore stone — "Stone-from-the-Shore" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus mineral list)
## *A stone named for where it comes from; no Egyptian word, no description and no identification is given in the sources retrieved* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The only locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter V "Mineral Remedies", in the alphabetical list of mineral drugs (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1206; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 36, "Stone-from-the-Shore", class mineral, "Bryan's English identification"). The list, as printed, reads at lines 1206-1208:

"Stone-from-the-Shore / Stone-from-the-Meeting-of-Wtahte-rs [sic, for Waters] / Stone-from-the-Parting-of-the- Waters Stone-from-Memphis Knife-stone Black Knife-stone 'Trash-from-the-Knife-Stone".

The names.csv row for *Stone-from-the-Shore* carries these as the forms in its group: Stone-from-the-Shore, Stone-from-the-Meeting-of-Waters, Stone-from-the-Parting-of-the-Waters, Stone-from-Memphis, Knife-stone, Black Knife-stone, Trash-from-the-Knife-Stone. The list is a head-word list of drugs the papyrus prescribes; it states no use, no prescription number and no identification for any item in it. The Codex's own Ebers list `_sources/ebers.csv` has no row for this item.

**What Bryan says about the list.** In the opening of Chapter V (lines 1133-1137), Bryan writes that few of the mineral drugs "are prescribed in their crude or native state", naming "Antimony, Calamine, Granite, Ink, Indigo, and Sulphur" as almost exhausting that class, and that "outside of these the source of origin of the drugs is strictly laid down, or various preparations made from the parent drug are prescribed", with the examples "the Iron must be Iron-from-Apollonopolis-parva", "the Soot must be Soot-from-the-bet'a-pot", "the Soda must be Soda-from-Upper-Egypt". Stone-from-the-Shore is a drug of this second sort: it is named by its place of origin. That is a statement about the form of the name; it says nothing of the stone's identity.

**Searches made.** The whole Bryan text on disk was searched for "shore", "Stone-from", "Meeting of", "Parting of the Waters" and "Memphis". "Shore" occurs only in the list (line 1206). So no prescription containing Stone-from-the-Shore was found in the text on disk, and none is described here. Of the items listed beside it, only two appear in running text: Stone-from-Memphis, ground up with seeds and grain in the "Milk-of-a-Woman-who-has-borne-a-Son" for seven days in a salve that "does not seem to have been very successful" (line 3299, in Bryan's discussion of the *uha* disease), and Knife-stone and Black Knife-stone (running text at lines 1363, 2120, 2776 and 2844, not read in full). Those are different items and are not used here to describe the shore stone.

**Identity.** Unknown. Bryan gives only the English rendering; no Egyptian word or mineral is attached to it, and the extract README (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/README.md`) records Bryan as saying that many further substances are "completely hidden from us under the guise of meaningless words" and so are not in his lists (that quotation was not checked against the Bryan text). The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis; for this one there is not even a hypothesis. It may be a stone gathered at a shore or a stone whose name refers to the shore for another reason; the texts retrieved do not say. [USER: verify]
