### Origin & Tradition

*Nepeta racemosa* — Caucasian or "blue" catmint, long grown as the ornamental *N. mussinii* — is an aromatic Lamiaceae of the Caucasus and northern Iran, one of the roughly 250 *Nepeta* species whose defining chemistry is the volatile iridoid **nepetalactone**. Traditionally the catmints occupy the classic aromatic-mint niche: a warming, carminative, diaphoretic, mildly calming herb taken as tea for chills, upset stomach, restlessness and to "bring out a sweat" in fevers — the same functional slot as true catnip (*Nepeta cataria*, in codex). As with most of the aromatic mints, the historical record attaches a **mint-concept** to catmint: pre-modern authorities described *kalaminthē* / the calamints and catmints as a loose group of pungent, warming, wind-dispersing mints, rarely resolving them to modern species. This entry honours the tradition while stating plainly that most hard data belong to *N. cataria* and other *Nepeta*, not to *racemosa* specifically.

**AT — Assyrian (aromatic-mint references) `[USER: verify]`:** Mesopotamian medical tablets list numerous aromatic pot-herbs and mints among carminative, digestive and fumigant simples. No secure identification of *Nepeta racemosa* (or even the *Nepeta* genus) in the Assyrian corpus is established; the tag marks the **aromatic-mint carminative tradition** the plant belongs to, not a confirmed tablet reading. **`[USER: verify the Assyrian attestation — the native name above is a placeholder, not a confirmed cuneiform reading.]`**

**DMM — De Materia Medica (Dioscorides), Καλαμίνθη (mint-concept):** Dioscorides treats the *kalaminthē* group (calamints/catmints) as warming, drying, pungent herbs — carminative and digestive, diaphoretic in fevers, emmenagogue (menses-promoting), and vermifuge, applied both internally as decoction and externally. This warming-aromatic doctrine is the mint-concept into which the catmints, including *N. racemosa*, fall; it is honest at the level of the aromatic-oil function rather than as a species-specific attestation.

**CM — Canon of Medicine (Ibn Sina), *naʿnāʿ al-hirr* / the cat-loved mint:** The Arabic tradition knew a "cat's mint" — an aromatic mint that excites cats — classed among the warm, dry mints used to settle the stomach, expel wind, promote sweat and menstruation, and gently soothe. Ibn Sina's mint doctrine (warm-dry, carminative, resolvent, emmenagogue) maps precisely onto the essential-oil pharmacology of *Nepeta*.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — mapping doctrine onto real chemistry.** The catmint doctrines converge on one molecule and its context. **(1) The "cat-exciting" signature** — the defining folk observation — is a direct readout of **nepetalactone**, the volatile iridoid monoterpene that triggers the feline response; the traditions were, in effect, naming the plant by its principal active compound's most visible bioassay. **(2) The carminative/diaphoretic/aromatic-dispersing doctrine** maps onto the essential-oil pharmacology of the whole terpene-and-nepetalactone volatile fraction — antispasmodic smooth-muscle relaxation of the gut (the "expels wind" claim) and cutaneous vasodilation/sweating from a warming aromatic oil. **(3) The mild calmative / "soothing" doctrine** maps, more weakly, onto sedative effects reported across *Nepeta* essential oils and onto nepetalactone's activity at the **TRPA1** sensory channel — the same target behind its insect-repellency. **(4) The emmenagogue doctrine** maps onto the volatile-oil uterine-stimulant caution shared by aromatic Lamiaceae, and is the basis of the pregnancy contraindication. The honest deflation: the cat-attractant and insect-repellent actions are **strongly** attested for nepetalactone; the human sedative/carminative doctrines are **plausible but thinly evidenced**, and mostly in other *Nepeta*.

### Active Components

**Iridoid monoterpene volatiles (the defining chemistry):**
- **Nepetalactone** (as *cis-trans* and *trans-cis* isomers) — the principal volatile iridoid of *Nepeta*; the **cat-attractant**, a potent **insect/tick repellent** (a **TRPA1 agonist**), and the carrier of the genus's mild sedative reputation. Isomer ratio strongly determines bioactivity: in catnip oils the *cis-trans* isomer drives tick repellency (Amiro et al. 2026). Total nepetalactone content varies enormously with growth stage, peaking around floral-bud/flowering (Gomes et al. 2024).
- **Nepetalactone-poor chemotypes exist:** some *Nepeta* essential oils are dominated instead by **α-pinene** or **menthone** rather than nepetalactone (Amiro et al. 2026) — a caution that "catmint oil" is not a fixed composition. *N. racemosa* chemotype should be verified before assuming a nepetalactone-rich oil. `[USER: verify chemotype]`

**Supporting essential-oil and phenolic fraction:**
- **Monoterpenes/sesquiterpenes** (α-pinene, menthone, caryophyllene-type) — carminative, antimicrobial, aromatic-dispersing character
- **Flavonoids and phenolic acids** (flavone glycosides, rosmarinic-type acids) — antioxidant; co-accumulate with the iridoids (Gomes et al. 2024); the broad *Nepeta* biological-activity base (Formisano et al. 2011)

**Data honesty:** the component and activity profile is drawn overwhelmingly from ***Nepeta cataria* and pooled *Nepeta* species**; dedicated pharmacology of *N. racemosa* specifically is **sparse**, and the entry says so wherever it matters.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Catmint is a gentle aromatic `[RESET]` herb — carminative, diaphoretic, faintly calming — plus a genuinely useful topical repellent.

**1. Carminative / digestive settling (RESET).** The essential-oil antispasmodic action supports the traditional "settles the stomach, expels wind" use — a mild after-meal or nervous-gut tea. Aligned with the broad antispasmodic/analgesic activity reported across *Nepeta* (Formisano et al. 2011).

**2. Diaphoretic, mild calmative for restlessness.** Warm aromatic tea to encourage sweating in early chill/fever and to take the edge off restlessness before sleep — the true-catnip role, extended by congener. Kept at low-tier ◑ Shen: real but modest.

**3. Topical insect/tick repellent (the best-evidenced action).** Nepetalactone is a well-documented arthropod repellent and TRPA1 agonist — a practical field/training-environment use as a botanical repellent, honestly the strongest thing this plant does.

**Deliberately NOT a strong sedative or anxiolytic** — the calming action is faint and thinly evidenced; do not position catmint as a hypnotic.

### Scientific Correlation

*According to PubMed, the following peer-reviewed sources were retrieved. Note the genus-level (not species-specific) nature of most evidence.*

- **Nepetalactone as insect/tick repellent (STRONG, genus/catnip-anchored):** Catnip (*Nepeta cataria*) essential oil rich in nepetalactone repelled *Ixodes scapularis* ticks, with the **cis-trans isomer** responsible for the activity (84 % repellency at 2 h) and electrophysiological suppression of host-cue response — Amiro et al., *J Chem Ecol* 2026 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10886-025-01680-5). Nepetalactone is a **TRPA1 agonist** driving arthropod aversion — Li et al., *J Adv Res* 2025 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2025.07.026). *Nepeta*-genus nepetalactone iridoids are established cat-attractants and insect-repellents — Yu et al., *Front Plant Sci* 2025 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2024.1514614). **Grade: STRONG for repellency of nepetalactone; MODERATE as applied to *N. racemosa*** (chemotype-dependent).
- **Nepetalactone content is stage- and cultivar-dependent (mechanistic honesty):** Total nepetalactones in catnip peak at floral-bud to partial-flowering and vary with propagation and environment — Gomes et al., *Molecules* 2024 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules29092001). Supports the dosing caveat that potency is not fixed. **Grade: STRONG (for the variability point).**
- **Broad *Nepeta* pharmacology (carminative/sedative doctrines — MODERATE/INDIRECT):** A review of *Nepeta* species documents essential-oil and phenolic constituents with antispasmodic (parasympatholytic), analgesic, antimicrobial, antioxidant and vasodilator activities — Formisano et al., *Chem Biodivers* 2011 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbdv.201000191). This underpins the carminative/diaphoretic/mild-calmative traditional doctrines at the **genus** level. **Grade: MODERATE, indirect** — supportive of the herbal class, not a species-specific efficacy trial.
- **Human sedative/anxiolytic effect size for *N. racemosa*:** **NULL for this run** — no controlled human trial of *N. racemosa* sedation/anxiolysis was retrieved. The calmative claim is genus-inferred and ethnobotanical. **Deflated: presented as mild tradition, not demonstrated hypnotic effect.**

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Primary Tier:** ◉ Qi — an aromatic Qi-mover: disperses stagnant/rebellious Stomach Qi (carminative), releases the exterior (diaphoretic) — the classic warm aromatic mint action.
**Secondary Tier:** ◑ Shen (mild) — a faint calming-of-spirit, at the lowest tier.
**Thermal/humoral:** warm, dry, pungent-aromatic, resolvent (Dioscorides' and Ibn Sina's warm-dry mint reading).
**Honest caveat:** classification is the aromatic-mint concept applied by congener from *N. cataria*; robust species-specific energetic data for *N. racemosa* are lacking.

### Contraindications

- **⚠️ Pregnancy — avoid.** The aromatic Lamiaceae emmenagogue tradition (Dioscorides, Ibn Sina) plus the volatile-oil uterine-stimulant caution shared by the catmints make internal use inadvisable in pregnancy; avoid emmenagogue doses. Caution in lactation. `[USER: verify]`
- **Essential-oil concentration** — do not ingest concentrated *Nepeta* essential oil; internal EO dosing is unestablished. Volatile monoterpenes at high dose are mucosal/CNS irritants.
- **Sedative interaction** — theoretical additive effect with CNS depressants; the effect is mild but combine cautiously.
- **Topical sensitisation** — patch-test the oil before broad skin use; possible contact irritation.
- **Chemotype uncertainty** — because *Nepeta* oils range from nepetalactone-rich to α-pinene/menthone-dominant, effects and safety vary by source material; do not assume a standard composition. `[USER: verify chemotype]`

**Distinguish from *Nepeta cataria* (True Catnip, in codex):** *N. cataria* is the classic, best-studied catmint and the source of nearly all the pharmacological data cited here; *N. racemosa* (= *N. mussinii*) is a related ornamental catmint sharing the nepetalactone chemistry but with a **distinct, less-characterised** essential-oil profile and thinner evidence base. Treat *N. racemosa* claims as congener-inferred until species-specific data exist.
