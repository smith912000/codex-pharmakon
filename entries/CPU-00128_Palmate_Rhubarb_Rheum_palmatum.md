### Origin & Tradition

Palmate rhubarb is a large-leaved perennial of the mountains of western China and the Tibetan borderlands, and the source of the most important purgative root of the Chinese materia medica — **Da Huang** (大黃), "great yellow," named for the deep yellow-orange of its cut root. (This is the medicinal rhubarb of the drug trade, distinct from the culinary garden rhubarb, *Rheum rhabarbarum*, this volume, whose stalks are eaten and whose leaves are toxic.) Medicinal rhubarb root was one of the most valued and lucrative articles of the overland and maritime trade between China and the West for centuries — "Turkey rhubarb" and "China rhubarb" commanding high prices in European pharmacy as the premier purgative.

In Chinese Medicine Da Huang is a principal and precisely-used drug: **bitter and cold, it drains downward and purges accumulation and heat** — the chief herb to "attack" heat-type constipation and the dynamic, high-fever "yangming" bowel-excess pattern, clearing heat by purgation. Beyond purgation it is used to **drain damp-heat** (jaundice, damp-heat dysentery), to **cool the blood and stop bleeding** from heat (paradoxically, in small/charred doses it is haemostatic), to **invigorate blood and dispel stasis** (for traumatic injury, masses, fixed pain), and to resolve toxicity (topically for burns and sores). It is a remarkably versatile drug whose action is dose- and preparation-dependent: large/raw doses purge vigorously; small or processed (steamed, wine-prepared, charred) doses act more gently or shift toward blood-moving/haemostatic effects.

The activity rests on two constituent classes pulling in opposite directions: **anthraquinones (sennosides)** drive the purgative action, while **tannins** are astringent and can be constipating — which is why preparation and dose so strongly shape the effect.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Purge heat-accumulation / constipation (CM core use).** The chief "attacking" purgative for heat-type constipation and high-fever bowel-excess (yangming) patterns — clearing heat by draining downward.
2. **Drain damp-heat.** For jaundice and damp-heat dysentery.
3. **Cool blood / stop bleeding from heat.** In small/charred doses, haemostatic for heat-bleeding (a dose-dependent reversal).
4. **Invigorate blood / dispel stasis.** For traumatic injury, abdominal masses and fixed, stasis-type pain (often wine-prepared).
5. **Resolve toxicity (topical).** For burns, sores and inflammatory skin lesions.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy, breastfeeding and menstruation — avoid.** Strong downward-draining and blood-moving action; uterine-stimulant; passes into breast milk. [USER: verify.]
- **Spleen/stomach deficiency-cold, no true excess/heat (CM).** A cold purgative is contraindicated where there is deficiency without accumulation.
- **Prolonged habitual use.** Anthraquinone laxatives should not be used chronically (dependence, electrolyte loss, melanosis coli).
- **Intestinal obstruction / acute inflammatory abdomen.** As for all strong purgatives.
- **Drug interactions.** Purgative electrolyte loss can potentiate cardiac glycosides; consider absorption interference.
