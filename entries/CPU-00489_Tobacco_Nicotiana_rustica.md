### Origin & Tradition

*Nicotiana rustica* — Aztec tobacco, wild tobacco, *mapacho* — is the potent, small-leaved, high-nicotine tobacco of the Americas, distinct from and far stronger than the milder *N. tabacum* of the global cigarette trade. It is one of the most important **sacred plants of the Western Hemisphere**: across Mesoamerica, the Amazon, and North America, tobacco was (and remains) the pre-eminent ritual plant — the offering, the purifier, the diagnostic and shamanic tool, the "master plant" that governs and protects the use of all others. To the Codex it is the paradigmatic **New-World entheogen-and-medicine**, and a stark case study in the razor line between sacrament and poison.

**Mesoamerican / Aztec Tradition (BM — Badianus / Nahua):**
In Nahua culture *picietl* (*N. rustica*) was a sacred and medical substance of the first rank — chewed with lime, taken as snuff, smoked, and applied topically. The Badianus Manuscript (1552) and Sahagún's Florentine Codex document tobacco in remedies for fatigue, pain, snakebite, skin conditions, and as a protective and strengthening agent; ground *picietl* was rubbed on the body of priests and travellers for strength and against fear, fatigue, and venomous creatures. It was inseparable from divination, offering, and the priestly office.

**South American / Amazonian Tradition (SA):**
In Amazonian shamanism *mapacho* (*N. rustica*) is the central and governing plant medicine — used as smoke (for blowing/soplada in healing and protection), as snuff, as licked paste (*ambil*), and in potent (and dangerous) liquid forms. It is considered the "master plant" and the shaman's primary ally: the plant that cleanses, protects, structures ceremony, diagnoses, and disciplines the other plant spirits. Its use is embedded in long apprenticeship and dietary discipline (*la dieta*), precisely because of its power and danger.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the master plant, the sacred smoke, the poison-teacher:**
Tobacco's doctrine across the Americas is that of the **governing ally and the purifying smoke** — the plant that is simultaneously offering, medicine, protector, and teacher, and whose smoke carries prayer and intention (a New-World parallel to the Old-World incense of ascent, frankincense I_300). Its symbolism is inseparable from its pharmacology: tobacco is a **purge and a discipliner** — it induces (in ritual doses) profound physical purging, sweating, and altered states, understood as cleansing of body and spirit, and it demands respect precisely because it can kill. The Codex reads *N. rustica* as the emblem of the **poison-teacher**: the sacred plant whose power and danger are the same thing, whose ritual use is hedged with apprenticeship and taboo, and which stands as the master-key of an entire hemisphere's plant medicine. Its tragic double is the modern global tobacco epidemic — the same alkaloid, stripped of ritual container and turned into an addictive commodity, becoming the leading preventable cause of death on earth. Sacrament and scourge are the same molecule in different containers.

### Active Components

- **Nicotine (and nornicotine, anabasine)** — the principal alkaloid; *N. rustica* contains markedly higher nicotine than *N. tabacum* (often several-fold). Nicotine is a potent **agonist at nicotinic acetylcholine receptors** (nAChRs) in the autonomic ganglia, neuromuscular junction, and CNS — producing, dose-dependently, stimulation then depression/blockade; it is a fast-acting neurotoxin and a powerfully addictive psychoactive. The ritual purging, tremor, sweating, and altered consciousness of high-dose tobacco are nicotinic effects.
- **Harmala alkaloids (β-carbolines) — trace** — small amounts of MAO-inhibiting β-carbolines have been reported in tobacco, of interest in the context of its psychoactive/ritual use.
- **Other Solanaceous constituents** — minor alkaloids and irritant compounds contributing to the topical and emetic actions.

### Scientific Correlation

*Attribution: nicotine's receptor pharmacology is established neuroscience; the note below references PubMed where relevant.*

The pharmacology of the active alkaloid is well established: **nicotine is a nicotinic acetylcholine receptor agonist** with potent central and autonomic effects and high addictive liability, and it is a genuine toxin (lethal human doses are small relative to the content of concentrated *N. rustica* preparations). Nicotine's CNS activity has been of research interest even in unexpected areas — for example, reviews of phytochemicals in dementia note nicotine (from *Nicotiana* species) among agents studied for cognitive effects (Howes & Perry, 2011, *Drugs & Aging*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2165/11591310-000000000-00000)) — but this is research context, not endorsement. The dominant, robust modern evidence about tobacco is epidemiological and unambiguous: **smoked/chewed tobacco is the leading preventable cause of death worldwide**, and nicotine is powerfully addictive. The Codex records the sacred/ethnobotanical reality and the lethal/addictive reality as the two faces of one plant.

*Evidence-grade note: nicotine pharmacology and tobacco's health harms are firmly established; the traditional ritual/medical uses are ethnographically documented, not clinically validated, and are inseparable from serious toxicity. No therapeutic dose is asserted.*

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity / lethality — absolute:** *N. rustica* is extremely toxic; ingestion of concentrated preparations can be fatal, and nicotine is absorbed through skin. No casual, self-directed, or recreational use of concentrated forms is safe.
- **Addiction:** nicotine is powerfully addictive; habitual tobacco use in any form carries dependence and, when smoked/chewed, the full burden of tobacco-related disease (cancer, cardiovascular and respiratory disease).
- **Cardiovascular / pregnancy / paediatric:** nicotine is contraindicated in pregnancy (fetal harm), in cardiovascular disease, and around children (child poisoning from ingestion is a real hazard).
- **Not a supplement or performance agent:** it has no legitimate role in a training/health-supplement context.
- **Evidence honesty:** ceremonial/medical traditions are documented ethnographically; the robust modern evidence is of harm and addiction. This entry is documentation, not endorsement.
