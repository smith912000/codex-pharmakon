# Stinking Hellebore / Bear's-foot / Setterwort / Setter-grass / Ox-heel / Pied-de-griffon

## *Helleborus foetidus* L. | Family: Ranunculaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Bitter, acrid, foul; violently cold and downward-driving in the Greco-Arabic reading (hot and caustic in the Arabic degree system — the traditions disagree, see below). Purgative, expulsive, extruding.

**Training Phase:** ☠️ **NONE — Toxicological reference only. This plant has no training phase because it has no safe use.**

---

### ☠️ LETHAL — READ THIS BEFORE READING ANYTHING ELSE

**This entry documents a plant that killed children.**

*Helleborus foetidus* contains **bufadienolide cardiac glycosides** — the same pharmacological class as toad venom and squill — alongside **protoanemonin**, a reactive vesicant lactone. It is a Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase poison with a gastrointestinal caustic bolted onto it. There is no therapeutic window. There is no traditional dose worth recovering. The historical use documented below — a violent vermifuge given to children to expel worms — is recorded here **as a cautionary tale about how a tradition can be internally coherent and lethally wrong at the same time**, not as a protocol.

**No dose is given in this entry. No dose will be given in this entry.** Any dosage figure encountered elsewhere for this species should be treated as a historical artefact, not an instruction. Suspected ingestion is a medical emergency: contact emergency services / poisons information immediately.

**This is the third hellebore in the codex.** Do not read across from the others:

| Binomial | *Helleborus foetidus* | *H. viridis* | *H. niger* |

Older sources use "hellebore" without a binomial, and the herbals conflate all three freely — plus, catastrophically, *Veratrum album* (white hellebore), which is not a *Helleborus* at all and is a steroidal-alkaloid poison with a completely different mechanism. **Never accept "hellebore" from a historical text as a species identification.**

### Origin & Tradition

**Assyrian Tablets (AT) — [USER: verify]**

The Nineveh medical corpus contains plant names in the *Uruanna* register that later translators have, at various points, glossed as hellebore-like purgatives. **This identification is not secure and this entry does not rely on it.** The genus *Helleborus* is a European and West-Asian montane taxon; *H. foetidus* specifically is a Western European plant (Britain, France, Iberia, Italy, Central Europe) whose native range does not obviously reach Mesopotamia. Any Assyrian attestation is far more plausibly a different Ranunculaceous or *Veratrum*-type drug — or an untranslatable name.

What can be said honestly: **the Mesopotamian pharmacy did possess a category of violent expulsive drugs administered to drive out an intruding agent**, and the *Ashipu*/*Asu* dual system supplied a rationale in which the intruder was simultaneously material and demonic. That category is the structural ancestor of the doctrine below. Whether *H. foetidus* was ever in it is unknown. **[USER: verify against the Thompson translations before this tag is treated as attested; consider demoting the AT tag to "structural analogue only".]**

**De Materia Medica (DMM) — ἑλλέβορος μέλας / *helleboros melas* (the black hellebore complex)**

Dioscorides describes hellebore, black and white, as one of the great purgative drugs of the Greek pharmacy: taken to evacuate downward (the black) or upward (the white, *Veratrum*), and indicated in mania, melancholia, epilepsy, dropsy, chronic joint disease and — pointedly — **the expulsion of intestinal worms**. The classical doctrine is not one of nourishment or correction. It is **extrusion**: the disease is a thing lodged inside the patient, and the drug's job is to make the body eject it, violently, from whichever end is nearer.

The Greek attestation for *H. foetidus* as a species is weak — *helleboros melas* most plausibly denotes *H. niger* or *H. cyclophyllus*/*H. viridis* depending on locality. What *H. foetidus* inherited was **the doctrine, not the citation**: as European vernacular medicine dispersed the classical purgative teaching across whatever hellebore grew locally, the fetid species — abundant on chalk and limestone in Britain and France — became the working drug of the poor. Its vernacular names record this: **setterwort** and **setter-grass**, **bear's-foot** (from the pedate leaf), **oxheel**, French ***pied-de-griffon*** (griffin's-foot).

**Canon of Medicine (CM) — Avicenna's *kharbaq aswad* (black hellebore) [USER: verify species]**

The *Qanun* treats black hellebore within the four-degree system as a drug of high degree — caustic, powerfully attractive of the coarse humours, indicated in melancholia and mania and in obstinate chronic disease, and explicitly flagged as dangerous, to be given only with correctives and never to the weak, the young, or the febrile. **This is the honest and important part of the Arabic reception: it is more cautious than the Greek.** Avicenna's system is doctrinally committed to the idea that potency and danger scale together, and it therefore built in a hedge — the drug is real, and the drug will kill you — that the vernacular child-vermifuge tradition simply discarded. The Arabic tradition was more right than its European successors, for reasons internal to its own theory. Which species is meant by *kharbaq aswad* is not securely recoverable. **[USER: verify.]**

**The setterwort / seton practice — veterinary (documented European folk-veterinary use)**

The most distinctive historical use of this species is not internal at all. In English and French folk-veterinary practice, a strip of *H. foetidus* root — the **"setter"** — was passed through a puncture in the dewlap or brisket of cattle, or the ear of a pig, and left in place. The wound suppurated. The animal ran a discharge for days or weeks. This was the intent. The practice is a **seton**: a deliberate counter-irritant drain, the veterinary cousin of the human issue-pea and blister-plaster, and its rationale is that disease-matter can be given a door and invited to leave through it.

This is the plant's second doctrine, and it is the more revealing one, because unlike the vermifuge use **it identifies exactly the right chemistry for exactly the wrong reason**.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

Two doctrines, both of extrusion, both mapping onto real chemistry — one onto the wrong molecule, one onto the right one.

**Doctrine 1: the purge as expulsion of the intruder.** Worms are a foreign occupant. A drug that empties the bowel with sufficient violence will carry the occupant out. The purge is not incidental to the cure — the purge *is* the cure, and its violence is the evidence that it is working. This is a **falsifiable, testable, and almost correct** piece of reasoning, and it is the reason children died. The doctrine's fatal move is the equation *stronger evacuation = better expulsion*, which licenses dose escalation without limit. It contains no stopping rule.

Where does this land chemically? The tradition believed it was buying a bowel drug. It was buying **two** drugs. The purge it observed is largely **protoanemonin** — the ranunculin-derived vesicant lactone released enzymatically when the tissue is damaged (chewed, macerated, infused), which blisters mucosa on contact and produces the vomiting, colic and catharsis the herbalist was titrating against. But the thing that killed the child was the **bufadienolides**, which are silent at the point of the purge, absorbed alongside it, and act on the heart. **The doctrine was titrating on the wrong endpoint.** The visible sign (evacuation) and the lethal mechanism (Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase inhibition, arrhythmia) are pharmacologically decoupled. A practitioner watching for "enough purging" is watching a gauge wired to the wrong tank. This is the precise anatomy of the failure, and it is why the vermifuge use produced fatalities rather than merely bad outcomes.

**Doctrine 2: the seton as a door for disease.** Here the doctrine says: make a wound, keep it open with an irritant, and the morbid matter will drain. Modern reading: the "morbid matter" is fictional, but the **counter-irritant is entirely real** — protoanemonin is a Michael-acceptor α,β-unsaturated lactone that alkylates thiols, which is exactly why it blisters tissue and exactly why the wound would not close. The tradition selected *H. foetidus* root over dozens of available plants because it worked, and it worked because of a specific reactive electrophile the tradition could not have known about. **The empiricism was excellent; the theory was decoration.** And note the honest asymmetry: the seton use, being external and applied to livestock, never delivered a cardiac dose. The doctrine that was more magical in its framing was the one that was less lethal in practice — because the route of administration, not the theory, set the toxicology.

**The fetor.** The crushed foliage stinks — hence *foetidus*, hence "stinking hellebore". Doctrine of signatures would have you read the stench as a mark of the plant's power over corruption, and some vernacular reading of that kind is likely. **This one deflates cleanly.** The odour is volatile, not the toxin; it correlates with nothing pharmacological; the killer molecules are non-volatile steroidal glycosides you cannot smell at all. The plant's most conspicuous sensory signal is a **null cue** — and worse than null, because it is a warning that points at the wrong hazard. It says "this is foul", and the naive inference is "so do not eat much of it", when the correct inference is "do not eat any of it, and the smell is not why".

### Active Components

**Bufadienolides (the lethal fraction).** C-24 steroidal cardiac glycosides bearing a six-membered α-pyrone ring at C-17 — structurally distinct from the five-membered butenolide of the cardenolides (digoxin, ouabain) but hitting the same target. Potterat et al. (2024) isolated **eighteen** from the aerial parts of *H. foetidus* specifically: twelve bufadienolides and six 19-norbufadienolides, aglycones and glucosides, including nine previously undescribed compounds ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2024.114329), PMID 39549941). The classical named members of the genus's glycoside set are **hellebrin** and its aglycone **hellebrigenin**; the older literature also uses **helleborin**/**helleborein** loosely and inconsistently, and those names should not be trusted to denote single compounds.

**Protoanemonin (the caustic fraction).** Not present in the intact plant. The plant stores **ranunculin**, a stable glucoside; on tissue damage, β-glucosidase liberates **protoanemonin**, an unstable α,β-unsaturated γ-lactone which then dimerises to the far less reactive **anemonin** (Sirak et al. 2025, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/0109298673294080240221115830), PMID 38425112). This is a **wound-activated chemical weapon**, and its instability is the reason drying and long storage reduce the vesicant hazard while doing essentially nothing to the bufadienolides.

**Other.** GC/MS profiling of *Helleborus* root extract shows a chemically complex matrix dominated by fatty acids (unsaturated > saturated > hydroxy), with antioxidant capacity that nonetheless resolves into a **pro-oxidant cellular phenotype** in vitro (Sarametidis et al. 2026, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biology15110852), PMID 42274502). Ecdysteroids are reported in the genus.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**None. There is no Kenetica use case for this plant.**

It is retained in the codex for four reasons, all of them documentary:

1. **Differential identification.** *H. foetidus* is common on chalk and limestone across Western Europe, evergreen, and flowers in late winter when nothing else does — which is exactly when a forager is most likely to encounter it and least likely to have anything to compare it with. It must be recognisable as a thing to leave alone.
2. **The titration-failure case study.** The vermifuge tradition is the clearest available example of a doctrine that failed *not* because it was mystical but because it selected a visible endpoint decoupled from the lethal mechanism. That failure mode is not confined to hellebore, and the case is worth teaching.
3. **The seton case study.** The inverse: a doctrine that was mystically framed, chemically correct, and safe — because the route of administration bounded the dose.
4. **Toxicological literacy.** Bufadienolide poisoning presents like digitalis poisoning, and that fact has direct clinical relevance.

### Scientific Correlation

**A note on data provenance, stated first because it determines everything below.** The pharmacology of *Helleborus* is dominated by *H. niger*, *H. thibetanus* and *H. odorus*. **This entry does not launder those data into *H. foetidus*.** Where a claim rests on a congener, it is labelled as such and graded down accordingly.

**1. Bufadienolides are present in *H. foetidus* and are cytotoxic — species-specific, strong.**
Potterat et al. isolated 18 bufadienolides/19-norbufadienolides from *H. foetidus* aerial parts and tested them on MCF-7 breast cancer cells: IC₅₀ values spanning **2.4 nM to >10 µM**, with potency strongly correlated to the presence of a **C-19 aldehyde group** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2024.114329), PMID 39549941). **Grade: STRONG** for the claim "this species contains potent bufadienolides", and unusually so — this is direct, recent, species-specific isolation with a structure–activity relationship attached. Note carefully what it does *not* establish: cytotoxicity in a breast cancer cell line is not a therapeutic claim, and the authors' framing (that the data "complement the scientific basis" for anthroposophic oncology use) is the authors' framing, not an efficacy finding. **Low-nanomolar cytotoxicity in vitro is equally consistent with "promising lead" and "extremely poisonous plant" — and the ethnobotanical record settles which.**

**2. The purge doctrine maps onto protoanemonin — mechanistically coherent, chemically established, clinically ungraded.**
Protoanemonin's antimicrobial and cytotoxic action was characterised by Mares (1987), who found MICs of 2.0–7.5 × 10⁻⁴ M against dermatophytes and yeasts and — the mechanistically decisive detail — that **the antifungal action is reversed by cysteine**, implicating thiol alkylation by the unsaturated lactone ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00437648), PMID 3587338). That mechanism is precisely what a vesicant and mucosal irritant needs. Müller et al. (2020) quantified protoanemonin in *Helleborus niger* whole-plant extracts at **0.0345–0.0662 mg/g**, showing it degrades substantially in storage, and concluded that "due to its antibiotic effects, but also its reactivity, protoanemonin is a therapeutically and toxicologically relevant constituent" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpba.2020.113370), PMID 32497949). **Grade: MODERATE for the mechanism, LOW for *H. foetidus* specifically** — the quantitation is *H. niger*, and I am not transferring the numbers. The ranunculin → protoanemonin → anemonin cascade is a chemotaxonomic marker of Ranunculaceae generally (Sirak et al. 2025, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/0109298673294080240221115830), PMID 38425112), so its presence in *H. foetidus* is a safe inference; its concentration is not.

**3. Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase inhibition as the mechanism of death — inferred from class, not demonstrated for this species. NULL for direct evidence.**
The doctrinal mapping I made above — purge doctrine titrating on protoanemonin while bufadienolides silently poison the sodium pump — is **a mechanistic inference from compound class**, not a finding I retrieved for *H. foetidus*. I found **no** study in this literature search demonstrating Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase inhibition by an *H. foetidus* bufadienolide, and **no** modern case series of *H. foetidus* poisoning. The class-level pharmacology of bufadienolides as cardiotonic steroids is well established and the inference is sound, but it is an inference. **Grade: INFERRED / DATA-SPARSE.** The absence of a modern *H. foetidus* poisoning literature is a **reporting gap, not a safety signal** — the plant fell out of use before modern toxicovigilance existed, which is why the fatalities are in herbals rather than in *Clinical Toxicology*.

**4. Congener bufadienolide bioactivity — real, and instructive about what "active" means.**
HTB50-2, a bufadienolide from *H. thibetanus*, induces ferroptosis in triple-negative breast cancer cells via the FOSL2/FOXC1 axis in vitro and in BALB/c mice (Liu et al. 2025, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/0109298673306760240802062909), PMID 39129288). Sarametidis et al. found *Helleborus* root extract induced a **pro-oxidant phenotype that reduced cell viability, inhibited growth in both bacterial and human aortic endothelial cells, and exacerbated pro-inflammatory indices** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biology15110852), PMID 42274502) — note that "inhibited growth in human endothelial cells" is a toxicity finding wearing a bioactivity coat. **Grade: MODERATE for congeners, NOT TRANSFERRED to *H. foetidus*.**

**5. The one human clinical dataset — and why it does not rehabilitate the plant.**
Vagedes et al. (2026) report a retrospective cohort of subcutaneous *Helleborus niger* **12x** in dementia patients, finding significant MMSE improvement versus control ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1758447), PMID 41960405). **Grade: VERY LOW, and off-topic to this entry.** Wrong species, wrong route, wrong preparation (12x is a homeopathic decimal dilution — by definition essentially devoid of the compounds discussed above), retrospective, unblinded, non-randomised, with the authors themselves calling it "preliminary evidence" needing randomised confirmation. **This study is about a dilution, not about a hellebore.** It is cited here only to pre-empt its misuse as evidence that hellebore is safe or medicinal. It is evidence of neither.

**Honest summary.** The chemistry is well characterised and species-specific. The doctrine's chemical correlate is identifiable and the failure mode is diagnosable. **The therapeutic case is zero.** Nothing retrieved supports any human use of *H. foetidus* in any preparation for any indication.

### Contraindications

☠️ **ABSOLUTE — INTERNAL USE IS PROHIBITED IN ALL PERSONS, AT ALL DOSES, IN ALL PREPARATIONS. THERE ARE NO EXCEPTIONS AND NO QUALIFYING CONDITIONS.**

- **Children.** The historical vermifuge use is the specific source of the recorded fatalities. Children are the population this plant killed.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding.** Absolute. Purgative, cytotoxic, cardiotoxic.
- **Any cardiac condition, and none.** Bufadienolides are Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase inhibitors; a healthy heart is not a defence.
- **Concurrent digoxin/digitoxin.** Additive cardiac glycoside toxicity. Also: bufadienolides may cross-react with digoxin immunoassays, which can corrupt the very test used to assess the patient.
- **Diuretics, especially potassium-wasting.** Hypokalaemia potentiates cardiac glycoside toxicity — the standard, and lethal, interaction.
- **Renal impairment.** Impaired clearance of glycosides.
- **Handling.** Protoanemonin is a vesicant. Gloves. Eye protection if cutting. Do not burn (irritant smoke).
- **Livestock.** Toxic to grazing animals; a known hazard on chalk pasture.

**If ingestion is suspected — emergency medical care immediately.** Bufadienolide poisoning presents as a digitalis toxidrome: GI upset first, then bradyarrhythmias, conduction block, hyperkalaemia, ventricular dysrhythmia. **Tell the clinicians it is a cardiac glycoside plant** — that single sentence redirects management to the correct pathway (cardiac monitoring, potassium, and consideration of digoxin-specific Fab fragments, which have precedent in other cardiotoxic plant poisonings). Do not attempt home management. Do not induce vomiting.

**A note on the codex's own risk surface:** this entry's greatest hazard is not that someone will use *H. foetidus*. It is that someone will read entry-4's yew, or the *H. thibetanus* ferroptosis literature, or the Basel MCF-7 nanomolar figures, and conclude that a deadly plant with a promising in-vitro cytotoxin is a plant with a use. **Low-nanomolar cytotoxicity is what poisons look like in a dish.** The gap between "kills cells" and "is a medicine" is where paclitaxel took thirty years and several thousand chemists. Hellebore has not made that crossing and there is no indication it will.
