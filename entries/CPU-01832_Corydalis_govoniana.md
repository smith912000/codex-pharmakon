# Corydalis govoniana — Bhuta-keshi (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and Uttara ch.60)

## *Corydalis govoniana, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Corydalis  Govoniana.", line 31481) against the Sanskrit name Bhuta-keshi (OCR "Bhuta-keB'i", line 31421). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. ### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The chapters print the Sanskrit name (as "Bhutakes'i") once, in a list of drugs for mental disorders; the Latin is the appendix's alone.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` (row 442) gives the name as "Bhuta-keB'i", the locus "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31421; id line 31481)" and the Latin "Corydalis  Govoniana.", with the note that the pairing was proposed by an agent from the two OCR columns and checked by script. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* A search of all chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) for Bhutakesi and its spellings (Bhuta-keshi, Bhutakeshi, Bhutakes'i) found one hit.

*Uttara ch.60 (line 291; Vol. III line 15938).* The chapter's heading reads (OCR "AmsCnusha-Pratishcdha") that it deals with "the (symptoms and) medical treatment of the disease brought on through super-human influences". The passage is `SS_Uttara_ch60.txt` lines 289 to 306, verse 29: "Matured clarified butter, Lasuna, Hingu, Siddharthaka, Vacha, Golomi, Ajalomi, Bhutakes'i (...), Jata (Gandha-mamsi), Kukkuti (a kind of bulb), Sarpagandha, Kana (Kshira-kakoli), Vishanika (Madhurika), Rishya-prokta, Vayastha, Shringi, Mohana-valli (Vata-patrika), Arka-..., Tri-katu, Lata (Priyangu), Anjana (Rasanjana), Srotonjana, Naipali, Haritala and other articles which have the efficacy of exorcising evil spirits, as well as the dungs, hairs, skin, Vasa, urine, blood, bile, nails, etc. of lions, tigers, bears, cats, elephants, horses, cows, dogs, Salyakas, lizards, camels, mongooses, etc., should be used in the preparation of oil and clarified butter which should be used internally as well as in snuffing and as unguents. Pills made of the above drugs should be used in sprinkling (wash) and their powdered compound in dusting (the body of the patient). A paste prepared with the above drugs should be used as plasters. The due and proper application of the oil, Ghrita, etc. thus prepared would, in a very short time, surely cures all sorts of mental disorders." After "Bhutakes'i" the translator prints a bracketed gloss that the OCR renders "{]3Li^miLms{)"; it is unreadable here (two copies of the file, in the chapter folder and in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 15938, give the same garble), so the Codex cannot say what plant the translator names. The elisions marked (...) above mark that gloss and a garbled word. [USER: verify]

The list is long and mixed; it joins plants (several with the translator's identifying brackets) with minerals (Rasanjana, Srotonjana, Haritala) and with animal products, and the text's own aim is the exorcising of evil spirits. Nothing in the passage separates Bhuta-keshi from the other items.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Corydalis* is a genus of the poppy family (Papaveraceae, in some systems the Fumariaceae), many of whose members contain isoquinoline alkaloids. That is a statement about the genus and not about this species. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. If the Bhutakes'i of Uttara 60 is this plant (a reading, not a text statement), it is one ingredient among dozens in an oil and ghrita meant to exorcise evil spirits and cure "all sorts of mental disorders". The Codex gives no protocol, and the recipe includes animal materials and minerals that make it unsuitable for any use. Mental disorders need medical care.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives one preparation that contains the name: an oil and a ghrita cooked with the listed drugs and animal products, used internally, as snuff and as unguent, with pills for sprinkling, powder for dusting and a paste for plasters (Uttara ch.60, verse 29). No quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
