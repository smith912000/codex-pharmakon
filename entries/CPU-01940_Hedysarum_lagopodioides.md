### Origin & Tradition

**Named in the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) only through the translator's Appendix No. 3, "Names of drugs with Latin synonyms".** This is a single-text entry. The appendix is printed in two columns, Sanskrit names on the left and Bhishagratna's Latin or English glosses on the right, and the e-text `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` splits the two columns into separate runs of lines. In that file the Sanskrit name stands at line 31659 as "Gsbngeruki" (the OCR garbles diacritics) and the Latin gloss "Hedysarum  lagopodioides." at line 31708. The extraction file `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` lists the Latin line as unpaired ("OCR column split"). The pairing here is ours, made by counting entries in the two columns from points where the pairs are unambiguous: Eranda to Ricinus communis, Gairika to "Red ochre", Gambhari to Gmelina arborea, Gandha-trina to "See Katrina", then Gangeruki to "Hedysarum lagopodioides", Gauri to "See Haridra" and Gayatri to "See Khadira", a run that continues correctly through Godhuma (wheat), Gokshura (Tribulus terrestris) and Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia). The count held at every checked anchor, but it remains a reconstruction from OCR and must be checked against the page scan. [USER: verify] 

**The name in the main text.** The form Gangeruki was not found in the chapter files. A near form, Gangeruka, stands in the Sutrasthana's list of cooling fruits: "The fruit of such trees as the Ashvatha, the Plaksha, the Audumvura, etc., which pass by the general name of Kshira-Vrikshas as well as those which are known as the Jamva, Rajadana, Todana, Tinduka, Vakula, Phanvana, Ashmantaka, Ashvakarna, Phalgu, Panishaka, Gangeruka, Pushkara, Varti, Villa and Vimvi, etc. are cooling, and astringent in their effect. They subdue the Pittam and Kapham, tend to produce a condition of parchedness in the system, and have a sweet and astringent taste" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1119 to 1128). The list is of tree fruits, and Hedysarum lagopodioides is a small herb, so the two names should not be taken as the same plant without further evidence. [USER: verify]

**No other text.** A search of the other source texts on disk for *Hedysarum lagopodioides* found nothing. Dioscorides has chapters for other species of *Hedysarum* (`Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 7801 and 7950), which are separate substances and are not used here.
