# Karkata-Shringi — कर्कटशृङ्गी (Karkaṭaśṛṅgī); Crab's-Claw Gall; Kakadshingi; Kakra-singhi

## *Pistacia chinensis* subsp. *integerrima* (≡ *Pistacia integerrima* J.L.Stewart ex Brandis) | Family: Anacardiaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā) — horn-shaped insect gall; Kāsahara (antitussive) / Grāhi group; paediatric respiratory dravya

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (Ayurveda).** Karkata-Shringi is not a root or leaf but a **gall** — a hollow, horn- or crab-claw-shaped growth induced on the branches of the *Pistacia* tree by aphid-family insects (*Baizongia*/*Slavum*-type gall-formers). Its Sanskrit name reads "crab's-horn" (*karkaṭa* = crab, *śṛṅgī* = horned), a direct botanical description of the curved, branching, hollow gall. The classical tradition prizes precisely this galled material, not the ordinary wood.

In Suśruta and the later corpus, Karkata-Shringi is a premier **Kāsahara** (antitussive / cough-relieving) and **śvāsahara** (anti-dyspnoeic) dravya, with additional *grāhi* (absorbent/antidiarrhoeal), *jvaraghna* (antipyretic) and *dīpana-pācana* (digestive) actions. Its signature clinical home is **respiratory disease in children** — cough, cold, bronchitis, hiccup, and the *kāsa-śvāsa* complex — where its astringent-bitter, warming, drying, Kapha-clearing profile loosens and settles productive cough while its grāhi quality checks the accompanying loose stool of childhood illness. It is a stock ingredient of paediatric and respiratory formulations across classical and later Ayurvedic pharmacy.

**Nighaṇṭu, Unani and folk continuity.** The Bhāvaprakāśa and other nighaṇṭus retain Karkata-Shringi among the cough-and-breath (*kāsa-śvāsa*) herbs. It is equally established in **Unani medicine** (as "Kakra Singhi," a warming, drying, respiratory and digestive remedy) and in Himalayan/Pakistani folk practice, where the galls are a household cough and gastrointestinal medicine. This tri-traditional continuity — Ayurvedic, Unani, folk — gives the substance an unusually broad and consistent ethnomedical record.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Horn Grown from Wounding:** Karkata-Shringi carries a doctrine unlike any root-tonic in this batch: it is medicine *born from injury*. The gall exists only because an insect wounded the tree, and the tree's defensive response — walling off, concentrating tannins and terpenes — is exactly what makes the material potent. Symbolically this is the pharmakon of the *reactive* rather than the *reservoir* register: where the Aṣṭavarga rhizomes are stored abundance, the śṛṅgī is concentrated defence, the tree's own astringent immune response crystallised into a horn. Its crab-claw / horn form reads in the doctrine of signatures as a "grasping, gripping" agent — *grāhi*, that which holds and checks flux (of cough, of stool, of loose secretion). In the Kenetica frame Karkata-Shringi is an **acute-response** dravya: warming, drying, gripping — the tool for settling a system in productive, leaking overflow (cough, discharge, loose bowel), especially the vulnerable respiratory terrain of children.

### Active Components

*P. integerrima* galls are comparatively well-studied phytochemically. Documented and genus-consistent constituents include: **flavonoids** — naringenin, sakuranetin, spinacetin, patuletin, and related flavanones (directly isolated from the galls, see Scientific Correlation) — **tannins** (the astringent, grāhi base), **triterpenoids**, **essential oils / terpenes** (α-pinene, terpineol and related, giving the aromatic, expectorant character), **phenolic acids**, and sterols. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Karkata-Shringi is a **respiratory-recovery / acute-settling** dravya:

- **Cough and breath:** the core use — loosening and settling productive cough, bronchitis, and *kāsa-śvāsa* patterns, particularly Kapha-Vāta type.
- **Paediatric respiratory maintenance:** its classical niche is children's cough/cold with accompanying loose stool; the *grāhi* action addresses both fronts at once.
- **Gut-gripping (grāhi):** astringent checking of loose secretions and mild diarrhoea; supportive digestive (*dīpana-pācana*) action.

It is warming and drying — an acute tool, not a restorative reservoir — and pairs naturally with honey, pippalī, or other kāsahara agents.

### Scientific Correlation

**Evidence grade: preclinical — real isolated-compound and mechanistic data exist for *P. integerrima* galls, but no retrieved controlled human antitussive trials.** The classical antitussive/paediatric reputation is well-established ethnomedically; the modern retrieved evidence is phytochemical and in-vitro/in-silico, not clinical outcome data.

According to PubMed:

- Rauf, Khan, Munir, Shabbir, Rashid et al. (2026), *Journal of Computer-Aided Molecular Design* — isolated **six flavonoids directly from *P. integerrima* galls** (a tetrahydroxy-flavanone, naringenin, a trihydroxy-methoxy-flavanone, sakuranetin, spinacetin, patuletin) and demonstrated potent in-vitro **xanthine oxidase inhibition** (patuletin 93%, outperforming allopurinol), with docking/MD and ADMET support — evidence relevant to anti-inflammatory/anti-hyperuricaemic potential. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10822-026-00767-9)
- Alsharairi (2025), *Current Issues in Molecular Biology* — review of *Pistacia* phenolic compounds documenting anticancer/cytotoxic activity across the genus (including *P. integerrima* subsp. contexts), situating the gall flavonoids within a broader validated phenolic pharmacology. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/cimb47060393)

Honest summary: the galls have a genuine, isolable flavonoid/tannin pharmacology with measured bioactivity, giving plausible mechanistic support to the astringent/anti-inflammatory reading; the specific classical antitussive and paediatric indications rest on tradition and ethnomedical continuity, not on retrieved controlled trials.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa:** Kaṣāya (astringent), Tikta (bitter); Madhura anurasa (faint sweet after-note)
- **Guṇa:** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry)
- **Vīrya:** Uṣṇa (warming)
- **Vipāka:** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva:** Kāsahara — a specific cough-settling action, and grāhi grip beyond taste/quality prediction
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha–Vāta śāmaka; mildly Pitta-vardhaka in excess (uṣṇa)
- **Karma:** Kāsahara, Śvāsahara, Grāhi, Jvaraghna, Dīpana, Pācana, Chardighna (anti-emetic), Stambhana (astringent-checking)
- **Gaṇa / group:** Kāsahara / respiratory and grāhi cluster (Suśruta); paediatric respiratory dravya; *not Aṣṭavarga*
- **Dhātu:** rasa (respiratory/digestive mucosa)
- **Srotas:** prāṇavaha (respiratory), annavaha (digestive)

### Contraindications

- **Pitta excess / heat states:** its uṣṇa (warming), rūkṣa (drying) nature can aggravate high Pitta, dryness, and inflamed/bleeding conditions; use cautiously in hot, dry constitutions.
- **Dry cough:** best suited to productive/Kapha cough; in purely dry (Vāta) cough pair with unctuous, demulcent anupāna to offset dryness.
- **Paediatric caution:** although classically a children's remedy, dosing must be weight-appropriate and practitioner-guided; not a substitute for medical assessment of a child's respiratory illness.
