# Sparganium ramosum — Sparganion (Dioscorides 4-21)

## *Sparganium ramosum, "Branched Bur Reed", the last of the names on the translators' SUGGESTED line for 4-21 ("Sparganium simplex — Reed Grass, Bur Reed Sparganium erectum, Sparganium ramosum — Branched Bur Reed"), and the name printed in the plate caption at line 8192* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED line (line 8194) prints three names of the bur-reed genus, *Sparganium simplex*, *S. erectum* and *S. ramosum*, with common names "Reed Grass, Bur Reed" and "Branched Bur Reed". The row takes the last, which is also the species of the plate caption. It was added from that line (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is *4-21. SPARGANION*, heading at line 8190, a plate caption at line 8192 ("Sparganium ramosum (female inflorescence) after FAGUET — 1894"), the SUGGESTED line at 8194 and the text at line 8195. It stands in Book Four ("Other Herbs & Roots"), after *4-20. XIPHION* (line 8183) and before *4-22. XURIS* (line 8197), both different plants. The Codex's own name extraction lists it (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, row 601), though that row's names column holds the plate caption and not the SUGGESTED line. The translators' general index prints "Sparganium ramosum 564" (line 17643), and their separate list of illustrations prints "Sparganium ramosum 562" (line 11869); the two page numbers differ and the Codex does not resolve them. [USER: verify]

The whole chapter reads: "Sparganium has leaves similar to a little sword but narrower and bending downward more, and on the top of the stalk are little balls in which is the seed. The root and seed are given with wine to those bitten by venomous creatures. It is also called xiphidion, or bolon."

The chapter gives a description (leaves similar to a little sword "but narrower and bending downward more"; little balls on the top of the stalk, in which is the seed), one use (the root and seed "given with wine to those bitten by venomous creatures") and two other names, *xiphidion* and *bolon*. It gives no habitat, no quality and no quantity. The text compares the leaves to a "little sword", and the preceding chapter, 4-20 *XIPHION* (line 8183), is a sword-leaved plant (SUGGESTED *Gladiolus illyricus*, "Sword Lily"; the text there compares its leaf to iris). That is a labelled observation about the neighbouring chapter, not a claim by the 4-21 text, and the Codex does not read the *xiphidion* alias as an identification with it.

**Overlap.** A chapter-level search of the entries folder for 4-21 found three files, none of which treats this chapter as a plant. I_1092 (*Gladiolus illyricus*, chapter 4-20) names 4-21 as the chapter that follows. The other two (III_274, II_95) match "4-21" only inside line ranges such as "2194-2198" and "2114-2118". No entry on 4-21 as its own plant was found.

### Active Components

The chapter prints none. It is a general fact that *Sparganium* (bur-reed) is a genus of marsh and shallow-water plants with round spiky fruiting heads, which the "little balls" of the text may describe. The composition of the plant was not read, and no claim is made that the plant has any action against venom. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides reports a root and seed given with wine to those bitten by venomous creatures. This is the text's report, the identification is the translators' and is open among three species of the genus, and the Codex gives no protocol. There is no evidence in the sources read that the plant counters venom, and a venomous bite is an emergency that needs medical care at once.

### Preparation & Dosing

4-21 gives one preparation: the root and seed "given with wine". It gives no quantity, frequency or duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter records no amuletic use; the names *xiphidion* and *bolon* are names only. The part of the discipline of attention that is present is the description by comparison (leaves like a little sword, but narrower and more bent), the note of where the seed is carried (little balls at the top of the stalk), and the naming of the parts used, root and seed. The plant is given no status beyond that of an antidote of the kind taken in wine. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant, and the sourced text is only a few lines. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

A bite from a venomous creature is an emergency, and nothing in this entry is a substitute for treatment. The identity of the plant rests on a translators' suggestion that offers three species, so no wild plant should be gathered or taken with wine on the strength of this entry. None of the traditional uses above is advice.
