# Udumbara (Cluster Fig) — Udumbara, Yajñāṅga, Sadāphala, Gūlar / Gular, Aṭṭi

## *Ficus racemosa* L. | Family: Moraceae

**Tradition Tags:** Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS) — member of the **Panchavalkala** (five astringent barks) and the **Kṣīrī-vṛkṣa** (milk-trees); Nyagrodhādi gaṇa; Caraka Saṃhitā; classical nighaṇṭus
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Kaṣāya (astringent) rasa · Śīta (cooling) vīrya · Kaṭu (pungent) vipāka · pacifies Pitta and Kapha (Kapha-Pitta-hara)
**Training Phase:** Containment · Cooling · Wound-consolidation · Metabolic stabilisation

### Origin & Tradition

The cluster fig is the sacrificial tree — *Udumbara*, whose wood, twigs, and cup-shaped ritual vessels are woven through the Vedic yajña (fire-sacrifice) itself. It is *Sadāphala*, "ever-fruiting," for the dense clusters of figs (the *racemosa* of its binomial) that erupt directly from its trunk and older branches — a cauliflorous habit that ancient observers read as a sign of ceaseless fertility. It is a **Kṣīrī-vṛkṣa** (milk-tree) and one of the classic **Panchavalkala** barks.

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (surgical-Ayurvedic tradition):** Udumbara bark is one of the five **Panchavalkala** astringent barks used together as a wound-wash, sitz-decoction, and healing agent for ulcers, mucosal lesions, and inflamed surfaces (*vraṇaśodhana/ropaṇa*). Within Suśruta's cooling-astringent groupings it serves the same containing, staunching purpose as its sibling figs — arresting discharge, cooling Pitta-heat, and consolidating tissue. Its latex and fruit are further used in the surgical-convalescent context for bleeding disorders and diarrhoeal states.

**Caraka Saṃhitā and internal medicine:** Udumbara is deployed for *raktapitta* (bleeding disorders), *atisāra/pravāhikā* (diarrhoea, dysentery), *prameha/mūtra* disorders (the "sweet-urine"/urinary group), *pradara* (excessive menstrual flow), and burning Pitta conditions — the astringent, cooling, absorbent (*saṃgrāhī*) triad. Its fruit is regarded as cooling and mildly nutritive; its bark as the principal medicinal part.

**Folk and pan-Indian use:** Bark decoction is a widespread folk antidiabetic and antidiarrhoeal; fruit and latex for bleeding, skin, and gum complaints. According to PubMed, a comprehensive review records that "it reduces blood glucose concentration due to the presence of β-sitosterol" and that isolated constituents display "antidiabetic, antioxidant, antidiarrhoeal, anti-inflammatory, antipyretic, antifungal, antibacterial, hypolipidemic, antifilarial, and hepatoprotection" activities (Yadav et al., 2015, *Pharmacogn Rev*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0973-7847.156356).

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Sacrificial Ever-Fruiting Tree:**
Udumbara is the tree of *yajña* and of hidden fecundity. Vedic ritual made its wood into the sacrificial post (*yūpa*), ladles, and the amulet-cup of kingship and prosperity; the tree is linked to abundance, fertility, and the god Viṣṇu. Its cauliflory — fruit bursting straight from the trunk, seemingly without flowers (the fig's true flowers are hidden *inside* the syconium) — gave it an ancient reputation as a plant that fruits "invisibly," an emblem of generative power working out of sight, and even a folk metaphor for the rare and the concealed. In Kenetica's reading of the astringent tetrad, Udumbara supplies the *cooling-nutritive* accent within the containment principle: it staunches and consolidates like its siblings, but its ever-fruiting, latex-rich, mildly nourishing character tempers pure astringency with a moist, sustaining note — the containing tree that still bears fruit. It sits between the pure staunching of Nyagrodha and the sweet refilling of the paired rasāyana Jīvantī: containment that does not fully dry.

### Active Components

- **Phytosterols:** β-sitosterol (a key hypoglycaemic principle), stigmasterol
- **Triterpenoids:** lupeol, β-amyrin, α-amyrin, friedelin-type triterpenes (reported)
- **Flavonoids / phenolics:** including racemosic-type acids and flavonol glycosides
- **Tannins:** condensed tannins underlying the bark's astringency
- **Coumarins, sterol glycosides, and other secondary metabolites** reported across bark, fruit, latex, and leaf

Per the review literature, many constituents "isolated from various parts of this plant possess useful pharmacological activities," with β-sitosterol specifically implicated in its blood-glucose-lowering action (Yadav et al., 2015, *Pharmacogn Rev*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0973-7847.156356). A GC/MS analysis of a fig-containing herbal tea likewise reported "β-sitosterol in the Attikka Ficus racemosa" (Mahmoud et al., 2016, *J Ethnopharmacol*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2016.10.073).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Udumbara is filed as a **cooling containment and metabolic-stabilisation agent** within the Ficus tetrad — the fig-drug for *staunching with a cooling, mildly nourishing accent*. Its astringent-cooling-absorbent profile serves leaking, discharging, over-heated, and dysregulated-metabolic states.

Primary Kenetica use cases:
- **Wound-consolidation:** as a **Panchavalkala** component for cleansing and healing ulcers and mucosal surfaces.
- **Arrest of discharge / bleeding:** diarrhoea, dysentery, bleeding disorders, excessive menstrual flow (*saṃgrāhī* / *stambhana*).
- **Metabolic stabilisation:** the well-documented traditional and preclinical antidiabetic / hypoglycaemic action (β-sitosterol-linked) for prameha-type disorders.
- **Pitta-cooling:** burning states, inflammatory heat, antipyretic use.
- **Adjunct anti-inflammatory / antioxidant support** where the tetrad is prescribed as a group.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, *Ficus racemosa* has the most consolidated modern review evidence of the astringent figs. A dedicated review of its phytochemistry, pharmacology, toxicology, and clinical data reports blood-glucose reduction attributed to β-sitosterol and a broad activity profile spanning "antidiabetic, antioxidant, antidiarrhoeal, anti-inflammatory, antipyretic, antifungal, antibacterial, hypolipidemic, antifilarial, and hepatoprotection," and notes that "some clinical trial data have also been provided" (Yadav, Nandy, Maity, Sarkar & Saha, 2015, *Pharmacogn Rev* 9(17):73–80) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0973-7847.156356). A systematic review of antioxidative hypoglycaemic herbal medicines lists *F. racemosa* L. among plants with "potential neuroprotective effects in animal models of diabetes" acting via reduced CRP and oxidative stress (Mirahmad et al., 2022, *Phytomedicine* 109:154615) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phymed.2022.154615). A clinical multi-herb tea containing *F. racemosa* reported hypoglycaemic, antihyperlipidaemic, and anti-inflammatory effects (Mahmoud et al., 2016, *J Ethnopharmacol*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2016.10.073).

**Evidence grade — MODERATE / PRECLINICAL, with early clinical signal.** Antidiabetic, antioxidant, antidiarrhoeal, and anti-inflammatory actions are supported by animal, *in vitro*, and review-level data, and the review notes some clinical trial data; however, robust standalone human RCTs are not established in the retrieved sources. Present metabolic and anti-inflammatory benefits as **traditionally-grounded and preclinically-supported.** Do not invent trial sizes or effect magnitudes beyond what the citations state.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṣāya (astringent), with Madhura (sweet) note in the fruit
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Guru (heavy), Rūkṣa (dry) — astringent-drying, less rough than pure Nyagrodha owing to fruit/latex
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha-Pitta-hara; may aggravate Vāta if used excessively dry
- **Karma (actions):** Saṃgrāhī (astringent/absorbent), Stambhana (arresting), Vraṇaropaṇa (wound-healing), Grāhī (bowel-binding), Raktastambhaka (haemostatic), Prameha-hara (antidiabetic tendency), Dāhapraśamana (relieves burning), Varṇya (complexion-improving)
- **Gaṇa membership:** **Panchavalkala** (five astringent barks) and **Kṣīrī-vṛkṣa** (milk-trees); allied to the Nyagrodhādi gaṇa
- **Prabhāva:** Its β-sitosterol-linked antidiabetic action and its ritual/"ever-fruiting" identity

### Contraindications

- **Vāta aggravation / constipation:** the dry, astringent, binding quality can worsen Vāta-dryness and constipation — pair with unctuous adjuvants.
- **Not a substitute for glycaemic medical management:** antidiabetic use is traditional/preclinical — do not present as proven monotherapy or delay standard diabetes care.
- **Kapha-heavy congestion:** its heaviness may not suit sluggish, high-āma states in excess.
