# Ba.nu — "Carbonate of Chalk" (Tibetan ba.nu)
## *A stone "shaped like a cow's udder", glossed "Carbonate of Chalk" by Clark (1995); no action is printed in the text read* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan Tradition (FMT).** The only locus is the Explanatory Tantra's materia medica, in Clark's 1995 translation (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 845; name-index row `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` line 23). The whole entry reads: "ba.nu: Carbonate of Chalk: [is a stone shaped like a cow's udder and is of uncertain color. When broken it smells like burnt horn. It is of smooth and coarse types, corresponding to male and female types, with a (transitional) neuter type.]"

Everything after the colon is in Clark's square brackets, which in this translation mark the commentary he supplies from later Tibetan authorities, not the root text. Unlike its neighbours, the entry prints no taste, no potency and no action: no disease is named as cured. The Tibetan word also appears in the index of Tibetan terms (line 1719, "ba. ba.nu. ba.spru.wa. ba.bla. ba.ru.ra."), which adds nothing about use. The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text was searched for "Banu", "Ba nu" and "chalk" and returned no entry for this drug. [USER: verify]

**Position in the text.** Clark's "Specific Section" lists eight classes of natural substances, and "(2) The Powers of Stone Medicines" begins at line 816. *Ba.nu* stands among the stone medicines at line 845, between *rdo.mkhris* (yellow ochre), *bshah.dkar* (stannic ore), realgar, orpiment and coal on one side and *rdo.rgyus* (actinolite, stibine) on the other (lines 840-846). The surrounding entries do print actions, for example "rdo.sol: Coal ... dissolves stones" (line 844) and calcite, "*chong.zhi*: Calcite ... halts diarrhoea, cures phlegm fever [and is a principle ingredient of essence-extraction compounds]" (line 853). Those are separate drugs. The calcite action is not transferred to *ba.nu*; the text does not equate them.

**Identity.** "Carbonate of chalk" is Clark's English gloss; it is not a mineral name in the usual sense (chalk is a calcium carbonate rock), and the entry's own description (an udder-shaped stone, uncertain colour, smelling of burnt horn when broken, with "male", "female" and "neuter" types) does not alone fix it. The Codex's name-index row classes the item "plant" (`names.csv` line 23) although it sits among the stone medicines in the text. The identification as a calcium-carbonate mineral rests on Clark's gloss only. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Stated generally. Chalk and limestone are calcium carbonate (CaCO3), often with small amounts of magnesium carbonate, silica, clay and iron oxides. Calcium carbonate reacts with acids, releasing carbon dioxide. The smell of "burnt horn" that the text reports when the stone is broken suggests an organic or sulphurous inclusion, but the text does not say which, and none is asserted. No figures were read in the sources retrieved.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved name the stone and describe it; they give no use. The Codex therefore has no application to report. Calcium carbonate as a modern material is an ordinary antacid and calcium source, but that is a statement about the chemical compound, not about what the Tibetan texts did with *ba.nu*, and it is not offered as a use of the stone.

### Contraindications

- **Unverified identity:** an unidentified stone should not be ingested.
- **Contaminants:** natural chalk and limestone can contain silica dust, heavy metals and organic inclusions; dust is a respiratory irritant.
- **Calcium load:** a calcium carbonate material taken internally in quantity can cause constipation and, over time, raise blood calcium, particularly in kidney disease; this is a general fact about the compound, not about *ba.nu*.
- **Conservation and law:** none known for chalk; the status of the Tibetan stone was not retrieved. [USER: verify]
