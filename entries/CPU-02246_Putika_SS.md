# Putika — Putika, Putikaranja (Sushruta Samhita)

## *A plant named Putika in the Sushruta Samhita, used as leaves, sprouts, twigs, seed oil and juice and burnt for alkali; no Latin name and no description is printed in any passage read, so the identification is unresolved, at low confidence. The text also names "Putikaranjas" among the leaf vegetables; whether that is the same plant is not stated.* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The text is Bhishagratna's English translation (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`, chapter files, and the Vol. I-III djvu files). The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the group list at Vol. I line 15387; this entry replaces it. A first-pass check proposed no identification, and none was found in the sources read: Bhishagratna's text prints no Latin name against Putika in the passages below, and the names file (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, line 47) records "group list (ch.38); no identification given here".

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The group lists (Sutra ch. 38, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`).* Putika is a member of two groups. The Varuna'di group (lines 44-52; Vol. I line 15387): "The drugs known as Varuna, Artagala, Shigru, Madhu-Shigru, Tarkari, Mesha-Shringi, Putika, Naktamala, Morata, Agnimantha, the two kinds of Sairiyaka, Vimvi, Vasuka, Vasira, Chitraka, Shatavari, Mlva, Ajashringi, Darbha, and the two kinds of Vrihati form the group known as the Varunadi." (The OCR prints "Xaktamala" for Naktamala.) The metrical text: "The group is possessed of the efficacy of reducing the deranged Kapham and fat and proves efficacious in cases of cephalaegia, Gulma and internal abscesses." It is also listed in the Salasaradi group (line 80): "... Arjuna, Tala, Shaka, Naktamala, Putika, Ashvakarna, Aguru and Kaliyaka." The group's action is stated for the group, not for any single member.

*Purgative drug (Sutra ch. 39, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` lines 27 and 43).* Putika is in a list of plants "possessed of purgative properties" (Chitraka, Kinihi, ... Eranda, Putika, Mahavriksha ...). The chapter says which part is used: "the leaves of Putika and Aragvadha" are used for the purpose set out for the group.

*Alkali (Sutra ch. 11, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch11.txt` lines 127 and 185).* In the making of caustic alkali (kshara), the wood, leaves, roots and fruits of a list of plants including Putika are "burnt down to ashes" (line 127). Later in the chapter, a strong alkali is made with the powders of "Danti, Dravanti, Chitraka, Langulaki, Putika-Pravala, Talpatri, Vidha, Suvarchika, Kanaka-Kshiri, Hingu, Vacha, and Visha" (line 185). The OCR hyphen makes it unclear whether "Putika-Pravala" is one name (a sprout of Putika) or two; this is not decided here. [USER: verify]

*In plasters, ulcers and recipes.* The text names Putika in a plaster for the type the OCR prints "Utputuka" (Sutra ch. 16, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch16.txt`; "Shampaka, Shigru, Putika, the fat and marrow of a Godha and the milk and bile of a she-deer, she-buffalo or sow", line 398); in a medicated lint for cleansing an ulcer (Sutra ch. 36, line 98, "Ajagandha, Ajashringi, Gavakshi, Langalahvaya, Putika, Chitraka, Patha ..."); and in Chikitsa ch. 1 (line 893), where the leaves of "the Eranda, Bhurja, Putika, or Haridra plants" are tied over an ulcer of deranged Vayu. In Chikitsa ch. 9 (line 436) a plaster of "the tender twigs of the Putika, Arka, Snuhi, Aragvadha and of the Jati flower with cow's urine" is said to be curative in "Svitra, ringworm, ulcer, bad types of haemorrhoids and sinus". In Chikitsa ch. 6 (lines 317-318) "the alkaline water of Putika" is taken with a paste of Chitraka, Putika and Nagara. In Chikitsa ch. 31 (line 52) the expressed oil of the seeds of "Karanja, Putika, Kritamala, Matulunga, Ingudi and Kirata-tikta" is used in malignant ulcers. In Uttara ch. 42 (line 388) "the duly fried tender sprouts of Putika and Nripa-Vriksha" are given for Gulma, in Uttara ch. 51 (line 91) Putika is in the Himsradi Ghrita for cough and asthma, and in Uttara ch. 54 (line 190) "the expressed juice of Putika" is taken with honey in a recipe in that chapter. So the plant is used as leaf, tender sprout, twig, seed oil, juice and ash.

*Putikaranja (Sutra ch. 46, `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 1760).* In the account of leaf vegetables: "The Putikaranjas are heat-making in their potency and alleviate oedema and anasarca. They are pungent in digestion, easily digestible and subdue the Vayu and the Kapham." The plural and the compound name are the text's. It is not stated that Putikaranja is Putika; the two may be one plant, two, or a plant and its relatives. Nothing in the sources read settles this. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent, and the identity is unresolved, so no composition is stated. In general terms, any plant whose ash is made into a caustic alkali yields potassium-rich plant ash, which is strongly alkaline in water; that is a property of the ash and not of the plant's own chemistry. No analysis was read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a group member (Varuna'di): lessening Kapha and fat, headache, Gulma and internal abscess. As a named drug: purgation (ch. 39), cleansing and dressing ulcers, skin disease (ch. 9), and a source of ash for alkali (ch. 11).
- **What the text does not say.** What the plant is, what it looks like, or where it grows.

The Codex does not translate these uses into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend the plant, which cannot be identified.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the text gives are those listed above: burnt to ash (ch. 11), pasted with cow's urine as a plaster (Chikitsa ch. 9), leaves applied to an ulcer (Chikitsa ch. 1), fried sprouts (Uttara ch. 42), seed oil (Chikitsa ch. 31), expressed juice with honey (Uttara ch. 54). Several recipes give weights of mixed drugs; none is a dose for Putika alone and none is reproduced here. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant cannot be identified from the text; do not guess a species or collect a plant under this name. [USER: verify]
- **Purgative and alkali.** The text uses it as a purgative and as a source of caustic alkali; both are strong actions. Do not take it, and do not make or use alkali.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Given the purgative use, avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Conditions in the text** (Gulma, skin disease, ulcers, cough, asthma) are medical conditions; use proper care.
