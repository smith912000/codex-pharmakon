# Padmaka — Padmakāṣṭha, Pitarohiṇī, "Wild Himalayan Cherry," Padam
## *Prunus cerasoides* D.Don | Family: Rosaceae
**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā); classical Ayurvedic nighaṇṭus; Himalayan folk medicine
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa — kaṣāya (astringent), tikta (bitter), madhura (sweet); Vīrya — śīta (cooling); Vipāka — kaṭu (pungent); primarily Pittahara and Kaphahara.
**Training Phase:** Cooling & Complexion (Varṇya) · Blood-cooling (Raktaprasādana) · Convalescent support

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS):** Padmaka — the fragrant, reddish **heartwood** (*padmakāṣṭha*) of the wild Himalayan cherry — is a well-attested classical dravya. Suśruta lists it among the cooling, Pitta-pacifying, blood-purifying woods and includes it in gaṇa groupings concerned with complexion, burning, thirst and skin. It is a canonical member of formulations such as the **great restorative ghee/lehas** and cooling decoctions; it appears in the *Aṣṭavarga*-adjacent "auspicious" nutritive groups only by association, but its firmest home is among the **varṇya (complexion-promoting)** and **dāhapraśamana (burning-relieving)** dravyas. Classically indicated in *rakta-pitta*, *dāha* (burning), *tṛṣṇā* (thirst), *jvara* (fever), *chardi* (vomiting), *kuṣṭha* (skin disorders) and to promote a clear complexion (*varṇa*).

**Himalayan folk medicine:** Across the western and central Himalaya the bark and heartwood are used for backache, joint disorders, and as a general cooling, astringent remedy. The tree — *padam* — is culturally prominent as an early-spring flowerer, and its wood is valued for both medicine and ritual.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Lotus in the Grain:** The name *Padmaka* is built from *padma*, the lotus — the supreme Indic emblem of purity rising unstained from muddy water, of the heart-centre, and of ordered, unfolding creation. The heartwood earns the name partly through its rosy, lotus-like hue and partly through a deeper doctrinal resonance: like the lotus, it is prized for what it does to *appearance and clarity* — it is a **varṇya** dravya, a promoter of complexion, and the classical mind read outer radiance (*varṇa*) as the visible sign of cooled, purified blood (*rakta-prasāda*). To take Padmaka is, symbolically, to invite the lotus into the bloodstream: to let heat settle, sediment fall, and the skin brighten as still water clears. That it is a *cherry* — a tree of brief, intense spring flowering in the high cold — folds in a further meaning of cool beauty and transience. In Kenetica's frame Padmaka is the *clarifier of the surface through the depths*: it works on the visible by cooling the invisible, treating the complexion as a readout of the blood.

### Active Components

*Prunus cerasoides* heartwood and bark contain flavonoids and flavan-3-ols, phenolic acids, and the phytosterol **β-sitosterol**, together with triterpenoids such as **ursolic acid** and glycosides including **docosyl ferulate** — the marker profile characterised in comparative *Prunus* work (see Scientific Correlation). ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica, Padmaka is a **blood-cooling, complexion-restoring astringent** — the wood to reach for in heat conditions that surface on the skin and in the blood: burning sensations, inflammatory or "hot" skin patterns, excessive thirst, and the flushed depletion of post-fever states. Its astringent-cooling signature also lends it to mild haemostatic and anti-emetic use in the classical *rakta-pitta / chardi* sense, and Himalayan practice extends it to musculoskeletal aches. It is a *quieting, settling* wood rather than a stimulant — its job is to reduce heat and turbulence so that tissue and complexion can normalise.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, direct pharmacological work on *Prunus cerasoides* exists but is limited and mostly comparative. A comparative study of Indian *Prunus* species against testosterone-induced benign prostatic hyperplasia in Wistar rats included *P. cerasoides* bark and confirmed its content of the markers β-sitosterol, docosyl ferulate and ursolic acid, reporting significant anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activity across the genus, with *P. cerasoides* showing a meaningful (though not the strongest) anti-BPH effect in the tested series (Jena AK et al., 2016, *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2016.05.052)). A 2023 review of woody-plant barks in bone-mending situates *Prunus* and related Rosaceous barks within the broader phytopharmacology of polyphenol- and mineral-rich bark used traditionally for musculoskeletal recovery, while cautioning that most such materials lack clinical validation (Joshi DD et al., 2023, *Saudi Pharmaceutical Journal* — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsps.2023.101714)).

**Evidence grade: LOW.** One animal comparative study and one review touch *P. cerasoides* directly; the antioxidant/anti-inflammatory findings are consistent with the classical cooling, rakta-pitta indications but do not amount to clinical proof. No human trials retrieved this run. Claims of complexion or blood-cooling efficacy remain traditional.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** kaṣāya (astringent), tikta (bitter), with a madhura undertone
- **Guṇa (qualities):** laghu (light), rūkṣa (dry)
- **Vīrya (potency):** śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive taste):** kaṭu (pungent) [some nighaṇṭus give madhura — `[USER: verify preferred source`]`]
- **Doṣa action:** Pittahara (primary) and Kaphahara; may aggravate Vāta if used dry and in excess
- **Karma (actions):** varṇya (complexion-promoting), raktaprasādana (blood-cooling/purifying), dāhapraśamana (relieves burning), tṛṣṇānigrahaṇa (relieves thirst), chardighna (anti-emetic), kuṣṭhaghna (skin-disorder), stambhana (astringent/haemostatic)
- **Srotas / systems:** raktavaha (blood), rasavaha (plasma), skin

### Contraindications

Cooling and drying — use cautiously in Vāta-cold and dry constitutions and in weak digestion (mandāgni) when used alone. As a Rosaceous bark it may contain cyanogenic glycosides; only properly processed heartwood/bark should be used and never in unregulated quantity.
