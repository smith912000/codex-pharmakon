# Sumukha — Sumukha (Sushruta Samhita)

## *Unresolved: a drug named Sumukha in the Sushruta Samhita, listed in the Surasadi group, in the pungent group and among the spices and herbs; the translation prints no Latin name, and the text says only that it has the properties of Surasa with one exception; confidence in any identification is low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The name is printed *Sumukha* in Bhishagratna's translation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder with the locus "SS Sutra 38, Surasa'di group (Vol. I line 15462)"; this entry replaces it. A search of all the chapter files (sutra, chikitsa, kalpa, uttara, nidana, sharira) and of the three Bhishagratna volumes found the name in three chapters of the *Sutrasthana* and nowhere else. Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 (Latin synonyms, Vol. III) was searched and does not list the name.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The Surasadi group (Sutra ch. 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 119 to 130; Vol. I line 15462).* "The Surasa'di Group: — The drugs known as Surasa, white Surasa, Fainjjhaka, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika form the group known as the Surasadi Gana." The metrical text follows: "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough." These are properties of the group and are not assigned to Sumukha singly.

*The pungent group (Sutra ch. 42, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` lines 324 to 331).* The chapter says that the Pippalyadi and Surasadi groups "and Shigru, Madhu-sigru, Mulaka, Lashuna, Sumukha, Shitashiva (camphor, Kushtha, Devadaru, Harenuka, Valguja-phalam, Chanda, Guggula, Mustha, Langalaki, Shukanasa and Pilu etc. and the components of the group known as Salasaradi gana collectively form the pungent group." Here Sumukha is named on its own, besides the Surasadi group that already contains it. The parenthesis after Shitashiva is the translator's.

*The spices and herbs (Sutra ch. 46, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1418 to 1423 and 1492 to 1496).* The list of "spices and herbs" reads "Pippali, Maricha, Shringavera, Ardraka, Hingu, Jiraka, Kustumvuru, Jamviraka, Sumukha, Surasa, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Kasamaraddka, Kalamala, Kutheraka, Kshavaka, Ksharapushpa, Shigru, Madhu-Shigru ...". The chapter then describes several of them. Of Surasa it says that it "generates the Pittam, relieves the colic and pain at the sides, while the Sumukha is said to be possessed of the same properties with the exception that it neutralises the effects of poisons brought about through the chemical combination (of several incompatible substances in the organism)." This is the only sentence in the sources read that speaks of Sumukha alone. The translator's "chemical combination" renders a Sushruta notion of poison from incompatible substances, and the passage gives no further explanation.

**What the sources do not give.** No Latin name, no description of the plant, no part used, no habitat. The neighbours in the lists (Surasa, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Kalamala) are aromatic herbs of the same group, and the comparison with Surasa suggests that Sumukha is a herb of that kind. The idea is only a lead from the list, and no text opened for this entry confirms it. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Sumukha alone. It is placed in the pungent group (Sutra ch. 42, lines 324 to 331), both singly and through the Surasadi group. Ch. 46 gives it the properties of Surasa, with the one difference stated above. The Surasadi group is described as subduing Kapham (ch. 38).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Sumukha is named in the Sushruta Samhita as a member of a therapeutic group (*gana*) (the Surasadi group, Sutra ch. 38), in the pungent group (ch. 42) and in the list of spices and herbs (ch. 46). A *gana* is a grouping by therapeutic action. That is a classification by action; it is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status, and the text read assigns Sumukha neither. The chapters of the *Chikitsasthana* on *rasayana* and *vajikarana* (ch. 26 to 31 on disk) were searched for the name and it does not occur in them. Nothing links it to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The status is unattested; only a therapeutic one is recorded.

**Practitioner's note.** A herb named in three lists, with one sentence that makes it a Surasa-like drug that also answers poisons. A cultivator has nothing to use from it until it is identified, and the record carries no long-term or life-nourishing claim.
