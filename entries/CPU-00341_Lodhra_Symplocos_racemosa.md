### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (Ayurveda — surgical tradition):** Lodhra is one of Ayurveda's paradigmatic **astringents (kaṣāya/saṃgrāhi/stambhana)**. Suśruta places it among the wound- and haemorrhage-controlling astringent clusters and uses it heavily as a **vraṇaśodhana–vraṇaropaṇa** (wound-cleansing and healing) dusting powder and wash, and as a **stambhana** (styptic/arresting) agent for diarrhoea (atisāra), bleeding disorders (raktapitta), and excessive discharges. Its single most celebrated role is in **Strī-roga (gynaecology)**: Lodhra is a keystone of formulae for **leucorrhoea (śveta-pradara), menorrhagia/metrorrhagia (rakta-pradara/asṛgdara), and uterine laxity**, where its astringent-cooling action is read as toning and "binding" a relaxed, over-secreting reproductive tract. It also anchors many **varṇya (complexion-improving)** and skin formulations — the classic Lodhra-Dhātakī-Manjiṣṭhā style face applications.

**Caraka & the later nighaṇṭus:** Caraka lists Lodhra among **saṃgrāhi** (absorbent-astringent, for diarrhoea) and **kaṇḍughna/kuṣṭhaghna** (skin) drugs. *Bhāvaprakāśa* and allied nighaṇṭus record two market types — **Lodhra** and the superior **Pathani/Paṭṭikā Lodhra** — indicated for pradara, atisāra, netra-roga (eye disease), raktapitta, and as a *cakṣuṣya* (eye tonic) wash. The **stem bark** is the principal medicinal part.

**Cross-tradition & folk use:** Beyond the Sanskritic core, Lodhra bark travelled through Unani and vernacular practice for **bleeding gums, spongy gums, conjunctivitis, ulcers, and dye-work** (the bark yields a mordant/dye, tying it to textile and cosmetic culture). Modern reviews record ethnobotanical use for eye, ear, skin, liver and bowel complaints, tumours, uterine disorders, asthma, fever, snake-bite and arthritis (Acharya et al., 2016).

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Binder and the Threshold:** Lodhra's whole identity is **holding-in**: it arrests flux, closes wounds, tones the loosened. In the doctrinal grammar of the doṣas, unchecked flow (atisāra, pradara, raktapitta) is Pitta-Kapha *escaping its vessel*; the astringent kaṣāya rasa is the taste of **containment** — it puckers, contracts, seals. Lodhra is thus a "threshold guardian," most at home at the body's exits (the mucosa, the uterus, the wound margin, the eye). Its deep cultural bond with **cosmetics and dye** — the substance that fixes colour and clears the complexion — reinforces the symbolic reading of a **fixing/finishing agent**: the plant that sets what should not run. This is precisely a RESET signature: consolidation, not stimulation.

### Active Components

Documented constituents of *S. racemosa* stem bark include **phenolic glycosides (symplocoside, salireposide), flavonoids (notably quercetin), triterpenoids (betulinic acid, oleanolic acid, acetyl-oleanolic acid, β-amyrin), the alkaloids loturine/colloturine/loturidine, ellagic acid, β-sitosterol, and a heavy tannin load**. The tannins and phenolics underwrite the astringent (kaṣāya) and styptic actions; flavonoids and triterpenoids account for the antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and reported anti-androgenic/oestrogen-modulating signals.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the evidence for *S. racemosa* is **preclinical and phytochemical** — animal, in-vitro, and review-level — and it aligns closely with the classical astringent/wound/gynaecological profile without reaching human trials.

- **Phytopharmacology review (traditional-use synthesis):** A comprehensive review catalogues *S. racemosa*'s traditional uterine, hepatic and leucorrhoea indications and documents reported **anti-cancer, hepatoprotective, antioxidant, anti-androgenic, anti-inflammatory, wound-healing and anti-diabetic** activities, mapped to symplocoside, betulinic/oleanolic acids and quercetin (Acharya et al., 2016, *J Ethnopharmacol*). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2016.01.043)
- **Hypolipidemic & antioxidant (rat):** An ethanolic bark extract (200–400 mg/kg) lowered elevated total cholesterol, triglycerides, VLDL and LDL, raised HDL, inhibited hepatic HMG-CoA reductase, and restored hepatic glutathione/SOD/catalase in Triton- and high-fat-diet–induced hyperlipidemic rats (Durkar, Patil & Naik, 2014, *Indian J Exp Biol*; PMID 24617014 — no DOI issued).
- **Antimicrobial / antibiofilm / antiproliferative (in vitro + biosafety):** Bark phytoconstituents (flavonoid-rich fraction) showed activity against drug-resistant *E. coli*, MRSA and *Salmonella*, disrupted biofilms, and were cytotoxic to cancer cell lines but not to normal Vero cells, with in-vivo acute-oral-toxicity biosafety in mice (Sood et al., 2020, *BMC Pharmacol Toxicol*). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40360-020-00453-y)

**Evidence grade: preclinical/traditional.** Multiple rodent and in-vitro studies corroborate antioxidant, hypolipidemic, antimicrobial and wound endpoints; **no controlled human trials** validate the gynaecological or any clinical use. Attribution: PubMed.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṣāya (astringent), Tikta (bitter)
- **Guṇa (quality):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** Pradara-hara / śoṇita-sthāpaka — a specific uterine-astringent and haemostatic potency beyond generic astringency; also cakṣuṣya (eye-benefitting)
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha-Pitta-śāmaka; strongly saṃgrāhi/stambhana (absorbent-arresting)
- **Karma (actions):** Stambhana (styptic/arresting), Saṃgrāhi (absorbent), Vraṇaropaṇa & Vraṇaśodhana (wound healing/cleansing), Raktapitta-hara (anti-haemorrhagic), Varṇya (complexion), Kaṇḍughna/Kuṣṭhaghna (skin), Cakṣuṣya (eye)
- **Gaṇa membership:** Astringent/wound clusters incl. Nyagrodhādi & Priyaṅgvādi-type groups (Suśruta)

### Contraindications

- **Constipating/drying:** the strong astringent action can worsen constipation, dryness, and Vāta states; avoid in atonic/dry bowel conditions.
- **Amenorrhoea / scanty flow:** its "binding" nature is theoretically inappropriate where the goal is to *promote* flow.
- **Pregnancy/lactation:** given anti-androgenic/oestrogen-modulating and uterine-directed signals, avoid medicinal internal use without expert supervision — no human safety data.
- **Hormone-sensitive conditions:** reported anti-androgenic/oestrogenic activity is uncharacterised in humans — treat as unknown.
- **Conservation:** *Symplocos* is flagged as at extinction risk from over-harvest; source sustainably.
- **No validated human dose:** internal concentrated extracts should be practitioner-guided.
