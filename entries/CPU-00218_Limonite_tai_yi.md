# Limonite tai yi (Tai Yi Yu Yu Liang) — Tai Yi Yu Yu Liang, "Limonitum" (Yang 1998); other name Shi Nao (Stone Brain)
## *A stone of the Divine Farmer's Classic that Yang glosses "Limonitum" (limonite, hydrated iron oxide); Yang's footnote 47 says it is the same substance as the next entry, Yu Yu Liang, the two being told apart by place of production* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Tai Yi Yu Yu Liang is printed in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) on PDF page 26, in the run of "Jades and Stones: Superior Class". The OCR of the page prints no heading; the heading is printed at the top of the page before (line 985, page 25) and of the page after (line 1060, page 27). The text (lines 1033-1038): "Tai Yi Yu Yu Liang (Limonitum)47 is sweet and balanced. It mainly treats cough and counterflow qi ascent, concretions and conglomerations, blood block, and leaking. It eliminates evil qi. Protracted taking may build endurance to cold or summerheat and hunger and make one an immortal with a body so light as to be able to fly a thousand l[i] [the OCR prints 'lie']. Its other name is Shi Nao (Stone Brain). It is produced in mountains and valleys." The word "li" (a Chinese distance) is restored from the OCR "lie"; [USER: verify] against the page image.

Yang's footnote 47 (lines 1051-1057): "Tai Yi Yu Yu Liang can be literally translated as the Surplus Provisions of Yu of Great Supremacy. Great Supremacy (Tai Yi) was the teacher of legendary Yu who was the founder of China's first empire. Therefore, this medicinal was regarded as a divine medicinal. Actually, however, it is the same substance as the next one (Yu Yu Liang). In ancient times, two forms of this single substance were identified based on their different areas of production." The OCR of the footnote is damaged in places; the quotation restores "were identified" from "wer{' idpntified". Footnote 48 (lines 1071-1075) adds that Tai Yi Yu Yu Liang was the one "produced in the mountains" and plain Yu Yu Liang the one believed "formed under water".

The next entry, Yu Yu Liang, is printed at lines 1039-1041 and 1061-1062 (across the page break): "Yu Yu Liang (Limonitum) is sweet and cold. It mainly treats cough and counterflow, cold and heat, vexatious fullness, red and white dysentery, blood block, concretions and conglomerations, and great fever. Taking it after it is sublimated may make one free from hunger, the body light, and prolong life. It is produced in pools and swamps." The spine row (CPU-00218) is for the Tai Yi form; its note says "high; id-uncertain". The two Yang entries differ in the printed quality (Tai Yi: "sweet and balanced"; Yu Yu Liang: "sweet and cold") and in some indications, so they are not word-for-word the same text, whatever footnote 47 says of the substance. `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` has a row for Yu Yu Liang only (line 15, "line 1039"), not for Tai Yi Yu Yu Liang; the list `_sources/shennong.csv` carries both, 太一禹余糧 (line 16) and 禹余糧 (line 17). The spine row for this entry names only the Tai Yi form, and the names row is for the plain form; the spine does not appear to carry a separate row for plain Yu Yu Liang. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Limonite is not a single mineral. The name covers hydrated iron oxides and oxyhydroxides, chiefly the mineral goethite, FeO(OH), with water, in earthy yellow-brown masses; it is the rust-coloured material of weathered iron deposits and the basis of yellow and brown ochre pigments. The spine's formula FeO(OH) is the general limonite formula. The sources read give no composition for the stone Yang translates.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records the stone for cough, concretions, blood block and "leaking", and says that long taking may build endurance to cold, summerheat and hunger and make one an immortal light enough to fly "a thousand li". The Codex does not endorse these claims and offers no protocol. The flight and immortality lines are the language of the Daoist elixir tradition and are not claims of fact. Swallowing iron-oxide earth is not recommended.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is printed in the monograph for Tai Yi Yu Yu Liang and no dose is given in the sources retrieved; the text says only "Protracted taking" (line 1035). The companion entry Yu Yu Liang prints a preparation, "after it is sublimated" (lines 1041, 1061), and no dose. The sublimation process itself is not described in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

Iron oxides are used as pigments, and iron is an essential mineral, but the iron in limonite is bound in insoluble oxide and is poorly available to the body. Impure natural iron earths can carry heavy metals such as arsenic or lead. No medical benefit of swallowing limonite for the conditions the Classic names is established. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- SBJ: "sweet and balanced" (Yang line 1033). Class: Superior (heading lines 985 and 1060). "Balanced" is Yang's rendering for neither cold nor hot, neither warm nor cool (his footnote 32, lines 849-855). Plain Yu Yu Liang is printed "sweet and cold" (line 1039). No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: an upper-grade drug (上品).* Tai Yi Yu Yu Liang stands between two printings of "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (Yang lines 985 and 1060); the page itself prints no heading, and the entry is placed in this as Mica (Yun Mu) is. Yang's preface says the superior class medicinals "mainly nourish life and correspond to heaven. They are nontoxic and taking them in large amounts and for a long time will not harm people. If one intends to make one's body light, boost the qi, prevent aging, and prolong life, one should base [one's efforts] on the superior class" (lines 352-358). The monograph prints its own lines: "Protracted taking may build endurance to cold or summerheat and hunger and make one an immortal with a body so light as to be able to fly a thousand li" (lines 1035-1037). Yang's footnote 47 says the name made the medicinal "a divine medicinal". The grade is a statement of the Classic as translated by Yang in 1998 and is not carried forward from later materia medica works. Yang's foreword (lines 312-328) warns that elixirs of this kind made some who took them ill.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: the Classic held it safe for long use and promised immortality of it. Limonite is rusty earth and the Classic's claims have no established support, so nothing here supports taking it. For a cultivator the entry is a record of how the Han-period elixir tradition raised an iron earth to the rank of a "divine medicinal". No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Natural iron earths vary in composition and may carry toxic metals; powdered stone sold as a remedy may be a different mineral. Mineral medicines are unstandardised and their sale is regulated in many places. The Classic's immortality and flight claims are not claims of fact.
