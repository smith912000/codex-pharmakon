# Amianthus (asbestos) — Amiantos Lithos (AMIANTOS LITHOS), "Amiantus stone"
## *A fibrous stone from Cyprus that is woven into cloth and comes out of the fire brighter and unburnt; the translator's heading to Dioscorides 5-156 reads "Amianthus — Asbestos, fibrous green Chrysolite", and the chapter itself describes the stone only by its behaviour in fire* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Amiantus stone is chapter 156 of Book Five of the *De Materia Medica*, in the section "METALLIC STONES", headed AMIANTOS LITHOS (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 10644-10647; the name list `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` carries it as 5-156, line 10644, and `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 882 has "Amiantos Lithos", class mineral, with the identification marked uncertain; the book's page index prints "AMIANTOS LITHOS 822" and "Amianthus 822", and an index entry "Asbestos 822"). The translator's heading (line 10646) reads "Amianthus — Asbestos, fibrous green Chrysolite". The whole chapter is one paragraph (line 10647): "Amiantus stone is found in Cyprus. It is like alumen scissile [5-123] which the workmen make webs of cloth from for a show, because put into the fire they take flame, but come out more bright, not burnt by the fire."

The chapter therefore says four things. The stone is found in Cyprus. It is "like alumen scissile", the cut or split alum of chapter 5-123. The workmen make "webs of cloth" from it, "for a show". And the proof of its nature is in the fire: it "takes flame" but comes out "more bright, not burnt". The chapter gives no medicinal use, no property, no preparation and no dose. It is in the list of stones as a curiosity of the craft, and the other "METALLIC STONES" around it are mostly given a use, for example 5-155 (the Judaean stone), 5-157 (sapphire stone, taken in a drink for scorpion stings) and 5-159 (selenite, filed and given to the epileptic).

*What the comparison means.* Chapter 5-123 (STUPTERIA, "Aluminium Sulphate, Potassium Sulphate — Alum", line 10468) describes the cut kind of alum as the best: "especially that which is new, white, without stones, with a strong smell, very astringent, and furthermore not compacted together like turf or slate, but opening its mouth wide like gray hairs spread-apart, such as that called trichitis, found in Egypt" (line 10470). The English words "gray hairs spread-apart" suggest what the comparison to a splittable, hair-like mineral was meant to convey for amianthus, but the amianthus chapter does not spell out the likeness, and the alum chapter is about a different substance, a salt.

*A name that must not be confused.* The text's own chapter headed ASBESTOS is 5-133 (line 10525), and it is not this stone. The translator's gloss there reads "Quicklime — Calx Viva", with the note "Asbestos refers to 'the unquenchable stone' — the action of cold water on quicklime." So in the *De Materia Medica* *asbestos* is lime and *amiantos* is the fibrous stone, the opposite of the modern usage in which "asbestos" names the fibrous minerals. This entry follows the modern identification the translator gives (the fibrous mineral) and keeps the text's two words apart. The word "Chrysolite" in the translator's heading is an old mineral name whose meaning has shifted over time. Whether "fibrous green Chrysolite" is what the translator means by chrysotile, the serpentine form of asbestos, is not stated, and the identification is an opinion, not a text. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, the asbestos minerals are fibrous silicates, of two broad kinds: chrysotile, a magnesium silicate of the serpentine group that forms curly fibres, and several amphiboles that form straight, needle-like fibres. The fibres are very strong, flexible, and resistant to heat and acid, which is why they could be spun and woven. Nothing links any constituent to a medicinal action, because the text gives none.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text records a fibrous stone that can be woven and passed through fire. It gives no medicinal use and the Codex gives none. The Codex warns against handling or using asbestos in any form: its fibres are a proven cause of lung disease, as set out under Scientific Correlation and Contraindications. The entry exists to record that the Greek materia medica knew a fire-proof woven stone from Cyprus, and to keep its name apart from the text's *asbestos*, which is lime.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives no preparation of the stone beyond the observation that cloth is woven from it and that it is tested in fire. It gives no medicinal preparation and no dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Asbestos minerals are fibrous silicates that do not burn and resist heat; they were used for fireproofing, insulation and woven fabric. Inhaling asbestos fibres is a proven cause of asbestosis (scarring of the lung), lung cancer and mesothelioma, a cancer of the lining of the lung or abdomen, often decades after exposure; there is no known safe level of exposure, and its use is banned or restricted in many countries. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM):** No quality is printed for amianthus in 5-156: not warming, cooling, drying, astringent or any other. The chapter gives its place of origin and its behaviour in fire only. The qualities printed in 5-123 for the moist kind of alum ("warming, astringent", line 10472) belong to that salt and are not carried over to the stone.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is asserted here. The Codex does not recommend handling, wearing, burning or ingesting it. Nothing in the text supports a cultivation use. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Inhalation:** asbestos fibres cause asbestosis, lung cancer and mesothelioma; never handle, cut, grind or burn asbestos-containing material, and if it is found in old buildings or products leave it to a licensed professional.
- **Ingestion and skin:** do not swallow it or apply it to the body.
- **Legal status:** asbestos is banned or tightly restricted in many countries; do not buy or trade it.
- **Identity:** in the *De Materia Medica* *asbestos* (5-133) is quicklime, a caustic substance, and *amiantos* is the fibrous stone; do not mix the two.
- **Identification:** the translator's gloss is an opinion; the text identifies the stone only by Cyprus, the likeness to cut alum, and the fire test.
