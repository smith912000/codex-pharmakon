# Peach Kernel — Táo Rén (桃仁)

## *Prunus persica* (L.) Batsch | Family: Rosaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ (Shennong Ben Cao Jing — *táo rén*, blood-stasis and bowel)

**Energetic Classification (TCM):** Neutral · bitter, sweet · slightly toxic (小毒). Enters HEART, LIVER, and LARGE INTESTINE. Invigorates blood and breaks blood stasis; moistens the intestines to move stool.

**Training Phase:** `[RESET]` (stasis-breaking, downward and outward-moving; a "clearing" agent)

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese (Shennong / SBJ):** Peach kernel (*táo rén*) is a foundational **blood-invigorating, stasis-breaking** drug in the Chinese materia medica, its use rooted in the early bencao tradition attributed to Shennong. Where apricot kernel (*xìng rén*) descends Lung qi, peach kernel works on the **blood level** — it is prescribed for stasis patterns: fixed stabbing pain, masses, amenorrhoea and post-partum retention, and traumatic injury. It anchors classical stasis formulas such as *Xue Fu Zhu Yu Tang* and *Tao Hong Si Wu Tang* (peach kernel + safflower/*hóng huā* being the paradigmatic stasis pair). Its second, softer action — like its Rosaceae cousin — is to **moisten the intestines** via fixed oil, easing dry constipation, especially where dryness accompanies blood deficiency.

The peach itself carries deep symbolic weight in Chinese culture (the peach of immortality, *xiāntáo*; peachwood as apotropaic against malign influences), so the plant sits at the intersection of pharmacy and cosmology in a way few drugs do.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Seed that Breaks the Stagnant:** The Codex reads *táo rén* as the pharmacological correlate of the peach's mythic role. The fruit grants longevity by *keeping things moving*; the kernel enforces the same law at the level of blood — it dissolves what has congealed, fixed pain, clots, masses. Doctrinally it is a **de-stagnation** agent: the tradition's insistence that stasis (瘀血) is the root of fixed, chronic, stabbing pathology finds its remedy in a seed. As with apricot kernel, the same cyanogenic chemistry that carries a toxicity warning is folded into the "slightly toxic" (小毒) grading — the tradition encoding potency and hazard in a single term.

### Active Components

- **Amygdalin** — the cyanogenic diglucoside shared with apricot kernel; yields HCN, benzaldehyde and glucose on β-glucosidase hydrolysis. Basis of the "slightly toxic" classification.
- **Prunasin** — cyanogenic monoglucoside intermediate.
- **Fixed oil (~40–45%)** — oleic/linoleic triglycerides; drives the intestine-moistening action.
- **Fatty-acid and sterol fraction, amino acids, prunasin-associated enzymes** — contribute to the seed's overall profile.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Framed as a `[RESET]` circulatory-clearing agent: traditionally a blood-mover for stasis-type presentations and a gentle bowel-moistener. **Kenetica does not present peach kernel as an anti-cancer therapy** — the amygdalin/laetrile evidence base is negative and the cyanide risk is real (see Scientific Correlation, and cross-ref the Apricot Kernel entry). Applied use is confined to **processed, correctly dosed, supervised** decoction contexts.

### Scientific Correlation

Attribution: findings from PubMed.

- **In-vitro anti-proliferative signal (preclinical only).** Peach and apricot kernel extracts produced dose-/time-dependent, biphasic effects and a possible S-phase block in HT-29 colon cancer cells — a cell-culture finding, not clinical evidence (Cassiem & de Kock, 2019, *BMC Complement Altern Med*) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-019-2437-4).
- **Amygdalin pharmacokinetics in a classical formula.** In a UPLC/QTOF-MS study of *Dahuang-mudan* decoction (which contains *Prunus persica*), amygdalin was among the constituents detected in plasma and its absorption was modulated by co-formulated mirabilite — evidence that peach-kernel amygdalin is bioavailable and that formula context alters it (Nong et al., 2018, *Biomed Chromatogr*) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/bmc.4423).
- **Anti-cancer "laetrile" claims — negative** (shared amygdalin chemistry): Cochrane found no efficacy and real cyanide-poisoning risk (Milazzo & Horneber, 2015, *Cochrane Database Syst Rev*) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005476.pub4).

**Evidence grade:** Blood-stasis clinical action — **traditional**, not RCT-verified here. Anti-cancer — **none / against.** Amygdalin bioavailability & anti-proliferative signal — **preclinical.**

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy — contraindicated.** Blood-breaking action can threaten pregnancy; classical caution.
- **Cyanogenic toxicity** — shares amygdalin with apricot kernel; do not self-dose raw kernels (see Apricot Kernel entry for documented poisoning).
- **Not a cancer therapy** — no efficacy evidence (Milazzo & Horneber 2015).
- **Bleeding tendency / anticoagulant use** — blood-invigorating action may compound bleeding risk; use with caution.
- **Children** — avoid unsupervised use given cyanogenic content.
