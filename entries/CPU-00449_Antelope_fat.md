# Antelope fat — "Fat-of-the-Antilope" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus)
## *The fat of an animal Bryan calls "the Antilope"; no Egyptian word, species or preparation of the fat is given in the sources retrieved, and the spine row's "Antilope / Gazella sp." is the list-maker's gloss* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The spine row reads "Antelope fat, Antilope / Gazella sp." with the note "Ebers; sae.saw-leipzig.de" (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 69). The website was not opened for this entry. The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*. Bryan's list of the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (Chapter VII; `Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` lines 1390-1404) does not contain the word "Antelope" or "Antilope". It has "Stag Gazelle Ram Goat" at line 1398 (name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 111, "Gazelle"). The name-index file therefore has no row for this animal, although the running text uses the word. Bryan does not say whether his "Antilope" and his "Gazelle" are one animal or two, and the Codex does not equate them. [USER: verify]

**The one prescription for the fat.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "antelope", "antilope", "gazelle", "oryx" and "ibex". The fat is named once, at line 2026, in the part of the book that Bryan devotes to anal complaints, after the section headed "The Abdomen" (heading line 1928) and before "Minor Medicine" (heading line 2091). Bryan introduces it at line 2000: "it occasions no surprise to find a large section devoted to relieving this sequela [of the foregoing treatments]. As usual the thing was done thoroughly. Internal remedies were first prescribed", then gives a drink "REMEDY TO COOL THE ANUS" of onion-meal, mouse tail, honey and water ("Strain and take for four days"), and continues: "A suppository was next recommended: 'ANOTHER TO REDUCE THE SMARTING IN THE ANUS. Fat-of-the-Antilope [quantity mark] Caraway [quantity mark]. Roll into a Pill and put in the Anus.'" The quantity marks are printed as single numerals in the text on disk (shown as "I") and cannot be read with certainty, so no proportion is stated. The next remedy, "ANOTHER TO HEAL THE DISEASED ANUS" (line 2040), begins with cow-horn and is a separate prescription.

**Related items, not this one.** Other parts of the same animal word appear in the text, and are listed because they are easily confused with the fat: "Antilope-dung" is one ingredient in a salve for bleary eyes (line 2735: "Myrrh, Onions, Verdigris, and Cyperus-from-the-North, with Antilopedung, Clear Oil, and Entrails-of-the-qadit-animal", painted on with a vulture's feather); "Gazelle's Ears" are an ingredient in a remedy that dries a discharge (line 3066); and "Gazelle's dung" occurs in a hair remedy with writing-fluid and hippopotamus fat (line 4030) and, burnt in a granary, to keep rodents from the *durra* (line 4239). The baldness pomade Bryan prints at lines 4023-4029 lists the fats of lion, hippopotamus, crocodile, cat, serpent and "the Egyptian-Goat" and does not name antelope fat. Two Codex project files under `Sources/EP_Ebers/` (`EP_Original_Hieroglyphic.md` line 371, `EP_Overview.md` line 190) print a similar pomade with an ibex fat or a wild-goat fat; they disagree with Bryan and with each other, and were not used. [USER: verify]

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say which animal Bryan's "Antilope" is, how the fat was prepared, how much was used, or why this fat was chosen for the complaint. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis; Bryan offers none beyond the English word.

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it. In general terms, ruminant fat (tallow) is mainly triglycerides, rich in saturated fatty acids, which makes it solid at room temperature and suitable as an ointment or suppository base. Caraway seed, the other ingredient, contains a volatile oil. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show a single suppository of antelope fat and caraway "to reduce the smarting in the anus", given after a drink for cooling the anus. There is nothing for a practitioner to apply: the animal is not identified, and no quantity is legible. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only the one preparation at line 2026: the fat and caraway rolled "into a Pill" and put "in the Anus", after the internal remedy of lines 2002-2018. The quantity marks in the text on disk are not legible. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution, and here the complaint is a local, post-treatment discomfort. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); Bryan prints no recitation with the antelope-fat suppository, though he summarises, and that is no proof the papyrus gives none. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The record is one suppository for anal smarting, with an unidentified animal and no legible quantity. A cultivator has nothing to do with it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** untreated animal fat is not sterile; rectal insertion of non-sterile material risks infection and injury.
- **Anal complaints:** pain, bleeding or discharge from the anus need medical assessment.
- **Unidentified animal:** the species is not given, and many antelope and gazelle species are protected; the status of any particular species cannot be given from the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
