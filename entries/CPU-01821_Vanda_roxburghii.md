**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The two names in the chapters.* "Nakuli" is printed once in the chapter files as a plant (`SS_Kalpa_ch08.txt` line 625). "Rasna" (OCR "Rdsnd") is printed on thirty-two lines of the chapter files (Sutra 36, 38 and 39; Chikitsa 5, 17, 18, 19, 22, 37 and 38; Uttara 28, 39, 51, 52, 57, 59 and 62; Kalpa 8; Sharira 10); the passages are listed below. The compound "Gandha-Nakuli" (`SS_Kalpa_ch07.txt` line 130 and "Gandhanakuli", `SS_Kalpa_ch08.txt` line 689) is a different Sanskrit name that the appendix lines read do not pair with this Latin, so it is not used. The "Nakulandhya" of the eye chapters (Uttara 1 and Uttara 7) is a disease name built on the word for the mongoose and is not this plant.

*Nakuli in a spider-bite remedy (Kalpa 8, lines 619 to 627).* In the chapter on insects, a bite by a spider "of the Tri-mandalak species is marked by a flow of black-coloured blood from the bite which is transformed into an open ulcer. It is also attended with deafness, impaired or cloudy vision and a burning sensation in the eyes. In such cases, a compound consisting of Arka roots, Rajani, Ndkuli and Pris'ni-parnika should be employed as snuff as well as in drink (Pana), enemas (Vastis) and ointments etc."

*Rasna in a plaster (Sutra 36, lines 7 to 11).* In the chapter of miscellaneous remedies for swellings: "A medicinal plaster, composed of Matulanga, Agnimantha, Devadaru, Mahaushdham, Ahimsra, and Rasna pasted together and applied to the seat of the affection, leads to the resolution of a swelling, due to the action of the deranged Vayu."

*Rasna in a group (Sutra 38, lines 105 to 117).* Rasna is named in the Arkadi group, "Nagadanti, Mayuraka, Bhargi, Rasna, Indrapushpa, Kshudrashveta, Mahashveta, Vrishchikali, Alavana and Tapasha-vriksha", of which the text says: "The group known as the Arkadi destroys Kaphani, fat, and the effects of poison. It acts as a vermifuge and a specific aseptic agent in the case of an ulcer and proves curative in diseases of the skin."

*Rasna among the Kapha-restoring drugs (Sutra 39, lines 112 to 122).* In the chapter on cleansing and soothing drugs: "The drugs known as Kaleyaka, Aguru, Tilaparni, Kushtha, Haridra, Shitashiva, Shatapushpa, Sarala, Rasna, Prakiryya, Udakiryya, Ingudi, Sumanah, Kakadani, Langalaki, Hastikarna, Munjataka, Lamajjaka, etc. and the drugs belonging to [several groups] are generally possessed of the efficacy of restoring the deranged Shleshma to its natural state."

*Rasna in an oil (Chikitsa 37, lines 70 to 78).* In the chapter on the *Anuvasana* and *Uttara* injections, the "Third Taila" lists Rasna among some thirty drugs "pasted together and cooked with the proper quantity of milk and oil. The oil thus prepared is highly efficacious in the disorders of the deranged Vayu."

*Rasna in the months of pregnancy (Sharira 10, lines 862 to 884).* Seven recipes are to be "successively given with milk to an enciente, from the first to the seventh month of her gestation, in the case of a threatened miscarraige or abortion". The fourth is "Ananta, Siriya, Rasna, Padma, and Madhuka (Yashtimadhu)", which by the order of the list is the fourth month's.

*Rasna in other recipes (read in context, one line each).* Rasna is an ingredient in: a gargle of Vacha, Ativisha, Patha, Rasna, Katuka-rohini and Pichu-marda in the chapter on diseases of the mouth (Chikitsa 22, lines 342 to 344); an oil mixed with the powders of Rasna and Sarala to heal a glandular swelling after it has been purified (Chikitsa 18, lines 71 to 75); a milk preparation cooked with Syama, Rasna, Sushavi, Prishniparni and others, and a plaster of Vilvapeshika, Tagara, Devadaru, Sarala, Rasna and others, in the chapter on the major Vata disorders (Chikitsa 5, lines 105 to 107 and 153 to 156); a plaster for the Vataja type in the chapter on hernia and hydrocele (Chikitsa 19, lines 210 to 213); a list of drugs applied in the chapter on erysipelas, sinuses and the breast (Chikitsa 17, line 123); the child's ghrita against Skanda-graha (Uttara 28, lines 21 to 23); febrile decoctions (Uttara 39, lines 1132 to 1136 and 1356 to 1359); a lambative in asthma (Uttara 51, lines 220 to 224); compounds in cough and asthma (Uttara 52, lines 253 to 257 and 320 to 324); a powder taken in aversion to food (Uttara 57, lines 48 to 51); an oil in the chapter on defects of urine (Uttara 59, lines 114 to 118); and a long compound in the chapter on insanity (Uttara 62, lines 154 to 157). It is in an *Agada* of Chandana, Rasna, Ela, Harenu, Nala, Vanjula, Kushtha, Lamajjaka, Chakra and Nalada for a spider bite (Kalpa 8, lines 630 to 634). In the chapter on the *Nirudha* enema (Chikitsa 38) it is named in at least eight decoctions and pastes for *Vasti* (lines 223, 329, 352, 388, 404, 495, 731 and 746), and in the chapter on the *Anuvasana* injection (Chikitsa 37) it appears in the "Third Taila" quoted above, the "Fourth Taila" (line 91), the "Fifth Taila (with clarified butter)" (line 113) and a further recipe (line 223). The passages read give no quantity of Rasna beyond the "one Pala" of several Chikitsa 38 decoctions (for example lines 352 and 388).

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Vanda* is a genus of epiphytic orchids (Orchidaceae). Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Rasna as an ingredient in a plaster for a swelling due to the deranged Vayu, as a member of a group of drugs against Kapha and of drugs that restore the deranged Shleshma, as an ingredient of oils, enemas, gargles and plasters in many chapters and of a pregnancy recipe, and Nakuli as an ingredient of a spider-bite remedy. These are the text's statements about Sanskrit names, and the Latin name the appendix attaches to them is the translator's, by position. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives these preparations, each with its locus. Sutra 36, lines 7 to 11: a plaster of Rasna with Matulanga, Agnimantha, Devadaru, Mahaushadha and Ahimsra. Chikitsa 37, lines 70 to 78: the "Third Taila", a milk-and-oil preparation used as an *Anuvasana* injection. Sharira 10, lines 868 to 884: a milk recipe for the fourth month of gestation. Kalpa 8, lines 624 to 627: Nakuli with Arka root, Rajani and Prishniparni as snuff, drink, enema and ointment. Chikitsa 38, lines 352 and 388: "one Pala" of Rasna in decoctions for the *Nirudha* enema. No other quantity of Rasna or Nakuli is given in the passages read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** the passages read print for Rasna its place in the Arkadi group, whose stated virtue is that it "destroys Kaphani, fat, and the effects of poison", is a vermifuge and an aseptic for ulcers and cures skin disease (Sutra 38, lines 113 to 117), and its place in the list of drugs that restore the deranged Shleshma (Sutra 39). No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Rasna or Nakuli in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text names Rasna in a plaster, in a group of Kapha-reducing drugs, in an oil for Vayu disorders and in a pregnancy recipe, and names Nakuli in a spider-bite remedy. A search of the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) found neither name, so the text assigns the plant no rejuvenative or generative status in the lines retrieved. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The pregnancy recipe is a physician's recipe for a stated complaint and not a practice for a cultivator. The Latin identification is the appendix's. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. In the Sushruta scheme the name is a plaster ingredient, a group member and a component of recipes given by a physician for stated complaints. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of a Latin name that the chapters do not give, and the name Rasna is not settled.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read print no warning for the drug itself. The pregnancy recipe (Sharira 10) is given for a threatened miscarriage under a physician and is not advice for a healthy pregnancy. The spider-bite and plaster uses describe emergencies that need medical care. Wild orchids are widely protected, and collecting them may be illegal.
