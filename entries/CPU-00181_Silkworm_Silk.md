### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) lists "Animals: Superior Class", "Middle Class" and "Inferior Class" in its contents (lines 770-776), but the text layer is readable only to PDF p.104 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/README.md`), so no animal monograph could be read. The project's list `_sources/shennong.csv` has one row whose characters include 蠶 ("silkworm"): 白僵蠶 (line 226, no pinyin, binomial or class; the characters read white, stiff, silkworm). That this row is the spine's silkworm is the project's assumption; no page read supports or contradicts it. Nothing of what the Chinese text says of it was read. [USER: verify]

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Silk appears in Bhishagratna's English as cloth and as ash, and the silkworm is not described. In *Sutrasthana* ch.14, on the treatment of bleeding after opening a vein, if the flow is excessive the edges of the wound are rubbed with a powder of *Lodhra*, *Priyangu*, *Madhuka*, *Pattanga*, *Gairika*, *Sarjarasa*, *Rasanjana*, *Shalmali* flowers, *Shankha*, *Shukti*, *Masha*, *Yava* and *Godhuma*, "or the edges of the wound should be lightly dusted with the burnt ashes of a silk cord (a piece of silk rolled up in the form of a cord), and firmly pressed with the tips of the fingers", and afterwards "the wound should be firmly tied up (with a piece of silk or linen)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch14.txt` lines 366-380). In *Sutrasthana* ch.18, the chapter on dressings and bandages of ulcers, the articles of bandaging include *Kshauma* ("cloth woven with the fibres of Atasi plant"), *Avika* (sheep's wool), "Dukulum (loom-silk), Kausheya (silk)", and *Chinapatta* ("Chinese cloth") (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch18.txt` lines 154-160). In *Chikitsasthana* ch.2, on traumatic wounds, after the intestines have been replaced the wound "should be bandaged with a piece of silk-cloth saturated" (the sentence runs on past the page break; `chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch02.txt` lines 373-374); in ch.4, among the "Measures beneficial to Vata-Vyadhi", "the wearing of woollen, silken, cotton or any other thick kind of garments" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch04.txt` lines 149-159); in ch.40 a stem covered with "a piece of silk cloth" and a medicated paste is burnt as a smoking stick (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch40.txt` lines 18-23). In *Sharira* ch.10 a newborn, "being wrapped up in silk", is laid on "a bed covered with a silken sheet" (`sharira/SS_Sharira_ch10.txt` lines 388-391). In *Uttara* ch.10 the translator renders *Kshauma* as "silk" ("folded inside a piece of silk (Kshauma)", `uttara/SS_Uttara_ch10.txt` line 79), whereas *Sutrasthana* ch.18 glosses *Kshauma* as "cloth woven with the fibres of Atasi plant" (line 156) and *Chikitsasthana* ch.4 as "Kshauma linen" (line 129); the translations disagree. ### Active Components

Silk is made of fibroin and sericin, proteins secreted by the silkworm to make its cocoon. Burnt silk is a protein char and ash. The sources read here use silk as cloth and as a burnt styptic powder; nothing is said of the larva or pupa as a drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. In the Sanskrit text silk is a dressing and a cloth, and its burnt ash one of several powders pressed on a bleeding wound. The Tibetan and Chinese attestations are a list row each. Nothing here supports taking silk or silkworm material by mouth. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation.

### Preparation & Dosing

SS: a silk cord is burnt and the ash dusted on a bleeding wound edge (Sutra ch.14, lines 373-376); silk cloth is used as a bandage (Sutra ch.14, ch.18; Chikitsa ch.2) and to cover a stem made into a smoking stick (Chikitsa ch.40). No quantity of ash is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Silk is a protein fibre used in sutures and dressings today; burnt textile ash would be unsterile and is not a recommended styptic. Silk proteins can provoke allergic reactions in some people. No clinical studies were retrieved for the traditional uses. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Burnt textile ash on a bleeding wound is not sterile and can infect it; wound bleeding needs proper dressing and medical care. Silk and silkworm products can cause allergic reactions, and dried or fungus-killed silkworm preparations sold as medicines are unstandardised. Silkworm farming is regulated in some places for disease control; the legal status of silkworm material as a medicine has not been checked here. [USER: verify]
