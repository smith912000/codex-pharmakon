# Sugandhaka — Sugandhaka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907); the same printed name stands for a rice, a spice-herb and a drug of the Surasadi group

## *A name that Bhishagratna prints in three different settings: in the Surasadi drug group (Sutrasthana ch. 38), in the list of Shali rices (ch. 20 and ch. 46), and in the list of spices and herbs (ch. 46). The translator gives no gloss and the text on disk offers no identification, and it is not shown that the three are one substance: unresolved. The spine's guess of sulphur has no support in any of the lines read. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not identified, and the name is used for more than one thing in the text. Nothing in this entry is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the Surasadi group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. A search of all the Sushruta text on disk (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`, the chapter files and the three Bhishagratna volumes) for "Sugandhaka" found the four places below. The first-pass guess recorded on the row ("sulphur candidate") is not borne out: in each place the name stands among plants or grains, and none of the lines read calls it a mineral.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

1. **The Surasadi group (the row's locus).** *Sutrasthana* ch.38 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 121; `Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15462). The list reads: "The drugs known as Surasa, white Surasa, Fainjjhaka, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika form the group known as the Surasadi Gana." The metrical text gives the virtue of the group as a whole: "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough." This is the group's virtue and not a statement about Sugandhaka alone.
2. **A rice.** *Sutrasthana* ch.20, the chapter on salutary and non-salutary regimen (`SS_Sutra_ch20.txt` line 49; Vol. I line 10105): among "the food stuffs of all human beings" the red Shali, Shastika, Kanguka and others, "the Pushpaka, the Karddamaka, the Shakunahrita, the Sugandhaka, the Kalama, the Nivara, the Kodrava". And *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 55; Vol. I line 19715): "There are several species of Shali rice such as, the Lohitaka, Shali, Kalama, Kardamaka, Panduka, Sugandhaka, Shakunahrita, Pushpandaka, Pundarika, Mahashali ..." Here Sugandhaka is a variety of rice, a food grain.
3. **A spice-herb.** *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 1421; Vol. I line 21081): "The spices and herbs include the Pippali, Maricha, Shringavera, Ardraka, Hingu, Jiraka, Kustumvuru, Jamviraka, Sumukha, Surasa, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Kasamaraddka, Kalamala, Kutheraka, Kshavaka, Ksharapushpa, Shigru ..." The run Sumukha, Surasa, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Kasamarddaka, Kalamala runs closely parallel to the Surasadi list in the first place, which suggests the same herb. That is an observation of the two lists, not a statement of the text. [USER: verify]

The rice and the herb are not equated, and neither is equated with any plant of the Codex. No recipe in the text on disk was found that names Sugandhaka as an ingredient.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unidentified.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Surasadi group: vermifuge, aseptic, calming *Kapha*, in catarrh, loss of appetite, asthma and cough (group-level). In ch.46 it is listed as a spice-herb, and elsewhere as a rice; the chapters do not give it a separate action in those lists.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no taste or potency, no identification.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend any plant for these uses.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives no preparation of Sugandhaka. For the groups in general the chapter closes with a rule: plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, *Ghritas* or potions, using two, three or four drugs of a group, or the whole group, at the physician's discretion (`SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 558 to 569). That is a rule for the groups, not a preparation of this drug. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in a drug-group list and in two food lists (rice; spices and herbs). None of the places found is in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns it no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic or dietary one only. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it. The popular sattvic, rajasic and tamasic food lists do not trace to Sushruta and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** A name used for a rice, a spice-herb and a group drug, with no identification. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without an identification nothing can be gathered or used. A cultivator reads it as a name in a record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified and the name has more than one use in the text; do not substitute any herb, grain or mineral for it. In particular, do not read it as sulphur.
- **Asthma, cough, catarrh.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
