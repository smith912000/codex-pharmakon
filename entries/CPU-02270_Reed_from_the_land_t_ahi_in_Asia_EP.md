# Reed-from-the-land-t’ahi-in-Asia — and "Female Reed" (Bryan 1930, English rendering of the Papyrus Ebers)

## *A reed that Bryan's English calls "Reed-from-the-land-t’ahi-in-Asia", listed with a "Female Reed"; the Egyptian word is not given in the text on disk and no botanical identification is printed. The identification is unresolved, at low confidence. Bryan prints a similar phrase, "Calmus-from-the-land-t’ahi-in-Asia", in an incense recipe; whether the two phrases name one item is not stated, and a sweet-flag or other aromatic reed is a possibility we do not adopt.* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Ebers papyrus among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry, and the text is a modern English rendering, not the hieratic: Bryan's 1930 translation, `Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`. The name index (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, line 81) holds the row "Reed-from-the-land-t’ahi-in-Asia, Bryan 1930, Ch. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; a first-pass check called it "unresolved (Reed (Bryan label))".

**Egyptian (EP).**

*The list (Ch. VI, "Plant Remedies", line 1329).* Bryan introduces the chapter by saying that "of the recognizable Plants, and of their preparations, there is a not inconsiderable showing as the following list will reveal" (line 1233). The list prints, on one line (1329), "Reed-from-the-land-t’ahi-in-Asia Female Reed", between the Poppy group and the Saffron group. The phrase "the land t’ahi in Asia" is Bryan's rendering of a place name; the text on disk does not explain it. Bryan's caution on the whole list is that many other substances are "completely hidden from us under the guise of meaningless words" (same passage).

*The one prescription found for "Female Reed" (line 2531).* In Chapter XIV, "Diseases of Women", under "ANOTHER TO LOOSEN A CHILD IN THE BODY OF A WOMAN", the printed lines read "Sea-salt" (at the foot of a page, headed 84) and then, over the page, "Clean Grain-of-Wheat Female Reed / Plaster the abdomen therewith." So Female Reed is one of the ingredients of an abdominal plaster, on the reading that the ingredient lines run on across the page break. The OCR makes the exact ingredient list uncertain. *The similar phrase "Calmus-from-the-land-t’ahi-in-Asia" (line 4199).* In a list headed (OCR) "SUBSTANCES TO USE IN ORDER TO MAKE PLEASANT THE SMELL OF THE [HOUSE] OR OF THE CLOTHES": "Dried Myrrh, Elderberries, Incense, Cyperus, Resin-of-Aloes, sebet-resin, Calmus-from-the-land-t’ahi-in-Asia, inekuun-grain, mastich, styrax", followed by "Crush, grind, make into one, and put on the fire." The place name matches the list entry, and "calmus" is Bryan's word. Whether Bryan renders the same Egyptian item as "Reed" in one place and "Calmus" in the other is not said in the text. *Other reeds in the text, not this entry.* Bryan's "calmus-reed" at line 1863 is a tube through which a patient inhales steam, and the "Fruitful-Part-of-Reeds" of the breast spell (lines 2551 and 2554) is a spoken-over ingredient of a magic formula. Neither is shown to be the reed of this entry, and neither is in his list. The Common Reed entry (`I_619_CommonReed_Phragmites_australis.md`) treats the reed generally and does not cite this line.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent, and the plant is unidentified, so no composition is stated. If the item is an aromatic reed, general knowledge suggests volatile oils; that is a guess about an unspecified item and is not attributed to it. No analysis was read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As "Female Reed": an ingredient of an abdominal plaster in a remedy to loosen a child in the body of a woman (line 2531). As "Calmus-from-the-land-t’ahi-in-Asia" (if the same): an ingredient burned to sweeten the air of a house or clothes (line 4199).
- **What the text does not say.** The part of the reed used, the preparation of the plaster, the amount, or the effect.

The Codex does not recommend an abdominal plaster for childbirth or any procedure on a pregnant woman. Labour and delay in labour are medical emergencies.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the text gives are a plaster applied to the abdomen (line 2531) and a mixture "crushed, ground, made into one" and put on the fire (line 4199). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Reeds are tall grasses of wet ground; several species were used in ancient Egypt for writing, building and matting, and aromatic reeds are known in the Near East. Which one is meant here is not known. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is not identified; do not guess a species. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy and childbirth.** The one use found is in a remedy for the body of a woman in labour or with a child; any such condition needs a midwife or physician, and nothing here is a treatment.
- **Burning mixtures.** Smoke from burning resins and reeds can irritate the lungs and eyes; ventilate any fire.
