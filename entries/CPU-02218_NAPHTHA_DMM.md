# Naphtha — NAPHTHA (Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 1-101); Latin naptha

## *Naphtha, which Dioscorides describes as "strained Babylonian asphaltus, white in colour": a bitumen (mineral pitch) product, in modern terms a light, volatile petroleum fraction. The translator's heading gloss is "Naptha — Bitumen — Asphalt — Mineral Pitch". Identification: bitumen or petroleum naphtha; basis: the chapter's own description and the translator's gloss; confidence high for the material, none for any further grade* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY AND FIRE CAUTION.** This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation. Naphtha and bitumen are flammable and toxic, and nobody should swallow, inhale or burn them for any purpose named here.

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk, under this Greek name.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it. Another placeholder, "naphtha [Dioscorides]", was retired into this row as the same substance.

**Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter is 1-101, NAPHTHA (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 4288 to 4292; index page 100). It follows 1-99 ASPHALTOS and 1-100 PISSASPHALTOS (lines 4278 to 4285), and a running header "GUMS from TREES" is printed just before it (line 4287). The text reads: "There is also some called naptha, which is strained Babylonian asphaltus, white in colour. Some is also found which is black. It attracts fire because it draws it to itself from a distance. It is good for bathing eyes and for white spots on the cornea." The paragraph that follows, headed "Properties of Asphaltos", reads "All bitumen is able to repress inflammation, close open cuts and sores, and disperse and soften..." and runs on to list uses by smell, smoke and drink, with the remark that "Pissasphaltos can do as much as pix [organic pitch] and bitumen mixed together" (line 4292). It is printed after the naphtha text and speaks of "all bitumen"; it is therefore the shared account of the bitumen chapters and not a statement about naphtha alone. ### Active Components

General, well-established facts only. Naphtha in the modern sense is a mixture of light hydrocarbons distilled from petroleum; bitumen is a heavy, viscous mixture of hydrocarbons that occurs naturally. Both are flammable, and the vapours are harmful to breathe. Dioscorides' "strained" asphaltus matches this general picture, but the chapter gives no analysis.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Flammable and toxic.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Bathing the eyes and white spots on the cornea (the naphtha paragraph); for bitumen in general, the shared paragraph lists inflammation, cuts, sores, congested vulvae, epilepsy, coughs and asthma, snakebite, colic, dysentery, toothache and gout.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose and no preparation for naphtha itself.

The Codex gives no protocol. Nobody should put a petroleum product in the eyes or take one by mouth on the strength of this record.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives no preparation or dose for naphtha. The shared bitumen paragraph describes uses (wine and castoreum as a drink, barley water as a suppository, a plaster with barley meal, wax and saltpetre) with no quantities (line 4292). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any preparation from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Petroleum naphtha is a recognised solvent and fuel, and contact with the eye, the skin or the lungs is harmful. The ancient reports of eye and wound uses have no modern clinical standing and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** The chapter prints no hot, cold, dry or moist grade for naphtha. The shared paragraph says bitumen "repress[es] inflammation", "close[s] open cuts and sores" and can "disperse and soften"; those are actions, not a graded temperament, and they are given for bitumen as a whole.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is attested for naphtha. What the chapter does offer is a discipline of attention: it separates a white naphtha from a black one, and notes the way it "attracts fire", a test an observer can make of the material. It records no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** A petroleum product with an eye-wash use that should not be repeated. Nothing in the record supports a cultivation use, and the fire hazard alone makes it unsuitable for any practice.

### Contraindications

- **FLAMMABLE.** Naphtha ignites at a distance from a flame, as Dioscorides himself notes. Never use it near a heat source.
- **TOXIC.** Do not swallow, inhale or apply to the eyes.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Given the toxicity, avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Epilepsy, asthma, wounds.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Identity.** "Naphtha" in the modern trade names a family of products; the ancient material cannot be matched to one.
