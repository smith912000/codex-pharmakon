# Sponge stones — Lithoi spoggon ("stones found in sponges", Dioscorides)
## *Stones found inside sponges, as the translator's heading renders the Greek; the text gives no description, no mineral identification and no place of origin* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The only locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-163, *Lithoi spoggon*, in the 2000 English edition of the Dioscorides herbal (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10679; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 936; the book index sends the reader to page 824). The whole chapter is two lines. The translator's suggested heading is "Stones found in Sponges" (line 10681), and the text reads: "Stones found in sponges (taken as a drink with wine) break stones in the bladder." (line 10682). That is all the chapter says: one material, one preparation (taken as a drink with wine), one use (stones in the bladder).

The chapter sits in the translation's section headed "Metallic Stones" (line 10677), between 5-162 *Ophites lithos* (serpentine, in the translator's gloss) and 5-164 *Lithokolla*, the "glue stone" (line 10683). Those neighbours are not described here; they are different materials. Dioscorides' own chapter on the sponges themselves, 5-138 *Spoggoi* (line 10550), was read in full. It names a male and a female sponge, the hardest called *tragi*, and gives their burning, their use on wounds, ulcers and eyes, and how they are whitened in summer sun and under the moon. It does not mention stones inside a sponge, so the connection between the two chapters is not made in the text read.

**What is not said.** The text gives no colour, size or hardness for these stones, does not say what kind of sponge yields them, does not say whether they were ever found or only reported, and gives no quantity of stone or of wine. The word "sponge" in the chapter is the translation's rendering of *spoggos*; the translator adds no identification of the stone. Whether the material is a mineral concretion, a grit or sand lodged in the sponge, or something else is not stated in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated. The text does not identify the stone, so any composition would be invented. In general terms, small hard particles trapped in a marine sponge would be whatever mineral grit the sponge had taken in with the seawater; that is a general statement and not a reading of the chapter.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show that Dioscorides recorded this material for one purpose, stones in the bladder. A cultivator has nothing to apply: the identity of the material is unknown, no dose is given, and bladder stones are a medical condition that calls for diagnosis and treatment, not a home remedy. No modern protocol is supplied.

### Contraindications

- **Unidentified material:** nothing sourced says what the stone is; unidentified mineral matter should not be swallowed.
- **Bladder stones:** pain, blood in the urine, or inability to pass urine need medical assessment; a retained stone or obstruction can damage the kidney.
- **Wine as vehicle:** unsuitable in pregnancy, with alcohol-interacting medicines, or for anyone avoiding alcohol.
- **Marine sponges:** natural sponge harvesting may be regulated or protected in some places; the legal status was not retrieved. [USER: verify]
