## The Pharmacological Miracle of Two Plants

There are approximately 80,000 plant species in the Amazon basin. In this incomprehensible biodiversity, across millennia of empirical experimentation by the indigenous peoples of the region, the curanderos, vegetalistas, and shamans of the Amazon arrived at one of the most pharmacologically precise discoveries in human history: that two specific plants, combined and cooked together, produce an oral psychedelic preparation that neither plant can produce alone.

This discovery represents something the Western pharmacological tradition, despite its apparatus of mass spectrometers, receptor binding assays, and randomized clinical trials, has never independently reproduced: the empirical identification, in a tropical forest of 80,000 species, of the one clinically significant pharmacological synergy involving a MAO-A inhibitor and an orally inactive DMT source — from plants growing meters apart in the same canopy — combined in a preparation whose mechanism of action was not understood by Western pharmacology until the late 20th century.

Ayahuasca (*Banisteriopsis caapi* + *Psychotria viridis*, or functional equivalent) is not merely a psychedelic preparation. It is the only known botanical system in which two entirely unrelated plants, from different families, producing entirely different classes of chemical compounds, combine through a specific drug-drug interaction to create a pharmacological effect that neither plant possesses. It is a discovery that inverts the Western assumption that empirical pharmacological knowledge requires formal scientific instrumentation. The Amazonian shamans had the knowledge; the mechanism came later.

Ayahuasca is also, as of 2026, the **most extensively clinically studied entheogenic preparation** in the history of psychedelic medicine, with multiple published randomized controlled trials, a systematic review body surpassing that of any other plant psychedelic, and active clinical research programs in Brazil, Spain, Canada, the United Kingdom, and the United States. The scientific investigation of ayahuasca has been pioneered largely by Brazilian researchers — particularly from the Universidade de São Paulo, the Universidade Federal Fluminense, and affiliated centers — reflecting the culture and geography in which the tradition lives.

## Origin & Tradition — The Vegetalista Lineage and Amazonian Pharmacological Knowledge

Ayahuasca (Quechua: *aya* = dead/ancestors; *huasca* = vine; "vine of the dead," "vine of the ancestors") is known under dozens of names across the Amazon: *yajé* (Colombia, Ecuador), *caapi* (Brazil, derived from the vine's botanical name), *natema* (Shuar, Ecuador), *nixi pae* (Huni Kuin/Kaxinawa, Brazil/Peru), *uni* (Shipibo-Conibo, Peru), among others. The multiplicity of names reflects the multiplicity of distinct Amazonian traditions that independently developed ayahuasca use — the preparation was not diffused from a single source but appears to have been developed (or independently rediscovered) by multiple indigenous groups across the Amazon basin.

The earliest physical archaeological evidence for *B. caapi* use in ceremonial contexts dates to approximately 1,000 BCE (Peruvian tomb sites), though the oral traditions of Amazonian peoples extend the practice far earlier. Written documentation by European observers begins with the mid-19th century botanical explorations of Richard Spruce, who collected *Banisteriopsis caapi* (naming it *Banisteria caapi* Spruce) among the Tukanoan peoples of the Rio Vaupés in what is now Colombia in 1852, and described its ceremonial use in detail.

The **vegetalista tradition** of the Peruvian Amazon is the most extensively documented form of ayahuasca practice for Western audiences. A *vegetalista* (also *curandero* or *ayahuascero*) is an Amazonian healer whose training involves not merely the study of plant knowledge but the cultivation of direct relationships with plant spirits — particularly through the *dieta*, a discipline of isolation, dietary restriction, and intentional plant consumption that the vegetalista undertakes to develop a relationship with specific plants (*plantas maestras* — master plants). The *dieta* may last weeks, months, or years for different plants and different stages of training. The vegetalista's relationship with ayahuasca specifically is understood as a teaching relationship: ayahuasca is the *maestra* who reveals other plant knowledge, diagnoses illness, and guides healing.

The ceremony context involves a *curandero* presiding over a small group (traditionally 4–15 people) in nighttime ceremony. The curandero administers ayahuasca brewed from *B. caapi* vine and *P. viridis* leaves (or local equivalent admixture plants), then guides the group through the experience using *icaros* — songs of power that are the primary therapeutic instrument of the vegetalista tradition. Icaros are understood as the language of communication with plant spirits and the auditory vehicle through which healing intent is transmitted. The curandero's skill is measured in large part by the depth and quality of their icaro repertoire.

Ayahuasca use is not limited to the vegetalista lineage. The **União do Vegetal (UDV)** and **Santo Daime** are syncretic Brazilian religious movements that incorporate ayahuasca as their central sacrament. Santo Daime, founded in the Brazilian state of Acre in the 1930s by Raimundo Irineu Serra (known as Mestre Irineu), blends Amazonian indigenous practice with Christian mysticism, African religious elements, and esoteric spiritualism. The União do Vegetal, founded in 1961 by José Gabriel da Costa (Mestre Gabriel), is a more structured religious organization that uses ayahuasca (*hoasca* in UDV terminology) in biweekly communal ceremonies (*sessões*) that combine prayer, doctrine study, and group ayahuasca consumption.

Both organizations have spread globally and have been central to the legal battles that have established precedents for religious psychedelic use. The **Gonzales v. O Centro Espírita Beneficente União do Vegetal** decision (US Supreme Court, 2006) — a unanimous 8-0 ruling written by Chief Justice John Roberts — held that the US government had failed to demonstrate a "compelling governmental interest" sufficient to override the UDV's religious freedom claim under RFRA (the Religious Freedom Restoration Act) regarding their sacramental use of *hoasca* tea. This ruling established the first legally protected psychedelic religious use in the US since the NAC peyote exemption — a landmark in the legal history of psychedelic medicine that has implications far beyond ayahuasca.

## The Two-Component Pharmacological Synergy — The Intellectual Core

The pharmacological genius of ayahuasca is the drug-drug interaction between its two botanical components. Understanding this synergy requires understanding three separate facts and their convergence.

### Fact 1: DMT Is Orally Inactive

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) — the primary psychedelic compound in *Psychotria viridis* and the compound responsible for the visionary experience of ayahuasca — is a potent serotonin 5-HT2A agonist when it reaches the brain. DMT is also produced endogenously in the human body, found in trace amounts in blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid — suggesting it plays a role in normal neurological function, though its endogenous function remains a subject of active research.

Smoked or inhaled DMT (the "5-minute trip" of freebase DMT smoking) reaches the brain rapidly through pulmonary absorption and produces an intense but brief psychedelic experience lasting 5–20 minutes. Intravenous DMT produces a similarly brief experience of extraordinary intensity. But orally consumed DMT — in the form of *Psychotria viridis* leaves or DMT-containing plant preparations — is completely pharmacologically inert.

The reason: **MAO-A (monoamine oxidase A)** — an enzyme found in high concentration in the gut wall enterocytes and in the liver — degrades DMT through oxidative deamination before it can enter systemic circulation. MAO-A is the evolutionary gatekeeper that prevents dietary monoamines from entering the bloodstream (the same enzyme that, when inhibited by MAOI drugs, makes the "cheese effect" — dietary tyramine hypertensive crisis — dangerous). DMT, as a monoamine (a substituted tryptamine), is efficiently destroyed by MAO-A in the gut before any meaningful blood concentration can be achieved.

Without something to disable MAO-A, orally consumed DMT cannot reach the brain. *Psychotria viridis* consumed alone is pharmacologically inactive as a psychedelic.

### Fact 2: *Banisteriopsis caapi* Is a Potent Oral MAOI

*Banisteriopsis caapi* — the "vine of the soul," the *ayahuasca* vine proper — contains three principal β-carboline alkaloids: **harmine**, **harmaline**, and **tetrahydroharmine (THH)**. These β-carbolines are potent inhibitors of MAO-A.

Critically, the harmine and harmaline in *B. caapi* are **RIMAs — Reversible Inhibitors of MAO-A**. This distinguishes them pharmacologically from the irreversible MAOIs used in psychiatry (phenelzine, tranylcypromine), which permanently inactivate the MAO-A enzyme and require weeks of dietary restriction due to the tyramine interaction. The β-carbolines' reversible inhibition is time-limited: they occupy the MAO-A active site competitively, disabling the enzyme during the period of their plasma presence (several hours) and then clearing — allowing MAO-A function to return. This reversibility makes the tyramine risk significantly lower than with irreversible MAOIs, though dietary caution during the ceremony period is still warranted.

Consumed in the doses present in a standard ayahuasca preparation, harmine and harmaline produce sufficient MAO-A inhibition in the gut and liver to prevent first-pass degradation of simultaneously administered DMT. The vine protects the chacruna. MAO-A inhibition is the key that unlocks DMT's oral bioavailability.

### Fact 3: The Convergence — The Only Known Oral DMT System

When *B. caapi* vine (β-carboline MAO-A inhibitors) and *P. viridis* chacruna (DMT source) are consumed together — as the combined ayahuasca preparation — the β-carbolines disable the MAO-A that would otherwise destroy the DMT before it reaches the brain. DMT, protected from first-pass metabolism, enters systemic circulation, crosses the blood-brain barrier (it is lipophilic and a substrate for the tryptamine transport system), and exerts its 5-HT2A agonist effects.

The result is a profoundly intense psychedelic experience lasting 4–6 hours following oral ingestion — an experience that requires two specific plants, from different families, producing entirely different compound classes, whose interaction produces an effect neither can produce alone. This is the only known oral route to DMT pharmacological activity in the documented botanical pharmacopoeia. It is not a coincidence. It is a discovery.

The probability of this specific pharmacological synergy being identified by chance in a forest of 80,000 species — without mass spectrometry, receptor binding assays, or MAO-A inhibition assays — is effectively zero from a random-search perspective. The Amazonian shamans did not find this by chance; they found it through a systematic, empirically rigorous, multi-generational process of pharmacological investigation that operated through a different epistemic framework than Western science but arrived at equally specific and reproducible conclusions. This is among the most intellectually humbling facts in the entire Codex.

## β-Carboline Pharmacology — The Vine's Chemistry

### Harmine

**Structure and mechanism:** Harmine (7-methoxy-1-methyl-9H-pyrido[3,4-b]indole) is the primary β-carboline in *B. caapi*. It was originally isolated in the 19th century from *Peganum harmala* (Syrian rue) — another plant used in traditional Middle Eastern preparations with MAO-inhibiting effects — and was called "telepathine" by early researchers who discovered its presence in ayahuasca.

**MAO-A inhibition:** Ki ~5 nM for MAO-A (highly potent, selective MAO-A inhibitor with significant selectivity over MAO-B).

**5-HT2A partial agonism:** Harmine shows mild 5-HT2A partial agonism — a minor psychedelic dimension in its own right at high doses, contributing to the overall ayahuasca effect.

**DYRK1A Kinase Inhibition:** One of harmine's most remarkable and clinically significant properties, entirely unrelated to its MAO-A inhibition, is its role as a specific DYRK1A (Dual-specificity Tyrosine phosphorylation-Regulated Kinase 1A) inhibitor. DYRK1A is encoded on chromosome 21 and is overexpressed in Down syndrome (trisomy 21). DYRK1A inhibition has been actively investigated as a potential therapeutic approach for cognitive impairment in Down syndrome, and harmine's DYRK1A inhibitory potency has made it a research lead compound in this field.

**Human β-cell regeneration:** In a landmark 2015 study published in *Nature Medicine*, **Dhir et al. (2015)** screened a library of compounds for the ability to stimulate human β-cell (insulin-producing pancreatic islet cell) proliferation in culture and identified harmine as the only known compound, natural or synthetic, capable of stimulating meaningful human β-cell proliferation *in vitro*. This has significant implications for Type 1 diabetes research — the discovery that harmine stimulates β-cell regeneration through DYRK1A inhibition opened an entirely new therapeutic avenue for diabetes that had not previously been considered in the ayahuasca pharmacological literature.

### Harmaline

**Structure and mechanism:** Harmaline (3,4-dihydroharmine; 7-methoxy-1-methyl-3,4-dihydro-9H-pyrido[3,4-b]indole) is the dihydro-reduced form of harmine. Slightly more potent MAO-A inhibitor than harmine (Ki ~0.5 nM); also shows the tremorigenic effects (rhythmic limb tremors) that characterize high-dose β-carboline exposure in animal models and occasionally in humans at high ayahuasca doses.

Harmaline's greater CNS penetration relative to harmine makes it the primary contributor to the immediate onset of the ayahuasca experience, while harmine's longer plasma half-life sustains the MAO-A inhibitory window through the duration of the DMT absorption.

### Tetrahydroharmine (THH)

THH (1,2,3,4-tetrahydroharmine; leptaflorin) is the most reduced β-carboline in *B. caapi* and, pharmacologically, the most pharmacologically distinct from the other two. Its MAO-A inhibitory activity is relatively weak — it contributes less to DMT protection than harmine or harmaline. Its primary pharmacological distinction is as a **serotonin reuptake transporter (SERT) inhibitor** — a mechanism functionally similar to SSRI antidepressants. THH's SERT inhibition, combined with the MAO-A inhibition of harmine and harmaline, produces a sustained serotonin-potentiating effect that extends beyond the acute experience period.

This is the pharmacological mechanism behind ayahuasca's documented lasting antidepressant effects: THH's SSRI-like action persists for hours after the DMT has cleared, and the combined MAO-A inhibition + SERT inhibition produces a serotonin-enhancing environment that outlasts the acute visionary experience. The documented antidepressant effect at 7-day follow-up in clinical studies (Palhano-Fontes et al. 2019) is mechanistically coherent with THH's sustained SERT inhibition.

## DMT Pharmacology — The Chacruna's Chemistry

### 5-HT2A Agonism

N,N-DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is a potent serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonist — the mechanism shared by all classical serotonergic psychedelics. 5-HT2A agonism in prefrontal cortical pyramidal neurons produces: the dissolution of default mode network (DMN) self-referential processing (the "ego dissolution" phenomenon); massive glutamate release in cortical and limbic regions; increased thalamocortical signal transmission (allowing sensory information that is normally filtered to reach consciousness); and a pattern of brain activity characterized by increased entropy and reduced default mode network connectivity. These effects are the neurobiological basis of the psychedelic experience — the sense of expanded perception, dissolution of ordinary self-boundaries, and access to novel cognitive and emotional states.

DMT's 5-HT2A agonism is of higher intrinsic efficacy than psilocin (the active form of psilocybin, I_195) — meaning that at full receptor occupancy, DMT produces a more complete receptor activation and a correspondingly more intense response. The duration of oral DMT activity in ayahuasca (4–6 hours) contrasts with smoked DMT's extremely brief window (5–20 minutes), entirely because the β-carbolines in *B. caapi* maintain the inhibition of MAO-A for the duration of the experience, sustaining plasma DMT levels that smoked DMT's rapid pulmonary absorption and distribution cannot.

### σ1 Receptor Agonism — The Neuroprotective Dimension

DMT is a potent agonist at sigma-1 (σ1) receptors — an ancient intracellular chaperone protein located primarily at the mitochondria-associated endoplasmic reticulum membrane (MAM). **Szabo et al. (2014)** proposed that DMT functions as an endogenous σ1 receptor ligand and documented DMT's neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory, and anti-apoptotic effects in CNS cell models via the σ1 mechanism.

The σ1 receptor activation by DMT has clinical implications beyond the psychedelic effect: σ1 agonism is neuroprotective under conditions of cellular stress (hypoxia, inflammation, excitotoxicity), anti-apoptotic (prevents programmed cell death), and modulates immune function in the CNS. This may contribute to the documented neuroprotective effects observed in long-term ayahuasca users in neuroimaging studies.

### TAAR1 Modulation

DMT is an agonist at Trace Amine-Associated Receptor 1 (TAAR1) — an intracellular G-protein coupled receptor expressed in monoaminergic neurons throughout the brain that modulates dopamine and glutamate systems. TAAR1 activation by DMT adds a modulatory layer to the dopaminergic effects of the experience and may contribute to DMT's reported mood-enhancing and motivational aftereffects.

## The Afterglow Mechanism — Why the Antidepressant Effect Outlasts the Experience

One of ayahuasca's most clinically significant properties is the durability of its antidepressant effect, which persists for days to weeks following a single ceremony. This durability requires explanation, because the acute DMT effect resolves within 4–6 hours and harmine's MAO-A inhibition is reversible within the same timeframe. The lasting antidepressant effect is not a continuation of the acute pharmacological state but a product of three distinct mechanisms that operate after the acute phase:

1. **THH-mediated SERT inhibition:** As described above, tetrahydroharmine's serotonin reuptake inhibition persists for hours post-experience and may contribute to the elevated mood and reduced depressive symptoms in the immediate post-ceremony period (24–72 hours).

2. **5-HT2A receptor downregulation:** 5-HT2A agonists — including all classical psychedelics — produce rapid downregulation (desensitization) of 5-HT2A receptors in prefrontal cortical regions following acute activation. Paradoxically, this receptor downregulation appears to produce antidepressant effects that outlast the acute agonism — similar to the mechanism proposed for rapid-acting antidepressants like ketamine, where a brief period of receptor stimulation followed by compensatory adaptation produces lasting neurochemical changes.

3. **BDNF upregulation:** Ayahuasca consumption is associated with documented upregulation of BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor) — the primary neurotrophin that drives synaptic plasticity, dendritic remodeling, and hippocampal neurogenesis. **Galvão-Coelho et al. (2020)** documented significant BDNF increase in peripheral blood following ayahuasca in healthy volunteers, and BDNF upregulation has been proposed as a primary mechanism of the sustained antidepressant effect. BDNF-driven hippocampal neurogenesis takes 2–4 weeks to produce mature new neurons — consistent with the documented time course of ayahuasca's antidepressant effect peaking at 1–4 weeks post-treatment.

The convergence of these three mechanisms produces an antidepressant effect whose duration far exceeds the acute pharmacological window — and that, in clinical studies, has proven significant even when compared against active placebo controls.

## Clinical Evidence — The Human Dataset

Ayahuasca now possesses the most extensive human clinical dataset of any psychedelic preparation in the published research literature. The following studies represent the most methodologically significant entries:

### Osório et al. (2015)
**Design:** Open-label, pilot study; n=6; treatment-resistant major depressive disorder (failed ≥2 antidepressants); single oral dose of ayahuasca (1.0 mL/kg of standardized brew, equivalent to approximately 0.85 mg/kg DMT and 3.4 mg/kg harmine).
**Results:** Significant reductions in HDRS (Hamilton Depression Rating Scale) scores at both 1-day and 7-day post-treatment follow-up (p<0.01 for both timepoints). Three of six subjects showed complete remission at 7 days (HDRS score ≤7). No serious adverse events. Authors concluded that a single oral dose of ayahuasca produced rapid and sustained antidepressant effects in treatment-resistant depression.
**Significance:** First published clinical study of ayahuasca in treatment-resistant depression; established the RCT feasibility and preliminary efficacy basis for subsequent larger trials.

### Palhano-Fontes et al. (2019)
**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; n=29 (14 ayahuasca, 15 placebo); treatment-resistant unipolar major depressive disorder (failed ≥2 antidepressants); single oral dose of ayahuasca vs. a matched placebo beverage (hydrochloride solution designed to mimic ayahuasca's bitter taste and mild physiological effects); published in *Psychological Medicine*.
**Results:** Significant antidepressant effect at day 7 post-treatment in the ayahuasca group vs. placebo (MADRS score difference: −10.3 points; p=0.019). Response rate (≥50% MADRS reduction): 64% ayahuasca vs. 27% placebo (p=0.04). Remission rate (MADRS ≤7): 36% ayahuasca vs. 7% placebo (p=0.05). Effect was significantly larger in the ayahuasca group than placebo at both day 1 and day 7.
**Significance:** The first and, as of 2026, only fully placebo-controlled RCT of ayahuasca for depression. Despite the small sample size, the between-group differences were statistically significant and clinically meaningful. This trial established ayahuasca's antidepressant efficacy as distinct from placebo response in treatment-resistant patients — the most methodologically demanding population for any antidepressant.

### Jiménez-Garrido et al. (2020)
**Design:** Naturalistic, longitudinal; n=50; religious Santo Daime and União do Vegetal members (regular monthly to biweekly ayahuasca use); psychological assessments at baseline, 6-month, and 12-month follow-up using validated scales (BDI, STAI, DASS-21, WHOQOL-BREF).
**Results:** Significant reductions in depression (BDI: −6.2, p<0.001) and anxiety (STAI: −7.4, p<0.001) scores at 6 months compared to baseline; maintained at 12 months. Significant improvement in quality of life subscores. No significant adverse psychiatric events. Mindfulness and emotional regulation scores improved significantly.
**Significance:** Largest longitudinal naturalistic study of regular ayahuasca users to date; demonstrates that sustained antidepressant and anxiolytic effects are not limited to clinical single-dose contexts but are characteristic of regular ceremonial use in the natural religious context where ayahuasca has traditionally been used.

### Hamill et al. (2019)
**Design:** Naturalistic, retrospective; n=29; participants with PTSD (PTSD Checklist [PCL] score ≥38) attending ayahuasca ceremonies at a traditional Shipibo healing center in Peru; assessed before ceremony and 1 month post-ceremony.
**Results:** Significant reduction in PCL scores: mean −32.2 points (from 57.8 to 25.6; p<0.001). 23/29 participants (79%) no longer met diagnostic threshold for PTSD at 1-month follow-up. Reductions in depression (PHQ-9) and anxiety (GAD-7) were also significant.
**Significance:** While limited by naturalistic design and absence of control group, the magnitude of PTSD symptom reduction is among the largest documented for any PTSD intervention in a single-session context. The results are consistent with the hypothesis that ayahuasca's mechanism — DMT-mediated fear memory reconsolidation interference and BDNF-driven hippocampal neurogenesis — may be particularly suited to trauma-related psychiatric conditions.

## Neuroimaging — The Brain Under Ayahuasca

Multiple neuroimaging studies have characterized the brain under ayahuasca's influence, producing a neuroscience of psychedelic experience that is now one of the most productive areas of translational psychiatry:

**Default Mode Network (DMN) suppression:** Ayahuasca produces robust reductions in activity and functional connectivity within the default mode network — the intrinsic brain network associated with self-referential processing, rumination, and the "narrative self." In treatment-resistant depression, the DMN is characteristically hyperactive and hyper-connected; its suppression by ayahuasca correlates with both the acute antidepressant response and the lasting symptom reduction at 7-day follow-up (Palhano-Fontes et al. 2015, *Human Brain Mapping*).

**Increased brain entropy:** Carhart-Harris et al. and subsequent groups have characterized psychedelic states as producing increased neural complexity (entropy) — a more diverse, less constrained pattern of brain activity that may allow the brain to escape the "attractors" of rigid depressive thought patterns.

**Preserved functional connectivity in long-term users:** Neuroimaging studies of long-term Santo Daime and UDV members show structural and functional differences from matched non-user controls, including enhanced gray matter density in regions associated with emotional regulation and interoception — consistent with long-term neuroplasticity effects of repeated ayahuasca use.

## Preparation — The Curandero's Art

Traditional ayahuasca preparation is not a simple extraction procedure but a skilled art requiring knowledge, time, and — in the traditional vegetalista framework — the right intention and ceremonial context.

**Traditional Amazonian preparation:**
The *B. caapi* vine is harvested, cleaned, and macerated by pounding (maceration increases the surface area and improves alkaloid extraction). The pounded vine sections and fresh *P. viridis* leaves (or alternative DMT-containing plants — *Diplopterys cabrerana*, *Mimosa hostilis*, among others, are used in some regional traditions) are layered alternately in a large cooking vessel (traditionally a ceramic or later metal pot) with water. The mixture is slowly boiled for 4–12 hours, with periodic water addition and reduction. Multiple decoctions may be made from the same plant material and combined. The finished brew is dark brown, bitter, intensely aromatic, and astringent.

The curandero's preparation may involve singing *icaros* over the brew throughout the cooking process — a practice understood as infusing the preparation with intentional healing energy. The brew is screened and strained, then stored. Quality (alkaloid content, ratio) varies significantly with vine age, leaf quantity, and preparation technique; there is no standardization equivalent to pharmaceutical production.

**Modern clinical preparation:**
Several Brazilian and Peruvian research groups have standardized ayahuasca preparations for clinical research — maintaining the traditional two-plant combination but controlling for alkaloid content via HPLC verification. The most widely used clinical preparation in published research is the brew produced by the Beneficente Spiritist Center Uniao do Vegetal in Brazil, standardized to approximately 0.85 mg/mL DMT and 3.4 mg/mL harmine per milliliter of brew, administered at 1.0 mL/kg body weight. This standardization allows dose-response characterization that traditional ceremonies cannot provide.

**Admixture plants (regionally significant additions):**
Many regional ayahuasca traditions include additional plants beyond *B. caapi* and *P. viridis* — the *plantas maestras* (master plants) whose effects are sought or whose spirits are called. Common admixtures include: *Brugmansia* spp. (angel's trumpet — tropane alkaloids, high-risk addition), *Nicotiana rustica* (tobacco — psychoactive MAO-A synergy), *Datura* spp. (highly dangerous, rarely used in skilled practice), and various other plants without established pharmacological profiles.

## The Curandero as Pharmacologist

In the vegetalista tradition, the *curandero* is not merely a ritual facilitator but the operator of a specific therapeutic technology that includes three distinct functional elements:

**1. Pharmacological preparation:** The brew itself — the two-component MAO-A inhibitor + DMT preparation.

**2. Acoustic therapy (icaros):** The curandero's songs during the ceremony are not incidental accompaniment but the primary therapeutic tool. Research on the neurological effects of rhythmic music during psychedelic states (Kaelen et al. 2018, Turkanis et al.) has documented that music profoundly shapes the content and emotional processing of psychedelic experiences — consistent with the traditional understanding that the icaro directs the healing. Some curanderos use icaros specific to particular illnesses, spirits, or healing intentions.

**3. Diagnostic and interventional presence:** The experienced curandero observes participants throughout the ceremony, identifies those in crisis, and intervenes with specific songs, physical contact, or additional plant preparations to manage the experience. This is not passive monitoring but active therapeutic guidance — a skill that takes years of training to develop.

The western clinical equivalent of this tripartite function is: pharmacotherapy + music-assisted therapy + experienced therapist presence — which is precisely the protocol recommended by the leading psychedelic therapy research groups (MAPS, Imperial College London, Johns Hopkins) for all psychedelic-assisted therapies. The curandero tradition arrived at this combination through empirical optimization; Western clinical research confirmed its value through RCTs.

## Legal Architecture — Religious Rights and Psychedelic Medicine

**Gonzales v. O Centro Espírita Beneficente União do Vegetal (2006):** The US Supreme Court unanimously (8-0) held that the Religious Freedom Restoration Act (RFRA) protected the UDV's use of hoasca tea in religious ceremonies against the federal government's attempt to prohibit it under the Controlled Substances Act. Chief Justice Roberts' opinion found that the government had failed to show a "compelling governmental interest" sufficient to override RFRA — establishing that religious psychedelic use, when part of a sincere religious practice, carries specific First Amendment protection that may override Schedule I scheduling in the United States.

This ruling has been cited in subsequent legal challenges involving peyote, kava, cannabis, and psychedelic mushrooms, and its implications for the broader legal landscape of psychedelic medicine in the US are significant.

**Brazil (ANVISA approval, 2010):** Brazil's national health surveillance agency (ANVISA) removed ayahuasca from its list of prohibited substances in 2010, allowing religious use by UDV and Santo Daime communities. Brazil was the first country to formally legalize ayahuasca for religious use through a regulatory process.

**Peru:** Ayahuasca has been declared "national patrimony" (2008, Supreme Decree 011-2011), acknowledging the traditional use of *B. caapi* as a cultural and spiritual heritage of the Peruvian indigenous peoples. Ceremonial use by curanderos is legal and internationally recognized.

**United States (current as of 2026):** DMT remains Schedule I. Religious exemptions (under RFRA) have been granted to several churches through DEA exemption applications, following the *Gonzales* precedent. Medical use remains in the clinical trial phase; Phase 2 trials for depression and PTSD are active at multiple US sites.

## Traditional Energetic Classification

**Energetic tier — ⊕ Yuan Primary / ☿ Shen Co-Primary:**

The Codex classifies ayahuasca as the single substance with the most complete dual Yuan-Shen engagement in the entire materia medica. Where iboga (I_134) operates primarily at the Yuan level — the constitutional reset, the addiction interruption, the GDNF-mediated reward circuit repair — ayahuasca operates simultaneously at Yuan and Shen with equal depth.

The **Yuan dimension** of ayahuasca: the biographical review quality of the ayahuasca experience — the systematic encounter with one's own history, particularly its unresolved and wounded dimensions — functions as a constitutional reorganization. The experience characteristically presents the material that must be integrated and resolved for the individual's deepest constitutional pattern to shift. The Amazonian concept of ayahuasca revealing one's *purpose*, one's *illness source*, one's *life direction* — and the clinical observation that ayahuasca produces lasting changes in personality, values, and behavioral patterns — is consistent with a Yuan-level intervention in the Codex's framework.

The **Shen dimension** of ayahuasca: the visionary experience, the encounter with archetypal imagery, the emotional processing of relational and traumatic material, the access to states of love, grief, clarity, and insight — these are the Shen's domain. Unlike iboga's Shen engagement (which is often described as an ancestor encounter or biographical film), ayahuasca's Shen engagement tends to be more relational, imagistic, and nature-connected — the Amazonian world of plant spirits, serpents, and cosmic geometry that constitutes the symbolic vocabulary of the *chacruna*'s DMT.

The co-primary classification reflects the inseparability of these two dimensions in the ayahuasca experience as reported by curanderos, ceremony participants, and clinical research participants across cultures. Ayahuasca does not choose between the Yuan reset and the Shen journey — it conducts both simultaneously.

**Ayurvedic mapping:**
Ayahuasca's constitutional action corresponds most closely to a Tridoshic *rasayana* at the level of the three body tissues most relevant to the nervous system — *Asthi* (bone/structural), *Majja* (marrow/nervous system), and *Shukra* (reproductive essence/constitutional vitality). The antidepressant, anti-addictive, and neural plasticity-enhancing effects map to *Medha Rasayana* — the category of Ayurvedic preparations that specifically target the mind, memory, and intelligence.

## Preparation & Dosing

**Clinical single-dose protocol (as used in published trials):**
- 1.0 mL/kg body weight of standardized brew (~0.85 mg/kg DMT equivalent + ~3.4 mg/kg harmine equivalent)
- Administered in a supervised clinical or ceremonial setting with experienced guide present
- Minimum 6–8 hours of dedicated ceremony time; participant should not be driving or operating machinery for 12 hours post-administration
- Integration session(s) required in the days and weeks following the experience

**Traditional ceremonial protocol:**
- Dose determined by curandero based on participant assessment; typically 50–150 mL of traditional brew
- Ceremony takes place at night; duration 4–8 hours including preparation and integration period
- Preparatory *dieta* (dietary and behavioral preparation): traditionally involves avoiding pork, alcohol, aged cheeses, sexual activity, and specific foods for 3–7 days before the ceremony; reduces tyramine and serotonin-related risk during the period of MAO-A inhibition

**Frequency:**
- Traditional: ceremonies may be held weekly or biweekly in UDV/Santo Daime context; in vegetalista traditional healing, frequency is determined by the curandero based on the patient's need
- Clinical research: most trials have used single-dose or 2–3 session protocols; no established evidence base for optimum frequency in treatment-resistant depression
- Integration time between ceremonies should be respected; the material arising from a ceremony requires time — typically weeks — for psychological processing before the next ceremony is appropriate

### Contraindications

**— The Absolute List —**

The contraindication profile of ayahuasca is dominated by its pharmacological identity as a combined MAOI + serotonin agonist preparation. The following contraindications are absolute, not relative:

**SSRI and SNRI medications (Absolute Contraindication — Serotonin Syndrome Risk):**
The combination of ayahuasca's MAO-A inhibition (preventing serotonin degradation) with SSRI/SNRI medications (preventing serotonin reuptake) + DMT's direct 5-HT2A agonism produces conditions for severe serotonin syndrome — a potentially life-threatening state of serotonin excess characterized by hyperthermia, muscle rigidity, tachycardia, hypertension, altered mental status, and seizures. This is not a theoretical concern — multiple documented cases of serious adverse events following ayahuasca ingestion in SSRI-using participants are on record. Minimum washout: 2 weeks for most SSRIs/SNRIs; **5 weeks for fluoxetine** (Prozac) due to its extremely long half-life and active metabolite. This is an absolute contraindication with no exceptions.

**Irreversible MAO Inhibitor medications (Absolute Contraindication):**
Combination with irreversible MAOIs (phenelzine, tranylcypromine, isocarboxazid) — typically used for treatment-resistant depression or social anxiety — is absolutely contraindicated due to hypertensive crisis risk from tyramine accumulation and synergistic serotonin excess. Minimum washout: 2 weeks after discontinuing irreversible MAOI before any ayahuasca exposure.

**Stimulants — amphetamines, cocaine, MDMA (Absolute Contraindication):**
Combined MAO-A inhibition + stimulant = severe hypertensive crisis (tyramine-equivalent mechanism) + cardiovascular event risk. Cocaine and amphetamines independently raise serotonin, norepinephrine, and dopamine levels; in the context of MAO-A inhibition, this produces potentially life-threatening cardiovascular and neurological emergencies.

**Sympathomimetics — ephedrine, pseudoephedrine, ephedra (*I_099*), phenylephrine:**
The same tyramine interaction mechanism applies to sympathomimetics. Decongestants, cold medications, and Ephedra-containing supplements must be discontinued before any ayahuasca ceremony period.

**Tyramine-rich foods during the ceremony window:**
While the reversible nature of β-carboline MAO-A inhibition reduces (but does not eliminate) the dietary tyramine interaction risk compared to irreversible MAOIs, dietary precautions during the 24-hour window surrounding the ceremony are appropriate: avoid aged cheeses, fermented meats (salami, prosciutto), aged/fermented foods (kimchi, sauerkraut), certain wines and beers (particularly unpasteurized), overripe or fermented fruits, and soy sauce in large amounts.

**Lithium (Absolute Contraindication):**
Multiple cases of generalized tonic-clonic seizures have been reported in individuals combining lithium with ayahuasca. The mechanism likely involves lithium's effects on serotonin signaling combined with ayahuasca's serotonergic stimulation. This is an absolute contraindication.

**Antipsychotics:**
5-HT2A antagonist antipsychotics (risperidone, quetiapine, olanzapine) will block DMT's 5-HT2A agonism and abort the psychedelic experience. Beyond the pharmacological futility of the combination, unpredictable interactions with the MAO-A inhibition and dopamine system modulation make this combination clinically inadvisable.

**Personal or family history of schizophrenia or psychosis-spectrum disorders:**
5-HT2A agonists can precipitate or worsen psychotic episodes in predisposed individuals. Family history of schizophrenia, or personal history of psychotic episodes, paranoid ideation, or borderline personality disorder with psychotic features, requires specialist psychiatric evaluation and constitutes a relative-to-absolute contraindication depending on severity.

**Cardiac conditions:**
While ayahuasca does not produce the direct hERG channel blockade of ibogaine (I_134), the sympathomimetic component of the experience (cardiovascular activation, blood pressure elevation, tachycardia during the peak phase) contraindicates use in anyone with unstable angina, recent myocardial infarction, severe hypertension, or significant arrhythmia without medical clearance.

**Pregnancy:**
Absolutely contraindicated. Uteroactive potential; potential fetal harm from cardiovascular fluctuations and psychophysiological stress of the experience; no safety data exists for prenatal ayahuasca exposure.

**Epilepsy / seizure disorders:**
The serotonergic stimulation and potential hyperthermia component of high-dose ayahuasca increases seizure risk in predisposed individuals. Contraindicated in active epilepsy without neurology consultation.
