# Phoenix sylvestris — Kharjura; Pinda-kharjura (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Phoenix sylvestris, the first of two Latin forms that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints on one line (OCR "Phoenix  sylvestris.     Dactylifera.", line 32040) against the Sanskrit name Kharjura (OCR "Eharjura", line 31964). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting from the end of each column; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print Kharjura and gloss it in English as the date palm; they give no Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed, so the botanical identity is open: the chapters say "date palm" and the appendix line prints two forms.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 509 gives the name as "Eharjura", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31964; id line 32040)" and the Latin as "Phoenix  sylvestris.     Dactylifera.", with the note that the pairing was proposed by a Sonnet agent from the two OCR columns and checked by script. The Latin line carries two forms: "Phoenix sylvestris." and "Dactylifera.". The batch key is the first. The Codex reads the second as the species epithet *dactylifera* with the genus understood from the first, which is a reading and not something the line states. The OCR reads the initial K of this page as E throughout ("Ehadira" for Khadira, line 31962; "Eetakl" for Ketaki, line 31960), so "Eharjura" is read as Kharjura.

**The first pairing, Kharjura, checked by counting.** The page (page number "71" at line 31901) prints its Sanskrit names at lines 31904 to 31986 and its Latin names at lines 31992 to 32052. Counted from the end, Kharjura is the eleventh Sanskrit entry from the last (Kharjura, Kimsuka, Kirata with Kirata-tikta bracketed, Kodrava, Kokilaksha, Kovidara, Koranta, Koshataki, Kramuka, Krishna, Krishna-jiraka), and the "Phoenix sylvestris" line is the eleventh Latin entry from the last (that line, "Butea frondosa", "Swertia Chirata", "Paspalum Scrobiculatum", "Hygrophila Spinosa", "Bauhinia Variegata", "Barleria Prionitis", "Luffa Amara", "Areca Catechu", "See Pippali", "Nigella Sativa"). The neighbours agree: Khadira with "Accacia Catechu" (line 32038), Kimsuka with "Butea frondosa" (32041), Kirata with "Swertia Chirata" (32043), Kodrava with "Paspalum Scrobiculatum" (32045), and Ketaki with "Pandanus Odoratissimus" (32036). The front of the page does not match as printed (39 Sanskrit entries against 37 Latin), because Katuka, Katuki and Katu-Rohini (lines 31952 to 31956) stand against the single line "Picrorrhiza Kurroa ; Helleborus Niger" (32032), which is the Codex's reading of an OCR with no bracket. **A second appendix name for a neighbouring Latin, Pinda-kharjura.** The appendix prints "Phoenix  Dactylifera." a second time (line 32577), which `names.csv` row 735 keeps as an unpaired Latin line. On the page of the P names that begins Palindi (line 32479), the 21st Sanskrit entry is Pinda-kharjura (OCR "Pinda-kbarjura", line 32517) and the 21st Latin entry is "Phoenix  Dactylifera." Counted from either end, Pinda-kharjura stands against it again: both columns hold thirty-six entries once the OCR double lines are read as single entries (Paribhadra with Patrijata, Parpata with Parpataka, Parusha with Parushaka, the two Pashana-bheda lines and Pattanga with Patranga), Pindailu stands against "Dioscorea globosa" (32576) above it, Pinditaka against "Randia Dumeterum" (32578) below it, and Pundarika against "Nelumbium speciosum" (32592) at the end of the column. Pinda-kharjura is therefore the appendix's name for *Phoenix dactylifera* alone and Kharjura its name for the line that prints both forms. Neither name is in the batch key beyond Kharjura, and the chapters print Pinda-kharjura nowhere. The pairing is positional. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Kharjura and its OCR variants (the OCR prints "Kharfura" once, Sutra 46 line 2175), for Pinda-kharjura, and for the English words "date" and "date-palm". The name is the translator's "date palm" in four places (Sutra 45 line 1391, Uttara 39 line 1071, Chikitsa 8 line 172 and Uttara 36 line 20); the Latin binomial never appears in a chapter.

*Sutra ch.42 (the specific properties of flavours).* `SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` lines 289 to 298: Kharjura is named between Draksha and Rajadana in the list of "the drugs forming the groups known as the Kakolyadi-Gana, as well as thickened milk, ghee, lard, marrow, ..." which "are generally included within the Madhura group".

*Sutra ch.46 (food and drink), the fruit.* `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1217 to 1232: "The fruits such as the Drakshas, Kashmaryas, Madhukas, Kharjuras, etc., have a sweet taste, are heavy of digestion and prove curative in cases of haemoptysis." and then, singly (lines 1228 to 1232): "Kharjuras are possessed of the virtue of arresting all bodily wastes. They prove beneficial in Urakshata, are pleasant, cooling in their potency, refreshing, heavy of digestion, sweet in taste, and prove curative in cases of haemoptysis."

*Sutra ch.46, the top-pith.* Lines 2014 to 2019: "The top-piths of such trees, as the Tala, Narikela, Kharjura etc., are sweet in taste and digestion. They prove curative in cases of haemoptysis, and are spermatopoietic. They subdue the Vayu and generate the Kapham in the body."

*Sutra ch.46, the best of fruits.* Lines 2166 to 2178: "Of fruits, those called Dadimva, Amalaka, Draksha, Kharfura, Parushaka, Rajadana and Matulunga should be considered as the most commendable and efficacious." (The words in the OCR are broken across a page header; spellings are normalised here.)

*Sutra ch.46, wines and flesh.* Line 2842: "Cocoanut and date palm wines should be used after having eaten the flesh of the cave-dwelling (guha-shaya) species." The wine is the preparation named here; the text gives no further word on the tree.

*Sutra ch.45 (liquid substances).* `SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 1391 to 1402: "The wine prepared from the juice of the date palm (Kharjuram) possesses properties, which are slightly different from those of the preceding kind. It tends to enrage the bodily Vayu, is clear, and imparts a relish to one's food, and reduces fat and Kapham. It is light, has a sweet and astringent taste, is pleasing and aromatic and increases the agility of the limbs and organs."

*Chikitsa ch.8 (fistula-in-ano).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch08.txt` lines 171 to 173: the incisions "should be made in the shape or form of a Kharjura-patra (leaf of the date-palm), Ardha-chandra (half-moon), Chandra-chakra (moon's disc), Suchi-mukha (needle's mouth), or Avammukha". Here the date-palm leaf is only a shape.

*Chikitsa ch.11 (urinary diseases, Prameha).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch11.txt` line 152: "a decoction of Guduchi, seeds of Tinduka, Kas'marya and Kharjura, mixed with honey, in a case of Sonita-meha."

*Chikitsa ch.17 (erysipelas, sinus and diseases of the mammary glands; the chapter file lacks its heading, which is printed in `Bhishagratna_Vol2_djvu.txt` line 1574).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt` lines 211 to 218: "Oil cooked with the decoction of the tender fruits of the Kumbhika, Kharjura, Kapittha, Vilva and the Vanaspatis ... and with the Kalkas of Musta, Sarala, Priyangu, Sugandhika, Mocharasa, Ahi-pushpa (Nagesvara), Rodhra and Dhataki flowers leads to a speedy healing up (granulation) of ulcers (Vrana) and traumatic sinuses."

*Uttara ch.36 (Naigamesha).* `SS_Uttara_ch36.txt` lines 17 to 20: medicated ghritas "should be duly prepared with the drugs of the Madhura group as Kalka, with the decoction of the Dasamula and with milk or with the head of a date-palm tree". The text does not say what the "head" is. [USER: verify]

*Uttara ch.39 (fever).* `SS_Uttara_ch39.txt` lines 1068 to 1074: "Pastes of ... Dadima mixed with sugar, Draksha and Kharjura (date) as well as gargles prepared from these drugs should be retained in the mouth with a view to removing its bad taste."

*Uttara ch.47 (alcoholism).* `SS_Uttara_ch47.txt` lines 276 to 279: "A compound consisting of Kharjura, Vetra, Karira, Parushaka, Draksha and Trivrit pounded together and dissolved in cold water should be taken, sweetened with sugar", for the sequel named Pana-vibhrama.

*Uttara ch.50 (hiccough).* `SS_Uttara_ch50.txt` lines 171 to 178: among four liquid compounds, "the kernel (inner pulp) of Kharjura and Pippali" mixed with honey.

*Uttara ch.52 (cough).* `SS_Uttara_ch52.txt` lines 273 to 276: "the compound prepared with Kharjura, Bhargi, Pippali, Piyala, Madhulika, Ela and Amalaka mixed in equal parts, and taken with a copious quantity of clarified butter, honey and sugar", for Pittaja, Kshataja and Kshayaja Kasa.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Kharjura, Kharjuri and the date palm found nothing.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Phoenix* is a genus of palms (Arecaceae), and the ripe fruits of the date palm are sweet and rich in sugars. That is a statement about the genus in general and not a finding of the Sushruta text, and nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the name the chapters print, the plant (if it is this one) is a sweet fruit with a stated cooling potency (Sutra 46), a tree whose juice is made into a wine (Sutra 45 and 46), the kernel and fruit named in several compounds for urinary, ulcer, alcohol-sequel, hiccough and cough complaints, and a leaf whose shape is a pattern for an incision (Chikitsa 8). The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations named are: the wine of the date-palm juice (Sutra 45); a decoction with Guduchi, Tinduka seeds and Kasmarya, with honey (Chikitsa 11); an oil cooked with the decoction of the tender fruits (Chikitsa 17); a paste with Dadima, sugar and Draksha, held in the mouth (Uttara 39); a compound pounded and dissolved in cold water with sugar (Uttara 47); the kernel with Pippali, with honey (Uttara 50); and an equal-parts compound taken with ghee, honey and sugar (Uttara 52). No quantity is given for Kharjura in any of these. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** Sutra 46 prints for the fruit "sweet in taste", "heavy of digestion" and "cooling in their potency", with "curative in cases of haemoptysis" (lines 1217 to 1232); for the top-pith "sweet in taste and digestion", "spermatopoietic", subduing the *Vayu* and generating the *Kapham* (lines 2014 to 2019). Sutra 42 places Kharjura in the *Madhura* group. For the wine of its juice Sutra 45 prints that it "tends to enrage the bodily Vayu", "reduces fat and Kapham" and has "a sweet and astringent taste" (lines 1391 to 1402). The text prints no *vipaka* by name for the fruit.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Kharjura in at least eleven chapters: as a member of the *Madhura* group, as a sweet fruit and one of the best fruits, as a tree with a spermatopoietic top-pith, as a wine, and as an ingredient in compounds for named illnesses. The text calls the top-pith spermatopoietic (Sutra 46), which is a property recorded in a food chapter and not a place in the vajikarana chapter. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The plant therefore carries no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status in the text as retrieved.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing beyond what the text calls food and remedy. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with Kharjura; it is a sweet fruit, a wine and an ingredient. A date is food, and nothing here makes it more.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints, for the wine, that it "tends to enrage the bodily Vayu" (Sutra 45). The identity is open between two forms, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Alcohol carries its own hazards, and the text devotes a chapter (Uttara 47) to its sequels. Haemoptysis, persistent cough and hiccough need medical care. No conservation information was read.
