# Lead white — 粉锡 *Fen Xi* ("powder tin"; "Galenitum Praeparatum", Yang 1998), with 锡镜鼻 *Xi Jing Bi* ("tin mirror knob"); alias 解锡 *Jie Xi*

## *Lead white, basic lead carbonate 2PbCO3·Pb(OH)2, the identification we give to 粉锡; of high confidence in the standard materia medica, but not stated by the Shennong text itself: Sun's note quotes the Shuowen that 锡 is a metal "between silver and lead", and Yang's translation reads "Galenitum Praeparatum" (prepared galena, lead sulphide), which differs. A lead compound and a cumulative poison: historical record only, plainly toxic* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** *Fen xi* is a monograph of the Divine Farmer's Classic. **Toxic substance: historical record only.** Lead white is a lead compound, and nothing in this entry is a recommendation. The Codex already has the combined entry II_101 (Lead), which treats lead and its oxides from several traditions and cites Yang's "Fen Xi (Galenitum Praeparatum)" at line 1321, and the entry II_70 (Tin-copper compound), which treats Yang's separate *Xi Tong Jing Bi*. This entry is the separate monograph of 粉锡 from Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction and does not repeat those entries.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the lower (下) classic, reads 味辛，寒 ("acrid, cold"). It governs 伏尸毒螫 ("hidden-corpse [illness] and stinging poison") and "kills the three worms" (杀三虫). It gives the alias **一名解锡** (*jie xi*, "dissolved tin"). It then adds a second item inside the same paragraph: **锡镜鼻** (a tin mirror's knob, or boss) "governs women's blood block, concretions and masses, [things] hidden in the bowel, cutting off pregnancy" (主女子血闭，症瘕，伏肠，绝孕). The habitat is 生山谷 ("mountain valleys"). A bracket in Sun's text says that the old text had two items here and that he has joined them (旧作二种，今并). **There is no 久服 ("taken long") clause.** (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 粉锡 *fen xi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 2975 and 2977)

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 gives only the place 生桂阳 ("Guiyang") (line 2979). No other layer prints a description of the substance.

**Sun's note (案).** The only gloss is the *Shuowen*: 锡，银、铅之间也, "*xi* is [a metal] between silver and lead" (line 2981). Sun's note at the end of the lower-class jade-and-stone list (line 3035) records the same decision: the old count was twelve, "*fen xi* and *xi jing bi* being two" (粉锡、锡镜鼻为二), and Sun has made the class nine items by joining them (and by joining three salts into one). The aliases 铅粉 *qian fen* and 胡粉 *hu fen*, by which the substance is known in later materia medica, are not printed in the Shennong text or in the layers read here. [USER: verify]

**Yang 1998 (second witness).** Yang prints "Fen Xi (Galenitum Praeparatum)" under "Jades and Stones: Inferior Class" (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 1321): "acrid and cold. It mainly treats hidden corpse and toxic [insect] bites. It kills the three [kinds of] worms." His note 71 describes "hidden corpse" as a disease with spells of pricking pain in the heart and abdomen, with swelling, distention and panting, which "returns to normal" when the spell passes, and which was thought to be caused by a hidden evil lying deep in the five viscera (lines 1337-1345). Yang prints the second item separately, as "Xi Tong ling Bi (Compound of Tin & Copper)" (line 1350): "mainly treats females' blood block, concretions and conglomerations lying in the intestines, and infertility", produced "in the mountains and valleys of Gui Yang" (lines 1350-1355). Sun's text joins the two and Yang does not.

**Identity.** The Shennong text names *xi*, a metal that the ancient word did not clearly distinguish from lead (the *Shuowen* places it "between silver and lead", and Yang's note in the Tin entry II_102 says tin was often confused with lead). "Powder *xi*" is the white powder made from the metal. The standard Chinese materia medica reads 粉锡 as *qian fen*, lead white (basic lead carbonate), a white pigment and cosmetic powder, and the Codex follows that for the entry's name; a tin-oxide reading was rejected. Yang's Latin, "prepared galena", would be a lead sulphide and does not agree with it. The Shennong text itself does not decide between them. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Lead white is basic lead carbonate, 2PbCO3·Pb(OH)2, a heavy white powder that has been used for centuries as a paint pigment and a face powder. Lead and its compounds are cumulative poisons that affect the nervous system, blood, kidneys and the developing brain; this is well established. A mirror-knob of tin or a leaded bronze would be a metal or alloy, not a powder, and nothing read states its composition. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice, and not to be taken. The classic records lead white for hidden-corpse illness, for poisonous stings, to kill "the three worms", and the mirror-knob for women's blocked blood, masses and infertility. The Codex records these as claims of the classic and nothing more. A lead compound taken in the body causes lead poisoning, whatever the classic claims for parasites. The modern record of white-lead cosmetics, which poisoned the people who used them, is a plain warning about the substance.

### Scientific Correlation

Lead white was the main white pigment of European and Asian painting and a cosmetic powder until its toxicity was recognised; its use in paints, cosmetics and medicines is banned or restricted in many places. Lead exposure causes anaemia, neurological damage and kidney damage, and no safe level of exposure is known for children. Lead does not act as a vermifuge in the body in any sense that is safe. The classic's claims for worms and hidden corpse have no modern support. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph stands in the lower classic. The preface says lower drugs are "assistants" that "treat disease to correspond with the earth", are mostly toxic, may not be taken for long, and are for those who want to remove cold-and-heat evil qi, break accumulations and cure illness (line 2935). The paragraph has a flavour, a nature, indications and an alias, and **no 久服 clause**. It therefore carries only the lower class's therapeutic frame and no cultivation status. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 粉锡 *fen xi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 2935 and 2975)

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug in the Chinese scheme, to be taken for a defined complaint and for a short course, if at all, and in this case never: a lead compound has no place in a practice that aims to keep a person well. The classic's own grading is the warning. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Do not ingest or apply.** Lead white is a poison. Do not swallow it, put it on the skin, on wounds, or on the face as a cosmetic.
- **Children and pregnancy.** Lead harms the developing brain and fetus; avoid all exposure.
- **Dust and pigment.** Powdered lead pigment can be inhaled or carried on the hands; antique pigments and old paint containing lead white are a hazard.
- **Infertility and blocked blood.** The mirror-knob's uses are historical claims; these conditions need medical care. In fact lead exposure is itself a cause of reproductive harm.
- **Poisoning.** If a lead compound has been swallowed, seek urgent poison-control or emergency care.
