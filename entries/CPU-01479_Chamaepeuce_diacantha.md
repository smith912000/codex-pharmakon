# Chamaepeuce diacantha — Chamaipeuke (Dioscorides 4-127)

## *Chamaepeuce diacantha (the translators' suggestion; "Fishbone Thistle")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) printed on the chapter's SUGGESTED line, not Dioscorides' own identification. The row was added from that line (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), heading *4-127. CHAMAIPEUKE* at line 9159, SUGGESTED line at 9160 ("Chamaepeuce diacantha — Fishbone Thistle"), text at line 9161.

The whole chapter reads: "Chamaepeuce (pounded into small pieces and taken as a drink in water) is good for disorders of the loins [digestive or procreative]. It is a herb that is all green, with little crumpled leaves and branches, and flowers similar to roses."

That is all the text says. It gives one use (a drink of the pounded plant in water for "disorders of the loins", where the bracketed gloss "[digestive or procreative]" is the translators' own, not Dioscorides'), and a very brief description: the herb is green throughout, with little crumpled leaves, branches, and flowers likened to roses. It prints no habitat, no season, no part used beyond "the plant", no alternative names and no dose. The neighbours are 4-126 Chamaikissos (heading at line 9151) and 4-128 Bouglosson (line 9162), both different plants. No existing entry on the spine covers this chapter; searches of Volume I for the chapter number, the Greek name and the English gloss found none.

The translators call the plant a thistle, but the text read describes crumpled leaves and rose-like flowers and mentions no spines. Whether the description fits the suggested species is not checked here. This is an identity doubt. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The constituents of the plant Dioscorides describes were not read. *Chamaepeuce* is placed by the translators among the thistles, a group of the daisy family (Asteraceae), but no composition of this plant is given in any source retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records a pounded herb drunk in water for disorders of the loins. This is the text's single claim. The Codex gives no protocol for it: the identification is the translators' and uncertain, and the text gives nothing to prepare from.

### Preparation & Dosing

4-127 gives the plant "pounded into small pieces and taken as a drink in water". No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No study of the plant was opened, and "disorders of the loins" is too loosely defined to match a modern condition. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter is two sentences: one use and one short description. It offers a cultivator only the discipline of attention that the cultivation spec describes for this tradition, how to know the herb by its green crumpled leaves and rose-like flowers, and it assigns the plant no status. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. With so brief a description and no dose, the honest answer is to do nothing with it.

### Contraindications

Nothing in the chapter warns of harm, and it gives no information on safety. An unidentified wild plant should not be drunk on the strength of a two-sentence description. None of the traditional uses above is advice. [USER: verify]
