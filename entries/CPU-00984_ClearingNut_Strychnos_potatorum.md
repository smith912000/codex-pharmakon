### Origin & Tradition

*Strychnos potatorum* is a moderate-sized deciduous tree of the Loganiaceae, native to the drier deciduous forests of peninsular India, Sri Lanka, Myanmar and parts of tropical Africa. Its dried ripe seeds — smooth, disc-like, greyish — are the article of trade, known across South Asia as **Nirmali**, **Kataka / Kattaka** (Sanskrit), **Chilla ginjalu** / **cleaning nut** / **clearing nut**. The species epithet *potatorum* means "of drinkers" — literally the drinkers' tree — a direct botanical fossil of its single most famous use: making foul water fit to drink.

**SS — Sushruta / Ayurveda.** The seed (*Kataka*) is one of the classical Ayurvedic drugs named specifically for **water purification**: the seed is rubbed against the wetted inner wall of an earthen pot, or a powdered pinch is stirred into turbid water, whereupon suspended silt and colloidal clay flocculate and settle, leaving the water visibly clear. This is the archetypal *kataka* action in the Sanskrit materia medica. Ayurveda also lists internal/medicinal uses of the seed — for the eyes (netya), in *prameha* (the diabetes/polyuria complex), in diarrhoea and dysentery, and in urinary complaints. **[USER: verify]** the precise Sushruta Saṃhitā chapter/verse before citing a specific locus; the *water-clarifier* identity and the broad indication list are well attested in the Ayurvedic stream, but the exact verse reference is not substantiated in this research run.

**CM — Avicenna / Unani.** The clearing nut entered the wider Greco-Arabic-influenced South Asian pharmacy and is carried in the Unani materia medica among cooling, cleansing seeds. **[USER: verify]** any specific *Canon of Medicine* passage — the Unani usage is real in the later composite tradition, but a direct Avicennan locus is **not** confirmed in this run and should not be printed as a quotation without checking.

The through-line across both named streams is coherent and unusually concrete: this is the **drinkers' seed** — a household water-clarifying agent first, a cooling medicinal seed second.

### ⚠️ Crucial Species Distinction — NOT the strychnine tree

The genus name *Strychnos* rightly alarms: it contains ***Strychnos nux-vomica*** (CPU-00266 in this codex), the strychnine tree, whose seeds are a rich source of the convulsant poisons **strychnine and brucine** and are lethal in small quantity. **A cultivator must not conflate the two.** The retrieved chemistry of *S. potatorum* seed centres on **coagulant seed proteins, a galactomannan-type gum/polysaccharide, and phenolics/flavonoids** — the constituents behind its water-clarifying and adsorbent behaviour — **not** on the indole convulsant alkaloids that define *nux-vomica*. *S. potatorum* is used, in tradition and in the modern water-treatment literature, as a **safe household coagulant**; *nux-vomica* is a violent poison. The two share a genus and nothing of their safety profile.

**Honesty caveat on the alkaloid claim:** in this research run I did not retrieve a direct quantitative assay stating "*S. potatorum* seed contains zero strychnine." The distinction rests on (a) the documented, characterised chemistry of the *potatorum* seed being coagulant-protein / polysaccharide / phenolic in nature, and (b) its long, unbroken use as a drinking-water aid with no toxidrome resembling strychnine poisoning. Treat "not a significant strychnine/brucine source" as **strongly supported by usage and characterised chemistry**, and flag exact per-seed alkaloid figures as **[USER: verify]** if a number is ever needed. Never substitute or co-store *potatorum* and *nux-vomica* seeds — misidentification is the single largest real hazard here (see Contraindications).

### Active Components

- **Coagulant seed proteins (polyelectrolytes).** Biochemically characterised cationic/anionic seed proteins are the active flocculating agents; isolated fractions bind and remove suspended colloids and even metal ions (e.g. Cd²⁺) from water via a polyelectrolyte "sweep/adsorption" mechanism.
- **Galactomannan / polysaccharide gum.** A natural seed polysaccharide contributes to the mucilaginous, capping/adsorbent behaviour (it has even been used as a green reducing/stabilising agent to synthesise metal nanoparticles).
- **Phenolics & flavonoids.** Measurable total phenolic and flavonoid content (e.g. ~30 mg GAE/g and ~11 mg rutin-eq/g dry weight in an optimised deep-eutectic-solvent extract), carrying the seed's in-vitro antioxidant and enzyme-inhibitory activity.
- **NOT the defining constituent:** the convulsant alkaloids strychnine/brucine that characterise *S. nux-vomica* — see the species-distinction section.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica, *Strychnos potatorum* is a **Foundation-phase "functional seed" signature** — the exemplar of a plant whose primary documented action is *physical/colloidal* rather than pharmacological:

- **Water clarification (headline use):** a traditional and modern-verified **natural coagulant** for removing turbidity (and, in research settings, some metal/dye load) from muddy water. This is the honest, evidence-backed core of the entry.
- **Cooling medicinal seed (traditional):** eye applications, *prameha*/diabetes-complex, diarrhoea/dysentery, urinary complaints — all **traditional-use** grade, with only preclinical mechanistic hints (below) and no species-specific clinical trials retrieved.

The teaching value is the discipline it forces: separating a *real, physical* action (coagulation) from a *claimed, untested* therapeutic one, and separating a plant from its dangerous genus-mate.

### Scientific Correlation

- **Water clarification / coagulation of turbidity: SUPPORTED (mechanism characterised).** Graded *documented traditional use with a verified physicochemical mechanism*. Isolated *S. potatorum* seed proteins act as coagulants and adsorb suspended colloids and metal ions (Langmuir-type, pseudo-second-order kinetics). This is the strongest claim in the entry. **Critical limit:** coagulation clears *turbidity*, it does **not** kill pathogens — clarified water still requires microbiological disinfection (boiling/filtration/chlorination) before it is safe to drink.
- **Metal/dye adsorption (water remediation): PRECLINICAL / laboratory.** Seeds adsorb Cd(II) and anionic azo dyes from aqueous solution in bench studies — environmental-remediation potential, not a health claim.
- **Antioxidant + α-amylase inhibition (antidiabetic mechanism): PRECLINICAL (in-vitro), single-extract.** A 2024 extraction-optimisation study measured DPPH antioxidant activity (~26%) and α-amylase inhibition (~47%) in an optimised seed extract. Enzyme-assay only — a *mechanistic hint* toward the traditional *prameha* use, **not** clinical antidiabetic evidence.
- **Antihyperglycaemic effect in diabetic rats: PRECLINICAL, and CONFOUNDED (multi-herb formula).** The one animal antihyperglycaemic study using *S. potatorum* did so inside an **eight-herb** Ayurvedic formulation ("Ilogen-Excel"), so no effect can be attributed to *S. potatorum* alone. Graded **insufficient for a species-specific claim.**
- **Eye / diarrhoea / urinary medicinal uses: TRADITIONAL, clinically UNPROVEN.** No species-specific human trials retrieved.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

In the Ayurvedic (SS) stream the seed is read as **cooling and cleansing**, with a sweet–bitter–astringent (*madhura–tikta–kaṣāya*) taste attribution, pacifying *pitta* and *kapha* and used to "clear" — a signature that runs from the literal clearing of water to the figurative clearing of the eyes and urine. In the Unani (CM) stream it sits among the cooling, cleansing seeds. These are **traditional attributions**, not laboratory-derived energetics; the physical coagulant action is the one part of this profile with a modern mechanistic correlate.

### Contraindications

- **Misidentification is the primary hazard.** Do not confuse or co-store with *Strychnos nux-vomica* (CPU-00266), whose seeds are strychnine/brucine-rich and potentially fatal. Source *potatorum* seed from a reliable supplier and verify identity.
- **Clarified ≠ safe.** Water treated with clearing nut is de-turbified, **not disinfected**. It can still carry bacteria, viruses and protozoa — always disinfect (boil/filter/chlorinate) clarified water before drinking.
- **Internal medicinal use is preclinical-only.** No species-specific clinical safety or dosing data were retrieved; internal doses are **[USER: verify]**. Do not present the traditional eye/diabetes/urinary uses as proven therapies.
- **Eye preparations:** risk of contamination/irritation; not to be used without qualified supervision.
- **Pregnancy/lactation, paediatric, renal/hepatic impairment:** no species-specific safety data retrieved — default to caution and treat as **[USER: verify]**.
- **Diabetes:** the antidiabetic evidence is in-vitro and/or confounded by multi-herb formulation; it must not replace glycaemic monitoring or prescribed therapy.
