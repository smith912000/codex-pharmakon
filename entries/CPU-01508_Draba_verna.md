# Draba verna — Draba (Dioscorides 2-187)

## *Draba verna (the translators' suggestion, from Linnaeus on the SUGGESTED line; "Common Europhila", with Draba olympica, D. arabis and D. nemoralis, "Witlow Grass", given as another reading)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) printed under the chapter heading, not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED block reads "Draba verna [Linnaeus], Europhila vulgaris — Common Europhila" and a second line adds "Draba olympica, Draba arabis, Draba nemoralis — Witlow Grass". The row takes the first Linnaean binomial. It has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *2-187. DRABA* at line 6162, SUGGESTED lines at 6163 and 6164, text at line 6165. The chapter stands in Book Two. The translators' name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 377. The next chapter, 2-188 *ERUSIMON* (line 6169), is a different plant.

The whole chapter reads: "Draba, a herb of about a foot high, has slender sprigs with leaves on both sides like lepidium, yet more tender and paler, and a tuft on the top with white flowers similar to elder. This herb is boiled with barley water (especially in Cappadocia) and the dry seed is mixed with sauces instead of pepper."

So the chapter is one description and one use, and both are about food. The herb stands about a foot high. It has slender sprigs, with leaves on both sides that resemble *lepidium* but are more tender and paler, and a tuft at the top of white flowers like elder. It is "boiled with barley water (especially in Cappadocia)", and the dry seed "is mixed with sauces instead of pepper". The chapter prints no other name, no habitat, no season of gathering and no medicinal indication. The comparison plant, *lepidium*, is not explained in the chapter, and its own chapter was not read for this entry. The translation's page marker "315" and the running head "HERBS WITH A SHARP QUALITY" follow at lines 6166 and 6168. The running head is the translation's section label, and whether Dioscorides himself grouped the plant by sharpness of taste is not stated in the chapter. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The constituents of the plant Dioscorides describes were not read. The translators' *Draba* belongs to the mustard family (Brassicaceae), a family whose members commonly carry sulphur-containing glucosinolates that give a pungent, peppery taste, which fits a seed used in place of pepper. Nothing is stated here about this particular plant. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The only uses Dioscorides gives are culinary: the herb boiled with barley water, and the dry seed as a pepper substitute in sauces. The Codex gives no protocol, and an uncertain identification (a small white-flowered herb that the translators place in one of several species) is no basis for foraging.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives two preparations: the herb "boiled with barley water (especially in Cappadocia)", and the dry seed "mixed with sauces instead of pepper". No quantity, frequency or duration is given for either. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
