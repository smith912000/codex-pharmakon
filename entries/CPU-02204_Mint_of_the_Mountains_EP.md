### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Bryan's *The Papyrus Ebers* among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the extraction of Bryan's plant list (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 71: "Mint-of-the-Mountains", locus "Bryan 1930, Ch. VI Plant Remedies (line 1305)", class guess plant, note "Bryan's English identification"). The extract's own README warns that "no Egyptian word and no binomial is given in these lists" and that the identifications are "Bryan's 1930 English renderings only" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/README.md`).

**Ebers (EP).** The name is printed as one head word in Bryan's alphabetical list of plant remedies, in his Chapter VI, "PLANT REMEDIES", in the run "Lettuce, Wild Lettuce; Linseed, Linseed-water; Mint-of-the-Mountains; Splinters-of-the-Mulberry-tree" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1305). The chapter's opening paragraph (line 1233) says that many substances have an identity "completely hidden from us under the guise of meaningless words", that this "applies equally to those substances which were drawn from the Plant world", and that "of the recognizable Plants, and of their preparations, there is a not inconsiderable showing as the following list will reveal". The list is therefore of plants Bryan thought recognisable. No other form of the name is printed in that group, so the entry has a single head word and no listed variants.

Bryan's own prose uses the name three more times, all in his general chapters on the remedies, and each time as a component of a prescription. These are Bryan's English paraphrases and not translations of a line of the papyrus, and the Egyptian text itself is not on disk.

- *Protection* (line 1381, in Bryan's survey of curious prescriptions): "a mixture of Scrapings-of-a-Statue and Mint-of-the-Mountains, cooked in Oil and Wax, to be taken for four days to 'protect against everything.'" The quotation marks around "protect against everything" are Bryan's.
- *A headache poultice* (line 2112, in his chapter on "minor medicine", the passage on headaches): "Mint-of-the-Mountains dissolved in Hippopotamus Oil along with Copper and Lead-vitriol". Bryan introduces headache remedies as "repulsive messes, their sole redeeming feature in our eyes being that they were not to be taken internally but rubbed in externally" (line 2093).
- *The same protective mixture, again* (lines 2200 to 2206, printed page 66): the scribe "rounds off the remedies with one by which the limbs 'could be protected against everything.' After mixing the Scrapings-from-a-Statue with Mint-of-the-Mountains and adding sasa-pieces, Oil, and Wax, one cooked the mixture, strained it (a not unnecessary precaution), and swallowed it for four days."

The "Scrapings-from-a-Statue" appear in Bryan's list of mineral remedies (line 1204), which is a separate row family in the Codex.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** In Bryan's paraphrase: as one of the components of a mixture swallowed for four days "to protect against everything" (the limbs, in his second account), and of a headache poultice.
- **What the text does not say.** No amount, no part of the plant, no preparation of the plant alone, and no account of what the plant was believed to do inside either mixture.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant. One of the mixtures also contains copper, lead-vitriol or other mineral substances, which are poisonous, and should not be swallowed or applied.

### Preparation & Dosing

Bryan gives the protective mixture as Scrapings-of-a-Statue and Mint-of-the-Mountains (with "sasa-pieces" in his second account), cooked in oil and wax, strained, and taken for four days (lines 1381, 2200 to 2206). The poultice is the plant "dissolved in Hippopotamus Oil along with Copper and Lead-vitriol" (line 2112). No proportions are printed. ### Cultivation Register

**Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution. In Egyptian practice the operative power (*heka*) joins the drug and the recitation in one intervention, but the recitation, if any, is not in Bryan's paraphrase of these mixtures, and no deity or spell is named for them. The phrase "protect against everything" is Bryan's rendering of the scribe's purpose, not a cultivation claim, and it is not taken as one.

**Practitioner's note.** A plant whose English name survives and whose identity does not, named in a protective four-day mixture and a headache poultice. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use, and the mixtures contain poisonous minerals. A cultivator should leave it as a historical curiosity.
