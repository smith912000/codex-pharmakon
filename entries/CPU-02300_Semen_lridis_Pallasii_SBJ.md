# Semen Iridis Pallasii — Li Shi 蠡实 (Shennong bencao jing); aliases Ju Cao 剧草, San Jian 三坚, Shi Shou 豕首, Li Shi 荔实

## *Li shi 蠡实, the seed (with a flower-and-leaf use) of a rush-like plant that the commentary compares to pu (cattail or rush) but smaller; Yang 1998 prints "Semen Iridis Pallasii" (Iris pallasii Fisch. ex Trautv.), the OCR giving "lridis", and adds a footnote that the name does not agree with the indications and its identity is questionable. Identification: an Iris of the ma lin type, per the Latin gloss and the commentary's plant notes; species uncertain. Basis: Yang's Latin and Sun's commentary. Confidence: medium at genus level, low at species level* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; the other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), is read as a second witness and attributed to Yang. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Semen lridis Pallasii [SBJ]", taken from Yang's English (where the OCR reads the Latin *Iridis* as "lridis"); this entry replaces it. A search of the other texts on disk for *Iris pallasii* and for the Chinese and pinyin names found nothing for this drug. Yang's pinyin "Li Shi" is shared with the gypsum-like stone 理石 (Yang line 1181, "Li Shi (Gypsum Fibrosum)"); the two are different drugs and are not to be confused.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 蠡实 *li shi* in the **middle classic** (卷二 中经; Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, heading at line 1931, with 卷二 中经 printed at lines 1929 and 1941). Sun's paragraph (line 1933) reads: 味甘，平 ("sweet, balanced"); 主皮肤寒热 ("governs cold and heat in the skin"), 胃中热气 ("heat qi in the stomach"), 寒湿痹 ("cold-damp impediment"), 坚筋骨 ("firms the sinews and bones"), 令人嗜食 ("makes people fond of food"); 久服，轻身 ("taken long: lighten the body"); 花、叶：去白虫 ("flowers and leaves: expel white worms"); 一名剧草，一名三坚，一名豕首 ("other names: *ju cao*, *san jian*, *shi shou*"); 生川谷 ("grows in river valleys").

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 1935) gives the aliases 剧草, 三坚, 剧荔华 (citing the *Yulan*), 泽蓝 and 豕首, the nature "Shennong and Huangdi: sweet, acrid, non-toxic", the habitat 生宛句 ("grows at Wanju") and the harvest 五月采 ("gathered in the fifth month"). The Mingyi bielu layer (line 1937) adds the alias 荔实 *li shi*, the habitat 生河东 ("grows in Hedong") and the harvest 五月采实，阴干 ("the seed gathered in the fifth month, dried in the shade"), which is the only text that names the part gathered for the drug: the seed.

Sun's note (案, line 1939) is the only description of the plant in the sources. It cites the *Shuowen*: 荔，草也，似蒲而小，根可作刷 ("*li* is a grass, like *pu* but smaller; the root can be made into brushes"); the *Guang ya*, which equates *li* with a name garbled in the OCR; the *Yueling* ("in the middle month of winter the *li* shoots come out") with Zheng's gloss 荔挺，马薤也 ("*li ting* is *ma xie*"); Gao You on the *Huainanzi* (荔马，荔草也); the *Tongsu wen* (一名马兰, "also called *ma lan*"); and Yan Zhitui, who says the plant grows in the plains and marshes of Hebei, is common and planted in courtyards in Jiangdong, and is there called *zao pu* ("early rush"), so that people do not recognise *ma xie*. These are the commentators' remarks, not the classic's, and they identify a rush-leaved plant with brush-making roots, which agrees with the identification of *li shi* as an *Iris* of the *ma lin* type.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 2961): "Li Shi (Semen Iridis Pallasii) is sweet and balanced. It mainly treats cold and heat in the skin, heat qi in the stomach, and wind, cold, damp impediment. It fortifies the sinews and bones and makes one desire food. Protracted taking may make the body light. Its flowers and leaves [i.e., Flos Et Folium Iridis Pallasii] are able to remove white worms [i.e., pinworms]. Its other name is Ju Cao (Sharp Weed). Another name is San Jian (Three Hard). It is also called Shi Shou (Pig Head). It grows in rivers and valleys." Yang files the drug in his **Middle Class** (PDF page 79, headed "Herbs: Middle Class"), in agreement with Sun. Yang's text lacks the Wu Pu and Mingyi layers. Two readings differ slightly: "wind, cold, damp impediment" for Sun's 寒湿痹, and "pinworms" is Yang's gloss on "white worms". Yang's footnote 177 (line 2974) is modern commentary, and its OCR is damaged; it reads, so far as it can be read: "The name of this medicinal does not agree with the description of its indications. Therefore, its identity is questionable." The footnote is not part of the classic, and the sources on disk do not say what Yang considers the correct identity to be.

**Identity.** The Latin "Iris pallasii Fisch. ex Trautv." is Yang's gloss. The commentary in Sun's note (a small rush-like plant, brush-making roots, the name *ma lan*) points to the *Iris* genus in general. Modern Chinese sources treat the plant as a species of the *Iris lactea* group, of which the Latin name used by Yang is generally regarded as a synonym; that is general knowledge not retrieved from a source on disk. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Cold and heat in the skin, heat qi in the stomach, cold-damp impediment; firming the sinews and bones; making people fond of food; and, with the flowers and leaves, expelling white worms. Taken long, it lightens the body.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation beyond drying the seed in the shade, no duration.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest: the seed, gathered in the fifth month and dried in the shade (line 1937). The classic's general rule for middle drugs is that they may be toxic or non-toxic and are weighed as the case requires (line 1677); it is a rule for the class, not a dose for this plant. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Iris* is a large genus of flowering plants, and the rootstocks and seeds of several species are generally regarded as irritant or mildly poisonous if eaten in quantity. That is a statement about the genus and is not carried over to this drug without an identification. The indications (heat qi in the stomach, cold-damp impediment) have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The paragraph stands in the middle classic (中经). The middle-class preface (Sun line 1677) reads 中药一百二十种为臣，主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经: the middle drugs are "the ministers, mainly nourishing the nature to correspond with humankind; non-toxic or toxic, weighed as appropriate; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the emaciated". The middle class is therefore not in itself the upper grade of long-term life-nourishing. The grade here rests instead on the drug's own paragraph, which carries a 久服 clause that promises 轻身, "lighten the body" (久服，轻身), a life-nourishing status that the Codex's grading rule for the Shennong text counts as attested. 輕身 has no pharmacological equivalent and is not weight loss. Sun and Yang agree on the grade, so no difference between them needs stating. The Wu Pu and Mingyi readings do not count toward the grade, and the text does not mention *jing*, *qi* or *shen* as acted on by this drug.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade seed that carries one long-use promise of a lightened body, in a plant whose identity Yang himself calls questionable. The classic gives no dose and no duration. A cultivator cannot responsibly act on that; the lesson is the grade clause and the doubt about identity, not a recipe.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** Yang's footnote calls the identity questionable. Do not substitute one *Iris* for another, or any other seed for it. Several *Iris* species are generally regarded as irritant.
- **Cold and heat in the skin, stomach heat, impediment, worms.** These are medical conditions in the classic's terms; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Long use.** The 久服 promise is the classic's, not a safety finding. No modern safety data were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]
- **Homograph.** Yang's pinyin "Li Shi" is also used for a different, mineral drug (理石).
