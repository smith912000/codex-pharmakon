### Origin & Tradition

Stavesacre is a tall Mediterranean larkspur whose hard, dark, intensely bitter and acrid seeds have one overwhelmingly famous use across two millennia: **killing lice.** Dioscorides records it among the strong and dangerous plants of Book IV, and its very Greek name (*staphis agria*, "wild raisin") and its country names — "lousewort," "lice-bane" — fix its reputation. The powdered seed, made into an ointment or wash, was the standard pediculicide of the ancient, medieval and early-modern world, used on the scalp and body to destroy head and body lice and their nits, and against scabies and skin parasites. It was a parasiticide of households, armies, prisons and ships for as long as lice plagued humanity.

The same acrid, irritant chemistry that kills lice makes the seed **toxic and dangerous internally.** It was used in older practice as a violent emetic and purgative — to "evacuate phlegm" and, dangerously, as a vermifuge — but its narrow margin and harshness made internal use perilous, and it is essentially obsolete as an internal medicine. Applied to the skin it is irritant; taken by mouth in more than minute amounts it causes burning, vomiting, and the neuromuscular toxicity characteristic of the **delphinium/aconite alkaloid family** to which it belongs.

Stavesacre's other notable afterlife is in **homeopathy**, where *Staphysagria* (prepared from the seed) is a well-known remedy with a distinctive symptom-picture classically associated with suppressed anger, indignation and the effects of grief or violation, as well as styes and certain urinary and skin complaints. This homeopathic framework is recorded here as part of the substance's cross-traditional life, distinct from any claim about the crude seed's material-dose pharmacology.

### Contraindications

- **Internal use — avoid.** Toxic aconite-type alkaloids; obsolete and dangerous internally; no antidote in the field.
- **Broken skin / mucous membranes / eyes.** Enhanced absorption and irritation; keep away.
- **Children.** Toxic seed; keep out of reach; risk of accidental ingestion.
