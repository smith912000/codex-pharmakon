### Origin & Tradition

Faber's fir is a tall true fir endemic to the high mountains of Sichuan, China — the characteristic conifer of Mount Emei and the western Chinese ranges. Like the firs and pines generally, it is valued less as a famous single drug than as a regional source of the conifer family's standard medicinal products: the **aromatic needles**, the **resin (fir balsam)**, and the **essential oil** distilled from them. It enters this Codex as a representative of the Himalayan/Sichuan high-conifer materia medica documented in regional and Tibetan-influenced sources (the canonical index draws on the *rGyud-bzhi* and Chinese regional traditions).

The fir's medicinal identity is the coniferous one shared with pine, juniper and cypress (this volume): a **warming, drying, resinous-antiseptic and expectorant** profile. The needle oil and resin are used (chiefly externally and by inhalation) for **respiratory** complaints — coughs, bronchial congestion, catarrh — the monoterpene-rich vapours loosening phlegm and acting as a mild bronchial antiseptic; for **rheumatic and muscular pain** as a warming liniment/rub; and as an **antiseptic** for the skin and air (fumigation). The resin (fir balsam) serves as a wound-dressing and plaster material. Across folk practice the firs are the mountain people's warming, cleansing, respiratory conifers.

The activity rests on the **monoterpene-rich essential oil** (the pinenes, limonene, bornyl acetate and relatives) and the **diterpene resin acids** of the balsam — the standard, real chemistry of the medicinal conifers.

### Contraindications

- **Essential oil — diluted/external.** Not for undiluted internal self-use; conifer oils can be irritant and renally taxing internally.
- **Asthma/sensitive airways.** Strong monoterpene vapours can irritate in some.
- **Skin sensitisation.** Possible with conifer resin/colophony.
