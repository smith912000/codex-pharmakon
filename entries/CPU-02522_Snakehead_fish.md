# Snakehead fish — 蠡鱼 *Liyu* (Shennong bencao jing), in the variant 鳢鱼 *liyu*
## *The 蠡鱼 of the Shennong classic, a freshwater fish; our identification is the snakehead, Channa argus (Cantor), of medium confidence, and Sun Xingyan's own note says the identity was already disputed and cannot be settled from the old texts* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** This entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The identification is ours and the text does not secure it: Sun's own note lists a series of conflicting identifications and ends, in effect, by saying they cannot be investigated further.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph begins with the heading 蠡鱼, with the variant noted in brackets, 《初学记》引作鳢鱼 ("the Chuxueji quotes it as 鳢鱼"). It reads 味苦，寒 ("bitter, cold"). It governs 湿痹 ("damp *bi*", a painful obstruction of the joints), 面目浮肿 ("swelling of face and eyes") and 下大水 ("brings down great water"). It gives an alias, 一名 followed by one character lost in the transcription (the fish name ends 鱼), and the habitat, which the transcription cuts short at 生池 ("grows in pools"; the following character, presumably 泽, is not on the page). There is no 久服 clause. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 蠡鱼 *liyu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1521)

**Other layers.** No Wu Pu line and no Mingyi line for this entry is on the page. (The 《名医》 line printed just above the heading, 生东海，表有文，采无时, appears to belong to the preceding entry and is not used.) The part of the animal is not named.

**Sun's note (案), 1799.** Sun says that Shuowen has one fish under the character he prints (the transcription loses the key characters), and that Mao Chang and Guo Pu gloss 鳢 with a different fish, so that they do not agree with Xu Shen. The Chuxueji quotes this entry as 鳢, and Sun suggests that the two characters (蠡 and 鳢) sound alike and produce the confusion: 不可得详, "it cannot be known in detail". He then collects the identifications: the Guangzhi calls it a *tunyu* "pig fish" (豚鱼, which the entry equates with 江豚, the river porpoise); the Bencao yanyi calls it today's "black carp" (黑鲤鱼) and says Daoists hold that a "star" on the head makes it taboo (道家以为头有星为厌); the Guangya gives a name read as the eel (鳗鲡); Lu Ji takes it as a 鲍鱼. He concludes that the opinions all differ and are hard to examine (说各不同，难以详究). The transcription confuses several look-alike characters, so the note cannot be followed precisely.

**Identity.** The modern snakehead *Channa argus* (Chinese 乌鳢) is the usual modern reading of the name, but the older commentaries point to a carp-like black fish, an eel, a porpoise or other fish. The Codex writes only what the paragraph says: a bitter, cold fish-drug for damp *bi*, swelling and great water. ### Active Components

The text prints no constituent and names no part. In general terms fish flesh is largely water and protein, with some fat; freshwater fish can carry parasites. Nothing more specific is stated without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, cold fish for joint obstruction, facial swelling and "great water". The Codex gives no protocol: swelling of the face and body can signal kidney, heart or other serious disease and needs medical assessment. Whether a fish is the fish meant is itself unsettled.

### Scientific Correlation

In general knowledge the northern snakehead is a freshwater predatory fish eaten in East Asia and an invasive species where it has been released outside its range. The classic's claims about damp *bi* and great water are its own terms. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The classic files 蠡鱼 in the upper grade (上品); its preface says upper-grade drugs nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic, and may be taken for a long time. On the grading rule used for this series, placement in the upper classic is itself the ground for (a). The paragraph itself has no 久服 clause and no promise to lighten the body or to extend life; it is a flavour, nature and indication list (damp *bi*, swelling of face and eyes, great water). The attestation is of the placement only, the identity of the fish is unsettled by Sun's own note, and the Codex does not read a further cultivation claim into the paragraph. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 蠡鱼 *liyu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1521)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The upper-grade placement is a frame and the paragraph lists only complaints; the identity of the fish is unsettled by Sun's own note. The entry is a record.

### Contraindications

- **Identification.** Do not take a fish as this drug on the strength of this entry.
- **Edema.** Swelling of face or body needs medical assessment.
- **Hygiene and ecology.** Freshwater fish should be well cooked; the northern snakehead is an invasive predator in some countries and release or transport of live fish is regulated there. [USER: verify local rules]
