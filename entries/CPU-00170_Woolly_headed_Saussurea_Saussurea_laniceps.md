### Origin & Tradition

*Saussurea laniceps* (Asteraceae) is a high-alpine perennial of the eastern Himalaya and the mountains of southwest China — Yunnan, Sichuan, and southeastern Tibet — growing at extreme elevation, typically between 3,500 and 5,000 metres on glacial moraine, talus, and frost-shattered scree. Its conspicuous adaptation is a dense felt of white woolly hairs (the *laniceps*, "woolly head") that sheathes the inflorescence, insulating the developing flowers against ultraviolet flux and the diurnal freeze–thaw of the snowline. It is one of several species traded under the Chinese drug name *xuě lián huā* ("snow lotus flower"), and one of the two species (with *S. medusa*) most prized in Tibetan and Chinese practice. The whole flowering aerial part, harvested at the brief alpine flowering, is the medicinal material.

The plant occupies a near-mythic register in the high-altitude pharmacopoeia: a warming, dispersing remedy gathered from the coldest, most inaccessible ground, used against the cold-damp afflictions of mountain life — rheumatic joint pain, cold in the womb, and the depletions of altitude and hard labour. Its rarity, slow growth (individual plants may take years to reach flowering), and intense collection pressure have driven it to threatened status; *S. laniceps* is a textbook case of harvest-driven decline, with documented reduction in plant stature over decades of selective picking of the largest flowering individuals.

**SBJ (Bencao tradition):** Snow lotus enters the later materia medica literature rather than the earliest classics, reflecting its peripheral, high-frontier origin. It is classified as warm and acrid-bitter, entering the Liver and Kidney channels, and is grouped among the substances that **dispel wind-damp** (*qū fēng shī*) and **warm the channels** (*wēn jīng*). Its reputation rests on the dual action of dispersing cold-damp obstruction in the joints while warming and supplementing the lower burner.

**FMT (Four Medical Tantras, rGyud bzhi tradition):** In Tibetan medicine snow lotus (*gangs thig pa* / *gangs lha me tog*, "snow deity flower") is a recognised high-mountain drug used for *grang ba* (cold) disorders — cold-type joint and back pain, cold settling in the kidneys and womb, and the recovery of bodily strength (*lus stobs*) in those depleted by altitude, exposure, and toil. It appears in compound formulas for *rtsa* (channel/vessel) and joint complaints of cold nature.

**BM (Bon and Himalayan folk medicine):** Across the Tibetan plateau and adjacent ranges, snow lotus is a celebrated folk remedy and tonic, taken in spirit-infusions and decoctions for cold-pattern arthritic pain, menstrual cold-stagnation, and as a restorative for high-altitude exhaustion. Its prestige as a difficult-to-obtain alpine "treasure herb" pervades the folk traditions of the region.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Cold-damp joint and tendon pain in cold-exposure training** `[SPECIALIST ONLY]` — Snow lotus's classical indication is precisely the cold-damp *bi* (painful obstruction) pattern: aching, stiff, cold-aggravated joints that ease with warmth and movement. For athletes and practitioners working in cold environments, the warming, channel-opening profile targets exactly this presentation. The flavonoid and lignan anti-inflammatory fractions provide a plausible mechanistic correlate to the traditional analgesic reputation.

2. **Warming the lower burner — cold-pattern recovery** `[SPECIALIST ONLY]` — Traditionally used to warm a cold womb and cold kidneys; in a recovery framework this maps onto cold-deficiency presentations of the lower body and pelvis, where warmth, gentle dispersal, and restoration of local circulation are the goal.

3. **High-altitude / cold-exposure constitutional restoration** `[SPECIALIST ONLY]` — Its folk reputation as a restorative for those depleted by altitude and exposure aligns with use as an adjunct after prolonged cold-environment exertion, where the body presents the cold, depleted, stiff pattern the herb was developed to address.

*Because of threatened conservation status and species-adulteration risk, snow lotus is a deliberately restricted, specialist-only entry: it should not be a routine reach.*

### Preparation & Dosing

**Decoction (classical):**
- Small quantities of the dried flowering herb (conventionally a few grams) simmered in water and taken warm; snow lotus is a low-dose, peripheral drug, not a bulk tonic
- Frequently combined with other wind-damp and channel-warming herbs in compound formula rather than used alone

**Spirit infusion (folk preparation):**
- Whole flowering heads steeped in grain spirit for an extended period; the medicated liquor taken in small measures for cold-pattern joint pain — the traditional Himalayan/plateau method
- External liniment application of the same infusion for localised cold joint pain is also traditional

**Sourcing caution:**
- Market "snow lotus" spans several *Saussurea* species and frequent substitution; ethically and legally sourced, correctly identified material is essential given the species' threatened status

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Primary Tier:** ◉ Qi — a warming, dispersing, channel-moving substance that disperses cold-damp obstruction and restores movement of Qi and Blood through cold-bound joints and the lower burner; secondary ◈ Jing through its traditional role in warming and restoring the kidneys and womb in cold-deficiency states

**TCM Channel:** Liver (肝, Gān), Kidney (肾, Shèn) — primary

**Energetic Action:** Dispels wind-damp (*qū fēng shī*); warms the channels and disperses cold (*wēn jīng sàn hán*); unblocks painful obstruction (*tōng bì zhǐ tòng*); warms and supplements the lower burner / Kidney-yang aspect (*wēn bǔ xià jiāo*)

**Tibetan (FMT):** A *grang ba* (cold-disorder) drug — warming, used against cold settling in the joints, channels, kidneys, and womb, and as a restorative of bodily strength in cold-exposure depletion

### Contraindications

- **Conservation / legal status:** *S. laniceps* is threatened by overharvesting; use only verified, ethically and legally sourced material — this is a contraindication of conscience as much as of safety
- **Heat patterns / yin deficiency with heat:** As a warming, acrid-dispersing drug, snow lotus is inappropriate in heat, yin-deficient heat, or febrile presentations
- **Asteraceae sensitivity:** The family and its sesquiterpene lactones carry allergic-sensitisation potential; avoid in those with known Compositae/daisy-family allergy
- **Pregnancy:** Channel-warming, blood-moving herbs traditionally used to act on the womb are contraindicated in pregnancy
- **Species substitution risk:** Misidentified or adulterated "snow lotus" may carry an unknown safety profile; positive identification is a safety prerequisite
