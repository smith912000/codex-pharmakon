# Elder — Elderflower / Elderberry / Sambucus / Sureau / Holunder / Ellhorn

## *Sambucus nigra* L. | Family: Viburnaceae (Adoxaceae)

**Tradition Tags:** EP, AT, DMM, CM
**Energetic Classification:** Flower — warm & dry (2nd degree), diaphoretic-relaxant; berry — cool & moist-tending, cleansing (Galenic reading; see note below)
**Training Phase:** Fortification / Surface-Release (the fever-and-catarrh gate)

> **Codex note:** This is the *tree* Elder — the tall hedgerow shrub with creamy flower-plates and near-black berries. It is **distinct from Dwarf Elder (*Sambucus ebulus*)** already filed in the Codex, a rank herbaceous plant with a harsher, more drastically purgative and more toxic profile. Do not substitute one for the other.

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ancient Egyptian (Ebers-tradition).** Elder grew around the Mediterranean basin and its flowers and berries entered the Egyptian domestic pharmacopoeia as cooling, cleansing and skin-soothing agents. Egyptian practice used elder-type flower washes and poultices for inflamed skin and for "drawing heat" from the surface — an application that survives almost unchanged into modern European folk use. *(Specific Ebers chapter attributions for Sambucus are debated; the genus's Mediterranean folk-medical continuity is genuine. **Assyrian / Mesopotamian (cuneiform tablets).** The Assyrian herbal tablets list a large repertory of hedgerow and tree drugs used as fever-sweats, poultices and washes. Elder is plausibly among the "sweat-raising" and skin drugs, but the Akkadian plant names in the tablets cannot be mapped one-to-one onto modern binomials with confidence. **Hedge the Assyrian attribution: [USER: verify]** — do not invent an Akkadian name.

**Dioscorides (*De Materia Medica*).** Dioscorides describes *aktē* (ἀκτῆ), the elder, distinguishing the tree elder from the dwarf *chamaiaktē*. He records the leaves, flowers and fruit as drying and "drawing," used cooked for the belly, as a diuretic-tending cleanser, and applied externally for burns, inflammations and boggy swellings. This is the doctrinal seed of elder's whole later European career: a surface-drawing, fluid-moving, cleansing drug.

**Avicenna (*Canon of Medicine*).** In the Arabic-Persian tradition Avicenna carries the Dioscoridean elder forward within humoral logic, classing its flowers as warming and *muḥallil* (resolvent / discutient) — able to open the pores, provoke sweat, and disperse thick catarrhal matter — while treating the fruit as more cleansing and laxative-tending. The flower's job is to **open the surface and move the sweat**; the berry's job is to **cleanse and evacuate**.

**European folk stratum ("the Elder Mother").** Across Northern and Western Europe the elder is the great domestic fever-tree: hot elderflower tea (often with peppermint and yarrow) is the classic bedtime diaphoretic for the onset of a cold or fever, and elderberry rob (a boiled-down syrup) is the winter drink "for colds and flu." The tree is hedged with taboo — the *Hylde-Moer* / Elder Mother is said to live in it, and one must ask her leave before cutting; wood was not to be burned or made into cradles. Sacredness and utility are fused: the tree that sits by every cottage door is also the one you must respect.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** The doctrine is a doctrine of the *surface* and of *flow*. Every tradition reads elder as something that moves fluid outward and downward — sweat through the skin, catarrh out of the head, water through the kidney, matter through the bowel. Map this onto the plant's real division of labour. The **flower** is dominated by flavonol glycosides (rutin, isoquercitrin, kaempferol derivatives), triterpenes and a small volatile fraction; a hot infusion delivers these plus simple warmth and water, producing genuine cutaneous vasodilation and sweating — the physiological event the "open the pores, resolve the thick matter" doctrine actually names. The **berry** is dominated by anthocyanins (cyanidin-3-glucoside and cyanidin-3-sambubioside), which are deep-violet, water-soluble, and antioxidant — the visible pigment *is* the "cleansing, blood-moving" virtue the doctrine intuited from the colour. The Elder Mother taboo, read pharmacologically, is a **cultural toxicology reflex**: the same tree carries a genuinely useful cooked flower/berry medicine *and* a genuinely poisonous raw green part (leaf, bark, unripe fruit, stem). A sacred prohibition that says "handle this tree with ceremony and never carelessly" is a memory device for a plant that rewards preparation and punishes casual raw use.

### Active Components

- **Anthocyanins (berry):** cyanidin-3-*O*-glucoside (C3G), cyanidin-3-*O*-sambubioside, cyanidin-3-sambubioside-5-glucoside — >98% of total anthocyanin content; measured total anthocyanin content in ripe berries ranges roughly 600–8000 mg/kg depending on ripeness and altitude (Stuppner 2020).
- **Flavonols (flower & berry):** rutin, isoquercitrin, quercetin, kaempferol glycosides.
- **Phenolic acids:** chlorogenic and related acids.
- **Triterpenes & sterols (flower):** ursolic/oleanolic acid types; small volatile-oil fraction.
- **Lectins & ribosome-inactivating proteins** (nigrin/ebulin-type in various tissues).
- **Cyanogenic glycosides:** sambunigrin and related compounds, concentrated in **leaves, stems, unripe fruit and green parts** — the toxic fraction (Senica 2016).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Elder is the **Surface-Release** botanical of the fortification phase: a warming diaphoretic flower tea to be taken hot at the first cold-shiver or catarrhal onset, and a cooked berry preparation as a winter antioxidant tonic through the cold-and-flu season. It is used where the picture is *early, superficial, catarrhal, feverish* — a tool for the beginning of an acute respiratory episode, not a rescue drug for an established severe infection. In Kenetica sequencing it pairs with warming aromatics (mint, yarrow, ginger) for the sweat, and is stood down in favour of stronger measures if fever escalates.

### Scientific Correlation

*According to PubMed:*

**Positive / supportive.** A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 312 long-haul air travellers found standardised elderberry extract significantly shortened total cold-episode duration (117 vs 57 diary-days) and reduced symptom scores versus placebo, though the number of colds did not differ significantly (Tiralongo 2016, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu8040182)). An earlier randomised placebo-controlled trial in 60 influenza-A/B patients reported symptom relief on average ~4 days earlier with elderberry syrup (Zakay-Rones 2004, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/147323000403200205)). These map directly onto the traditional "for colds and flu" indication.

**Negative / null — the honest counterweight.** A later FDA-IND, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT in 87 PCR-confirmed influenza patients in US emergency rooms found **no benefit** of elderberry on either duration or severity; a post-hoc signal even suggested that elderberry taken *without* oseltamivir was associated with outcomes ~2 days *worse* than placebo (Macknin 2020, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-020-06170-w)). A systematic review of elderberry fruit concluded the antiviral evidence, while "promising," rested on small exploratory studies and needed more rigorous confirmation (Vlachojannis 2010, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.2729)).

**Mechanism & a theoretical caution.** The berry's antiviral and vascular-protective claims are pinned on its anthocyanins, chiefly cyanidin-3-glucoside; a review of elderberry in the SARS-CoV-2 context notes both antiviral and endothelial/anti-inflammatory activity but explicitly flags that anthocyanins modulate cytokine and endothelial responses and that this cuts both ways — the same immunomodulation raises a *theoretical* concern in hyper-inflammatory ("cytokine-storm") states, and the authors call for in-vitro/in-vivo work before recommending it (Festa 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfbc.14091)).

**Evidence grade.** *Low-to-moderate and genuinely mixed.* Two small positive RCTs, one adequately-powered negative RCT that directly contradicts them, and reviews urging caution. Elder is defensible as a traditional early-cold/flu supportive measure; it is **not** established as an antiviral cure, should **not** be oversold, and the cytokine-storm concern means it should be used conservatively in severe or hyper-inflammatory illness.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic / Unani (Avicenna, attested):** Flower warming and *muḥallil* (resolvent), pore-opening, sweat-provoking; fruit cooling-cleansing and laxative-tending.
- **Greek humoral (Dioscorides, attested):** Drying, drawing, cleansing; internal for the belly, external for inflammation and burns.
- **TCM:** *Not attested* for *Sambucus nigra* in the classical Chinese materia medica — do not assign a Chinese energetic reading.
- **Ayurvedic (rasa/guna/virya/vipaka):** *Not attested* as a classical Ayurvedic dravya — do not fabricate a rasa profile.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian (EP).** The person in the Egyptian corpus is plural — *ka*, *ba*, *akh* and the *ib* (heart as seat of judgement) — and the operative power in a prescription is *heka*, in which spell and drug are two halves of one intervention. None of that is attested for elder. The Ebers corpus is indication-organised, recording what was done for inflamed skin or surface heat rather than what a substance does to a person's vital constitution, and in elder's case even the species identification behind any papyrus attribution is uncertain (the entry itself flags the chapter number for verification). With neither a secure substance identity nor a recorded *heka* recitation for this plant, no cultivation status can be assigned. Cultivation status: **(c) absent** — the Egyptian material here is therapeutic and, at that, provisional.

**Assyrian (AT).** Mesopotamian medicine classifies illness partly by causal agency — a divine hand, a ghost, a broken oath — so a drug is characterised in part by what agency it opposes. The standing blocking warning applies in full: elder's identification among the Akkadian hedge-and-tree drugs cannot be mapped one-to-one onto *Sambucus nigra* with confidence, the entry itself declines to invent an Akkadian name, and no deity, demon or tablet attribution is claimed. Under that warning no cultivation status is asserted and the attestation, such as it is, remains a hedged therapeutic one. Cultivation status: **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides describes *aktē* (ἀκτῆ) and carefully distinguishes the tree elder from the dwarf *chamaiaktē*, recording the leaves, flowers and fruit as drying and drawing — cooked for the belly, diuretic-tending, applied externally to burns and inflammations. He carries no subtle-body system with which to assign a cultivation status, and none should be manufactured for him. What the tradition genuinely offers a cultivator is his discipline of attention: the separation of the true tree elder from its more drastic dwarf relative, the judgement of which part to take and how to prepare it — a real practice of provenance and sensory assay, not a vital-body doctrine. Cultivation status: **(c) absent** as to any vital-constitution status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** Avicenna carries the Dioscoridean elder into humoral logic, classing the flower as warming and *muḥallil* (resolvent / discutient) — opening the pores, provoking sweat, dispersing thick catarrhal matter — while treating the fruit as more cleansing and laxative-tending. The strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus is the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture, the finite vital endowment whose depletion *is* ageing; but that is a frame of the whole regimen, not a status the tradition assigns to a pore-opening surface drug, and elder is not claimed as a *mufarrih* (cardiac gladdener strengthening the *ruh*). Elder's standing here is functional — it opens the surface and moves the sweat — not conservational. Cultivation status: **(b) framed:** attested within humoral doctrine, with no cultivation status sourced.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a longevity substance, and no tradition on the tag line treats it as one. Elder is a surface-release tool for the beginning of an acute episode — a hot flower infusion to open the pores and raise a sweat at the first cold-shiver, a cooked berry preparation as a seasonal antioxidant drink — used early and superficially and then stood down. The one piece of genuine cultivation-adjacent wisdom the tradition carries is negative and prudential: the Elder Mother taboo and Dioscorides' care in distinguishing the tree encode a real toxicology — only ripe, fully cooked fruit is used, and every raw green part is left alone. What a cultivator does with elder is respect the preparation, use it at the surface, and stop.

### Contraindications

- **RAW berries, leaves, bark, stems and unripe fruit are toxic.** They contain **cyanogenic glycosides (sambunigrin type)** and lectins/ribosome-inactivating proteins; ingestion of raw or insufficiently processed material causes nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, diarrhoea and weakness. Documented cluster poisonings have followed drinking juice pressed from raw, uncooked elderberries (including berries, leaves and stems). **Cyanogenic content is highest in green parts and unripe fruit and falls as berries ripen** (Senica 2016, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jsfa.8085); Stuppner 2020, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/s20174983)). **Berries must be fully cooked and only ripe fruit used; never juice or eat them raw.**
- **Theoretical cytokine-storm / hyper-inflammatory caution** (Festa 2022): use conservatively in severe active infection; do not position as a substitute for antiviral therapy.
- **Do not confuse with Dwarf Elder (*S. ebulus*)**, which is more drastic and more toxic.
- Autoimmune disease, pregnancy/lactation, and children: insufficient safety data for high-dose extracts — default to cautious culinary doses. [USER: verify house policy]
- Possible additive effect with diuretics (flower) and with immunomodulating drugs.
