# Vicia sepium — Aphake, aphace (Dioscorides 2-178); "Bush Vetch"

## *Vicia sepium [Bauhin, Linnaeus], the fourth name on the translators' SUGGESTED line for 2-178, glossed "Bush Vetch [Mabberley]"; an "other usage" line gives Lathyrus aphaca, Aphaca vulgaris and Lathyrus segetum ("Yellow Vetchling")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text on disk lists the plant under this name (the name indexes in `Sources/*/extract/names.csv` and the list files in `_sources/` were searched). The entry rests on one short chapter of one text. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), printed under the chapter heading, and not Dioscorides' own identification. The Codex does not state the identity as certain, and the translators print a different genus (*Lathyrus*, the yellow vetchling) as an "other usage" on a second line. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *2-178. APHAKE* at line 6073, SUGGESTED lines at 6074 and 6075 ("Aphace, Sylvestris vitia, Os mundi [Fuchs], Vicia sepium [Bauhin, Linnaeus] — Bush Vetch [Mabberley]" and "[other usage] Lathyrus aphaca, Aphaca vulgaris, Lathyrus segetum — Yellow Vetchling"), and the text at line 6076. Directly after the text stands a plate caption naming *Orobanche ramosa* (line 6077), which is not a plant of this chapter, and the page number 303 (line 6078). The chapter stands in Book Two, in the section "Lachana: Vegetables" (pages 243 to 304; line 31), after *2-177. MEDICE* (line 6070) and before *2-179. PRASON* (line 6086). The name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 368, and the translators' index prints "Vicia sepium 303" and "Vetchling 303" (line 17963).

The whole chapter reads: "Aphaca is a small-leaved little shrub that grows in the fields, higher than lentils. The pods that are found thickly on it are bigger than lentil pods. They contain three or four little seeds smaller than lentils. These little seeds are astringent, as a result they stop both excessive discharge of the bowels and the stomach. They are [eaten] as the lentil is, fried or bruised and boiled [vegetable]."

The chapter is a plant description followed by one use and one way of eating. It describes the plant by comparison with the lentil in every sentence: a "small-leaved little shrub" in the fields, "higher than lentils", with pods that are "bigger than lentil pods", holding "three or four little seeds smaller than lentils". It records one medicinal quality, that the seeds "are astringent" and so "stop both excessive discharge of the bowels and the stomach". It then says they are eaten "as the lentil is, fried or bruised and boiled". The word in square brackets, "[eaten]", and the note "[vegetable]" are the translators' bracketed insertions. The chapter prints no other names.

**Overlap.** A chapter-level search of the entries folder for 2-178, the Greek heading, *Aphaca*, *Aphake*, *Vicia sepium* and *Bush Vetch* found no entry on this chapter. I_588 (*Grass Pea, Lathyrus sativus*) describes "cultivated vetch-peas (the *lathyros/aphake* cluster)" as "nourishing but flatulent and hard to digest" (its line 19), without a chapter number. Chapter 2-178 does not say that, and the Codex does not read I_588 as an entry on this chapter. I_694 (*Common Vetch, Vicia sativa*) and I_477 (*Fava Bean, Vicia faba*) are other vetches, and the translators' index lists *Vicia faba* at another page (247). The neighbouring chapter 2-177 *MEDICE* (line 6070) is glossed as lucerne. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The text prints no constituent, and no analysis of the plant Dioscorides describes was read. It is a general fact that *Vicia* and *Lathyrus* are genera of the pea family (Fabaceae), and that the seeds of several pea-family vetches and vetchlings can be toxic if eaten raw or in quantity. The chapter's "astringent" is Dioscorides' description of an effect and is not a statement of composition. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the translators' bush vetch or of the yellow vetchling. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records seeds that are "astringent", stopping looseness of the bowels and stomach, and that they are eaten like lentils. These are Dioscorides' claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex draws no eating or medical protocol from a one-paragraph chapter and an identification that is only a suggestion. Wild vetch-type seeds are not to be eaten on the strength of this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives two ways of eating: the seeds "fried or bruised and boiled", as the lentil is. It gives no quantity, no frequency, no duration and no method for the medicinal use. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] It is a general, well-established fact that some seeds of the pea family, including some vetches and vetchlings, contain compounds that are harmful when eaten in quantity, and that persistent diarrhoea needs medical assessment. Nothing is stated about the effect of *Vicia sepium*, or of *Lathyrus aphaca*, in particular.

### Cultivation Register

What the chapter gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a habitat (the fields) and a set of size comparisons with a known plant (the lentil: the plant is higher, the pods are bigger, the seeds are smaller), which together make a field check for recognition. It gives no smell or taste assay, no provenance and no adulteration test. The chapter assigns the plant no cultivation status. The identification is the translators', and they print two different genera. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The transferable point is the method of describing an unknown plant by comparing each of its parts to a known one. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

Chapter 2-178 prints no warning. The identification is only the translators' suggestion, and the translators offer two different genera, so no plant or seed should be gathered or eaten on the strength of this entry. Some vetch and vetchling seeds are harmful if eaten in quantity, which the chapter does not address. Persistent diarrhoea needs medical assessment and should not be managed by an unidentified seed. The text gives no dose and no safety information for pregnancy or children. [USER: verify]
