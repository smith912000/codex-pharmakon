# Roof iris — Yuan Wei 鸢尾 (Shennong bencao jing); Mingyi alias Wu Yuan 乌园

## *Iris tectorum Maxim., the roof iris (Chinese yuan wei, "kite tail"), a blue-flowered iris with a creeping rhizome; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence, since the texts describe no plant, and the entry's own commentary leaves open whether it is the same plant as she gan (Belamcanda), with Iris dichotoma a historical candidate* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** The only text read for this entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Its other edition on disk, Yang 1998, also lists the drug (names.csv line 170, "Yuan Wei", pharmaceutical Latin "Radix Iridis Tectori"); it is the same tradition and is not read here. Other iris entries on the spine are other species from other traditions (for example I_1108 *Iris ensata*, I_1302 *Iris nepalensis*, both rGyud-bzhi) and are not merged with it. The Shennong drug she gan, which Sun's note discusses next to this one, is the separate spine entry I_1241 (*Belamcanda chinensis*, tag SBJ).

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 鸢尾 in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 鸢尾 *yuan wei*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3101). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，平 ("bitter, even"); 主蛊毒邪气 ("governs gu poison and evil qi"), 鬼注 ("ghost infusion"), 诸毒 ("the various poisons"), 破症瘕积聚 ("breaks lumps, masses and accumulations"), 去水 ("removes water") and 下三虫 ("brings down the three worms"); habitat 生山谷 ("grows in mountain valleys"). The paragraph has no alias, no part and no 久服 clause.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 3103) says only 治蛊毒, "it treats gu poison", quoted from the Yulan. The Mingyi bielu layer (line 3105) adds the alias 一名乌园, and: 生九嶷山。五月采, "grows on Mount Jiuyi; gathered in the fifth month".

Sun Xingyan's note (案, lines 3107 to 3109) is short and is the only place in the entry that bears on identity. It quotes the Guangya: 鸢尾，乌 [a character is missing]，射干也, "yuan wei, wu [?], is she gan", and a bracketed remark in the note says this should perhaps read "yuan wei is wu yuan; wu [?] is she gan; they are two things" (疑当作鸢尾，乌园也；乌 [?]，射干也。是二物). It then quotes the Tang bencao note: 与射干全别, "it is entirely different from she gan". So the note records a doubt whether the Guangya equates the two, and both the bracketed remark and the Tang note take them to be different things. The entry gives no description of the plant, no leaf, flower or root. The Codex's identification rests on the modern convention only. [USER: verify]

An appendix of drug pairings at the end of the volume has the line 黄环 鸢尾为使, "huang huan: yuan wei is its envoy" (line 4523). It stands under the entry of a different drug (huang huan) and says nothing about yuan wei's own nature.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, iris rhizomes contain isoflavones (tectorigenin and related compounds are known from this species) and irritant substances. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, even plant for gu poison, "evil qi", ghost infusion, various poisons, lumps and accumulations, water and the three worms. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose, and flags a lower-grade drug with a history of toxicity concerns: see Contraindications.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Mingyi layer gives the harvest month (the fifth); the Shennong paragraph names no part and no preparation. For toxic drugs generally the classic's preface gives a rule: begin with a dose like a grain of millet; if the disease leaves, stop; if not, double; if not, increase tenfold (若用毒药疗病，先起如黍粟，病去，即止。不去，倍之；不去，十之。取去为度) (line 4125). That rule is general, the paragraph does not say this plant is toxic, and the Codex does not use the rule as a dose for it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The roof iris is a widely grown ornamental, and its rhizome has a place in modern Chinese pharmacy as a substitute for she gan. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The classic's categories ("gu poison", "ghost infusion", "the three worms") are not modern diagnoses.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味苦，平, "bitter, even" (line 3101). No other quality is printed in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph for 鸢尾 reads 味苦，平 and files the plant in the lower grade (下品). The classic's preface (line 2935) says the lower medicines "are for treating disease, to correspond to earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to clear cold-and-heat evil qi, break accumulations and heal illness, base it on the lower classic" (主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经). The paragraph has no 久服 clause; it is an indication list for poisons, accumulations and worms. The register is therefore (b): a lower-grade drug whose grade is the tradition's own warning against long use. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 鸢尾 *yuan wei*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3101)

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug in the Chinese scheme, taken, if at all, for a defined complaint and then stopped. Nothing here supports long use, and the tradition's own grading is the warning. The plant behind the name is also not settled by the texts.

### Contraindications

- **Lower-grade drug:** the classic's preface calls the lower medicines mostly toxic and not for long use (line 2935); the paragraph does not say this plant is toxic, and no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Irritation:** iris rhizomes are generally regarded as irritant to the mouth and stomach. [USER: verify]
- **Identity:** she gan (I_1241) and other irises are not interchangeable with this drug; the note records doubt on whether the Guangya equates them.
- **Pregnancy and nursing:** no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given.
