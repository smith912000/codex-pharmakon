### Origin & Tradition

Calcite is crystalline **calcium carbonate** — the most common carbonate mineral on earth, the substance of limestone, marble, chalk, stalactites, and (as clear "Iceland spar") the famous double-refracting crystal. In the Chinese materia medica it is **Fāng Jiě Shí** — "square-cleaving stone," named for the perfect rhombohedral cleavage by which calcite splits into clean parallelograms — and it belongs to the family of **cold, heavy, heat-clearing stone-medicines** alongside gypsum (I_343), with which it has historically been confused, substituted, and paired. It is one of the "earth/stone medicines" (*do sman* / *shí yào*) whose action is cooling by mineral weight.

**Chinese Tradition (Fāng Jiě Shí):**
Fāng Jiě Shí is acrid-bitter and cold, entering the Stomach. Its classical action is to **clear heat and drain fire**, closely paralleling raw gypsum: it is used for febrile heat, thirst, and Stomach-fire patterns, and historically the two clear cooling stones (calcite and gypsum) were sometimes interchanged in practice — a genuine source of pharmacological confusion the tradition itself noted. As a calcium-carbonate mineral it also carries the **antacid** action common to carbonates (neutralising stomach acid).

**Tibetan Medicine (FMT — cong-zhi):**
Calcite and the calcium "stone medicines" are used among the cooling agents for hot disorders and, reflecting their calcium content and structural role, in bone-and-mineral contexts within Tibetan pharmacy.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the clear stone that splits true:**
Calcite's signature is its **perfect cleavage and (in clear spar) its transparency and double refraction** — a stone that splits cleanly along true planes and, uniquely, shows a doubled image through its body. The "square-cleaving" name encodes the mineralogical fact as a kind of signature: a stone of *clarity, order, and clean division*. Energetically the Codex groups it with the cold, heavy, sinking stones whose office is to **cool and clear** — the mineral weight that settles and drains the body's excess fire, close kin to gypsum. Its double-refracting clarity (the property that made Iceland spar a scientific curiosity and, by legend, a navigator's "sunstone") lends it a secondary symbolism of *clear seeing and true division* — the stone that clears heat and the stone that clarifies vision, a fitting minor doctrine for a cooling mineral of the clear spar.

### Active Components

- **Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃)** — the mineral itself; a poorly-soluble carbonate whose two established pharmacological actions are (1) **acid-neutralising** (antacid — carbonate reacts with stomach acid) and (2) a source of **calcium**. Calcite and aragonite are the two common crystalline forms of CaCO₃; calcite is the stable rhombohedral form.
- **Calcium ions and trace minerals** — modest soluble calcium given low solubility; variable trace elements by source. The precise basis of its traditional systemic "heat-clearing" action (as with gypsum) is not well-established pharmacologically.

**Primary domain: RESET — a cooling, heat-clearing / acid-neutralising stone medicine, gypsum-adjacent. Formula-based, short-course.**

Calcite's Kenetica role mirrors gypsum's but is minor and largely historical: a **cold, heavy heat-clearer** for febrile/Stomach-heat patterns within formula, and — by its carbonate chemistry — a simple **antacid** for acid-excess stomach discomfort. ### Scientific Correlation

*Attribution: calcite's identity and antacid action are established mineral/chemistry facts; its traditional systemic heat-clearing use has essentially no controlled clinical literature as an isolated agent — stated honestly.*

Calcite is unambiguously **calcium carbonate**, whose **antacid** (acid-neutralising) action and role as a **calcium source** are textbook chemistry and pharmacology (calcium carbonate is a standard OTC antacid and calcium supplement). Its traditional Chinese use as a **cold, fire-draining stone** parallels gypsum but rests on humoral-energetic theory and long tradition rather than on controlled trials, and — as with gypsum — the mechanism by which a poorly-soluble mineral would produce a systemic antipyretic effect is **not established**. This entry grades calcite as **chemically well-defined (with genuine antacid/calcium activity) but lacking single-agent clinical evidence for its traditional heat-clearing indication**, and notes the real historical confusion between calcite and gypsum in the materia medica. No efficacy statistics are asserted, because none are verifiable for the isolated mineral.

### Contraindications

- **Cold / deficiency patterns; Stomach cold; no true heat:** contraindicated — a cold-draining mineral only for genuine heat/excess.
- **Weak digestion:** heavy-cold nature is hard on weak Spleen/Stomach; use in formula, short-course.
- **Antacid interactions/excess:** as calcium carbonate, excessive antacid use can cause hypercalcaemia, milk-alkali syndrome, and rebound acid; it also binds/interferes with the absorption of some drugs (e.g. certain antibiotics, iron, thyroid hormone) — separate dosing.
- **Identity confusion:** historically confused/substituted with gypsum — verify the actual mineral.
- **Evidence honesty:** single-agent clinical data for the traditional heat-clearing use are lacking; the robust facts are its antacid/calcium chemistry.
