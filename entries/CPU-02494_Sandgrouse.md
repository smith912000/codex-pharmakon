# Sandgrouse — قطا (*qaṭā*, as printed in both texts)

## *Sandgrouse: the flesh of the sandgrouse, a ground bird of dry country (Pterocles species; the Arabic name qaṭā is conventionally the sandgrouse; species not fixed)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM, TBN

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Two-text entry.** Named by Avicenna's *Canon* and by Ibn Qayyim's *al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī* among the texts on disk, and by no others here.

**Avicenna (CM).** Locus: *Canon* Book II, entry *qaṭā* (قطا), Arabic text line 17732 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; the entry is lines 17733-17735. There is no description line (the *māhiyya* line just above the heading, line 17731, belongs to the previous entry). Temperament: "weak in heat, severe in dryness" (ضعيف الحرارة شديد اليبوسة; lines 17733-17734). Digestive organs: it "benefits dropsy" (ينفع من الاستسقاء). Excretory: it "benefits from diarrhoea" (ينفع من الاستطلاق; lines 17734-17735).

**Ibn Qayyim (TBN).** Locus: *al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī*, entry *laḥm al-qaṭā* (لحم القطا), Arabic text line 6689 in `Sources/TBN_Prophetic/text/IbnQayyim_TibbNabawi_OpenITI_Shamela0023649.txt`, running to line 6690, within the discussion of the flesh of birds. "The flesh of the sandgrouse: dry; it generates black bile (*sawdāʾ*), and it holds the nature (the bowel); it is among the worst foods, except that it benefits dropsy" (يابس، يولد السوداء، ويحبس الطبع، وهو من شر الغذاء، إلا أنه ينفع من الاستسقاء; lines 6689-6690). No hadith is cited and none is graded.

**Where they agree.** Both call it dry and both say it benefits dropsy (*istisqāʾ*) and binds the bowel (Avicenna: diarrhoea; Ibn Qayyim: "holds the nature"). They differ in tone: Avicenna lists it as a remedy; Ibn Qayyim calls it one of the worst foods and gives dropsy as the exception.

**Identification.** The modern identification (sandgrouse, *Pterocles*) is the conventional one for *qaṭā*; the texts give no description, so species and certainty cannot be improved. Moderate confidence.

### Active Components

The texts give no composition. Sandgrouse flesh is lean game-bird meat; nothing more can be stated without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only: a game bird's flesh as a food-medicine for dropsy and diarrhoea, rated poor food by Ibn Qayyim. Not a recommended practice; sandgrouse are wild birds, and neither text offers a protocol.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** "Weak in heat, severe in dryness" (*Canon* Book II, entry *qaṭā*, lines 17733-17734). **Prophetic medicine (TBN).** "Dry"; generates black bile; holds the bowel (*al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī*, lines 6689-6690).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The *Canon* entry is a remedy for dropsy and diarrhoea. It carries no cultivation status.

**Prophetic medicine (TBN).** *(c) Absent.* Ibn Qayyim's note is a dietetic judgment ("among the worst foods") with no hadith, no grading and no *sunna* reference. It falls under food and drink in the six necessary things as ordinary regimen only.

**Practitioner's note.** The only guidance both texts give is a caution: a dry, binding bird that Ibn Qayyim ranks poorly and that Avicenna keeps for dropsy. Nothing supports it as a practice for cultivation.

### Contraindications

Both texts imply a drying, binding food (black-bile in Ibn Qayyim's terms), so caution in constipation. Sandgrouse are wild and in places protected; legal status varies. [USER: verify] Wild game carries ordinary food-hygiene risks.
