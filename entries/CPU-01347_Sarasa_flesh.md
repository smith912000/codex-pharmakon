# Sarasa flesh — Sarasa (also printed S'arasa and Sdrasa), flesh of a bird of the Plava family, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the bird Bhishagratna prints as "Sarasa" in the Plava group of Sutrasthana ch.46; the spine list glosses it "crane", and the Vol. 3 glossary entry that appears to belong to it reads "An Indian crane"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Sarasa is named in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 251 lists "Sarasa (crane)", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1"). The chapter divides animals of marshy places (*Anupa*) into five groups, the second being the *Plavas* (divers or swimmers) (lines 659-664). The group is defined: "Birds such as the Hansa, Sarasa, Kraimcha, Chakravaka, Kurura (belong also to the Prasaha group) Kadamva, Karandava, Jivan Jivaka, Vaka, Valaka, Pimdarika, Plava, Sarari-mukha, Nandimukha, Madgu, Utkrosha, Kachaksha, Mallikaksha, Shuklaksha, Pushkarashayika, Konalaka, Ambukukkutika, Megharava and Shvetacharana etc. belong to the Plava family. These birds are found to move about in large flocks" (lines 732-745; the names are tidied from the OCR). The group text: "The flesh of any one of this family is cooling, demulcent, and spermatopoietic and destroys the deranged Vayu. It proves beneficial in cases of haemoptysis, is sweet in taste and of digestion, and is possessed of laxative and diuretic properties" (lines 747-751). The chapter then takes one member singly, the Hansa (lines 751-756); for the Sarasa it gives nothing by name.

Other places. In *Uttarasthana* ch.39 (fever), after listing venisons that "some authorities" would allow a fever patient who is used to animal food, the text continues: "Several authorities, however, do not recommend the use of the meat of Sarasa, Krouncha, Mayura (peacock), Kukkuta (cock) and of Tittira in cases of fever, owing to its heaviness (as regards digestion), as well as to its heat-making potency. (We, too, subscribe to this opinion with a certain limitation) The use of the flesh of these animals may, however, be recommended in a case of fever in moderate quantity and at proper time provided the fever is marked by a preponderance of the deranged bodily Vayu" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt` lines 908-918; "Sarasa" is printed "Sdrasa" in the OCR, and "Mayura" "Mayuta"). This is the only line found that speaks of the Sarasa's flesh by name. The ch.46 after-drinks name the drink for the Plava family: "Asava of sugar-cane (vinegar) should be taken after having eaten the flesh of an animal of the Plava (diving) family" (ch.46 lines 2864-2866).

**Identity.** The text gives no gloss for the Sarasa in the passages read. The spine list gives "crane". The Vol. 3 glossary has, in an OCR table with the entries and glosses in separate columns, an entry printed "Ssirasa (Ardea Siberice)" and, by column order, the gloss "An Indian crane" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` lines 30922 and 30956); the Latin name is printed as OCR'd and was not checked. The Uttara ch.39 list places the Sarasa beside the Krouncha, which Kalpa ch.1 glosses "heron" (`SS_Kalpa_ch01.txt` line 176). Which bird is meant is not settled by the lines read. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Bird flesh is muscle meat: protein, with fat and iron that vary with the bird and what it eats. Wild water birds can carry avian influenza viruses, *Salmonella* and parasites. No analysis specific to this entry was read, and the bird's identity is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text records the Sarasa as a food bird of the water-bird family, and records a physicians' dispute about whether its meat suits a fever patient because it is heavy and heating. Nothing in the sources supports taking it as a medicine. Cranes are protected in many countries, and several crane species are threatened. The Codex does not recommend the flesh for any complaint and gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation or accompaniment the lines read give is the drink after the meal: sugar-cane *asava* after the flesh of an animal of the Plava family (ch.46 lines 2864-2866). Uttara ch.39 allows the flesh of the Sarasa and others only "in moderate quantity and at proper time" in a fever marked by *Vayu* (lines 915-918). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Wild birds, especially water birds, can carry avian influenza and foodborne bacteria; poultry and game birds must be cooked through. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 for the *Plava* group as a whole (lines 747-751): cooling; demulcent; spermatopoietic; destroys deranged *Vayu*; beneficial in haemoptysis; sweet in taste and in digestion; laxative and diuretic. In Uttara ch.39 the flesh of the Sarasa is called heavy and heat-making in potency by the authorities the text cites, and Sushruta's author says "We, too, subscribe to this opinion with a certain limitation" (lines 908-918). The two statements differ, group "cooling" in ch.46 against "heat-making" in ch.39; the lines read do not reconcile them. No taste (*rasa*) is printed for the Sarasa by name. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Sarasa is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and the *ojas* principle (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food in general, not about this flesh. The Plava group is called "spermatopoietic" (line 748), a property listed in a food group and not a placement in a *vajikarana* chapter, so it is not read as a *vajikarana* status. The only by-name remark, in Uttara ch.39, is a caution. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for the Sarasa. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta the Sarasa is one of many water birds eaten as food, and the one thing said of it by name is that its flesh is heavy and heating and best avoided in fever unless *Vayu* predominates. A person who does not eat birds has nothing to adopt.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** the bird is unglossed in the passages read and rests on a list gloss ("crane") and an OCR glossary; do not substitute a species on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Food safety:** wild water birds can carry avian influenza, *Salmonella* and parasites; cook through.
- **The text's own caution:** heavy and heat-making in fever, allowed only in moderate quantity and when *Vayu* predominates (Uttara ch.39).
- **Legal and conservation status:** cranes and most wild water birds are protected in many countries, and some crane species are endangered. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing a bird for food is a moral question for many traditions, and meat is restricted in some; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
