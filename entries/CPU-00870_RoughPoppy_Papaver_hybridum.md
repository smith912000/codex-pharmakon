# Rough Poppy / Round Prickly-headed Poppy / Bristly Poppy / Pavot hybride / Amapola híbrida

## *Papaver hybridum* L. | Family: Papaveraceae

**Tradition Tags:** EP, AT, DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Cool, moist, mildly narcotic in the Greco-Arabic reading of the field-poppy group (Avicenna: cold in the second/third degree). Sedating, softening, quieting. **Classification inherited from the group, not established for this species.**

**Training Phase:** Reference / Botanical Discrimination — **not a working drug**

---

### ⚠️ THIS IS NOT OPIUM POPPY. IT ALSO IS NOT, QUITE, ANYTHING ELSE.

Two errors are available here and they run in opposite directions. The first is to see a red poppy and think opium. The second, in overcorrecting, is to treat *P. hybridum* as an established mild sedative because *P. rhoeas* is one. **Both are wrong, and the second is the one this entry is written to prevent.**

| | **THIS ENTRY** | Opium Poppy |
|---|---|---|
| Binomial | *Papaver hybridum* | *P. somniferum* |
| Flower | Small, deep crimson-scarlet, black basal blotch | Large; white/lilac/pink/red |
| Capsule | **Small, rounded-globose, densely covered in stiff ascending bristles** | Large, smooth, glaucous |
| Latex | Sparse, white | Copious white latex — **the drug** |
| Alkaloid class | **Rhoeadine-type**, protopine, aporphine, proaporphine | **Morphinan** — morphine, codeine, thebaine |
| Opioid activity | **None demonstrated** | The defining one |
| Legal status | Unregulated weed | Internationally controlled |

**There is no morphine here.** The rhoeadine-type alkaloids that characterise the field-poppy group are structurally unrelated to the morphinans and have no established µ-opioid activity. The bristly, round, un-milkable capsule is the field diagnostic: **if the seed-head is a spiny ball, it is not an opium poppy, and if you are trying to score latex off it you will get nothing but scratches.**

**The congener problem, stated up front.** *P. rhoeas* (corn poppy), *P. dubium* (long-headed poppy) and *P. argemone* (prickly long-headed poppy) are already in the codex. This entry adds the fourth of the European red field-poppy set, and it must be honest about what that addition is worth: **the species-specific evidence for *P. hybridum* is essentially absent.** Everything below that looks like pharmacology is congener-based, and the entry says so at every point rather than once in a footnote.

### Origin & Tradition

**Ebers Papyrus (EP) — the Egyptian poppy question [USER: verify]**

The Ebers corpus contains preparations that later commentators have read as poppy — most famously the remedy for quieting a crying child, glossed in some translations as involving poppy capsules with honey. **This identification is contested and the species behind it is unrecoverable.** *P. somniferum* was known in the Eastern Mediterranean by the second millennium BCE and is the far likelier referent for any genuinely narcotic Egyptian poppy preparation. *P. hybridum* does occur around the Mediterranean basin and into North Africa as a weed of cultivation, which makes its physical presence in Pharaonic Egypt plausible — **but presence is not attestation.** No Egyptian text identifies this species. The EP tag here marks **the poppy category's antiquity in Egyptian medicine, not a claim about *P. hybridum*.** **[USER: verify against the Bryan/Joachim translations; consider whether the EP tag survives.]**

**Assyrian Tablets (AT) — *ḫul gil* [USER: verify]**

The Nineveh corpus's ***ḫul gil*** ("joy plant") is a long-standing and probably correct identification of the opium poppy, and the Sumerian-derived name carries the affective claim — this is a plant that changes mood. The Assyrian pharmacy plainly had a narcotic poppy. **It plainly did not have *P. hybridum* as a distinct entity**, or if it did, no surviving name marks it out from the poppy category. The AT tag is a category tag. **[USER: verify.]**

This pattern — real ancient poppy tradition, unrecoverable species — repeats across all four tags on this entry. **State it once, honour it throughout: the field poppies inherited a doctrine built on their dangerous cousin.**

**De Materia Medica (DMM) — μήκων ῥοιάς / *mēkōn rhoias*, the wild/field poppy**

Here the ground firms up, but only slightly. Dioscorides distinguishes the poppies, and treats a **wild or "erratic" poppy** — *mēkōn rhoias* — separately from the cultivated somniferous one. The field poppy's indications are the mild end of the poppy doctrine: **a decoction of the heads or petals taken in wine or as a syrup to induce sleep, to soothe cough and hoarseness, and to quiet the restless.** It is not given for surgical anaesthesia, not for the dying, not for the mania that black hellebore claims. It is the **domestic, low-stakes poppy** — the one you give a coughing child at night.

*Mēkōn rhoias* is conventionally read as *P. rhoeas*, and the Greek name is the direct etymological source of that binomial. **There is no reason to think Dioscorides had *P. hybridum* in view as a separate drug.** What he had was a category — *the red field poppies* — inside which *hybridum*, *dubium* and *argemone* were, and remained for the next eighteen centuries, invisible.

**Canon of Medicine (CM) — *khashkhāsh barrī* (wild poppy)**

Avicenna, working in the four-degree system, classifies the poppies by cooling power. The cultivated black poppy (opium) sits at the extreme — cold in the fourth degree, stupefying, dangerous, a drug of last resort with explicit warnings about respiratory arrest. **The wild poppy is placed markedly lower** — cooling, soporific, indicated in catarrh, cough, insomnia and heat of the head, given as syrup, and treated as safe enough for ordinary domestic use.

This is the doctrinally interesting move, and it deserves credit: **the Arabic system correctly ranked the two poppies on a single axis, and correctly placed the field poppy far from the opium poppy on it, purely on observed effect.** It did this without any concept of a receptor, without knowing that the two plants make chemically unrelated alkaloids. The degree system was, in this instance, doing real work — it encoded a genuine pharmacological discontinuity as a difference of magnitude on a continuous scale. **That is also its limitation:** it encoded a *categorical* difference (morphinan vs. rhoeadine; opioid vs. not-opioid) as a *quantitative* one, which implies — falsely — that enough wild poppy would eventually do what opium does. It will not. The scale was right about the ordering and wrong about the topology.

**European vernacular — the field-poppy syrup**

The most concrete tradition attaching to this group is the **red poppy syrup**: petals or capsules infused, sugared, given for night cough and disturbed sleep, especially to children. It persisted into the twentieth century across France, Spain, Italy, the Balkans and Anatolia, and it survives in the pharmacopoeial use of *P. rhoeas* petals (*Rhoeados flos*) as a colouring and mild adjuvant. Çoban et al. record it directly and contemporaneously: *P. rhoeas* "is used to make a cough syrup for children, as a tea for disturbed sleep, for pain relief and as a sedative in folk medicine" in Turkey ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/13880209.2017.1340964), PMID 28633584).

**Whether *P. hybridum* was ever swept into this syrup is a question no source answers.** Given that it grows in the same cornfields, flowers at the same time, and is red — almost certainly yes, incidentally and unrecorded. That is the honest position: **inclusion by proximity, not by selection.**

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

The poppy doctrine is **quieting** — the drug that stops a thing that will not stop. Cough that will not settle. A child that will not sleep. Pain that will not let go. Every tradition on this entry reads the poppies through the same lens, and the field poppy is the doctrine's gentle end: **the same power, less of it.**

**Map that onto real chemistry and it breaks — cleanly, and instructively.**

The doctrine's implicit claim is a **potency gradient**: opium is the strong poppy, the field poppy is the weak poppy, and they differ in degree. The chemistry says they differ in **kind**. Çoban et al.'s isolation from *P. rhoeas* aerial parts recovers twelve alkaloids across five structural classes — proaporphine (mecambrine), aporphine (roemerine), promorphinan (salutaridine), protopine (protopine, coulteropine), and **seven rhoeadine-types** (rhoeadine, rhoeagenine, isorhoeadine, isorhoeagenine, glaucamine, glaudine, epiglaucamine) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/13880209.2017.1340964), PMID 28633584). Not one is a morphinan. Salutaridine is a **promorphinan** — a biosynthetic waypoint on the road to morphine — and its presence tells you the field poppies possess part of the pathway and **do not complete it**. The gradient the doctrine imagined is real as a metabolic map and false as a pharmacological one.

So what was the syrup doing? Three candidate answers, and the honest one is that we do not know which:

1. **A real, mild, non-opioid sedation** from the rhoeadine/protopine/aporphine set — plausible, unestablished.
2. **A demulcent effect** — mucilage and sugar coating an irritated throat, which is most of what any cough syrup has ever done, and which requires no alkaloid at all.
3. **Placebo plus night-time plus a parent who has decided the child will now sleep.**

**The doctrine cannot distinguish these, and neither can the literature.** What the doctrine *did* get right is the **direction and the ceiling** — it never claimed the field poppy could do what opium does, never used it for surgery, never described dependence. A tradition that consistently declines to over-claim is telling you something true about the drug: **there was never enough there to over-claim about.** The strongest evidence for the field poppy's mildness is eighteen centuries of nobody getting addicted to it.

**The signature that isn't.** Red flower, red blood, battlefield poppies — the symbolic freight is enormous and post-hoc. Nothing in the anthocyanin pigment is pharmacologically relevant, and the pharmacopoeial use of *Rhoeados flos* is explicitly as a **colourant**. The most culturally loaded feature of this plant is the one with the least chemistry behind it. **Deflate it.**

### Active Components

**Stated honestly: essentially nothing is published on *P. hybridum* specifically.** The single retrieval touching it directly — Nazari et al. (2025), a phytochemical and molecular survey of Iranian *Papaver* species — reports interspecific variation in phenolics, flavonoids, anthocyanins, codeine and papaverine across the genus, but **the species-level assignments in the retrieved record are not resolvable to *P. hybridum* with confidence** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2025.1635867), PMID 40901552). It establishes that Iranian *Papaver* species differ chemically. It does not tell us what *hybridum* contains.

**Expected, by congener inference (NOT species-verified):**

- **Rhoeadine-type alkaloids** — rhoeadine, rhoeagenine, isorhoeadine, isorhoeagenine, glaucamine, glaudine. The chemotaxonomic marker of the section. Documented in *P. rhoeas* (PMID 28633584) and as major alkaloids in *P. armeniacum* (Phillipson et al. 1981, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2007-971686), PMID 17401828).
- **Protopine-type** — protopine, coulteropine, cryptopine.
- **Aporphine / proaporphine** — roemerine, mecambrine, nuciferine, pronuciferine.
- **Promorphinan** — salutaridine. **Present; does not confer opioid activity.**
- **Anthocyanins** — cyanidin glycosides, the red pigment.
- **Absent** — morphine, codeine as major constituents; **no morphinan pharmacology.**

**The chemical-race warning.** Phillipson et al. demonstrated that within a single *Papaver* species there exist **distinct chemical races** with entirely different dominant alkaloid classes — *P. fugax* and *P. armeniacum* each showing at least three races (benzyltetrahydroisoquinoline-, proaporphine/aporphine-, morphinane-, or rhoeadine-dominant), and *P. tauricola* at least two ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2007-971686), PMID 17401828). **This is the single most important fact in this section.** It means that even if someone published a *P. hybridum* alkaloid profile tomorrow, one population's profile would not license claims about another's. Congener inference in this genus is not merely weak — **the genus is actively documented as hostile to it.**

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Not a working drug. Reference status only.**

Retained for:

1. **Discrimination.** *P. hybridum* is the red field poppy with the **round bristly capsule**. Learning the four-species set (*rhoeas* smooth-globose, *dubium* long smooth, *argemone* long bristly, *hybridum* round bristly) is the practical content of this entry, and it matters because "red poppy" is not an identification.
2. **The kind-vs-degree case study.** Avicenna's degree system encoding a categorical pharmacological discontinuity as a quantitative one is a clean, teachable failure of an otherwise excellent framework.
3. **The congener-inference stress test.** This is the codex's best example of a species where the honest answer is *we do not know*, and where the genus's documented chemical races make the usual inferential shortcut illegitimate rather than merely uncertain.

**If a field-poppy preparation is wanted, the species with an actual evidence base is *P. rhoeas*** — and that base is thin. There is no reason to reach for *hybridum* and no basis on which to.

### Scientific Correlation

**Headline grade: VERY LOW / DATA-SPARSE. The case for *P. hybridum* is congener-based throughout, and this section's main finding is a null.**

**1. Species-specific evidence for *P. hybridum*: essentially NULL.**
This literature search retrieved **one** record touching *P. hybridum* by name — a phytochemical/molecular diversity survey of Iranian *Papaver* (Nazari et al. 2025, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2025.1635867), PMID 40901552) — and **zero** studies of *P. hybridum* pharmacology, sedation, cough, toxicity, or clinical use of any kind. **Grade: NULL.** Not "weak evidence". **No evidence.** This is the entry's central finding and it should not be softened: *P. hybridum* is a plant with a traditional category membership and no science.

**2. The traditional sedative/cough use — attested ethnographically, unvalidated pharmacologically, and only for the congener.**
Çoban et al. (2017) document the folk use directly — *P. rhoeas* as children's cough syrup, sleep tea, sedative, in Turkey and northern Cyprus — and isolated twelve alkaloids from eleven wild populations, finding significant **antimicrobial** activity (best extract: MIC 1.22 µg/mL vs *S. aureus*, 2.4 µg/mL vs *C. albicans*, driven by roemerine) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/13880209.2017.1340964), PMID 28633584). **Grade for the ethnography: GOOD. Grade for sedative efficacy: NULL — the study tested antimicrobial activity, not sedation.** This is worth pausing on: the best-designed study of the field poppy retrieved here documents the sedative tradition in its own introduction and then **does not test it**. The antimicrobial result, while real, is orthogonal to every traditional indication. **The tradition's central claim remains untested even in the well-studied congener.**

**3. Rhoeadine alkaloids are real and are the group's marker — established, mechanistically unexplained.**
Rhoeadine-type alkaloids are confirmed as major constituents in *P. rhoeas* (PMID 28633584) and *P. armeniacum* (PMID 17401828). **Grade: STRONG for occurrence in the section, INFERRED for *P. hybridum*, NULL for mechanism.** I retrieved **no** study establishing a receptor target, a CNS action, or any pharmacodynamic account of rhoeadine. **The molecule that the entire "mild sedation" story rests on has no known mechanism.** That is not a hedge; it is the state of the field.

**4. Deflating the "poppy = opioid" doctrine — the strongest honest claim available.**
The alkaloid isolations above establish the field poppies as rhoeadine/protopine/aporphine plants with **salutaridine as their furthest advance toward the morphinans, and no further** (PMID 28633584). **Grade: STRONG.** This is the one thing this entry can say with confidence, and it is a negative: **the field poppy is not a weak opium, it is a different plant that happens to be red.** Avicenna's degree-ordering was right; his implied continuity was wrong.

**5. Miscellaneous congener bioactivity — real, and irrelevant to every traditional indication.**
*P. rhoeas* flower extract shows antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity in vitro with no cytotoxicity to skin cells, in a study whose actual subject is **cosmetic dyes** (Bujak et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27030922), PMID 35164187). *P. rhoeas* ovule extracts show cytotoxicity against HL60 leukaemia cells (Ovidi et al. 2020, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules25081850), PMID 32316453). **Grade: LOW, and non-transferable in both directions** — wrong species, wrong plant part, wrong indication. Neither speaks to cough or sleep. Cited to close them off, not to open them.

**6. What the poppy literature is actually about, and why that matters here.**
The bulk of modern *P. rhoeas* research is **agronomic**: it is Europe's most damaging broadleaf arable weed, and the literature is dominated by herbicide resistance — Koreki et al. sequenced 21,100 plants across 422 French populations, finding 24 resistance-endowing ALS alleles of multiple independent origins ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ps.7791), PMID 37752099). **Grade: N/A — but diagnostically important.** The field poppies are studied intensively as weeds and almost not at all as medicines. **The silence around *P. hybridum*'s pharmacology is not an accident of my search; it is the shape of the field.** Nobody is looking, because there is no reason to.

**Honest summary.** *P. hybridum* is a real plant, in a real traditional category, with real congeners that have real alkaloids and one weak, untested indication. **The species itself is a blank.** This entry documents the blank rather than filling it.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian (EP).** The Ebers corpus is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint — a crying child quieted, a cough soothed — not what a substance does to the *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or the *ib* as seat of judgement, and it assigns no vital-constitutional status to any poppy. Even the poppy identification here is a modern hypothesis over an unrecoverable species, and the operative *heka* — the recitation that would have accompanied any genuine prescription — is not attached to *P. hybridum* by any text. There is no cultivation framing to report, and it would be doubly invented: once for the tradition, once for a species Egypt never named. **(c) absent.**

**Assyrian (AT).** The Nineveh pharmacy classified illness by causal agency — the hand of a god, a ghost, a broken oath — so a drug is partly defined by which agency it opposes; but the poppy the *asû* and *āšipu* knew is *ḫul gil*, the opium poppy, and no surviving name marks *P. hybridum* out from that category. Under the standing blocking warning in force for every Assyrian substance identification, deity attribution and tablet reference, no status of any kind is claimed for this species. **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides treats *mēkōn rhoias*, the wild or erratic poppy, as a mild soporific for sleep, cough and hoarseness, conventionally read as *P. rhoeas*; he carries no subtle-body system that could assign a cultivation status, and there is no reason to think he had *hybridum* in view as a distinct drug at all. What the tradition offers a cultivator is his discipline of attention — distinguishing the field poppies by capsule and habit, judging the wild from the cultivated by observed effect rather than lore. That is a real practice of discrimination, and it is exactly the practice this entry is built around; it is not a vital-body doctrine. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** Avicenna places the wild poppy (*khashkhāsh barrī*) cold and soporific, well below the cultivated black poppy on the four-degree scale, safe enough for domestic use in catarrh, cough and heat of the head. The strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus is the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture — a finite vital endowment whose depletion *is* ageing — but that is a longevity frame for the whole regimen, and it is not a status the tradition assigns to a cooling narcotic; no *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed, and even the therapeutic classification is inherited by the field-poppy category rather than established for this species. **(b) framed:** attested therapeutically at category level, no cultivation status sourced.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a substance a cultivator takes to build or conserve anything. Across all four tags *P. hybridum* is not even worked as a drug — it is a category member that inherited a doctrine built on its dangerous cousin and was never separately tested against it. The one honest practice the traditions transmit is Dioscorides's: learn to tell the four red field poppies apart by their seed-heads, and do not mistake a bristly ball of a capsule for opium. That is discrimination, not cultivation. There is no longevity use, no vitalising use, and no defined therapeutic course to take and then stop — because there is no species-specific ground to stand one on.

### Contraindications

**General:** *P. hybridum* is **not recommended for internal use in any person**, on the grounds of absent data rather than demonstrated harm. The distinction matters: this is not a poison. It is an unknown.

- **Alkaloid-bearing foliage — caution.** The aerial parts carry isoquinoline alkaloids of unquantified concentration. Protopine-type alkaloids have known biological activity; the total alkaloid load of this species is unmeasured. **Do not treat the foliage as inert vegetable matter.** Do not eat as a wild green.
- **Children.** The tradition's core use was paediatric, which is precisely why this is the population to withhold it from. **A traditional child indication is a reason for more scrutiny, not less** — see I_761 (*Helleborus foetidus*), where the paediatric tradition was the lethal one.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding.** Avoid. No data.
- **Chemical-race variability.** Documented across the genus (PMID 17401828). A preparation from one population is not the same drug as one from another. This alone forecloses any dosing scheme.
- **CNS depressants, alcohol, sedatives.** Theoretical additive risk. Unquantified — which is the problem, not a reassurance.
- **Identification.** Round bristly capsule. If the capsule is smooth and glaucous and the plant is milky, you have *P. somniferum* and a legal problem in most jurisdictions.
- **Skin/eye.** Latex may irritate.

**No overdose syndrome is documented for this species, and no reassurance should be drawn from that.** No poisoning literature exists because no one uses it, not because it has been shown safe.
