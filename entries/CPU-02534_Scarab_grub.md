# Scarab grub — 蛴螬 *Qicao* (Shennong bencao jing), also 蛴蛴 (as printed)
## *The white grub, the larva of a scarab beetle, in the modern identification Holotrichia diomphalia Bates, the Chinese cockchafer grub (Scarabaeidae); of medium confidence, since other Holotrichia or Anomala larvae are also cited and the classic gives no description* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The identification is ours: the Shennong paragraph names no animal features, and the Mingyi layer gives only a habitat (manure and straw heaps).

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the middle (中) classic, reads 味咸，微温 ("salty, slightly warm"). It governs 恶血 ("malign blood"), 血瘀 ("blood stasis"; the Yulan has 血瘴), 痹气 ("obstructed qi"), 破折 and "blood below the ribs, hard, full and painful" (血在胁下坚满痛), "closed menses" (月闭), and in the eye 淫肤 ("spreading skin") and 青翳白膜 ("blue/green opacity, white membrane"). It gives an alias that begins with a character lost in the transcription (一名 followed by a blank, 蛴), and the habitat 生平泽, "grows in level marshes". There is no 久服 clause. The text prints no toxicity statement. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 蛴螬 *qicao*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2701)

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 adds the aliases 齐 and 勃齐 (the first character of one is lost), and the habitat and harvest: 生河内人家积粪草中，取无时，反行者，良: "lives in heaps of manure and straw in people's homes in Henei; taken at no set time; those that walk backwards are good".

**Sun's note (案).** Sun cites Shuowen (partly illegible in the transcription), which glosses the name together with 蝤 and 蝎, names of wood-dwelling grubs; the Guangya list of names (including 地蚕, "earth silkworm"), and the Erya with Guo Pu, who says the grub is "in manure and earth", while the 蝤蛴 (a similar word) is "in wood". The Fangyan lists regional names (east of the pass, Liang-Yi, Qin-Jin; they include 蠹 and 天蝼). The Liezi, Tianrui chapter, says "the root of the *wuzu* becomes the *qicao*", and the Bowuzhi says the grub "moves on its back, faster than with its feet". Sun adds that Shuowen has no entry for the character, so a borrowed character is used.

**Identity.** The animal in the Mingyi layer lives in manure and straw, which fits a soil/compost white grub of a scarab, the usual modern reading. The commentaries, however, run the name together with wood-boring grubs (蝤蛴). Our identification (*Holotrichia diomphalia*) is the modern drug's species and is of medium confidence. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The text prints no constituent. In general terms a beetle larva is chiefly water, protein and fat, with chitin in the cuticle. Soil grubs from manure carry bacteria and parasites. Nothing more specific is stated without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice, and not an animal to be handled or taken for these purposes. The classic records a salty, slightly warm drug for "malign blood", stasis, closed menses, pain below the ribs and eye opacities. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends none. Eye opacities, blocked menses and pain below the ribs all need medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives no preparation, part or dose. The only practical statement is the Mingyi layer's "taken at no set time; those that walk backwards are good". No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

In general knowledge the larvae of scarab beetles are soil-dwelling pests of crops, and the larvae of *Holotrichia* are traditionally dried for use in Chinese pharmacy. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 蛴螬 in the middle grade (中品); its preface says middle-grade drugs nourish the nature, and some are toxic. The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise; it lists blood-related complaints, menses and the eye. The Mingyi bielu, not the Shennong text, gives the manure habitat and the "walk backwards" rule. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 蛴螬 *qicao*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2701)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The middle grade carries the classic's own note that some drugs in it are toxic, though this paragraph states none. The Codex does not recommend taking the animal.

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity and hygiene.** The Shennong paragraph states no toxicity, but the middle grade is stated by the classic to include toxic drugs, and a grub from manure carries bacteria and parasites. Do not take it.
- **Menses and pregnancy.** The text lists "blood stasis" and "closed menses" among its targets; no pregnancy information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics.** The Codex does not recommend harvesting animals for these uses.
