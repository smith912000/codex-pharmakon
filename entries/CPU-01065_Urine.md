# Urine (various animals) — Peri Ouron (Urine) / the Urine Group of the Sushruta Samhita / Cow urine, Human urine in the rGyud-bzhi
## *Urine of cow, buffalo, goat, ewe, mule, horse, elephant, ass, camel and man, as the three texts name them* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM, SS, FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 2-99, *Peri Ouron*, headed in the translation "Urine — CAUTION" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 5377). The chapter opens with a man's own water: "A man's own water (taken as a drink) is an antidote against viper bites, deadly medicines and dropsy as it begins. And it is applied with hot cloths for the bites of sea vipers, sea scorpions, and sea dragons [2-15]." It continues with dogs' urine as "a warm pack for those bitten by mad dogs", and, with saltpetre, to clean leprosy and itchiness. "Older urine is a better cleanser for achor [scaly eruption of the scalp], dandruff, psoriasis and hot eruptions", represses gangrenous ulcerations, is put into purulent ears, and is boiled in a pomegranate rind "to dry out worms in the ears". "The urine of an incorrupt boy (sipped) is good for asthma", and, boiled in brass with honey, cleans the scar of a healed wound, small white corneal ulcers and dim vision; the same urine with Cyprian brass "makes a glue for soldering gold together". Other animals: "Bull's urine pounded together with myrrh and dropped in the ears lessens earache"; boar's urine "similar", and "taken as a drink it breaks and expels stones in the bladder"; "Two cups of urine of a goat taken in a drink with spica nardi with water every day (is said) to expel urine through the bowels, and dropped in the ears it cures sores of the ears"; "Urine of an ass is said to cure inflamed kidneys". The next chapter, 2-100, is the separate "Lyncurium — Urine of a Lynx" (line 5384), which the text says is "thought" to turn into stone as it is passed and "has only a foolish report"; it is not a variety of this entry.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Bhishagratna's translation, *Sutrasthana* ch.45, which "treats of liquids", has "The Urine Group" after the groups of waters, milks, oils and gruels (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 1686-1764). It opens: "The urine of cows, buffaloes, goats, sheep, mules, horses, and camels are commonly hot and bitter, and leave a saline after-taste. They are light and are used as purifying agents, and will prove curative in diseases due to the derangement of the Vayu or Kapham, as well as in worms in the intestines, obesity, poisoning, abdominal glands, piles, ascites, cutaneous affections, oedema, non-relish for food, and jaundice. In general they act as cardiac stimulants (Hridyam) and appetisers." A footnote at line 1702 reads: "The urine of a cow, she-buffalo, ewe and she-goat should be taken and used, while similar secretions of the male should be taken where man, camel and elephant should be indicated." The verses then restate the group as "pungent, strong, hot, light" with a saline after-taste: blood-purifiers, "reduce corpulency, act as vermifuges and are anti-toxic", subduing deranged *Vayu* and *Kapham* (the English prints "hot and bitter" in the prose and "pungent" in the verses; the two are as Bhishagratna printed them). The individual urines follow in the varieties below. Cow urine also appears in a *rasayana* recipe in *Chikitsasthana* ch.28, the chapter on elixirs "which tend to improve the memory and invigorate the mental faculties as well as to increase the duration of human life" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch28.txt` lines 12 and 43-55): in a variant of the Avalguja recipe for *Kushtha*, jaundice and abdominal dropsy, the powdered seeds of the black Avalguja are stirred "with the urine of a cow (instead of with treacle)" and a continuous course for a month "would improve the memory and intellectual faculties of the user".

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Urine is the eleventh of the thirteen medicines of animal origin in the Explanatory Tantra ch.20, as Clark translates it (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` lines 1148 and 1196-1197): "Human urine: cures septic disorders, is anthelmintic and is prophylactic against infectious fevers. Cow urine (especially that of the superior red-brown cow): purges lymph disorders and chronic fevers." Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` lines 3561-3562): "Human urine cures infections and ein disorders. It is also a prophylactic against epidemic diseases. Cow urine cleanses chhuser disorders and cures chronic hot disorder." In the chapter on normal health (Clark ch.23; the preliminary of the rejuvenation procedure, lines 1270-1273) the purgative compound of "the three (myrobalan) fruits, rock salt, longpeper, wild ginger, Sweet Flag, turmeric, Embelia, raw cane sugar and cow's urine" is given "as an abdominal cleanser", and the text says that attempting rejuvenation "without such cleansing would be fruitless, like dyeing a stained cloth"; later "taking (Leadwort) combined with cow's urine cures leucoderma and leprosy" (line 1273; Men-Tsee-Khang lines 3918 and 3929 give the same).

### Varieties

The ten varieties from the Sushruta and Tibetan texts follow, in the order Sushruta gives them in the verses, then the Tibetan pair. Dioscorides's animals are in the paragraph after them.

**Cow urine** (SS; *Sutrasthana* ch.45 line 1716; FMT; Clark line 1197, `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 374). Sushruta: "pungent, strong and hot, and does not generate Vayu on account of its being saturated with alkali. It is light, stomachic, slightly generates the Pittam, and subdues the Vayu and Kapham." It is beneficial in colic, abdominal glands, ascites and distension, and is "used for the purposes of purging and enematas (Asthapanam)". The text adds: "In cases, which prove amenable to the use of urine, the urine of a cow should be used to the exclusion of that of any other animal, even to that of an ox" (line 1724). The Tibetan text: "especially that of the superior red-brown cow", purges lymph disorders and chronic fevers (Clark); cleanses *chhuser* disorders and cures chronic hot disorder (Men-Tsee-Khang). It is also a named ingredient in the preliminary cleanser before rejuvenation, and the vehicle with Leadwort in the Tibetan text (above), and the vehicle for black Avalguja seeds in Sushruta's *Chikitsasthana* ch.28.

**Buffalo urine** (SS; line 1727). The urine of a she-buffalo "proves beneficial in piles, abdominal dropsy, colic, cutaneous affections, Meha, imperfect action of emetics or purgatives, constipation, oedema, abdominal glands, and jaundice."

**Goat urine** (SS; line 1732). The urine of a she-goat "has a pungent, bitter taste. It slightly agitates the bodily Vayu and proves curative in cases of cough, dyspnoea, consumption, jaundice and chlorosis."

**Ewe urine** (SS; line 1735). "Contains alkali, and has a bitter pungent taste. It subdues the deranged Vayu, and is heat-making in its potency." It is beneficial in cough, enlarged spleen, abdominal dropsy, dyspnoea, consumption and obstinate constipation.

**Mule urine** (SS; line 1687). The text names mules only in the opening list of the group (with cows, buffaloes, goats, sheep, horses and camels), whose shared qualities are given above. No separate paragraph on mule urine was found.

**Horse urine** (SS; line 1744). "Appetising and pungent in taste, strong, and heat-making in its potency. It subdues the deranged Vayu and Kapham, and cures mental aberrations. It is usually recommended in cases of ringworm and worms in the intestines."

**Elephant urine** (SS; line 1749). "A bitter and saline taste. It is keen and purgative, and subdues the Vayu and enrages the Pittam. It is commonly used in the treatment of (Kilasa) leucoderma and in the preparation of alkalies."

**Donkey urine** (SS; line 1755). "The urine of an ass tends to neutralise the effects of poison generated through the chemical action of two different substances in the organism. It is strong and proves curative in cases of chronic dysentery. It is a vermifuge and subdues the Vayu and Kapham, and is appetising."

**Camel urine** (SS; line 1760). "Proves beneficial in cases of oedema, leprosy, abdominal dropsy, insanity, worms in the intestines, piles and in diseases due to the action of the deranged Vayu."

**Human urine** (SS, line 1764; FMT, Clark line 1197, `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 373). Sushruta gives one line: "Human urine is strong anti-toxic." The Tibetan text: cures septic disorders, is anthelmintic and prophylactic against infectious fevers (Clark); cures infections and *ein* disorders and is prophylactic against epidemic diseases (Men-Tsee-Khang). Dioscorides's "man's own water" and "incorrupt boy" urine are in the next paragraph.

**Dioscorides's urines** (DMM 2-99). Man's own water and a boy's, dog's, bull's, boar's, goat's and ass's urine, with the uses quoted in the Greek paragraph above. Dioscorides also notes that older urine is the better cleanser and that a boy's is the one to sip for asthma.

*Synthesis of the varieties.* The Sushruta text separates the urines by source animal, one paragraph each, with a shared opening for the group in its list (cow, buffalo, goat, sheep, mule, horse, camel) and a footnote that the female's urine is used for cow, buffalo, ewe and goat. It ranks the cow first, in words: the cow's "to the exclusion of that of any other animal". The Tibetan text names only two, human and cow, and singles out the red-brown cow as "superior". The Greek text puts a man's own urine first, then names the animals by the complaint each serves. Cow urine is the one variety named by both Sushruta and the Tibetan text, and human urine by all three; in each place the cow's is given for a reason of its own (Sushruta's alkali, the Tibetan red-brown cow), and the three texts' accounts are set side by side, not equated.

### Active Components

Mammalian urine is mostly water, with urea, creatinine, uric acid (or allantoin in some species), electrolytes (sodium, potassium, chloride, ammonium), and small amounts of other metabolites, pigments and hormones. Composition varies with species, diet, hydration and time of day. Older urine becomes more alkaline as urea breaks down to ammonia, which fits Dioscorides's remark that older urine "is a better cleanser" in the general chemical sense of that word, and Sushruta's note on cow urine being "saturated with alkali"; the texts do not say this themselves. Animal urine can also hold bacteria, parasites, and any drugs, toxins or hormones the animal has taken in. No quantities are given here because none were read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Three traditions record urine as an internal and external drug, and Sushruta and the Tibetan text give cow urine a place in purification before and within rejuvenation regimens. The Codex has no protocol for drinking or applying urine from any animal or person and none is offered. Raw animal urine is a possible route for infection (see Contraindications), no dose is given for the uses read, and some of the uses (viper bites, mad-dog bites, poison antidotes, stones, kidney inflammation) are emergencies where the folk remedy could delay real care. The Dioscorides translator's own heading "CAUTION" is kept here.

### Preparation & Dosing

Dioscorides gives preparations without a measure except one: "Two cups of urine of a goat taken in a drink with spica nardi with water every day" (2-99). Others: urine sipped (the boy's), applied with hot cloths, boiled in a pomegranate rind (ears), boiled in brass with honey (eyes), boiled with cyprinum (womb). Sushruta names urine's uses in purging and enemata (*Asthapana*, cow urine) and as vehicle for black Avalguja seeds: "half a Pala weight every morning after the sun has ceased to look red", followed in the afternoon by boiled rice with clarified butter and unsalted *Amalaka* soup, for a month (*Chikitsasthana* ch.28, lines 43-55). The Tibetan text gives the cleansing compound's ingredients but no amounts in the lines read. No other dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Urea is a well-established, mild topical keratolytic and moisturiser in dermatology, and it is the salt-free end-product of protein nitrogen in mammals; neither fact validates drinking or applying urine. Urine from a healthy person is usually near-sterile when it leaves the bladder but picks up bacteria on the way out. Animal urine can transmit leptospirosis. No clinical studies of the traditional uses were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Sutrasthana* ch.45 prints for the group: "hot and bitter" with a "saline after-taste" in the prose, and "pungent, strong, hot, light" with a "saline after-taste" in the verses (line 1686 ff.). It prints for the cow's urine "pungent, strong and hot", light, "stomachic", "slightly generates the Pittam", subduing *Vayu* and *Kapham*; for the goat "pungent, bitter"; for the ewe "bitter pungent" and "heat-making in its potency"; for the horse "pungent", "strong, and heat-making in its potency"; for the elephant "bitter and saline", "keen and purgative", "enrages the Pittam". Buffalo, camel and human urines are given by their actions only, and the donkey's is called "strong". Sushruta's category for the group is the "Urine Group" of liquids, and the text does not print a *rasa*, *virya* and *vipaka* triple for each.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** No warming or cooling quality is printed for urine; the chapter says older urine is "a better cleanser" (2-99).

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The translated lines print actions only for human and cow urine (Clark line 1197), and no taste or potency; the Men-Tsee-Khang text reads *ein* and *chhuser* and "chronic hot disorder" (lines 3561-3562).

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What chapter 2-99 offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention: the caution in its heading, the instruction that older urine is the better cleanser, which urine to use for which complaint, and the distinction of the sipped boy's urine from the others. It records and does not endorse; the lynx "urine" in 2-100 he calls a "foolish report".

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The urine group sits in *Sutrasthana* ch.45 on liquids, as purifying and *Vayu*/*Kapham*-pacifying agents, and not in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter. In *Chikitsasthana* ch.28, a *rasayana* chapter, cow urine is the vehicle for one variant of a black-Avalguja elixir; the memory, intellect and long-life claim belongs to the recipe and the course of treatment, and the text does not credit the urine itself with it. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for urine in the lines read. The sattvic and rajasic food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed.* Urine is one of the thirteen animal medicines of the Explanatory Tantra. Cow urine takes part in the abdominal cleansing that the chapter on normal health makes the first step of rejuvenation ("without such cleansing" the practice "would be fruitless"), and as the vehicle for Leadwort. Its role is preparatory and purgative and is not given as rejuvenating in itself. No essence-extraction (*bcud len*) use of urine was found in the lines read, and no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition; none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here supports drinking or applying urine for cultivation. The sources use cow urine as a purgative and cleansing agent, and the strongest sentence about it, Sushruta's "to the exclusion of that of any other animal", is a ranking among urines, not a claim about vitality. The Tibetan text puts cleansing before rejuvenation, and Dioscorides's own heading says "CAUTION". For a cultivator the useful lesson is the order of work in the Tibetan chapter: clear first, then build.

### Contraindications

- **Infection.** Animal and human urine can carry bacteria, parasites and viruses, including the agent of leptospirosis. Never put it on wounds or in the eyes or ears. [USER: verify specifics]
- **Toxicity.** Urine concentrates whatever the animal has eaten or been dosed with. Kidney and liver disease are a particular reason not to drink it.
- **Emergencies.** Viper bite, mad-dog bite, poisoning, bladder stones and inflamed kidneys, uses named in Dioscorides, need medical care.
- **Dose unknown.** Sushruta and the Tibetan text give no dose for most uses; Dioscorides gives one.
- **Pregnancy.** Dioscorides names urine in a womb remedy; no safety data was retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Legal and ethics.** Collecting urine from camels, elephants or other animals touches animal-welfare and, for protected species, conservation law.
