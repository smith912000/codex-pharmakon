# Black Poplar — Balm of Gilead (poplar-bud type) · Peuplier noir · Unguentum Populeum tree · Poplar Bud

## *Populus nigra* L. | Family: Salicaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, CM, DMM, SBJ
**Energetic Classification:** Warm and drying, resolvent and resinous; Galenic "hot in the first, dry in the second"
**Training Phase:** Foundational — Soothe & Resolve (topical anti-inflammatory)

---

### Origin & Tradition

The black poplar is a large riparian tree of European and West-Asian floodplains. Its **leaf-buds**, gathered in late winter and early spring before they open, are coated in a sticky, fragrant, amber resin — the fabled "poplar bud" or European "Balm of Gilead"-type resin. This resinous exudate, not the wood, is the classical drug; it is also the principal botanical source of European **poplar-type propolis** (bees gather the same resin from the buds).

**Assyro-Babylonian (AT):** Poplar and its relatives appear among the tree-resins and bark drugs of the cuneiform medical tablets, where resinous and astringent tree exudates were compounded into salves and poultices for inflamed swellings, wounds, and painful joints — the deep substrate from which the later Mediterranean resin-ointment tradition grew.

**Dioscorides & the Graeco-Roman stream (DMM):** In *De Materia Medica*, Dioscorides describes the poplars (*Populus*): the resinous buds and the ointment made from them were used for bruises, inflammation, and to soothe pain, and the resin was noted for its warming, resolving quality. This is the taproot of the medieval **Unguentum Populeum** ("populeon" / poplar ointment), a cooling-yet-resolvent salve of poplar buds compounded with other herbs, carried in European pharmacopoeias for centuries as a dressing for haemorrhoids, burns, and inflamed skin.

**Avicennan / Unani (CM):** Within the Graeco-Arabic synthesis the poplar bud resin was classed among warming, resolvent, mildly analgesic topicals — used in liniments and ointments for joint pain and inflammatory swellings, and internally in small amounts as an expectorant and febrifuge, consistent with its salicylate content.

**Chinese medicine (TCM):** *Populus* barks and buds enter Chinese and border-region practice among the Salicaceae "clear-heat / dispel-wind-damp" drugs, used for inflammatory pain, coughs, and skin sores — parallel logic to the willow-bark (*Salix*) analgesics of the same family.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** The poplar bud is the tree's protected, over-wintering "eye" — a concentrated resin the plant uses to seal and defend the dormant shoot against microbes and cold. Every tradition read the sticky, aromatic balm as a *sealing, soothing, resolving* substance. Modern chemistry confirms the intuition: the same resin the bud uses for self-defence is rich in antioxidant flavonoids and salicylates — antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory compounds — which is precisely why bees collect it to sterilise the hive as propolis. Symbol (the healing balm) and pharmacology (a natural antiseptic-anti-inflammatory resin) coincide.

### Active Components

- **Salicylates / salicin & populin** (populin = benzoyl-salicin) — the aspirin-family precursors that account for anti-inflammatory and analgesic-antipyretic action.
- **Flavonoids** — pinocembrin, pinostrobin, pinobanksin (and its acetate), chrysin, galangin, pinostrobin chalcone; the antioxidant core shared with poplar-type propolis.
- **Phenolic acids** — *p*-coumaric acid, caffeic acid and their esters (e.g. methyl-butenyl-*p*-coumarate, benzyl salicylate).
- **Sesquiterpene/volatile fraction** — α-, β-, γ-eudesmol and related terpenoids giving the balsamic aroma.
- **Tannins and lignins** — astringent, barrier-forming constituents of the bud tissue.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica *Populus nigra* bud is a **topical resolvent and mucosal-protective resin**: an anti-inflammatory, mildly antiseptic salve constituent for bruises, haemorrhoids, chapped and inflamed skin, minor burns, and aching joints, and a soothing ingredient in oral/throat sprays. Its salicylate and flavonoid load make it a gentle, botanically standardisable alternative to bee-collected propolis where a consistent, contaminant-free resin is wanted. Secondary internal use (as a small-dose expectorant/febrifuge in traditional tinctures) is recognised but subordinate to the topical role.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, poplar-bud chemistry and bioactivity are well characterised. Kuś et al. profiled *Populus nigra* bud extracts by GC-MS and UHPLC-DAD-QqTOF-MS, quantifying pinostrobin, pinocembrin, pinobanksin derivatives, *p*-coumarate esters and **benzyl salicylate**, and confirming the buds as the precursors of poplar-type propolis ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpba.2018.05.041), PMID 29852355). Mattoli et al. (2026) demonstrated by targeted and untargeted metabolomics that a standardised *P. nigra* bud extract clusters compositionally with European poplar-type propolis while additionally retaining **salicylates, lignins and tannins** from bud tissue; functionally the extract showed concentration-dependent antioxidant activity, inhibited biofilm at sub-MIC levels, and — formulated as an oral spray — reduced **LPS-induced IL-6 release by 39%**, evidencing a real anti-inflammatory and mucosal-protective action ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules31111836), PMID 42280141).

**Evidence grade:** Phytochemistry is **strong** (multiple analytical studies). Anti-inflammatory/antioxidant/antimicrobial activity is **moderate** (mechanistic and in-vitro/formulation data; limited controlled clinical trials on the bud specifically). Traditional topical use is **well-attested and mechanistically coherent** via salicylates and flavonoids. Graded **moderate**, trending strong for topical/antioxidant claims.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic/Unani:** Warm and drying, resolvent (*mohallil*) and mildly analgesic; a resin that "ripens and disperses" inflammatory swellings.
- **Greek (Dioscoridean):** Warming, pain-soothing, resolving; the basis of the cooling-resolvent poplar salve.
- **Chinese (by family analogy):** Bitter, cool-to-neutral, clears heat and dispels wind-damp — aligned with the Salicaceae analgesic group.

### Contraindications

⚠️ **SALICYLATE-CONTAINING RESIN — treat with aspirin-class caution.**

- **Salicylate / aspirin allergy or intolerance:** contraindicated. The bud contains salicin/populin and benzyl salicylate; cross-reactivity with aspirin and other salicylates is possible (skin, respiratory, or systemic hypersensitivity).
- **Children and teenagers:** avoid internal salicylate-containing preparations in children/adolescents, especially during viral illness (fever, influenza, chickenpox), because of the risk of **Reye's syndrome** associated with salicylates.
- **Anticoagulant / antiplatelet interaction:** salicylates may add to the effect of warfarin, DOACs, heparin, aspirin, or NSAIDs — risk of increased bleeding. Use internal preparations cautiously (or avoid) in anticoagulated patients and stop before surgery.
- **Propolis / bee-product cross-allergy:** because poplar buds are the botanical source of poplar-type propolis, people allergic to **propolis, bee products, or Balsam of Peru / balsams** may react to the bud resin (allergic contact dermatitis). Patch-test topical preparations.
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** avoid internal salicylate use, particularly in the third trimester (salicylate effects on fetal circulation/labour); topical occasional use with medical advice. [USER: verify]
- **Peptic ulcer / GI sensitivity, asthma with salicylate sensitivity, renal impairment:** caution with internal use.
