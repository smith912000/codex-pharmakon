# Riddhi — Ṛddhi, Vṛddhi-pair ("Ṛddhi-Vṛddhi"), an Aṣṭavarga orchid; "Prosperity/Increase"
## *Habenaria intermedia* D.Don | Family: Orchidaceae
**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā); Aṣṭavarga group; classical rasāyana / Jīvanīya tradition
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa — madhura (sweet); Vīrya — śīta (cooling); Vipāka — madhura (sweet); primarily Vātapittahara, Jīvanīya (life-promoting), Rasāyana.
**Training Phase:** Deep Restorative (Rasāyana) · Ojas-building / Jīvanīya · Reproductive & tissue nourishment

> ⚠️ **Conservation & substitution flag:** *Habenaria intermedia* is a rare, slow-growing, wild-harvested high-Himalayan terrestrial orchid. Ṛddhi (with its pair Vṛddhi) is one of the eight *Aṣṭavarga* drugs that are **endangered / commercially scarce and routinely substituted** in the market. Authentic material is often unavailable; classical formulae increasingly use approved substitutes (*pratinidhi dravya*). Any product labelled "Riddhi" should be treated as **likely substituted unless provenance is verified.** Wild collection is a conservation concern; do not source wild-harvested tubers.

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS) & the Aṣṭavarga:** Ṛddhi is one of the **Aṣṭavarga** — the classical group of eight rare, cooling, sweet, nourishing "life-promoting" (Jīvanīya) drugs (Jīvaka, Ṛṣabhaka, Meda, Mahāmeda, Kākolī, Kṣīrakākolī, Ṛddhi, Vṛddhi). These eight are the aristocrats of the rasāyana pharmacopoeia: prized ingredients of the great rejuvenative and nutritive formulations, most famously **Cyavanaprāśa** and the classical **Aṣṭavarga**-based ghees and jams. Ṛddhi and Vṛddhi are always named as a **pair** ("Ṛddhi-Vṛddhi"), botanically identified in modern pharmacognosy with *Habenaria* spp. (Ṛddhi ≈ *H. intermedia*; Vṛddhi ≈ *H. edgeworthii*). They are classically indicated as *jīvanīya* (life/vitality-promoting), *bṛṃhaṇa* (bulk/tissue-building), *balya* (strengthening), *śukrala* (reproductive-promoting), *vayaḥsthāpana* (age-sustaining) and for *kṣaya* (wasting), debility and rakta-pitta.

**Rasāyana tradition:** Because they are sweet, cooling, unctuous and heavy, the Aṣṭavarga drugs are the archetypal *ojas*-building substances — used precisely where the body's deep vitality (ojas) and reproductive/tissue essence (śukra, śukra-dhātu) are depleted.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — Prosperity Made Flesh:** The names of this pair are not medical terms but **deities of abundance**. *Ṛddhi* and *Vṛddhi* are personified goddesses of *prosperity, increase and growth* — Ṛddhi ("wealth, accomplishment, magical attainment") is a consort/attendant of Gaṇeśa alongside Buddhi and Siddhi; *Vṛddhi* means "growth, increase, augmentation." To name a medicine after these figures is a precise doctrinal statement: these are the drugs of *increase* — of tissue, of vitality, of the reproductive essence, of the very substance of the body. Where most of the materia medica removes something (heat, toxin, obstruction), the Aṣṭavarga *adds* — they are the pharmacology of accumulation, filling out the wasted and the depleted. The tuberous, moist, sweet orchid, swelling underground in thin cold Himalayan soil, is a fitting body for a goddess of increase: a small store of concentrated nourishment hidden in hard ground. In Kenetica's frame Ṛddhi is the *principle of nourishing increase* — the deep restorative that rebuilds the substance of the organism rather than merely calming or clearing it, the mythic promise that what has been wasted can be filled again.

### Active Components

Modern phytochemical work on *Habenaria intermedia* reports isolation of several compounds including the phenanthrene/stilbenoid-type constituent **loroglossol** and related biomarkers (see Scientific Correlation), alongside the mucilaginous, glucomannan-rich, nutritive tuber matrix characteristic of terrestrial orchids. The genus more broadly yields dihydrophenanthrenes, bibenzyls and flavonoids. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica, Ṛddhi is a **deep nutritive rasāyana of the "increase" class** — reserved for genuine depletion: wasting (*kṣaya*), convalescent debility, reproductive/tissue depletion, and the dry, over-fired, run-down states that call for building rather than clearing. Its signature is *bṛṃhaṇa* (anabolic tissue-building) combined with cooling sweetness — it nourishes and moistens, supports ojas and śukra, and is immunomodulatory in the classical *rasāyana* sense. It is not for acute conditions or for Kapha-heavy, congested constitutions; it is the slow restorative given (almost always in compound, e.g. Cyavanaprāśa) to rebuild a depleted system over time. **In practice, given scarcity, Kenetica treats Ṛddhi primarily as a doctrinal/formulary member whose role is usually filled by an approved substitute.**

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, direct science on Ṛddhi as such is **very thin** — a search this run for "Habenaria intermedia Riddhi Astavarga" returned **zero** indexed results, and the broader Aṣṭavarga search likewise returned nothing, an honest reflection of how little validated work exists on these scarce orchids. The one substantive on-target study is a 2023 phytochemistry/pharmacology paper on *Habenaria intermedia* D. Don (Orchidaceae) — described as an "unexplored" and "prominent Rasāyana plant" — which isolated and characterised seven compounds from the tubers (five quantified by a new HPLC-PDA method), and reported that **loroglossol** and the extract showed **immunomodulatory and anti-NLRP3-inflammasome activity**, promoting spleen-cell proliferation and IL-2/TNF-α/IFN-γ secretion and counteracting cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression (Kumar C et al., 2023, *ACS Omega* — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.3c03071)). This is genuinely supportive of the classical *rasāyana / immunomodulatory* framing.

**Evidence grade: VERY LOW (single preclinical study).** One in-vitro/in-vivo immunomodulation paper exists; there are **no human trials**, and the Jīvanīya/ojas/śukra claims are traditional. The immunomodulatory finding is real and directionally consistent with rasāyana use but is not clinical proof.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** madhura (sweet)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** guru (heavy), snigdha (unctuous), often picchila (slimy/mucilaginous)
- **Vīrya (potency):** śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive taste):** madhura (sweet)
- **Doṣa action:** Vātapittahara (pacifies Vāta and Pitta); increases Kapha
- **Karma (actions):** jīvanīya (life-promoting), bṛṃhaṇa (anabolic/tissue-building), balya (strengthening), rasāyana (rejuvenative), śukrala/vājīkara-supportive (reproductive), vayaḥsthāpana (age-sustaining), immunomodulatory (classical bala/ojas), raktapittahara
- **Srotas / systems:** rasavaha & raktavaha (plasma/blood), śukravaha (reproductive), ojas/immunity, mucosa

### Contraindications

Heavy, sweet, unctuous and Kapha-increasing — contraindicated or used with care in obesity, diabetes/āma-medas conditions, congestion, high Kapha, and weak digestion (mandāgni) where it can cause heaviness and sluggishness. Not for acute infection or high-āma states. Because authentic material is rare, **adulteration and misidentification are the primary practical hazard** — an incorrectly substituted "Riddhi" carries unknown effects.
