# Bird brain (steer backbone also noted) — "Brain-of-the-uuat-bird" (Bryan's rendering of the Ebers Papyrus; "uuat" is an untranslated Egyptian word in his text)
## *The brain of a bird Bryan does not identify, named by an Egyptian word he leaves untranslated; the spine's "unspecified bird sp." is a list gloss, and the "steer backbone" of the spine row was not found in the Bryan text* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*. The spine row reads "Bird brain (steer backbone also noted)", "unspecified bird sp.", note "Ebers; sae.saw-leipzig.de; id-uncertain | R4 applied: UNWRITABLE: unresolved identification" (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 93). The website named in the note was not used. The identification is indeed unresolved: the bird is not named in English anywhere in the passage found. The parts-of-animals list in Chapter VII (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` lines 1405-1416) includes "Brain" but ties it to no animal, and "Bird-of-Passage" (line 1395) is a separate head word in the animal list.

**Where a bird's brain is prescribed.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "bird", "brain", "backbone", "steer", "bull" and "ox". One bird-brain prescription was found.

- **After pulling out an in-growing eyelash, lines 2882-2939**, in Chapter XVI "Diseases of the Eyes" (heading lines 2720-2721). Bryan's series is headed "TO PREVENT THE HAIR GROWING INTO THE EYE AFTER IT HAS BEEN PULLED OUT" (line 2882); its first recipe is incense ground in lizard's dung with the blood of cow, ass, pig, dog and stag, with collyrium, "placed on the part whence the Hair has been pulled out so that it may not grow again". Later alternatives are bat's blood with the rim of a new *hennu* vessel and honey, then "Fat-of-an-Ox, Olive Oil, Entrails-of-Moles" put on the fire, and then (lines 2935-2939): "ANOTHER / Brain-of-the-uuat-bird ... Smear a Vine-leaf therewith and put in the place whence the Hair has been pulled out." The page numbers visible in the text run through "103" in this series.

**Other bird parts, printed separately, not counted here.** "Heart-of-the-mesa-Bird" in a tapeworm cake (line 1963); "Excrement-of-the-adu-Bird" (line 1362); the "Neck-of-the-gabgu-bird", its blood and its egg for the hair (lines 4033, 4044, 4056); "Excrement-of-the-henut-bird" for pterygium (line 2842); "Blood-of-the-nehur-bird" (line 3841); and a "per-baibait-bird with Honey" for splinters (line 2335). These are different birds named by Egyptian words, and Bryan does not equate them with the *uuat*-bird.

**Steer backbone.** The spine row says "steer backbone also noted". The Bryan text on disk contains no steer, bullock or ox backbone. "Backbone" occurs in the Bryan text only for the woman's backbone smeared with swordfish bones (line 2515), a remedy "to strengthen the *mer* of the Backbone" (line 3199) and a patient's "backbone" in a verse (line 3740). No steer-backbone prescription was found, so nothing is written about it. [USER: verify]

**What is not said.** Nothing in the sources retrieved says what bird the *uuat* is, how the brain was prepared, or how much was used. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. The 1987 translation, the Egyptian word and the website named in the spine row could change any reading above. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it, since the bird is not identified. In general terms, brain tissue is mostly water with lipids (including cholesterol and phospholipids) and protein. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The one use the sources retrieved show is a bird's brain smeared on a vine leaf and placed where an eyelash had been pulled out, to prevent re-growth into the eye. Animal tissue near the eye is an infection risk. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); no recitation is printed with this prescription in Bryan's summary, which is no proof the papyrus gives none. ### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** raw animal tissue near the eye risks infection; eye disease needs medical care.
- **Disease from wild birds:** wild birds carry zoonotic infections, and the bird here is not identified.
- **Conservation and law:** many wild birds are protected, and the species is unknown; the status was not retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** the material is a killed animal.
