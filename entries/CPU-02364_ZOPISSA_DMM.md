# ZOPISSA — zopissa, apochyma (Dioscorides, *De Materia Medica* 1-98)

## *The resin and wax scraped off ships, called zopissa by some and apochyma by others; Dioscorides adds that some have used the same name for pine resin. Identification: ship-scraped pitch (resin with wax), soaked in sea water, as the text itself says; the translator's suggested heading is "Resin scraped off ships — Zopissa". No binomial is given for the resin. Confidence: medium that it is a pine-resin material, from the surrounding chapters, and high that the text means the hull scrapings* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the name index and filed as a plant; this entry replaces it. The first-pass guess was "zopissa (pine pitch scraped from ships)". That stands, with the wax added from the text.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is the 2000 English edition of *De Materia Medica* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`). The chapter is 1-98, headed "ZOPISSA" (line 4275), and the translator's suggestion line reads "Resin scraped off ships — Zopissa" (line 4276). The whole chapter is three sentences (line 4277):

> "Some call the resin (which together with wax is scraped off ships) zopissa, and others call it apochyma as it is soluble because it was steeped in sea water. Some have called the resin of the pine tree by the same name."

So the text says four things about the material and nothing else: it is resin together with wax; it is scraped off ships; it is called zopissa by some and apochyma by others, the latter name being explained by its being soluble after steeping in sea water; and the name zopissa is also used by some for the resin of the pine tree. **The chapter gives no property, no use and no preparation.** The name index (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, row 100: "Resin scraped off ships — Zopissa", class "plant") and the source list (`_sources/dioscorides.csv`, line 99: "Zopissa, Resin scraped") add nothing further. The printed index of the translation lists the name on page 99 ("zopissa 99", "apochyma 99").

**Where it sits.** Chapter 1-98 follows the chapters on pitch pine and its products in Book I: the sub-products of pitch (1-95 "Pisselaion", whose translator's suggestion is "Pinus mughus, Pinus nigra, Pinus maritima, Pinus pinea, Pinus rigida, Peuce — Pitch Pine"), soot from moist pitch (1-96), and dry pitch (1-97, `Pissa Xera`). The statement about dry pitch is the only property-bearing neighbour: "It is warming and softening, removing pus, dispersing tubercula [nodules] and pannus [opaque thickening of cornea with veins], and filling up ulcers. It is effective mixed with wound medicines." That sentence is about dry pitch, not about zopissa, and nothing licenses reading it onto this drug. The chapter that follows (1-99, `Asphaltos`) opens the bitumens. The ship-scrapings are thus filed with the pitches, and the sentence about the pine-resin use of the name is the one link the text itself makes.

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, pine pitch and resin consist chiefly of resin acids and turpentine-type volatile terpenes, and ship's pitch was applied to hulls mixed with wax; the scraping that Dioscorides describes would be a weathered mixture of resin, wax and salt-soaked debris. This is a statement of general chemistry and does not describe the ancient material in particular. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Pine resin and its distillates are irritant to skin and mucosa in strength, and modern hull coatings and scrapings can contain biocides and heavy-metal antifouling compounds. [USER: verify] No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Dioscorides prints no quality (warming, cooling, astringent or other) for zopissa itself. The warming and softening quality printed in 1-97 belongs to dry pitch and is not assigned here.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is supplied here. What he offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention (provenance, name, synonym and adulteration), and this short chapter shows that discipline in miniature: it fixes a material by where it comes from (a ship), what it is made of (resin and wax) and what it is called (zopissa, apochyma, and the pine-resin use of the same name). It records no use, so it cannot record an amuletic one. No cultivation status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes zopissa a cultivation substance. The one thing the chapter teaches is that a trade name can mean two different materials, ship-scrapings and pine resin, and a cultivator reading old texts should check which is meant before assuming an identity.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The name covers two materials in this chapter (ship-scrapings, and pine resin). Do not treat any resin as "zopissa" on the strength of the name.
- **Ship scrapings.** Hull deposits can contain modern antifouling paints, tar and salts. Do not ingest or apply them.
- **Resins and allergy.** Pine resin is a recognised contact allergen. [USER: verify]
- **No use is recorded.** The chapter gives no indication; any medical use would be an invention.
