### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT), under this binomial only by the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary.** No other text read for this entry names the plant, and Clark 1995 prints neither the binomial nor the name "Dhewa". This entry rests on one glossary line for the binomial and on running-text lines for the Tibetan name that the glossary does not print beside it. The Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The extraction note reads: "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, line 261). The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (lines 5179 to 5261).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, the "D" block (the letter at line 5179). The glossary prints its columns in separate runs. The phonetic names run from "Dali" (line 5181) to "Drita sazin" (line 5196), and the sequence begins "Dali / Dhambu kara / Dhatig Dharyaken / Dhawa / Dhenda / Dhenrog / Dhewa / Dhong ga ..." (lines 5181 to 5188). The scientific names begin "Rhododendron laudandum" (line 5215), "Hippuris vulgaris Schisandra spenanthera Lepidium apetalum Arisaema j acquemontii Croton tinglium" (line 5217), "Ricinus communis L." (line 5218), "Corydalis melanochlora" (line 5219) and "Cassia fistula" (line 5220). Counting in order, "Dhewa" is the eighth phonetic name and "Corydalis melanochlora" is the eighth scientific name, with "Dhong ga" ninth against "Cassia fistula". The English column carries "-" at this place (line 5228), so it gives no help. The Codex therefore reads the glossary as pairing "Dhewa" with *Corydalis melanochlora*, by column position only and not as a printed row. [USER: verify]

*Checking the count.* Several names in the block can be checked against other lines. "Dhambu kara" stands second against *Hippuris vulgaris*, and Clark's *hdam.bu.ka.ra* line (1057) gives "Hippuris vulgaris" first. "Dhong ga" stands ninth against *Cassia fistula*, and Clark's *dong.ga* line (990) gives "Cassia fistula Linn". "Drita sazin" stands last against *Fragaria nubicola*, and Clark's *hbri.ta.sa.hdzin* line (1058) gives wild strawberry species. In the Men-Tsee-Khang running text "Dhenda", the sixth name, and "Dhong ga" both stand in the group of purgatives (line 3661: "Arura, Dhenda, Dhong ga, Shri khenda, Dhurji, Thamu, ... are the group of medicines that are purgatives"), and *Croton tiglium* in the sixth scientific place is generally known as a drastic purgative. The count therefore holds at the second, sixth, ninth and last places, and by that count the eighth place is *Corydalis melanochlora*. The count rests on order and not on rows, and several lines hold more than one name (lines 5183, 5188 and 5217). [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text on "Dhewa".* The text prints the name in three places and gives no Latin name in any. In chapter 20 (which ends at line 3583), within the efficacy passage (line 3456): "Dhewa cures epidemic and Tsa tri cures tripa disorders." The sentence before it reads "Ji tsher cures epidemic dis¬ eases, acts as an antidote against poison and eliminates hot disorder of the kidneys", so the printed "epidemic" after Dhewa is read as the same word and not completed by the Codex. In chapter 21 (which ends at line 3689), line 3601: "Giwam, Sergyi metok, Bongnga karpo, Gadhur, Ji tsher, Dhewa, Parpata and Traagkang are the group of medicines that cure contagious disorders" (the sentence continues on line 3602). The index (line 7323) lists "Dhewa 210,220". That the "Dhewa" of these passages is the glossary's "Dhewa" rests on the identical spelling. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995.* A search of `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` for "Dhewa", "Corydalis melanochlora" and the likely transliterations found no line. The Clark materia medica run has other *Corydalis* lines (*re.skon*, line 1002; *lug.ngal*, line 1106) on other Tibetan names, which are treated in I_1257 and I_1258 and are not this drug.
