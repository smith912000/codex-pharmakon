# Natron — "Natron", "Red Natron", "Refined Natron", "Natron-water", "Meal-of-Natron" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus mineral list)
## *A salt named "Natron" in Bryan's alphabetical list of the mineral drugs of the Ebers Papyrus; Bryan gives no Egyptian word and no composition, and the sources retrieved do not identify it further* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter V "Mineral Remedies", the alphabetical list of mineral drugs (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1191: "Natron Red Natron Refined Natron Natron-water Meal-of-Natron"; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 23, class mineral, "Bryan's English identification; forms listed: Natron | Red Natron | Refined Natron | Natron-water | Meal-of-Natron"). The list is a head-word list and gives no use, no prescription number and no identification. The spine row's formula for natron (a sodium carbonate and bicarbonate mixture) is the list row's and is not stated by Bryan. Bryan's opening of the chapter (lines 1133-1137) says that few mineral drugs "are prescribed in their crude or native state" and that "the Soda must be Soda-from-Upper-Egypt". He does not say whether "Soda" is the same drug as "Natron"; the two words are printed separately and the question is left open here. [USER: verify]

**Where Natron is prescribed in Bryan's running text.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "natron" and "soda". The passages, with the heading under which each falls, are these.

- Line 1962, in the chapter section headed "The Abdomen" (heading line 1928): for tapeworm, "One cake was composed of Herbs-of-the-Field and Natron, baked into a cake with Cow's Bile."
- Line 2102, in "Minor Medicine" (heading line 2091): "REMEDY TO DRIVE OUT THE PAIN IN THE HEAD", an ingredient list that includes Natron beside onion, *setseft*-seeds, cooked sword-fish bone and honey, ending "Smear the Head therewith for four days."
- Lines 2180-2181, in "Minor Medicine": limbs "could be made supple by smearing them with a liniment composed of Natron, Beans, Honey, Incense, and Sweet Myrrh, beaten up with Hippopotamus Oil, Crocodile Oil, Oil-of-the-Shadfish, [...] and Oil-of-Two-Days"; and a longer poultice of ass's dung, honey, figs, date-juice, sea-salt, wine yeast, bullock's fat, Natron and grains, "cooked and applied as a poultice".
- Lines 2609 and 2613, in "Diseases of the Skin" (heading line 2607): "REMEDY FOR THE THIGH Onions Dried Beans Red Natron Sea-salt Sour Milk. Make into a poultice. It heals at once"; and for generalised itching, "a Poultice of Corn, Natron, Clay-from-the-Gate, Onions, Incense, and Refuse-of-Dates". Line 2632, for itching with "blood corrosion": "A concoction of Elderberries, Linseed, Wormwood, Natron, and various native plants, rubbed in Semen-of-a-Man, Yeast-of-Wine, and Juice-of-the-Wild-Date, was warmed and applied as a poultice."
- Line 3189, in "The Nervous System" (heading line 3148), the section on the *met* (heading line 3165): "REMEDY TO MAKE THE MET SUPPLE", a long list of woods, resins, grains and plants which ends "Red-lead Fresh-Lead-earth Natron Fat-of-the-Bullock".
- Lines 3409-3411, in "The Heart and Circulatory System" (heading line 3303): "There are Two Vessels to his Thigh. When he suffers from his Thigh and both Thighs are atremble, say thou for it: 'This Vessel that runs to the region of his Thigh has conceived the [...] disease.' Use against it: Fresh Milk Wormwood Natron. Cook into one and make the Patient drink for four days." The gap in the quotation is in Bryan's text.
- Lines 4122, 4139 and 4142, in "Cosmetics" (heading line 4087): a face-pap of bullock's bile, whipped ostrich egg, oil, dough, "Refined Natron" and *hautet*-resin, mixed with fresh milk (line 4122); "some Sea-salt could be brought to one's abode, there rubbed up with Honey and Red Natron, and the colour of the skin altered by anointing the body with the sticky mixture" (line 4139); and "REMEDY TO BEAUTIFY THE SKIN. Meal-of-Alabaster Meal-of-Natron Sea-salt Honey. Mix into one in this Honey and anoint the body therewith" (lines 4142-4144).
- Lines 4220 and 4232, in "Domestic Hints" (heading line 4158): "TO DRIVE VERMIN OUT OF THE HOUSE. Do thou sprinkle it with Natron-Water. By that means they are dispersed" (line 4220); and, against a serpent in its lair, "the choice of Natron, which we are to put into the hole" (line 4232).
- Line 1131, in Bryan's account of vehicles: "Natron Water" is one of the kinds of water prescribed.

**"Soda" in Bryan's running text, not equated.** "Soda-from-Upper-Egypt" is a drug in four eye passages: with lizard's dung, collyrium and honey for pterygium (line 2843); crushed with ebony shavings in water for the "drawing-together of the pupil" (line 2981); "Collyrium, Red-lead, and Soda" beaten into a paste for squint (line 2995); and, dissolved in spring-water, an eye-cooler that Bryan calls "positively alarming in spite of the Scribe's special note appended to the prescription: 'It heats it!'" (line 3017). Whether this is the same drug as Natron is not stated by Bryan, so these passages are listed as related and not counted as Natron uses. [USER: verify]

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say where the Natron came from, what separated "red" from "refined" Natron, or whether the Natron-water was a solution of the salt. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis; Bryan's English word is the only identification the text on disk offers. Nunn 1996 and the website cited in the spine row were not opened for this entry.

### Active Components

None can be stated from the papyrus. In general terms, natron as the word is used today is a naturally occurring mixture of hydrated sodium carbonate with sodium bicarbonate, usually with sodium chloride and sulphate as impurities. These are alkaline salts: in solution they are soapy, they neutralise acids, and they irritate skin, eyes and mucous membranes. This is a general statement about the modern mineral, not a reading of Bryan.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show Natron prescribed in plasters, liniments, drinks and washes for the head, limbs, skin and thigh, as a worm-cake, in cosmetics, and as a household vermin and serpent deterrent. The papyrus gives no quantity for any of these, and Natron is a caustic alkaline salt. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only what Bryan reports. Natron is mixed in a poultice with onions, beans and sour milk (line 2609); beaten up in a liniment with hippopotamus and crocodile oil (line 2181); cooked with wormwood and fresh milk and given as a drink "for four days" (line 3411); cooked into a worm-cake with herbs and cow's bile (line 1962); rubbed with honey and sea-salt as a body wash (line 4139); and used as "Natron-Water" for sprinkling (line 4220). No quantity is printed in Bryan's summary for any of these. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution. The one place where speech is printed beside a Natron drug is the thigh-tremor remedy (lines 3409-3411), where the physician is told to "say thou for it" a formula about the vessel that "has conceived the [...] disease" before giving Natron with milk and wormwood; this shows spell and drug as parts of one intervention (*heka*) for that complaint, and it does not carry over to the Natron in the other Natron passages, where Bryan prints no recitation, though he summarises and that is no proof the papyrus gives none. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The record is a salt used in many ordinary preparations, mainly external, with no quantity and no cultivation role. A cultivator has nothing to do with it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Alkalinity:** natron-type salts are caustic in strong solution; contact with eyes, broken skin or mucous membranes causes injury, and swallowing a concentrated solution is dangerous.
- **Eye use:** several Bryan passages put "Soda" in the eye; the Codex does not recommend any mineral salt in the eye.
- **Unspecified material:** the Natron of the papyrus is not described beyond Bryan's English word, so what is sold under the name today cannot be taken as the same material.
- **Wound and animal-matter mixtures:** the cited compounds mix Natron with animal gall, dung and semen; these carry infection risk and are not recommended.
