# Indian Lettuce — Shan Wo Ju (山莴苣), Indian Wild Lettuce, Bitter Lettuce

## *Lactuca indica* L. | Family: Asteraceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SBJ, SS
**Tags (previous line, retained for reference):** FMT (Avicennan/Unani), SBJ (Shennong), SS (Sushruta)
**Energetic Classification:** Cool & Dry (2nd degree) — cooling, sedative, bitter-relaxant (Galenic)
**Training Phase:** Descent / Settling — quieting an over-active nervous field

### Origin & Tradition

*Lactuca indica* is a tall East- and South-Asian wild lettuce, eaten as a leafy vegetable and valued for the milky bitter latex (a lactucarium-type exudate) that weeps from cut stems. It is one of the culturally important "unconventional food plants" of China, Korea, Vietnam and India, occupying the same doctrinal niche across traditions: a cooling bitter that calms, settles the stomach, and gently sedates.

**Avicennan / Unani (CM):** Avicenna's *Qanun* treats cultivated and wild lettuces (*khass*) as cold and moist herbs that quiet heat, dull sharp appetites, promote sleep and temper the "hot" passions. The bitter wild lettuces specifically were understood as stronger, drier and more narcotic than garden lettuce — closer to the *lactucarium* the later European pharmacopoeias called "lettuce opium." *L. indica*, as the Asian wild-lettuce representative, inherits this doctrine of the cooling soporific bitter.

**Shennong / *Shennong Bencao Jing* lineage (SBJ):** In the Chinese materia medica *shan wo ju* (mountain lettuce) is a cooling, bitter, slightly cold herb directed at "heat toxin" — used traditionally for clearing heat, cooling blood, resolving swellings and settling irritable digestion. The bitter latex is the signature. This aligns the plant with the broad Chinese class of cooling detoxicant bitters.

**Sushruta / Ayurvedic tradition (SS):** The Ayurvedic reading of bitter milky lettuces places them as *tikta* (bitter) and *kashaya* (astringent) *pippalika*-type greens: cooling, *pitta*-pacifying, mildly sedative and digestive, taken as a pot-herb to reduce heat and restlessness. As with the other traditions, the medicinal weight sits on the bitter principle rather than the leaf as mere food.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Every tradition converges on the same image — a plant that *bleeds white and quiets*. The milky latex reads symbolically as a calming, maternal, sleep-bringing fluid, and this is not merely poetic: the bitter sesquiterpene lactones concentrated in that latex (lactucin, lactucopicrin and relatives) are the very compounds shown experimentally to produce sedation and analgesia. The symbol and the pharmacology point the same way, which is why Kenetica files *L. indica* under **Descent / Settling** — the plant of coming down.

### Active Components

The characteristic actives are **guaianolide sesquiterpene lactones** of the lactucarium group — lactucin, lactucopicrin (intybin), 11β,13-dihydrolactucin and related bitters — the same class that defines *Lactuca virosa* and *Cichorium intybus*. Alongside these, *L. indica* carries **lignans and neolignans**, an **abscisic-alcohol glycoside**, phenolic acids, flavonoids and triterpenes. According to PubMed, Ha and colleagues (2025, *Natural Product Research*) isolated three previously undescribed compounds — indicalactucas A–C, comprising two lignans and an abscisic-alcohol glycoside — together with six known neolignans from the leaves of *L. indica* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2025.2591177)).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Used as a **mild sedative, sleep-supportive and digestive-relaxant bitter**. In the Descent/Settling phase it is deployed to lower nervous over-activation — the "wired but tired" state — to ease the transition into rest, and to calm an irritable, heat-tending gut. The bitter latex stimulates digestive secretion (the classic bitter reflex) while the sesquiterpene lactones act centrally to quiet locomotor drive. It pairs the somatic (bitter → digestion) with the central (lactucin → sedation) in a single plant, which is its main pedagogical value here.

### Scientific Correlation

Two lines of real evidence support the traditional profile. First, on **chemistry and anti-inflammatory activity**: according to PubMed, Ha et al. (2025) not only characterised the new leaf constituents of *L. indica* but showed that one isolated compound significantly inhibited nitric-oxide production in LPS-activated RAW 264.7 macrophages (IC₅₀ 15.8 µM), a recognised in-vitro anti-inflammatory readout ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2025.2591177)). Second, on the **sedative/analgesic mechanism of the lactucarium bitters**: according to PubMed, Wesołowska et al. (2006, *Journal of Ethnopharmacology*) demonstrated in mice that lactucin and lactucopicrin — the guaianolide bitters characteristic of the *Lactuca*/*Cichorium* latex — produced analgesia comparable to ibuprofen in hot-plate and tail-flick tests, and that lactucin and lactucopicrin (but not 11β,13-dihydrolactucin) additionally reduced spontaneous locomotor activity, i.e. were sedative ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2006.03.003)).

**Evidence grade: EMERGING (chemistry) + traditional (whole-plant clinical).** Note honestly: the Wesołowska sedation/analgesia data come from lactucin/lactucopicrin isolated from *L. virosa* and *Cichorium intybus*, **not** from *L. indica* directly — they support the *mechanism* of the shared bitter class, not a species-specific clinical claim. The species-specific published work (Ha et al.) is phytochemical and in-vitro. No human trials of *L. indica* for sedation or sleep have been retrieved.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Bitter (苦), cold (寒); clears heat, cools blood, resolves toxin/swelling; broadly Heart/Stomach/Liver-directed in the cooling-detoxicant class.
- **Ayurveda:** *Rasa* — tikta (bitter), kashaya (astringent); *Guna* — laghu (light), ruksha (dry); *Virya* — shita (cooling); *Vipaka* — katu (pungent); pacifies *pitta* and *kapha*, may aggravate *vata* in excess.
- **Galenic / Unani:** Cold and dry (2nd degree); soporific, sedative, appetite-tempering — the classical *khass* / lettuce doctrine, stronger in the bitter wild forms.

### Contraindications

Sedative bitters may potentiate CNS depressants (alcohol, benzodiazepines, sedating antihistamines, opioids) — avoid concurrent use or use with caution. High doses of lactucarium can cause drowsiness, nausea and, historically, anticholinergic-type effects; keep to small amounts. Avoid before driving or operating machinery. Asteraceae-family allergy (ragweed, daisy, chamomile cross-reactivity) is a contraindication. Caution in pregnancy and lactation (insufficient safety data; emmenagogue reputation of bitter latex). Not a substitute for evaluation of chronic insomnia or pain.
