# Choraka — Choraka, also glossed Taskara (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *An aromatic drug of the Eladi group whose botanical identity is not established in the sources read; Bhishagratna writes "Taskara (Choraka)" once, which equates two Sanskrit names but gives no plant. Identification: unresolved. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not identified. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The names.csv row gives "no identification given here". A search of all the Sushruta text on disk for "Choraka" found the places below.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

1. **Group list.** *Sutrasthana* ch.38, the Eladi group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15509; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 168). The list reads: "Ela, Tagara, Kushtha, Mansi, Dhyamaka, Tvaka, Patra, Naga-pushpa, Priyangu, Harenuka, Vyaghranakha, Shukti, Clianda, Sthauneyaka, Shriveshtaka, Chocha, Choraka, Valaka, Guggulu, Sarjarasa, Turushka, Kunduruka, Aguru, Sprikka, Ushira, Bhadradaru, Kumkuma, Punnaga and Keshara." The metrical text gives the virtue of the group as a whole: "subduing the action of Vayu and Kapham and in neutralising the effects of poison. It is a cosmetic and arrests the eruption of pimples and other vegetations on the skin such as rash, urticaria etc. and checks the itching sensation incidental thereto" (Vol. I lines 15512 to 15520). This is the group's virtue, not a statement about Choraka alone.
2. **A medicated oil for the Vayu.** *Chikitsasthana* ch.37, on the treatment by oil enema (*Anuvasana*) and *Uttara* enema (`text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch37.txt` lines 68 to 80; Vol. II line 26320). Under the "Third Taila" a long list of drugs is pasted together, ending "Pushkara and Taskara (Choraka)", and "cooked with the proper quantity of milk and oil. The oil thus prepared is highly efficacious in the disorders of the deranged Vayu. Injected into the bowels in the manner of an Anuvasana-vasti, it speedily cures Gridhrasi (sciatica), lameness, haunch-back, Adhya vata, urinary diseases, obstinate constipation of the bowels (Uda-varta), impaired digestion and weakness of the body." The gloss "(Choraka)" after "Taskara" is Bhishagratna's.
3. **An antidote.** *Kalpasthana* ch.5, on snake-bite (`text/kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch05.txt` line 507; Vol. II line 31101). The "Ekasara Agada" is made of "Somaraji seeds and Somaraji flowers, Katabhi, Sindhuvara, Choraka, Varuna, Kushtha, Sarpa-gandha, Saptala, Punarnava, flowers of Shirisha, Aragvada and of Arka, Shyama, Amhashtha, Vidanga, Antra, Ashmantaka, black earth and Kuravaka", which "should be applied singly or in combination of two or three to destroy the effects of poison" (verse 31).
4. **The glossary.** Bhishagratna's Vol. III has a glossary of names with Latin equivalents; "Choraka" is in it (Vol. III line 31530), but in this e-text the Latin column is out of line with the Sanskrit column, so no identification is taken from it. [USER: verify against the printed page]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unidentified.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Eladi group: calming Vata and Kapha, counteracting poison, and clearing rash and itch (group-level). As a named oil ingredient: disorders of deranged Vayu, given by oil enema. As a named antidote ingredient: poison from snake-bite, singly or in combination.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no taste or potency, no identification.

The Codex gives no protocol. In particular, it gives no antidote and no enema, and does not recommend any plant for snake-bite or for the other conditions named. Snake-bite is a medical emergency.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are an oil cooked with milk and a paste of many drugs (ch.37), and an *agada* applied singly or in combinations of two or three (Kalpa ch.5). No quantity is given for Choraka in either. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in a chapter 38 group list, an oil for deranged Vayu and an antidote. None of the places found is in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns it no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it. One line, "weakness of the body" among the conditions the oil cures (ch.37), is a remedy for a complaint and not a *rasayana* status.

**Practitioner's note.** An unidentified aromatic named in a group list, an oil and an antidote. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without an identification nothing can be gathered or used. A cultivator reads it as a name in a record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified; do not substitute any herb for it.
- **Snake-bite, sciatica, urinary disease.** These are medical conditions; use proper care. Seek emergency care for a snake-bite.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Companion ingredients.** The recipes contain many other drugs whose safety is not addressed here.
