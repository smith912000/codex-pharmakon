# Indian Pokeweed — Shang Lu (商陸), Pokeberry-of-the-East, Poke Root

## *Phytolacca acinosa* Roxb. | Family: Phytolaccaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, DMM, SBJ, SS
**Energetic Classification:** Cold and dry in the third degree (Galenic); strongly downward-draining, purgative
**Training Phase:** Purgation & Clearing (advanced only — never a foundation-phase agent)

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Dioscorides (De Materia Medica — DMM):** The Greek tradition knew several *Phytolacca*-type pokeweeds and related berry-bearing purgatives. Dioscorides catalogued a family of drastic, berry-laden herbs used cautiously as evacuants and for external application to swellings and "hard" tumours, always with the warning that the root and unripe fruit were dangerous if the dose was misjudged. The consistent classical instinct — that such plants "draw downward" and expel — maps onto the modern saponin/emetic pharmacology.

**Shennong / Chinese Materia Medica (SBJ):** In the Chinese tradition Shang Lu (商陸), the root of *Phytolacca acinosa*, is a classical drastic purgative and diuretic. It is grouped with the "downward-draining, water-expelling" agents: prescribed traditionally for oedema (水肿), ascites, distension of the abdomen, and difficult urination, and applied externally to carbuncles, abscesses and swellings. Two forms were recognised — the raw root (more toxic, for external use and drastic draining) and the vinegar-processed (酢) root, tempered to reduce toxicity. Classical texts flag it as a low-grade, toxic drug to be used only when strong water-draining is unavoidable.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS):** Indian pokeweed grows across the Himalayan foothills and northern India, where allied *Phytolacca* roots entered folk and classical practice as tikshna (sharp, penetrating) agents for oedema, skin eruptions and glandular swellings — again a strong, cathartic, "cutting" reputation rather than a gentle tonic.

**Avicenna (Canon of Medicine — AT):** The Unani stream absorbed the Dioscoridean pokeweeds as cold, drying, drastically evacuant simples, reserved for expelling excess phlegm and watery humours and for dissolving hard swellings externally — never for the weak, the depleted, or the pregnant.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Every tradition read this plant the same way — a downward, expelling, water-clearing force whose deep crimson berries and swollen taproot signalled both potency and danger. The symbolism of "draining the flood" is not merely poetic: the triterpenoid saponins (esculentosides) that give the root its purgative and diuretic action are genuinely membrane-active and pro-secretory, so the ancient "expels water" reading tracks the real chemistry. The blood-red berry that stains the hand was, across cultures, a warning colour — a plant to be respected, processed, and never taken lightly.

### Active Components

- **Triterpenoid saponins — esculentosides A–L** (oleanane-type, aglycone esculentogenin/oleanolic-acid derivatives): the principal bioactive and toxic constituents; responsible for GI irritation, purgation, diuresis and anti-inflammatory effects.
- **Phytolaccagenin / oleanolic-acid-type sapogenins.**
- **Mitogenic lectins / pokeweed-type antiviral proteins** (ribosome-inactivating and mitogenic proteins characteristic of the genus): drive the immune/mitogenic and cytotoxic profile.
- **Histamine and γ-aminobutyric acid** in some tissues; **oxalate** and **resins**.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Within Kenetica, Indian Pokeweed is filed strictly as an **advanced purgation/clearing agent**, not a daily tonic. Its legitimate niche is the same one every source tradition assigns: strong, downward water-draining and the topical resolution of stubborn swellings. It is a "specialist's tool" — deployed briefly, at a low, carefully controlled dose, for a specific draining objective, then withdrawn. It has no place in foundation, restoration, or maintenance phases, and no place in any regimen for a depleted, pregnant, or fragile practitioner.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the saponin **esculentoside A (EsA)** — the signature constituent of *Phytolacca* root — has a genuine, if early-stage, pharmacological literature. Xiao et al. (2004) showed that EsA potently inhibited production of the inflammatory mediators IL-1 and prostaglandin E2 from macrophages and suppressed IL-2 from activated lymphocytes, proposing this as a mechanism for its anti-inflammatory action ([DOI](https://doi.org/) — PMID 15169638). More recently, Zeng et al. (2021) reported that EsA inhibited the human coronavirus HCoV-OC43 at the attachment/penetration stage in vitro and, by molecular docking and dynamics, bound the SARS-CoV-2 spike glycoprotein, positioning it as a candidate spike-blocking lead ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2021.06.017) — PMID 34111482). This partially corroborates the traditional antiviral/anti-swelling reputation.

**Evidence grade: Preliminary / traditional.** The anti-inflammatory and antiviral data are real but confined to in-vitro and computational work on isolated esculentoside A — there are no controlled human trials of the whole root, and the plant's dominant clinical reality is toxicity. Efficacy claims for oedema and swellings remain **traditionally graded**, not clinically proven.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Bitter, acrid; cold; **toxic (有毒)**. Channels: Lung, Spleen, Kidney, Large Intestine. Actions: drains water and disperses accumulation; reduces swelling and dissipates nodules (external).
- **Ayurveda:** Rasa — tikta/katu (bitter, pungent); Guna — tikshna, laghu (sharp, light); Virya — ushna-leaning in action though cooling in classical humoral read; Vipaka — katu. Karma: strong shothahara (anti-oedema) with cautionary toxicity.
- **Galenic / Unani:** Cold and dry, strongly evacuant; a phlegm- and water-expelling simple of the third degree, reserved and always corrected.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The classical stream knows the drastic, berry-laden purgatives of this kind as evacuants and external applications to hard swellings, always with the caution that root and unripe fruit are dangerous when the dose is misjudged. Dioscorides carries no subtle-body system into which such a plant could be graded, so there is no cultivation status to record. What the tradition offers a cultivator is the discipline of attention it demands — right identification within a dangerous kin-group, recognition that the useful downward-drawing action rides alongside a genuine poison, and the judgement to keep the two apart. That is a practice of provenance and dose-discipline, not a vital-body doctrine. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The Unani stream absorbed the Dioscoridean pokeweeds as cold, drying, drastically evacuant simples of the third degree — reserved for expelling watery humours and phlegm and for dissolving hard swellings externally, and always "corrected" (processed) before use. The tradition's central cultivation idea, the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture, is a longevity frame of temperate regimen; a violent evacuant is if anything its antithesis, spending the body rather than conserving it, and no *mufarrih* or *ruh*-strengthening role is claimed for this simple. Its standing is purely therapeutic and short-course. **(b) framed:** claimed by the tradition, no cultivation status sourced.

**Chinese — Bencao (SBJ).** *Shang Lu* 商陸 (the root of *Phytolacca acinosa*) is entered in the *Shennong Bencao Jing* 神農本草經 in the **lower grade** (下品, *xia pin*) — explicitly the grade of toxic, potent drugs used to attack disease on a short defined course, and expressly *not* the upper-grade (上品) substances taken long-term to nourish life. The classic assigns it the water-draining, accumulation-dispersing, swelling-reducing offices later systematised as *xie shui* 瀉水; the grade itself is the tradition's own warning. This is a genuine cultivation status, and it is a negative one: within the *san pin* 三品 hierarchy Shang Lu is placed among the drugs that spend the body to clear an excess, the opposite pole from *yang sheng* 養生. Cultivation status: **(a) attested** — lower grade (下品), *Shennong Bencao Jing*, the grade of disease-attacking drugs not for long-term use.

**Ayurveda (SS).** In the Himalayan and north-Indian sphere allied *Phytolacca* roots entered folk and classical practice as *tikshna* (sharp, penetrating) *shothahara* (anti-oedema) agents for swellings, skin eruptions and glandular masses — a strong, cutting, cathartic reputation, not a tonic one. No *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative-vitality) assignment is recorded for this species in the sources retrieved, and the sharp cathartic profile is the antithesis of the depletable-*ojas*-conserving rejuvenatives. Its standing is therapeutic and cautionary. **(b) framed:** used within the tradition, no cultivation status sourced.

**Practitioner's note.** This is a specialist's draining tool, never a cultivation substance — and one tradition says so in its own grading: the Chinese classic files Shang Lu in the lower grade, the toxic drugs taken briefly to attack a defined excess and then stopped. Every stream agrees in substance — a downward, water-expelling, swelling-dissolving force with a real poison in the root and unripe berry. A cultivator's honest use of it is abstention: it has no place in any nourishing, restorative or long-term regimen, and internal use belongs only to qualified practitioners of the source traditions, at corrected-root doses, for a bounded draining objective.

### Contraindications

⛔ **TOXIC PLANT — SERIOUS POISONING AND DEATHS REPORTED.** This is one of the most heavily flagged entries in Volume I.

- **Pregnancy — ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION.** The root is an abortifacient-class drastic purgative; never use in pregnancy or when pregnancy is possible. Avoid in lactation.
- **The raw root, unripe fruit and seeds are emetic, purgative and poisonous.** Triterpenoid saponins cause violent gastrointestinal irritation — nausea, vomiting, cramping, profuse diarrhoea — and, at higher exposure, **neurological toxicity**: dizziness, weakness, tremor, disturbed vision, hypotension, tachycardia, respiratory depression and convulsions.
- **Mitogenic lectins / antiviral proteins** add haematological and immunological toxicity; the same proteins can cause dangerous reactions if the sap contacts broken skin or is inhaled as dust.
- **Never self-administer internally.** Do not exceed pharmacopoeial processed-root doses even under practitioner care.
- Contraindicated in children, the elderly, the debilitated, and in kidney or GI disease.
- **In poisoning, seek emergency medical care immediately** — management is supportive; there is no specific antidote.
