# Chamara flesh — Chamara (चमर), "Chamara" (Bhishagratna 1907); the translator's glossary prints "Chamara (Bos or yak Grunniens)"
## *The flesh of the chamara, an animal of the shore-dwelling group in Sushruta's food chapter; the project list row reads "yak", and the translator's glossary prints "Bos or yak Grunniens" in its name column and, by list order, "A sort of deer" in its meaning column* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Chamara* is named twice in the *Sutrasthana* chapter on food and drink, ch.46 (*Annapana-vidhi*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`). The chapter opens by saying that food is "the chief source of their bodily strength and complexion, as well as of the albuminous (Ojas) principle in their organisms" (lines 8-12), and goes on to list the flesh of animals by group. The first mention is in the *Anupa* group, "animals which are generally found to frequent marshy places", divided into five groups, of which the first is the *Kulacharas* (those "frequenting the shores of pools and lakes"; lines 659-662, running from page 487 to page 488). The text then names the members of the *Kulachara* group (page 488, lines 671-680): "Of these, the elephant, the Gavaya, buffalo, Ruru (an animal of the deer species which casts its antlers and wanders roaming about in the forests in autumn) Chamara, Srimara (an animal of the Zebra species with green and red stripes), Rohita (red deer), boar, rhinoceros, Gokarna, Kalapuchchaka, together with the Nynku (a species of antlered deer) and the wild cow, etc., frequent the cool shores of swamps and lakes, and are accordingly included within the group of Kulacharas (shore-dwellers)." The *chamara* is the fifth name in that list, directly after the *ruru*, whose gloss the translator gives as a deer.

The second mention is the sentence about the flesh, on page 489 (lines 712-714): "Similarly, the flesh of the Chamara is demulcent, cures an attack of cough, is sweet in taste and digestion and subdues the deranged Vayu and Pittam." The word "similarly" links it to the preceding sentence on the *ruru*, which is the last of a run of flesh sentences beginning with the elephant (line 688). The list row is `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 242, "Chamara (yak)", citing "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1".

*Identity.* The ch.46 text prints no gloss for the name. Bhishagratna's glossary in volume 3 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`) prints in its name column, at line 30446, "Chamara (Bos or yak Grunniens)". The OCR separates the name column from the meaning column, and the meanings line up with the names by list order (this order puts "A Greek partridge" with *Chakora* and "Ruddy goose ... Brahminy duck or goose" with *Chakra-baka*, which fits); by that order the meaning printed for *Chamara* is "A sort of deer" (in the meaning column, lines 30451-30475). So the glossary appears to give both a yak (*Bos grunniens*) and a deer for the same name, and I have not resolved which is meant. The project list row says "yak". [USER: verify] The chapter itself groups the animal with elephant, buffalo, deer, boar and rhinoceros as one that frequents the cool shores of swamps and lakes. The Sanskrit name was not analysed in the sources read.

*The group.* Immediately after the list the chapter gives the properties of the group (page 488, lines 682-686): "The flesh of an animal of this group is spermatopoietic and destroys the deranged Vayu and Kapham. It is sweet in taste and digestion, cooling, tonic, demulcent and diuretic, and increases the quantity of Kapham." The single sentence on the *chamara* (lines 712-714) is its own, and it differs from the group sentence: it speaks of *Vayu* and *Pittam*, where the group sentence speaks of *Vayu* and *Kapham*.

A search of the whole Sushruta text on disk (`text/sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`, and the three Bhishagratna volumes) for *chamara* found only ch.46 (lines 674 and 712) and the glossary.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, the flesh of a large grazing mammal, whether a bovid or a deer, is mostly water and muscle protein, with fat and small amounts of vitamins and minerals. Nothing links any constituent to the actions the text gives.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced use is dietary: the flesh of the *chamara* is demulcent, is said to cure an attack of cough, is sweet in taste and digestion, and subdues disturbed *Vayu* and *Pittam* (ch.46, lines 712-714), as a food, in a chapter that lists the flesh of many animals by their effect on the humours. The Codex does not recommend eating the flesh of a wild animal whose identity is uncertain. If the animal is the wild yak, it is a protected, threatened species; a cough that lasts needs a clinician. The entry exists to record the text.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives no preparation of this flesh and no amount. The sentence describes the flesh as food and says nothing of how it is cooked or how often it is taken. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Cough has many causes, from a simple cold to asthma, reflux, infection and heart or lung disease, and it is assessed and treated by clinicians. I have retrieved no evidence that the flesh of any animal treats it. Wild-animal meat can carry bacteria and parasites. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** For the animal itself the text prints: flesh demulcent; sweet in taste (*rasa*) and in digestion (*vipaka*); it cures an attack of cough; it subdues deranged *Vayu* and *Pittam* (ch.46, lines 712-714). For the *Kulachara* group of which it is a member (lines 682-686) the text prints: spermatopoietic; destroys deranged *Vayu* and *Kapham*; sweet in taste and digestion; cooling; tonic; demulcent; diuretic; increases *Kapham*. The group qualities apply to the *chamara* by group membership, which is an inference from the classification and not a sentence about the animal; the one sentence about the animal gives no potency (*virya*) of its own.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The ch.46 frame is food as the source of strength, complexion and *ojas* (lines 8-12), but the chapter assigns *ojas* to food in general and not to this flesh. The *Kulachara* group sentence calls the flesh of the group "spermatopoietic" and "tonic" (lines 683-685), and the sentence about the *chamara* itself does not use either word; in any case a property in a food list is not a placement in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and the whole Sushruta text on disk, searched for the name, finds it only in ch.46. So the substance has only a dietary and therapeutic status. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. The sattvic and rajasic food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The sourced use is a dietary one for cough and for disturbed *Vayu* and *Pittam*, in a food chapter, and the animal cannot be certainly identified from the text. The Codex does not recommend taking the flesh of a protected wild animal. If cough persists, have it assessed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** wild-animal meat can carry bacteria and parasites; it should be fully cooked, and wild-caught meat is not recommended.
- **Cough:** a cough that lasts more than a few weeks, or with blood, fever or breathlessness, needs a clinician; the text's "cures an attack of cough" is a historical dietary statement and not a treatment.
- **Conservation and law:** the wild yak is a threatened, protected species in its range; if the *chamara* is a deer, many deer are protected or regulated. Check local law before any hunting or trade.
- **Identity:** the Sanskrit text names no species; the project list says yak, and the translator's glossary prints both "Bos or yak Grunniens" and, by list order, "A sort of deer".
- **Ethics:** the Codex does not recommend taking an animal for these uses.
