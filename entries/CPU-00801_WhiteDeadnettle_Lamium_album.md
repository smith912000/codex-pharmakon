# White Deadnettle / Archangel / Bee Nettle

## *Lamium album* L. | Family: Lamiaceae

**Tradition Tags:** DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Cool, drying, astringent (mild); moistening-resolving at the mucosa

**Training Phase:** Foundation — a gentle astringent-vulnerary for the apprentice's shelf, not a heroic remedy

---

### Origin & Tradition

White deadnettle is the plant that wears the stinging nettle's coat and carries none of its sting. It grows the same square stem, the same toothed, wrinkled, nettle-shaped leaves, and hides among true nettles in hedgerow and waste ground — but it is a mint (Lamiaceae), not a nettle (Urticaceae), and its hairs do not bite. Brush a stand of it and nothing happens. This single fact — a nettle that is safe to grasp — is the seed of its entire doctrine.

**Doctrine of the Signatures (the mimic).** To the reader of signatures, a plant that *imitates* a dangerous plant while being harmless is announcing its office: it is the gentle correction of what the nettle does harshly. Where the true nettle inflames the skin, the deadnettle was read as soothing inflamed surfaces. The "archangel" name (Herba archangelica in old herbals, and the folk name *Archangel*) reinforced the benign reading — a heavenly, protective counterpart to a plant of small violence.

**European Folk & Materia Medica.** The dried flowering tops and the white corollas ("white flowers," *flores lamii albi*) were the medicinal part in Central and Eastern European domestic practice. Two uses dominate the record. First, as a mild **astringent vulnerary** — a wash or poultice for wounds, and a gargle for inflamed throat and mouth. Second, and most tellingly, for **leucorrhoea** — abnormal whitish vaginal discharge, the "whites." The plant with the conspicuously *white* flower was matched to the *white* discharge: a colour signature, doctrine of like-treats-like written in petal and symptom. It was also taken as a mild **expectorant** for catarrh, and the flowers were a benign, honey-scented tea.

**Chinese Medicine (TCM).** *Lamium* is not a classical *Ben Cao* drug; white deadnettle enters the Chinese-language pharmacological literature chiefly through modern phytochemistry rather than through the canon. Its close chemical kin — the shanzhiside-type iridoids — connect it to the *Lamiophlomis / Phlomis* materia medica used for bleeding and trauma, which is the honest basis of the CM tag here. **[USER: verify]** whether a specific classical CM attestation for *L. album* exists, or whether the CM tag should be softened to "congener-linked."

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** The leucorrhoea signature is the entry's central teaching case: a doctrine built on colour (white flower → white discharge) with no mechanistic reason a flower's petal-pigment should govern a mucosal secretion. Yet the *underlying* use is not baseless. The plant is rich in astringent tannins and in iridoid and phenylethanoid glycosides with genuine anti-inflammatory action on mucosa — so an astringent, mildly anti-inflammatory wash could plausibly reduce mucosal irritation and discharge regardless of the flower's colour. The doctrine chose the right plant for the wrong reason. That is the honest shape of it: the signature is decoration; the tannins and iridoids are the mechanism. We deflate the colour-magic and keep the astringent chemistry.

### Active Components

According to PubMed, the flowering herb contains three well-characterised classes plus supporting groups:

- **Iridoid glycosides** — lamalbid, shanzhiside methyl ester, 8-acetylshanzhiside methyl ester, and the antiviral pair lamiridosins A and B. These are the plant's signature secoiridoid-type constituents ([Czerwińska 2018, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules23112770); [Zhang 2009, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/np900549e); [Bubueanu 2019, DOI](https://doi.org/10.2478/acph-2019-0026)).
- **Phenylethanoid / phenylpropanoid glycosides** — verbascoside (acteoside) and phlinoside D, the main antioxidant-anti-inflammatory phenolics ([Czerwińska 2018, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules23112770)).
- **Flavonoids** — rutoside (rutin), isoquercetin, quercetin and derivatives, and tiliroside; plus the polyamine conjugate tricoumaroyl spermidine, newly reported in the species ([Sulborska 2020, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules25246025); [Baumli 2026, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jchromb.2026.124994)).
- **Phenolic acids & tannins** — caffeic, chlorogenic, ferulic, gallic, p-coumaric, protocatechuic and others, localised to glandular trichomes of the corolla; the tannins carry the astringency ([Sulborska 2020, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules25246025)).
- **Fatty acids** — linoleic and linolenic acids and hydroxylated fatty acids contribute to the bioactivity profile ([Baumli 2026, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jchromb.2026.124994)).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In the Kenetica frame, white deadnettle is a **cooling, mildly astringent mucosal remedy** — a Foundation-phase plant for surface work: irritated mucous membranes, minor catarrh, small wounds, and the general class of "weeping, over-secreting surfaces." Its action is gentle and cumulative rather than dramatic. The astringent tannins tighten and dry a relaxed, over-moist surface; the iridoids and phenylethanoids temper the inflammatory signalling underneath. It is a plant to reach for when the tissue picture is *lax and leaking* rather than *hot and acute*.

The traditional leucorrhoea use is reframed here honestly: not as colour-magic but as a topical astringent-anti-inflammatory wash for irritated genito-urinary mucosa, with the strong caveat that discharge is a symptom requiring diagnosis, not a condition to self-treat.

### Scientific Correlation

The evidence is **moderate for anti-inflammatory/antioxidant activity, thin-to-absent for the specific traditional indications, and honest about its nulls.**

- **Anti-inflammatory (mechanistic, supported).** Isolated constituents — verbascoside, phlinoside D, and iridoids — inhibited IL-8 secretion by human neutrophils by roughly 29–50% at 25 µM; notably, shanzhiside methyl ester did **not** significantly inhibit IL-8, a clean negative within the same study, and the compounds were generally weaker against TNF-α than IL-8. This grades the anti-inflammatory claim as *real but selective*, not blanket ([Czerwińska 2018, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules23112770)).
- **Antioxidant / enzyme inhibition (supported, preliminary).** Ethanolic extracts showed DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging and inhibition of α-amylase, α-glucosidase, lipase and acetylcholinesterase in an on-plate (HPTLC effect-directed) screen — promising but *in vitro* and preliminary ([Baumli 2026, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jchromb.2026.124994)).
- **Haemostatic (animal, supported for one extract).** In Wistar rats, the butanolic *L. album* extract shortened bleeding and reversed anticoagulant effect comparably to vitamin K, attributed to 8-acetylshanzhiside methyl ester; the *L. purpureum* extract did **not** show this in the anticoagulant test — again, an honest split result ([Bubueanu 2019, DOI](https://doi.org/10.2478/acph-2019-0026)).
- **Antiviral (in vitro).** Lamiridosins A/B inhibited hepatitis C virus *entry* (IC50 ≈ 2.31 µM); this is an interesting molecular finding with no bearing on traditional use ([Zhang 2009, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/np900549e)).
- **Anti-inflammatory in stroke model (animal).** An *L. album* extract altered caspase-3 and COX-2 expression in a rat middle-cerebral-artery-occlusion model — congener-to-indication distance is large; do not read as a stroke therapy ([Khanaki 2022, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3897/folmed.64.e60562)).

**The null that matters:** there is **no controlled clinical evidence** retrieved this run for leucorrhoea, wound healing, or expectorant use in humans. The colour signature ("white flowers → white discharge") has **no pharmacological basis** — it is a naming coincidence pressed into doctrine. What survives scrutiny is a generic astringent, mildly anti-inflammatory tissue action consistent with the tannin/iridoid/phenylethanoid chemistry. Data-sparse: say so plainly to any student.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Cool and drying, with a mild astringent-resolving quality. It answers *lax, over-moist, weeping* surface presentations, not hot acute inflammation. In the Kenetica humoral map it sits among the gentle mucous-membrane tonics — closer to a corrective than a purge.

### Contraindications

None serious are recorded; the acute-toxicity tests in rats found no toxicity for the extracts ([Bubueanu 2019, DOI](https://doi.org/10.2478/acph-2019-0026)). Nonetheless: **pregnancy caution** — the documented haemostatic/uterine-adjacent traditional uses and the lack of safety data in pregnancy mean it should be avoided in pregnancy unless directed by a qualified practitioner. Abnormal vaginal discharge or non-healing wounds require medical diagnosis, not self-treatment. Possible (rare) Lamiaceae sensitivity. **[USER: verify]** interactions with anticoagulant therapy, given the demonstrated vitamin-K-like haemostatic action.
