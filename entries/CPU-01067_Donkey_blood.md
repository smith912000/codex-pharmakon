# Donkey blood — Ass's Blood (Bryan's Ebers) / Donkey blood (rGyud-bzhi)
## *Blood of the domestic donkey or ass; the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary prints the donkey as "Aquus asinus Linnaeus" (sic)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP, FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The English text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 *The Papyrus Ebers*, a translation with summaries; the Egyptian words are not printed. The spine row (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 85, "Donkey's blood", *Equus asinus*, source field "Ebers; Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus") is a list row whose second source was not relied on. The text locus is in Bryan's Chapter on the eyes, under the heading "To prevent the hair growing into the eye after it has been pulled out" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` lines 2879-2894): "Incense-ground-in-Lizard's-dung, Cow's Blood, Ass's Blood, Pig's Blood, Dog's Blood, Stag's Blood, Collyrium, Incense: Crush, rub into one in the different kinds of Blood, and place on the part whence the Hair has been pulled out so that it may not grow again." This follows a remedy for trichiasis (ingrown eyelashes) of myrrh with lizard's blood and bat's blood, "Tear out the Hairs and put thereon" (lines 2862-2870), and is followed by "Another" of bat's blood, the rim of a new *hennu* vessel and honey (lines 2896-2905). In his survey of the animal materia medica Bryan names the Ass as taking "pride of place": "For the relief of suffering humanity he sacrificed his blood, dung, ears, fat, head, hoof, jawbone, liver, milk, tallow, teeth, testicles, and semen" (lines 1427-1428). Of the Ebers donkey parts, the blood appears in Bryan's text only in the one compound above. The donkey is thus listed by Bryan in a mixture of five bloods; no remedy using donkey blood alone was found. Whether Bryan's "Ass" always renders the same Egyptian word cannot be told from the translation. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** In Clark's translation of the Explanatory Tantra ch.20, under "Blood", one of the thirteen medicines of animal origin: "Pig blood: collects [scattered types of] poisoning and brown phlegm. Donkey blood: cures rheumatism and lymph disorders in the joints." (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 1176; the "Blood" heading is at line 1171). The list of bloods in that section is deer, goat, wild yak and Hodgson's antelope, pig, donkey, rooster's comb and womb blood (lines 1172-1176). Men-Tsee-Khang 2011: "Donkey blood cures arthritis and an excess accumulation of chhuser in the joints" (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` lines 3540-3541). The two versions render the first disorder differently ("rheumatism" in Clark, "arthritis" in Men-Tsee-Khang) and the second as "lymph disorders" and "chhuser". The spine's rGyud-bzhi row is `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 359 ("Donkey blood", id uncertain: yes). The Men-Tsee-Khang glossary on animals prints "Donkey" with "Aquus asinus Linnaeus" (lines 7034-7036, as OCR'd).

Dioscorides's chapter on blood (2-97, line 5363) names the blood of many animals, among them goose, duck, pigeon, hare, bull and stallion horses, and does not name the ass; the entry is therefore not tagged DMM. His ass's liver (2-42) and ass's hooves (2-44) are other substances; the hoof is entry III_19.

### Active Components

Blood is a fluid tissue of cells (red cells, white cells, platelets) in plasma. In general, red cells carry haemoglobin, so blood is a source of iron and protein; plasma carries albumin, globulins, clotting factors, lipids, hormones and electrolytes. Donkey blood is equine blood, with no ingredient recorded in the texts as the active one. No quantities are given here because none were read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources record donkey blood for two purposes: as one of five bloods in an Egyptian compound applied to prevent regrowth of a pulled eyelash, and, in the Tibetan text, as a medicine for joint disorders. The Codex has no protocol for either, and the reasons are plain: raw animal blood is an infection risk, the sources give no dose, the eye is a place where an unsterile animal product can cause blindness, and the use means killing or bleeding the animal. Donkey-hide gelatin, which has its own entry in the Codex (III_03, E-Jiao), is a different substance and is not covered by what is said here.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation given is the Egyptian one, as Bryan renders it: the bloods and collyrium and incense (the latter ground in lizard's dung) crushed together, "rubbed into one in the different kinds of Blood", and placed on the part where the hair has been pulled out. The Tibetan texts give the action only, with no method in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Blood carries a risk of transmitting blood-borne and zoonotic pathogens; equines carry some, such as glanders and equine encephalitis viruses (these are not found in all regions). Rheumatism and arthritis are conditions of the joints with many causes; no established treatment uses donkey blood. Eyelash regrowth after plucking is a biological fact and is not affected by animal blood. No clinical studies of the traditional uses were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** None is printed; Bryan's translation gives the compound by ingredient and complaint.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The translated lines print an action only ("cures rheumatism and lymph disorders in the joints", Clark; "cures arthritis and an excess accumulation of chhuser in the joints", Men-Tsee-Khang), with no taste, potency or humour assignment.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* The only locus is an eyelash remedy in which donkey blood is one of five bloods rubbed with incense and collyrium. It records what was done for a complaint. It does not say what the blood does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); Bryan reports no recitation with this compound, and since he summarises, that is not proof the papyrus gives none. No deity is attributed. Because Ebers identifications are modern hypotheses, "Ass's Blood" is Bryan's English rendering, and the spine row (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 85) rests on a list that cites Wikipedia as well as the papyrus. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Donkey blood is one of the seven bloods in the Explanatory Tantra ch.20 list of animal medicines and is given for one purpose, joint disorders of the *lymph* (*chhuser*) kind. The lines read name no essence-extraction (*bcud len*) use of any animal blood, and no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition; none is asserted. The spine marks the rGyud-bzhi row "id uncertain".

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do. Donkey blood in these sources is a plain drug for a plain complaint. A cultivator would take from the entry only that the Tibetan text lists blood by animal and by disorder, and that the Egyptian compound sets five bloods side by side with no distinction.

### Contraindications

- **Infection.** Raw animal blood can carry bacteria, viruses and parasites; never apply it to broken skin or the eye, or drink it. [USER: verify specifics]
- **Eye.** The Egyptian use is at the eyelid margin; an unsterile product there risks infection of the eye.
- **Dose unknown.** No dose is given in the sources read.
- **Ethics and law.** Using blood means bleeding or killing the animal. Donkeys are protected from cruelty by animal-welfare law in many places; trade in donkey products is regulated in some.
