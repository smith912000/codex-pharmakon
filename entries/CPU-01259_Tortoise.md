# Tortoise — "Tortoise", "Tortoise-shell", "Brain-of-a-Tortoise", "Tail-of-a-Tortoise" (Bryan 1930, Ebers Papyrus; no Egyptian word printed in the list)
## *The tortoise, as Bryan's English identification names it; the list gives no Egyptian word and no binomial* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The only text on disk for this tradition is C. P. Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). In his chapter VII, "Organic Remedies", Bryan says a full list of the animal materia medica "would not be possible", because "the identity of a considerable number of the animals mentioned is still beyond us", and then lists those "whose identity is not in doubt, and who were called upon to sacrifice themselves in the cause of the Healing Art" (about lines 1385-1392). The tortoise is in that list: "Tortoise Lizard Mole Vulture" (line 1394). The extract row (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, row 101, "Bryan 1930, Ch. VII Organic Remedies (animals) (line 1394)") records the same and adds that Bryan's list gives "no Egyptian word or binomial". Identification is therefore Bryan's English word, and in this corpus the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis, since the papyrus is organised by complaint, not by substance. The Egyptian word and the species of tortoise are not stated in anything on disk.

Bryan also says what was taken from the tortoise. After the list of animals he lists the parts the Egyptians took, and says of the tortoise: "They also betrayed an acquaintance, amateurish perhaps, but still an acquaintance with psychology when they limited their Tortoise preparations to his brain, of which they knew so little, and his shell, of which they knew so much" (lines 1425-1426). That is Bryan's summary, and the recipes below show it is not complete: one prints a tail.

The recipes in which Bryan's text names the tortoise, in the order they occur:

1. *Abscess (the "uases" abscess).* "One that cures the abscess by 'making the blood fall' is composed of Man's Milk, Granite, Tortoise-shell, and Trash-from-the-Knifestone" (line 2355). Bryan notes that the scribe did not describe the abscess.
2. *Stinking ulcer.* "ANOTHER REMEDY TO DRIVE OUT A STINKING ULCER IN THE BODY OF A MAN OR WOMAN: Ege-of-an-Ostrich, Tortoise-shell" (line 2362). A paste of "Ostrich Egg, Tortoise-shell, Thorns-of-the-am-tree" is printed, to "Warm and anoint therewith", with Bryan's remark "But DON'T GET TIRED DOING IT" (line 2694). An unguent of ostrich egg ground with tortoise-shell and thorns of the *am*-tree is also given "to check excessive granulation" (line 2411), which Bryan doubts.
3. *The tail.* In the women's section that Bryan introduces at about line 2504, the recipe "ANOTHER: Tail-of-a-Tortoise, Shell-of-a-Beetle, *sefet*-oil, *sert*-juice, Oil. Crush into one and poultice therewith" (line 2541). Bryan's printing of the recipe gives no complaint of its own. It is the only recipe I found that uses a part other than brain or shell.
4. *Eyes, leucoma (white growth).* Bryan: "Finally they resorted to Magic, plus the Brain-of-a-Tortoise mixed with Honey" (line 2804). The recipe is headed "ANOTHER TO DRIVE OUT THE WHITE GROWTH IN THE EYES", and the text printed is a spell: "There is a Shouting in the Southern Sky in the Darkness. There is an Uproar in the Northern Sky. The Hall of Pillars falls into the Waters. The Ship-folk of the Sun-god beat their oars so that the heads at his side fall into the water. Who leads hither what he finds? I lead forth what I find. I lead forth your heads. I lift up your necks. I fasten what has been cut from you in its place. I lead you forth to drive away the God of Fevers and all possible Deadly Arts." It is followed by the direction "A formula to repeat over the Brain-of-a-Tortoise that is mixed in Honey, and then laid on the Eyes" (lines 2806-2819). I quote it as Bryan prints it and name no deity beyond the words he prints.
5. *Eyes, squint.* "TO DRIVE AWAY SQUINTING IN THE EYES: Tortoise-brain, *abra*-ointment. Apply to the Eyes" (lines 2997-3004). Bryan comments that the tortoise was chosen "because of the comparative rarity of cross-eyed tortoises in Ancient Egypt" (line 3009), a remark of his own wit and not a reason given by the papyrus.
6. *Ear.* "MAKE FOR HIM THE REMEDY WHICH DRIES THE WOUNDS: Head-of-the-*amamu*-animal, Gazelle's Ears, Tortoise-shell, *annek*-plant. Very frequently this stops it completely" (lines 3066-3067), in the part of the book on ear, nose and mouth. The *amamu* animal and the *annek* plant are Bryan's untranslated Egyptian words and I do not identify them.
7. *Hair.* In the hair chapter, among the remedies to preserve the hair and remove grey hairs: "a third is composed of Tortoise-shell and the Neck-of-the-*gabgu*-bird, cooked in Oil" (line 4033). The last of the three closing prescriptions "to remove superfluous hairs, recommends the frequent rubbing in of a mixture compounded of Tortoise-shell crushed and heated in Fat-from-the-Hoof-of-a-Hippopotamus" (line 4074).

I also looked at `Sources/EP_Ebers/EP_Original_Hieroglyphic.md`, a project file of Ebbell and Bryan renderings. At line 450 it prints "Prescription 412": "For the aaa-disease in the eyes: bile of a tortoise; honey; verdigris [malachite]. Apply to the eyes." Bryan's text, which is the one source on disk I can check, says the tortoise preparations were limited to brain and shell, and I found no tortoise bile in it. I do not rely on the project file's recipe, and its attribution cannot be checked here. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, a tortoise shell is a bony shell covered by plates of keratin, a protein, with calcium phosphate in the bone. The brain is soft nervous tissue, mostly water and lipid. Nothing in the entry links any constituent to the uses given.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Ebers material, as Bryan prints it, uses tortoise-shell in remedies for ulcers, an abscess, an ear discharge and hair, the brain in eye remedies for a white growth and a squint, and once a tail. The Codex does not recommend any of them: ulcers and an ear discharge need examination, an eye growth or squint needs an eye examination, and animal tissue on an eye, an ulcer or the scalp carries infection. Many tortoise species are threatened and protected in law. The entry is a record of what Bryan reports.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted: ingredient lists, with Bryan's printed directions "Warm and anoint therewith", "Crush into one and poultice therewith", "Apply to the Eyes", and for the brain in honey "laid on the Eyes". Bryan gives no quantity for any ingredient and no length of course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. A white growth in the eye may be a cataract, a corneal scar or another condition and is assessed by an eye specialist; applying animal brain or any unsterile material to the eye risks serious infection and loss of sight. Squint in a child needs early assessment. Ulcers that stink are usually infected and need cleaning and treatment. Keratin is a largely indigestible protein. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Egyptian (EP):** None. Bryan's list and the recipes quoted give no quality (hot, cold, moist, dry) for the tortoise or its parts. The Ebers corpus is organised by complaint and records what was done for a condition, not a classification of the substance.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. The tortoise appears in recipes for ulcers, an abscess, the ear, hair and the eyes, and none assigns it a place in the person. The frame that does apply is *heka*: in the Egyptian practice spell and drug are components of one intervention, and the tortoise brain in honey for the white growth is printed with a spell to be repeated over it (lines 2806-2819). That is the one recipe in which a recitation and the tortoise are joined in what I read. For the other recipes Bryan reports no recitation, and since he summarises, that is no proof that the papyrus gives none. The spell does not mention the tortoise, and Bryan names no deity as attached to it.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The tortoise appears in the Ebers material as a source of shell and brain in ordinary remedies, one of them joined to a spell, and the Codex does not recommend the medical uses. What the record does show is that the drug and its recitation were one act, so that taking the brain without the words would have been, for the papyrus, half the prescription. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Eyes:** do not put animal brain, honey from an unknown source or any unsterile material on the eye; white growths and squint need an eye examination.
- **Ulcers, abscess and ear discharge:** need examination; do not apply animal shell, brain or ash to open sores or into the ear.
- **Infection and hygiene:** wild-animal tissue carries bacteria and parasites; reptiles in particular carry *Salmonella*.
- **Conservation and law:** many tortoise and turtle species are threatened or protected in law; the Codex does not recommend taking any animal for these uses.
