V).** `Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`, lines 1193 and 1194, print the oil group as "Oil Castor Oil Olive Oil White Oil Oil-of-Two-Days Oil-of-the-Earth (Petroleum) Oil-from-the-Top-of-the-des-jar Pieces-of-dried Oil". - **Bryan's list of plants (Ch. VI).** The whole Bryan text was searched for "Moringa", "Behen" and the word "ben", with no hit.
- **Oils named in the prescriptions.** Besides the ones above, the prescriptions print "sefet-oil" (lines 2325 and 2541), "aau-of-samu-oil" (line 2688), "abra-oil" (line 2718, in a course of head-anointing: Fish-oil on the second day, Hippopotamus Oil on the third, "abra-oil" on the fourth), "Fresh Oil" and "Goose-oil" (line 2644). Bryan gives no identification for "sefet", "samu" or "abra" in these lines. They are not equated here with any tree oil, and no line read links any of them to *Moringa*.
- **Ghalioungui list rows.** `_sources/ebers.csv` has two further rows, "Moringa" (row 134) and "Moringa Aptera" (row 135), both attributed to the Ghalioungui 1987 translation, Glossary I (Medicaments), with identification uncertain. The Ghalioungui text is not on disk. These rows are the only place in the repository's Ebers files where moringa is named as an Ebers drug, and they are list rows with no locus. The spine row names *M. peregrina* while the Ghalioungui row is headed "Moringa Aptera", and no source on disk says whether these are meant as one tree. [USER: verify]

**A file not relied on.** `Sources/EP_Ebers/EP_Original_Hieroglyphic.md` prints a line about "oil of moringa" in an Ebers-style prescription and gives the species as *Moringa oleifera*. The line was not found in the Bryan text, the file is a hieroglyphic overview of unclear provenance, and the task rules bar it as authority. It is not used here, and its conflicting species (*M. oleifera* against the row's *M. peregrina*) is reported as a source-data conflict.

### Active Components

Seed oil from trees of the genus *Moringa* is generally known as a pale, stable oil rich in oleic acid, and in antiquity such oils were used as perfume bases and ointments. The oil of the tree on this row, as an Ebers material, was not read. [USER: verify]
