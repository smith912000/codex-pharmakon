# Ashwagandha — Winter Cherry · Indian Ginseng · Asgandh · Aśvagandhā

## *Withania somnifera* (L.) Dunal | Family: Solanaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SS, FMT, CM
**Energetic Classification:** Warming and drying in the second degree; unctuous (*snigdha*) despite its heat — a rare "hot yet building" tonic in the Unani-Galenic frame.
**Training Phase:** Foundation / Consolidation — a Rasāyana anchor for the rebuilding phase, bridging into Restoration through its *somnifera* sedative pole.

---

### Origin & Tradition

Few plants carry their whole doctrine inside their two names. The Sanskrit *aśvagandhā* splits into *aśva* (horse) and *gandha* (smell): the fresh root smells rankly of horse. But the classical commentators never let the etymology rest on odour alone — the name simultaneously promises that the one who takes it acquires *the strength and virility of a stallion*. The Latin binomial supplies the opposite pole: *somnifera*, "sleep-bringing." One organism, two doctrines — a daytime tonic of vigour and a night-time bringer of rest. Modern pharmacology, unusually, finds a mechanism that reconciles both.

**Sushruta Samhita & the Ayurvedic Rasāyana canon (SS).** Ashwagandha is classed as a premier *Rasāyana* (rejuvenative), a *balya* (strength-giver), and a *medhya* (intellect/nootropic) drug. In the *bṛṃhaṇa* (nourishing, tissue-building) therapeutics it is prescribed for emaciation, *kṣaya* (wasting/consumption), debility of old age, and to promote *śukra* (reproductive essence/semen). Its *rasa* is predominantly bitter and astringent with a sweet *vipāka* (post-digestive effect), and it is *vāta-kapha* pacifying — the doctrine of a warm, grounding, anabolic drug that settles the nervous, mobile *vāta* while building tissue. The horse-strength doctrine is explicit and formulaic: ashwagandha is the archetypal *vājīkaraṇa*-adjacent tonic, given to restore the depleted.

**The Four Tantras / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** In the Tibetan materia medica the root (transliterated variously as *a-shwa-gandha* / *ba-dzi-gandha* in the imported Ayurvedic stratum) enters as a warming tonic for *rlung* (wind) disorders — the Tibetan analogue of *vāta*. *rLung* governs mobility, mind, sleep, and the nervous animation of the body; its derangement produces insomnia, anxiety, restlessness, palpitations, and wasting. A warm, heavy, oily, sedating drug is the textbook *rlung*-pacifier, and ashwagandha is deployed exactly there — for sleeplessness and nervous exhaustion. **Avicenna / the Canon of Medicine (CM).** In the Perso-Arabic Unani tradition the drug is *asgandh* (اسگند), naturalised from the Sanskrit. Unani physicians classed it as hot and dry, a *muqawwī* (strengthener) and aphrodisiac, used for general debility, "cold" nervous conditions, and to fortify the reproductive faculty — the same strength-and-virility doctrine arriving through a different door. **Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** The horse is the traditional emblem of contained power — musculature, stamina, potency, nervous energy under harness. The "smell of a horse" doctrine is not merely a sensory joke: it is a signature-of-strength claim, a folk-Paracelsian reading in which the beast whose vigour the drug confers is inscribed in its very odour. Map this onto the chemistry and the metaphor holds with surprising fidelity. Ashwagandha's active principles are the **withanolides** — steroidal lactones built on an ergostane skeleton, i.e. molecules structurally adjacent to the body's own cholesterol-derived steroid hormones. The "strength of a stallion" doctrine sits atop a class of compounds that genuinely modulate the steroidal/endocrine axis: clinical work shows suppression of the catabolic stress-steroid cortisol and, in some trials, elevation of testosterone — the exact hormonal signature (low catabolism, higher anabolism) that a "strength-building" agent should produce. The horse doctrine is, in effect, a pre-modern intuition about steroid balance.

The *somnifera* pole is the reconciling insight. A naïve reading would call a "strengthener" and a "sleep-bringer" contradictory. But the unifying physiology is the **hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis**: chronic stress elevates cortisol, which simultaneously wastes muscle, degrades sleep, and corrodes mood. A drug that damps HPA overdrive would, by a single action, build tissue *and* restore sleep — vigour by day, rest by night. The two names are not opposites; they are two visible faces of one endocrine correction. The ancient nomenclature encoded a mechanism the pharmacology only named in this century.

### Active Components

- **Withanolides** — the defining C28 steroidal lactones: **withaferin A** (the most studied, strongly anti-inflammatory and pro-apoptotic in vitro), **withanolide A**, **withanolide D**, and the **withanoside** glycosides (withanosides IV–VII, sitoindosides). Roots are richer in the glycowithanolides; leaves concentrate withaferin A.
- **Steroidal alkaloids** — withanine, somniferine, tropine-class bases (historically credited with the sedative effect).
- **Sitoindosides VII–X** — glycosylated withanolides linked to the anti-stress and immunomodulatory activity in animal models.
- **Iron, and a modest saponin/phytosterol fraction** contributing to the traditional "building" reputation.

Commercial extracts are typically standardised to total withanolides (commonly 1.5–5%); named actives in the trial literature include **KSM-66** (root-only, aqueous) and **Shoden** (a high-withanolide root-and-leaf extract).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Ashwagandha is the Codex's principal **adaptogenic anchor for the Foundation phase** — the drug given when a practitioner is depleted, over-aroused, sleeping poorly, and losing ground on physical work. Its dual pole makes it unusually versatile: dosed by day it blunts the stress response and supports strength adaptation; dosed in the evening it shortens sleep-onset latency and deepens rest. It is the reference example in the Codex of a *single botanical spanning two training phases* — a bridge drug from Consolidation (tissue-building under load) into Restoration (parasympathetic recovery). Pair with a stimulant-sparing protocol; it is subtractive (it removes a stress brake) rather than additive (it does not stimulate).

### Scientific Correlation

Based on articles retrieved from PubMed, ashwagandha has a genuinely stronger clinical evidence base than most traditional tonics — though the trials are predominantly small, Indian, single-centre, and frequently sponsored by extract manufacturers, so the effect sizes should be read as promising rather than settled.

**Cortisol / stress (the HPA doctrine).** Chandrasekhar et al. (2012), a 60-day double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 64 chronically-stressed adults on 300 mg twice-daily full-spectrum root extract, reported significant reductions across all stress scales (P<0.0001) and a substantial fall in serum cortisol (P=0.0006) versus placebo ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0253-7176.106022)). Lopresti et al. (2019), a 60-day RCT of 240 mg/day Shoden extract in stressed adults, found significantly greater reductions in morning cortisol (P<0.001) and DHEA-S (P=0.004), with a significant fall on the Hamilton Anxiety scale ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000017186)). The 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis by Arumugam et al. (9 RCTs, 558 patients) confirmed pooled benefit on the Perceived Stress Scale (MD −4.72), Hamilton Anxiety (MD −2.19) and serum cortisol (MD −2.58 µg/dL) versus placebo ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.explore.2024.103062)). This is the best-supported claim: the HPA-damping mechanism proposed above is directly instrumented by measured cortisol drops.

**Sleep (the *somnifera* doctrine — supported).** Langade et al. (2020), a double-blind placebo-controlled study in 80 subjects (healthy + insomnia arms), found root extract significantly improved sleep-onset latency and sleep efficiency (both P<0.0001), total sleep time (P<0.002) and Hamilton Anxiety, with the largest gains in the insomnia subgroup ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2020.113276)). The species epithet's promise is, unusually for a folk sedative claim, borne out in controlled data.

**Strength / testosterone (the horse doctrine — real but narrower).** Wankhede et al. (2015), an 8-week RCT in 57 resistance-training men on 300 mg twice daily, reported significantly greater gains in bench-press 1-RM (+46.0 vs +26.4 kg; P=0.001) and leg-extension strength, larger arm and chest muscle size, reduced exercise-induced muscle damage (creatine kinase), a greater rise in testosterone (+96.2 vs +18.0 ng/dL; P=0.004), and greater fat-mass loss ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-015-0104-9)). The stallion-strength doctrine thus has genuine, if single-trial and industry-adjacent, human support. **Honest deflation:** the testosterone signal is inconsistent across studies — in Lopresti's stress trial, testosterone rose in males over time but the change was *not* statistically significant versus placebo (P=0.158) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000017186)). The endocrine "virility" claim is best stated as: reliably anti-catabolic (cortisol down), variably pro-androgenic (testosterone sometimes up).

**Evidence grade:** Moderate-to-good by herbal standards for stress/anxiety/sleep (multiple RCTs + meta-analysis, converging on a plausible HPA mechanism); weaker/single-trial for strength and testosterone; near-universal caveat of small samples, short duration, and sponsor involvement.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Ayurveda (SS):** *Rasa* — tikta (bitter), kaṣāya (astringent), with madhura (sweet) undertone · *Guṇa* — laghu/snigdha (light yet unctuous) · *Vīrya* — uṣṇa (heating) · *Vipāka* — madhura (sweet). *Karma:* Rasāyana, balya, medhya, vājīkaraṇa-supportive; *vāta-kapha* pacifying, *pitta* aggravating in excess.
- **Tibetan (FMT):** warming, heavy, oily — a *rlung* (wind) pacifier for insomnia and nervous depletion.
- **Unani / Galenic (CM):** hot and dry (2nd degree), *muqawwī* (strengthener) and *bāh* (aphrodisiac); unctuous building quality.
- **TCM:** not classically attested — ashwagandha is not a Chinese materia medica drug; any *ren shen*-analogue framing is modern cross-mapping, not traditional.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** As *asgandh* (اسگند) the root is classed hot and dry, a *muqawwī* (strengthener) and *bāh*/aphrodisiac worked for cold nervous debility and to fortify the generative faculty. The strongest cultivation idea the Unani corpus carries is the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture — the finite vital endowment whose depletion is ageing, so that longevity practice is conservation rather than acquisition — and a warming, unctuous strengthener sits naturally within that regimen of husbanding the innate heat. But that is the frame of the whole restorative regimen, not a discrete cultivation status the tradition assigns to this species, and, as the entry flags, it is uncertain whether *asgandh* belongs to the *Canon* proper or arrives through the later Unani compendia that naturalised it from Sanskrit. Attested as a strengthener; no per-substance cultivation status independently sourced. **(b) framed.**

**Ayurveda (SS).** Here the cultivation status is explicit and canonical. Aśvagandhā is named among the ten *balya* (strength-giving) drugs of the *balya mahākaṣāya* in the Charaka Saṃhitā (Sūtrasthāna 4), and it enters the *vājīkaraṇa* (generative-vitality) therapeutics as a formulary ingredient in the same text's Cikitsāsthāna (the *vājīkaraṇa* ghṛta of Cikitsāsthāna 2), consistent with the tradition's reading of it as a *rasāyana* (rejuvenative), *balya* and *medhya* drug that builds the *dhātus*, restores the wasted, and promotes *śukra*. This is a genuine formal standing in Ayurveda's own cultivation vocabulary — *rasāyana* and *vājīkaraṇa* are Sushrutan/Caraka divisions, not modern glosses — attributed to *this* species by a named classical primary text. Cultivation status: **(a) attested** (Charaka Saṃhitā, *balya mahākaṣāya* Sū. 4; *vājīkaraṇa* formulation Ci. 2).

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** In the Tibetan materia medica the root enters through the imported Ayurvedic stratum as a warm, heavy, oily drug deployed to pacify *rlung* (wind) — the humour governing mobility, mind and sleep, whose derangement gives insomnia, restlessness and wasting. Sowa Rigpa holds that medicine is the third of the four treatment branches and reaches the symptom, not the spiritual root (the three *nyes pa* deriving from the three poisons), and it recognises no formal category of "substances for subtle-body practice"; the essence-extraction discipline of *bcud len* is a distinct genre and is not attested for this root in the sources retrieved. So while the drug is genuinely used, no cultivation status in the tradition's own terms is recorded for it. **(b) framed** (used as a *rlung*-pacifier; no *bcud len* or cultivation status attested).

**Practitioner's note.** Of the three tagged traditions, only Ayurveda gives ashwagandha a true cultivation standing — and it is a strong one: a *rasāyana*/*balya* root taken over weeks, classically in milk with ghee or honey (*kṣīrapāka*), to rebuild the depleted and, in the *vājīkaraṇa* register, to restore generative vigour. A cultivator uses it exactly there: a warm, grounding, anabolic tonic for the over-aroused and wasted, dosed for a defined rebuilding phase rather than stimulated with. The Unani strengthener frame agrees in spirit; the Tibetan use is real but symptomatic. The modern adaptogen/HPA reading is a separate register and should not be mistaken for the classical *rasāyana* claim, though the two point the same direction.

### Contraindications

- **Thyroid.** Ashwagandha can raise thyroid hormone output (case reports and mechanistic data of increased T4/T3). **Caution or avoid in hyperthyroidism and in those on thyroid-hormone replacement** (risk of additive thyrotoxic effect). Reassuringly, the dedicated safety RCT by Verma et al. (2020), 300 mg twice daily for 8 weeks in 80 healthy adults, found *no* statistically significant change in thyroid parameters, hepatic or haematological markers, and no adverse events ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctim.2020.102642)) — so the thyroid risk is a real but dose/host-dependent signal, not a universal effect. Monitor in thyroid patients.
- **Pregnancy.** Traditionally regarded as abortifacient/emmenagogue; **avoid in pregnancy.** Caution in lactation (insufficient safety data).
- **Autoimmune disease.** Immunostimulant/immunomodulant activity — use caution in lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, Hashimoto's, MS, or with immunosuppressant therapy.
- **Sedative / CNS-depressant interaction.** Additive with benzodiazepines, barbiturates, alcohol, and other sedatives — the *somnifera* effect compounds.
- **Hepatotoxicity.** Rare idiosyncratic liver-injury case reports exist in the pharmacovigilance literature (typically self-limiting cholestatic/hepatocellular injury on cessation). Uncommon against a broadly reassuring safety record, but discontinue with any signs of jaundice or hepatic dysfunction.
