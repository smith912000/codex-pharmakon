# Azure stone hollow — Kong Qing (空青) "Azuritum" (Yang 1998); with Ceng Qing (曾青), Bai Qing (白青), Bian Qing and Fu Qing (膚青)
## *The blue copper carbonate ore in the Divine Farmer's Classic, Yang 1998 "Azuritum"; Kong Qing is the round form with a hollow centre, and the other Qing entries are given by Yang as shapes or colours of the same ore, with Fu Qing carrying a footnote that it may be Malachitum* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Kong Qing is printed in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) on PDF page 23 under "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (heading line 907; names row `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` row 5, line 908; list `_sources/shennong.csv` 空青). The text (lines 908-913): "Kong Qing (Azuritum) is sweet and cold. It mainly treats clear-eye blindness and deafness, brightens the eyes, disinhibits the nine orifices, frees the flow of the blood vessels, and nurtures the essence spirit. Protracted taking may make the body light, prolong life, and prevent senility. It is able to transform copper, iron, lead, and tin into gold. It is produced in mountains and valleys." Yang's footnote 37 (lines 927-931) says: "This medicinal and the next three are all derived from the same mineral source. They all exist in nature in the form of ore. However, this ore may be found in different shapes and, hence, the three different ingredients. Kong Qing is a round shape with a hollow center. Ceng Qing is found in stratified layers. Bian Qing is found in short cylinders. Bai Qing is the same substance as Bian Qing but is white." "Azuritum" is the label Yang prints for all of them. The spine's note is "high", and its merge note records that the row for Bai Qing (CPU-00209) was folded into this one as a variety.

### Varieties

**Ceng Qing** (SBJ; Yang line 914, PDF p.23, Superior Class). "Sour and a little cold. It mainly treats eye pain, relieves tearing and wind impediment, disinhibits the joints, frees the nine orifices, and breaks concretions and conglomerations, accumulations and gatherings. Protracted taking may make the body light and prevent senility. It is able to transform into gold and copper. It is produced in mountains and valleys" (lines 914-919). Yang's footnote 37 says it is found in stratified layers. Listed in `_sources/shennong.csv` as 曾青.

**Bai Qing** (SBJ; Yang line 920, PDF p.23, Superior Class). "Sweet and balanced. It mainly brightens the eyes, disinhibits the nine orifices, [treats] deafness and evil qi below the heart, provokes vomiting in people, and kills various toxins and the three [kinds of] worms. Protracted taking may enable one to communicate with the spirit light, make the body light, prolong life, and prevent senility. It is produced in mountains and valleys" (lines 920-926). Footnote 37: "Bai Qing is the same substance as Bian Qing but is white." Footnote 39 (line 937) glosses "the three worms" as parasites within the body, citing Chao Yuan-fang for pinworms, roundworms and red worms. This is the variety that was a separate spine row (CPU-00209) before it was folded in.

**Bian Qing** (SBJ; Yang line 948, PDF p.24, Superior Class). "A little cold and nontoxic. It mainly treats eye pain, brightens the eyes, and [heals] fracture [caused by] falls as well as welling abscesses and swellings, and refractory incised wounds. It breaks accumulations and gatherings, resolves toxic qi, and disinhibits the essence spirit. Protracted taking may make the body light and prevent senility. It is produced in mountains and valleys" (lines 948-953). Footnote 37: found in short cylinders. It is not in the project's list `_sources/shennong.csv` (no row 扁青), though the names file has it.

**Fu Qing** (SBJ; Yang line 1207, PDF p.31, under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class", heading line 1168). "Acrid. It mainly treats gu toxins, snake toxins, and all toxins of vegetables and meats as well as malign wounds. Its other name is Tui Qing (Pushing Green-blue). It is produced in mountains and valleys" (lines 1207-1210). Yang's footnote 60 (line 1217): "This medicinal may also be Malachitum. Its identity is not certain." Listed as 膚青 (`_sources/shennong.csv` line 130). Fu Qing is the one Qing entry in the Middle class and is not an ore shape in footnote 37.

*Synthesis of the varieties.* The Classic separates the Qing entries by name and gives each its own flavour and indication; the only grounds the translator gives for the separation are shape and colour (round and hollow, stratified, cylindrical, white), in footnote 37. Four of the five (Kong, Ceng, Bai, Bian) stand in the Superior class and are each said to be taken "protracted" to "make the body light" or "prevent senility"; Kong Qing alone "nurtures the essence spirit", Bian Qing "disinhibits the essence spirit", and Bai Qing alone adds communication with "the spirit light". Kong and Ceng Qing carry the Classic's transmutation clause (into gold, or gold and copper). Fu Qing is set apart in rank: it is Middle class, it is "acrid" and not sweet or sour, it treats toxins and malign wounds, it has none of the long-taking lines, and Yang doubts whether it is azurite at all. The Classic ranks the four Superior entries first by class, and gives no ordering among them. Bai and Bian Qing are, by footnote 37, one substance in two colours.

### Active Components

Azurite is a deep blue basic copper(II) carbonate, Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2. It dissolves in acids and releases copper ions, and it is commonly found together with green malachite in the oxidised zones of copper deposits. Copper compounds are astringent and irritant and toxic when swallowed. The sources read give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records the ore for eye complaints, deafness, the nine orifices and (Fu Qing) poisons, with the elixir-tradition aims of a light body and a long life. A copper salt taken by mouth is toxic, and the Codex does not endorse the Classic's long-taking or transmutation claims. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is printed for any of the five entries. Ceng and Kong Qing are said to "transform" metal, which is an alchemical statement and not a preparation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Azurite and malachite have been used since antiquity as pigments, and copper compounds have antimicrobial and astringent properties. Copper salts swallowed in more than trace amounts cause vomiting and gastrointestinal, liver and kidney injury. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: an upper-grade drug (上品), for Kong Qing, Ceng Qing, Bai Qing and Bian Qing; Fu Qing is middle-grade.* Kong Qing stands under "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (Yang line 907). The preface says superior class medicinals "mainly nourish life and correspond to heaven. They are nontoxic and taking them in large amounts and for a long time will not harm people" (lines 352-355), while the middle class "mainly nurture personality" and "may or may not be toxic" (lines 385-387). The monograph's own cultivation lines are "nurtures the essence spirit" and "protracted taking may make the body light, prolong life, and prevent senility" (lines 908-913). The text prints no *jing* claim and none is made here. For Bai Qing the text adds "communicate with the spirit light", which Yang's footnote 36 explains as a supernatural ability sought by Daoist adepts through "self-cultivation and the ingestion of elixirs". The paragraph speaks of Kong Qing, and of each variety by name, as printed; it is not widened to the whole of azurite. Fu Qing is Middle class and has no cultivation line. The "nontoxic" statements are the Classic's, in an elixir tradition that Yang warns made adepts ill (introduction, lines 321-328); the substance is a copper salt.

**Practitioner's note.** Four upper-grade entries and one middle-grade entry for a blue copper ore, in a tradition that held upper-grade stones safe for long use. The Codex cannot repeat that claim for a copper salt. Nothing here supports taking azurite; its use as a cultivator is as a record of the Daoist elixir aim. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Contraindications

Copper compounds are toxic by mouth, and powdered mineral preparations are unstandardised and may be contaminated with arsenic, lead or other metals. Identification is uncertain (Yang's footnote 60), so azurite, malachite and similar blue-green stones cannot be treated as the same substance. Purchase of mineral medicines is regulated in many places. The Classic's statements that the ore turns base metals into gold are alchemical and are not claims of fact.
