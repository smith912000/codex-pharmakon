### Origin & Tradition

**Ebers (EP).** In Bryan's English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`), the alphabetical list headed "MINERAL REMEDIES" has, after the oils of the "O" group, a line "Opal-resin" standing alone (line 1196), before the "Salt" group (line 1198). The names.csv row (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, "Opal-resin") carries the note "Bryan's English identification". The list gives names only.

A single use is read in the book, in Bryan's chapter on the diseases of the eyes (line 2734): a number of salves are given in each of which Collyrium figures, and "still another" is made "with Writing-fluid, Myrrh, Opal-resin, Arabian-woodpowder, and Saltpetre-from-Upper-Egypt". The salve is one of several for the eyes and the passage does not say what the opal-resin contributed.

The name's meaning is not explained. The word "resin" suggests a plant exudate, which would conflict with the "mineral" heading; "opal" may be Bryan's attempt at an Egyptian word that was never identified. Neither reading is adopted.
