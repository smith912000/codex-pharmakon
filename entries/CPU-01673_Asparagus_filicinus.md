# Asparagus filicinus — nye.shing, Nye shing (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Asparagus filicinus, the first of three binomials Clark 1995 prints for nye.shing (with Asparagus racemosus Willd and Polygonatum falcatum); the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary prints a different Asparagus by position beside the name "Nye shing"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** **Named only by the Tibetan texts in this row.** No other text read for this entry names the plant under this name. Both loci are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance", and the row's binomial is the first of three on Clark's line, the other two being a second *Asparagus* and a *Polygonatum*, a different genus. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed names (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1143).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The entry reads: "nye.shing: Asparagus filicinus: Asparagus racemosus Willd: Polygonatum falcatum: [has eighteen names. It has a tall, thin, thorny stalk and a seed resembling an iron pea. The root has bitter and astringent tastes and] cures disorders of the xiphoid process."

Clark's preface (line 82) says "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". So the eighteen names, the description (tall, thin, thorny stalk, iron-pea seed) and the bitter and astringent tastes of the root are the commentary layer. Outside the brackets are Clark's own identifications (the three Latin names, editorial and not root text) and the root-text clause "cures disorders of the xiphoid process". The text thus gives one root-layer indication and no taste or power outside the brackets.

The next entry, *ra.mnye* (line 1144, *Polygonatum*), ends with a sentence outside the brackets: "Both Asparagus racemosus and Polygonatum promote longevity and cure lymph disorders." It names the second binomial of *nye.shing* and not *Asparagus filicinus*. In the sweet-tasting group of chapter 19 (line 765, "Groups of Medicines Having the Same Taste" at line 764), Clark's list reads "liquorice, raisins, saffron, bamboo concretion, cassia pods, Polygonatum cirrifolium, Asparagus racemosus, Angelica, sugar-candy ...", which also prints *Asparagus racemosus*, and the whole line is outside the brackets. Clark's name index prints *nye.shing* (line 1696) and *Asparagus filicinus* (line 1557).

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (Explanatory Tantra).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. The name "Nye shing" appears in three places. In the first part (the Root Tantra, "Basic Tantra", therapeutic methods) a note (line 1144) reads: "The five roots are Ra nye, Nye shing, Chawa, Bhatu and Zema", to the passage on medicinal butters (line 1116). In chapter 19 (tastes), line 3204 lists "Shingngar, Gundrum, Gurkum, Chugang, Dongga, Ra nye, Nye shing, Chawa, sugar, molasses, honey, meat, butter and other similar substances" as "the group of medicinal substances having a sweet taste". In chapter 20, in the efficacy of herbal medicines (the section begins at line 3428), line 3502 reads: "Nye shing and Ra nye prolong life and cure chhuser disorders." That "Nye shing" is a form of *nye.shing* is a reading from the identical spelling, supported by the order of the two texts (*nye.shing* then *ra.mnye* in Clark, "Ra nye, Nye shing" in the Men-Tsee-Khang lists) and by the agreement that both prolong life. The text does not state it. [USER: verify]

*The glossary prints another Asparagus beside the name.* In the Men-Tsee-Khang Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, the "N" block (lines 5539 to 5570) prints nine phonetic names with "Nye shing" sixth (line 5541), and nine scientific names with "Asparagus adscendens" sixth (line 5563), and the English "Wild asparagus Marsh mallow" (line 5566). By column position the glossary's binomial for "Nye shing" is *Asparagus adscendens*, not *Asparagus filicinus*. The glossary does not print them on one row, and the Codex does not state the pairing. This is an identity doubt between Clark and the glossary for one Tibetan drug. The row stands for the Tibetan drug and not for one species. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Asparagus* is a genus of the family Asparagaceae and that roots and shoots of *Asparagus* species are generally known to contain steroidal saponins. Clark's third binomial is a *Polygonatum*, a genus of the same family. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the Tibetan drug, which may not be this species. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a drug that cures disorders of the xiphoid process (Clark line 1143, outside the brackets), that belongs to the sweet-tasting group and the "five roots" in the Men-Tsee-Khang text, and that, with *ra.mnye*, is said to "prolong life and cure chhuser disorders" (Men-Tsee-Khang line 3502) and to "promote longevity and cure lymph disorders" (Clark line 1144, naming *Asparagus racemosus*). These are the texts' own claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give one preparation in which the name stands: the "five roots" in medicinal butter (Men-Tsee-Khang lines 1116 and 1144). Clark names the root as the part (line 1143, "The root has bitter and astringent tastes", inside the brackets). No quantity, frequency or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. The Men-Tsee-Khang text places "Nye shing" in the sweet-tasting group (line 3204). The two tastes do not agree, and the Codex does not resolve it. No power is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica says of the group that it promotes longevity and cures lymph disorders (Clark line 1144, naming *Asparagus racemosus* and *Polygonatum*), and the Men-Tsee-Khang text says that "Nye shing and Ra nye prolong life" (line 3502). A life-prolonging action is a claim about what the drug does. Neither line uses the term *bcud len* (essence extraction) or assigns a place in a practice, so it is graded (b). The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. The phrase "highly recommended in essence-extraction compounds" in the *ra.mnye* entry is inside the brackets and belongs to *Polygonatum*, not to *nye.shing*. The rejuvenation chapters (Clark chapter 23, Men-Tsee-Khang chapter 23) were read for names and do not name *nye.shing*. The link from this binomial to the longevity lines rests on Clark's second binomial and on a spelling.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The Tibetan drug is a "five roots" drug said to prolong life, and the species behind it is not settled. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. The botanical identity is uncertain (three binomials in Clark and a fourth in the glossary), so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Disorders of the xiphoid process need medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
