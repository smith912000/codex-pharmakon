# Schrebera swietenioides — Mushkaka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation); glossary "Schrebera Switenioides"

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under the Sanskrit name Mushkaka.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Bhishagratna's English translation (1907 to 1916), read in the chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and in the volume files `Bhishagratna_Vol{1,2,3}_djvu.txt`. The spine row carries the Latin binomial as its name and the incoming check said "Key misspelled; ID block also lists Cyperus rotundus; verify alignment". The alignment was verified here (below). A search of the other texts on disk for *Schrebera* and *Mushkaka* found nothing outside the Sushruta Samhita.

**How the identification was checked.** The glossary "APPENDIX" in Vol. III prints the Sanskrit names on one leaf and the identifications on the following lines. In the name block the first entries of the leaf are "Mushkaka" (line 32344), "Mustaka", "Masta", then the *N* names ("Nadika", "Naga-dantika", "Naga-kesara", and onward). In the identification block (line 32408 region) the first three entries are "Schrebera Switenioides.", "Cyperus Rotundas.", and then a blank slot, followed by "Corchorus Olitorius.", "Helistropium Indicura." and "Mesua Ferrea." They line up in order: Mushkaka with *Schrebera*, Mustaka with *Cyperus rotundus* (compare the existing *Musta* row, `CPU-00368`), and the *N* group beginning with *Nadika* (*Corchorus*), *Naga-dantika* (*Heliotropium*) and *Naga-kesara* (*Mesua ferrea*) in the same order. The "Cyperus" line is therefore the identification of the next name (*Mustaka*), not a second identification of *Mushkaka*. The slot that would belong to "Masta" is blank or illegible in this scan, which is one more reason the pairing is marked for checking. The alignment depends on a stable column order across an OCR'd page, so it is a position-based reading and not a printed pairing. [USER: verify against the printed page]

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Sanskrit name is attested in the following places that were read.

1. *The Mushkakadi group (Sutra ch.38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 133 to 134; Vol. I line 15474).* "The group of medicinal drugs known as the Mushkakadi consists of Mushkaka, Palasha, Dhava, Chitraka, Madana, Shinshapa, Vajra-Vriksha and Triphala." The metrical text after it says the group "is possessed of the therapeutic virtue of destroying fat and removing the defects of semen", and that "Meha, piles, jaundice, chlorosis, gravels and urinary calculi in the bladder are the diseases which yield to its curative efficacy" (same chapter, Vol. I line 15478 onward). The group is named after Mushkaka, the first drug, and this is the only place that gives it a group action in the text.
2. *Skin diseases (Chikitsa ch.9, `SS_Chikitsa_ch09.txt` line 505; Vol. II line 16148).* In the chapter on cutaneous affections (Kushtha), old clarified butter is boiled "with Aragvadha, Sapta-parna, Patola, Vrikshaka, Naktamala, Nimba, the two kinds of Haridra and Mushkaka", and this medicated ghrita is given as a remedy.
3. *Urinary disease (Chikitsa ch.11, `SS_Chikitsa_ch11.txt` line 90; Vol. II line 16806).* In the chapter on Prameha, an alternative to a decoction is "an Aksha (two Tola) measure of the Kalka (powders) of Sala, Kampillaka and Mushkaka (both of them) sweetened with honey and the expressed juice of Amalaka". This is the one place in the passages read where a measure is attached to a preparation that contains Mushkaka; the measure belongs to the powder of the three and is not a dose for Mushkaka alone.
4. *Diseases of the mouth (Chikitsa ch.22, `SS_Chikitsa_ch22.txt` line 390; Vol. II line 20776).* A translator's footnote reports that, for the alkaline water for a mudga soup, Shivadasa says it should be prepared "from the ashes of Mushkaka, Apamarga, etc.", while Dallana says Yava-kshara. This is a commentator's variant.
5. *Poisoned food and drink (Kalpa ch.1, `SS_Kalpa_ch01.txt` line 392; Vol. II line 29276).* An anjana (eye application) is prepared "with the expressed juice of Mesha-shringi, Varuna-bark, Mushkaka or Ajakama", with a cow's bile.

The text read does not print the botanical identity of Mushkaka in the running chapters; that comes only from the glossary.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As the head of the Mushkakadi group: destroying fat, removing defects of semen, and a list of urinary and other complaints (Meha, piles, jaundice, chlorosis, gravel). As an ingredient: a medicated ghrita for skin disease, a powder for Prameha, and an eye application against a poison effect.
- **What the text does not say.** It gives no single-drug dose for Mushkaka, no account of the tree, and no cultivation or rasayana use.

The Codex does not translate "Meha", "defects of semen" or "chlorosis" into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this tree.

### Preparation & Dosing

Preparations given by the text: the medicated ghrita boiled with the Kushtha drugs (Chikitsa ch.9, line 505); the powder of Sala, Kampillaka and Mushkaka in honey and Amalaka juice, "an Aksha (two Tola) measure" of the powders (Chikitsa ch.11, line 90); the anjana prepared with expressed juice (Kalpa ch.1, line 392); the ash for alkaline water (a commentators' variant, Chikitsa ch.22, line 390). The Aksha measure is attached to the three-drug powder and is not a dose for this plant. No dose is given in the sources retrieved for Mushkaka alone. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Schrebera swietenioides* is a tree of the Indian subcontinent in the olive family, and its wood is used as timber. Whether the classic's drug is this tree is the glossary's identification and has not been checked against another source here. The classic's "meha" and "defects of semen" have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** The Mushkakadi passage gives the group the action of "destroying fat and removing the defects of semen" (Vol. I line 15478 onward). It prints no rasa, virya or vipaka for Mushkaka, and none is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Mushkaka heads a therapeutic group in the chapter on the classification of drugs by action, and the group's action is the removal of fat and of "defects of semen" and the cure of urinary disorders. That is a curative frame. It is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no text read places Mushkaka in either discipline, so no cultivation status (*ojas*, *rasayana*, *vajikarana*) is attested for it.

**Practitioner's note.** A drug of the therapeutic groups, used in ghritas and powders for stated complaints. Nothing in the passages read supports a cultivation use, and the identification of the plant itself rests on an OCR'd glossary column. A cultivator should not treat it as a tonic.

### Contraindications

- **No warning is printed** for Mushkaka in the passages read. That is not evidence of safety. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved; avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The species is the glossary's, and the alignment is position-based. [USER: verify]
- **Group action.** The group's stated action of "destroying fat" belongs to the whole group (with Palasha, Dhava, Chitraka, Madana and others, some of which are strong drugs) and must not be assigned to Mushkaka alone.
