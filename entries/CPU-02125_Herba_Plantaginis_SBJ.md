# Che Qian Zi 车前子 (Shennong bencao jing); alias Dang Dao 当道; the Yang 1998 gloss "Herba Plantaginis"

## *Plantago asiatica L., the Chinese plantain, whose seed (zi) the classic names; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name, the description Sun Xingyan quotes from Guo Pu ("large leaf, long spike, fond of growing by the roadside"), and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence at species level, since the classic describes no plant itself and Yang 1998 glosses the heading "Herba Plantaginis", which is the whole herb and not the seed* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) is read as a second witness and attributed to Yang. The row began as an unverified placeholder, "Herba Plantaginis [SBJ]", taken from Yang's English gloss at line 1869. The Chinese heading is 车前子, "plantain seed", and the Latin gloss names the herb. The mismatch is recorded here and not resolved. Other plantain entries already exist in the Codex (see Contraindications, Identity); this row is kept for the Shennong drug under its own name.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 车前子 in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, 卷一 上经, `Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 433 for the heading, line 435 for the 味 paragraph). It reads: 味甘，寒，无毒 ("sweet, cold, nontoxic"); 主气癃 ("governs *qi long*, dribbling urinary block"), 止痛 ("stops pain"), 利水道小便 ("disinhibits the water passageways and urination"), 除湿痹 ("removes damp impediment"); 久服，轻身、耐老 ("taken long, lightens the body and withstands ageing"); 一名当道 ("one name Dang dao", with a note that the *Yulan* adds a further name, 牛舌, and the *Daguan* edition reads 牛遗); 生平泽 ("grows on plains and in marshes").

The Mingyi bielu layer (line 437) gives the aliases 虾蟆衣 and 牛遗 (two further aliases are lost to unreadable characters in this e-text), the habitat 生真定邱陵阪道中 ("grows in the hills, slopes and roadways of Zhending") and the harvest 五月五日采，阴干 ("gathered on the fifth day of the fifth month and dried in the shade"). Sun's note (案, line 439) cites the *Shuowen*, *Guangya*, *Erya*, Guo Pu, Sun Yan and Lu Ji for the old names. Guo Pu is quoted as saying that the plant is the contemporary 车前草, 大叶长穗，好生道边，江东呼为虾蟆衣 ("large leaf, long spike, fond of growing at the roadside; in Jiangdong called *xiamayi*"). The *Shuowen* is quoted for 其实如李，令人宜子 ("its fruit is like a plum and makes people suited to bear children"); the character for the plant is lost in the e-text, so that clause is reported as printed and not relied on. Line 441 adds 喜在牛迹中生，故曰车前当道 ("it likes to grow in ox tracks, hence 'before the cart, on the road'") and that 幽州人谓之牛舌草 ("people of Youzhou call it ox-tongue herb"). These are the only descriptions of the plant in the sources read.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 1869): "Che Qian Zi (Herba Plantaginis) is sweet and cold. It is nontoxic, mainly treating qi dribbling urinary block. It relieves pain, disinhibits the water passageways and [hence] urination, and eliminates damp impediment. Protracted taking may make the body light and slow aging. Its other name is Dang Dao (Obstacle on the Road). It grows in plains and swamps." The page sits in Yang's Superior Class, and the two editions agree on the grade.

**Drug-relations table.** In the table of relations (Sun's e-text line 4277) the entry for 麦门冬 (mai men dong) reads 地黄、车前为使 ("Di Huang and Che Qian are its envoys"). That line is about mai men dong, and gives Che Qian as an envoy to it. It is not an indication for Che Qian.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Dribbling urinary block, pain, damp impediment, and (on long taking) lightness of body and resistance to ageing.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation beyond drying, no duration for "protracted taking".

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms (*qi long*, *shi bi*) into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend the seed or the herb. Anyone with urinary retention or joint pain should seek ordinary medical care.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest: gathered on the fifth day of the fifth month and dried in the shade (line 437). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The genus *Plantago* contains the common plantains and the psyllium species, and the seed coats of several species are rich in mucilage, which swells in water. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The classic's *qi long* and damp impediment have no direct modern equivalent and are not translated into one.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the upper classic, whose preface (Sun's e-text, line 43) reads 上药一百二十种，为君，主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经: the upper drugs are "the lord", "mainly nourishing life to correspond with heaven, nontoxic; much or long taking does not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body and add qi, not age, and extend the years, they are rooted in the upper classic". The drug's own paragraph carries the promise: 久服，轻身、耐老, "taken long, lightens the body and withstands ageing" (line 435). Yang's English agrees (line 1869). The grading follows the rule of 2026-10-30 (Sun's grade plus a life-nourishing clause in the drug's own paragraph). The status is the classic's own, and is not a claim about any effect in a body today. The words 轻身 and 耐老 are not translated into a modern pharmacological effect.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade plant in the Chinese scheme, which the classic says may be taken long without harm and names for a defined complaint (urinary block, damp impediment). The text gives neither a dose nor a duration, so a cultivator has no sourced course to follow. In practice the entry is a record of how the tradition graded the plant, and nothing here replaces medical care for urinary or joint symptoms.

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity.** The classic marks it nontoxic (无毒). That is the classic's statement, not a safety guarantee.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, kidney or urinary disease, drug interactions.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Urinary block and joint pain.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Identity.** Seed (*zi*) and herb (*cao*) are different parts, and Yang's gloss "Herba Plantaginis" does not match the heading. Several *Plantago* species are used under the name, and the Codex already holds entries for the greater plantain (`I_469_Plantain_Plantago_major.md`, which also carries the SBJ tag, and `I_189_Plantain_Plantago_major.md`). This row is likely to overlap them. The species here is our identification, of medium confidence. [USER: verify]
- **Damaged text.** Several characters in Sun's note and in the Mingyi aliases are lost in the e-text.
