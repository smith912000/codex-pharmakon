# Dendrobium — Shi Hu 石斛 (Shennong bencao jing); alias Lin Lan 林兰, "wood orchid"

## *Dendrobium officinale Kimura & Migo, the orchid whose stems are one of the sources of the Chinese drug shi hu; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence at species level, since several Dendrobium species are sold under the name shi hu and the classic gives no description of the plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** The principal text read is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Its other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), also has the drug, as "Shi Hu (Herba Dendrobii)" (line 1698), and is read here as a second witness, attributed to Yang. This row began as an unverified placeholder ("Herba Dendrobii [SBJ]") and is now written from the text.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 石斛 in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 石斛 *shi hu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 521). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味甘，平 ("sweet, balanced"); 主伤中 ("governs damaged centre"), 除痹 ("removes impediment"), 下气 ("brings the qi down"), 补五脏虚劳、羸瘦 ("supplements depletion-taxation and emaciation of the five storehouse organs"), 强阴 ("strengthens yin", a term the text does not explain); 久服，浓肠胃、轻身、延年 ("taken long: thickens the intestines and stomach, lightens the body, extends the years"); alias 一名林兰 ("also called wood orchid"); habitat 生山谷 ("grows in mountain valleys"). Sun notes in a parenthesis that the *Yulan* quotes the alias 禁生 here and that the edition he calls 观本 prints it as "black characters"; I read this as marking it as a later addition. [USER: verify]

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 523) gives the flavour as judged by three authorities and nothing else: "Shennong: sweet, balanced; Bian Que: sour; Li shi: cold" (神农：甘，平；扁鹊：酸；李氏：寒). Only the first agrees with the Shennong paragraph.

The Mingyi bielu layer (《名医》曰, line 525) adds two aliases, 禁生 and 杜兰 (a third is lost to a missing character in the digitised text), the habitat 生六安水傍石上 ("grows at Liu'an on stones beside water"), and the harvest: 七月、八月采茎，阴干 ("the stems are gathered in the seventh and eighth months and dried in the shade").

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 527) is one line: the *Fanzi jiran* says shi hu comes from Liu'an. It is the commentator's, not the Shennong text's.

Yang 1998 (line 1698) renders the same paragraph in English, as "sweet and balanced", "damaged center", "fortifies yin", and "Protracted taking may thicken [i.e., fortify] the stomach and intestines, make the body light, and prolong life", with the alias "Lin Lan (Wood Orchid)". His footnote 96 (line 1713 onward) is a modern commentary: it says dendrobium "is particularly able to supplement the spleen and stomach to eliminate vacuity heat and generate fluids", and then quotes the Qing commentator Zhang Zhicong on the way the classic's upper-class indications are phrased. That is later medical commentary and not the classic's wording; the Codex does not carry it into the Shennong paragraph.

**Where it sits in the classic.** The drug is listed among the upper medicines in the contents (line 45). An appendix of drug relations, later than the main text, says of it: 石斛 陆英为使；恶凝水石、巴豆；畏白僵蚕、雷丸 ("shi hu: lu ying is its envoy; it dislikes ning shui shi and ba dou; it fears bai jiang can and lei wan", line 4291). The preface (line 4117) says that drugs standing in "dislike" or "opposed" relations should not be combined; the Codex draws no further conclusion. The same appendix says of shao yao that it "dislikes shi hu" (line 4361).

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the stems of *Dendrobium* species are rich in mucilaginous polysaccharides, and the genus also yields alkaloids (for instance dendrobine from *D. nobile*) and bibenzyl compounds; the proportions vary by species, and the classic's drug may have been any of several. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a sweet, balanced stem for damaged centre, impediment, qi, depletion and emaciation, and, when taken long, for thickening the gut, lightening the body and extending the years. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose. Another Codex entry, I_171 (Noble dendrobium, *Dendrobium nobile*, tags SBJ and FMT), treats a related species chiefly through later Chinese practice; this entry stays with the classic's own text.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the sources read is the harvest: the stems are gathered in the seventh and eighth months and dried in the shade (Mingyi layer, line 525). The preface (line 4121) says that drugs differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Dendrobium* is a very large genus of orchids; several species are cultivated and traded for Chinese medicine, and orchids as a family are covered by the international wildlife-trade convention (CITES), so wild-collected stems are regulated. The classic's claims (lightening the body, extending the years) have no modern pharmacological equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味甘，平, "sweet, balanced" (line 521). The Wu Pu layer records "sweet, balanced" for Shennong, "sour" for Bian Que and "cold" for Li shi (line 523), a disagreement the Codex does not settle.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The paragraph for 石斛 files the stem in the upper grade (上品). The classic's preface (line 43) says the upper medicines "nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic; taken much or long they do not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body and benefit qi, not age and extend the years, base it on the upper classic" (主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经). The drug's own 久服 clause reads 久服，浓肠胃、轻身、延年, "taken long: thickens the intestines and stomach, lightens the body, extends the years", which includes the lightening of the body and extension of years named in the preface. The register is therefore (a). This records the classic's grade and wording and not a claim that the plant does these things. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 石斛 *shi hu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 521)

**Practitioner's note.** The classic grades this stem as a long-use, life-nourishing drug, and that grade is its whole cultivation status. It is no licence for long use of any product sold as dendrobium: no dose, preparation or course length is given, and the species behind the name is not settled by the text. The honest use of the entry is as a record of how the classic framed the drug.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** many *Dendrobium* species and mixtures are sold as shi hu, and they are not interchangeable; a product's botanical identity should be checked before any use.
- **Appendix cautions:** the appendix says shi hu "dislikes" ning shui shi and ba dou and "fears" bai jiang can and lei wan (line 4291). These are historical pairings and not a modern interaction list.
- **Conservation:** wild orchids are protected in many places; do not collect from the wild.
- **Pregnancy, nursing, medication and doses:** no safety information was read. No dose or duration is given; "taken long" is not a basis for a regimen. [USER: verify]
