# Pandanus odoratissimus — Ketaki; Trina-s'unya (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Pandanus odoratissimus, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Pandanus  Odoratissimus.", line 32036) against the Sanskrit name Ketaki (OCR "Eetakl", line 31960). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting from the end of each column; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print Ketaki, and the translator's bracket "Trina-s'unya (Ketaki)" in Chikitsa ch.18 ties a second name to it; no chapter prints a Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 507 gives the name as "Eetakl", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31960; id line 32036)" and the Latin as "Pandanus  Odoratissimus.", with the note that the pairing was proposed by a Sonnet agent from the two OCR columns and checked by script. The OCR reads the initial K of this page as E throughout ("Ehadira" for Khadira, line 31962; "Eharjura", line 31964; "Eimsuka", line 31966), so "Eetakl" is read as Ketaki. The Latin line carries a stray comma in front of it in the OCR (",  Pandanus  Odoratissimus."). The name appears once in the appendix, and the Latin appears once.

**The pairing, checked by counting.** The page (page number "71" at line 31901) prints its Sanskrit names at lines 31904 to 31986, from Kanchana to Krishna-jiraka, and its Latin names at lines 31992 to 32052. Counted from the end, Ketaki is the thirteenth Sanskrit entry from the last (Ketaki, Khadira, Kharjura, Kimsuka, Kirata with Kirata-tikta bracketed, Kodrava, Kokilaksha, Kovidara, Koranta, Koshataki, Kramuka, Krishna, Krishna-jiraka), and "Pandanus Odoratissimus." is the thirteenth Latin entry from the last (Pandanus, "Accacia Catechu", the "Phoenix sylvestris" line, "Butea frondosa", "Swertia Chirata", "Paspalum Scrobiculatum", "Hygrophila Spinosa", "Bauhinia Variegata", "Barleria Prionitis", "Luffa Amara", "Areca Catechu", "See Pippali", "Nigella Sativa"). The neighbours agree: Khadira with "Accacia Catechu" (line 32038), Kimsuka with "Butea frondosa" (32041), Kirata and Kirata-tikta with "Swertia Chirata" (32043), Kodrava with "Paspalum Scrobiculatum" (32045), Kokilaksha with "Hygrophila Spinosa" (32046), Krishna-jiraka with "Nigella Sativa" (32052), and, one entry above, Kes'a-raja with "Eclipta Erecta" (32034). Counted from the front the columns do not match as printed: the Sanskrit side has 39 entries once the three bracketed pairs (Kanku and Kangu, Karkaru and Karkaruka, Kirata and Kirata-tikta) are taken as one each, and the Latin side has 37. The difference is explained by Katuka, Katuki and Katu-Rohini (lines 31952 to 31956), three names that stand against the one Latin line "Picrorrhiza Kurroa ; Helleborus Niger" (32032). That is the Codex's reading; the OCR keeps no bracket there. The pairing is therefore by position and is firm at the end of the column, where Ketaki lies. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Ketaki and for OCR and spelling variants (initial K read as E, final i read as l or t, Ketaka, Kevada), and for the second name Trina-s'unya in its spellings. The hits are four.

*Sutra ch.6 (the chapter on the seasons, Ritucharya).* `SS_Sutra_ch06.txt` lines 340 to 349, in the description of the forepart of the rainy season (Pravrit): "... Kadamva, Nipa, Kutaja, and the Ketaki trees begin to flower." The tree is named there as a marker of the season and for nothing else.

*Chikitsa ch.18 (glandular swellings, Apachi, tumour and goitre).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt` lines 96 to 99, under the treatment of the Pittaja type of Granthi: "As an alternative, hot plasters compounded of the roots of the Trina-s'unya (Ketaki), or Muchukunda mixed with sugar, should be constantly applied to the affected part." The bracket "(Ketaki)" is the translator's gloss, and it is the text's only link between the two names.

*Sutra ch.46 (food and drink).* `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1282 to 1285, in the run on fruits: "Fruits such the Karira, Akshaka Pilu and Trinashunya have a sweet, bitter and pungent taste, and are heat-making in their potency. They subdue the Vayu and Kapham." The chapter then goes on to Pilu alone. This is the only passage in the chapters that gives a taste or a potency under either name, and it holds only if Trinashunya is the Chikitsa 18 Trina-s'unya, which the translator's bracket says and the Codex does not independently show. [USER: verify]

*Uttara ch.42 (Gulma).* `SS_Uttara_ch42.txt` lines 285 to 297: "Compounds consisting of Sarjika-Kshara, Kushtha and the ashes of Ketaki taken with oil*, or of Sarjika-Kshara, Kushtha and Saindhava taken with tepid water, would subdue an attack of the deranged bodily Vayu." A footnote (lines 289 to 291) says: "According to Dallana, Sarjika-Kshara, Kushtha and Yava-Kshara with oil would form the 1st compound and the Ketaka-Kshara with oil would form a separate compound." The text gives no quantity for the Ketaki ashes; the "one Pala weight" in the lines above belongs to another compound.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Ketaki, Ketaka and Trina-s'unya found nothing.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Pandanus*, Ketaki, Trina-s'unya, Trinashunya and "screw pine" found no entry on this plant. The entries that count the same appendix page (page "71") for other names are **I_1369** (*Cucumis utilissimus*), **I_1375** (*Panicum italicum*), **I_1387** (*Alangium hexapetalum*), **I_1391** (*Barleria prionitis*) and **I_1402** (*Hygrophila spinosa*); none covers Ketaki. **I_1413** (*Phoenix sylvestris*, Kharjura) is the next entry of this batch and sits two entries below Ketaki in the same column. Nothing is merged. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Pandanus* is a genus of the screw-pine family (Pandanaceae), and the fragrant male flower-spathes of *P. odoratissimus* (*P. fascicularis*) are used as a source of scent; that is a statement about the genus and not a finding of the Sushruta text. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the names the chapters print, the plant (if it is this one) is a marker of the rainy season (Sutra 6), a root applied hot as a plaster for one type of glandular swelling (Chikitsa 18), a fruit named among those with a sweet, bitter and pungent taste (Sutra 46, under Trinashunya), and an ash taken in oil in a disorder of the deranged *Vayu* (Uttara 42). The Codex gives no protocol and no dose, and none of these is offered as a recommendation.

### Preparation & Dosing

Two preparations are given, both without a quantity. The first is the hot plaster of the roots of Trina-s'unya (Ketaki) "mixed with sugar" (Chikitsa 18, lines 96 to 99). The second is the ashes of Ketaki, with Sarjika-Kshara and Kushtha, "taken with oil" (Uttara 42, lines 285 to 286), with a separate reading by Dallana of a Ketaka-Kshara (lines 289 to 291). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** under the name Trinashunya only, Sutra 46 prints "a sweet, bitter and pungent taste" and heat-making potency, and says the fruits "subdue the Vayu and Kapham" (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1282 to 1285). It prints no *vipaka* for the group. Under the name Ketaki the chapters print no *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka*. The link between the two names is the translator's bracket in Chikitsa 18 and is not tested by the Codex.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Ketaki in four chapters, as a tree that flowers at the start of the rains, a root plaster, an ash taken in oil for deranged *Vayu*, and (as Trinashunya) a fruit with a stated taste and potency. Those are seasonal and therapeutic contexts. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The plant therefore carries no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status in the text as retrieved.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with this plant: it is a season marker and a plaster and ash for a swelling and for deranged *Vayu*. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of a Latin name that the chapters do not give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this plant under either name. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Glandular swellings and attacks of abdominal pain need medical assessment and not a plaster or an ash. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read.
