# Gu Sui Bu (骨碎补) — "Mender of Shattered Bones" — Drynariae Rhizoma

## *Drynaria fortunei* (Kunze) J. Sm. | Family: Polypodiaceae

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ, FMT

**Energetic Classification:** Warm; bitter; entering Kidney and Liver channels. Tonifying and simultaneously blood-invigorating — the combination that defines the trauma drug.

**Training Phase:** Repair. Structural rebuilding after damage. Not a session substance; not a state substance. A drug of the interval between injury and function.

---

### Origin & Tradition

**骨碎补** decomposes with no ambiguity whatsoever:

- 骨 (*gǔ*) — bone
- 碎 (*suì*) — shattered, broken into fragments, crushed
- 补 (*bǔ*) — to mend, supplement, patch, tonify

**"Mender of shattered bones."** The name *is* the indication. There is no doctrine to reconstruct, no signature to decode, no cosmology to deflate — the tradition simply stated what the drug was for and named it that. In a materia medica full of plants named for their colour, their habitat, their discoverer, or their resemblance to a phallus, Gu Sui Bu is named for its clinical claim. That directness is the entry's first fact and it changes the evidential posture: there is nothing here to deflate, only something to test.

The drug is the dried rhizome of an **epiphytic fern** — a creeping, scaly, orange-brown rhizome that climbs tree trunks and rock faces across southern China, Taiwan, and into Southeast Asia. It grips. It fractures and regrows from fragments. The fern habit matters for the cultivator (below) and, incidentally, gives a plausible secondary signature — a rhizome that knits itself back together across breaks — but the tradition did not need it. The name came from use.

**Chinese medicine (中医 / SBJ).** Gu Sui Bu occupies a precise and slightly unusual position. It is classified among the **kidney-tonifying** drugs (the kidney governing bone, 肾主骨), but it is used in the **trauma and orthopaedics** department (骨伤科) — it is a *bonesetter's* herb, not merely a tonic. The classical indication set:

- **Fracture, bone trauma, contusion, sprain** — internally and applied topically over the injury. This is the flagship use and it is unusual in that it is *acute*: most kidney tonics are for chronic depletion, this one goes in at the time of the break.
- **Kidney-deficiency bone weakness** — chronic lumbar and knee soreness, weak bones in the elderly. The chronic wing of the same claim.
- **Loose teeth and toothache from kidney deficiency** (肾虚牙痛, 牙齿松动) — teeth being the "surplus of the bone" (齿为骨之余) in Chinese physiology. Note this: it is a *doctrinal deduction* — if the drug mends bone, and teeth are bone's overflow, the drug should fix teeth. That deduction turns out to have retrievable support, which is rare.
- **Chronic diarrhoea, tinnitus, deafness from kidney deficiency** — the standard kidney-channel cluster. [USER: verify] before printing; these are secondary and I have not confirmed them against a specific text.
- **Topically for alopecia areata** (tincture applied to the scalp) — [USER: verify].

**Tibetan and Himalayan use (FMT).** *Drynaria* species are used across the Himalayan and Tibetan-influenced ranges for bone injury and joint complaints. ⚠️ **I cannot substantiate the Tibetan attestation from anything retrieved this run**, and the species used across that range is frequently *Drynaria propinqua* or another congener rather than *D. fortunei*. **[USER: verify] the Tibetan indication, its native drug name, and — critically — the species.** Do not print an FMT subsection claim without a source. The tag stands provisionally on the tradition's known use of *Drynaria* rhizome; the specifics do not.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.**

There is no signature to unpick, so the doctrinal work here is different: what does "kidney governs bone" *mean*, and does the chemistry cooperate?

The Chinese claim is not that the drug is a bone-food. It is that bone is downstream of a **systemic regulatory capacity** (the kidney), and that failing bone reflects a failing regulator rather than a local deficit. Fix the regulator, the bone follows. Teeth follow too, because they are the same tissue-category further downstream.

Map that onto real chemistry and something interesting happens. Drynaria's flavonoid fraction acts through the **oestrogen receptor**: its stimulatory effects on osteoblastic function were **abolished in the presence of the ER antagonist ICI 182780** (Wong et al. 2013, *Br J Nutr*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114512005405); via PubMed). An ER-mediated, systemically-distributed hormonal signal that regulates osteoblast/osteoclast balance across the whole skeleton — including the jaw and periodontium — is *structurally* the same kind of claim as "a systemic regulator governs bone, and teeth are its overflow." The tradition described a systemic endocrine-like control of bone without any endocrinology. That is a genuine convergence and the strongest doctrinal moment in this entry.

**But deflate the specific mechanism honestly.** "Kidney governs bone" is not vindicated as anatomy — the renal organ is not doing this, and Drynaria is not a kidney drug in any biomedical sense. What is vindicated is the *shape* of the claim (systemic regulator → bone), not its content. And the ER-mediated mechanism carries a consequence the tradition never anticipated and would not have liked: **an ER-active drug is a phytoestrogen**, with everything that implies for contraindication. See below.

Deflate one more thing. The traditional acute-fracture use — Gu Sui Bu given *at the time of the break* — is the least supported claim in the entry despite being the one in the name. Almost all retrieved evidence is for **osteoporosis and bone density**, not for accelerating union of an acute fracture. That gap is stated plainly in Scientific Correlation.

### Active Components

- **Naringin** — the marquee compound and the best-characterised. A flavanone, present in the rhizome, identified in GSB extract by HPLC and shown to carry anti-osteoporotic activity in its own right (Peng et al. 2022, *J Ethnopharmacol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2022.115565); via PubMed). Administered alone to ovariectomised rats it caused significant amelioration of bone strength, bone mineral content, and trabecular bone amount (Lee et al. 2014, *J Ethnopharmacol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2014.10.016); via PubMed). Reported mechanisms include increased osteoblast proliferation via **BMP-2** upregulation and inhibited osteoclast activity via reduced **RANKL**, with anti-inflammatory action via NF-κB suppression (Yin et al. 2015, *Zhongguo Gu Shang*, PMID 25924506; via PubMed — English abstract of a Chinese-language review; no DOI issued).
- **Total flavonoid fraction (DFTF)** — the active fraction, not a single molecule. Alongside naringin, two further isolated compounds were characterised and shown active: **(2S)-5,7,3′,5′-tetrahydroxy-flavonone 7-O-neohesperidoside** and **5,7-dihydroxychromone 7-O-neohesperidoside** (Wong et al. 2013, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114512005405)).
- **Other phenolics** — ten phenolic compounds isolated from Drynariae Rhizoma include **neoeriocitrin, hesperidin, caffeic acid 4-O-β-D-glucopyranoside, kaempferol 3-O-rhamnoside 7-O-glucoside**, and kaempferol glycosides (Ahn et al. 2024, *Pharmaceuticals*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph17081061); via PubMed).
- **Polysaccharide (DFPW)** — a distinct, purified homogenous polysaccharide with independent anti-osteoporotic activity in OVX rats (Sun et al. 2020, *J Cell Mol Med*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcmm.15072); via PubMed). Note: this means the drug has **at least two mechanistically independent active classes**, which is a real finding and an argument against reducing Gu Sui Bu to "naringin."

⚠️ Naringin is famously the bitter principle of **grapefruit**. Do not import grapefruit's CYP3A4/OATP interaction literature into this entry as a *Drynaria* claim — grapefruit's interaction is attributed principally to furanocoumarins (bergamottin, DHB), not naringin, and no *Drynaria*–drug interaction study was retrieved this run. Flagged as an open question, not a known interaction.

**Cultivator's note.** *D. fortunei* is an **epiphytic fern** — it grows on trees and rock, not in soil, and requires an epiphytic medium (bark, coarse orchid mix), high humidity, warmth, and shade. It propagates readily by **rhizome division**, which is the practical route: a cut section of rhizome with a growing tip will re-establish. This is genuinely cultivable, unlike the two preceding entries in this sequence, and there is no conservation contraindication I can substantiate for *D. fortunei* — [USER: verify] current CITES/national status before asserting either way.

**⚠️ Processing — the scales must be removed.** The fresh rhizome is densely covered in brown scales (鳞片). Traditional processing (*燙去毛* / scorching or singeing off the hair-scales, often in hot sand) removes them before the rhizome is sliced and dried. This is not cosmetic: the scales are the reason the crude drug is described as irritant, and unprocessed material is not the pharmacopoeial article. **Do not use unscaled rhizome.** [USER: verify] the specific processing standard (砂燙 sand-scorching vs. singeing) against your pharmacopoeia.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Gu Sui Bu is a **bone-substrate drug** with an unusually favourable mechanistic profile: it appears to push osteoblasts up *without* shutting osteoclasts down.

That last point is worth dwelling on. In the zebrafish glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis model, GSB enhanced osteoblast activity (increasing collagen I, osteopontin, osteonectin) and repressed resorption (decreasing *mmp9*, *mmp13a*) — but the authors specifically observed that **bone resorption activity was retained after GSB treatment, which would be beneficial for maintaining normal bone remodelling** (Peng et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2022.115565)). This is the opposite of the bisphosphonate problem, where resorption is suppressed hard and the resulting bone is dense but poorly remodelled. A drug that raises formation while permitting remodelling is, if it holds up, the pharmacologically preferable shape.

Use cases in the repair phase:

- **Post-fracture and post-trauma structural rebuilding** — the classical use, with the caveat that the *acute union* claim is unevidenced and the *bone density* claim is well-evidenced. Use it for the long tail of recovery, not as an expectation that the break knits faster.
- **Bone loss in depletion or from glucocorticoids** — the GIOP model is directly relevant to any practitioner on long-term steroids.
- **Dental and periodontal repair** — see below; the tooth claim is the surprise of the entry.
- **Not for acute inflammatory heat**, not for yin-deficiency-heat patterns, not in pregnancy.

### Scientific Correlation

**Overall grade: PRECLINICAL-HEAVY, ONE SMALL RCT, AND THE HEADLINE INDICATION IS THE LEAST TESTED.** This is one of the better doctrine↔evidence cases in the book — the direction of effect is consistent across cells, zebrafish, mice, rats, dogs, and one human trial — but "consistent preclinical signal" is not "proven drug," and the specific thing the plant is *named for* is the thing nobody has tested properly.

**Human clinical — GRADE: WEAK-TO-MODERATE. ONE SMALL RCT, AND NOT FOR THE PRIMARY ENDPOINT.** Eighty elderly women with concurrent postmenopausal osteoporosis and hyperlipidaemia were randomised to Drynaria vs control and followed 12 months. Drynaria "effectively improved patients' BMD and lipid profiles," with attenuation of IL-1β, IL-18, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-8 and of NLRP3/ASC/caspase-1/NF-κB, via Notch1–NLRP3 inflammasome suppression (Lu et al. 2021, *Gynecol Endocrinol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/09513590.2021.2015760); via PubMed). ⚠️ Read the limits carefully: **n=40 per arm; single centre; the stated primary aim was lipid profile, not bone; BMD improvement is reported in the abstract without effect size; no blinding described in what I retrieved.** This is the only human evidence I found. It is a real randomised controlled trial and it points the right way. It is not sufficient to call Drynaria an established treatment for anything.

**Anti-osteoporotic in ovariectomised rodents — GRADE: STRONG PRECLINICAL, REPLICATED ACROSS LABS AND FRACTIONS.**
- Water extract, OVX rats, 1–6 months: alleviated OVX-induced reductions in bone torsion force and bone ash; corrected histomorphometric changes in prehypertrophic/hypertrophic chondrocytes; mechanism via increased **osteocalcin**-involved endochondral ossification; **no systemic or tissue toxicity** at 0.5–1 g/kg/day (Lee et al. 2014, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2014.10.016)).
- Flavonoid fraction, OVX mice, 6 weeks: dose-dependent improvement in BMD *and bone strength* at femur, tibia and L1; stimulated osteoblast proliferation and ALP; raised OPG/RANKL mRNA ratio; **effects abolished by ER antagonist ICI 182780** (Wong et al. 2013, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114512005405)).
- Polysaccharide, OVX rats, 12 weeks: improved trabecular area, thickness and number; reversed declines in BMD and mineral content (Ca, P, Mg); reduced serum ALP and urinary DPD; improved maximum load, energy, Young's modulus, maximum stress (Sun et al. 2020, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcmm.15072)).

Three independent groups, three different preparations, two species, and — importantly — **biomechanical endpoints, not just densitometry**. Bone that is denser *and stronger* is a meaningfully better result than bone that merely images denser.

**Glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis — GRADE: MODERATE PRECLINICAL, MECHANISTICALLY DETAILED.** GSB significantly reversed dexamethasone-induced calcification delay in zebrafish larvae; raised collagen I, osteopontin, osteonectin; lowered *mmp9* and *mmp13a*; naringin identified by HPLC as one of the constituents responsible; resorption activity retained (Peng et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2022.115565)).

**Bone formation in healthy animals — GRADE: WEAK.** Gusuibu extract in drinking water for 5 weeks increased bone volume/tissue volume ratio by **6.45%** and trabecular number by **10.00%** in normal male BALB/c mice by micro-CT (Wong & Rabie 2006, *Phytother Res*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.1842); via PubMed). Small effect, small study, healthy animals — but note it is the only retrieved evidence that the drug does anything in a *non-diseased* skeleton.

**Dental / periodontal — GRADE: MODERATE PRECLINICAL, AND THE MOST SURPRISING RESULT HERE.** In sixteen surgically-created Class II furcation defects in four mongrel dogs, guided tissue regeneration plus *Drynaria fortunei* granules with β-TCP alloplast produced significant increases (P<0.001) in osteoblastic count, cementum layer thickness, percentage of collagen in bone matrix, and ALP immunoreactivity vs GTR+alloplast alone, at both 4 and 8 weeks (Afifi et al. 2018, *J Periodontol*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/JPER.17-0655); via PubMed). ⚠️ n=4 dogs. But consider what this is: the classical claim "**teeth are the surplus of the bone, therefore the bone-mender fixes loose teeth**" was a pure doctrinal deduction from a physiological metaphor — and a controlled surgical model in a second mammal found periodontal regeneration. That is the tradition's *inference method* producing a hit. Worth flagging precisely because the inference had no right to work.

**Bone cell activity in vitro — GRADE: MODERATE, AND USEFULLY MESSY.** Crude Gu-Sui-Bu at 1 mg/ml on rat bone cells increased intracellular ALP and raised medium ACP and PGE2 — but **downregulated osteopontin and osteonectin mRNA** and made multinucleated **osteoclast formation more active** than control, leading the authors to conclude the major effect is probably mediated via osteoclast activity (Sun et al. 2002, *Biomaterials*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0142-9612(02)00038-8); via PubMed). ⚠️ **This directly contradicts the zebrafish data** (which found osteopontin and osteonectin *up*). Report the contradiction; do not smooth it. Crude extract at high concentration on isolated cells is not whole-animal dosing, and the discrepancy is a reason for humility about mechanism.

**Neuroprotection / anti-Alzheimer's — GRADE: WEAK, IN VITRO ONLY.** Ten phenolics from Drynariae Rhizoma inhibited AChE, BChE, BACE1 and MAO-B; two compounds reduced sAPPβ and β-secretase expression by >45% at 1.0 µM and decreased Aβ aggregation by ~40% and ~80% in a thioflavin T assay (Ahn et al. 2024, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph17081061)). Enzyme-inhibition screening. No animal, no human, no traditional indication it corresponds to. Interesting; not a use case; do not repeat as a claim.

**NULLS AND ABSENCES — the honest accounting:**

- **⚠️ NO STUDY OF ACUTE FRACTURE HEALING IN HUMANS WAS RETRIEVED.** The drug is called *mender of shattered bones* and I found no human fracture trial. Every osteoporosis result in this entry is a result about *bone density in a chronic model*, which is a different claim from *this break knits faster*. **State this plainly wherever the entry is summarised.** The name is the indication, and the indication is untested.
- **Almost every animal model is oestrogen-deficient female** (OVX rats, OVX mice). The classical indication is sex-neutral and largely traumatic. The evidence base is skewed to a population and pathology the tradition was not describing.
- **No retrieved evidence for the tinnitus, deafness, chronic diarrhoea, or alopecia indications.** Not weak — none.
- **No retrieved pharmacokinetic or drug-interaction study.**
- **Contradictory in vitro mechanism** (osteopontin/osteonectin, osteoclast direction) between Sun et al. 2002 and Peng et al. 2022. Unresolved.
- **The Tibetan/FMT attestation is unsubstantiated in this run.** [USER: verify] or cut the tag.

Net: a fern whose name is a clinical claim, with a consistent multi-model preclinical signal for bone density and strength, one small positive RCT, a plausible ER-mediated mechanism, an unusually good remodelling profile, an unexpected win in the teeth — and no evidence at all for the fracture.

### Contraindications

- **⚠️ PREGNANCY — avoid.** Gu Sui Bu is a **blood-invigorating** (活血) drug, a class traditionally contraindicated in pregnancy, *and* it is demonstrably ER-active. No pregnancy safety data was retrieved. Avoid in pregnancy and lactation.
- **⚠️ YIN DEFICIENCY WITH HEAT (阴虚火旺) — the classical caution, and it stands.** A warm, drying, bitter tonic in a pattern of depleted fluids and rising heat aggravates. Also avoid in blood-deficiency heat and in any active febrile or inflammatory presentation. This is the one contraindication the tradition states clearly and it should not be softened.
- **⚠️ ER-ACTIVE — treat as a phytoestrogen.** The flavonoid fraction's osteoblastic effects were abolished by the ER antagonist ICI 182780 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114512005405)). This is a mechanism the tradition had no way of knowing about and it creates contraindications the classical literature does not list: **hormone-sensitive cancers (breast, endometrial), a history of same, and concurrent tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors, or hormone replacement therapy.** Anyone in those categories should not take Drynaria on a herbalist's say-so.
- **⚠️ REMOVE THE SCALES.** Unprocessed rhizome retains its dense brown scales and is not the pharmacopoeial drug. Traditional processing scorches them off in hot sand before slicing. Unscaled material is described as irritant. Do not use it, and do not accept crude material from a supplier who has not processed it.
- **Bleeding risk — mechanistic caution, not a documented event.** A 活血 blood-invigorating classification implies theoretical caution with anticoagulants and antiplatelets and around surgery. No interaction study was retrieved. Flagged as a hypothesis to check, not a known interaction.
- **Naringin / grapefruit — an open question, not a known interaction.** Naringin is grapefruit's bitter principle, but grapefruit's CYP3A4 interaction is attributed chiefly to furanocoumarins rather than naringin, and **no *Drynaria* interaction study was retrieved this run.** Do not assert a grapefruit-type interaction. Do not assert its absence either.
- **Toxicity:** reassuringly, the water extract at 0.5–1 g/kg/day for up to 6 months induced **no systemic or tissue toxicity** in rats ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2014.10.016)). That is a real and unusually well-documented safety signal for a herbal drug — but it is a rat signal, not a human safety profile.
- **Species and nomenclature risk.** *Drynaria fortunei* / *D. roosii* / *Drynariae Rhizoma* are used interchangeably in the literature, and other *Drynaria* species are used across the Himalayan range. ⚠️ Congener data is **not** species-specific data. Demand the binomial.
