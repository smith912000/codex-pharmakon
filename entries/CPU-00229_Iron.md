# Iron — Tie, Tie Luo, Tie Jing (Yang 1998) / Lauha, Krishna-Lauha (Bhishagratna) / Iron-from-Apollonopolis-parva (Bryan 1930) / Ios Siderou, iron rust (Dioscorides)
## *Iron, the metal (Fe), with its filings, rust and slag; the printed forms are Bryan's "Iron-from-Apollonopolis-parva", Yang's "Ferrum", "Frusta Feni" and "Frusta Ferri", and Dioscorides' "Iron Rust" and "Iron Slag"* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP, AT, DMM, CM, SBJ, SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** Bryan 1930 lists "Iron-from-Apollonopolis-parva" in his Chapter V mineral remedies (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1177; `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, "Bryan's English identification") and notes (line 1137) that the source of the drugs is "strictly laid down": "Thus, the Iron must be Iron-from-Apollonopolis-parva". It occurs in two recipes read. In a remedy "to drive out pterygium" it stands with red-lead, Arabian wood powder, calamine, ostrich egg, saltpetre, sulphur and honey, "made into one and applied to the Eyes" (lines 2847-2851). In a charm against alopecia (line 4069): "O Shining One, Thou who hoverest above! O Xare! O Disc of the Sun! O Protector of the Divine Neb-apt! To be spoken over Iron, Red-lead, Onions, Alabaster, Honey. Make into one and give against." Bryan then remarks, in his own voice, that "we still retain the Iron Tonic of the Ancients" (line 4073); that is Bryan's modern comparison, not a statement in the papyrus.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** There is no chapter on iron as a metal in Book 5 (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`); its derivatives are treated. 5-93 IOS SIDEROU, "Iron Rust" (line 10289; the translator adds "CAN CAUSE BLOOD INFECTIONS ON OPEN CUTS"): "Iron rust is astringent, and applied it stops women's excessive discharges [menstrual flow]. A decoction (taken as a drink) causes inconception [birth control], and rubbed on with vinegar it heals rashes such as measles. It is suitable to use for whitlows, pterygium [membrane on eye], scabbed eyelids, and venereal warts, and it strengthens the gums. Rubbed on it helps gout, and thickens loss of hair [alopecia]. Iron made burning hot then quenched in water or wine, and the liquid (taken as a drink) is good for the abdominal cavity, dysentery, spleen, cholera, and diarrhoea." 5-94 SKORIA SIDEROU, "Iron Slag" (line 10293): suitable for as many things as the rust "but to a lower degree"; taken in a drink with vinegar and honey it helps with aconite poisoning. 5-90 LEPIS STOMOMATOS (line 10267) is marked UNKNOWN by the translator ("stoma is a little mouth"); its scales have "the same strength as scales of brass", and the spine reads it as "steel scale". **Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` lines 1211-1214, p.31), under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class": "Tie Luo (Frusta Feni) is acrid and balanced. It mainly treats wind heat, malign wounds, sores, flat abscesses, scabs, and [evil] qi within the skin. Tie (Ferrum) mainly fortifies the sinews and cultivates endurance to pain. Tie Jing (Frusta Ferri) mainly brightens the eyes and is able to transform copper. [Ferrum] is produced in plains and swamps." Yang's note 62 quotes Chen Cang-qi (8th century) on iron dust, a later source; note 63 says that "in some versions of this book, these three substances are treated in three separate passages", in others in one. These are the folded forms *Tie Luo* (iron flakes) and *Tie Jing* (iron essence) of the spine.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Lauha* and *Krishna-Lauha* (black iron). In the Metal group of *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 2133-2135): "Iron generates Vayu, is cooling in its potency, allays thirst and subdues the deranged Pittam and Kapham." *Krishna-Lauha* is one of the seven *Trapvadi* drugs of ch.38 (`SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 439-448), after *Rajata* (silver) and before *Suvarna* (gold) and *Lohamala*; the spine reads *Lohamala* as the rust, which the translator does not gloss in the passage read. [USER: verify] In *Chikitsasthana* ch.10, the chapter on major cutaneous disease (*Maha-Kushtha*), an *Ayaskriti* (iron compound) is prepared by plastering thin leaves of steel with the five salts, heating them red-hot, immersing them in a *Triphala* decoction sixteen times and in a fire of *Khadira* wood, then pounding them fine (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch10.txt` lines 184-208). It is taken with honey and ghee, with a meal "devoid of salt and acid articles". "The use of a Tula measure of this medicinal iron preparation ... leads to the recovery of Kushtha, Meha (urinary complaints), obesity, oedima, jaundice, insanity and epilepsy and makes the patient live for one hundred years. The use of each additional Tula weight of the preparation adds a century." The next recipes are the *Aushadha* and *Mahaushadha Ayaskriti* (line 210 on). In ch.12, on the sequelae of *Prameha*, thin steel foils are immersed in a fermenting decoction buried in barley for three or four months to make *Loharishta* (lines 228-237, "a highly efficacious remedy"; the editor's footnote gives Vagbhata's recipe). *Silajatu* is said in ch.13 (line 25-35) to carry the essence of six metals including "black-iron", the iron-derived kind being "progressively more efficacious". Ch.1 (lines 448-451) places metals with the earthy substances. In *Uttarasthana* ch.12 (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch12.txt` lines 144-146) powdered "dead" iron is among the metals of a scarifying collyrium.

### Active Components

Iron is the element Fe. As filings, rust (iron oxides and hydroxides) or slag it is a poor and variable source of absorbable iron. Iron is an essential nutrient (a part of haemoglobin and of many enzymes), and the body has no way to excrete extra, so surplus accumulates.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources use iron and rust as a drying, astringent or tonic-sounding drug for skin, eyes, bleeding and gut complaints, and the Sanskrit text ties an elaborately prepared iron compound to a course of centuries-long longevity inside a skin-disease chapter. Medicinal iron as an anaemia treatment belongs to modern medicine and is out of scope for these sources; iron supplements should be taken only for a diagnosed deficiency. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation drawn from the old texts.

### Preparation & Dosing

SS ch.10 (above) gives the *Ayaskriti* method with a measure of "a Tula" for a whole course and no daily dose; the unit is not defined in the lines read. DMM 5-93 says iron is made burning hot and quenched in water or wine and the liquid drunk. SBJ and EP give no preparation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Iron deficiency anaemia is common and treatable with iron salts; iron overload and acute iron poisoning (especially in children) are dangerous. Dirty, rust-contaminated wounds carry a tetanus risk (the spores live in soil); the Greek translator's note about "blood infections on open cuts" points the same way. No clinical studies were retrieved for the traditional uses. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* "Iron-from-Apollonopolis-parva" is named in Bryan's list (line 1177) and in the two recipes read. Because the Ebers material is organised by complaint, the identity of each named substance is a modern hypothesis, and the form is Bryan's rendering. The alopecia charm shows *heka* at work: the invocation is to be "spoken over" the iron, red-lead, onions, alabaster and honey, so spell and drug are one intervention and dropping the recitation loses half the prescription. The invocation names "the Disc of the Sun" and "the Divine Neb-apt" as Bryan prints them; no further attribution is made. Nothing is said of the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*.

**Assyrian (AT).** *(c) Absent, no locus.* Nothing was read. The blocking warning is in force. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What 5-93 and 5-94 offer is a discipline of attention to rust and slag: how the rust is used, how the metal is quenched, and the translator's warning about open cuts. No cultivation status is assigned.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent, and the attestation rests on a list row.* The only locus is `_sources/avicenna.csv` line 54, with no chapter reference. Innate heat and radical moisture cannot be tied to iron without a source and are not tied to it here. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: middle grade.* Tie Luo, Tie and Tie Jing stand under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (Yang p.31). The preface (lines 348-400) says medium-class medicinals "mainly nurture personality", "may or may not be toxic", to be weighed "before putting them to their appropriate use", for those who "intend to control disease, supplement vacuity, and replenish exhaustion". The entries are about wounds, sores, sinews, endurance and the eyes; none carries a longevity or body-lightening line. No *jing* action is asserted.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, with a longevity claim inside a disease chapter.* *Lauha* is named in the ch.46 Metal group and the ch.38 *Trapvadi* group. The *Ayaskriti* passage in the *Kushtha* chapter (ch.10) says the preparation "makes the patient live for one hundred years" and that each further *Tula* "adds a century to the duration of the user's life"; the preparation of "all kinds of Loha (zinc, copper, lead and gold)" is said to follow the same mode (line 207-208). Those lines are not in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and a text search of the *rasayana* chapters (*Chikitsasthana* ch.27-30) found no iron in them; it is not graded (a). Bhishagratna's *Loharishta* footnote shows the commentators disagreeing over which iron to use. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is printed for iron. The meal after the *Ayaskriti* is to be without salt and acid, a regimen specific to that recipe.

**Practitioner's note.** Two sources give iron a place beyond treatment: Sushruta's longevity line for the prepared *Ayaskriti* (inside a skin-disease chapter, with a strict meal rule) and the Chinese classic's middle-grade listing, which has no longevity line. Nothing sourced supports taking iron for cultivation. Iron in the body is a nutrient with a ceiling, and a cultivator who suspects deficiency should be tested, not guess. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Iron supplements and iron-containing preparations are a leading cause of poisoning in young children. Taking iron without a documented deficiency risks overload, especially in people with haemochromatosis. Traditional "bhasma" or burnt-iron preparations are not standardised and may contain other metals. Rust on open wounds carries infection risk. The Greek note that an iron-rust decoction "causes inconception [birth control]" is recorded as a report, not as a method.
