# ICHTHUOKOLLA — fish glue (Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 3-102)

## *Glue made from the intestines of a "whale fish", as Dioscorides describes it; the translator's gloss is "Fish Glue". The identification as fish glue is the text's own and is of high confidence; the species is not settled (Dioscorides says only "a whale fish"), and the modern term isinglass, for glue from fish swim bladders, is a later and general usage. The row's `check` guess is confirmed; the spine's class "plant" in the Dioscorides name list is wrong for this item, which is animal* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is chapter 3-102 in the 2000 English edition (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 7465), in Book 3, between 3-101 KOLLA (glue) and 3-103 IXOS (mistletoe birdlime). The index lists it at page 487 (line 12167), and the English index gives "Fish Glue 487" (line 15717). The row began as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The whole chapter reads: "That called fish glue is from the intestines of a whale fish. The best is made in Pontus — white, somewhat rough, not scabby, very quickly melted. It is good to include in head plasters, medicines for leprosy, and medicines for making facial skin smooth." The translator's heading is "SUGGESTED: Fish Glue". Three things are therefore stated: what the material is (from the intestines of a "whale fish"), the mark of the best sort (made in Pontus, white, somewhat rough, not scabby, quick to melt), and the uses (as an ingredient in head plasters, in medicines for leprosy, and in medicines for making the skin of the face smooth). Dioscorides gives no method of making the glue, and no dose.

A search of the other texts on disk found no further treatment of fish glue under this name in Dioscorides. The nearby chapter 3-101 KOLLA is a different glue and is not folded in here.

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, such glues are made by boiling collagen-rich animal tissue, and collagen is a structural protein of connective tissue, skin and swim bladders; the glue is largely gelatin-like. These are general facts and not claims from Dioscorides. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** An ingredient in head plasters, in medicines for leprosy, and in medicines for making facial skin smooth.
- **What the text does not say.** The condition for which the head plasters are used, the form of the leprosy medicines, the proportion, the dose, or any internal use.

The Codex gives no protocol. The material is animal-derived (fish or marine-mammal tissue; the "whale fish" is not identified), which raises ethical and conservation questions, and the "leprosy" of Dioscorides is a historical skin category and is not translated into a modern diagnosis.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the text is that the glue is "included" in head plasters, leprosy medicines and skin-smoothing medicines. The method of making the glue itself is not given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Gelatin and fish glues (isinglass) are collagen-derived and are used today in food clarification and adhesives. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] Dioscorides' claims for skin and leprosy are not translated into modern equivalents.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM (2000 English ed., 3-102).** No quality such as warming, cooling or astringent is printed for this drug. The only qualities printed are those of the best sort for sale: white, somewhat rough, not scabby, very quickly melted. None is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is attested for this material. What the chapter does offer a practitioner is a discipline of attention: the text names the place of best production (Pontus) and gives sensory marks for judging the glue (white, somewhat rough, not scabby, quick to melt). That is provenance and assay, and it carries no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here supports a cultivation use. The chapter is a short record of a plaster and skin ingredient, with a place of origin and tests of quality, and a cultivator would do nothing with it.

### Contraindications

- **Animal and ethical.** Derived from the tissue of a fish or marine animal; unsuitable for anyone who avoids animal products. The "whale fish" is unidentified, and some marine mammals are legally protected. [USER: verify]
- **Allergy.** Fish-derived proteins can provoke allergy. No statement in the sources retrieved; this is a general caution.
- **Leprosy and skin disease.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Identity.** The source animal is not settled by the text.
