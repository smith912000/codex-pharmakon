# Sarshapa — Sarṣapa / Rājikā (black-brown seed) / Kaṭu-sneha source (Sanskrit); Rai, Sarson (Hindi); Brown/Indian Mustard (English); Kadugu (Tamil)
## *Brassica juncea* (L.) Czern. | Family: Brassicaceae (Cruciferae)
**Tradition Tags:** EP, AT, DMM, CM, SBJ, SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda (Suśruta, Caraka):** Sarṣapa is the archetypal **kaṭu (pungent) rubefacient** of the classical texts. Suśruta treats mustard both as a seed drug and as an oil source (sarṣapa taila), and its most characteristic use is **external**: ground into a paste (kalka) or poultice, it draws heat and circulation to the surface — a counter-irritant for cold, stiff, painful joints, for Kapha-type swellings, and for skin conditions where stagnation must be broken. Suśruta's surgical orientation makes mustard useful as a warming, cleansing application (lepa) and as a medium for medicated oils in massage (abhyaṅga) directed at Vāta-Kapha stiffness. Internally, in small dose, it is a sharp digestive and Kapha-cutter; in larger dose it is emetic. The distinction between white/yellow (Gaura sarṣapa) and black-brown (Rājikā) seed is drawn in the classical nighaṇṭus, with Rājikā the more pungent.

**Cross-cultural culinary-medical continuum:** Mustard is one of humanity's oldest condiment-medicines. In folk practice across India, Europe and East Asia the seed and its flour serve as a warming plaster for chest congestion and rheumatic pain, and the pungent isothiocyanates released on crushing are the active principle. The leaf (mustard greens) is a nutrient-dense vegetable with its own bioactive profile.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Drawing Heat:** Sarṣapa's doctrinal signature in Kenetica is *surface mobilisation* — heat that is called outward. Where Cavya ignites the inner hearth, mustard **draws to the periphery**: it reddens the skin, opens the pores, pulls stuck circulation to where it can move. Symbolically it is the *counter-irritant principle* — introducing a controlled, superficial heat to break a deeper, colder stagnation, the therapeutic logic of "a small fire to move a large cold." In the training vocabulary it belongs to the bodywork and recovery arc: the rubefacient massage, the warming poultice on a seized muscle, the deliberate stimulation of a dormant region. Its sharpness (tīkṣṇa) is also its warning — mustard's heat, mishandled, blisters. The doctrine is *dose and boundary*: the same drawing power that heals stiffness will scald skin if left too long. It teaches the difference between stimulation and injury.

### Active Components

The defining chemistry is the **glucosinolate–myrosinase system**. Intact seed stores glucosinolates (chiefly sinigrin); on crushing/wetting, myrosinase hydrolyses these to pungent **isothiocyanates** (notably allyl isothiocyanate), the rubefacient and antimicrobial principle. According to PubMed, non-targeted metabolomics of *B. juncea* leaves annotated 171 metabolites and identified glucosinolates, isothiocyanates and phenolics as the compounds positively correlated with antibacterial/antivirulence activity (Ibrahim et al., 2023, *Food Res Int*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2023.112742)). A separate study identified the antimicrobial 2S-albumin storage protein **napin** in *B. juncea* seed extract as a bioactive antibacterial peptide (Chandrashekar et al., 2021, *Molecules*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules26072080)).

- **Sinigrin** (glucosinolate) → **allyl isothiocyanate** — pungency, rubefacient, antimicrobial
- **Myrosinase** — the activating enzyme; pungency only develops on tissue disruption
- **Napin** (2S albumin peptide) — antibacterial
- **Phenolics & flavonoids** — antioxidant, antivirulence (leaf)
- **Fatty-acid-rich fixed oil** (sarṣapa taila) — massage/vehicle base

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Rubefacient poultice / plaster** — the primary use: ground seed applied externally to cold, stiff, painful joints and muscles to draw circulation and break Kapha stagnation. Recovery-bodywork phase.
2. **Warming massage medium** — mustard oil as the base for abhyaṅga in Vāta-Kapha stiffness and cold extremities.
3. **Sharp internal Kapha-cutter (small dose)** — appetiser and decongestant; larger doses are emetic and are not a casual internal remedy.
4. **Topical antimicrobial adjunct** — the isothiocyanate/napin antibacterial activity gives a plausible mechanistic basis for traditional use on stagnant skin conditions (graded preclinical).

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, *B. juncea* shows **documented preclinical antimicrobial and antioxidant activity**. Leaf extracts demonstrated significant antibacterial activity against *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Enterococcus faecalis*, with the red-leaf var. *integrifolia* giving the strongest antihemolytic effect (~99% inhibition against *S. aureus*) and reducing alpha-hemolysin gene transcription ~4-fold; phenolics, glucosinolates and isothiocyanates were the correlated bioactives (Ibrahim et al., 2023, *Food Res Int*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2023.112742)). The seed antimicrobial peptide napin showed a determined MIC of 0.5 mg/mL and MBC of 1.5 mg/mL against test strains, with molecular docking against bacterial LpxC and topoisomerase targets (Chandrashekar et al., 2021, *Molecules*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules26072080)).

**Evidence grade:** In-vitro and animal-model level for antimicrobial/antioxidant/antivirulence actions; the rubefacient counter-irritant effect of allyl isothiocyanate is well-established pharmacologically. No formal clinical-trial validation of the Ayurvedic poultice indications is cited here; those remain traditional-plus-mechanistic.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṭu (pungent); Rājikā seed the most pungent
- **Guṇa (quality):** Tīkṣṇa (sharp, penetrating), Snigdha (unctuous, as oil), Sūkṣma (subtle/permeating)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating — markedly so)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva (special effect):** Rubefacient/counter-irritant drawing action — mobilises surface circulation; oil is a superior penetrating massage vehicle
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha-Vāta hara; strongly Pitta-vardhaka (aggravates Pitta and heat/blood)
- **Karma (actions):** Kaphahara (decongestant), Vātahara (external, warming), Dīpana (in small internal dose), Chardana (emetic in large dose), Vedanāsthāpana (topical pain-relief via counter-irritation), Krimighna (antimicrobial), Lekhana (scraping/cleansing on skin)
- **Gaṇa membership (Suśruta):** Pungent/rubefacient drugs; taila-varga (oil-yielding group)

### Contraindications

- **Skin:** never leave a mustard poultice on until blistering — allyl isothiocyanate causes chemical burns; contraindicated on broken skin and in sensitive/paediatric skin without dilution and close monitoring.
- **Pitta / inflammatory / bleeding states:** internal use aggravates hyperacidity, gastritis, ulcer, and heat/blood disorders.
- **Eyes / mucous membranes:** highly irritant vapour and contact — keep away from eyes.
- **Pregnancy:** sharp heating pungent internally is traditionally cautioned.
- **Large internal doses are emetic** and not a benign remedy.
