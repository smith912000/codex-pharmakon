Four neighbouring chapters of Book 5 are the candidates, and none is asserted here to be the same substance. 5-117 MISU, "Misy — Copiapite — yellow translucent Hydrous Silicate of Iron" (line 10435): "Cyprian misy must be chosen — looking like gold, hard, and if broken a golden colour, glistening like a star. It has the same strength as chalcitis [5-115], and is burnt the same way"; "That from Egypt ... is the best and most effective". 5-118 MELANTHERIA, translator's heading "UNKNOWN" (line 10438): it "grows together in the manner of salt at the mouths of mines out of which brass is dug", the best being "a sulphurous colour [yellow], smooth, even and clear, and on touching water it quickly turns black. **Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Kasisa* is in the *Ushakadi* group of *Sutrasthana* ch.38 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 274-278): "Ushaka (alkaline earth) Saindhava salt, Shilajatu, the two kinds of Kasisa, Hingu and Tutthaka". The group's metrical text (lines 280-283): "It destroys kapham mucous), absorbs the fat of the body and proves curative in cases of stone or gravel in the bladder (urinary calculi), dysuria and abdominal glands". Bhishagratna glosses *Kasisa* "sulphate of iron" in several places (*Chikitsasthana* ch.6 line 260, ch.20 line 143; *Uttara* ch.18 line 155, ch.50 line 177), and `_sources/sushruta.csv` lists it as "ferrous sulfate FeSO4". Uses read: in the ingredients of the *Shodhana Varti* (aseptic plug) and plasters and oils for cleansing an ulcer or open swelling (ch.36 lines 99-112, 123-130); a "pulverised compound consisting of Kasisa, Saindhava, Kinva, Vacha" (ch.36 line 139) and a compound of Kasisa, Saindhava, Kinva and Kuruvinda under the heading *Avasadanam* (ch.36 lines 208-209), for neither of which was the stated purpose read; a "pulverised compound of Kasisa, Tuttha, and Gorochana" applied after the part has been lubricated with clarified butter and honey (*Chikitsasthana* ch.18 lines 514-518; the complaint is named on earlier lines that were not read); an oil with *Kasisa*, *Haritala* and other drugs used as an unguent against polypus (ch.6 lines 259-264); *Kasisa* with honey and others, administered "in cases of Hicca" (hiccup; *Uttara* ch.50 lines 172-180); and with *Srotonjana* and powdered metals in a *Lekhana Puta-paka* (Bhishagratna: a preparation with "Lekhana or scraping properties"; *Uttara* ch.18 lines 150-156). *Pushpa-kasisa* is a separate name (ch.36 line 196), not identified here.

### Active Components

Green vitriol is, in chemistry, ferrous sulphate, usually the pale-green heptahydrate FeSO4·7H2O. It is water-soluble, astringent and metallic in taste, and in air it slowly oxidises to yellow-brown iron(III) compounds. The ancient lists use "vitriol" for several sulphate minerals, so a given text's material may be iron, copper or a mixture; for Sushruta the translator's gloss says iron.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. In the sources the substance is an astringent and cleansing dressing, an eye and ulcer material, and a drug in compounds, always under a physician and without a stated dose. Ferrous sulphate is today a licensed iron supplement, but the Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation for any traditional use and does not link the traditional material to it.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only preparations the texts give. DMM 5-117: misy is "burnt the same way" as chalcitis; 5-119: "those which are not burnt are considered to be stronger than the burnt; except for salt, wine sediment, saltpetre [potassium nitrate], chalk, and other similar things". SS: powder (*churna*) in plugs and plasters (ch.36), powder with *Tuttha* and *Gorochana* (*Chikitsasthana* ch.18), with honey (*Uttara* ch.50). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

Ferrous sulphate is an astringent and an iron source, and the astringent action is consistent with its use as a dressing or plug. It is an irritant and in quantity a poison, especially to children. No clinical studies were retrieved for the traditional uses. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Iron sulphate is an irritant to the gut and in overdose is a serious poison, particularly to children. The ancient name "vitriol" covers other metal sulphates (copper, zinc), which are also toxic. Traditional preparations are unstandardised. The Dioscorides soru chapter itself says it is "more poisonous and causes nausea". Do not use in place of medical care.
