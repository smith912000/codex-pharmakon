# Chopped-up Chaff — as printed by Bryan 1930 (Ebers papyrus)

## *Chaff, the husk and cut straw left from threshed grain, "chopped up" and soaked in water; this is a descriptive English label in Bryan's translation and not a plant with a botanical name. ### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Ebers papyrus among the texts on disk, in Bryan's 1930 English.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it. A search of the Ebers text on disk (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`) for "chaff" found the list row and one recipe, and nothing else. The spine has no row that holds it already (searched under chaff).

**Egyptian Papyrus Ebers (EP).** Bryan's Chapter VI, "Plant Remedies", lists "Chopped-up Chaff" as a single item, directly after "Rotted Cereals" and before "Coriander" (`PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` lines 1259 to 1262; names.csv row, Ch. VI, line 1260). The list row has no forms under it.

The one recipe is in Bryan's chapter on minor medicine. After remedies for the thigh and for the front of the shins, he writes: "In the remedy for a Squashed Leg there are, some will see, the glimmerings of the first Bran Poultice", and prints (lines 2139 to 2145):

> Another for a squashed leg. Chopped-up Chaff soaked in Water. Lay thereon as a Poultice in order to heal him at once. It is also to be used for any Limb that thou wishest.

So the preparation is the chaff, chopped and soaked in water, laid on the injured leg as a poultice, and the text adds that the same may be used on any limb. This is the whole of what the sources read say. The word "Another" shows that the papyrus gives a first remedy for the squashed leg just before it, but the lines read do not print that earlier remedy. [USER: verify]

**Bryan's remark.** The phrase "glimmerings of the first Bran Poultice" is Bryan's own comparison with later practice. It is modern commentary and not part of the Egyptian text, and it does not show that chaff and bran were the same thing to the scribe.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, cereal chaff is the dry husk and fine straw of threshed grain, mostly cellulose and other plant fibre with little else. Soaked in water it becomes a soft, absorbent mass. None of this was read in a source for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** A poultice for a "squashed leg", said to heal it "at once", and usable on any limb.
- **What the text does not say.** No amount, no time of application, no cereal.

The Codex gives no protocol for this material. A crushing injury needs proper medical care, and plant waste is not a clean dressing.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. A moist poultice of soft plant material holds warmth and moisture against the skin; it is not a treatment for a crush injury. Chaff and straw can carry mould spores, dust and bacteria, and putting them on broken skin carries an infection risk. The Ebers term "squashed leg" is not translated here into a modern diagnosis. ### Contraindications

- **Wounds.** Do not put chaff, straw or similar plant waste on an open wound; it can introduce infection and spores. A crushed limb needs medical care.
- **Allergy.** Grain dust and chaff provoke allergy and breathing trouble in some people.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
