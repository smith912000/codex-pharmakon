# Wild Mignonette — Yellow Mignonette / Base Rocket / relative of *ḫaṣītu*-class dye-herbs / Ἠρύγγιον-adjacent Resedaceae of Dioscorides / *Ṣabbāgh* dye-herbs of the Arabic materia medica

## *Reseda lutea* L. | Family: Resedaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, DMM, EP
**Energetic Classification:** Hot and dry in the first degree (Galenic reading, by analogy with the pungent/mustard-adjacent group); drying, attenuating, discutient
**Training Phase:** Phase II — Purification / Clearing (topical drawing and light diuretic support; never a central agent)

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Reseda lutea* is the wayside cousin of dyer's rocket (*Reseda luteola*), the great yellow-dye plant of the ancient Mediterranean and Near East. Its status in the manuscript record is therefore complicated in an instructive way: the Resedaceae were unambiguously present, cultivated, traded and used across the Assyrian, Greek and Arabic worlds — but overwhelmingly as **dye-stuffs and minor drawing-poultices**, not as major internal medicines. An honest entry must say this plainly rather than inflate the plant into a healer it never was.

**Assyrian / Mesopotamian (AT).** The Neo-Assyrian medical and lexical tablets record a substantial vocabulary of dye-plants and "yellowing" herbs alongside the drug-plants, and the boundary between the two categories is porous: substances that stained cloth yellow were also applied in salves and plasters, on the reasoning that what visibly *enters and changes* a fibre will also enter and change flesh. The Resedaceae sit inside that class. The attestation is at the level of plant-family and use-category, not a securely identified single tablet naming *R. lutea* — the identification of Akkadian plant names with modern binomials is contested, and the Codex does not pretend otherwise. **Category: drawing/staining externals.**

**Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* treats the reseda-type herbs among the wayside pot-herbs and minor discutients — the class of plants gathered green, bruised, and laid on hard swellings, bruises and inflamed glands to *disperse* them. This is the classical **διαφορητικόν** (discutient / dispersing) category: not a cooling remedy that quenches, but a warming, thinning one that opens the pores and lets the stagnant matter out. The reputation of the reseda group as a poultice for indurations belongs to this doctrinal slot. Dioscorides does not build a systemic pharmacology around it.

**Avicenna / Unani (EP).** In the Arabic-Persian tradition, plants of this kind enter the *Qānūn*'s scheme as **hot, dry, attenuating (*mulaṭṭif*) and mildly diuretic (*mudirr al-bawl*)** — the family of pungent, mustard-adjacent herbs whose sharpness "cuts thick humours." The Arabic *materia medica* is far more interested in the reseda group as a dye (*ṣibgh*) and a minor externally applied resolvent than as an internal drug. Where an internal use is given it is diuretic and deobstruent, at low dose, in the same register as the milder cresses.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.**
The three traditions converge on one doctrine: **this is a plant that *draws out* and *stains through*.** That is not a metaphor floating free of chemistry — it is a fairly precise folk-description of the plant's actual defensive chemistry.

*Reseda lutea* is a **glucosinolate plant**. Confirmed by direct phytochemical analysis of wild-growing Croatian Resedaceae, *R. lutea* carries phenylalanine-derived glucosinolates (glucotropaeolin and relatives) together with indole glucosinolates, alongside a Leu-derived aliphatic profile in the genus ([Đulović, Tomaš & Blažević, *Molecules*, 2023 — DOI: 10.3390/molecules28041753](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules28041753)). Glucosinolates are inert until tissue is crushed; then endogenous **myrosinase** hydrolyses them to **isothiocyanates** — the sharp, penetrant, blistering, mustard-class molecules.

This is exactly the "hot and dry, attenuating, discutient" doctrine, stated in the older vocabulary. The Galenic *diaphoretikon* — a herb that must be **bruised** to work, that produces heat and redness on the skin, that "disperses hard swellings" — is a description of myrosinase-triggered isothiocyanate release and the resulting counter-irritant rubefaction. The Assyrian intuition that a staining plant also *penetrates flesh* points, chemically, at the same two-part chemistry: the flavonoid/luteolin fraction supplies the yellow that binds fibre, while the glucosinolate–myrosinase system supplies the sharpness that bites tissue. The doctrine and the chemistry are the same fact seen from two ends.

The corollary is equally important and equally doctrinal: a counter-irritant is *not* a systemic drug. The traditions never made *R. lutea* a champion, and the modern data give no reason to promote it.

### Active Components

- **Glucosinolates** — phenylalanine-derived (glucotropaeolin and rhamnosyloxy-benzyl types) plus indole glucosinolates (glucobrassicin class); documented in *R. lutea* by UHPLC-DAD-MS/MS (Đulović et al., 2023, DOI above).
- **Myrosinase** (thioglucoside glucohydrolase) — the activating enzyme; measurable in *R. lutea* floral material and responsible for converting the above to **isothiocyanates** ([Carloni et al., *J Chem Ecol*, 2026 — DOI: 10.1007/s10886-026-01736-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10886-026-01736-0)).
- **Isothiocyanates** — the pungent, electrophilic hydrolysis products; the actual bioactive endpoint.
- **Flavonoids of the luteolin/apigenin class** — the dye principle of the genus; *Reseda* is used industrially as a natural yellow dye ([Chajii et al., *Nat Prod Res*, 2026 — DOI: 10.1080/14786419.2026.2664129](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2026.2664129)). Note: quantitative luteolin content in *R. lutea* specifically is best documented for *R. luteola*; treat *R. lutea* as the weaker dye plant of the pair.
- **Metal-accumulating capacity** — *Reseda* spp. occur on heavily metal-contaminated mine tailings in phytoremediation surveys ([Oujdi et al., *Plants*, 2024 — DOI: 10.3390/plants13131811](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants13131811)). Directly relevant to sourcing: this plant colonises polluted ground.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

A **minor Phase II external**. Its role in the training sequence is narrow and specific: a bruised-herb **drawing poultice** for localised, stagnant, cold-hard swellings — the classical discutient indication — and, at the doctrinal level, a teaching plant. It is the clearest simple example in Volume I of the *bruise-to-activate* principle (an inert glycoside plus a latent enzyme, joined only by damage). Practitioners are directed to it to learn that principle, not to rely on it as a therapeutic.

It is **not** recommended as an internal agent in Kenetica. The traditional diuretic use is real but thin, and the modern safety data to support internal dosing do not exist.

### Scientific Correlation

The honest position: **there are essentially no human clinical data on *Reseda lutea*.** No trials. The correlation available is chemical and mechanistic, and it is genuine as far as it goes.

1. **The chemistry matches the doctrine.** Đulović, Tomaš & Blažević (*Molecules* 28:1753, 2023; DOI: 10.3390/molecules28041753) quantified the glucosinolate profile of wild *R. lutea* by UHPLC-DAD-MS/MS, confirming Phe-derived and indole glucosinolates. This is the mechanistic substrate for every "hot, dry, sharp, drawing" claim in the classical record.

2. **The activation system is real and produces isothiocyanates in vivo.** Carloni et al. (*J Chem Ecol* 52(4), 2026; DOI: 10.1007/s10886-026-01736-0) characterised myrosinase activity in *R. lutea* flowers, produced GSL-enriched extracts, and detected isothiocyanates **in the gut of honey bees fed the extract**, with a significant reduction in *Nosema (Vairimorpha) ceranae* load (44.6% for *R. lutea* extract). Two things follow, and both matter. First: the glucosinolate→myrosinase→isothiocyanate cascade in this plant demonstrably runs and demonstrably kills a parasite — the classical "cutting" action is not fiction. Second — and this is the part a herbal text usually suppresses — **the same extract reduced feeding and survival in a concentration-dependent manner.** The plant's antimicrobial edge and its toxicity are the same edge.

3. **The dye principle is confirmed but industrial, not medical.** Chajii et al. (*Nat Prod Res*, 2026; DOI: 10.1080/14786419.2026.2664129) optimised *Reseda*-based wool dyeing. Relevant only as corroboration of the flavonoid fraction.

**Evidence grade: TRADITIONAL (mechanistic support only).** No human efficacy data for any indication. Chemistry supports the *rationale* of the classical external use; nothing supports an internal therapeutic claim. Any practitioner presenting this plant as a clinically validated remedy is overstating the record.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic / Unani:** Hot and dry (1st degree, by class); *mulaṭṭif* (attenuant), *muḥallil* (resolvent/discutient), weakly *mudirr al-bawl* (diuretic). Corrective: use externally; if taken internally, mucilage-forming vehicles moderate the sharpness.
- **Greek (Dioscoridean):** διαφορητικόν — dispersing; applied bruised to hard swellings.
- **TCM:** Not attested. *Reseda lutea* has no place in the Chinese materia medica; the Codex does not manufacture one.
- **Ayurveda:** Not attested. No rasa/guna/virya/vipaka assignment is offered, because no genuine Ayurvedic tradition of this plant exists to draw from.

### Contraindications

- **Skin.** Isothiocyanate-releasing plants are rubefacient and can blister. Do not apply to broken skin or leave a poultice in place beyond the point of stinging. Patch-test first.
- **Internal use.** Not advised. The only *in vivo* feeding study located (Carloni et al., 2026, honey bees) showed **dose-dependent reductions in consumption and survival**. That is an animal-toxicity signal, not a safety signal.
- **Pregnancy and lactation.** Avoid. No safety data whatsoever.
- **Brassicales sensitivity / mustard allergy.** Cross-reactivity is plausible on chemical grounds (shared glucosinolate–isothiocyanate chemistry). Avoid.
- **Thyroid.** Glucosinolate-rich plants are, as a class, potentially goitrogenic on sustained internal intake. A further reason not to take this one internally.
- **Contaminated-ground harvest.** *Reseda* accumulates heavy metals from mine tailings (Oujdi et al., 2024). Harvest site is a safety issue, not merely a quality issue.
