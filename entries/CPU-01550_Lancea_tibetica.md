# Lancea tibetica — pa.yag, Pa yak (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Lancea tibetica, the first of three binomials Clark 1995 prints for pa.yag (with Salvia miltiorrhiza and Viola dissecta), and the only one the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints ("Tibetan lancea")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** **Named only by the Tibetan texts in this row.** No other text read for this entry names the plant under this Tibetan name. Both loci are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. *Pa.yag* is a drug name and not a botanical one. Clark prints three binomials for it, and the row takes the first. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance", and the three are not shown to be one species. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed names (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 (line 1110).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). Clark's preface (line 81) says that "headings have been added" to the outlines, and his chapter 20 note 12 says that this section "does not appear as a separate section in the original text", with no agreement among scholars about which substances belong in it. So the heading is Clark's. The entry reads: "pa.yag: Lancea tibetica: Salvia miltiorrhiza: Viola dissecta: [has ten names. The leaves grow close to the ground, and it has a blue flower with a tinge of red and a seed in the shape of an animal's heart. It is also known as gyag.snying.lug.(snying) and bya.rog.nor.bu]. The root of pa.yag [has sweet and slightly bitter tastes], heals lung diseases, drains lung pus [and disperses womb tumors]."

The square brackets matter. Clark's preface (line 82) says "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". So the part of the entry outside the brackets, that the root "heals lung diseases, drains lung pus", is the root-text layer as Clark gives it. The description (ten names, leaves close to the ground, a blue flower with a tinge of red, a seed shaped like an animal's heart, the two further names *gyag.snying.lug.(snying)* and *bya.rog.nor.bu*), the sweet and slightly bitter tastes and the dispersal of "womb tumors" are in the bracketed commentary layer. Clark's name index (line 1609) prints *Lancea tibetica* with no further information, and his index of Tibetan names (line 1712) prints *pa.yag*.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (Explanatory Tantra, chapter 20).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. The materia medica summary says, at line 3482: "The root of the Pa yak heals lungs disorders and drains pus from the lungs." The printed page number is 210, and the index (line 7459) gives "Pa yak 210". This repeats the two actions Clark gives outside his brackets and adds nothing. The Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients prints its columns in separate blocks, but in this stretch the three lines for the entry come out together: the phonetic name "Payak" (line 5606), "pa yak" (line 5608) and the scientific name "Lancea tibetica" (line 5610), between the entries for *Pawo serpo* (Phytolacca acinosa) and *Pema gesar* (Bombax ceiba). The English name "Tibetan lancea" is at line 5661, in a separate column that uses "-" for missing names, between "Pokeberry" and "Silk-cotton tree", so it is paired with *Payak* by position only. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a root used for "lung diseases" and to drain "lung pus", and, in the commentary layer, to disperse "womb tumors". These are the texts' claims. The Codex gives no protocol. Lung disease with pus, and tumours of the womb, need medical assessment, and the identity of the drug is itself uncertain across three species.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation and no quantity for *pa.yag*. Clark and the Men-Tsee-Khang text name only "the root" as the part used. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Clark prints, in brackets, "sweet and slightly bitter tastes" for the root (line 1110). No power (cool or warm) is printed in the lines read. The Men-Tsee-Khang text prints no taste or power for the drug at line 3482.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The two texts claim the drug for lung disease and lung pus, and Clark's bracketed layer adds womb tumours. Neither line read assigns it any cultivation or essence-extraction status; the words *bcud len* and "essence-extraction" do not occur in the entry or in the Men-Tsee-Khang line. Clark's preface (lines 69 to 70) names other substances as examples of essence-extraction ingredients (sundew, angelica, *Polygonatum*, salep, certain flowers, calcite and mineral exudate) and does not name *pa.yag*, so that passage is the setting only. The cultivation spec adds that medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. The drug therefore carries a therapeutic status in this tradition and no cultivation one in the lines read.

**Practitioner's note.** The sources give a cultivator no practice with this root. It is a lung remedy with no stated preparation or amount, and its identity is uncertain, so the honest answer is that there is nothing here to take up.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. Lung disease with pus is a serious condition that needs medical care and must not be left to a home preparation. The botanical identity is uncertain (three species in Clark), so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No safety information for pregnancy was read, and the commentary layer's "womb tumors" use should not be self-treated. [USER: verify]
