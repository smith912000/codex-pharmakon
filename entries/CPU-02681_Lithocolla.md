# Lithocolla — Lithokolla (LITHOKOLLA), "glue stone"
## *A cement of marble (or Parian stone) and bull's glue that Dioscorides describes as a probe-like instrument heated red for turning back eyelashes; the translator's heading reads "Lithocolla — Marble & Glue", with the gloss "literally glue stone"; the identification is the translator's and is of high confidence for what the chapter describes* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The only text on disk that names this material is the *De Materia Medica* of Dioscorides, in the 2000 English edition. The row began on 8 November 2026 as an unverified placeholder whose key was the translation gloss "Glue literally"; this entry replaces it. A search of the same text for *lithocolla*, *lithokolla* and *glue stone* found only the chapter heading, the chapter body and the two index entries (the Greek name index lists LITHOKOLLA at page 824 and the English index lists Lithocolla at page 824).

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Lithocolla is chapter 164 of Book Five, in the section on stones (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading line 10683, body line 10687). The chapter is one sentence: "Lithocolla — a mixture of marble or Parian stone and bulls glue — used as a probe made red-hot is able to turn back the eyelashes." The translator's suggestion line (10685) reads "Lithocolla — Marble & Glue" and the next line "literally glue stone". The neighbouring chapters are 5-163 (the stones found in sponges, taken in wine for bladder stones) and 5-165 (Ostrakites lithos, fossil oyster shell).

What the chapter says: the material is a made mixture, not a stone dug from the ground; its parts are marble or Parian stone (the text gives both) and bull's glue; it is shaped as a probe (an instrument), heated red-hot; and the use is on the eyelashes, to turn them back. What the chapter does not say: the proportions, how the mixture is set, the heating method beyond "red-hot", how the lashes are treated, any pain or risk, any internal use, or any quality test. ### Active Components

The source names the parts only: marble (or Parian stone) and bull's glue. In general terms, marble is a form of calcium carbonate, and animal glue is a gelatin-rich protein material made by boiling hides or bones. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** A heated probe of this material used to turn back the eyelashes.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no internal use, no healing claim in the Codex's sense.

The Codex does not recommend applying a heated probe to the eye or lid, and does not translate the Greek procedure into a modern treatment.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Heated metal or ceramic instruments applied to the eyelid carry a clear risk of burns, and the lid margin and eye are delicate; this is why the chapter reads as a record of an ancient surgical technique and not as a recommendation. Turned-in eyelashes (trichiasis) are today treated by trained clinicians. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Little to map: the only body part named is the eyelashes, which the chapter says a heated probe of the mixture can turn back. In modern anatomy this is the margin of the eyelid, where the lash follicles sit, so the claimed site is the eyelid edge and the lashes that grow from it. The chapter names no other organ, tissue, channel or energy, and prints no quality of warming, cooling, astringency or similar. The entry records that the mixture's parts are marble or Parian stone and bull's glue, and that the chapter gives no internal use, no pain or risk statement, and no quality test. The entry notes that the chapter describes the material as a made mixture and not as a dug stone, in a single sentence. The mapped claim is therefore limited to the eyelid margin and its lashes.

### Contraindications

- **Do not heat any probe and apply it to the eye or eyelid.** Burn and eye injury are the obvious risks.
- **Animal product.** Bull's glue is an animal-derived material; relevant for those who avoid such products.
- **Identity.** The chapter describes a mixture, not a single stone; modern use of the word "lithocolla" may differ. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved.
