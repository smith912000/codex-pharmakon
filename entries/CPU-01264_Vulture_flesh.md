# Vulture flesh — "Vulture meat" (Clark 1995; Men-Tsee-Khang 2011); Clark notes "fourteen names" but prints none in the line read
## *The meat of the vulture, a bird the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary pairs with Aegypius monachus Brisson; Clark prints no binomial for it* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The Explanatory Tantra (*bshad rgyud*), chapter 20, has a section "(8) Medicines of Animal Origin" listing thirteen kinds of animal product (Clark 1995, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` lines 1147-1148). Under "Meat" (line 1167) Clark prints: "Vulture meat: [has fourteen names]. It increases [digestive] heat, removes goiters [and dispels spirits]." Square brackets in Clark's text mark wording that is not in the main verse (I did not confirm his exact convention), so "digestive" and "and dispels spirits" are bracketed additions in his rendering. The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3529) reads: "Vulture meat generates heat and removes goitres." It has no clause about spirits. The two agree on heat and on goitre; they differ on whether the heat is "digestive" and on spirits. [USER: verify] The next line in Clark is peacock meat, which belongs to a different material.

The name list `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 345 has the row "Vulture meat" with the source "rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Efficacy of Medicinal Substances)", identification-uncertain flag "yes". The Men-Tsee-Khang glossary (lines 6904-6906) sets "Vulture" beside "Aegypius monachus Brisson", a pairing made by list order in an OCR-split two-column glossary and therefore not certain. [USER: verify]

Vulture flesh is also a dietary item in the same Tantra. Clark's classification of meats (line 672) puts "the vulture, red vulture, harrier hawk, crow, owl and sparrowhawk" among the birds of prey, and the Men-Tsee-Khang text has the same group (line 2914). The next lines say the flesh of birds of prey "has coarse, light and sharp powers which increase the digestive heat, disperse tumors, increase flesh and eliminate all cold disorders" (Clark line 673; Men-Tsee-Khang line 2931). In the regimen for phlegm disorders, treatment "should be by means of mutton, the meat of fish, wild yak, lynx and vulture, as well as by old grain, hot dumplings made from barley flour, matured grain beer" and similar warm, light, coarse things "in small quantities" (Clark line 1415; Men-Tsee-Khang line 4433 for the *baekan* regimen). So the Tibetan texts give vulture flesh two roles, a listed medicine (line 1167) and a food for phlegm disorders (line 1415).

*Other texts on disk, not folded in.* In the Sushruta Samhita (Bhishagratna) *Gridhra* (vulture) is named in the *Prasaha* group of "carnivorous birds that suddenly dart on their prey" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 546-549), whose flesh the text says is "specially beneficial in cases of consumption and kindred wasting diseases" (about line 556). In the chapter on diseases of vision, "the flesh of a vulture" with the flesh of an *Ena* deer, rock salt, honey and clarified butter is prepared and applied to the eye, and "the lard of vultures, snakes and cocks" with liquorice is pasted with honey as a salve (`SS_Uttara_ch17.txt` lines 280-285; Dallana's note at line 297). The list row is `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 210. Sushruta is not on this entry's tag line. These are recorded as positive loci for a possible SS tag (reported to the editor, not applied). The Ebers papyrus (Bryan 1930) names the vulture only for blood, egg and feather (see the Vulture entry, III_205).

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, flesh is mostly water and muscle protein, with fat and small amounts of vitamins and minerals. A carrion-eating bird's flesh can also carry the bacteria and parasites of what it feeds on. Nothing links any constituent to the actions the Tantra gives.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts credit the flesh with raising heat and removing goitre, and name it, among other meats, for phlegm disorders. The Codex does not recommend eating or taking it. Vultures are among the most threatened birds in the world and are protected in law in most countries, their flesh is not sold as food in most places, and a wild carrion-eater is a hygiene risk. A goitre is a swelling of the thyroid or neck and needs a clinician's assessment. The entry exists to record the text.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation of vulture meat as a medicine. As a food for phlegm disorders the text says only that the listed meats and warm foods are to be taken "in small amounts" (Men-Tsee-Khang line 4433) or "in small quantities" (Clark line 1415). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Goitre has several causes, including too little iodine in the diet, and is assessed and treated by clinicians; I have retrieved no evidence that vulture flesh treats it. Wild-bird meat can carry bacteria and parasites. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT):** The Tantra assigns qualities to the group, not to the vulture by name. All meats are "sweet tasting and have a sweet post-digestive taste" (Clark line 673; Men-Tsee-Khang line 2923). The flesh of birds of prey has "coarse, light and sharp powers" (Clark line 673; "rough, light and sharp qualities" in Men-Tsee-Khang line 2931), which raise digestive heat, disperse tumors, increase flesh and eliminate cold disorders. The vulture is in that group (Clark line 672; Men-Tsee-Khang line 2914), so these qualities apply by group membership, which is an inference from the classification and not a sentence about the vulture. The Materia Medica line itself gives only the action "increases heat".

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Vulture meat is a listed medicine (Clark line 1167) and a food in the regimen for phlegm disorders (line 1415); both are therapeutic placements. The wider frame does not reach it. The three humors are rooted in attachment, hatred and obscuration (Clark line 51), and attachment, hatred and closed-mindedness are "the three causes" of wind, bile and phlegm disorders (line 147), so disease is at root a spiritual condition and medicine is one support. The chapter on routine behaviour calls for refraining from the ten negative actions, the first being "taking life" (line 616). The rejuvenation passage in chapter 23 (lines 1266-1275) does not name vulture meat, and the lines read give it no *bcud len* use. The Tibetan tradition has no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice, and none is asserted here. The bracketed clause "dispels spirits" in Clark's rendering of the meat line is a therapeutic claim about spirits-related illness, and the Men-Tsee-Khang text omits it; it is not read as a cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The vulture appears as a medicinal meat and as a warm, light food for phlegm disorders; for a cultivator the tradition's own lesson is the first of the ten negative actions, and the Codex does not recommend taking a vulture's life or its flesh. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** flesh of a wild carrion-eating bird can carry bacteria and parasites; it should not be eaten or applied.
- **Goitre and swellings:** a neck swelling needs a clinician; the Tantra's "removes goiters" is a historical statement and not a treatment.
- **Conservation and law:** vultures are threatened and protected in most countries; the Codex does not recommend taking any animal for these uses.
- **Ethics:** the Tibetan text itself lists "taking life" first among the ten negative actions (Clark line 616).
