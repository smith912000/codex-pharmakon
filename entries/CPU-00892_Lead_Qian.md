**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Seven chapters in Book 5 (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`), every one headed TOXIC. 5-95 MOLUBDOS PEPLUMENOS, "Washed Lead" (line 10296), beaten in a lead mortar with a lead pestle under water until the water is black, strained and made into tablets; "It is able to cool, and is an astringent to close the pores, fill up sores and wounds, and deter fluids that gather in the eyes ... ### Active Components

Lead is the element Pb, a soft, dense, bluish metal. The materials in the texts are lead's compounds: litharge (lead monoxide, PbO, yellow), red lead or minium (Pb3O4, orange-red), white lead or cerussa (basic lead carbonate, which forms when lead is exposed to vinegar vapour and carbon dioxide, as in Dioscorides 5-103), and galena (lead sulphide, PbS, the ore). All are toxic; lead compounds are cumulative poisons that affect the nervous system, blood and kidneys, and the compounds' fumes and dusts are as hazardous as the swallowed salts.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Lead's compounds appear in the sources as astringent plasters and eye applications, as an antiworm and antitoxic group (Sanskrit), as a ritual-spoken red pigment (Egypt), and as a drug of the lower Chinese grade. None supports taking lead for health, and the Dioscorides headings call it TOXIC. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only preparations the texts give. DMM 5-95: lead beaten under water in a lead mortar, strained and tabletted; 5-96: lead plates layered with sulphur and fired; 5-103: lead plate over vinegar, the deposit ground and sieved, shaped into lozenges for external use. SBJ: Qian Dan "when sublimated, it turns back into Jiu Guang (Nine Lights)". SS: the metal as a rubbed substance ("Trapu (killed lead)" in curd for a week); no method of "killing" is given in the lines read. EP: Bryan gives the proportions quoted above for the heart remedy. No other dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Lead salts are astringent, which is consistent with the Greek wound and eye uses, and lead plaster was a standard European dressing for centuries. Lead is also a cumulative poison with no safe level, which is why the use ended. No clinical studies were retrieved for the traditional uses. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(b) Framed, thinly.* The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. The one frame red-lead sits inside is *heka*: in Bryan's rendering the alopecia mixture of iron, red-lead, onions, alabaster and honey is "to be spoken over", so spell and drug are components of one intervention. No cultivation status for lead is attested in the lines read.

**Assyrian (AT).** *(c) Absent, no locus.* Nothing was read. The blocking warning is in force. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What these chapters offer a cultivator is the discipline of attention, and in lead's case a protective one: the headings say TOXIC; the burning chapter says to close the nostrils against the vapour; and cerussa is "among those things which taken internally kill". The origin of the best material (Rhodes, Corinth, Lacedaemon; 5-103) and the washing steps are recorded as craft knowledge.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent, and the attestation rests on a spine row.* No Avicenna text or list row was found. Innate heat and radical moisture cannot be tied to lead without a source and are not tied to it here. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: Inferior Class.* Qian Dan and Fen Xi, the two entries merged into this one by the spine, stand under the heading "Jades and Stones: Inferior Class", and the paragraph speaks of those two entries by name; Yang has no bare lead entry. The preface (lines 391-396) says the inferior class "mainly treat disease and correspond to earth. They are usually toxic and cannot be taken for a long time. If one intends to eliminate cold and heat and evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and cure disease, one should base [one's efforts] on the inferior class." The Qian Dan line itself says "Protracted taking may enable one to communicate with the spirit light", which sits oddly with a class that "cannot be taken for a long time" and is recorded as printed, not endorsed.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* *Sisa* is claimed by *dravyaguna* (the *Trapvadi* group and the ch.46 metal group) and, in the *Silajatu* passage, by the metals that give that resin its virtue. It is not named in the *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapters searched (*Chikitsasthana* ch.26-30); the *Silajatu* passage is in the diabetes chapter (ch.13), and the one longevity sentence near the metals is for the iron preparation in the *Kushtha* chapter (ch.10 lines 207-208), whose method is said to serve "all kinds of Loha (zinc, copper, lead and gold)". No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim for lead is printed in the lines read.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator to take. Lead is, in these sources, an astringent plaster, an eye applicant, an antiworm metal and a spoken red pigment; the Chinese classification is the lowest grade, "toxic" and not for long use, and the Greek headings agree. The only discipline the sources teach is protective: do not breathe the vapour, do not swallow it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved except the ratios for the Egyptian heart remedy. ### Contraindications

Lead and all its compounds (red lead, litharge, white lead, galena, and "killed" or "ash" preparations) are poisonous and cumulative; children and pregnancy are most at risk. Heating them releases toxic fume. Use in medicines, cosmetics and paints is banned or restricted in many places. Do not use. Ritual and spoken uses are ritual, not therapy.
