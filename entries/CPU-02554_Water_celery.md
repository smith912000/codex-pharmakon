# Water celery — 水靳 *Shuijin* (Shennong bencao jing), also 水英 *shuiying*
## *A water-growing vegetable of the carrot family; our identification is Oenanthe javanica (Blume) DC., the water dropwort / water celery, of medium confidence (Oenanthe sinensis is an alternative). The name 水靳 is printed with a character that is probably a variant of 芹, and the classic's own text gives no description* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The identification is ours.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the lower (下) classic, reads 味甘，平 ("sweet, balanced"). It governs 女子赤沃 ("women's red discharge"), "stops bleeding" (止血), "nourishes the essence" (养精), "protects the blood vessels" (保血脉), "adds qi" (益气), and "makes a person plump, sturdy and fond of eating" (令人肥健、嗜食). The alias is 一名水英 ("water flower/hero") and the habitat 生池泽, "pools and marshes". There is no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 水靳 *shuijin*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 4089) Sun's closing line for the group, 上菜，下品二种。旧同, says that this and one other vegetable (苦瓠 on the preceding page) are the two lower-grade vegetables, as in the old arrangement.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 adds only the origin 生南海 ("the South Sea"). No part, month or Wu Pu line is on the page.

**Sun's note (案).** Sun cites Shuowen (芹, "Chu mallow/葵"; and a second character, glossed as a vegetable), the Zhouli (which has a pickle of that vegetable), the Erya (芹 = 楚葵), Guo Pu ("today's water celery vegetable") and the Zilin (the herb grows in water; its root can be wound on vessels; another vegetable "like garlic, growing in water"). Sun does not say outright that 水靳 and 芹 are the same plant, but the glosses he collects point that way.

**Identity.** The glosses (water-growing 芹, "water celery") point to a water-side umbellifer, so *Oenanthe javanica* is a reasonable reading; the Mingyi origin "South Sea" and the printed character 靳 leave the identity less secure. ### Active Components

The text prints no constituent. In general terms the water dropwort is a water-side umbelliferous vegetable eaten in East Asia. Other water-side umbellifers (hemlock water dropwort, *Oenanthe crocata*) are highly poisonous, which is the reason the identity matters. No analysis was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records a sweet, balanced vegetable-drug for women's red discharge, bleeding and weakness, which "makes a person plump, sturdy and fond of eating". The Codex gives no protocol. Bleeding or abnormal discharge needs medical assessment.

### Scientific Correlation

In general knowledge the water dropwort (*Oenanthe javanica*) is eaten as a vegetable in parts of East Asia, while some other *Oenanthe* species are among the most poisonous plants of Europe and North America, so a plant must never be gathered from water on a name alone. ### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 水靳 in the lower grade (下品); its preface says lower-grade drugs treat disease, are mostly toxic, and are not for long use. The paragraph has no 久服 clause. It does carry clauses that sound nourishing ("nourishes the essence", "makes a person plump and sturdy"), but without a long-use promise and under the lower-grade frame, so the Codex records (b) and does not read a cultivation status into it. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 水靳 *shuijin*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 4089)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A lower-grade vegetable-drug with nourishing-sounding clauses that the classic does not tie to long use. Do not gather water plants on this entry's authority: some look-alikes are deadly.

### Contraindications

- **Identification.** Some water-side umbellifers are deadly poisons; do not gather any plant on the strength of this entry.
- **Bleeding and discharge.** Need medical assessment.
