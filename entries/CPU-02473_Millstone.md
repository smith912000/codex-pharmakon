# Millstone — ḥajar al-raḥā (حجر الرحى)

## *Stone of the grinding mill, kind of rock not stated; the identification is the plain meaning of the Arabic and the text does not say which rock* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** The entry is two lines long: Book II, entry *ḥajar al-raḥā*, Arabic text lines 13416 to 13418, in the series of stones after *ḥajar al-yahūd* (Jew's stone), *ḥajar al-isfanj* (sponge stone) and *al-ḥajar al-labanī* (milk stone). It has no "what it is" section. The text does not say which rock is meant, how the stone is to be prepared, or what "from it" (the Arabic *ʿanhu*) refers to.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The whole entry reads: "Swellings and pustules: the steam of vinegar from it prevents bleeding (*nazf*) and prevents hot swellings" (بخار الخل عنه يمنع النزف ويمنع الأورام الحارة). That is one use under one heading, with no temperament, no dose and no other author. The wording suggests vinegar poured on a heated stone and its vapour used, but the text does not say so, and the Codex does not supply the method. The dust of the millstone (*ghubār al-raḥā*) appears separately in the Canon as an ingredient: it is named with frankincense and myrrh as a binder for shell-flesh on wounds (line 17216), among the "gluey" agents in a list (line 26386) and in a wick with frankincense dust, aloe, vinegar and egg white (line 26405). Those lines were read; their surrounding chapters were not studied, and they are a different drug (the dust) from the entry's stone.

### Active Components

A millstone is a rock, usually sandstone, granite or a volcanic stone, and its composition depends on the quarry. The text gives none, and the entry does not say which stone is meant. The vinegar named in the entry is dilute acetic acid; reaction with a carbonate rock would release carbon dioxide, but the text names no such rock. [USER: verify.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The text records a stone's vinegar steam against bleeding and hot swellings. Bleeding and inflammation need medical care, and no protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

None is given beyond the vinegar steam of the entry. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify.] Steam from vinegar is not known to stop bleeding.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

None is given. The entry has no temperament, only the action under "swellings and pustules."

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The entry is a two-line external remedy and says nothing about the vital faculties. **(c) Absent: no cultivation framing is attested for the millstone.**

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing to practise here. The entry shows how brief a Canon stone entry can be.

### Contraindications

- Heated stones and steam burn; vinegar vapour irritates the eyes and airways.
- Bleeding that does not stop needs medical care.
