# Elephant's Milk (Sushruta Samhita)

## *Milk of the she-elephant, as named in Sushruta Sutra ch.45; the animal in an Indian text is most probably the Asian elephant (Elephas maximus), which is our identification, of medium to high confidence, since the English text says only "she-elephant"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta Samhita only, among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The English text is Bhishagratna's translation, Sutra ch.45, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`. The Sushruta Samhita (Bhishagratna's English translation, as on disk) opens its milk group in Sutra ch.45 with the sentence "The milk of a cow, she-goat, she-camel, ewe, she-buffalo, mare, she-elephant, or of a woman, is what generally comes to the use of man" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` line 415). The group paragraph that follows is about milk as a whole, not about one animal: "heavy, sweet, slimy, cold, glossy, emollient, laxative and mild" (line 422), "rejuvenating and aphrodisiac" (line 453), "increases the duration of life, and acts as a vitaliser" (line 457) and, through kindred properties, "augments the quality of bodily albumen (Ojah)" (line 461).

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The she-elephant is the seventh of the animals in the list at line 415. Her milk is then described (lines 520-522): "The milk of a she-elephant is sweet though it leaves an astringent after-taste. It is spermatopoietic, heavy, demulcent, cooling and tonic. It invigorates the eyesight." The same chapter describes the curd made from it (line 617): "light in digestion, subdues Kapham, and is heat-making in its potency. It impairs digestion, leaves an astringent after-taste and increases the quantity of fecal matter." Its clarified butter is "astringent in taste, and brings about a suppression of stool and urine. It is bitter, light, and stomachic (Agnikara), and proves curative in cutaneous affections (Kushtha), poisoning, worms in the intestines, and derangements of the Kapham" (line 842). Her urine is described separately (line 1749) and belongs to a different entry. The text adds the general rule that milk milched in the morning is heavy and in the evening is cooling and eye-invigorating (lines 524-534).

### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms the milk of any mammal contains water, protein, fat, lactose and minerals; no figure for elephants was read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The milk is sweet with an astringent after-taste, "spermatopoietic, heavy, demulcent, cooling and tonic", and it "invigorates the eyesight" (line 520-522).
- **What the text does not say.** No disease for the milk itself, no dose, no preparation beyond the curd and butter described.

Elephant milk is not obtainable and the animal is protected. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no dose. The milk is described fresh, as curd (line 617) and as clarified butter (line 842), each with its properties and no quantity. The general rules on boiling milk (lines 534-543) are for milk in general. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. The Asian elephant is an endangered species and elephant milk is not a human food or medicine in any modern system the Codex retrieved. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (ch.45).** Rasa: sweet, with astringent after-taste. Qualities printed: heavy, demulcent, cooling. Action printed: spermatopoietic, tonic, eye-invigorating (lines 520-522). No *vipaka* is printed for the milk itself.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The milk is placed in the dietetic chapter, with "spermatopoietic" and "tonic" among its qualities (line 521), which sit near the Sushrutan field of *vajikarana*. The sentence does not name *vajikarana* or *rasayana* and gives no *ojas* claim for this milk. The group paragraph (lines 419-461) says milk as a whole is rejuvenating, increases the duration of life and augments *ojas* (rendered "bodily albumen (Ojah)" by the translator). Those words are printed for the group and are not repeated under this animal, so this entry does not carry them across: the status is framed, not attested for this milk alone. 

**Practitioner's note.** A sweet, heavy, cooling milk with a tonic and eye-invigorating description, in a list of milks of which only the cow's and the goat's are ordinary. A cultivator has nothing to do with it beyond reading the group statement on milk.

### Contraindications

- **Conservation and law.** The Asian elephant is a protected species; milk from wild or captive elephants is not advisable to seek.
- **Hygiene.** Raw milk of any animal can carry infection.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The species is our reading of "she-elephant" in an Indian text.
