# Horn salt — rwa.tsha (Clark 1995); Ra tsha (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011); "Horn Salt"

## *An artificial salt "of no fixed color" named horn salt (the Tibetan *rwa.tsha*), listed with ash salt among the salty-taste medicines and said to cure cold disorders of the vessel organs; the text gives no chemical identification, so the material is left as an unidentified artificial salt, and the Codex does not equate it with sal ammoniac, of low confidence* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** This is a single-tradition entry, read in two English versions of the Explanatory Tantra. The main locus is Clark 1995 (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). In the materia medica of Explanatory Tantra ch.20, the section on salts and related minerals, line 932 reads: "rwa.tsha and thal.tsha: Horn Salt and Ash Salt: [are both artificial salts of no fixed color and] cure cold disorders of the vessel organs." The square brackets are Clark's, marking the commentarial material that he adds to the root text. So the text names two salts together under one entry (horn salt, *rwa.tsha*, and ash salt, *thal.tsha*), calls both artificial, says they have no fixed colour, and gives one action. The Tibetan names are as Clark prints them. Horn salt is the entry for this row; ash salt (*thal.tsha*) has its own row on the spine (Ash salt, CPU-02399). The row began as an unverified placeholder on 13 October, and on 30 October the check kept it and rejected a first-pass match with sal ammoniac (CPU-00204); this entry replaces it.

*Where the salts stand in the chapter.* Clark's line 767 gives the taste group: "The group of medicines having salty taste includes rock salt, wood salt, horn salt, black 'sanchal' salt, white mineral salt, mirabilite, soda salt (saltpetre), white rock salt, ash salt, aragonite (Tartar salt) and salsoda (sodium bicarbonate)." In ch.20 itself the salts run: lake salt (*rgya.tsha*, line 927), rock salt (*rgyam.tsha*, line 928), wood salt (*lche.myang.tsha*, line 929), black 'sanchal' salt (*kha.ru.tsha*, line 930), white mineral salt (*tsabs.ru.tsha*, line 931), then horn salt and ash salt (line 932), white rock salt (*mdze.tsha*, line 933), borax (*tsha.la*, line 934) and soda salt (*lba.tsha*, line 935). Horn salt is the sixth of these.

*The second version.* The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`) spells the names differently. Its salty-taste list (line 3211) runs "Gyam tsha, Gya tsha, Che nyang tsha, Ra tsha, Kharu tsha, Tsabru tsha, Ze tsha, Wa tsha, Zae tsha, Thai tsha, Shing tsha, Yabak khyara, Bultog". Its action list (line 3400) says: "Ra tsha and Thai tsha cure cold disorders of the vessel organs." That sentence is the same as Clark's for *rwa.tsha* and *thal.tsha*, and the order of the names matches, so "Ra tsha" is read here as that text's spelling of horn salt and "Thai tsha" as ash salt. The match is an inference from the identical sentence, not a statement in the text. [USER: verify] The same text places Ra tsha and Thai tsha with Tsabru tsha, Gya tsha and Gyam tsha in "the group of medicines that cure cold disorders associated with baekan" (lines 3622-3627).

*A term not to be confused with it.* The Men-Tsee-Khang notes to that chapter (line 3274) explain "Wa tsha" as "an extremely bad tasting salt found in northern Tibet. It looks like a mass of sugar and cures and prevents goitre", and line 3400 says "Wa tsha eliminates goitre". That is a separate name in the same list; whether it is related to *rwa.tsha* is not stated. The same notes explain "Thai tsha" (line 3288): "Thai tsha literally means artificial ash salt. It is made by burning different kinds of woods." That is a gloss for ash salt, not horn salt, and the notes give no gloss for "Ra tsha". [USER: verify]

*What the texts do not say.* Neither text says what horn salt is made from, how it is made, or why it is called horn. Clark prints no chemical identification, and the translator's gloss gives only the word "artificial". The English "horn salt" is a translation of the Tibetan name (*rwa* is the Tibetan word for horn, a reading ours and not printed). [USER: verify] A previous entry for sal ammoniac (`II_116_Sal_Ammoniac.md`) searched the same lines and found nothing linking these salts to ammonium chloride; the same result holds here.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. The two English versions say only that it is an artificial salt of no fixed colour. In general terms an "artificial salt" made by burning or evaporation could be a mixture of alkali and neutral salts, and the sources do not fix which. The idea that it is sal ammoniac, or a salt made from horn, is a guess that nothing in the text supports. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.** The record is of a manufactured salt of unknown make, listed among the salty medicines for cold disorders of the vessel organs. The Codex does not translate "cold disorders of the vessel organs" into a modern diagnosis and gives no protocol. An artificial salt of unknown composition cannot be assumed safe to swallow.

### Preparation & Dosing

Neither text gives a preparation, a method of manufacture, a mixture, a form or a dose for horn salt. The salty taste group is used in compounds and as external hot compresses in the Men-Tsee-Khang text (line 3231: "Compresses with heated salt induce sweating, generates body heat and improves appetite"), but that statement belongs to the salty taste in general and not to horn salt in particular. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Salts in strong solution are irritant; taken in quantity, soluble salts of sodium and potassium cause vomiting and electrolyte disturbance, and alkaline salts are caustic. Whether horn salt was one of these cannot be told from the text. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT):** The text gives taste, potency and post-digestive taste by class and not for each substance. Horn salt is in the salty-taste group (Clark line 767). For the salty taste the Men-Tsee-Khang text says it "relaxes stiffness of the body, clears bowel obstructions and blocked energy channels" and that "excess intake of salt" has the harms listed there (line 3231). It says the salty taste "pacifies loong and baekan disorders" and that, "with the exception of Che nyang tsha and Gyam tsha, all other salty substances aggravate tripa disorders" (line 3251); Ra tsha is not among the two exceptions. And it says that "the post-digestive taste of the sweet and salty tastes is sweet" (line 3262). These are statements about the salty taste and its group, applied here by the text's own rule, and not statements printed for horn salt. Clark prints for the entry only the action, "cure cold disorders of the vessel organs". No potency is printed for it.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested for horn salt. The tradition's frame, in which the three *nyes pa* (*rlung*, *mkhris pa*, *bad kan*) rise from the three poisons and medicine is the third of four treatment branches supporting practice and not reaching the root, is the context in which the entry sits. The text assigns horn salt no *bcud len* use and no status beyond a therapeutic action. No category of substances for subtle-body practice is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** An artificial salt of unknown make, listed in the old pharmacopoeia for cold disorders. Nothing here supports ingestion or any cultivation use. The tradition's own entry is one line.

### Contraindications

- **Unknown composition.** Do not swallow or apply a salt of unknown make. Artificial salts can be caustic, and mineral medicines sold unregulated may be contaminated with other metals.
- **Identity.** An unidentified artificial salt; not sal ammoniac on this evidence. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Cold disorders.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
