# Boar liver — HEPAR KAPROU (Dioscorides 2-48), "Boar's Liver"
## *The liver of the wild boar, dried and drunk in wine against bites; the translator prints "Boar's Liver" and no binomial* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

This entry is one short chapter of one text. The pig as an animal (flesh, tooth, bone, stool, with the Tibetan "Boar" glossary line) is in `III_41_Pig.md`; pig blood in `III_23_Pig_blood.md`.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 2-48 *HEPAR KAPROU* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading line 5127, text line 5129; `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` row "HEPAR KAPROU, 2-48 (line 5127)"; Osbaldeston and Wood 2000). The chapter, headed "Boar's Liver", reads in full: "The liver of a boar (taken whilst it is) fresh, dried and pounded into small pieces and taken as a drink with wine, is a remedy against the bites of snakes and beasts." The parenthesis is the translator's. As the English stands, the liver is taken fresh and then dried and pounded, then drunk in wine. It is an antidote use, for bites of snakes and "beasts". The chapter gives no amount, no time and no ritual, and it states no quality of the liver (warming, drying and so on).

The liver chapters sit together in Book 2, among the parts of living creatures: goat's liver (2-47, line 5125) is for night blindness, and the smoke or the dripping fluid of the roasting liver is applied; the liver of a mad dog (2-49, line 5130) is eaten roasted by those it has bitten, "thought to keep them safe from the fear of water", and the tooth of the biting dog is tied to the arm as a precaution; both are in their own chapters and are separate from this one. The boar appears in Dioscorides' book under other chapters too: the pig and its fat (2-87, lard; 2-94, fats) are in `III_37_Lard.md`, and "wild boars'" fat is prepared like bulls' suet in 2-90 (line 5317). Those chapters are not about the liver.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, liver is rich in protein, iron (as haem), vitamin A (retinol), vitamin B12, folate and copper, and is the organ in which an animal stores and processes many substances.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Greek chapter records boar liver as a drink with wine against snake and animal bites, without outcome. A bite from a snake or a possibly rabid animal is an emergency that this does not treat. The Codex gives no protocol, and it recommends no one hunt a boar for this.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation is the one above: liver "(taken whilst it is) fresh, dried and pounded into small pieces and taken as a drink with wine" (2-48, line 5129). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Liver from pigs and wild boar can carry hepatitis E virus, bacteria such as *Salmonella*, and parasites, and wild boar can carry *Trichinella*; liver must be cooked through. Animal liver is very high in vitamin A, which is toxic in excess, especially in pregnancy. No liver, raw or dried, is known to neutralise snake venom; antivenom is the treatment. ### Contraindications

- **Bites and stings:** snakebite and bites from animals that may carry rabies need immediate medical treatment; liver is not a substitute.
- **Hygiene and infection:** raw or undercooked pig and boar liver can transmit hepatitis E, *Salmonella* and parasites; wild-killed meat carries added risk.
- **Vitamin A:** liver is a concentrated source; avoid in pregnancy.
- **Conservation and law:** wild boar are hunted under licence in many places and are an invasive pest in others; local law applies. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and diet:** pork is forbidden in some faiths and avoided by many people; the Codex recommends no animal food.
