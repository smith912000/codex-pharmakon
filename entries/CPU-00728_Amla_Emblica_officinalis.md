# Amla — Amalaki, Indian Gooseberry, Yu Gan Zi (余甘子), Dhatri

## *Phyllanthus emblica* L. (syn. *Emblica officinalis* Gaertn.) | Family: Phyllanthaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, DMM, SBJ, SS
**Energetic Classification:** Cooling yet balancing — sour-astringent, drying; a rare tridoshic "cooling tonic" in the Galenic-Unani frame.
**Training Phase:** Rejuvenation / Rasayana (deep tonification and antioxidant consolidation)

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Sushruta / Ayurvedic tradition (SS).** Amla is one of the great champions of the Indian materia medica. In the classical Ayurvedic corpus (Charaka and Sushruta Samhitas) *Amalaki* is a premier **rasayana** — a rejuvenating, longevity-promoting drug — and one of the three fruits of **Triphala** (with *Haritaki* and *Bibhitaki*). It is prized as the base of *Chyawanprash*, the classical restorative jam. Ayurveda gives it an unusual profile: it carries five of the six tastes (all but salt), with sour (*amla*) dominant, and is one of the few cooling drugs said to pacify all three doshas — Vata by its sourness, Pitta by its cooling *virya*, and Kapha by its drying, astringent post-digestive effect.

**Avicennan / Unani tradition (CM).** As *Amlaj*, amla is a well-established Unani drug — a cold, dry astringent used as a cardiac and cerebral tonic (*muqawwi-e-qalb*, *muqawwi-e-dimagh*), a digestive strengthener, and a hair and scalp tonic. Avicenna's tradition classes it among the cooling astringent fruits that tone the viscera and check excessive discharge.

**Dioscoretic / Mediterranean transmission (DMM).** The "emblic myrobalan" entered the Greco-Arabic-European stream through the myrobalan trade as one of the medicinal myrobalans imported from India, used as a cooling astringent and stomachic. Its Mediterranean use is a genuine case of eastward-sourced materia medica absorbed into the Dioscoretic-Galenic pharmacy rather than a native Greek drug.

**Shennong / Chinese tradition (TCM).** In Chinese medicine amla is *Yu Gan Zi* (余甘子, "the aftersweet fruit," naming the sweet aftertaste that follows its sourness). It is classed as sour, sweet, astringent, and cool, entering to clear heat, benefit the throat, moisten the lung, generate fluids, and harmonise digestion — the drug also being important in the Tibetan tradition.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** Amla's symbolic reputation as a fruit of longevity and regeneration — the *rasayana* par excellence — maps onto a genuine and now well-characterised pharmacology: an exceptionally high content of **vitamin C stabilised by tannins** (which slows its oxidation) plus a dense load of hydrolysable tannins and gallic-acid derivatives. The tradition's "rejuvenation" corresponds to measurable antioxidant, lipid-lowering, and glucose-modulating actions. Where Viper's Bugloss (CPU-00725) shows the doctrine of signatures pointing the wrong way, amla shows tradition and pharmacology in genuine convergence.

### Active Components

- **Vitamin C (ascorbic acid)** — high content, unusually stabilised against oxidation by co-occurring tannins.
- **Hydrolysable tannins — emblicanin A and B**, punigluconin, pedunculagin — the characteristic antioxidant tannin complex.
- **Gallic acid, ellagic acid, and gallotannins** — major phenolic actives.
- Flavonoids (quercetin, kaempferol), chebulinic/chebulagic acids, and mucilaginous polysaccharides.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Amla is filed as a **flagship Rejuvenation / Rasayana-phase agent** — the archetype of a cooling antioxidant tonic used in the consolidation arc after clearing and building work. Its cross-tradition role is coherent: cardiometabolic support (lipids, glycaemia), antioxidant and anti-inflammatory consolidation, digestive toning, and general restorative use. It is one of the few entries in Volume I where daily tonic-style use is both traditionally sanctioned and clinically supported.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, amla has a **stronger human clinical evidence base than most botanicals in this volume**, concentrated on cardiometabolic endpoints.

- Setayesh et al. (2023, *Diabetes Metab Syndr*) — a systematic review and meta-analysis of five RCTs — found amla supplementation significantly reduced C-reactive protein (p = 0.002), fasting blood glucose (p < 0.001), LDL-cholesterol (p < 0.001), total cholesterol (p < 0.001), and triglycerides (p < 0.001), and significantly raised HDL-cholesterol (p < 0.001). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dsx.2023.102729)
- Usharani et al. (2019, *BMC Complement Altern Med*) — a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n = 59) in metabolic syndrome — reported that standardised *Phyllanthus emblica* extract at 250 mg and 500 mg twice daily significantly improved endothelial function, oxidative-stress biomarkers, hsCRP, and the full lipid panel over 12 weeks, with the 500 mg dose most effective and no discontinuations for adverse events. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-019-2509-5)
- Martinez et al. (2025, *Nutrients*) — a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n = 166 enrolled) — found *Phyllanthus emblica* extract, alone or with chromium and shilajit, alongside exercise and diet, showed evidence of improved insulin sensitivity, blood lipids, and vascular measures in adults with metabolic-syndrome risk factors. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17122042)
- Manjunatha et al. (2001, *Indian J Physiol Pharmacol*; PMID 11211574 — no DOI registered in PubMed) — an early randomised clinical trial showed amla-based Chyawanprash reduced postprandial glycaemia and lowered LDL and the LDL/HDL ratio more than vitamin C alone.

**Evidence grade: MODERATE–STRONG (human RCT + meta-analytic).** Multiple randomised controlled trials and a meta-analysis converge on lipid-lowering, glucose-lowering, and anti-inflammatory effects in metabolic populations. Trials are generally small and heterogeneous, so effect sizes should be read as promising rather than definitive — but this is one of the better-evidenced botanicals in the codex.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Ayurveda:** *Rasa* — five tastes, sour (*amla*) predominant, salt absent; *Guna* — light (*laghu*), dry (*ruksha*); *Virya* — cooling (*shita*); *Vipaka* — sweet (*madhura*). Tridoshic; premier *rasayana*.
- **TCM (*Yu Gan Zi*):** Sour, sweet, astringent, cool. Clears heat, generates fluids, benefits throat and lung, harmonises the middle.
- **Galenic / Unani (*Amlaj*):** Cold and dry; astringent tonic — cardiac, cerebral, digestive, and hair/scalp tonic.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The emblic myrobalan is not a native Greek drug and does not appear in *De Materia Medica*; it entered the Greco-Arabic-European stream later through the myrobalan trade, as the entry itself records — so the tag marks an eastward-sourced absorption into the Dioscoretic-Galenic pharmacy, not a Dioscoridean entry. Dioscorides carries no subtle-body system in any case; the tradition offers a cultivator only its discipline of attention — the sourcing and assay of an imported astringent fruit. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** As *Amlaj*, amla is a well-established Unani drug: a cold, dry astringent tonic (*muqawwi*) of the heart, brain, viscera and scalp that checks excessive discharge. The strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus, the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture across the whole regimen, is a frame of the constitution and is not the same as this substance's recorded tonic indications; no cultivation status is sourced beyond that therapeutic tonic role, and no *mufarrih* constituent claim is made. **(b) framed:** attested as a Unani tonic, no cultivation status sourced.

**Chinese — Bencao (SBJ).** In Chinese medicine amla is *Yu Gan Zi* 余甘子, the "aftersweet fruit," classed as sour, sweet, astringent and cool — clearing heat, generating fluids, benefiting the throat and lung, and harmonising the middle. It is not, however, a drug of the classical Shennong stratum: it enters Chinese materia medica through later, southern- and Tibetan-transmitted compilations, and no *san pin* 三品 grade is recorded for it in the sources retrieved. It therefore carries a therapeutic, not a cultivation, status within *yang sheng* 養生. **(b) framed**, with the attestation belonging to the later Chinese stratum rather than the Shennong classic.

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Amalaki* is a premier *rasayana* — the formal Ayurvedic rejuvenation discipline — named in the classical corpus, standing as the base of *Chyavanaprāśa* and as one of the three fruits of *Triphalā*, and given the rare profile of a cooling drug said to pacify all three doshas. Its *rasa* carries five of the six tastes with sour dominant, *virya* cooling, *vipaka* sweet. This is a genuine substance-specific cultivation status, not a therapeutic indication alone: rejuvenation taken to extend and restore the constitution. **(a) attested** — the *rasayana* standing of *Amalaki* is recorded in the classical Ayurvedic corpus (Charaka and Sushruta Saṃhitās; Meulenbeld, *A History of Indian Medical Literature*).

**Practitioner's note.** This is one of the few entries where a cultivator has an attested, daily, long-arc use: in the Ayurvedic scheme amla is a *rasayana* fruit taken — as *churna*, as *Chyavanaprāśa*, or in *Triphalā* — for rejuvenative consolidation, and the modern cardiometabolic trials happen to converge on the same restorative role. Outside Ayurveda the other traditions give it only a cooling-astringent tonic frame with no cultivation grade. A cultivator uses it in the consolidation arc, coordinating with a clinician where anticoagulant or glucose-lowering drugs are in play.

### Contraindications

- **Astringent and cooling** — large or prolonged doses may aggravate cold/deficient digestive patterns or contribute to dryness/constipation in susceptible individuals.
- **Antiplatelet / glucose-lowering interaction potential:** given demonstrated effects on lipids, platelet aggregation, and blood glucose, use caution with anticoagulant/antiplatelet drugs and with hypoglycaemic agents — monitor and coordinate with a clinician; consider withholding before surgery.
- **Acid sensitivity:** the high vitamin-C and organic-acid content may aggravate hyperacidity or reflux in some when taken on an empty stomach.
- Pregnancy and lactation: culinary amounts are traditional food use; concentrated therapeutic extracts should be used only with professional guidance [USER: verify].
- Compound products (Chyawanprash, Triphala) contain additional herbs with their own cautions — assess the whole formula, not amla alone.
