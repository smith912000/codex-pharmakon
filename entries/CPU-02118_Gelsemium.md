# Gelsemium — 钩吻 *gou wen*, "Hook Lip" (also 野葛 *ye ge*, "Wild Kudzu"; 断肠草 *duan chang cao*, "gut-breaking grass")

> **TOXICITY CAUTION. This plant is potentially lethal.** *Gelsemium elegans* is among the most poisonous plants of China and has caused deaths from very small amounts, including from mistaken identity. This entry is a historical record of what one early Chinese text says. It is not a recommendation, not a preparation guide, and not a basis for any use. Nothing here should be gathered, handled, prepared or taken.

## *Gelsemium elegans* (Gardner & Champ.) Benth., heartbreak grass, the toxic woody climber of southern China; the identification is the modern standard one for 钩吻 and is of high confidence for the plant, though Shen Kuo (quoted by Sun Xingyan) doubted that the drug in the old materia medica was this plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** A search of the other traditions' texts in `Sources/` for *Gelsemium* found nothing. The entry rests on one tradition, in Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction and Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 translation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from Yang under the name *Herba Gelsemii Elegantis*; this entry replaces it.

**The name line is missing in the file.** Sun's file prints no heading 钩吻 for this entry: the line above the 味 paragraph is only "（《御览》作 ）" (a note that the *Yulan* has a variant, with the character lost). The identification of the entry as 钩吻 comes from the job and is confirmed by Sun's own 案 and Wu Pu line below, which name 钩吻 and 野葛. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), the Shennong text proper.** The entry sits in the lower classic (下经). Sun's 味 paragraph is split across two lines and has a lost character: "味辛，温。主金创乳[…]，中恶风，咳逆上气，水肿，杀鬼注（旧作疰，《御览》作注，是）蛊毒。一名野葛。生山谷。" ("Acrid, warm. It governs incised wounds, breast [?] [a character is missing; Yang reads 'breast-feeding tetany'], being struck by malign wind, cough with counterflow and rising qi, water swelling; it kills ghost-influx [鬼注] and *gu* poison. Another name: Wild Kudzu [野葛]. It grows in mountains and valleys.") Sun's parenthesis records that the old text wrote 疰 and the *Yulan* wrote 注, which Sun prefers. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 钩吻 *gouwen*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 3199-3201). The paragraph does not say that the plant is poisonous.

**Second witness, Yang 1998 (attributed to Yang).** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, lines 3304-3306 and 3340) reads: "acrid and warm. It mainly treats incised wounds, breast-feeding tetany, malign wind stroke/OS cough and counterflow qi ascent, and water swelling. It kills demonic influx and *gu* toxins. Its other name is Ye Ge (Wild Kudzu)." The "/OS" is an OCR fragment in the Yang file. Yang's note 204 (line 3332) opens: "Gou Wen (Herba Gelsemii Elegantis) is fatally poisonous. However, its true identity is controversial. Its Chinese name suggests that once any animal has a bite of it, it will die immediately." That is Yang's commentary and not the Shennong text.

**Wu Pu layer (3rd century, quoted by Sun).** Line 3203 reads (the first characters are damaged): "秦，钩肠，一名毒根，一名野葛。神农：辛；雷公：有毒，杀人。生南越山，或益州，叶如葛，赤茎大如箭、方，根黄。或生会稽东治，正月采。" ("…, hook-intestine [钩肠]; other names 'poison root' [毒根] and 'wild kudzu'. Shennong: acrid; Lei Gong: toxic, *kills people*. It grows in the mountains of southern Yue or Yizhou; the leaf like kudzu, the stem red, as thick as an arrow, square, the root yellow. Or it grows at Dongzhi in Kuaiji; gathered in the first month."). Here the flavour is attributed to Shennong, and the explicit statement "kills people" to Lei Gong, a different authority.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later additions, quoted by Sun).** Line 3205: "生傅高山及会稽东野" ("It grows on Mount Fugao and in the eastern wilds of Kuaiji").

**Sun's own note (案).** Lines 3207-3209 cite the *Guangya* ("莨 is 钩吻"), the *Huainanzi* (a viper bite is cured by applying 和堇; Gao You: "和堇 is 野葛, a poison"), the *Bowuzhi*, Tao Hongjing ("some say 钩吻 is 毛莨"), and Shen Kuo's *Bu bitan*: "the people of Min call it 吻莽, also 野葛; the people of Lingnan call it 胡蔓; commonly it is called 断肠草. This herb is the most poisonous thing among people and is not used in medicine. I fear that what the bencao puts forth is a different thing and not this 钩吻." (Sun's text of Shen Kuo, line 3209.) The *Bowuzhi* quotation includes a boiled remedy for the poison; it is not repeated here and must not be relied on. This note matters: the old materia medica listed 钩吻 as a drug with indications, while the later writer holds that the plant of that name was never medicine.

**Name overlap.** The alias 莨 appears here (*Guangya*, line 3207) and also in Wu Pu's alias list for 乌头 (line 3053 of the Sun file), so the two poisonous drugs share a name in the early record. The Codex notes this and does not resolve it.

**Identity.** The plant's description in Wu Pu (leaves like kudzu, red square stem, yellow root) and the later names 断肠草 and 胡蔓 are the modern grounds for *Gelsemium elegans*. Shen Kuo's doubt is the early counter-evidence, and Yang notes that the identity "is controversial". Confidence: high for the plant behind the name; moderate for whether the Shennong monograph meant it. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. As a general fact, *Gelsemium elegans* contains indole alkaloids (including gelsemine, koumine and gelsenicine) that affect the nervous system and respiration; all parts of the plant are poisonous, and poisoning has caused deaths. This is a general statement and not drawn from the Codex's sources. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The Codex has no use case for this plant. The Shennong text lists an acrid, warm drug for incised wounds, a tetanus-like condition in nursing women, malign wind, cough and water swelling, and a power to kill *gu* poison. These are the text's terms; the Codex does not translate "ghost influx" or "*gu* poison" into modern diagnoses. There is no protocol. The plant must not be used.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text proper gives no preparation. Wu Pu gives the first month for gathering (line 3203). No dose is given in the sources retrieved, and none is supplied. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

*Gelsemium elegans* poisoning is documented in clinical toxicology, with vomiting, dizziness, muscle weakness, respiratory failure and death in severe cases; this is a general fact. No clinical studies of therapeutic use were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ.** The Shennong text prints "味辛，温" (acrid, warm). Wu Pu attributes "acrid" to Shennong and "toxic, kills people" to Lei Gong (line 3203). Sun's appendix of drug relations (附：诸药制使, from line 4191; a later compilation) lists "钩吻 半夏为使；恶黄芩" (line 4435; "[Pinellia] as its envoy; averse to [Scutellaria]"); it is not the Shennong text proper.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic (下经), whose preface (line 2935) says lower drugs "treat disease to correspond with the earth; many are toxic and cannot be taken long; for those who wish to remove cold and heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings and cure illness, take the lower classic". The entry has no 久服 clause. The class is the one the preface bars from long use; the grade is a warning and not a status. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 钩吻 *gouwen*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3199)

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this plant. It is a lower-grade drug in the text's own scheme and a lethal poison in the commentary, and its identity as medicine was doubted even by a Song writer. The honest practice is to read the entry as a record and keep away from the plant.

### Contraindications

**Potentially lethal. Do not use.** Wu Pu records that Lei Gong called it "toxic, kills people" (line 3203); the later commentary calls it "the most poisonous thing among people" (Shen Kuo, line 3209) and Yang calls it "fatally poisonous" (note 204). All parts of the plant are poisonous. Poisoning is a medical emergency: contact emergency services at once. The plant must not be used in pregnancy, by children, or in any preparation, and the early record itself doubts which plant the drug name meant. Legal restrictions apply in some places.
