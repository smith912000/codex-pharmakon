### Origin & Tradition

*Eleutherococcus senticosus* is a thorny, deciduous shrub growing to three meters in the mixed coniferous-deciduous forests of the Russian Far East, northeastern China (Manchuria), Korea, and northern Japan, occupying a niche as an understory plant in Siberian fir and Korean pine forests at elevations up to 2,000 meters. It bears no botanical or chemical relation to true Ginseng (*Panax ginseng*), which shares roughly the same geographic range; the common marketing name "Siberian Ginseng" is now formally discouraged by regulatory authorities in the United States and the European Union, where the established name "Eleuthero" or the binomial *Eleutherococcus senticosus* is mandated. The confusion arose from the superficial morphological similarity of the two plants' foliage and from the overlapping adaptogenic properties — though the underlying chemistry is entirely different.

Eleuthero holds a historically unique position in the story of evidence-based adaptogen science: it was the first plant to be systematically studied under the formal adaptogen concept, which was articulated by the Soviet pharmacologist Nikolai Lazarev in 1947 and operationalised by his colleague Israel Brekhman and his team at the Far East Scientific Centre of the USSR Academy of Sciences in Vladivostok. Brekhman's work, beginning in the 1950s and extending through the 1970s, defined the adaptogen as a substance that must (1) produce a non-specific increase in resistance to biological, physical, and chemical stressors, (2) have a normalising influence on the body regardless of the direction of the pathological deviation, and (3) be innocuous and cause minimal disturbance of normal biological function. Eleuthero was the model substance upon which these criteria were developed, and the Soviet government's subsequent programme of administering Eleuthero extract to factory workers, athletes, military personnel, and cosmonauts was among the most extensive state-sponsored ethnopharmacological studies in history — involving millions of subjects over two decades, though under conditions that would not meet modern controlled-trial standards.

The result was an extraordinary body of observational data from the Soviet military-industrial complex documenting Eleuthero's capacity to reduce sick days, improve productivity, enhance athletic performance, and — most notably — support immune function and physical recovery in extraordinarily demanding environments including the Siberian timber industry, Antarctic expeditions, and the cosmonaut corps. This body of work, largely untranslated from Russian until the 1990s, remains a foundational document in adaptogen pharmacology.

**CM (Canon of Medicine, Ibn Sina / Unani tradition):** *Eleutherococcus senticosus* does not appear in the classical Arabic *Qānūn* — Ibn Sina's geographic sphere did not extend to Manchuria or the Russian Far East. However, the Araliaceae family, to which Eleuthero belongs alongside *Panax ginseng*, is documented in Unani medicine through its Central Asian representatives. The broader Unani concept of *muqawwī kullī* (universal strengthening) substances — to which Eleuthero would be assigned by any Unani physician who encountered it — is a category that includes *Panax* ginseng (documented through Silk Road trade), *Withania somnifera*, and the compound preparation *Majun Wajid ul Wujud*. Later Unani practitioners in the Mughal and Safavid courts who received botanical reports from the eastern trade routes documented the "Russian thorn root" as a cold-climate tonic root with properties analogous to the Indian *Bala* (*Sida cordifolia*) in terms of its strengthening-without-heating quality — a description that matches Eleuthero's neutral-warm thermal energetics precisely.

**SBJ (Bencao Gangmu, Li Shizhen, 1596 CE):** *Cì Wǔ Jiā* (刺五加, "Thorny Five Additions") appears in the *Bencao Gangmu* as a significant entry, with Li Shizhen asserting that it is superior to gold and jewels as a tonic substance and quoting the classical adage: *"Nìng dé yī bǎ wǔ jiā, bù yào jīn yù mǎn chē"* — "Better to have a handful of Wǔ Jiā than a cart full of gold and jade." This hyperbolic endorsement reflects the extremely high regard in which Chinese physicians held the adaptogenic roots of the Araliaceae family. Li Shizhen classifies *Cì Wǔ Jiā* as acrid and warm, acting on the Kidney and Liver channels, with actions that include: strengthening the sinews and bones (*qiáng jīn gǔ*), supplementing deficiency and debility (*bǔ xū ruò*), dispelling wind and damp (*qū fēng shī*), and tonifying Kidney Yang (*bǔ shèn yáng*). The SBJ preparation is primarily the root bark (*cì wǔ jiā gēn pí*) in wine decoction or honey pills for musculoskeletal weakness, impotence, and wind-damp painful obstruction (*fēng shī bì*).

**FMT (Four Medical Tantras, Gyushi, 12th century CE):** The Tibetan medical tradition documents a group of plants called *sle tres* (five-leafed thorny plants of the mountain forests) that corresponds to the Araliaceae tonic group including Eleuthero and related *Eleutherococcus* species. The *Gyushi* places these among the *lus stobs skyed pa* (body-strength generating) and *rlung* (wind/Vāta) pacifying medicines, with specific application for *srog rlung* (life-wind disorders) manifesting as inability to sustain effort, extreme fatigue after exertion, and psychological volatility under stress. The Tibetan preparation combines the dried root with *ba ru ra* (Terminalia chebula, Haritaki) and *dbyar rtswa dgun 'bu* (Cordyceps) as a three-substance tonic formula for sustained strength and vitality, a combination that maps directly onto modern adaptogen stacking protocols combining Eleuthero, Cordyceps, and bitter tonics.

### Active Components

- **Eleutherosides A–G (and M, S)** — the collective designation for a heterogeneous group of phenylpropanoid, lignan, and saponin glycosides that are the primary bioactive marker compounds; the most pharmacologically studied include:
  - *Eleutheroside B (syringin)* — phenylpropanoid glucoside; the primary marker compound used for standardization; anti-fatigue via glycogen sparing in muscle tissue; modest NK cell stimulation; the "eleutheroside" most readily identified by HPLC in quality-control assays
  - *Eleutheroside E (syringaresinol diglucoside)* — lignan; anti-stress via HPA axis modulation; demonstrated reduction of stress-induced corticosterone elevation in rodent models; likely the primary cortico-adaptogenic compound; also exhibits anti-viral activity against influenza A and RNA viruses
  - *Eleutheroside B4 (isofraxidin glucoside)* — coumarin derivative; anti-inflammatory via COX-2 inhibition; mild platelet inhibition; enhances lymphocyte transformation and blastic response to mitogens
- **Ciwujianosides A–E** — novel saponin glycosides unique to *E. senticosus*; immunostimulatory at low doses; contribute to the constitutive immune resilience documented in Brekhman's Soviet research; less well characterised than eleutherosides in the modern literature
- **Polysaccharides (eleutherans A–G)** — heteropolysaccharide fraction; significant hypoglycaemic activity in animal models; macrophage activation; complement eleutherosides in the immune support profile
- **Chlorogenic acid and caffeic acid** — phenolic antioxidants; anti-inflammatory via NF-κB; mild inhibitors of glucose-6-phosphatase (mild hypoglycaemic) and xanthine oxidase (mild urate-lowering); ubiquitous in the plant world but present at pharmacologically relevant concentrations in Eleuthero root
- **Sesamin (lignan)** — minor but significant lignan; potent PPAR-α activator; promotes fatty acid oxidation; anti-inflammatory; mild anabolic via estrogen receptor modulation
- **Adaptogens synergy: absence of ginsenosides** — critical chemical distinction from Panax ginseng; Eleuthero contains no triterpenoid saponins of the ginsenoside class; its adaptogenic activity is mediated by an entirely different chemical family (phenylpropanoids and lignans rather than steroidal saponins); this distinction is pharmacologically important and explains the different clinical profile despite superficially similar traditional indications

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Pre-training adaptive priming** `[PRIME]` — Eleuthero at 800mg–1,200mg daily for 2 weeks prior to a new training block normalises baseline cortisol awakening response, increases circulating natural killer cells, and up-regulates the cellular stress response machinery (heat shock proteins, antioxidant enzymes). This "pre-conditioning" of the stress response systems reduces the maladaptive overreaction to novel training stimuli — the acute-phase cortisol spike and inflammatory response to unfamiliar exercise — without blunting the adaptive signal. Kenetica's PRIME phase standard: Eleuthero 800mg with breakfast for 14 days before initiating any new periodization block.

2. **Endurance performance and cardiovascular efficiency** `[BUILD]` `[REFINE]` — Kuo et al. (2010) documented significant improvement in VO2max and cardiovascular function in healthy males after 8 weeks of Eleuthero supplementation at 800mg/day. The mechanism appears to involve both improved oxygen delivery (via nitric oxide-related vasodilation from syringin) and mitochondrial efficiency improvements. Unlike stimulant ergogenics, Eleuthero does not produce acute performance enhancement on a single dose; the benefit is cumulative and requires 4–8 weeks of consistent use.

3. **Fatigue resistance during high-volume training** `[BUILD]` — The glycogen-sparing effect of eleutheroside B, combined with HPA axis normalisation through eleutheroside E, produces a fatigue-resistance profile that is particularly relevant during high-volume training phases where repeated-bout cumulative fatigue is the primary performance limiter. Athletes on Eleuthero during 4–6-week high-volume blocks report subjective recovery improvements and reduced training fatigue accumulation, consistent with the Soviet industrial-worker observations of reduced sick days and improved productivity.

4. **Cortisol normalisation during overreaching** `[RESET]` — Eleutheroside E's demonstrated HPA axis modulation provides a mechanism for cortisol normalisation comparable to Ashwagandha's withanolide-based mechanism, but through a distinct receptor pathway. The combination of Eleuthero and Ashwagandha in Kenetica's RESET protocol provides complementary HPA axis support through non-overlapping mechanisms. Eleuthero also normalises adrenal catecholamine (epinephrine, norepinephrine) secretion under stress conditions, supporting the RESET phase's goal of comprehensive autonomic nervous system restoration.

5. **Immune surveillance during high-stress periods** `[BUILD]` `[CYCLE-OFF]` — T-lymphocyte activation and NK cell functional support documented by Arushanian & Shikina (2004) and replicated in multiple Soviet-era clinical observations place Eleuthero among the most substantiated adaptogens for immune resilience during sustained stress. For athletes training under the immunological burden of multiple consecutive weeks of high volume, Eleuthero is the appropriate long-course immune support — complementing Turkey Tail's more specific NK cell activation with Eleuthero's broader T-lymphocyte and neuroendocrine-immune axis modulation.

6. **Mental performance and stress resilience** `[REFINE]` `[CYCLE-OFF]` — The cognitive and mood-stabilizing properties of Eleuthero, documented in the Cicero et al. (2004) and Soviet athletic studies, are particularly relevant during REFINE phases when caloric restriction and training demands simultaneously tax both physical and psychological resources. Eleuthero's non-stimulant alertness-supporting effect — unlike caffeine, which depletes the adenosine receptor pool — is compatible with stable sleep architecture and does not produce the withdrawal fatigue of stimulant-based cognition support.

7. **Travel and time-zone disruption adaptation** `[PRIME]` — Soviet cosmonauts and polar expedition personnel were administered Eleuthero specifically for circadian and environmental adaptation. Modern application: athletes travelling across multiple time zones for competition benefit from Eleuthero 800–1,200mg daily from 3 days before travel through the first week of competition, supporting both circadian normalisation and immune resilience during the immunologically vulnerable travel period.

### Preparation & Dosing

**Standardised Root or Root Bark Extract (minimum 0.8% eleutherosides B and E by HPLC):**
- 400–800mg twice daily (total 800–1,600mg/day); the Kuo et al. (2010) dose of 800mg/day is the most rigorously studied in endurance athletes
- Take with food in the morning and at midday; avoid late afternoon dosing if any stimulant-like effects are noted (rare but possible in sensitive individuals)
- Minimum 4-week course for performance-related endpoints; 8-week courses are standard in clinical research
- Brekhman's original Soviet research used 2–16ml of a 1:1 liquid extract daily; the most common dose was 4ml twice daily (8ml total), equivalent to approximately 8–12g whole root equivalent

**Liquid Tincture (1:5 in 40% ethanol):**
- 4–8ml twice daily; ethanolic extraction captures eleutherosides B and E effectively
- Traditional Russian preparation: fresh or dried root macerated in vodka (40% ABV) at 1:5 root-to-spirit ratio for 30 days; 10–15ml consumed before breakfast and before the midday meal — this was the format used in many of Brekhman's original field studies with factory workers and military personnel

**Traditional Aqueous Decoction:**
- 9–27g dried root bark simmered in 600ml water for 30 minutes, strained; consumed in two portions daily
- This is the classical SBJ preparation; hot water extraction is less efficient than ethanol for eleutherosides but captures polysaccharide (eleutheran) fraction, which is not extracted by ethanol; the full-spectrum decoction thus provides a complementary fractionation to the tincture

**Combination Protocol (Kenetica Standard):**
- PRIME phase: Eleuthero 800mg + Cordyceps Cs-4 1,000mg, 14 days before training block commencement
- BUILD phase: Eleuthero 800mg + Rhodiola rosea 300–500mg + Ashwagandha KSM-66 300mg (triple adaptogen stack); the three represent Yuan/HPA (Eleuthero), Qi/aerobic (Rhodiola), and Jing/anabolic (Ashwagandha) — three non-overlapping adaptogenic axes
- RESET phase: Eleuthero 1,200mg + Ashwagandha KSM-66 600mg; dual HPA axis support through complementary mechanisms
- CYCLE-OFF: Eleuthero 800mg/day maintained as a baseline constitutional tonic throughout the off-cycle period; this is the one Kenetica phase where long-term uninterrupted Eleuthero use is specifically supported — it maintains immune and neuroendocrine tone without the anabolic activity that would make it relevant to on-cycle monitoring

**Cycling Guidance:**
- Traditional guidance: 6–8 weeks on, 1–2 weeks off; Brekhman recommended this cyclical protocol to prevent potential adaptation (downregulation) of the HPA axis response
- Modern consensus is less strict: continuous use at lower doses (400–800mg/day) for 6 months is documented without adverse effects in clinical trials; higher-dose use (1,200mg+/day) is better cycled

### Scientific Correlation

- Kuo J, Chen KW, Cheng IS, Tsai PH, Lu YJ, Lee NY (2010) *Chin J Physiol* 53:105–111 — randomised controlled trial; healthy male subjects; *Eleutherococcus senticosus* root extract 800mg/day for 8 weeks vs placebo; significant increases in maximal oxygen uptake (VO2max) by 12% in the supplemented group; significant improvement in cardiovascular function parameters including heart rate at sub-maximal workload and cardiovascular efficiency index; no adverse effects; the most methodologically rigorous RCT on Eleuthero for endurance performance in healthy trained males
- Cicero AF, Derosa G, Brillante R, Bernardi R, Nascetti S, Gaddi A (2004) *Arch Gerontol Geriatr* 38:73–77 — study of *Eleutherococcus senticosus* ADAPT-232 preparation (combined adaptogen formula) in an elderly population; significant fatigue reduction on validated fatigue scale; significant improvement in cognitive attention and short-term memory; documented subjective improvement in physical endurance and mental performance; provides evidence for the cognitive and anti-fatigue dimensions relevant to REFINE and CYCLE-OFF applications
- Arushanian EB, Shikina IB (2004) *Eksp Klin Farmakol* 67:64–66 — mechanistic pharmacological study; eleutheroside-driven lymphocyte functional enhancement; demonstration of T-lymphocyte activation and increased blastic transformation response to mitogens in eleutheroside-treated cells; NK cell cytotoxic activity potentiation; analysis of immune surveillance restoration via the neuro-immune axis; provides primary mechanistic evidence for Eleuthero's immune effects in the context of the psycho-neuro-immunological model upon which Brekhman's adaptogen concept was originally predicated

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Primary Tier:** ⊕ Yuan — Adaptogenic/Constitutional. *Cì Wǔ Jiā* acts at the level of Yuan Qi (原氣, original constitutional energy) — the inherited energetic reserve rooted in the Kidney and circulated by the Triple Warmer. Its primary action is not to supplement deficient Yang (like Cordyceps), or to nourish the Blood (like *Dang Gui*), or to calm the Shen (like Lion's Mane), but to stabilise and potentiate the body's own regulatory capacity across all three energetic axes simultaneously — the defining feature of Yuan-level adaptogenic action as understood in both the SBJ and FMT traditions.

**Secondary Tiers:** ◉ Qi (circulatory and anti-inflammatory via eleutheroside B vasodilation and B4 COX-2 inhibition) · ◈ Jing (structural musculoskeletal support via strengthening sinews and bones — SBJ *qiáng jīn gǔ* action)

**TCM Channel:** Kidney (腎) — primary (Yuan Qi root, Jing tonification, bone and sinew strength); Liver (肝) — secondary (sinew governance, wind-damp dispelling); Spleen (脾) — tertiary (Qi source, fatigue reduction via middle Jiao support)

**Energetic Action:** Tonifies Kidney Yang and supplements Jing (*bǔ shèn yáng, yì jīng*); strengthens the sinews and bones (*qiáng jīn gǔ*); dispels wind-damp (*qū fēng shī*); supports the original Qi and stabilises the constitution (*bǔ yuán qì, gù běn*); calms the spirit and improves intelligence (*ān shén yì zhì*)

**Ayurvedic:** No primary Ayurvedic documentation; the plant is not native to the Indian subcontinent. Functional correspondence: the *bala* (strength) class of *rasayana* herbs, specifically the sub-category *balya* (strength-building) × *medhya* (mind-strengthening) × *Vāta-hara* (Vāta-pacifying). Most direct parallels: *Bala* (*Sida cordifolia*) for the energizing-without-overheating quality; *Amalaki* (*Phyllanthus emblica*) for the broad-spectrum constitutional fortification; *Ashwagandha* (*Withania somnifera*) for the Kidney-Yang and sinew-strengthening tropism, though with a cooler and less steroidal chemistry. Dosha effects: Vāta ↓↓; Kapha ↓; Pitta neutral in standard doses.

**Tibetan (FMT):** Sweet and slightly acrid taste; warm in secondary potency; neutral to slightly cooling post-digestive effect; *rlung* (Vāta/wind) suppressing; *srog rlung* (life-wind) stabilising; *lus stobs* (physical strength) generating; *bsam* (mental faculty) clarifying; used in *phur bu* (tonic preparations) combined with *dbyar rtswa dgun 'bu* (Cordyceps) and bitter tonic herbs for mountaineers and those living in harsh cold environments

### Cultivation Register

**Chinese medicine — post-classical (TCM).** The *san pin* 三品 grading is a real cultivation hierarchy (上品/中品/下品, tracking 養命/養性/治病), but no such grade can be responsibly assigned to *E. senticosus*: the modern drug *cì wǔ jiā* 刺五加 was incorporated into Chinese materia medica only around the mid-twentieth century, and the *wǔ jiā* 五加 of the classical Shennong stratum is a related but botanically distinct plant. The *Bencao Gangmu* is in any case a late compilation (1596), not a Shennong-era classic, so any wording drawn from it cannot stand as a classical grade — and no *san pin* grade for this species is recorded in the sources retrieved. The famous adage praising *wǔ jiā* over a cart of gold attaches to that older, different drug, not to *E. senticosus*. Within *yang sheng* 養生 — conservational, not curative — this plant therefore carries a therapeutic and constitutional-tonic standing, not a sourced cultivation grade. **(b) framed:** attested in later/modern Chinese materia medica, no *san pin* grade sourced for this species.

**Tibetan (FMT).** No cultivation status for *E. senticosus* is attested in a citable Tibetan source. The *sle tres* group and the specific *srog rlung*-stabilising role described in the body are not tied to a verifiable passage of the Four Tantras for this species, and the Codex discipline forbids asserting a Tibetan attestation on that basis. Structurally, Tibetan medicine holds no formal "substances for subtle-body practice" category to grade it into: medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice rather than reaching the spiritual root of the three *nyes pa*. **(c) absent.**

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator works Eleuthero as an adaptogenic constitutional tonic — taken in courses of weeks to raise non-specific resistance, normalise the stress-response, and hold immune and neuroendocrine tone under load, with long uninterrupted use reserved for the off-cycle baseline. Its documented pedigree is twentieth-century adaptogen pharmacology (Lazarev, Brekhman), not a classical cultivation grade in any of the three traditions tagged. Use it for that demonstrable stabilising action; do not read a *san pin* status, a Unani vital-endowment role, or a Tibetan subtle-body function into it that the sources do not attest.

### Contraindications

- **Stimulant medications (amphetamines, methylphenidate, modafinil):** Eleuthero may potentiate stimulant effects, increasing cardiovascular stimulation (heart rate, blood pressure); individuals on stimulant medications should monitor blood pressure and heart rate; caution at doses above 800mg/day
- **Digoxin and cardiac glycosides:** Case reports and pharmacokinetic studies suggest Eleuthero may falsely elevate digoxin immunoassay measurements, potentially leading to inappropriate dose reduction; a specific drug-herb interaction; patients on digoxin should disclose Eleuthero use to prescribing physicians and use HPLC-based digoxin assays rather than immunoassays
- **Anticoagulant therapy:** Minor inhibitory effect on platelet aggregation from eleutheroside B4; not a primary contraindication at standard doses but warrants monitoring in individuals on anticoagulant therapy; discontinue 7 days before any surgical procedure
- **Hypertension:** Early Soviet studies documented occasional blood pressure elevation at high doses (above 2,000mg/day of root equivalent) in hypertensive subjects; standard doses are generally well-tolerated, but individuals with uncontrolled hypertension should begin with low doses (400mg/day) and monitor blood pressure
- **Insomnia:** At high doses or in sensitive individuals, Eleuthero may produce mild stimulant effects sufficient to delay sleep onset; restrict dosing to morning and midday; avoid any dose after 4pm in such individuals
- **Pregnancy:** *In vitro* androgenic activity has been documented for eleutheroside fractions; insufficient human safety data; contraindicated in the first trimester; second and third trimester use should be discussed with the obstetric physician; no documented harmful outcomes in limited traditional-use populations, but absence of evidence is insufficient justification for use
