# Green Hellebore

## *Helleborus viridis* L. | Family: Ranunculaceae

**Tradition Tags:** DMM, CM
**Tags (previous line, retained for reference):** AT, DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Cold–Dry; violently downward-purging, with a hot local counter-irritant surface action

**Training Phase:** **NONE. This plant has no training phase. It is documented, not used.**

---

> # ☠️ DEADLY — DO NOT USE. NO DOSE IS GIVEN IN THIS ENTRY BECAUSE NO SAFE DOSE EXISTS.
>
> *Helleborus viridis* contains **cardiac glycosides of the bufadienolide class (hellebrin, deglucohellebrin, hellebrigenin glycosides)** and the **protoanemonin** irritant system. The pharmacology is: violent vomiting and purgation, mucosal and skin ulceration, bradycardia progressing to lethal arrhythmia, and death. The therapeutic window that the ancients believed they were working inside **does not exist** — and the classical literature itself records fatalities from its own remedy. This entry exists to explain a doctrine and to bury it. **Nothing in it is a protocol. Do not prepare this plant. Do not ingest any part of it. Do not handle it without gloves.** If ingestion is suspected: this is a cardiac-glycoside emergency — contact emergency services / poison control immediately. Do not induce vomiting; the plant is already doing that, and the vomiting is not the danger.

### Origin & Tradition

The hellebores are the drug of Anticyra, and Anticyra is the place you sent the mad. *"Naviget Anticyram"* — let him sail to Anticyra — was the Roman idiom for *he is insane*, precisely because the Phocian town's trade was hellebore and hellebore was what you gave a madman. No plant in the European corpus carries a heavier psychiatric doctrine, and none has a worse ratio of doctrinal grandeur to pharmacological reality. The hellebores were the ancient world's answer to madness. What they actually were was a class of cardiotoxic emetic-cathartics that killed a proportion of the people they were given to, and the ancients knew it, wrote it down, and kept prescribing.

**Greco-Roman Classical Tradition (AT / DMM) — the Anticyra doctrine.** Ancient practice divided the drug into **white hellebore** (*Veratrum album*, Melanthiaceae — a completely different plant, and the source of much of the surviving confusion) and **black hellebore** (the *Helleborus* group, Ranunculaceae, to which *H. viridis* belongs alongside *H. niger* and *H. foetidus*). *Helleborus viridis* — **green hellebore**, **bear's-foot**, from the shape of the divided leaf — sits in the black-hellebore family of use.

The classical indication is threefold and all three follow from one theory:
1. **Madness and melancholy** — the headline use, and the reason for Anticyra.
2. **A drastic purge** — the mechanism by which (1) was supposed to be achieved.
3. **Dropsy, and the moving of stubborn obstructions** — the same drastic action pointed at a different accumulation.

The **doctrine** is pure Hippocratic humoralism executed with total consistency. Melancholy — *melas kholé*, **black bile** — is a *material substance* that has accumulated in the body and darkened the mind. Madness is not a disorder of thought; it is a *superfluity of a fluid*. And the treatment of a superfluity is *evacuation*. Black hellebore was believed to be the specific purgative of the black humour — a drug with an elective affinity for black bile, drawing it downward and expelling it through the bowel. Cure the body of the substance and the mind returns.

The tradition took its own risk seriously in a way that modern romanticisers of it do not. Classical practice hedged hellebore with elaborate preparatory regimen, dose graduation, and explicit warnings; the ancients' own writings record **fatal doses and deaths**, and hellebore's danger was proverbial, not discovered later [USER: verify the specific classical passages and their attributions — Hippocratic corpus, Theophrastus, Dioscorides, Pliny, Celsus — before citing any of them as a quotation or a specific claim].

**Mediaeval and Early-Modern European Herbal Tradition (DMM).** The Anticyra doctrine survives, attenuated, into the herbals: green hellebore root as a **purge, an emmenagogue, a vermifuge**, and for **dropsy** — the melancholy indication increasingly ornamental as a citation of the ancients rather than a live practice. The mediaeval contribution is the **counter-irritant** use: **the root inserted into an incision or a seton** in the skin or dewlap, deliberately creating a running, suppurating sore. The doctrine here is *derivation* — disease is drawn out of the interior to a manufactured exit on the surface. A blister raised on purpose is a door you built for the illness to leave by.

**Veterinary and Rural Folk Continuum.** This is where the plant survived longest and where it did the most concrete work. **Green hellebore root as a veterinary counter-irritant** — the classic use being the root passed through the dewlap of cattle to raise a discharging sore in respiratory and general disease — and as a **livestock purge and vermifuge**. Alongside, in human folk practice, its use as a **vermifuge for children**, which is the single most lethal application in this entry's history: an unstandardised cardiotoxic purge given by dose-guess to the smallest bodies. The persistence of the plant in Southeast European ethnomedicine is documented — *"the ethnomedicinal use of Helleborus species has a very long tradition"* in the region (Franz et al., 2017) — and *H. purpurascens* remains in living Balkan and Carpathian use, as does *H. purpurascens* in Romanian practice (Grigore et al., 2021).

#### Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer

Map "purges black bile, therefore cures madness" onto the real chemistry and you get one of the most instructive failures in the corpus, because **the doctrine's first half is pharmacologically true and its second half is a category error.**

**"It purges" — TRUE, and the chemistry is exactly what the doctrine describes.** Two independent irritant systems drive it:

- **Protoanemonin.** The Ranunculaceae signature. The plant stores the harmless glycoside ranunculin; tissue damage releases an enzyme that cleaves it to **protoanemonin**, a reactive, unstable lactone that alkylates protein thiols on contact. It is a *vesicant* — it blisters skin and ulcerates mucosa. Protoanemonin has been directly quantified in *Helleborus niger* extracts (0.0345 and 0.0662 mg/g in fresh preparations; 0.0896 ± 0.0125 mg/g across batches) and the authors' own conclusion is blunt: *"Due to its antibiotic effects, but also its reactivity, protoanemonin is a therapeutically and toxicologically relevant constituent"* (Müller et al., 2020). Protoanemonin dimerises spontaneously to the far less reactive **anemonin**, which is why the dried plant is less violent than the fresh — a fact the tradition observed empirically and explained wrongly.
- **Bufadienolides.** These are the reason for the vomiting, and the reason for the deaths. Cardiac glycosides inhibit **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase**; that inhibition is emetic via the chemoreceptor trigger zone, and it is cardiotoxic everywhere else. The doctrine's "violent evacuation upward and downward" is an accurate clinical description of acute cardiac-glycoside poisoning.

So the ancients correctly identified a plant that evacuates the gut with great force. **Their observation was excellent. It is the only part of this doctrine that survives.**

**"Therefore it removes black bile" — FALSE, because black bile does not exist.** There is no such substance. There is nothing in the vomitus or the stool that is melancholy. The doctrine's central object is a fiction, and the drug's action on that fiction is therefore not an action at all. The theory that made hellebore rational is void.

**"Therefore it cures madness" — FALSE, and worse than merely false.** What the drug actually delivered to a patient with a psychiatric illness was: violent purgation, dehydration, electrolyte derangement (**and hypokalaemia potentiates cardiac-glycoside toxicity** — the purge and the poison amplify each other, which is a mechanism the ancients could not have seen and which makes their graduated-dose safety regimen worse than useless), profound prostration, and a cardiac glycoside load in a body that had just lost its potassium. The patient who appeared calmer afterward was calmer because they were poisoned into exhaustion. **The doctrine mistook toxicity for efficacy.** This is the corpus's canonical example of that error, and it should be taught as such.

**The counter-irritant doctrine — "draw the disease to the surface."** This one maps cleanly onto protoanemonin's vesicant chemistry: the root *does* raise a running sore, reliably, by direct chemical alkylation of tissue. The doctrine describes a real, reproducible effect. What it gets wrong is the interpretation: the discharge is not disease leaving. It is the tissue you destroyed, liquefying. The mechanism is confirmed; the meaning is invented.

**The symbolic layer proper.** Hellebore's grip on the European imagination is that it made madness *treatable* — it converted an unbearable, meaningless affliction into a substance with a location and an exit. That is an enormous psychological service and it has nothing to do with pharmacology. The drug's real function was to let physicians act. Anticyra was a destination, and a destination is a form of hope. **Hold that separate from the chemistry, or you will repeat the error in a modern idiom.**

### Active Components

**⚠️ Note on sourcing:** the modern phytochemical literature retrieved here concentrates on *H. niger*, *H. foetidus*, *H. thibetanus*, *H. purpurascens* and *H. cyclophyllus*. **Species-specific quantitative data for *H. viridis* were not retrieved in this run** [USER: verify — a *H. viridis*-specific bufadienolide assay should be sourced before any figure is attributed to this species]. The class assignment below is genus-level and is presented as such. It does not soften the hazard: it is the *class* that kills.

- **Hellebrin** — the characteristic *Helleborus* cardiac steroid, identified in *H. purpurascens* (Franz et al., 2017) and isolated from *H. cyclophyllus* (Tsiftsoglou et al., 2022). Cytotoxic at IC50 0.008 mg/L across a cancer cell panel.
- **Deglucohellebrin** — isolated from *H. cyclophyllus* (Tsiftsoglou et al., 2022) and from *H. thibetanus* (Guo et al., 2024).
- **Hellebrigenin-3-O-β-D-glucoside** and related bufadienolide glycosides — isolated from *H. thibetanus* (Guo et al., 2024).
- **Bufadienolides and 19-norbufadienolides** — eighteen isolated from *H. foetidus* alone, nine previously undescribed; **cytotoxic IC50 range 2.4 nM – >10 µM**, potency strongly correlated with a **C-19 aldehyde group** (Potterat et al., 2024).
- **Bufadienolide rhamnosides** — from *H. niger*; the most potent showed IC50 values down to **0.0016 µM** with **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase identified as the molecular target** by JFCR39 panel screening (Yoshizawa et al., 2024).
- **Protoanemonin** (from ranunculin) — quantified in *H. niger* at 0.0345–0.0662 mg/g fresh, 0.0896 ± 0.0125 mg/g across batches; dimerises to **anemonin** (Müller et al., 2020).
- **Ecdysteroids** — 20-hydroxyecdysone and polypodine B in *H. cyclophyllus* (Tsiftsoglou et al., 2022); further ecdysteroids from *H. niger* (Yokosuka et al., 2021; Yoshizawa et al., 2024).
- **Hellethionins** — cysteine-rich proteins reported in *H. purpurascens* (Franz et al., 2017).
- **Steroidal saponins** — reported across the genus (Franz et al., 2017; Li et al., 2019).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**There is no use case. This entry is a boundary marker.**

*Helleborus viridis* is retained in the Kenetica corpus for three reasons, none of them practical:

1. **It is the corpus's worked example of a doctrine whose observation was sound and whose theory was void.** The ancients saw a violent purge. They were right about the purge. Everything they built on top of it was constructed on a humour that does not exist.
2. **It is the corpus's worked example of toxicity mistaken for efficacy** — the failure mode that makes drastic remedies immortal. A drug that visibly, dramatically *does something* will always be credited with doing the thing you wanted, and hellebore is where that error is most legible because the "something" was cardiac poisoning and the "thing you wanted" was sanity.
3. **It is the historical ancestor of a live problem.** The bufadienolide chemistry is not inert or uninteresting — it is genuinely potent, genuinely targeted at Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, and genuinely under investigation. That is precisely why the plant must be walled off from practice: real potency is what makes a poison plausible.

**No Kenetica practitioner has any reason to touch this plant. Not at low dose, not homeopathically-diluted-but-mishandled, not topically, not as a "traditional experience." The tradition's own body count is the argument.**

**Why the graduated-dose tradition made it worse, not better.** The classical practice of building the dose was constructed on the assumption of a therapeutic window. Bufadienolides at 0.0016–2.4 nM IC50 against cells, acting on Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, in a plant of variable and unstandardised glycoside content, in a patient being simultaneously dehydrated and stripped of potassium by the drug's own purgation — **hypokalaemia sensitises the myocardium to cardiac-glycoside toxicity.** The regimen that was supposed to make hellebore safe was, mechanistically, a protocol for turning a sublethal dose into a lethal one. The ancients' safety practice was inverted.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** Black hellebore (*helléboros mélas*) is given a systematic account in *De Materia Medica* (Book IV), where Dioscorides sets out its use as a drastic downward purge for the melancholic and the mad and warns explicitly about its danger and its dosing; *H. viridis* belongs to the black-hellebore (*Helleborus*, Ranunculaceae) group that stands behind that testimony, though the classical record cannot be pinned to this species and is further corrupted by the perennial confusion with white hellebore (*Veratrum album*). Dioscorides carries no subtle-body doctrine and assigns the plant no status touching the practitioner's own vitality; what the tradition offers a cultivator is his discipline of attention — the season and manner of gathering the root, the sensory assay of its acridity, the guard against a substitute — which for a plant this lethal is a discipline of avoidance, not of use. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** Hellebore (*kharbaq aswad*, black hellebore) is carried in the Unani materia medica as a hot, drastic evacuant purge worked against melancholic and phlegmatic surplus, the humoral cousin of the Greek indication. The strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus — the conservation of the innate heat and radical moisture that ageing depletes, a longevity practice of husbanding a finite endowment — is a frame of the whole regimen and is not a status the tradition assigns to a violent cardiotoxic evacuant, which spends the body rather than conserves it; no *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed for it, and none would be coherent. The substance is attested therapeutically with no cultivation status recorded. **(b) framed.**

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing here for a cultivator to take, in any tradition. Across the Greek and Unani records this is a drastic, dangerous evacuant given for a defined crisis and then stopped, and this entry's own governing judgement is that no defined crisis justifies it now — the plant is documented and buried, not used. The one transferable practice is Dioscorides' discipline of attention turned to its proper end here: the ability to identify the plant, distinguish it from *Veratrum*, and refuse it. Cultivation, for green hellebore, is the trained refusal.

### Contraindications

# ☠️ ABSOLUTE — THIS PLANT IS NOT USED. THERE ARE NO RELATIVE CONTRAINDICATIONS BECAUSE THERE IS NO PERMITTED USE.

- **☠️ FATAL CARDIOTOXICITY.** Bufadienolide cardiac glycosides inhibit Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase (target confirmed by panel screening; congeners active at low nanomolar concentrations). Effects: bradycardia, AV block, ventricular arrhythmia, cardiac arrest, death. **There is no home-assessable dose. There is no safe dose. Glycoside content is unstandardised and varies by plant, part, season and preparation.**
- **☠️ VIOLENT EMESIS AND PURGATION** — with dehydration and **hypokalaemia, which potentiates the cardiac-glycoside toxicity**. The purge and the poison compound each other. This is why graduated dosing does not confer safety.
- **☠️ PROTOANEMONIN — VESICANT.** Blisters and ulcerates skin and mucosa on contact. **Gloves mandatory for any handling of the fresh plant.** Never touch the eyes.
- **☠️ NEVER GIVE TO CHILDREN.** The historical paediatric vermifuge use is the deadliest thing in this entry's record: an unstandardised cardiotoxin, dosed by guess, into the smallest bodies.
- **☠️ ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED** with digoxin, digitoxin or any cardiac glycoside (additive lethality); with any potassium-depleting agent (loop/thiazide diuretics, corticosteroids, other purgatives); with calcium; and in any pre-existing cardiac, renal or electrolyte disorder — but note that these are listed for **clinical recognition of the poisoning**, not to imply that the plant is usable in their absence. **It is not usable in anyone.**
- **☠️ Pregnancy — historically used as an emmenagogue/abortifacient. This is a mechanism of maternal death, not a therapy.**
- **☠️ Genotoxicity.** A *Helleborus* cardiac glycoside causes DNA double-strand breaks (Ma et al., 2018).
- **☠️ Aerial parts are NOT safe.** Hellebrin and deglucohellebrin were isolated from aerial parts (Tsiftsoglou et al., 2022). The "the danger is in the root" folk belief is false.
- **☠️ Livestock and pets.** Grazing animals are poisoned by this plant. Garden hellebores are a recognised domestic hazard.
- **☠️ Homeopathic and anthroposophic dilutions are pharmaceutical manufactures made under regulated conditions.** Their existence is not evidence that the plant can be prepared safely by anyone else, and does not licence domestic preparation.

**⚠️ IF INGESTION IS SUSPECTED — MEDICAL EMERGENCY.** Treat as cardiac-glycoside poisoning. Contact emergency services / poison control immediately. Do not wait for symptoms; do not attempt herbal or domestic management. Bring the plant if it can be done without delay.
