# Sea lavender — laymūsūn (ليموسون)

## *The Greek leimonion in Arabic dress, here filed as sea lavender (Limonium); the identification is the modern one and is uncertain, because the Canon gives no description of the plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** Book II, entry *laymūsūn*, Arabic text lines 14687 to 14690. The Dioscorides tradition on disk has the same Greek name in chapter 4-16 *LEIMONION*, and the Codex already has an entry on it, I_1202_Statice_thouini (tagged DMM). The two entries are not merged here: this one rests on the Canon alone, and I_1202 on Dioscorides alone. They are listed as an overlap in the report.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The entry is three lines and has no "what it is" section. Properties: "its fruit (*thamara*) is astringent and dry" (ثمرته قابضة يابسة). Excretory organs: "it benefits looseness of the bowels and [the flux of] blood, given to drink in wine, and likewise for the bleeding of the menses; and the draught is *iksūthāfan*" (ينفع من استطلاق البطن والدم يسقى في شراب وكذلك لنزف الحيض والشربة إكسوثافن). The word *iksūthāfan* is Greek *oxybaphon*, the vinegar-cruet measure, which the English Dioscorides on disk renders "acetabulum"; the identification is mine from the sound of the word and is not in the text. [USER: verify.] The same measure word occurs in the Canon's *māzaryūn* entry (line 14978 to 14979). No amount is given.
This matches the content of Dioscorides 4-16 as quoted in I_1202: "an acetabulum of the seed ... taken in a drink with wine is able to help dysentery and abdominal cavities, and stop women's excessive bloody menstrual discharges." Avicenna's lines are shorter and name the fruit rather than the seed. The Canon does not name Dioscorides in this entry.

**Identity doubt.** The word *laymūsūn* gives the Greek name, not the plant. The English Dioscorides prints two different plants under the chapter: wintergreen (*Pyrola*) and, as "other usage", sea lavender (*Limonium*). The Canon entry has no description with which to choose. "Sea lavender" in the entry name is the spine's reading. [USER: verify.]

### Active Components

None is given. If the plant is a *Limonium*, the genus belongs to the leadwort family and its roots are generally described as tannin-rich and astringent, which would fit the Canon's word. [USER: verify.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The text records an astringent fruit drunk in wine for loose bowels and for menstrual bleeding. Dysentery, bleeding from the gut and heavy menstrual bleeding are conditions for medical care; no protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

Fruit given to drink in wine; the draught is one *iksūthāfan* (line 14690). The amount of that measure is not given in the text. No dose in modern units is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

Astringent plant materials rich in tannins are well known; nothing about this plant is established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Avicenna: the fruit astringent and dry (line 14689). No degree of heat is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** A three-line entry of binding uses, with nothing on the vital faculties. **(c) Absent: no cultivation framing is attested.**

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing to practise here. The entry shows the Canon repeating a Greek drug in its own shorter form.

### Contraindications

- Bleeding and dysentery need medical care.
- The plant is not securely identified; no identification should be drawn from the name.
