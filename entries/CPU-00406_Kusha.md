### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** This entry needs a ruling. The spine row names *Desmostachya bipinnata*. The Latin printed against the Sanskrit word in the text on disk is *Poa cynosuroides*. Neither source read says that the two binomials are one grass, so the link is partly an inference. [USER: verify] The same glossary prints "Poa cynosuroides." against *Darbha* as well (line 31601), and Sushruta lists *Kusha* and *Darbha* as two separate drugs in the same group (Sutra ch.38, line 533), so the glossary puts one Latin name against two Sanskrit names that the text itself separates. The existing entry `I_493_Darbha_Imperata_cylindrica.md` (spine row CPU-00409, tagged CM, SBJ, SS) is headed "Darbha / Kusha" and identifies the grass as *Imperata cylindrica*. That conflicts with the Latin name in the Sushruta glossary, and this entry does not edit it. [USER: verify] The word is also a lookalike in the translation: at Chikitsa ch.3 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch03.txt`, lines 22 and 217) Bhishagratna's "Kusa" in brackets means a splint made from bark, not the grass. Two further grass names in the same group, *Kasha* and *Nala*, have their own entries (`I_915_Kasha.md`, `I_916_Nala.md`).

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

*The ganas (Sutra ch.38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`).* Kusha stands in two groups.

- The Viratarvadi group (lines 57 to 64): "The drugs known as Virataru, the two kinds of Sahachara, Darbha, Vrikshadani, Gundra, I\'ala [OCR for Nala], Kusha, Kasha, Ashmabhedaka, Agnimantha, Morata Vasuka, Vasira, Bhalluka, Kuruntaka, Indivara, Kapotavanka, and Shvadanstra enter into the composition of the group known as the Viratarvadi." The metrical text (lines 66 to 73) says the group "subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles."
- The Pancha-Trina group (lines 531 to 539): "The group consisting of the five medicinal herbs (of the grass species) and known as Kusha, Kasha, Nala, Darbha, Kandekshuka, is called the Pancha-Trina." The metrical text reads "Cases of Haemoptysis, renal defects or of uninary diseases are found to speedily yield to the curative efficacy of the compound internally administered through the medium of cow's milk." The summary at the end of the chapter (lines 545 to 552) says that the Trina-Panchamula, "standing in the bottom of the list", "is endued with the property of killing the deranged Pittam". The five grasses are therefore the Trina-Panchamula of other chapters.

Membership of these groups is a grouping by therapeutic action, grade (b) at most.

*Appendix 3 glossary (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`).* "Kusa" is printed at line 32083 on the page of K-names. The page's Latin block begins at line 32094, and the sixteen names are counted down in step with it from "Krita-vedhana". Counted that way, "Kunda" pairs with "Jasminum Multiflorum," (line 32111), "Kuruvaka" with "Barleria Cristata." (line 32113), "Kusa" with "Poa Cynosuroides." (line 32115) and "Kushmanda" with "Benincasa Cerifera." (line 32117). The count was checked from both ends of the block and agrees. The pairing is by OCR column position, not by a printed link.

*Passages read, in the order of the book.*

- **Sutra ch.2** (`SS_Sutra_ch02.txt`, lines 40 to 56), the initiation of a student: "blades of Kusha grass should be strewn over it" (the plastered sacred ground), and the teacher "should lay down a blade of Kusha grass tied up in the form of a knot, known as the Brahmana, along the side of the sacred cushion to his right, and kindle the sacred fire".
- **Sutra ch.8** (`SS_Sutra_ch08.txt`, line 27), a footnote on instruments: "The Kushapatram is so called from its resemblance to the blade of a Kusha-grass." (A name of a surgical instrument.)
- **Sutra ch.39** (`SS_Sutra_ch39.txt`, lines 20 to 36), the purgative drugs: "... Chitraka, Kinihi, Kusha, Kasha, Tilvaka, Kampillaka, Ranwaka, Patala, Puga, Haritaki ... are possessed of purgative properties. Of these the roots of plants, which precede Tilvaka in the above list, should be used for purgative purposes." Kusha precedes Tilvaka, so its root is the part named. The Pitta-soothing list of the same chapter closes with the Trina-Panchamula group (lines 108 to 110).
- **Sutra ch.43 and ch.44** (`SS_Sutra_ch43.txt`, line 24; `SS_Sutra_ch44.txt`, lines 127 and 338): Kusha blades serve as a container and a binding in the preparation of purgatives and emetics: Madana fruits "should be stored in a box made of the blades of Kusha grass", a split sugar-cane paste is "tied up (with the blades of Kusha grass)", and Danti and Dravanti roots are placed "in a box of Kusha grass firmly tied up and plastered with a layer of clay" and put in a dung-cake fire.
- **Chikitsa ch.5** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt`, lines 115 to 119): an oil is cooked with a decoction that includes "Trina-pancha-mula" (the five grass roots) for the patient.
- **Chikitsa ch.6** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch06.txt`, lines 2 and 5), footnotes: "Some explain that a third decoction should be that of Triphala, Darvi, Patola and Kus'a grass taken together. — Dallana."
- **Chikitsa ch.7** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch07.txt`, lines 38 to 56), the treatment of Pittaja *Asmari*: a ghee "cooked with the decoction of Kus'a, Kas'a, S'ara, Gundra, Itkata, Morata ..." with a paste of Shilajatu, Madhuka and the seeds of Indivara, Trapusha and Ervaruka "would speedily bring about the disintegration of Pittaja As'mari (calculi, etc.)". A footnote at line 298 says "The urine-purifying drugs are the Trina-Panchamulas, Gokshura, Kasamarda, Pashanabheda, etc."
- **Chikitsa ch.16** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch16.txt`, lines 94 to 105), in the chapter on abscesses: "Karanjadya Ghrita: A Karsha measure of each of the following drugs, viz., the tender leaves and fruits of the Naktamala ... Priyangu, Kus'a-roots, Nichula-bark, Manjishtha, sandal wood, Ushira, Utpala, Sariva and Trivrit should be cooked with a Prastha measure of clarified butter." The text says this ghee "will cure malignant ulcers (Dushta-Vrana) and act as a purifier in sinus and recent ulcers, etc., burns and scalds, deep sores and even deep-seated sinuses." A footnote reports that the commentator Chakrapani reads the list differently.
- **Chikitsa ch.29** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch29.txt`, lines 1 to 6 and 119 to 123), the chapter on *rasayana* that arrests innate morbid tendencies and decay, in the rite of the Soma juice: the patient "may, after taking his meal in the evening and hearing the benedictory words, lie down on a mattress of Kus'a-grass covered with black-deer skin and thus pass the night among his friends and may take cold water when thirsty." Kusha is the bedding in the rite and not the *rasayana* drug.
- **Chikitsa ch.32** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch32.txt`, lines 88 to 92 and 109 to 113), on fomentation: a pipe made of "Kus'a, Kas'a, etc." for steam, and a patient laid "on a mattress (made of Kus'a, Kas'a, etc.)" over a fire of boiled grain.
- **Kalpa ch.5** (`SS_Kalpa_ch05.txt`, lines 55 to 60), in the antivenom chapter: a man learning the mantras "should forego sexual intercourse, animal diet, wine, honey, etc., should be self-controlled and clean in body and spirit and ... shall lie on a mattress of Kus'a-grass."
- **Uttara ch.17** (`SS_Uttara_ch17.txt`, lines 311 to 316): a collyrium "should be placed inside the blades of Kusa grass and preserved for a month inside the mouth of a (dead) black cobra". That is a recorded preparation and not an endorsement.
- **Uttara ch.60** (`SS_Uttara_ch60.txt`, lines 94 to 96 and 242 to 244), on the spirit-seizures: a person seized by a Pitri (ancestral) *graha* "offers oblations on Kus'a-grass and libations of water", and the Pitri grahas "should be propitiated with the offerings made on Kus'a grass together with Madhavi and Kunda flowers on the banks of a river".
- **Sharira ch.5** (`SS_Sharira_ch05.txt`, lines 236 to 240), on dissection: the decomposed body is scraped "with a whisk made of grass-roots, hair, Kusa blade or with a strip of split bamboo".

Only the Chikitsa ch.29 line is in a rasayana or vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30), and it names the grass as bedding, so no grade (a) status is claimed.

### Active Components

Grasses are generally known to be rich in silica and fibre, and the rhizomes of many perennial grasses carry starch and sugars. The composition of the grass named in the text was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Sushruta records Kusha as a ritual seat and bedding, a binding and a container, a root in purgatives, urinary and calculus remedies and a medicated ghee for ulcers, and a fomentation pipe and mattress. The Codex gives no protocol for this plant. Its ritual roles are the text's own and are not endorsed as practice.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted above: a decoction of the five grass roots in a ghee (Chikitsa ch.7), a ghee for ulcers (Chikitsa ch.16) and a root as a purgative (Sutra ch.39). Chikitsa ch.16 (lines 94 to 105) is the one place where a measure is given, "A Karsha measure of each" drug and "a Prastha measure of clarified butter", for the compound as a whole and not for Kusha alone. The Karsha and Prastha are units the lines read do not define. No dose of Kusha for a patient is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** The text prints no *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* for Kusha in the lines read. Sutra ch.38 (lines 531 to 552) says the Pancha-Trina group, to which Kusha belongs, "kills the deranged Pittam", and Sutra ch.39 puts its roots among the purgatives.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Kusha is a named grass in two groups, a root in purgatives and urinary remedies, and a plant of many ritual and practical roles: a seat for the teacher's rite (Sutra ch.2), a mattress for a man learning the antivenom mantras (Kalpa ch.5), and the bedding on which the person taking the Soma *rasayana* lies (Chikitsa ch.29). Chikitsa ch.29 is a rasayana chapter that names Kusha, but only as bedding, so it is not a drug of the elixir, and the passage assigns the grass no status. The ch.38 gana is a grouping by action, not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. Nothing read ties Kusha to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana* as a substance. The status is unattested, and the identification with *Desmostachya bipinnata* rests on an OCR-paired appendix that prints a different binomial and prints the same one for Darbha. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with the grass as a drug. As a seat or mattress it is the text's own ritual frame, which the Codex records and does not prescribe. The abstentive answer is the correct one for the root: any medicinal use is a matter for a qualified practitioner.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Grass blades have sharp edges (Sushruta names a surgical blade after them), so handle them with care. The purgative, urinary, ulcer and eye uses above are conditions needing medical care, and none of them is advice. Do not use a root as a purgative at home, do not apply a dressing to an ulcer or burn, and do not put anything in the eye. The collyrium kept in a dead cobra's mouth (Uttara ch.17) is history only. Do not use in pregnancy.
