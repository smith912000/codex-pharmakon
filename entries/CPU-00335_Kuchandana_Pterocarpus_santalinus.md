# Kuchandana (Red Sandalwood) — Rakta-candana, Raktacandana, Kucandana, Red Sanders, Ruby Wood, Lāl Candan

## *Pterocarpus santalinus* L.f. | Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS) & classical Āyurveda:** *Rakta-candana* — "red sandal" — is one of the two great *candana* (sandalwood) dravyas of Āyurveda, paired with white/yellow *Śveta-candana* (*Santalum album*). Though botanically unrelated to true sandalwood, Rakta-candana is classed with it functionally: it is the **paradigmatic cooling, Pitta- and Rakta-pacifying wood**. Suśruta and the classical texts deploy it for *Pitta-prakopa* (Pitta excess), *Rakta-doṣa* (blood disorders, bleeding, heat in the blood), *dāha* (burning sensations), *tṛṣṇā* (thirst), *jvara* (fever), *visarpa* and inflammatory skin conditions, and as a *varṇya* (complexion-enhancing) and *vraṇa-ropaṇa* (wound-healing) agent applied as a cooling paste (*lepa*). The heartwood — deep blood-red, dense, and durable — is ground to a paste on stone with water for external application, or given in fine decoction/*kaṣāya* internally. Its cooling *vīrya* is considered among the most reliable in the materia medica.

**The sacred red pigment:** Rakta-candana's blood-red heartwood is a primary source of ritual red colour across Indian temple practice — the *tilaka* and deity-marking pigment, and a component of ceremonial pastes. Its colour (from santalin pigments) associates it inescapably with blood, and thus, by the Āyurvedic doctrine of *sāmānya* (like-affecting-like tempered by cooling potency), with the **treatment of the blood** (*rakta*) itself — a red, cooling wood for a heated, disordered *rakta dhātu*.

**Endemism and conservation — a required flag:** *Pterocarpus santalinus* is **endemic to a small region of the southern Eastern Ghats** (chiefly Andhra Pradesh, India). Its extraordinary dense red timber ("Red Sanders") has made it one of the most heavily smuggled woods in the world; the species is **listed on CITES Appendix II** and is IUCN-assessed as threatened/over-exploited. **Any use must be from legal, sustainably sourced, and documented material only.** This conservation status is itself part of the substance's contemporary identity and must accompany every reference to it.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Red Wood That Cools the Blood:**

Kuchandana is the Codex's supreme figure of the **cooling paradox**: it is blood-red — the colour of heat, of Pitta, of Agni, of the inflamed *rakta* — and yet its action is the most decisively *cooling* of the woods. It carries the appearance of fire and the potency of water. This inversion is its whole symbolic charge. To grind red sandal on stone and apply the cool crimson paste to a burning brow is to answer heat *with its own image made cold* — the medicine that meets fire not by opposition but by transmutation, wearing fire's colour while extinguishing its excess.

Because it is simultaneously *the pigment of the sacred mark* and *the cooler of the blood*, Kuchandana bridges the ritual and the therapeutic with unusual completeness. The red *tilaka* on the brow is not only ornament: it is a cooling, Pitta-quieting, contemplation-supporting application placed at the seat of attention. In Kenetica this locates Kuchandana in the **Cooling & Pacification** phase with a strong **contemplative / ritual-support** overtone: the substance for states of over-heat, over-intensity, inflamed drive, and burning restlessness — applied to *settle the fire without denying it*, and to mark the threshold of practice in the colour of life.

Its endemic rarity and endangered status add a final doctrinal layer: **the medicine one must not exhaust.** Kuchandana teaches restraint at the level of sourcing — a cooling agent whose own over-harvest is a heat upon the world.

### Active Components

According to PubMed, the characteristic heartwood constituents of *Pterocarpus santalinus* include:

- **Santalins A and B** — the red pigment compounds giving the heartwood its colour.
- **Savinin, calocedrin, pterolinus K and L** — lignans/isoflavonoid-class constituents.
- **Pterostilbene** and related stilbenes — with antioxidant/metabolic interest.
- **Lupane- and oleanane-class triterpenes** (bark, leaves) and **sesquiterpenes and polyphenols** (heartwood), per phytochemical review — roughly 85 constituents reported (Bulle et al., 2016, *Pharmacognosy Reviews* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0973-7847.176575); Dahat et al., 2021, *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2021.114127)).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

- **Cooling & Pacification phase (signature):** the premier cooling, Pitta-Rakta-quieting agent — for heat states, inflammation, burning sensation, over-intensity, and "heated blood." Applied as cooling *lepa* externally; internally as a heat-clearing *kaṣāya*.
- **Contemplative / Ritual-support:** as the sacred red pigment — the cooling *tilaka* at the brow, marking and settling the seat of attention before practice.
- **Grounding / complexion (varṇya):** traditional skin and complexion application, wound support, and a settling, cooling ground for over-driven states.

It is the cooling counterweight to the batch's warming entry (Meṣaśṛṅgī) and pairs with the other cool astringent woods (Tinisha, Shinshapa).

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, evidence for *Pterocarpus santalinus* is **preclinical and review-level** — a broad panel of reported activities, no robust human clinical trials retrieved this run.

- A therapeutic-potential review reports antioxidant, antidiabetic, antimicrobial, anticancer, and anti-inflammatory activities, plus hepatoprotective, gastroprotective and neuroprotective effects, attributed to constituents including santalins A/B, savinin, calocedrin, pterolinus K/L and pterostilbenes (Bulle et al., 2016, *Pharmacognosy Reviews* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0973-7847.176575)).
- A phytochemistry/pharmacology review with a network-pharmacology component centres the heartwood's traditional and validated **anti-inflammatory** use and catalogues ~85 constituents, calling for systematic study to substantiate traditional claims (Dahat et al., 2021, *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2021.114127)).

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical / traditional. The anti-inflammatory and antioxidant directions are the best-supported and align with the classical cooling, Pitta-Rakta-pacifying use; human efficacy is not established. Grade specific therapeutic claims as *traditionally strong, scientifically preliminary*.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Tikta (bitter), Kaṣāya (astringent), with secondary Madhura (sweet)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry), Snigdha in the sweet-secondary sense (mild)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling) — markedly so; among the most cooling woods
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Doṣa action:** Pitta-Rakta śāmaka foremost; also Kapha-reducing. Vāta may aggravate in excess (dryness).
- **Karma (actions):** Dāhapraśamana (relieves burning), Raktaprasādana (blood-clearing), Pittaghna, Varṇya (complexion), Vraṇaropaṇa (wound-healing), Tṛṣṇānigrahaṇa (thirst-quelling), Jvaraghna (antipyretic), Viṣaghna (traditional anti-toxic)
- **Prabhāva (specific effect):** its intense cooling on the *rakta dhātu* despite its blood-red colour — the "red that cools the blood."
- **Dhātu/Srotas:** *raktavaha* srotas primarily; also *rasavaha* and skin (*twak*).

### Contraindications

- **Vāta-predominant / cold / dry states:** the strong cooling and dryness can aggravate Vāta and cold conditions.
- **Depleted, low-*agni* states:** cooling potency may further dampen digestive fire.
- **Pregnancy/lactation:** insufficient safety data internally; avoid without guidance.
- **Provenance / legality (critical):** Red Sanders is CITES Appendix II and heavily counterfeited — never use undocumented, illegally harvested, or adulterated wood. Conservation duty is a contraindication in itself.
- Not a substitute for medical treatment of fever, inflammation, or blood disorders.
