# Alavana — Alavana (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation, 1907)

## *Alavana, a plant drug listed in the Arkadi group of the Sushruta Samhita. Identification: unresolved. The translator's footnote gives two commentators' readings, Kakamardanika (Dallana) and Jyotishmati (Shivadasa), and follows Shivadasa; no binomial is printed for either in the passages read; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk. This is a single-text entry.** Spellings in quotations are normalised silently from the OCR where the damage is plain (for example Nandi for the printed Xandi); the line numbers cited give the text as printed. The spelling "Alavana" was searched in all three volumes of Bhishagratna (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, `Vol2`, `Vol3`). It occurs twice, listed below. The botanical Appendix 3 of Vol. III has no line for it (a name "Alavu" is there, paired with Lagenaria vulgaris, but it is a different word and is not equated here).

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The first place is Sutrasthana ch. 38, the Arkadi group (`Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15450; chapter file `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`): "... Rasna, Indrapushpi, Kshudrashveta Mahashveta, Vrishchikali, Alavana and Tapasha-Vriksha, enter into the composition of the group known as the Arkadi Gana." The metrical text reads: "The group known as the Arkadi destroys Kaphani, fat, and the effects of poison. It acts as a vermifuge and a specific aseptic agent in the case of an ulcer and proves curative in diseases of the skin."

The second place is Chikitsasthana ch. 17, in a passage on the treatment of sinuses (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt` line 261; Vol. II line 18784). After the alkali thread (Kshara-sutra) for sinus and fistula, the text says "The use of a plug made of the following drugs, viz., the bark and fruit of the Ghonta, (the five officinal kinds of) salt, Laksha, Puga and the leaves of the Alavana" and the plug is meant for the sinus. The translator's footnote (Vol. II line 18790) reads: "'Alavana' has been explained by Dallana as 'Kaka-mardanika' and by Shivadasa as 'Jyotishmati'. Shivadasa is, however, followed in practice in this case." This is the only passage that names a part used (the leaf) and an identification, and the identification is a commentators' disagreement, not a result.

**Jyotishmati in the same text.** The name Jyotishmati occurs in the errhine list of Sutra ch. 39 ("Vacha, Jyotishmati, Karanja, Arka, Alarka ..." `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` line 52). That is a separate entry in this Codex and is not folded in here. [USER: verify whether the Arkadi-group Alavana is the same drug as the sinus-plug Alavana.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Arkadi group: destroying Kapha and fat, countering poison, killing worms, cleansing ulcers and curing skin disease (Sutra ch. 38). As the leaf of a sinus plug (Chikitsa ch. 17).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation of the leaf beyond making the plug, no duration.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this drug.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation is the plug (Varti) of Chikitsa ch. 17 line 261, made of the substances named above together with the leaves of Alavana. Chapter 38 closes with a rule for all the groups: "Only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group, or a similar number of drugs chosen from the different groups, or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety, or in combination with another, should be used according to the indications of any particular case, as the physician, in his discretion, would determine" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, near the end; Vol. I, ch. XXXVIII). It also says that the groups "will be more elaborately dealt with later on in the chapter on Therapeutics". This is a rule of method for the groups, not a dose for this drug. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Alavana. The only qualities are those of the whole Arkadi group (Sutra ch. 38, as quoted above).

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is attested in the Sushruta Samhita, in the therapeutic groups of Sutrasthana ch. 38 and the other passages cited above, and its role there is therapeutic: groups are said to subdue Kapha, Vata or Pitta, to act as vermifuge or aseptic agents, and so on. Chikitsa ch. 26 (the vajikarana chapter, "increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons") and chs. 27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) were searched under the spellings printed here (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` to `ch30.txt`, and ch. 31) and the name was not found in them. The drug therefore carries no rasayana or vajikarana status in the sources retrieved, and nothing is said of ojas for it. The register is (b): framed by the therapeutic gana, with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** An unresolved group-listed drug, whose one named use is the leaf in a plug for a sinus. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use.

### Contraindications

- **Identity unresolved.** Two commentators equate it with two different plants. Do not substitute any plant.
- **Open sinus or wound.** The only use named is surgical, and the text is a historical record of a physician's practice, not self-care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
