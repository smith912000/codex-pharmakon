# Wu Jiu 乌韭 / 烏韭 (Shennong bencao jing); Yang 1998 "Herba Cotyledinis Malacophyllae"

## *A lower-class drug of the Shennong bencao jing that "grows on stones in mountain valleys"; the botanical identity is unresolved. Yang 1998 prints the provisional Latin "Herba Cotyledinis Malacophyllae" (a Cotyledon, hence the spine's "Cotyledon sp."), but his own footnote says there is no consensus, and Sun Xingyan's commentary points to a stone- or wall-growing "stone clothes" (a moss- or lichen-like growth) instead. Identification: unresolved; confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not identified. The classic lists it in the lower class, which its preface calls "mostly toxic". This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name.

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`); Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) is a second witness and is attributed to Yang. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder "Cotyledon sp." (genus-level, taken from Yang's pharmaceutical Latin by stem matching); the first-pass check read the Chinese name as Wu Jiu and kept the genus label with low confidence. This entry is written under the Chinese name and corrects the identification as described below. A search of the other texts on disk for the Chinese and pinyin names and for *Cotyledon* found nothing for this drug. The spine's existing *Cotyledon* rows (CPU-01493 and CPU-01494) are Dioscorides' plants and are a different matter.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 乌韭 in the lower classic (下经, the lower classic, grade per Sun's monograph list; Sun ed., entry 乌韭, line 3505, paragraph at line 3507). Sun's text reads: 味甘，寒 ("sweet, cold"); 主皮肤往来寒热 ("governs alternating cold and heat of the skin"), 利小肠膀胱气 ("disinhibits the qi of the small intestine and the bladder"); 生山谷石上 ("grows in mountain valleys, on stones"). There is no 久服 clause and no Wu Pu or Mingyi layer printed for this entry.

Sun's commentary (案, line 3509) is the main evidence on what the plant was. It quotes the Guangya: 昔邪，乌韭也 ("*xi xie* is *wu jiu*"), and distinguishes by place: on a house roof it is called *xi xie*, on a wall *yuan yi* (垣衣). It quotes the *Xishan jing*, which says that *bi li* (萆荔) "looks like *wu jiu*". It quotes the Tang commentary (《唐本》注): "it is *shi yi*" (石衣, "stone clothes"), "also called *shi tai* (石苔) and *shi fa* (石发)". The line ends in the e-text with the Guangya's further statement that *shi fa* is *shi yi* and is cut off there. The characters 乌韭 mean "black chive". The commentary therefore describes a low growth on stone or walls, of the kind the names "stone moss" and "stone hair" suggest, and says nothing of a succulent plant. **Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 3686): "Wu Jiu (Herba Cotyledinis Malacophyllae) is sweet and cold. It mainly treats alternating skin cold and heat and disinhibits the small intestine and urinary bladder qi. It grows in mountains and valleys." His footnote 230 (line 3701) reads: "There is as yet no consensus concerning the identity of this medicinal. Therefore, this identification is only provisional." So Yang himself marks "Cotyledon" as a stopgap, and the genus *Cotyledon* (a group of succulents, leaves fleshy) does not sit well beside the commentary's "grows on stones" growth of the *shi yi* type. The two witnesses are not reconciled in the sources retrieved.

**Where this differs from the spine's first-pass label.** The spine row carries "Cotyledon sp." with confidence low. This entry keeps it only as Yang's provisional Latin and does not adopt it as the identification. The entry is a real attestation (the name and the line name a drug), but the identification stays open.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unidentified.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Alternating cold and heat in the skin, and disinhibiting the small-intestine and bladder qi.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no part used, no harvest time or toxicity for this drug.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend any plant for these uses.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose for this drug. The preface contains a general rule of method for toxic drugs, which begins with a very small amount and stops when the illness goes. It is a rule of method for the class and not a dose for this plant. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. With the identity open, no pharmacology can be stated. Plants of the genus *Cotyledon* are succulents, and several are known to be toxic to livestock; mosses and lichens are a different matter. Neither fact tells us which plant the classic meant. The classic's "alternating cold and heat in the skin" has no modern equivalent and is not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic, whose preface (Sun ed. line 2935) reads 下药，一百二十五种，为左使。主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经: the lower drugs are "the left envoys, mainly treating disease to correspond with earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to remove cold and heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and heal illness". The drug's own paragraph has no 久服 clause and no promise of lightening the body or of not ageing. The register is therefore (b): framed by the lower class, whose own preface is the warning, and with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug, unidentified, named for skin-level cold and heat and for the gut and bladder. The tradition's own grading is the warning, and nothing in the record supports any cultivation use. A cultivator should not use it, and could not identify it.

### Contraindications

- **Possible toxicity.** The lower class is "mostly toxic" by the classic's own preface; this drug's own toxicity is not stated. Do not ingest it. Do not gather it.
- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified. Yang's *Cotyledon* is provisional and may be wrong. Do not substitute any succulent, moss or lichen.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Alternating chills and fevers, urinary trouble.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
