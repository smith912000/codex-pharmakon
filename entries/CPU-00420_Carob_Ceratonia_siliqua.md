# Carob / St John's Bread / Locust Bean / Kharrūb / Caroube / Johannisbrot / Carrubo

## *Ceratonia siliqua* L. | Family: Fabaceae

**Tradition Tags:** EP, DMM

**Energetic Classification:** Sweet with an astringent finish; cool and drying in the pod's tannin aspect, moistening and demulcent in the gum's. Nutritive-binding. A drug that both feeds and holds.

**Training Phase:** Foundation — Nutritive & Astringent Practice

---

### THE NAME IS A UNIT — AND THE UNIT IS A LIE

Greek **κεράτιον** (*keration*), "little horn," names the carob pod for its curved shape. The seed inside it was a weight. That weight became the **carat** — still, today, the unit in which every diamond on earth is priced, and (via the same root) the fineness measure of gold.

The received story is that the carob seed was chosen because its mass is extraordinarily, almost supernaturally constant — nature's own standard weight, uncorruptible by the merchant's thumb. **It is a beautiful story and it is false.**

Turnbull et al. sampled seeds from a collection of carob trees and measured a coefficient of variation of **23%** — against an average of **25%** across 63 species reviewed from the literature. Carob seeds are, in a word, **ordinary**. The paper's second half is the interesting one: in a perception experiment, observers could discriminate carob seed weight differences of around **5% by eye**, and the variability of pre-metrication carat weight standards is *also* around 5% ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2006.0476), PMID 17148413).

**The constancy was never in the seed. It was in the hand that sorted it.** Generations of traders picked out the outliers by eye, and the discarded tails of the distribution became a myth about the tree. This is the cleanest specimen in the codex of a doctrine that is entirely real in its *effect* — the carat worked; commerce ran on it for millennia — and entirely wrong about its *cause*. Hold that pattern. It recurs below, in the pharmacology, in a subtler form.

### Origin & Tradition

**Ebers Papyrus / Egyptian practice (EP) — *ḏꜣrt* (djaret)**

Carob is among the more frequently invoked plant drugs of the Egyptian medical corpus, appearing as **ḏꜣrt** — a sweet pod used both as a foodstuff and as a vehicle and active in compounded remedies. The Egyptian deployment is characteristically **gastro-intestinal and paediatric**: carob figures in preparations against loose bowel, against "eating" (wasting) in children, and as a sweet excipient carrying harsher drugs. It also served the sweetening function that honey served, in a country with no cane sugar.

The Egyptian reading is thus **nutritive first, binding second** — the pod is food that happens to stop the flux. **[USER: verify the specific Ebers paragraph numbers against a critical translation (Bardinet or Ghalioungui) before any are cited by number.]**

**De Materia Medica (DMM) — κεράτια / *keratia*, "the Egyptian fig"**

Dioscorides places carob squarely in the astringent-nutritive register. The pods are **stomachic and binding**; the fresh pod is described as loosening the belly while the **dried** pod binds it and stays the flux. That distinction — *fresh loosens, dried binds* — is not decorative. It is the single most chemically literate observation in the classical record on this plant, and we will vindicate it below.

The vernacular names preserve the Greek: Latin *siliqua* (the pod, and later a Roman coin-weight), and via the Vulgate's rendering of the prodigal son's swine-fodder, the medieval European identification of carob as the **"husks"** of Luke 15 — famine food, the last thing before starvation. The parallel Christian legend that the pods were the "locusts" eaten by John the Baptist in the wilderness gives us **St John's Bread** and **locust bean**, and hence *locust bean gum* — the name under which the seed's endosperm now sits in the food code as **E410**. **[USER: verify — the "locusts of John the Baptist" identification is a devotional tradition, not a philological result, and most scholars read the Greek ἀκρίδες as actual insects.]**

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

**Doctrine 1: sweetness that binds — the paradox, and its resolution in chemistry.**

Every tradition that handled carob noticed the same contradiction. The pod is *sweet* — up to roughly half its dry weight is sugar, mostly sucrose with glucose and fructose — and sweetness in every classical energetic system means *nourishing, moistening, building*. Yet the same pod **puckers the mouth** and **stops diarrhoea**, and astringency means the opposite: drying, binding, restraining. The tradition filed carob as a nutritive **and** an astringent and did not resolve the tension.

**The chemistry resolves it, and the resolution is that there are two drugs in one fruit.**

- The **pulp** carries the sugars *and* a heavy load of **condensed tannins (proanthocyanidins)** and **gallotannins**. The pucker is tannin–protein cross-linking on the oral mucosa — the same reaction, on gut mucosa, that firms a stool. Sweet *and* binding, in the same tissue, from different molecules.
- The **seed endosperm** carries **galactomannan** — the gum — which is neither sweet nor astringent but **viscous**, and which binds by an entirely different mechanism: it does not precipitate protein, it thickens water.

The tradition perceived one plant with a contradictory nature. It was in fact holding three organs with three chemistries — pulp sugar, pulp tannin, seed gum — and the "paradox" is an artefact of not having separated them. **This is what a doctrine looks like when it is reporting a real observation through an inadequate ontology: not wrong, under-resolved.**

**Doctrine 2: fresh loosens, dried binds — VINDICATE.**

Dioscorides' distinction maps onto something real and specific. The fresh pod's high free-sugar load, delivered in bulk, is an **osmotic** cathartic — the same principle as any poorly absorbed sugar. Drying concentrates the tannin fraction relative to the water and shifts the polyphenol profile toward higher-molecular-weight, more protein-avid condensed forms. **Fresh: osmotic load wins. Dried: tannin wins.** The Greek got the direction right, the dose-dependence right, and the preparation-dependence right, from tasting and watching. Grade the *doctrine* as high; grade the *mechanism as stated by the tradition* as absent, because there wasn't one.

**Doctrine 3: the horn, and the famine reading — DEFLATE, gently.**

*Keration*, little horn; the pod is a curved horn; the doctrine of signatures duly attached horn-associations — strength, abundance, the cornucopia — and the Christian reading loaded the same pod with poverty and penitence. **These are literary facts, not pharmacological ones.** They are worth keeping in the entry because they explain the plant's *cultural* trajectory — carob is famine food in Europe and a luxury flavouring in the same century in Egypt — but nothing follows from a horn shape. There is no cornucopial chemistry.

**What is worth taking seriously on its own terms** is the framing shared by the Egyptian and Greek readings: that this is a **food that is a drug**, and that the medicine is inseparable from the eating. That framing is precisely what the modern evidence base vindicates. Carob's real clinical wins are all in the register of *dietary management*, not therapy. The tradition never asked it to be a therapeutic agent, and it isn't one.

### Active Components

**Galactomannan (locust bean gum, LBG; carob bean gum; E410)** — the seed endosperm polysaccharide. A **mannose backbone with galactose side-branches** at roughly a 4:1 mannose:galactose ratio [USER: verify the ratio against a primary carbohydrate-chemistry source]. **Indigestible by human enzymes**, hence negligible systemic bioavailability — the fact on which its infant-safety case rests (Meunier et al. 2014, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yrtph.2014.06.023), PMID 24997231). Its entire pharmacology is **rheological**: it makes water thick.

**Condensed tannins / proanthocyanidins and gallotannins** — the pulp's astringent principle and the doctrinal load-bearer. Carob pulp fibre is characterised in the clinical literature as an **insoluble fibre "very rich in polyphenols"** — one trial's test article was 84% insoluble polyphenols (Ruiz-Roso et al. 2010, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11130-009-0153-9), PMID 20094802). **No quantitation of individual proanthocyanidin oligomers in *C. siliqua* was retrieved this run — [USER: verify].**

**Sugars** — sucrose predominant, with glucose and fructose. The nutritive fraction and the osmotic one. **Exact percentages not retrieved this run — [USER: verify against a food-composition table.]**

**Insoluble dietary fibre (non-gum)** — cellulose, lignin, and the polyphenol-bound fibre matrix of the pulp. The lipid-active fraction (below).

**Not present, and this matters:** carob contains **no caffeine, no theobromine, and no tyramine** — the entire basis of its use as a cocoa substitute, and the reason it is one of the few "chocolate-adjacent" foods with no methylxanthine pharmacology whatsoever. **[USER: verify against a compositional analysis before this is stated as an absolute.]**

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**The codex's clearest example of a traditional food-drug whose modern evidence base is real, narrow, and entirely unglamorous.**

**Primary: paediatric regurgitation, as thickened feed.** This is carob's strongest indication anywhere in the codex — not because the effect is large, but because it has been tested properly, repeatedly, in the right population, with the right comparator. LBG-thickened anti-regurgitation formula reduces regurgitation frequency. It is first-line **dietary management**, and it is explicitly what guidelines prefer over acid suppression in this population (Barbieur et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/MOP.0000000000001147), PMID 35855545).

**Secondary: acute childhood diarrhoea, as adjunct to oral rehydration.** Carob bean juice alongside standard ORS. One good trial (below). **Adjunct, never replacement** — ORS is the therapy; carob modifies the stool.

**Tertiary: lipid management, as a dietary fibre.** Real, modest, food-shaped. Not a lipid drug.

**Not recommended for:** anything requiring systemic exposure. LBG does not get absorbed; the pulp's polyphenols are largely fibre-bound. **A plant whose active is defined by not being absorbed cannot be a systemic agent, and no amount of polyphenol enthusiasm changes that.**

**Cultivator's note.** *Ceratonia siliqua* is a slow, long-lived, drought-hard Mediterranean evergreen — dioecious (or polygamous), so a fruiting stand needs a male, and grafted named cultivars are the only sane route to a predictable pod. It fruits on old wood after years, not seasons. **This is a tree you plant for a successor.** That temporal fact is not incidental to the doctrine: every tradition that used carob heavily was a tradition that stayed put.

### Preparation & Dosing

| Preparation | Traditional context | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **LBG-thickened infant formula** | Modern; descends from the Egyptian/Greek binding use | **0.35–0.5 g LBG per 100 mL** is the tested clinical range (0.4 g/100 mL, PMID 38542810; 0.42 g/100 mL, PMID 33215022; 0.35 and 0.45 g/100 mL, PMID 15097434). ### Scientific Correlation

**Headline grade: GOOD for infant regurgitation (RCT-level); MODERATE for acute paediatric diarrhoea (single trial); MODERATE for lipids; NULL for everything the doctrine implies about systemic action.**

**1. Infant regurgitation — GOOD, and the codex's best-tested traditional binding claim.**
A multi-country multi-centre RCT randomised 103 term infants with regurgitation to LBG-containing AR formula (0.4 g/100 mL, with scGOS/lcFOS and postbiotics) or unthickened control. The primary endpoint was **non-inferiority of stool consistency** — i.e. the trial was designed to test the *safety* worry, that LBG loosens stool — and the test group's stools were **not looser**. **Regurgitation frequency was significantly lower** in the LBG group (p ≤ 0.028), with adequate growth in both arms (Salvatore et al. 2024, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu16060899), PMID 38542810). A crossover RCT in 30 infants found **both** 0.45 and 0.35 g/100 mL LBG formulas cut regurgitation episodes by **~50%** versus control, with the lower concentration equally effective and easier to suck through a teat (Miyazawa et al. 2004, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/00005176-200405000-00004), PMID 15097434). A real-life observational study in **2,604** infants over one month found regurgitation frequency and volume significantly reduced (p < 0.001), **complete resolution in 48%**, reduced crying, and stools that softened but stayed within physiological range (Tounian et al. 2020, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5223/pghn.2020.23.6.511), PMID 33215022). **Grade: GOOD.**

**Now the deflation, because it is owed.** Four pooled trials of pectin/starch/LBG thickening complexes in 392 infants found the best efficacy in the formula with the **highest pectin and lowest starch** content — and the authors of the field's own review conclude that **no specific thickening agent can be recommended, for lack of comparative trials** (Dupont & Vandenplas 2019, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/apa.15015), PMID 31529540; Barbieur et al. 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/MOP.0000000000001147), PMID 35855545). **The evidence supports *thickening*. It does not establish that carob is the best thickener, or a special one.** LBG is the most studied because it got there first, and "most studied" is a fact about history, not superiority.

**2. The mechanism is viscosity, and it is not gastric emptying — an instructive null.**
Twenty Thai infants on carob bean formula showed **statistically significant improvement in vomiting quantity (p < 0.001) and frequency (p < 0.0001)** and improved weekly weight gain (121 → 221 g/week, p = 0.005) — while **gastric emptying half-time did not significantly change** (116 → 149 min, p = 0.154) (Vivatvakin & Buachum 2003, PMID 12810410 — *no DOI indexed*). **Grade: MODERATE, and the null is the point.** The gum does not speed the stomach; it makes the feed too viscous to come back up easily. Supporting this from the physics side: LBG solution viscosity is **insensitive to gastric juice exposure and to pH change** (Marciani et al. 1998, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmre.1998.1560), PMID 9799679) — the thickening survives the stomach, which is exactly what the indication requires and is not true of every thickener.

**3. Acute paediatric diarrhoea — MODERATE, single trial, and it is a good one.**
Eighty children aged 4–48 months with acute diarrhoea and mild-moderate dehydration were randomised to WHO ORS alone or ORS + carob bean juice. In the CBJ arm, **diarrhoea duration was shortened by 45%, stool output reduced by 44%, and ORS requirement decreased by 38%.** Hypernatraemia occurred in three ORS-only patients and none in the CBJ arm; no metabolic problems were attributable to CBJ. The authors' own framing — that CBJ "may have a role... after it has been technologically processed, and that further studies would be justified" — is appropriately cautious (Akşit et al. 1998, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-3016.1998.00093.x), PMID 9620567). **Grade: MODERATE.** Randomised, real endpoints, clinically meaningful effect sizes, explicit ancient-Anatolian traditional provenance stated by the investigators. **Downgraded from GOOD by:** n=80, single centre, single trial, 1998, and no replication retrieved this run. **This is a result that has been sitting unreplicated for over twenty-five years.**

**4. Lipids — MODERATE, and smaller than the headline numbers suggest.**
A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel trial gave 58 hypercholesterolaemic volunteers 15 g/day of carob pulp preparation in bread and a fruit bar for 6 weeks: **LDL cholesterol fell 10.5% (p = 0.010)**, LDL:HDL fell 7.9% (p = 0.058 — *not* significant), and triglycerides fell 11.3% in women only (p = 0.030) (Zunft et al. 2003, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00394-003-0438-y), PMID 14569404). **Grade: MODERATE** — proper design, modest effect, honest reporting.

A second double-blind placebo-controlled trial in 88 hypercholesterolaemic volunteers on 4 g twice daily of insoluble carob fibre reported far larger falls — TC −17.8%, LDL −22.5%, LDL:HDL −26.2%, TG −16.3% — **but reports them as change from baseline within the treatment arm**, noting only that the placebo group showed no significant change over time (Ruiz-Roso et al. 2010, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11130-009-0153-9), PMID 20094802). **Grade: LOW-MODERATE — downgraded specifically for the within-group framing.** A −22.5% LDL from 8 g/day of fibre would be statin-adjacent and would have been replicated to death by now. It has not been. Treat the Zunft figure as the honest one.

**Attribution caveat:** the 6-month WSDF trial reporting −6.4% TC and −10.5% LDL used a **mixture** of psyllium, pectin, guar gum *and* locust bean gum (Jensen et al. 1997, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0002-9149\(96\)00672-8), PMID 9024732), and the paediatric fibre review likewise treats LBG as one of several interchangeable soluble fibres (Kwiterovich 1995, PMID 7494671 — *no DOI indexed*). **Neither is evidence for carob.** They are evidence for viscous fibre, of which carob is an instance.

**5. The nutritive doctrine — NULL as a pharmacological claim.**
Carob pod is food. It has calories and sugar. **That is a nutritional fact, not a therapeutic finding**, and no tradition's "nutritive" grading survives translation into a pharmacological claim. Filed as null so that nobody later mistakes a macronutrient for a mechanism.

**Honest summary.** Two doctrines, two verdicts. **The binding doctrine is vindicated**, and vindicated twice over by different chemistry: tannins bind mucosa, galactomannan thickens lumen. **The nutritive doctrine is a description of a food.** The astringent–nutritive "paradox" that puzzled the classical tradition dissolves the moment you separate pulp from seed. And the mechanism the modern world actually exploits — viscosity, acid-stable, unabsorbed — is one no tradition could have named, arrived at by tasting and watching, from a tree whose seed we still weigh diamonds against for reasons that turn out to be about human eyes rather than plant physiology.

### Contraindications

**General:** a food-grade drug with a narrow but sharp set of real cautions. None of these are theoretical.

- **Preterm and low-birth-weight infants — AVOID.** This is the entry's one serious warning. The safety case for LBG rests on its **indigestibility and negligible systemic bioavailability**, and it holds for **term-born infants from birth onwards**. The same review documents **limited case reports of possible adverse effects in preterms receiving the thickener inappropriately** (Meunier et al. 2014, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yrtph.2014.06.023), PMID 24997231). Thickeners in preterms are a specialist decision, not a parental one. **[USER: verify against current national neonatal guidance before any clinical use.]**
- **Do not exceed the tested concentration.** The clinical range is 0.35–0.5 g/100 mL. Higher-concentration formula was harder for infants to suck through a teat (PMID 15097434), and the whole safety margin is concentration-defined (PMID 24997231). **More gum is not more binding; it is a feeding obstacle.**
- **Viscous fibre load — real GI consequences.** In rats, LBG among viscous indigestible polysaccharides **elevated pancreatic-biliary secretion, decreased digestibility, and enlarged digestive organs** (Ikegami et al. 1990, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jn/120.4.353), PMID 2158535). **Grade: rodent, high-dose, not directly transferable** — but it names the mechanism of the flatulence, bloating and reduced nutrient digestibility that any viscous gum will produce at load.
- **Tannins — non-haem iron.** The pulp is polyphenol-dense. Separate from iron supplements and iron-rich meals by ≥2 h. Caution in iron-deficiency anaemia. May reduce absorption of concurrently taken alkaloidal drugs.
- **Any viscous gum delays or reduces co-administered drug absorption.** Separate oral medication from a gum load. Applies to LBG as it does to guar and psyllium.
- **Oesophageal or GI stricture / dysphagia / swallowing disorder — caution.** Viscous unabsorbed gums are a bolus-obstruction hazard in a narrowed lumen. Take with adequate fluid.
- **Diarrhoea in children:** carob bean juice is an **adjunct to oral rehydration, never a substitute.** Dehydration kills; stool consistency does not. Three patients were excluded from the CBJ trial for excessive vomiting (PMID 9620567).
- **Legume cross-reactivity.** Fabaceae. Rare but reported IgE sensitisation to LBG exists as a class concern. **[USER: verify against an allergy reference; no primary study retrieved this run.]**
- **Identification.** Not confusable in the field — a large evergreen Mediterranean tree with pinnate leathery leaflets and hanging brown horn-shaped pods. **The confusion is commercial, not botanical:** "locust bean gum" (this plant's seed), "carob flour" (this plant's roasted pulp), and "carob powder" sold as a cocoa substitute are three different articles with three different chemistries, and product labelling routinely blurs them.

- **Guar (*Cyamopsis tetragonoloba*)** — the other galactomannan; the direct rheological comparator, and the control on any claim that carob's gum is special.
- **Psyllium (*Plantago ovata*)** — the viscous-fibre comparator with the far larger lipid evidence base; the measure of how modest carob's lipid effect is.
- **Tragacanth (*Astragalus gummifer*)** — the classical gum exudate; the Greco-Arabic tradition's other "gum that thickens."
- **Manna ash (*Fraxinus ornus*)** — codex; the *other* sweet Mediterranean exudate-food. Carob is sweet and binds; manna is sweet and purges. **The sugar decides.**
- **Tamarisk (*Tamarix gallica*, I_765)** — the desert astringent whose sweet exudate is the manna candidate; the counterpart entry in this batch. Both are sweet-and-astringent; only carob resolved into a validated drug.
