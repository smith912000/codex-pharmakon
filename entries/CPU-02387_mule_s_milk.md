# Mule's milk — milk of a she-mule (Sushruta Samhita, translator's footnote)
## *The milk of a she-mule, which Bhishagratna's footnote adds to Sushruta's list of milks on the strength of the construction of the original sentence; the identification as the milk of a hybrid horse-donkey is the text's plain meaning, confidence medium* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry, and the attestation is a translator's footnote.** On the project's spine (CPU-02387) only the *Sushruta Samhita* is attested for the milk of a she-mule. The row began on 13 October 2026 as an unverified placeholder from `_nonplant/nonplant_master.csv` (curated 8 October, confidence probable); this entry replaces it. The locus is Bhishagratna's English.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The locus is *Sutrasthana* ch.45 (the chapter on liquids), `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`, footnote at lines 441-444 to the milk group. The milk group opens (lines 413-416, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`): "The milk of a cow, she-goat, she-camel, ewe, she-buffalo, mare, she-elephant, or of a woman, is what generally comes to the use of man." The text then describes milk in general (lines 419-462) as "the white fluid essence of drugs and cereals ... the best of all nutritive substances", "heavy, sweet, slimy, cold, glossy, emollient, laxative and mild", and, among a long list of benefits, "rejuvenating and aphrodisiac" and one that "increases the duration of life" (lines 450-457). Those lines belong to milk as a group and are not specific to any one animal. The footnote, which is attached to the line "The milk of a cow, she-goat, she-camel, ewe, she-buffalo, mare, she-elephant, or of a woman" (line 415) but printed on the page with the discussion of the benefits of milk, reads: "From the construction of the present sentence in the original texts, we are warranted to include the milk of a doe, or of a she-mule, or of a cow-rhinoceros in the list, as they sometimes prove beneficial for external applications."

**What that means for the entry.** The footnote is the translator's own, and gives his reading of the construction of the Sanskrit sentence. The substance is named only there. A mule is the offspring of a donkey and a horse; the footnote's "she-mule" is taken at its plain meaning. The footnote says only that such milk "sometimes prove[s] beneficial for external applications"; it names no disease, gives no property of taste or potency, and no dose. The Sushruta chapters on disease and treatment (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`) were not searched for further mention of this milk. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The English is "a she-mule". Female mules are usually infertile and rarely lactate, so whether the text means a hybrid or a loosely used word for a single-hoofed animal is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The Sushruta text names no constituent. Milk is, in general, an emulsion of fat in a water solution of lactose with proteins, minerals and vitamins; composition varies by species. Because the mule is a single-hoofed animal, the passage on the milk of the Ekashapha animals (see the Mare's milk entry, CPU-02385) may be meant to cover it; the footnote does not say so and the point is not asserted here. ### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* The animal's milk appears only in a translator's footnote that treats it as an extension of the milk list for external use. The praise of milk as "rejuvenating", life-prolonging and "Ojah"-augmenting (lines 450-461) is addressed to the listed milks taken as food, and nothing in the footnote carries it over. No placement in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter was found, and no claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* is made. The sattvic, rajasic and tamasic food lists are Samkhya and later categories and are not Sushrutan.

### Contraindications

- **Not a food or medicine here.** The text does not recommend taking this milk internally.
- **Hygiene.** Raw milk of any animal can carry infection.
- **Species.** The mule is a domestic hybrid and is not a protected animal.
