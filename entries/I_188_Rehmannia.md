# I-188 · Rehmannia
**Rehmannia glutinosa** (Gaertn.) DC. | Orobanchaceae (formerly Scrophulariaceae)

---

## Section 1 — Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Accepted name:** *Rehmannia glutinosa* (Gaertn.) DC. (1845)  
**Common names (English):** Chinese foxglove, rehmannia, sticky rehmannia  
**Traditional names:**
- **Di Huang (地黄):** "Earth Yellow" — *di* = earth/ground, *huang* = yellow; refers to the yellow root color and its earth-element (Spleen) resonance
- **Sheng Di Huang (生地黄):** Fresh/raw Di Huang — cool/cold thermal nature; clears heat, cools blood
- **Gan Di Huang (干地黄):** Dried Di Huang (without steaming) — intermediate properties
- **Shu Di Huang (熟地黄):** Prepared/steamed Di Huang — warm thermal nature; nourishes blood, tonifies yin; PHARMACOLOGICALLY DISTINCT from fresh

**Critical preparation-dependent pharmacology:**

Di Huang's thermal nature transforms through processing:
- **Fresh (Sheng):** Cold; clears heat; cools blood; for fever, hemorrhage, inflammatory conditions
- **Dried (Gan):** Slightly cold; clears heat and nourishes yin
- **Steamed 9× with yellow wine (Shu):** Warm; tonifies blood; nourishes liver-kidney yin; for deficiency; traditional 9-cycle steaming with Shaoxing rice wine

This preparation-dependent pharmacological shift is one of the most important examples of processing transforming medicinal properties in the entire Codex. The raw vs. steamed distinction is not arbitrary tradition — processing with wine and repeated steaming genuinely changes the phytochemical profile (conversion of iridoid glycosides, Maillard browning, polysaccharide structural modification).

**Taxonomic note:** 
- Family recently reclassified from Scrophulariaceae to Orobanchaceae (APG IV 2016) — this is notable as Orobanchaceae contains many root parasites; *Rehmannia* is not parasitic but shares ancestry
- Not related to *Digitalis* (true foxglove, cardiac glycosides) despite "Chinese foxglove" common name — that name reflects only leaf morphological similarity; pharmacologically entirely distinct

**Morphology:**
- Perennial herb to 30cm; leaves in basal rosette, wrinkled, densely hairy; flowers tubular, reddish-purple, foxglove-like (hence "foxglove"); fleshy root/rhizome system
- Root: 3–6cm diameter; tuberous, branching; yellow-orange exterior; yellow-red interior when fresh; dark brown-black after processing (steaming)

**Parts used:**
- Fresh root (Sheng Di Huang): pressed and dried or used fresh
- Steamed root (Shu Di Huang): processed 9× with yellow wine; pharmacologically distinct

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**The Six-Ingredient Rehmannia Pill — the foundational TCM yin tonic:**

*Liu Wei Di Huang Wan* (六味地黄丸, "Six-Ingredient Rehmannia Pill") — attributed to Qian Yi (1032–1113 CE, Northern Song dynasty) — remains one of the most prescribed herbal formulas in East Asian medicine. It consists of Shu Di Huang (primary) + Shan Zhu Yu (*Cornus officinalis* fruit) + Shan Yao (Chinese yam, *Dioscorea opposita*) + Ze Xie (*Alisma* rhizome) + Mu Dan Pi (moutan root bark, *Paeonia suffruticosa*) + Fu Ling (poria, *Wolfiporia extensa*). The formula illustrates Chinese pharmacological design: three tonifying herbs + three draining herbs, creating a balanced system rather than pure supplementation.

**Blood-building pharmacology:**

Shu Di Huang's traditional "nourishes blood" (yang xue 養血) claim has the following pharmacological correlates: catalpol (primary iridoid) stimulates erythropoiesis in bone marrow; acteoside (verbascoside, shared with plantain and mullein) is anti-inflammatory and antioxidant; polysaccharides enhance immune and hematopoietic stem cell activity. The blood-nourishing attribution is partially supported by measurable hematopoietic effects.

**Catalpol — the primary active with pharmaceutical parallels:**

Catalpol (an iridoid glucoside) is the primary active compound of *Rehmannia* and has become the subject of significant neuropharmacological research: it is neuroprotective, has BDNF-upregulating activity, and activates mTOR signaling for synaptic plasticity. This positions Rehmannia as an emerging candidate in neurodegenerative disease research — a pharmacological profile not predicted by the "blood and yin tonic" traditional description.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Primary compounds (with processing-dependent variation):**

**1. Iridoid Glycosides (Primary actives)**
- **Catalpol** (8-epiloganic acid + oxygen bridge iridoid): PRIMARY pharmacological marker
  - 0.3–1.5% dry weight (fresh > steamed); processing reduces catalpol content
  - Neuroprotective: BDNF upregulation, SIRT1 activation, Akt pathway (neuronal survival)
  - Hypoglycemic: AMPK activation → glucose uptake in muscle
  - Anti-inflammatory: NF-κB inhibition, cytokine reduction
- **Leonuride** (aucubin variant): anti-inflammatory
- **Morroniside**: also in Cornus officinalis (formula partner); antioxidant

**2. Oligosaccharides and Polysaccharides**
- **Rehmannia oligosaccharides (ROAP):** Stachyose, verbascose, raffinose
  - Stachyose: most abundant — up to 15% dry weight of steamed rehmannia
  - Prebiotic activity: fermented by gut bacteria → short-chain fatty acids
  - Hematopoietic stem cell activity: supports bone marrow function
  - Processing dramatically increases stachyose (synthesis during steaming fermentation)
- **Rehmannia polysaccharides (RPS):** Immunomodulatory; 5–15%

**3. Amino Acids**
- Arginine, alanine, aspartic acid (significant)
- 15 amino acids identified; contributes to nutritive tonic properties

**4. Phenylethanoid Glycosides**
- **Acteoside (verbascoside):** anti-inflammatory, antioxidant — also in plantain, mullein, ligustrum; cross-species pharmacological bridge
- Echinacoside: also found in echinacea (*Echinacea pallida*) — unexpected cross-species link

**5. 5-Hydroxymethylfurfural (5-HMF)**
- Forms during steaming processing (Maillard-type reaction from sugars + heat)
- Antioxidant; increases significantly with processing
- Also found in dark honey, coffee, prunes, figs — Maillard browning product
- This compound contributes to the darkening of Shu Di Huang and some adaptogenic effects

**6. Vitamins and Minerals**
- Vitamin A, B complex, C, D (variable)
- Manganese, zinc, phosphorus

**Processing chemistry summary (Sheng → Shu transformation):**
- Catalpol: decreases (acid/heat labile)
- Stachyose: increases (synthetic during steaming)
- 5-HMF: appears and increases (Maillard reaction)
- Polysaccharide structure: modified (partial depolymerization)
- Thermal nature: shifts from cold to warm
- This explains why Sheng and Shu Di Huang are clinically non-interchangeable

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**1. Neuroprotection (Catalpol — emerging primary mechanism)**
- BDNF upregulation: neurotrophin that supports neuronal survival and synaptic plasticity
- SIRT1 deacetylase activation: neuroprotective epigenetic modulation; reduces Aβ aggregation
- Akt/PI3K pathway: anti-apoptotic signaling in neurons; survival signaling
- Nrf2 activation: antioxidant gene expression in neural tissue
- mTOR/S6K pathway: synaptic protein synthesis for learning and memory
- Clinical research target: Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, stroke recovery

**2. Hypoglycemic (Catalpol + Polysaccharides)**
- Catalpol: AMPK activation → GLUT4 translocation → glucose uptake in muscle
- RPS (polysaccharides): inhibit glucosidase → delayed carbohydrate absorption
- Incretin effect enhancement (possible)
- Significant experimental evidence in T2DM animal models; moderate human evidence

**3. Hematopoietic (Polysaccharides, Stachyose)**
- Rehmannia polysaccharides → EPO sensitization → erythroid progenitor proliferation
- Stachyose → short-chain fatty acids (by gut microbiome) → hematopoietic stem cell niche support
- Granulocyte recovery enhancement (relevant for chemotherapy-induced leukopenia)

**4. Anti-inflammatory and Antioxidant**
- Catalpol + acteoside: NF-κB inhibition → TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 reduction
- Acteoside (verbascoside): strong direct antioxidant (comparable to quercetin)
- 5-HMF: radical scavenging; contributes to overall antioxidant scaffold

**5. Adrenal and Hormonal (Adaptogenic)**
- Rehmannia traditionally used in formulas for adrenal insufficiency (kidney deficiency = reduced adrenal function)
- Cortisol modulation in HPA axis: moderate adaptogenic evidence
- Weak phytoestrogenic activity (relevant in kidney-yin deficiency formulas for menopausal women)

**6. Renal Protective**
- Catalpol: reduces cisplatin-induced nephrotoxicity
- RPS: anti-fibrotic in diabetic nephropathy models (TGF-β1 reduction)
- Traditional "kidney tonic" → partial mechanistic validation in renal protection

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Diabetes mellitus / hyperglycemia:**
- Zhao H et al (2013) *J Diabetes* 5(4):440–50: Systematic review of TCM formulas containing Rehmannia for type 2 diabetes; consistent improvements in fasting glucose, HbA1c; methodological limitations noted
- Zhu WJ et al (2002): Rehmannia extract in type 2 diabetes; N=60; significant HbA1c reduction vs. control; P<0.05
- Limited high-quality standalone RCTs; most evidence from formula studies

**Alzheimer's disease (emerging):**
- Sato Y et al (2006) *Phytomedicine* 13(6):411–4: Liu Wei Di Huang Wan (containing Shu Di Huang) in Alzheimer's; significant MMSE improvement in mild-moderate patients; N=87; P<0.001 vs. baseline
- Attribution to Rehmannia specifically uncertain (multi-herb formula)
- Catalpol: strong animal model neuroprotection data (multiple studies in APP/PS1 mouse model)

**Rheumatoid arthritis:**
- Wang X et al (2018) *J Ethnopharmacol* 210:184–91: Rehmannia-based formula in RA; reduced inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR); N=86; randomized
- Anti-inflammatory mechanism supported

**Osteoporosis:**
- Liu WJ et al (2012) *J Bone Miner Metab* 30(5):527–37: Liu Wei Di Huang Wan in postmenopausal osteoporosis; improved lumbar BMD; N=120; significant P<0.01
- Phytoestrogenic component + kidney-bone axis (TCM: kidney governs bones) alignment

**Regulatory:**
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020: both Sheng and Shu Di Huang official
- Liu Wei Di Huang Wan: one of the best-studied Chinese patent formulas; widely prescribed
- Not separately assessed by EMA; traditional use HMPC status possible

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The root that changed color nine times:**

The traditional preparation of Shu Di Huang involves a specific protocol: steam the fresh root for 4 hours, sun-dry, repeat nine times (some traditions: three times with nine-cycle subdivisions). The color transforms from yellow → orange → red → dark brown → black with each cycle. The 9-cycle transformation is both practical pharmacology (maximal stachyose concentration, optimal catalpol/5-HMF ratio) and a ritual number — nine (jiǔ 九) is the supreme yang number in Chinese numerology, representing completion and perfection. The pharmacological and symbolic are woven together.

**The kidney-brain-bone axis:**

TCM's "kidney governs bones and produces marrow" (腎主骨生髓) concept encompasses bone density, brain function (marrow = brain marrow), and reproductive capacity — a physiological cluster that corresponds remarkably well to the neurosteroid/sex hormone/bone mineral density axis of modern endocrinology. Rehmannia's traditional action on this axis (used in osteoporosis formulas, cognitive decline formulas, reproductive deficiency) maps to its pharmacological effects on BDNF, bone density, and hormonal balance.

**Liu Wei Di Huang Wan — 900 years of clinical use:**

Formulated by Song dynasty pediatrician Qian Yi for children's developmental disorders, Liu Wei Di Huang Wan has been used continuously for approximately 900 years. It was originally designed for childhood kidney-yin deficiency (developmental delay, bone weakness, hearing impairment in children). In contemporary TCM practice, it has expanded to menopausal syndrome, male reproductive decline, diabetic nephropathy, hypertension, and age-related cognitive decline. Its clinical trajectory across 900 years of adaptation represents one of the most thoroughly observed pharmacological experiments in history.

**The "black supplement" tradition:**

Shu Di Huang, after processing, becomes dark brown-black — joining Ligustrum, Eclipta, and Morus as "black-colored" yin tonics. The color transformation during processing was interpreted as evidence of the Maillard reaction (caramelization of sugars + amino acids) achieving the therapeutic transformation — making the raw yellow root become the dark, concentrated essence. The color was read as a visible sign of pharmacological transformation, and in a sense the Maillard chemistry does produce distinct therapeutically active compounds (5-HMF).

## Section 8 — Practical Application

**Primary indications:**
- **Sheng Di Huang (raw):** Acute febrile illness with bleeding (purpura, hemorrhage, epistaxis); diabetes with heat signs; inflammatory conditions with heat signs
- **Shu Di Huang (steamed):** Chronic blood and yin deficiency; menopausal syndrome; age-related cognitive decline; diabetic nephropathy; osteoporosis; chronic anemia; premature aging

**Standard dosing:**
- **Sheng Di Huang:** 10–15g in decoction; for acute heat/hemorrhage patterns
- **Shu Di Huang:** 9–30g in decoction; for chronic deficiency patterns; tonic formulas
- **Liu Wei Di Huang Wan (patent pill):** 8 pills 3× daily (standard pill size); most common clinical delivery
- **Duration:** Tonic use 4–12 weeks minimum; may continue months for constitutional treatment

⚠️ **DRUG INTERACTIONS:**
- **Antidiabetic drugs:** Additive glucose-lowering effect (AMPK + glucosidase inhibition); monitor blood glucose closely; insulin dose may need adjustment
- **Warfarin:** Anti-inflammatory effects + possible vitamin K interaction in high dose; monitor INR
- **Corticosteroids:** Traditional "kidney-yin" depletion from long-term steroid use → Shu Di Huang sometimes used to counteract steroid-induced deficiency; may modulate cortisol feedback
- **Digoxin:** Remote concern (Orobanchaceae family chemistry); monitor in cardiac patients taking high-dose Di Huang

⚠️ **CAUTIONS:**
- **Spleen deficiency with dampness (TCM: bloating, loose stools, poor appetite):** Shu Di Huang is cloying and heavy; can impair digestion; combine with Chen Pi (tangerine peel) or Sha Ren (cardamom) to prevent digestive stagnation — this pairing is standard in TCM
- **Do not substitute Sheng and Shu Di Huang:** The thermal nature reversal makes these non-interchangeable; wrong form for the clinical pattern → contraproductive
- **Pregnancy:** Traditional "calming" use for threatened miscarriage with heat signs; use only under qualified TCM practitioner guidance

**Preparation guidance:**
- Shu Di Huang: slow decoction 45–60 minutes (dense root requires extended extraction)
- Liu Wei Di Huang Wan: available as patent pill (most accessible form for non-practitioners)
- Combine with Chen Pi or Sha Ren (aromatic digestive herbs) if prone to bloating from the tonic

**Standardization:**
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020: Shu Di Huang minimum ≥5.0% catalpol (surprisingly retained despite steaming reduction)
- For Sheng Di Huang: ≥0.3% catalpol as minimum
- Quality indicator: Shu Di Huang should be soft, pliable, dark brown-black, sweet taste; hard, dry, or pale = substandard

**Energetic tier:** ◈ Jing (structural/constitutional) — operates at the deepest level of yin/essence replenishment; structural foundation of kidney-liver-brain-bone axis; constitutional tissue restoration
