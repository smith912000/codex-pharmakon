# Plain clay — طين مطلق (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *Ṭīn muṭlaq: clay of any place taken without a qualifier, the best being the free (*ḥurr*) clay of sun-warmed ground. Confirmed from the Arabic; it is an unspecified clay, not a named earth (confidence medium)* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *ṭīn muṭlaq* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 13586-13593).

*What it is.* الماهية: هو طين كل المواضع (13587): "it is the clay of all places". *Temperament.* الطبع: كله مبرد (13587): "all of it is cooling".

*Actions.* الخواص: مجفف جال (13587): "drying, cleansing". The free clay of sun-warmed ground dries swollen (*rahil*) bodies without stinging, because of its sticky quality, if not mixed with burnt matter such as potsherd or sun-burnt walls; it has a dissolving power, and if washed again it becomes a mild drier, balanced in hot and cold (13587-13590).

*Cosmetic.* يشد اللحم الرهل (13590-13591): "it firms flabby flesh". *Swellings:* with cerate on scrofula (*khanāzīr*) and hard swellings (13591). *Digestive organs:* dropsical and wasted people are painted with the clay of sun-warmed ground and are clearly benefited, and it heals much of the flesh (13591-13593). No preparation beyond these, source author or dose is given.

### Active Components

General fact only: clays are hydrated aluminium silicates with variable mineral content. The passage names no constituent.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Canon records ordinary clay, cooling and drying, painted on swollen or wasted bodies and on scrofulous swellings. Historical record only. The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this material.

### Preparation & Dosing

Painted on the body; with cerate (*qayrūṭī*) on swellings; washed a second time to moderate it (lines 13588-13593). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General fact only: clay applied to skin is a drying, adsorbent paste; it does not cure dropsy or wasting. Soil can carry tetanus spores and other organisms. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **CM (Avicenna).** Cooling (*mubarrid*) for all of it; drying and cleansing; the washed clay moderate in hot and cold (lines 13587-13590).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The passage (*Canon* Book II, entry *ṭīn muṭlaq*, lines 13586-13593) says nothing of innate heat, radical moisture, the *ruh*, or any life-lengthening or conserving use. It is a materia-medica entry organised by temperament and by organ. No cultivation status is attested, and none is imported.

**Practitioner's note.** A cooling, drying clay painted on the body. Nothing in the passage bears on conserving innate heat. Nothing to practise.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene.** Field clay can carry tetanus spores and other infection; never apply to broken skin.
- **Dropsy and wasting.** Medical conditions that need proper care.
- **Identity.** Unspecified clay; do not equate with a named earth.
