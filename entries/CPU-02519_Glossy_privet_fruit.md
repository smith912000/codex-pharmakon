# Glossy privet fruit — Nu Zhen Shi 女贞实 (Shennong bencao jing)

## *Ligustrum lucidum W.T.Aiton, glossy privet (the evergreen Chinese privet), here its fruit; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence, supported by the Sun Xingyan note's remarks that the tree keeps its leaves in winter* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The only text read for this entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk found *Ligustrum* only in a Unani tag list that is not a text (`CM_Avicenna/extract/spine_avicenna_tags_unmatched.csv`), not a source for this drug. I_591 (*Ligustrum vulgare*, common privet) is a different species from a different tradition and is not this entry.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 女贞实 ("nu zhen fruit") in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 女贞实 *nu zhen shi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1293). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，平 ("bitter, even"); 主补中 ("governs mending the centre"), 安五脏 ("calms the five viscera"), 养精神 ("nourishes the essence-spirit"), 除百疾 ("removes the hundred illnesses"); 久服，肥健、轻身、不老 ("taken long: plump and sturdy, lightens the body, no ageing"); habitat 生山谷 ("grows in mountain valleys"). No alias, part other than the fruit, or preparation is given.

The Mingyi bielu layer adds: 生武陵，立冬采, "grows at Wuling; gathered at the start of winter" (line 1295).

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 1297) quotes the Shuowen (桢，刚木也, "zhen is a hard wood"), the Shan hai jing's Eastern Mountains section (many zhen trees on Mount Tai), Guo Pu (it is the "nu zhen"; "its leaves do not fall in winter", 叶冬不凋), the Mao Shi and Lu Ji (a tree "like the chu", also called gou gu, with white, smooth grain, whose fruit is the "wood gadfly seed" and can be compounded into medicine), the fu of Sima Xiangru (which has nu zhen), and Yan Shigu ("the nu zhen tree is evergreen winter and summer, never losing its leaves, bitter and with integrity, hence the name"). Finally he cites Chen Cangqi (a Tang author) that it is "dongqing" (冬青, "winter green"). These are the commentators' and not the Shennong text's. The Chen Cangqi gloss is the only link in the note to the later name dongqing; the Codex does not use it to read any later text into this one. Several characters in these quotations are missing in the transcription.

### Active Components

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, even fruit that mends the centre, calms the five viscera, nourishes essence-spirit and removes "the hundred illnesses", and when taken long makes one plump and sturdy, lightens the body and prevents ageing. These are the classic's categories; the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses or effects. It gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph names the part (the fruit, 实) and the season in the Mingyi layer (start of winter), and no preparation. The classic's general preface (line 4121) says medicines differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Ligustrum lucidum* is a widely planted evergreen tree of the olive family (Oleaceae), and its fruit is used in modern Chinese pharmacy. ### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The paragraph for 女贞实 reads 味苦，平 and files the fruit in the upper grade (上品). The classic's preface (line 43) says upper medicines "nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic; taken much or long they do not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body and benefit qi, not age and extend the years, base it on the upper classic" (主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经). The fruit's own text agrees with the grade in two places: its indications include 养精神 ("nourishes the essence-spirit"), and its 久服 clause reads 久服，肥健、轻身、不老, "taken long: plump and sturdy, lightens the body, no ageing". The register is (a). This records the classic's grade and wording; it does not claim that the fruit does any of these things, and it does not translate 精神 into another tradition's terms. The Mingyi bielu adds only the place and season. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 女贞实 *nu zhen shi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1293)

**Practitioner's note.** The classic grades this as a long-use, life-nourishing fruit, and that grade is its whole cultivation status. A cultivator reading the entry has the grade and the wording to go on, and no dose, no preparation and no course length: the text gives none. Take it as a record of how the classic framed the drug, not as an instruction.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** common privet (*Ligustrum vulgare*, I_591) and other privets are not this drug; the berries of common privet are generally regarded as poisonous if eaten, so a privet must not be substituted on appearance.
- **Pregnancy and nursing:** no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given; the classic's "taken long" is not a basis for a regimen.
