# Phlomis tuberosa — lug.mur, Lugmur (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Phlomis tuberosa, the second of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for lug.mur (after "(Leucas) Phlomis kawaguchii Murata"); the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text prints no tuberosa and sets a different Phlomis, younghusbandii, against the name "Lugmur" by column position* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi, read in two modern editions, and under this binomial on Clark's edition alone.** The editions are Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, both in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. They are two translations of one tradition's text and are not two independent traditions. A search of the Dioscorides, Ebers, Assyrian, Sushruta and Shennong text on disk for *Phlomis* found only Dioscorides lines for other drugs: chapter 4-104 (line 8953), whose translators' names are *Phlomis fructiosa* (as printed) and *Phlomis floccosa* (line 8961), and chapter 3-113, *Leukas* (line 7543), whose SUGGESTED line (7544) lists "Phlomis biflora". Those are the translators' identifications for Greek drugs and are not read as this one. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (line 164) the row is keyed by the Tibetan name *lug.mur*, with the identification "(Leucas) Phlomis kawaguchii Murata; Phlomis tuberosa" and the locus "Clark 1995 (line 1062)". The spine row (CPU-01767) took the **second** binomial, a **later** name, not the first. Clark's first binomial, *Phlomis kawaguchii*, has no spine row of its own as far as the files read show. Clark's Latin index prints both (line 1626: "Phlomis kawaguchii Phlomis tuberosa") and also "(Leucas) Phlomis kawaguchii" (line 1609).

**Identity doubt.** Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance" and (line 68) that "the data given in some of these is occasionally suspect". Here the line gives two species of *Phlomis*, with a parenthesised "(Leucas)" before the first, and the commentary text then refers to the drug as "Leucas". The second edition prints a third species (below). The Tibetan name does not fix the species and *Phlomis tuberosa* is one candidate. The Codex does not choose among them. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, materia medica run (lines 1062 to 1065).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 ("The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines", line 792). The entry stands within "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997), which runs to "(8) Medicines of Animal Origin" (line 1147). It follows *snyi.wa* (line 1061) and is split by a page break (the page number "106" is on line 1063). It reads:

"lug.mur: (Leucas) Phlomis kawaguchii Murata: Phlomis tuberosa: [is also known as pi.pi.lug.mur. It has furry, yellowish-green leaves, square petioles and brown flowers. The fruit has sweet taste and cures lung disorders. Both Codonopsis and Leucas] cure chest fevers and common cold."

Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries. The second name, the description, the taste and the lung action are inside the brackets. "Cure chest fevers and common cold" is outside them and is the root-text layer as Clark's marks stand; but it is a clause shared with the preceding drug (*snyi.wa*, "Codonopsis"), so the root layer does not give this drug an action of its own. The drug's name is not translated in Clark's line. Clark's index of Tibetan names lists *lug.mur* (line 1758).

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (read through the name, not the binomial).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, chapter 20, within the run headed "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (line 3428), line 3446: "Nyiwa and Lugmur cure heart bum and the common cold." "Bum" is the printed OCR spelling and is quoted as it stands; the Codex does not correct it. The index lists "Lugmur 209" (line 7428). The glossary's "L" block prints "Lugmur" as the sixth of nine phonetic names (line 5437) and "lug mur" among its transliterations (line 5441). The scientific names (lines 5445 to 5448) put *Phlomis younghusbandii* sixth, so counted in order *Lugmur* stands against *Phlomis younghusbandii* by position (the same count is checked by the neighbours in I_1328). The glossary does not print them in one row. The Men-Tsee-Khang text prints no *Phlomis tuberosa*.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Phlomis tuberosa*, *Phlomis kawaguchii* and *lug.mur* found no entry on this plant. I_1054 (*Codonopsis convolvulacea*) quotes the same Clark lines (1061 to 1065) for the preceding drug, including the shared clause "cure chest fevers and common cold". The sibling spine row *Phlomis younghusbandii* (CPU-01768) is the next entry in this batch and covers the glossary reading. I_1327 and I_1328 (the two *Pedicularis* rows) and I_1258, I_1256, I_1255 and I_1022 print neighbouring names of the same glossary "L" block. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Phlomis* is a genus of the mint family (Lamiaceae), whose members generally have square stems, which agrees with Clark's "square petioles". Which compounds this species or the Tibetan drug holds is not shown by the lines read, so no statement about its composition is made.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a drug that cures "chest fevers and common cold" (Clark, line 1065, shared with *snyi.wa*) and "heart bum and the common cold" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3446, shared with *Nyiwa*), and Clark's commentary adds that its fruit "cures lung disorders". These are the texts' claims in their own terms, attached to this binomial only through the name, and they are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose. A fever or a lung illness needs medical care, and no herb should be used for either on the strength of this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no quantity, no frequency and no duration. Clark's commentary names the fruit as the part with the sweet taste; neither edition says which part is taken. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Clark's line 1062 prints, inside the brackets, "sweet taste" for the fruit (line 1065). No power and no post-digestive taste is printed. The Men-Tsee-Khang lines read (3446, 5437) print no taste or power. The colours and leaf description are descriptions of the plant and not qualities.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Clark's materia medica gives this drug a name, two identifications and a shared root-text clause, and the commentary layer describes it and says the fruit is sweet and cures lung disorders. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. A search of both Tibetan texts for the drug's names (*lug.mur*, *Lugmur*, *Phlomis*) found it only in the lines cited above, the glossary and the index. It is not in the rejuvenation chapter (Men-Tsee-Khang chapter 23, lines 3900 to 3950; Clark's chapter 23 is headed "Normal health", lines 1262 to 1276), and Clark's materia medica names other drugs as essence-extraction ingredients (for example lines 853, 971 and 1144), none of them this one. Its framing is therapeutic only, and it attaches to the Tibetan name and not securely to this binomial.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a chest-fever and common-cold drug in two short sentences, one of several candidate species for a Tibetan name, with no preparation and no dose. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. Fevers and lung illness need medical care; no preparation or dose is given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Because the species is one of several candidates, a plant could not be matched to the Tibetan name from this entry in any case. No conservation information was read.
