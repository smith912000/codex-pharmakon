# Rhododendron laudandum — Dali, da lis (rGyud-bzhi, Men-Tsee-Khang glossary by column position)

## *Rhododendron laudandum, the first binomial on line 5215 of the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary's "D" block, which column order sets against "Dali" (da lis); Clark 1995 prints no Rhododendron laudandum, and gives da.li only as the name of the white flower of its ba.lu* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi, read in two modern editions, and under this binomial on the Men-Tsee-Khang edition alone.** The editions are Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, both in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. They are two translations of one tradition's text and are not two independent traditions. A search of the Shennong (Yang 1998), Sushruta, Ebers and Assyrian texts on disk for *Rhododendron* found only Yang line 3387, "Yang Zhi Zhu (Flos Rhododendri Sinensis)", a drug of another species, which is not read as this one. The Dioscorides text prints "rhododendron" at line 8782 only as one of the names of the Greek drug rododaphne, which is not this plant. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (line 255) the row is keyed by the **scientific name** "Rhododendron laudandum", with the locus "Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients (line 5215)" and the note "Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)". Line 5215 prints "Rhododendron laudandum Rhododendron": the key is the **first** of the two tokens, and the second, bare "Rhododendron", is not a binomial. The English column of the block begins at line 5222 with "-", so the Codex reads the second "Rhododendron" as the English (common) name set against the same row, which is not certain. [USER: verify] Clark prints no *Rhododendron laudandum*.

**Identity doubt.** The binomial is set against the Tibetan name by column position only, as set out below. Clark's one Rhododendron entry, *ba.lu* (line 1121), prints *Rhododendron anthopogonoides* and "Rhododendron affcephalanthum (white type)", and within it gives *da.li* as the name of the flower of the white type. The two editions therefore agree on the genus and the Tibetan name and may differ on the species, and the Codex does not choose. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance" and (line 68) that "the data given in some of these is occasionally suspect". The Men-Tsee-Khang editors' note (line 686) says that its botanical terms rest on the information of its own Materia Medica Department, and it names no earlier flora. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (the "D" block).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients. The glossary prints its columns in separate runs. The phonetic names (lines 5181 to 5196) begin "Dali", "Dhambu kara", "Dhatig", "Dharyaken", "Dhawa", "Dhenda", "Dhenrog", "Dhewa" and end with "Drita sazin". The transliterations (lines 5198 to 5213) begin "da lis", "'dam bu ka ra", "da trig", "dar ya kan". The scientific names begin at line 5215 with "Rhododendron laudandum"; line 5217 reads "Hippuris vulgaris Schisandra spenanthera Lepidium apetalum Arisaema j acquemontii Croton tinglium", then "Ricinus communis L." (5218), "Corydalis melanochlora" (5219) and "Cassia fistula" (5220). The English column (lines 5222 to 5229) reads "-", "Chinese magnolia vine", "-", "Snake plant", "-", "Castor oil plant", "-" and "Golden shower tree". Counted in order, "Dali" is the first phonetic name, "da lis" the first transliteration and *Rhododendron laudandum* the first scientific name. The count is checked on three neighbours by Clark: "Dhambu kara" is second against *Hippuris vulgaris* (Clark's *hdam.bu.ka.ra*, line 1057), "Dhatig" is third against *Schisandra* (Clark's *da.trig*, line 994) and "Dharyaken" is fourth against *Lepidium apetalum* (one of the identifications Clark prints for *dar.ya.kan*, line 1011). The glossary does not print the pairing in one row, so it is by position only. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text.* Chapter 21, "the groups and methods of compounding medicines" (colophon at line 3689), line 3623 (page 221) lists "Dali" in a group: "Jamdre, Jitanga, Dali, Zira nagpo, Lalaphue, E mong, Subka, Che tsha, Tsabru tsha, Gya tsha, Gyam tsha, Ra tsha and Thai tsha are the group of medicines that cure cold disorders associated with baekan." In chapter 30, "the actual therapeutic methods" (colophon at line 4452), line 4435 (page 277 to 278 as printed) reads, in the treatment of baekan disorders: "Medicinal treatments include decoctions made of salty and hot medicinal substances, Sedu, and Dali in a powdered form with calcinated powder as the main component." The index lists "Dali 32,221,278" (line 7313). The page "32" is not located in the text by the search for the name, and the Codex does not interpret it. "Baekan" is a Tibetan term that the editors keep untranslated (note at line 686). The text gives *Dali* no taste, no part used and no single-drug action sentence in chapter 20 as far as the search showed.

*Clark 1995, read through the name, not the binomial.* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 ("The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines", line 792), within "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (heading at line 997), line 1121: "ba.lu: Rhododendron anthopogonoides: Rhododendron affcephalanthum (white type): [has three names, for example (bdud.rtsi.)da.li (name of the flower of the white type) grows on high shady hills. It has a white stem, blue leaf and a white or dark flower. The white type has sweet, bitter and astringent tastes and smooth power]. da.li cures phlegm disorders, hot and cold reactive conditions, [lung diseases, first stage oedema and hoarseness caused by any of the three humors, and is a recommended ingredient in essence-extraction compounds]." Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries, so the three names, the description, the tastes and power, and the clause on essence extraction are the commentary layer, and "(bdud.rtsi.)" and "(name of the flower of the white type)" are oral instruction in round brackets. The sentence "da.li cures phlegm disorders, hot and cold reactive conditions" is outside the brackets. In the Root Tantra, under "5. Methods of Treatment" (line 171) and "MEDICINES" (line 179), line 188 names "calcinated powders (tres.sam) e.g., of pomegranate, white flower of Rhododendron anthopogonoides" for phlegm disorders. Clark's index lists "bdud.rtsi. (da.li.)" (line 1708).

The two editions agree in kind: Clark's *da.li* is for phlegm disorders, and the Men-Tsee-Khang *Dali* is in the group for cold baekan disorders and is given in powder for baekan; Clark's line 188 names a powder too. The Codex reads *Dali* and *da.li* as one drug from the spelling and that agreement; neither text says so. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Rhododendron laudandum* and *Dali* found no entry on this plant. I_1345 (*Rhododendron anthopogonoides*, CPU-01781) is the sibling row from Clark's line 1121 (*ba.lu*), written in the same batch, and it quotes the same Clark sentence; whether the two rows are one drug is not settled by the texts, and they are not merged. I_1307 (*Lepidium apetalum*), I_1259 (*Corydalis melanochlora*), I_1274 (*Euphorbia helioscopia*) and I_1336 (*Pinus tabulaeformis*) print other binomials of the same glossary "D" block. I_1228 and I_1053 are entries on drugs in the same Men-Tsee-Khang group list (line 3623).

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Rhododendron* is a genus of the heath family (Ericaceae), and that many species are known to contain grayanotoxins in leaves and flowers. Which compounds this species or the Tibetan drug holds is not shown by the lines read, and nothing is stated here about its composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record *Dali* among the medicines that "cure cold disorders associated with baekan" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3623) and as a powder in the treatment of baekan disorders (line 4435), and Clark's *da.li* as a flower that "cures phlegm disorders, hot and cold reactive conditions" (line 1121). These are the texts' claims, attached to this binomial only through column position and spelling, and they are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources give one preparation form: *Dali* "in a powdered form" among decoction ingredients, "with calcinated powder as the main component" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 4435). The sentence gives no part used, no quantity, no frequency and no duration, and it does not say how the powder is made. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Tibetan terms ("baekan", "cold disorders", "phlegm") are not modern diagnoses or measured outcomes, and the identity rests on column position, so a study of any *Rhododendron* would not bear directly on the Tibetan drug.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** The Men-Tsee-Khang lines read (3623, 4435, 5181 to 5220) print no taste, power or post-digestive taste for *Dali*; they print its place in the group for cold baekan disorders, which is a use group and not a taste. Clark's commentary layer (line 1121) prints "sweet, bitter and astringent tastes and smooth power" for the white type of *ba.lu*, whose flower name is *da.li*; that is attached to the Tibetan name and not to this binomial.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Men-Tsee-Khang text gives this drug by what it does: it belongs to the group for cold baekan disorders and is used as a powder in baekan treatment. The only line that connects the Tibetan name to essence extraction is Clark's bracketed commentary on *ba.lu*, which says that *da.li* "is a recommended ingredient in essence-extraction compounds" (line 1121). That statement is from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentary layer and not the root-text layer; it is attached to this entry's name only by the Codex's reading of *Dali* as *da.li*; and the Men-Tsee-Khang lines read (3623, 4435) and its rejuvenation chapter (chapter 23, lines 3906 to 3950) do not repeat it. The cultivation spec locates *bcud len* inside the Four Tantras and says no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. The Codex therefore states no cultivation status for this plant. Medicine is the third of four treatment branches in this tradition, supporting practice without reaching its root, and the framing here is therapeutic.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a baekan drug in a group list and a powder in one treatment sentence, with no part used and no dose, and its Latin name rests on column position. A later commentary's note on essence extraction belongs to a trained physician of the tradition and not to a reader of this entry. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. It is a general fact that *Rhododendron* leaves and flowers of several species are poisonous and that honey from their nectar has caused poisoning; this is not shown for this species in the lines read. The identity is open, the method of making the powder is not given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No safety information for pregnancy was read.
