# Euphorbia pallasii — thar.nu (Clark 1995), Tharnu / Thamu (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011) (rGyud-bzhi); printed "Euphorbia pallassii"

## *Euphorbia pallasii, spelled "Euphorbia pallassii" in Clark 1995, where it is the first of four Latin names printed for the Tibetan drug thar.nu (line 1134) and is named again, by itself, in the purgation class (line 1225); the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text prints no Latin name for the drug in its running text, and its glossary sets another spurge against the same Tibetan name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi (Four Tantras), read in two modern translations, Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011. The binomial *Euphorbia pallasii* is Clark's alone; the Men-Tsee-Khang text names the drug only by its Tibetan name. The botanical identity is open.** Both texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Sushruta texts on disk found no *Euphorbia pallasii*: Yang 1998 prints "Li Shi (Semen Iridis Pallasii)" at line 2961, which is an *Iris* and not this plant, and Dioscorides names other *Euphorbia* species. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (line 212) the row is keyed by the Tibetan name *thar.nu*, with the identification "Euphorbia pallassii; Baliospermum sinulatum; Euphorbia wallichiana; Croton tiglium", the locus "Clark 1995 (line 1134)" and the note "more than one Latin name given; key uses the first". The spine binomial is the first of the four Latin names, so the key is the first name. The spelling is normalised from the printed "pallassii" (lines 1134, 1225 and the Latin index at line 1583) to "pallasii"; the other three names are Clark's alternatives for the same Tibetan drug, and none is an entry of its own except as noted under Overlap.

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, chapter 20, "Specific Section", "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (heading at line 997; chapter heading at line 792).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, line 1134, directly after *dur.byid* (line 1133): "thar.nu: Euphorbia pallassii: Baliospermum sinulatum: Euphorbia wallichiana: Croton tiglium: [has eight names. It has a thick, heavy stalk and taproot, and red flower, petiole and seed. Euphorbia has hot taste, removes stones and pustules and in calcinated form is anti-emetic.] Jatropha and Euphorbia purge all hot and cold disorders." Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries. Inside the brackets, the commentary layer, are the eight names, the description of the plant, the hot taste, the removal of stones and pustules and the anti-emetic use in calcinated form. Outside the brackets, as Clark's marks stand, are the Tibetan name, the four Latin names, which are Clark's identifications and not the root text's (his preface, line 83, says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance"), and the sentence "Jatropha and Euphorbia purge all hot and cold disorders". That sentence closes the pair of entries *dur.byid* and *thar.nu*, so it is shared with the drug above it. Clark's name index lists *thar.nu* (line 1703).

*Clark 1995, chapter 21, "The Principles of Medicines: Compounding of Medicines" (heading at line 1205).* Line 1225: "The category of medicines inducing purgation includes (long-tipped) Chebulic myrobalan, Castor Oil seed, Cassia pods, Euphorbia sp., Physic nut (Baliospermum montanum), Euphorbia pallassii, Rheum emodi (a type of rhubarb), Lactuca dissecta (a type of larkspur), Physalis peruviana, Rheum nobile, Stellera chamaejasme and chhu.rtsa (Rheum spiciforme)." This is the one place where the spelling *Euphorbia pallassii* stands alone in Clark's running text, as the sixth of twelve purgatives. The parentheses in the sentence are Clark's marks for glosses from the oral teachings of his two teachers (preface, line 82).

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (read through the Tibetan name, not the binomial).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. In chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents line 225), line 3495, page 211: "Dhurji and Tharnu purge all forms of hot and cold disorders." In chapter 21, "The Group and the Method of Compounding Medicine" (contents line 229), line 3661, page 223: "Arura, Dhenda, Dhong ga, Shri khenda, Dhurji, Thamu, Chum tsa, Ngonbu, Tronbu, Tsi takmo, Rechagpa and Chhu tsa are the group of medicines that are purgatives." The index lists "Thamu 211, 223". The text gives no Latin name for the drug. "Tharnu" (line 3495) and "Thamu" (line 3661, the glossary, the index) are read here as one name in two OCR forms, because the transliteration is "thar nu" and the drug stands beside Dhurji in both lines; this is a reading of the letters *m* and *rn* and not a printed correction. [USER: verify]

*The two purgative lists agree in order.* Clark's list at line 1225 has twelve items and the Men-Tsee-Khang list at line 3661 has twelve. The sixth item is *Euphorbia pallassii* in one and "Thamu" in the other; the fifth is Clark's "Physic nut (Baliospermum montanum)", a drug whose entry *dur.byid* is immediately above *thar.nu* in Clark, and "Dhurji" in the other; the twelfth is *chhu.rtsa* in one and "Chhu tsa" in the other. The agreement of position is the Codex's reading and neither text states it. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (the "T" block, lines 5858 to 5982).* The glossary prints its columns in separate runs. The phonetic names include "Thangtrom" (line 5887), "Tharam" (5888) and "Thamu" (5893), the transliterations "thang khrom" and "tha rams" (5890 to 5891) and "thar nu" (5895), and the scientific names, from line 5928, include "Plantago depressa" (5960) and "Euphorbia wallichii" (5964, with "Wallichi's spurge" at 5966). Counted in order, "Thamu" is the eleventh phonetic name and "thar nu" the eleventh transliteration, and the glossary's scientific name for it, by position, is *Euphorbia wallichii*, with *Plantago depressa* set against "Tharam" (which Clark's *tha.ram* confirms). The glossary therefore prints a different spurge from Clark's first name for the same Tibetan drug. The pairing is by column position and is not printed in a row. [USER: verify]

**Identity: Clark's four names against the glossary's one.** Clark's list (*Euphorbia pallassii*, *Baliospermum sinulatum*, *Euphorbia wallichiana*, *Croton tiglium*) crosses two genera of the spurge family, and the glossary prints *Euphorbia wallichii*. Whether *wallichii* and *wallichiana* name one species the Codex has not checked. The row stands for the Tibetan drug and not for one species, and the Codex does not choose among the names. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Euphorbia pallasii*, *pallassii*, *thar.nu*, *Tharnu* and *Thamu* found no entry on this binomial. **I_1276** (*Euphorbia wallichii*, tag FMT) is an entry on the same Tibetan drug, keyed to the glossary's binomial, and rests on the same Clark line (1134) and the same Men-Tsee-Khang lines (3495, 3661). The two are counted separately because their spine keys differ; they are not merged. **I_1304** (*Jatropha glandulifera*, *dur.byid*, Clark line 1133) and **I_106** (*Euphorbia fischeriana*, tags FMT and TCM) are entries on the neighbouring drug, and **I_1306** (*Lactuca dissecta*), **I_1333** (*Physalis peruviana*) and **I_1343** (*Rheum spiciforme*) quote the same purgation sentence (line 1225) for their own plants. **I_196** (Purging Croton, *Croton tiglium*, tags FMT, SBJ) is an entry on Clark's fourth Latin name; I did not find *thar.nu* in it. **I_1274** (*Euphorbia helioscopia*) and **I_1275** (*Euphorbia stracheyi*) are the other Tibetan spurge rows; the Dioscorides spurges (I_221, I_1083 to I_1087) rest on another text. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Euphorbia* is a large genus of the spurge family (Euphorbiaceae), and its species are generally known for a milky latex that irritates skin and mucous membranes; *Baliospermum* and *Croton*, the other genera among Clark's names, belong to the same family. That is a statement about the genus and family, not a finding of the Tibetan texts, which do not say which plant, which part or which preparation is meant. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record one action for this drug: it purges. Clark has "Jatropha and Euphorbia purge all hot and cold disorders" (line 1134) and lists *Euphorbia pallassii* among the medicines "inducing purgation" (line 1225); the Men-Tsee-Khang text has "Dhurji and Tharnu purge all forms of hot and cold disorders" (line 3495) and lists "Thamu" among the purgatives (line 3661). That is the tradition's own regimen and not a Codex protocol. The identity is open and the drug is a purgative, so the Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

Clark's commentary says that the drug "in calcinated form is anti-emetic" (line 1134, inside the brackets, so the commentary layer); no method of calcination is given. Neither text names a part, a form or a compound for the purgative use. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The drug is in the rGyud-bzhi as a purgative: it is named in the materia medica (Clark line 1134; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3495) and in the purgative group of the compounding chapter (Clark line 1225; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3661). Its place is therapeutic. The rejuvenation chapter (chapter 23: Clark lines 1264 to 1276; Men-Tsee-Khang lines 3904 to 3945, contents line 235) calls for internal cleansing before the course, "without such cleansing" the course would be "fruitless, like dyeing a stained cloth" (Clark line 1270) and "of no benefit" (Men-Tsee-Khang line 3918); the compound it names for that cleansing is the three myrobalan fruits with other drugs, and the drug of this entry is not in it. No line read assigns the drug any status in rejuvenation or in *bcud len* (essence extraction). The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this drug on the strength of the texts. It is a purgative of the physician's course, and the one thing the rejuvenation chapter says about purging is that cleansing comes before the course, by other compounds. Nothing here supports taking a spurge, which may be a strong irritant, on the strength of this entry.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for the drug. Many spurges are skin and mucous-membrane irritants and several are toxic when eaten; as the identity is open, no plant should be gathered, handled or taken on the strength of this entry. The texts' own regimen puts purgation under a physician. No conservation information and no safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
