# Magnetite — Ci Shi (Yang 1998) / Magnes (Dioscorides) / khab.len, lodestone (Clark 1995) / magnet, Ayaskanta (Bhishagratna)
## *Magnetite, magnetic iron oxide (Fe3O4), the natural lodestone; the translators gloss it "Magnetitum" (Yang), "Magnetite — Magnetic Iron Oxide" (Dioscorides) and "Lodestone: Magnetic Ore" (Clark)* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

### Origin & Tradition

The blocking warning is in force: no identification, tablet number or deity is offered. The tag rests on the spine row. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 5-148 MAGNES (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10603), translator's heading "Magnetite — Magnetic Iron Oxide": "That lodestone which draws iron easily is the best, with an azure colour [blue], and thick but not too heavy. It is able to draw out thick fluids if thirty grains are given with honey and water." A parenthesis then records reports ("They say that this can discern a woman who is chaste from one that plays the adulteress with another man", and that it, carried by two men, "frees them from all strife and causes harmony", and "when worn on the chest it soothes people"). Dioscorides reports these with "they say" and does not vouch for them. The entry is among the "metallic stones".

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No Avicenna locus was found. `_sources/avicenna.csv` has no magnetite or lodestone row (its mineral rows are Borax, Iron, Mercury and Salt) and no Avicenna text is on disk. No *Canon* chapter reference exists to give. The tag rests on the spine row. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` lines 1175-1178, p.30), under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class": "Ci Shi (Magnetitum) is acrid and cold. It mainly treats generalized impediment due to cold dampness, pain in the limb joints, inability to grip things, and continual soreness and aching. It eliminates great fever, vexatious fullness, and deafness. Its other name is Xuan Shi (Red Stone). It is produced in mountains and valleys." Yang's note 58 (line 1196) adds, in the translator's voice: "This medicinal is able to boost the kidney qi and supplement the essence and marrow. Therefore, it is used to treat kidney vacuity deafness and blurred vision." That note describes later use and is not part of the classic's entry.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Bhishagratna renders *Ayaskanta* as "magnet" (Vol. I, *Sutrasthana* ch.27, the chapter on extracting splinters, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch27.txt` line 23, among the means of extracting a loose *Shalya*: "by applying a magnet (Ayaskanta)"; and line 87, "A loose, unbarbed arrow, lodged in a wound with a broad mouth and lying in an Anuloma direction, should be withdrawn by applying a magnet to its end"). In ch.7, among the minor surgical accessories (*upa-yantras*), "a magnet" stands with rope, a hammer, the palms of the hands and "fire, and medicine" (`SS_Sutra_ch07.txt` line 190). The text does not say lodestone or magnetite, and no taste, potency or internal use is printed for it. The group lists of ch.38 and ch.46 do not contain it.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Explanatory Tantra ch.20, stone medicines (Clark 1995, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 823): "khab.len: Lodestone: Magnetic Ore: [This has four names and also is of four types: hgugs.byed, gchod.byed, hbigs.byed and skor.byed. It is of superior, medium and ordinary qualities. The superior type is found to the south of Mount Meru, where there is a lion shaped rock of lodestone. Therefore the needle of the compass points north. Medium quality lodestone comes from China and can hold ten needles together in a chain. The ordinary type is dark brown and can attract needles.] Lodestone expels arrowheads [bullets, shrapnel, etc.] through the digestive tract and cures disorders of the brain, bones and channels." The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3352, p.198) gives: "Khablen expels foreign bodies and cures disorders of the brain, bone and nerves." Clark's iron entry (line 807) adds that iron "comes from iron-ore, lodestone, mdung.rtse rock and from animals".

### Active Components

Magnetite is the mixed iron oxide Fe3O4, a black, strongly magnetic mineral; a "lodestone" is magnetite that is naturally magnetised. It contains iron in two oxidation states. It is practically insoluble and is not a standardised source of anything for the body.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources use the stone for its attracting property: Sushruta as a surgical aid to withdraw an iron arrowhead, Tibetan medicine to "expel arrowheads" through the gut, Dioscorides to "draw out thick fluids", the Chinese classic for impediment and heat. A swallowed magnet or powder gives no sourced benefit, and swallowing several magnets or magnetic fragments is dangerous (see Contraindications). The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation.

### Preparation & Dosing

DMM 5-148: "thirty grains are given with honey and water" (a quantity as printed, no unit named for "grains" in the translation). SBJ, SS and FMT print no preparation or dose for it. Apart from the DMM line, no dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Magnetite is a magnetic iron oxide, and small magnets attract each other and iron objects through the gut wall; magnet ingestion injuries are a recognised emergency. No clinical studies were retrieved for the traditional uses. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian (AT).** *(c) Absent, no locus.* Nothing was read. The blocking warning is in force. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. He records the amuletic reports about the stone (a test of chastity, harmony between carriers, comfort when worn on the chest) without endorsing them; recording is not endorsing. What 5-148 offers a cultivator is the discipline of attention: the best lodestone is judged by how readily it draws iron, by colour and by weight. No cultivation status is assigned.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent, and the attestation rests on a spine row.* No magnetite row or text was found. Innate heat and radical moisture cannot be tied to the stone without a source and are not tied to it here. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: middle grade.* Ci Shi stands under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class" (Yang p.30). The preface (lines 348-400) says medium-class medicinals "mainly nurture personality", "may or may not be toxic", to be weighed "before putting them to their appropriate use", for those who "intend to control disease, supplement vacuity, and replenish exhaustion". The classic's own text for Ci Shi lists only joint, heat and deafness indications, with no longevity or body-lightening line. Yang's note 58 on kidney qi, essence and marrow is the translator's note on later use; the classic's text does not assert it, and no *jing* action is asserted here.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* The magnet (*Ayaskanta*) is an instrument, not a drug, in the lines read: it withdraws a loose arrow (*Sutrasthana* ch.27) and is listed among minor accessories (ch.7). No *rasayana* or *vajikarana* placement, and no *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim, is printed. Whether *Ayaskanta* is natural magnetite is the translator's rendering, not stated in the text.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed.* Lodestone is a therapeutic stone medicine of ch.20: it expels foreign bodies through the digestive tract and treats disorders of the brain, bones and channels (Clark line 823; MTK line 3352). Medicine is the third of the four treatment branches and supports practice. No *bcud len* use and no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice was found for it.

**Practitioner's note.** Of the six traditions tagged, one (Chinese) grades the stone as a middle-grade drug for impediment, heat and deafness; the others use or report it for its attracting property. Nothing sourced supports taking magnetite for cultivation, and the Greek amulet reports are reports. A cultivator has nothing to do with it beyond reading the record. The Greek line gives "thirty grains ... with honey and water" as a quantity, not as advice. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Do not swallow magnets or magnetic stones: two or more can pinch bowel wall between them and cause perforation, which is an emergency. Powdered magnetite is not a standardised drug. Keep magnetic stones away from implanted devices such as pacemakers. Amuletic uses (chastity test, harmony charm) are recorded as reports only.
