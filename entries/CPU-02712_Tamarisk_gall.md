# Tamarisk gall — جوز الطرفاء (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II; Persian *kazmāzak*)

## **Jawz al-ṭarfāʾ* 'tamarisk nut', which the Canon equates with the *kazmāzak*; the modern identification is the woody gall borne by tamarisk (*Tamarix* sp.). The identification is ours, from the Arabic name and the standard modern reading of *jawz al-ṭarfāʾ* as the gall, and is of medium confidence; the Canon text does not itself say 'gall' in the lines read* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms tamarisk galls are rich in tannins, which fits the astringent actions listed. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Stopping bleeding, toothache (gargle with vinegar), and hardness of the spleen (decoction).
- **What the text does not say.** Any dose, and the tree the gall comes from.

The Codex does not translate the Canon's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this material.

### Preparation & Dosing

A gargle with vinegar and a decoction in water and vinegar are the only preparations given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only: galls are plant growths caused by insects or mites and are often very high in tannins, which are astringent. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]
