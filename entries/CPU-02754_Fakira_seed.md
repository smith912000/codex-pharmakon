# Fakira seed — فاكرة (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *Fākira: a seed like a chickpea, with a grain like mahlab and, inside, a black grain like hemp seed, brought from al-Safāla. The plant is not identified, and the place name is read here as Sofala on the East African coast only as a guess (confidence low)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk, so far as searched.** This is a single-text entry. The spine row attests Avicenna alone (Canon Book II, heading *fākira*, Arabic text line 16877 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`).

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *fākira* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 16877-16881).

*What it is.* حب يشبه الحمص له حب كالمحلب وفي جوفه حب أسود كالشهدانج يحمل من السفالة (16878-16879): "a seed resembling the chickpea (*ḥimmaṣ*); it has a grain like *maḥlab*, and in its hollow a black grain like *shahdanaj*; it is brought from *al-safāla*". *Maḥlab* is the kernel of the mahaleb cherry and *shahdanaj* is hemp seed; both glosses are ours. *Al-Safāla* is a place name; Sofala is a possible reading and is not given by the text. [USER: verify]

*Temperament and actions.* الطبع: حارة يابسة في الثالثة (16879): "hot and dry in the third". الخواص: فيها تحليل وقبض (16879): "in it is dissolving (*taḥlīl*) and astringency".

*Digestive organs.* يدخل في الأدوية المصلحة للمعدة والكبد الباردتين وينفع من سوء الاستمراء البارد (16880-16881): "it enters the medicines that set right the cold stomach and liver, and benefits cold bad digestion".

*Excretory organs.* ينفع من الإسهال البارد ويعقل البطن (16881): "it benefits cold diarrhoea and binds the belly". No source author, no preparation and no dose are given.

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Canon records a hot, dry, astringent imported seed for cold conditions of stomach and liver and for cold diarrhoea. The Codex gives no protocol, cannot identify the seed, and does not recommend it.
