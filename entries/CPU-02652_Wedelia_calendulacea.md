### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry, and it is short by necessity: the only attestation found is the Appendix glossary of Bhishagratna's English translation (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`). A search of the running text of the Samhita (all chapter files and the three volume files) for the Sanskrit name Markava, and for *Wedelia*, returned the glossary lines only. A search of the other texts on disk found nothing. The spine row carries the Latin name from the glossary column as its name.

**How the identification was checked.** The glossary "APPENDIX" prints Sanskrit names on one leaf and identifications after them. The name block of leaf 73 runs (OCR spellings): "Magadhi", "Maha-koshataki", "Maha-nimba", "Maha-tikta", "Maha-valli", "Maha-vriksha", "Makushtha", "Malati", "Mallika", "Mana" and "Manaka" (bracketed together), "Mandara", "Manduka-parni", "Manduki", "Manjishtha", "Mansi", "Marich", "Matrisha", "Markati", "Markava" (line 32239 region), "Maruvaka", "Masha", "Masha-parni", "Masura", "Matulunga", "Mesha-shringi", and so on. The identification block (lines 32262 onward) runs "See Pippali", "Luffa Pentandra", "Melia Azedarach", "Andrographis Paniculata", "Sida Rhomboidea", "See Snuhi", "Phaseolus Aconitifolius", "Aganosma Caryophyllata; Jasminum Grandiflorum", "Jasminum Sambac", "Arum Indicum", "See Paribhadra", "Hydrocotyle Asiatica", "See Brahmi", "Rubia Cordifolia", "Nardostachys Jatamansi", "Piper Nigrum, Black pepper", "Amaranthus oleraceus", "See Suka-simbi", "Wedelia Calendulacea" (line 32308), "Ocymum Caryophyllatum", and so on. Read in step, *Magadhi* is paired with the cross-reference "See Pippali"; *Markati* with "See Suka-simbi"; *Markava* with *Wedelia calendulacea*; and *Maruvaka* with *Ocimum caryophyllatum*. The pairings that can be checked from other evidence in the same leaf (Magadhi and Pippali; Manjishtha and Rubia; Marich and black pepper; Matulunga and Citrus medica) all line up, which supports the position reading. The Sanskrit name is garbled in the OCR text ("M££rkava"), so the reading "Markava" is ours. [USER: verify] The match is a position-based reading of an OCR'd column and not a printed pairing. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Wedelia calendulacea* is a creeping herb of South Asian wet places and is related to the herb widely called false daisy, which is why the two names are often confused. The classic's use of any name for it is not shown in the lines read. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]
