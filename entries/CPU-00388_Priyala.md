# Priyala — Piyala, Piyalam (Sushruta, Bhishagratna 1907; spine spelling "Priyala")

## *Buchanania Latifolia* (as printed against "Piyala" in Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 glossary; the spine row carries *Buchanania lanzan* Spreng.) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** The spine spells the name *Priyala* and names the tree *Buchanania lanzan*. Every Sushruta passage found prints *Piyala* (once *Piyalam* as a noun with its ending), and no passage read prints *Priyala*. The Latin printed against it in the text on disk is *Buchanania latifolia*. The two binomials are generally treated as names of one tree, but neither source read says so, so the link is partly an inference. [USER: verify] The separate Sushruta name *Priyangu* (Appendix 3 pairs it with "Aglaia Roxburghiana.", line 32589) is a different drug and is not treated here. No earlier Volume I entry for this tree was found.

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

*The gana (Sutra ch.38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`).* In the Nyagrodhadi group (lines 344 to 351) the list reads "... Chorakapatra, the two sorts of Jamvu, Piyala, Madhuka (Maula), Rohini, Vanjula ..." (line 347). The group's metrical text (lines 353 to 360) says it is beneficial in ulcer, cures disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones, is astringent (*Sangrahi*), is curative in haemoptysis, is "an anti-fat", and "assuages the burning sensation of the body". Membership of the group is a grouping by therapeutic action, grade (b) at most.

*Appendix 3 glossary (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`).* The name is printed as "Piyjtla" (OCR, line 32525) in the Sanskrit column, with "Plaksha" on the next line (line 32527). The page's Latin column is a separate block. Counted down by position, "Pita-Sala" pairs with "Pterocarpus Marsupium." (line 32580), "Piyala" with "Buchanania Latifolia." (line 32581), and "Plaksha" with "Ficus Infectoria." (line 32582). The count was checked from the ends of the block and agrees. The pairing is by OCR column position, not by a printed link.

*Passages read, in the order of the book.*

- **Sutra ch.42** (`SS_Sutra_ch42.txt`, the chapter on the specific properties of flavours; lines 291 to 300), the sweet group: "Madhura Groups: — The drugs forming the groups known as the Kakolyadi-Gana, as well as thickened milk, Ghee, lard, marrow, Shali and Shashtika rice ... Ankalodya, Piyala, Pushkara, Vijaka, Kashmarya, Madhuka (Moula), Draksha, Kharjura ... are generally included within the Madhura group." Piyala is listed as a sweet substance.
- **Sutra ch.46** (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, the chapter on food and drink), the fruit group: Piyala is named in the list of fruits at lines 985 to 991. At lines 1084 to 1085 the text reads "The Piyalam is heavy, cooling and spermatopoietic." "Spermatopoietic" is Bhishagratna's word for the original term, and the same word is used for the Amalaka fruit at lines 1020 to 1021 of the same chapter. At lines 1333 to 1335, in the passage on the kernels of fruits, "The pith of the Piyala fruit has a sweet taste, is spermatopoietic and subdues the Vayu and Pittam."
- **Chikitsa ch.2** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch02.txt`, lines 486 to 496), the chapter on traumatic wounds: an oil cooked with a long list of drugs including "the tender leaves of Kshiri trees, Piyala seeds, raw and tender Tinduka fruit, or with as many of them as would be available, should be regarded as a good healing remedy in respect of all non-malignant traumatic sores or ulcers."
- **Uttara ch.40** (`SS_Uttara_ch40.txt`, lines 607 to 611), diarrhoea: "The discharge of blood (in a case of Atisara) may be stopped by a potion consisting of the barks of Piyala, S'ahnali, Plaksha, S'allaki, and of Tinis'a pasted together with milk and mixed with honey." (OCR spellings: the file prints "Piydla" and "S'dhnali".)

The word "spermatopoietic" in Sutra ch.46 is a property of the fruit as food, in a chapter on food, and it is not a *vajikarana* chapter. No rasayana or vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) was found to name Piyala, so no grade (a) status is claimed.

### Active Components

Fruits of trees of this kind are generally known to contain fats and starches in the kernel, and the barks are generally known to contain tannins, which are astringent. The composition of the tree named in the text was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Sushruta records Piyala as a sweet substance, a fruit and kernel that is cooling and "spermatopoietic", a seed in a wound oil, and a bark in a bleeding-diarrhoea potion. These are the text's own claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol for this tree.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted above: seeds in an oil for wounds (Chikitsa ch.2) and bark pasted with milk and honey for bleeding diarrhoea (Uttara ch.40). No quantity is given for Piyala in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** Sutra ch.46 prints "heavy, cooling and spermatopoietic" for the Piyala fruit (lines 1084 to 1085) and "a sweet taste", "spermatopoietic" and "subdues the Vayu and Pittam" for its pith (lines 1333 to 1335). Sutra ch.42 puts it in the Madhura (sweet) group. No *vipaka* is printed for it in the lines read, and "cooling" is the nearest the text comes to *virya*.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Piyala is a sweet substance in the flavour chapter, a member of the Nyagrodhadi group, a fruit and kernel described in the food chapter as cooling and "spermatopoietic" (the translator's word), and a seed or bark in wound and diarrhoea remedies. The Sushruta frame for it is therapeutic and dietetic. The ch.38 gana is a grouping by action, not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. Nothing read ties Piyala to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*, and none of the rasayana or vajikarana chapters searched (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) names it. The status is unattested, and the identification with *Buchanania lanzan* rests on an OCR-paired appendix that prints a different binomial. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this tree. In Sushruta it is a sweet, cooling fruit and kernel and a seed or bark in remedies for wounds and bleeding diarrhoea. The abstentive answer is the correct one: any use is a matter for a qualified practitioner, and the text supplies no long-term regimen.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. The wound and bleeding-diarrhoea uses above are conditions needing medical care, and none of them is advice. Tree seeds and kernels can provoke allergy in people sensitive to nuts, so a kernel food should be avoided by anyone with a tree-nut allergy. Do not use medicinally in pregnancy. The name is uncertain between the spine and the text, and it should not be used to buy or harvest material.
