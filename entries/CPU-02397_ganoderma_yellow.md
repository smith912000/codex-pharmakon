# Ganoderma, yellow — Huang Zhi 黃芝 / 黄芝 (Shennong bencao jing); alias Jin Zhi 金芝 ("Gold Ganoderma")

## *One of the six colour-named "zhi" 芝 of the Shennong classic, a fungus (the zhi are the polypore "immortal herb" of the Chinese tradition); the species is not settled. The pre-filled guess "Ganoderma aureum" is only Yang 1998's Latin-form colour label ("Ganodenna Aurea", OCR-damaged), not a published species, and no text on disk names a species for this drug. Identification: fungus of the zhi group, by the Chinese name and Yang's label; species unresolved; confidence low* | *(Volume IV — Fungal/Algal, shelved in Volume I)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Yellow zhi is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 English edition (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) and a plain e-text (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_plain_daizhige.txt`) are read as second and third witnesses. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "ganoderma, yellow", taken from the name list at `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` (row "Huang Zhi", "Herbs: Superior Class, PDF p.36 (line 1396)", Latin label "Ganodenna Aurea"). The line reference 1396 is a line of Yang's text, which is where this drug's paragraph begins.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), Sun Xingyan edition.** The entry is 黃芝 in the upper classic (卷一 上经), `Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 629 to 633. The paragraph reads 味甘，平 ("sweet, balanced"); 主心腹五邪 ("governs the five evils of the heart and abdomen"); 益脾气 ("boosts spleen qi"); 安神 ("quiets the spirit"); 忠信和乐 ("loyalty, trustworthiness, harmony and joy"); 久食，轻身、不老、延年、神仙 ("taken for a long time: lightens the body, no ageing, prolongs years, immortal-spirit"); and the alias 一名金芝 ("one name Jin zhi, Gold zhi"). The Mingyi bielu layer (line 643) says of the six zhi together that 黄芝，生嵩山 ("yellow zhi grows on Mount Song") and that 六芝，皆无毒，六月、八月采 ("all six zhi are non-toxic, gathered in the sixth and eighth months"). Sun's long note (案, line 645) quotes the *Baopuzi*, "Xian yao pian" (as Sun cites it), for the colours of the zhi, and for yellow it reads 黄者，如紫金 ("the yellow is like purple gold"); line 647 adds that all of them are 光明洞彻，如坚冰也 ("bright and clear through, like hard ice"). That description is the only account of how the yellow zhi looks in the sources read, and it is a quotation in a later commentary, not the classic. Sun notes at line 639, after the purple zhi, that the old text had "six kinds" which he has merged (旧作六种，今并).

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (lines 1396 to 1423, across his pages 36 and 37; his OCR is badly damaged here): "Huang Zhi (Ganoderma Aurea) is sweet and balanced. It mainly treats the five evils in the heart and abdomen, boosts the spleen qi, quiets the spirit, and [cultivates] loyalty, honesty, gentleness, and a carefree mind. Protracted taking may make the body light, prevent senility, and prolong life so as to make one an immortal. Its other name is Jin Zhi (Gold Ganoderma). It grows in mountains and valleys." The "grows in mountains and valleys" clause (生山谷) is also in the plain e-text, `Shennong_plain_daizhige.txt` line 109, which gives the same paragraph. Yang's footnote 80 (line 1405 onward) is modern commentary on "the five evils" and lists several readings, and concludes that "probably" the vacuity, repletion, bandit, mild and regular evils are meant. Yang's footnote 79 (line 1400) says: "In this book, Ganoderma is divided into six types depending on its color. Because Ganoderma is one of the best medicinals, it is called Immortal Weed. It is not only able to prevent and treat disease but is said to even cultivate virtues." That is Yang's commentary, not the classic's wording.

**Where the drug is not found.** A search of the other texts on disk for the Chinese and pinyin names found nothing further for this drug. The red zhi (Chi Zhi) is also named in the rGyud-bzhi material and has its own entry (I_110); that attestation does not extend to yellow zhi.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The five evils in the heart and abdomen; it boosts spleen qi and quiets the spirit, and the paragraph names the qualities 忠信和乐 (loyalty, trustworthiness, harmony, joy) beside those actions.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no way of eating it, and no species. The classic describes no plant.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms (the five evils, spleen qi, "spirit") into modern diagnoses. Nobody should forage or buy an unidentified fungus because of a classical name: the identity of the yellow zhi is not established, and wild fungi can be poisonous. No protocol follows from this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic says 久食 ("taken for a long time") and says nothing of preparation. The only handling statement is the Mingyi layer's harvest note for all six zhi, gathered in the sixth and eighth months (line 643). The preface to the classic (Sun line 43 for the upper class; Yang's preface lines 352 to 358) says that upper drugs may be taken in large amounts and for a long time without harming people. That is a statement about the class, and it is not a dose for this fungus. ### Scientific Correlation

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* Yellow zhi stands in the upper classic (上经), whose preface (Sun line 43) reads 上药一百二十种，为君，主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经: the upper drugs are "sovereigns, mainly nourishing life (养命) to correspond with heaven, non-toxic; taken in large amounts and for a long time they do not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body (轻身), boost qi, not age (不老) and prolong years (延年), base [the effort] on the upper classic". The drug's own paragraph carries the life-nourishing promise: 久食，轻身、不老、延年、神仙 (Sun line 631; Yang line 1396 onward; plain e-text line 109). The grade is the Sun edition's and Yang places the drug in the same class (Herbs: Superior Class), so there is no difference to state. The paragraph also names a quality of character, 忠信和乐, as an effect. *Qing shen* 輕身 is the tradition's own phrase and is not to be read as weight loss or as any pharmacological effect. This attestation is for the yellow zhi named in the classic, and it does not establish that any mushroom now sold or foraged is that zhi. The Neidan (inner alchemy) reading of the zhi is an overlay that cultivators bring and is not a *bencao* category; it is not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade drug in the classic's own scheme, the kind taken long-term for nourishing life, but with no identified species, no dose and no preparation in the record. A cultivator can read this as the tradition's own account of what an upper-grade fungus was thought to be, and should not act on it by eating any fungus.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The yellow zhi has no settled species. Do not gather or eat wild fungi on the strength of this entry; many are poisonous and some are lethal.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, people on medicines.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Medical conditions named.** "Evils in the heart and abdomen" is not a modern diagnosis. See a clinician for abdominal or cardiac symptoms.
