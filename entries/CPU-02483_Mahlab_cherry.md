# Mahlab cherry — maḥlab (محلب)

## *Prunus mahaleb L., the St Lucie cherry, whose kernel is the spice mahlab; the identification is the modern one, matched from the name, because the Canon entry has no description of the plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** Book II, entry *maḥlab*, Arabic text lines 15298 to 15305. The entry has no "what it is" section. It begins with the choice of the drug and does not say that it is a kernel or a cherry. The spine's botanical name is therefore the modern reading of the Arabic word. A separate Canon entry is also worth noting: line 16878 begins an entry describing a "grain like the chickpea, which has a grain like *al-maḥlab*", and so compares another drug to this one; it was not opened further.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Choice: "its best is the white of pearly, clear colour" (أجوده الأبيض اللون اللؤلؤي الصافي). Temperament: "hot in the first, not severe in dryness" (حار في الأولى ليس بشديد اليبس). Actions: "a gentle polisher, dissolving, soothing pains" (جلاء لطيف محلل مسكن للأوجاع). Joints and nerves: "good for pains of the flank and the back." Chest: "beneficial for fainting (*al-ghashy*), drunk with honey water." Excretory organs: "beneficial for colic and for the stone in the kidney and bladder, beneficial to the back, drunk with honey water." No dose is given in the entry, and the entry quotes no other author.
In recipes the Canon names "peeled *ḥabb al-maḥlab*" (the grain or kernel of mahlab) twice, which suggests that the drug is the kernel. (1) In the treatment of jaundice (heading at line 36327): "or peeled *ḥabb al-maḥlab*, of which two dirhams are given to drink" (line 36451). (2) In the section on compound medicines (heading at line 40390), in lines that also deal with kidney remedies: "peeled, pounded *ḥabb al-maḥlab*, two *mithqāls*, saffron one *mithqāl*, rhubarb half a *mithqāl*, kneaded with honey; the draught is four dirhams" (lines 40418 to 40419). These two doses belong to those recipes and are quoted here as the only quantities the Canon gives for the kernel. The passages were read in the few lines quoted only.

**Identity doubt.** The name matches the modern spice and the kernel use in the recipes, but the entry does not describe the plant, and "cherry" is the spine's. The identification is likely rather than shown. [USER: verify.]

### Active Components

The Canon names none. As a general fact, the kernels of *Prunus mahaleb* contain coumarin, which gives the spice its smell. [USER: verify.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The text records a mild, warming, pain-soothing drug taken in honey water for fainting, colic and urinary stone. Fainting, colic and stone are conditions for medical care; no protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

In the entry: drunk in honey water (lines 15302 and 15305). In recipes: two dirhams, peeled (line 36451); two *mithqāls* in a compound with saffron and rhubarb, draught four dirhams (lines 40418 to 40419). No dose is given in the entry itself. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

Coumarin is a known plant constituent with anticoagulant relatives; the text does not discuss it. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Avicenna: hot in the first degree, not severe in dryness (line 15300 to 15301); gently polishing and dissolving.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The entry lists a gentle, warming drug for fainting, colic and stone and says nothing on the vital faculties. **(c) Absent: no cultivation framing is attested.**

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing to practise here. The entry is mainly an example of how Avicenna ranks a drug by colour and clarity (a "pearly" white kernel) before saying what it does.

### Contraindications

- Fainting, kidney or bladder stone and colic need medical care.
- Coumarin-containing spices in large amounts: a general caution only, not in the text. [USER: verify.]
