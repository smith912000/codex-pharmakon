### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** This plant is named only by Dioscorides (DMM). It was added 2026-10-09 from the extraction list (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) at the locus 4-123, line 9128.

**Identity note.** The binomial is the translators' (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) suggestion and not Dioscorides' identification. The SUGGESTED line (line 9129) reads: "Cacalia verbascifolia, Inula candida, Senecio thapsoides — Cacalia, Wild Caraway, Tassel Flower", and the next line (9130) adds "Cacalia alpina — Alpine Cacalia". A further note (line 9131) says: "According to Sprengel, this is Bupleurum longifolum [Loudon]." So the translators list several genera across the daisy family and a possible umbellifer, and the chapter's own description is brief. The identity is unsettled. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter sits in Book Four ("Other Herbs & Roots"), between 4-122 ION (violet) and 4-124 BOUNION.

*4-123 KAKALIA* (heading at line 9128; text at line 9132). The whole chapter reads: "Cacalia bears white leaves of a good size with a stalk in the middle of them, straight and white, and a flower similar to bryony; it grows on hills. The root of this (steeped in wine like tragacanth and licked or chewed by itself) cures coughs and roughness of the throat. The grains that come after flowering are pounded into small pieces and smeared on with wax ointment to keep the face smooth and without wrinkles. It is also called leontice."

So the text gives: a description (large white leaves, a straight white central stalk, a flower likened to bryony), a habitat (hills), two uses (the root for coughs and roughness of the throat, the seed-grains for the skin of the face) and one other name, *leontice*. It gives no dose.

The note at line 9131 is also why this chapter turns up in another entry: I_886 (Hare's Ear, *Bupleurum*) mentions 4-123 only as the place where the Loudon note puts a *Bupleurum*. It is not the same row.

### Active Components

*Cacalia* in older botany is a genus-name for plants now placed in the daisy family (Asteraceae, tribe Senecioneae), and *Senecio* and its relatives are generally known to contain pyrrolizidine alkaloids in many species, which are liver-toxic. Whether this applies to the plant that Dioscorides describes, or to *C. verbascifolia*, *Inula candida* or *Bupleurum longifolum*, was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides records a hill plant whose root, steeped in wine and licked or chewed, is used for coughs and a rough throat, and whose seed-grains are smeared on in wax ointment as a facial cosmetic. The Codex gives no protocol for it. The identity is uncertain and the genus-level resemblance to alkaloid-bearing plants is a reason for caution.

### Preparation & Dosing

Two preparations are printed (4-123). The root is "steeped in wine like tragacanth and licked or chewed by itself". The grains are "pounded into small pieces and smeared on with wax ointment". ### Contraindications

None is printed in the chapter. As a general caution, an unidentified plant must not be taken on the strength of a translator's suggested binomial. Some members of the daisy-family genera named here are known to contain liver-toxic alkaloids, and the root is described as taken by mouth. Do not use in pregnancy. The chapter's cosmetic use on the face is likewise not advice.
