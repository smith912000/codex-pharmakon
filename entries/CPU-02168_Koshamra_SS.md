# Koshamra — Koshamra (Sushruta Samhita)

## *Unresolved: a tree named Koshamra whose sour fruit, kernel and seed oil are described in the Sushruta Samhita; the translation gives no Latin name. A later Indian equation with Schleichera oleosa (Lour.) Oken is possible but was not read in any source and is of low confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The Sanskrit name is printed *Koshamra* throughout Bhishagratna's English translation (Kaviraj Kunja Lal Bhishagratna, Sushruta Samhita, Vol. I). The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from a name extraction, with the locus Sutra 38, Vol. I line 15687; this entry replaces it. The translation gives no Latin name for the word in any passage read, so the identification is unresolved.

**Ayurveda (SS).** The word occurs in five places in the Sutrasthana, all read for this entry.

*The gana (Sutra ch. 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 346; Bhishagratna Vol. I line 15687).* "The Nya'grodha'di Group:— The drugs known as Nyagrodha, Audumvara, Ashvattha, Plaksha, Madhuka, Kapitana, Kakubha, Amra, Koshamra, Chorakapatra, the two sorts of Jamvu, Piyala, Madhuka (Maula), Rohini, Vanjula, Kadamva, Vadari, Tinduki, Sallaki, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Bhallataka, Palasha, and Nandi-Vriksha, combinedly form the group known as the Nyagodhradi Gana." The metrical text (lines 353 to 360) says the group is "beneficial in cases of ulcer, cures all disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones and all sorts of secretions of the body in addition to its astringent properties (Sangrahi) and proves curative in a case of haemoptysis. It is an anti-fat and assuages the burning sensation of the body." This is a property of the group as a whole. The text does not say which member does what.

*The acid group (Sutra ch. 42, `SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` line 313).* "Koshamra, Bhavya, Paravata, Vetraphala, Lakucha, Amla-Vetash, Dantashatha and curd, whey, Sura, Shukta, Sauvira, Tushodaka and Dhanyamla, etc. are generally included within the acid group."

*The seed oils (Sutra ch. 45, `SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 1012 to 1014).* "The Oils obtained from the seeds of Tumvi, Koshamra, Danti, Dravanti, Shyama, Saptala, Nilika, Kampiilaka, and Siiankhini, are bitter, pungent and astringent in their tastes. They serve to cleanse the system from all impurities and baneful principles through their purgative properties. They act as purifying agents in respect of malignant ulcers, and prove curative in diseases due to the deranged Vayu and Kapham, as well as in skin-diseases" (the sentence runs on past the lines read).

*The fruit chapter (Sutra ch. 46, `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`).* Koshamra is listed in the fruit group (line 992, with Amlika, Naranga and Jamvira following it). Line 1101: "The Koshamra is possessed of properties nearly identical with those of the Tamarind." The tamarind's properties are given just before (lines 1095 to 1100): "Unripe Tintidika (tamarind) subdues the Vayu, and tends to generate the Pittam and Kapham in the organism, while in its ripe state it is astringent, heat-making and stomachic. It destroys the Vayu and Kapham and restores a relish for food." At lines 1349 to 1352 the kernels "of such fruits as the Vijapuraka, Shampaka and Koshamra are sweet in digestion, appetising, demulcent, and subdue the Vayu and Pittam."

**What the sources do not give.** No Latin name, no description of the tree, no statement of where it grows. Later Indian usage is commonly reported to equate *kosamra* with *Schleichera oleosa* (a tree whose fruit is sour and whose seed yields an oil), which would fit the sour fruit and seed oil read here. That equation was not found in any text opened for this entry and is offered only as a lead. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent, and the identity is unresolved, so no composition is stated. In general terms, a sour fruit generally owes its taste to organic acids, and a seed oil is a fat; this is stated of the class of material only, not of this plant.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a gana member: ulcers, disorders of the uterus and vagina, haemoptysis, burning of the body, excess fat (group action). As a seed oil: a purgative purifier of malignant ulcers and a remedy for Vayu, Kapham and skin disease (Sutra ch. 45). As a fruit: akin to tamarind (Sutra ch. 46).
- **What the text does not say.** The plant's identity, any single-drug use outside these passages, or any rasayana or vajikarana use.
- The Codex does not translate these terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol and recommends nothing.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources give no recipe and no dose for Koshamra alone. The seed oils are named in Sutra ch. 45 (lines 1012 to 1014), without a method of extraction or an amount. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. A sour fruit and a purgative seed oil are both ordinary categories of plant material, but nothing here can be said of this plant until it is identified. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** For the fruit, "nearly identical" to the tamarind (Sutra ch. 46, line 1101), whose taste is printed as astringent when ripe, and which is "heat-making" (lines 1095 to 1100). The acid group (Sutra ch. 42, line 313) places it with the sour substances. The kernels are "sweet in digestion" and subdue Vayu and Pittam (ch. 46, lines 1349 to 1352). The seed oil is "bitter, pungent and astringent" (ch. 45). These are the text's own qualities, taken from different parts of the plant, and they are not combined into one rating here.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Koshamra is named in the Sushruta Samhita as a member of a therapeutic group (gana) and in the other places listed under Origin & Tradition. The gana is a grouping by therapeutic action. It is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status, and the text read assigns Koshamra neither. A search of the whole chapter set on disk (sutra, chikitsa, kalpa, uttara, nidana, sharira) for the name found no occurrence in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters. The fruit and kernel are described among foods (ch. 46) and the seed oil among oils (ch. 45). Nothing links it to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The status is unattested, and only a therapeutic one is recorded.

**Practitioner's note.** A sour fruit, a kernel and a purgative seed oil of an unidentified tree. The tradition records it as a foodstuff and a remedy, and nothing more. A cultivator has nothing here to act on until the tree is identified.
