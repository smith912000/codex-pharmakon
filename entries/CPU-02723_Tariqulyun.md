**Avicenna, Canon (CM), quoting Dioscorides.** The entry is *ṭarīqūlyūn* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text line 13677, to line 13687). It opens: قال ديسقوريدوس (13678), "Dioscorides said". The plant: ينبت في السواحل في أماكن منها إذا فاض ماء البحر غطاها وليس هو في جوف الماء ولا هو بناء عنه, "it grows on the shores in places which the sea-water covers when it floods, yet it is not in the water's depth nor far from it" (13678-13679). Leaf like the leaf of *aṭāṭīs* but thicker (the plant named *aṭāṭīs* is not identified here); a stem about a span long, split at the top (13679-13680). "It is said that the flower of this plant changes its colour three times in the day: at morning it is white, at midday it leans to purple (*furfīr*), and in the evening it is deep red" (13680-13684). Root white, sweet-smelling, "warms the tongue when tasted" (13684-13685).

Temperament: مائل إلى حرارة, "leaning to heat" (13685). Excretion: إذا شرب منه مقدار درخميين بشراب أسهل من البطن الماء وأدر البول, "if two drachms (*dirkhamayn*) of it are drunk in wine, it loosens the belly of water and promotes urine" (13685-13686). Poisons: "it is made to repel the harm of poisons before the other antidotes" (السموم: وقد يتخذ لدفع ضرر السموم قبل سائر البادزهرات, 13686-13687).
