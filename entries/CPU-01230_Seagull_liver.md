# Seagull liver — *Hepar aithuas* (Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 2-50), "liver of *mergus* [seagull]"
## *"Liver of Seagull", the translator's heading; the text says "dried liver of mergus [seagull]", the bracket being the translator's*  | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The only tradition attesting this material in the sources retrieved is Dioscorides' *De Materia Medica*, in the 2000 English edition (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`; "text" below). The chapter is 2-50, headed *HEPAR AITHUAS* ("liver of the *aithua*"), at line 5137 (the spine's locus, `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`: "2-50 (line 5137)", translator's identification "Liver of Seagull", class animal). The Greek *aithua* is a sea bird; the translator renders it "seagull" and prints the Latin *mergus* in the text, but neither the text nor the translator's gloss gives a binomial, and the Latin genus name *Mergus* is in modern usage that of the mergansers, diving ducks, and not of the gulls, so the identity is open. [USER: verify]

The whole chapter, line 5139, reads: "Two spoonfuls of dried liver of mergus [seagull] (taken as a drink with honey water) expels the afterbirth." In the text as printed this sentence is followed on the same line by the heading of chapter 2-51. The chapter gives one part (the liver), one preparation (dried, taken in honey water), one quantity ("two spoonfuls") and one use (to expel the afterbirth). Whether "afterbirth" means the placenta retained after a delivery is not stated beyond the word.

The chapter is the fourth in a run of liver chapters. Chapter 2-47 (*Hepar aigos*, goat's liver, line 5125, on the same line as the end of 2-46) gives the roasting fluid for night blindness; 2-48 (*Hepar kaprou*, boar's liver, line 5127) gives the dried liver in wine "against the bites of snakes and beasts"; and 2-49 (*Hepar kunos lussontos*, liver of a mad dog, line 5130) gives an amuletic note, where the edition adds the warning "RABIES IS HIGHLY COMMUNICABLE". Dioscorides records the amulet there without vouching for it. The seagull chapter itself contains nothing of that kind. The index to the edition lists "Liver of Seagull 200" and "Mergus 200" (lines 16341 and 16607).

The Tibetan text on disk names a *Mergus merganser* in a list of "animals which live in wet places" (Clark 1995, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, line 672, "bittern (*Anas platyrhynchos* or *Mergus merganser*)"), but that line is a dietary classification, uses no liver and is not Dioscorides' seagull; it is not treated as an attestation. No other tradition on the tag line, and no text outside Dioscorides on disk, was found naming a seagull liver.

### Active Components

Bird liver is rich in protein, iron, vitamin A and other fat-soluble vitamins, and holds glycogen, since the liver is the main store and processing organ of the body. These are general facts about liver as a tissue. The chapter names no constituent and this entry names none. Sea birds feeding on fish may also concentrate environmental contaminants in the liver, which is another general fact.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The use recorded is obstetric: a drink to expel the afterbirth. A retained placenta is an emergency that needs medical care. The Codex's application layer has nothing to add, and no protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives a quantity: "Two spoonfuls of dried liver of mergus [seagull] (taken as a drink with honey water)" (line 5139). The size of the spoonful, the volume of honey water, and the number of doses are not stated. A spoonful as Dioscorides measures it is not defined in the passage, so this is not converted to a modern quantity. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek / Dioscorides.** No quality (warming, astringent, or the like) is printed for the seagull liver in chapter 2-50, and none is supplied here.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. Chapter 2-50 is one sentence giving a part, a preparation, a quantity and a use. It records no provenance, season or assay such as makes the discipline-of-attention frame useful elsewhere in the work, and it assigns no status to the person taking the remedy.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing in the source for a cultivator to use. The chapter is a single obstetric remedy, and a cultivator who needs help with childbirth needs a midwife or a physician, not a bird's liver.

### Contraindications

An obstetric condition such as a retained placenta needs urgent medical care and not a household remedy. Sea-bird liver can hold heavy metals and other environmental contaminants, and wild birds carry avian pathogens. Many gull and sea-bird species are protected by law and may not be taken. These are general cautions and not claims from the text.
