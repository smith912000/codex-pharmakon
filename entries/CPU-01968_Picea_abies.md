# Picea abies — "Picea abies" (translator's suggestion for Dioscorides 5-43, *Oinos retinites*, rosin wine)

## *Picea abies (L.) H.Karst., the Norway spruce, a tall conifer of northern and mountain Europe; the identification is the translator's, printed as the first of two suggestions for the rosin wine of De materia medica 5-43, so the name is a suggestion for the resin Dioscorides mixes in, not a plant he describes; confidence low to medium* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Dioscorides translation only, as a suggested identification.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Picea abies [DMM]". The name is not Dioscorides' own: it is printed in the "SUGGESTED" line that the translator (Osbaldeston, 2000; `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`) puts under each chapter heading.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Chapter 5-43, *Oinos retinites* (text line 9991). The suggestion line (9993) reads "Picea abies, Pinus mugo — Retinites — Rosin from Spruce Firs, Pines — Rosin Wine". The chapter (line 9994) says: "Rosin wine is made in many nations. It is abundant in Galatia because the grapes remain unripe because of the cold, and the wine grows sour if it is not mixed with Picea resina [spruce]. The rosin is pounded with the bark, and a quarter pint is mixed to nine gallons of wine; some strain it after it is boiled, taking away the rosin; others leave it alone. Growing old, these become sweet. They all cause headaches and vertigo, and yet are digestive and urinary. They are good for those with dripping mucus and coughs, for the abdominal cavity, dysentery, dropsy, and women's menstrual flows, and it is a syringe for deep ulcers. The black is more binding than the white." The bracketed "[spruce]" after *Picea resina* is the translator's. The drug of the entry is therefore a wine with rosin in it; the tree is the translator's guess at the source of the rosin.

The index of the translation (line 17000) lists "Picea abies 764". A search of the text for the name finds it only in this suggestion line. The resin chapter 1-92 (line 4240) carries a different suggestion list ("Picea alba, Pinus sylvestris, Abies pectinata, Picea excelsa — White Spruce, Silver Fir ... Larch"); that chapter belongs to another spine row and is not folded in here. No other tradition's text on disk was found to name this tree.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The rosin wine is "digestive and urinary" and "good for" dripping mucus and coughs, the abdominal cavity, dysentery, dropsy, women's menstrual flows, and as "a syringe for deep ulcers" (line 9994). The text also says it causes headaches and vertigo.
- **What the text does not say.** It names no tree, so nothing is said of the spruce itself.

The Codex gives no protocol. A resin-flavoured alcoholic drink is not recommended.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives a method for the wine: rosin pounded with the bark, "a quarter pint" to "nine gallons" of wine, strained after boiling or left in (line 9994). This is a quantity for making a batch and is not a dose for drinking. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] **Do not use any quantity from this entry.**

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Wine flavoured with pine resin survives today as retsina in Greece. Spruce resin is a mixture of resin acids and terpenes, and alcohol is a toxin of the liver and brain in excess. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** The chapter prints no humoral quality for the rosin wine. The only qualities are the actions listed above and "The black is more binding than the white" (line 9994). A quality printed for the sister chapters (5-44 "same effects", 5-48 "warming, digesting, cleansing, and purging") is not carried over. No classification is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is manufactured here. What the chapter offers a practitioner is a discipline of attention to provenance and making: the wine is "abundant in Galatia", the rosin is pounded with the bark, and the maker may strain it or leave it in. The chapter records the drawbacks (headache and vertigo) as it records the uses. No cultivation status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A medicinal wine from a trade in resin, with its own warning printed in the chapter. A cultivator has nothing to take from it beyond the habit of noting where a material comes from and what it does to the head.

### Contraindications

- **Alcohol.** The wine is an alcoholic drink; it is not suitable in pregnancy, for children, for people with liver disease, or before driving.
- **Headache and vertigo.** The text itself says it causes them.
- **Resin.** Conifer resins can cause contact allergy.
- **Identity.** The tree is the translator's suggestion; the Greek is not identified. [USER: verify]
