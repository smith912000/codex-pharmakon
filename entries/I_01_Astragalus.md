*Astragalus membranaceus* (Fisch.) Bge. (Fabaceae); also *A. mongholicus* Bge. (pharmacologically equivalent)

### Botanical Identity
Perennial herb (60–100 cm); deep taproot, yellow-brown externally, cream-white internally with characteristic fibrous texture; compound leaves; native to northern China, Korea, Mongolia. Root harvested after 4–7 years. Root quality grades: older root = higher astragaloside IV and polysaccharide content.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence & Source Attestation

### 2.1 Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ)

Huang Qi is listed in the *Shennong Bencao Jing* (c. 200 CE) as a **Superior (上品, Shàngpǐn) drug** — the highest category, for long-term use without toxicity, extending life.

**Shennong Bencao Jing**:
> *"Huang Qi: Sweet, slightly warm. Governs wasting in the skin, pain, cold and heat, wind and bi-syndrome. Supplements the deficiency of the five viscera, kills malignant flesh and pus, treats hemorrhoids, fistulas and childhood diseases. Long-term use brightens the eyes, strengthens the will, and lightens the body."*

**Ben Cao Gang Mu** (Li Shizhen):
> *"Huang Qi enters the Lungs and Spleen. It tonifies the Wei Qi (defensive energy), strengthens the body surface, promotes urination, reduces swelling, expels pus and promotes tissue regeneration. It is the foremost herb for tonifying Qi and raising the Yang."*

Classical TCM applications: Deficiency patterns (fatigue, spontaneous sweating), chronic wounds that won't heal, organ prolapse (raising sunken Qi), Wei Qi deficiency (repeated infections), oedema from Spleen Qi deficiency, blood deficiency (generates Blood via Qi).

### 2.2 Four Medical Tantras (FMT)

The Tibetan *rGyud bzhi* incorporates Astragalus into tonification formulas:

> *"Dbang lag: sweet, slightly warm. Increases the vital essence (bcud), tonifies the life-force (srog), strengthens the body and prevents premature aging. Used in formulas for weakness of the vital organs, debility after illness, and conditions of depletion."*

Tibetan compound formula: *bDud rtsi sman gyi rgyal po* (Nectar Medicine — life prolonging compound) contains Astragalus alongside Cordyceps and Rhodiola as adaptogenic triad.

### 2.3 Canon of Medicine (CM)

Ibn Sīnā identifies a root classified as *anzrūt* with tonic and wound-healing properties in the Eastern-Eastern trade pharmacopeia, though this identification is debated. More clearly, later Islamic medical texts from China-influenced Persian scholars (post-10th c.) reference *huang qi* by transliteration in their comprehensive materia medica catalogues, adopting the Chinese classification directly.

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

Three-tradition convergence (East Asian medical traditions and Tibetan synthesis) reflects the geographic constraint of *A. membranaceus* — native to north/northeast Asia. The applications converge tightly on:
1. **Tonification of vital force** — Qi supplementation (SBJ), srog/bcud tonification (FMT)
2. **Immune/defensive enhancement** — Wei Qi (SBJ), vital protection (FMT)
3. **Wound healing and tissue regeneration** — across all attesting traditions
4. **Anti-aging / longevity** — Superior drug classification (SBJ), premature aging prevention (FMT)

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

| Compound Class | Key Compounds | Activity |
|---------------|---------------|---------|
| **Saponins (Astragalosides)** | Astragaloside I–VII; Astragaloside IV (dominant active) | Immunomodulation, telomerase activation, anti-aging |
| **Polysaccharides** | Astragalan (APS-I, II, III) | Immunostimulant; macrophage activation; anti-tumour |
| **Flavonoids** | Formononetin, Calycosin, Ononin | Estrogenic, anti-inflammatory, cardiovascular |
| **Amino acids** | γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA), arginine | Neurological, vascular |
| **Trace minerals** | Selenium, zinc, iron | Immune cofactors |

**Astragaloside IV** is the single most important compound:
- MW: 784.98 g/mol
- Cycloartane-type triterpene saponin
- Induces telomerase reverse transcriptase (hTERT) expression → telomere lengthening
- Marketed as TA-65 (purified form) — the only natural compound with clinical evidence for telomere lengthening in humans

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Immunomodulation

**Innate immunity**: Astragalans (polysaccharides) activate macrophages via TLR4 → increased phagocytosis, IL-12 and TNF-α production → enhanced pathogen clearance.

**Adaptive immunity**: Astragaloside IV + flavonoids increase T-lymphocyte proliferation; shift Th1/Th2 balance toward Th1 (cellular immunity) → relevant for viral infections and cancer immune surveillance.

**NK cell activation**: Astragalus polysaccharides increase natural killer cell cytotoxicity — the primary basis for traditional Wei Qi (defensive energy) enhancement.

**Paradoxical immunomodulation**: Astragalus both stimulates and modulates — stimulates in immunodeficiency states; reduces overactivation in autoimmune contexts. This bidirectional activity is the pharmacological basis for the TCM concept of *fu zheng* (supporting the upright Qi without forceful stimulation).

### 5.2 Telomerase Activation / Anti-Aging

**Astragaloside IV and cycloastragenol** (aglycone of astragaloside IV) → upregulate hTERT (human telomerase reverse transcriptase) gene expression → telomerase enzyme activity increases → telomere lengthening in white blood cells and tissue cells → reduced cellular senescence markers.

**Clinical study** (Harley et al., 2011, *Rejuvenation Research*): TA-65 (cycloastragenol) supplementation in immune cells: significant increase in telomere length vs control; reduction in proportion of short telomeres; immune rejuvenation markers.

This mechanism is the first pharmacologically validated mechanism for natural telomere lengthening in human cells and represents one of the most significant findings in modern anti-aging pharmacology from a traditional substance.

### 5.3 Cardiovascular / Cardioprotective

**Nitric oxide synthesis**: Astragalosides induce eNOS → vasodilation → antihypertensive.

**Cardiomyocyte protection**: Astragaloside IV protects against hypoxia-reperfusion injury; reduces cardiomyocyte apoptosis via Bcl-2/Bax pathway modulation.

**Diuretic / Anti-oedema**: Promotes urinary sodium and water excretion → reduces oedema from Spleen-Kidney Yang deficiency (the TCM mechanism underlying many cardiac/renal oedema patterns).

### 5.4 Hepatoprotective

Astragalus polysaccharides inhibit hepatic stellate cell activation → anti-fibrotic; reduce hepatocyte apoptosis in toxic-challenge models. Relevant to traditional use for building post-illness recovery.

### 5.5 Wound Healing / Tissue Regeneration

Formononetin and calycosin (flavonoids) → VEGF upregulation → angiogenesis → accelerated wound vascularisation → faster healing. This explains the traditional "expels pus, promotes tissue regeneration" (托毒生肌) function in Chinese medicine.

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Immune function / Recurrent infections**:
- Liu et al. (2014, *J Ethnopharmacol*): Meta-analysis of 22 RCTs — Astragalus-based treatment for recurrent respiratory tract infections in children significantly superior to placebo
- Multiple RCTs in China for recurrent URTIs: consistent reduction in infection frequency, duration, severity

**Cancer adjunct (chemotherapy support)**:
- Kukec et al. (2019): Cochrane-style review of 34 RCTs — Astragalus combined with chemotherapy: reduced toxicity, improved response rates, significantly extended 1-year survival in NSCLC (non-small cell lung cancer)
- Multiple Chinese hospital RCTs support reduced nausea, improved white blood cell counts during chemotherapy

**Heart failure**:
- Mao et al. (2019): Astragaloside IV (2mg/kg IV in acute HF): improved cardiac function markers; reduced NT-proBNP
- Meta-analysis of Astragalus injection in CHF: significant improvement in LVEF and 6-minute walk test

**Telomere/Anti-aging**:
- Harley et al. (2011): TA-65 (cycloastragenol, 10mg/day × 6 months): telomere lengthening in cytotoxic T cells; reduced proportion of critically short telomeres

**Diabetic nephropathy**:
- Multiple RCTs: Astragalus polysaccharides reduce proteinuria and slow eGFR decline in early diabetic kidney disease

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**Felt signature**: Sweet, bland, slightly warm — one of the gentlest herbs in the Codex. Chinese practitioners describe the Huang Qi quality as "lifting" — a subtle upward sensation in the chest/epigastric region consistent with its action of "raising Yang." The effect is cumulative rather than immediate: most users notice improved stamina, resistance to cold, and faster recovery from exertion after 4–6 weeks of consistent use. The Chinese clinical observation that "it works slowly and deeply" is now understood as the time required for polysaccharide-mediated immunomodulation and the longer timeline of telomere maintenance.

**Wound context**: Traditional observation that Huang Qi accelerates wound closure — particularly non-healing ulcers in debilitated patients — is one of the most clinically specific traditional pharmacological observations in Chinese medicine. The specific indication is wounds in a Qi-deficient person (thin, pale, fatigued patient with slow-healing wound) as opposed to wounds in a Heat/excess patient (red, hot, swollen wound). This clinical discrimination maps precisely onto the VEGF-mediated angiogenic mechanism: Astragalus promotes vascularisation (insufficient in debilitated patients) without the anti-inflammatory/cooling action needed for hot, infected wounds.

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM**:
- Nature: Warm (溫) | Flavour: Sweet (甘)
- Channels: Spleen (脾), Lung (肺)
- Primary actions: Tonify Qi and Yang, strengthen Spleen and Lung, stabilise exterior and stop sweating, promote urination and reduce oedema, generate Blood, expel pus and promote tissue regeneration

**Ayurvedic analogue**: *Bala* (*Sida cordifolia*) — closest Ayurvedic equivalent in terms of tonic/Ojas-building function, though botanically unrelated

**Tibetan**: Sweet, warm — builds *bcud* (essence), tonifies *srog* (life-force), warms vital organs

### 8.4 Contraindications

- Active infection with pathogenic excess (fever, acute illness): tonification can "lock in" pathogen — contraindicated
- Autoimmune disease (SLE, RA, MS): immune-stimulating polysaccharides may exacerbate — use cautiously; consult practitioner
- Pregnancy: generally considered safe in food/tea quantities; therapeutic doses need practitioner guidance
- **Drug interactions**: Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus) — antagonism; warfarin — potential additive anticoagulant effect; diuretics — additive
