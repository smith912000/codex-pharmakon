# Ailanthus altissima — Chu Shi (fruit) (Shennong Bencao Jing)

## *Ailanthus altissima, as the pharmaceutical Latin "Fructus Ailanthi Altissimi" that Yang 1998 prints for the entry Chu Shi; the Chinese characters for the name are not in the Codex's own Shennong list* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong text (SBJ).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The entry is in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`). The Latin name is the translator's pharmaceutical Latin and was matched to the binomial by stem in the extraction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv`, row 66). The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex's own list of Shennong names (`_sources/shennong.csv`) has no row for this name, so no Chinese characters are given here. [USER: verify]

**Chinese — Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ).** *Chu Shi, PDF page 47.* The page marker `[[p47]]` is at line 1816 and the entry is at line 1832. No class heading is printed on this page. The governing heading is "Herbs: Superior Class" at line 1775 on the page marked `[[p46]]` (line 1774), and the next heading, "Herbs: Superior Class" again, is at line 1856 on `[[p48]]` (line 1855). The entry therefore stands under the superior-class heading by position (fifty-seven lines and one page break below it), not directly beneath it. The text reads (lines 1832 to 1836):

"Chu Shi (Fructus Ailanthi Altissimi) is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats impotence, boosts the qi, replenishes the skin and muscles, brightens the eyes, and [makes one] wise and intelligent so as to forsee [the future]. Protracted taking may make one free from hunger, prevent senility, and make the body light. It grows in mountains and valleys."

The entry carries no footnote number and gives no other name. The translator therefore offers no commentary on this entry, and the Codex adds none. The entry prints no toxicity statement.

*The class heading and what the preface says of the class.* Yang's table of contents prints "Herbs: Superior Class" (line 711). The preface (lines 352 to 355 and 383 to 384) says of the superior class: "There are 120 superior class medicinals which are used as sovereigns. They mainly nourish life and correspond to heaven. They are nontoxic and taking them in large amounts and for a long time will not harm people. If one intends to make one's body light, boost the qi, prevent aging, and prolong life, one should base [one's efforts] on the superior class." Yang's commentary (lines 356 to 357) says: "they are nontoxic medicinals that are able to nurture life and therefore bestow longevity." The count printed in the preface (120) and the count in Yang's commentary ("119 superior class medicinals, 120 middle class medicinals, and 122 inferior class medicinals", lines 365 to 366) differ. The commentary does not say why, and this entry does not rely on either count.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Ailanthus*, *Chu Shi* and *altissima* found no entry on this plant. I_784 (*Brucea javanica*) names *Ailanthus* once, in its cross-references (line 152), as a plant of the same family; it is not an entry on Chu Shi.

### Active Components

General composition, stated without numbers: *Ailanthus altissima* is a tree of the family Simaroubaceae, a family known for its bitter quassinoid compounds. The composition of the fruit, and of whatever the Shennong text meant by Chu Shi, was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Shennong text lists the entry's actions in its own terms (impotence, boosting the qi, replenishing the skin and muscles, brightening the eyes, wisdom and foresight, freedom from hunger, preventing senility, making the body light). These are the text's claims and not Codex protocols. The plant's identity rests on a translator's Latin name, and the Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Cultivation Register

**Chinese — Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: the superior class (上品), by position.* The class is assigned by the printed heading "Herbs: Superior Class" (line 1775, `[[p46]]`) and repeated at the top of the next page (line 1856, `[[p48]]`). The entry on `[[p47]]` has no heading directly above it, so the grade rests on its position between the two. In the preface (lines 352 to 355, 383 to 384) the superior class "mainly nourish[es] life", is nontoxic, and is the class on which one bases the effort "to make one's body light, boost the qi, prevent aging, and prolong life". The entry itself says: "boosts the qi" and "Protracted taking may make one free from hunger, prevent senility, and make the body light."

The phrase "make the body light" is *qing shen* 輕身, which the cultivation spec says has no pharmacological equivalent and must not be translated into one. Yang's own note on that phrase (lines 373 to 378, in a stretch where the OCR order is scrambled) ties such phrases to Daoist writing on elixirs; it is the translator's commentary and not the classic's. "Free from hunger" and "wise and intelligent so as to forsee [the future]" are recorded in the text's own words and left untranslated. The entry asserts no *jing*, *shen* or *neidan* action, and none is added here. The identity of the plant is as printed by the translator; the status is the status of the entry.

**Practitioner's note.** A superior-class entry in the Chinese scheme, whose class the preface calls nontoxic and for long taking. The grade is the classic's; the plant is not known with certainty, and the Codex has no dose, no preparation and no clinical record to set beside it. Nothing here supports taking a tree fruit on the strength of this entry. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The Shennong entry prints no warning. The preface calls the superior class "nontoxic", which is a statement about the class and not a safety finding for this plant or for the tree's fruit. The identity rests on a translator's Latin name, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Impotence and eye complaints need medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
