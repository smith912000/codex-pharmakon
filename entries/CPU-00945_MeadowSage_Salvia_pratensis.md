# Meadow Sage / Meadow Clary

## *Salvia pratensis* L. | Family: Lamiaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, DMM
**Energetic Classification:** Warm–Dry (2nd degree) — *inferred by genus analogy to* Salvia officinalis*, not independently attested for* S. pratensis
**Training Phase:** Foundation — Signature Study

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Salvia pratensis* L. — meadow sage, meadow clary — is a hardy perennial of the mint family (Lamiaceae) native across Europe, western Asia and North Africa, and widely naturalised in temperate grassland worldwide. It is a plant of unimproved hay meadows, roadside verges and calcareous grassland: an erect flower-spike 30–60 cm tall carrying whorls of violet-blue two-lipped flowers over a basal rosette of wrinkled, aromatic leaves. Unlike the grey-felted culinary sage, its foliage is greener and only lightly aromatic, and the plant is a wild meadow species rather than a garden herb. It is easy to raise from seed, tolerant of poor dry soil, and valued today more as a pollinator plant than as a medicine.

That last point is the honest spine of this entry. **Meadow sage's medicinal reputation is largely borrowed.** The famous "sage" of the European and Unani record — the sage of longevity proverbs, sore-throat gargles and memory tonics — is *Salvia officinalis* (common/garden sage), and the famous "clary" is *Salvia sclarea*. Both are already in the codex. *S. pratensis* is a genuine wild sage that shares the genus name and the aromatic Lamiaceae signature, and so has quietly inherited a materia-medica standing that the classical texts almost certainly assigned to its more medicinal cousins.

**DMM (Dioscorides / Greek herbal stream).** Dioscorides' *De Materia Medica* describes *elelisphakos* — the Greek "sage" — used for wounds, as an emmenagogue and for coughs. The plant Dioscorides had in view is best identified with the aromatic culinary/Mediterranean sages (the *S. officinalis*/*S. fruticosa* group), not the near-scentless meadow sage of northern grassland. A specific *S. pratensis* attestation in the Dioscoridean stream cannot be substantiated. **[USER: verify]** — treat the DMM tag here as a genus/name inheritance: the classical *elelisphakos* is the medicinal Mediterranean sage, and meadow sage rides on the shared name.

**CM (Avicenna, *Canon of Medicine*, Unani).** Avicenna's *maryamiyya* / sage entry likewise describes the aromatic medicinal sage of the Mediterranean and Near East, prized as warming, drying, a tonic to the nerves and a styptic. There is no basis for reading *S. pratensis* specifically into the *Canon*. **[USER: verify]** — the CM tag is retained only as a genus marker; the substance Avicenna described is the officinalis-type sage.

The training lesson of meadow sage is therefore a lesson in **name inheritance**: how a plant can accumulate an apparent tradition simply by sharing a genus with a celebrated relative. The cultivator studies it precisely to learn to separate the reputation of a name from the evidence for a species.

### Active Components

Species-specific phytochemistry for *S. pratensis* is now reasonably well characterised and is consistent with a typical polyphenol-rich Lamiaceae:

- **Rosmarinic acid** — the dominant hydroxycinnamic acid, confirmed as the predominant phenolic in *S. pratensis* extracts; reported across Mediterranean *Salvia* at high levels (rosmarinic acid ranged 9,400–38,800 µg/g across seven wild sages including *S. pratensis*).
- **Other phenolic acids** — caffeic, protocatechuic, *p*-coumaric and ferulic acids identified by HPLC in *S. pratensis* aerial parts; the species is notably richer in hydroxycinnamic acids (caffeic, *p*-coumaric, ferulic) relative to *S. sclarea*.
- **Flavonoids** — including apigenin derivatives (e.g. apigenin-7-*O*-glucuronide reported across the genus); meadow sage samples were among the richest in flavonoid derivatives (expressed as rutin) among common Lamiaceae teas surveyed in Romanian pharmacies.
- **Essential oil** — a linalool-rich volatile fraction with sesquiterpene derivatives (GC-MS of *S. pratensis* aerial parts), distinct from the linalyl-acetate-dominated oil of *S. sclarea*.
- **Tocopherols** — present in the lipophilic (supercritical CO₂) fraction, as across the genus.

**Congener note — do NOT import to this species:** the compounds most people associate with "sage" — high **α/β-thujone**, **1,8-cineole** and **camphor** in the essential oil, and **carnosic/carnosol** diterpene antioxidants — are hallmarks of *Salvia officinalis*. *S. pratensis* essential oil is reported as linalool-dominant, and **its thujone content is not assumed here.** Any thujone caution below is stated as a genus/precautionary note, not a measured *S. pratensis* value.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica, meadow sage is filed as a **Signature Study** rather than a front-line remedy. Its documented, species-specific activity is antioxidant and antimicrobial in vitro (see Scientific Correlation), which places it among the general polyphenol-astringent "meadow tonics" — a mild aromatic bitter/astringent suitable in principle for the same domestic register as other sages (a gargle-type wash, a general antioxidant tisane) but **without the specific throat, menopausal or cognitive indications that belong to *S. officinalis***.

The cultivator's use-case is threefold: (1) as an easy, drought-tolerant pollinator and polyphenol crop; (2) as a teaching specimen for genus/name inheritance; and (3) as a locally-abundant, low-cost source of rosmarinic-acid-rich extract where the more prized sages are unavailable — a role the herbicide/antioxidant literature explicitly frames.

### Preparation & Dosing

| Preparation | Method | Register / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Aromatic tisane | Dried aerial parts, hot-water infusion | Traditional domestic use; no established therapeutic dose for *S. pratensis* specifically |
| Hydroethanolic extract | 50–70% ethanol maceration | 50% ethanol gave best total-polyphenol yield in the Lamiaceae tea survey; research/artisan preparation, not a dosed medicine |
| Gargle / topical wash | Cooled strong infusion | Astringent domestic use by analogy to sage; external only |

**No fabricated doses.** There is no validated human dosing schedule for *S. pratensis*. Doses established for *S. officinalis* (e.g. standardised leaf extracts in cognition/menopause trials) are **congener data and must not be transferred** to meadow sage. Treat all internal use as traditional/food-herb level.

### Scientific Correlation

Claims graded for *S. pratensis* specifically:

- **Antioxidant (radical-scavenging) activity — MODERATE, species-specific in vitro.** Multiple studies confirm strong DPPH/ABTS activity of *S. pratensis* extracts, correlated with rosmarinic acid and total phenolics. *Graded: preclinical / in vitro, species-specific.*
- **Antimicrobial activity — WEAK–MODERATE, species-specific in vitro.** Broad-spectrum but modest MICs for hydroethanolic extract; stronger when used to biosynthesise silver nanoparticles. *Graded: preclinical / in vitro.*
- **Phytotoxic / herbicidal activity — species-specific in vitro/greenhouse.** Ethanolic extracts inhibited seed germination of model plants. *Graded: preclinical.*
- **Anti-acetylcholinesterase / anti-α-glucosidase (neuroprotective, hypoglycaemic) — GENUS-LEVEL signal.** Reported across Mediterranean *Salvia*; among the seven species screened, cholinesterase and α-glucosidase inhibition were attributed to particular taxa and rosmarinic-acid content, not singled out as an *S. pratensis* strength. *Graded: preclinical, genus-level.*
- **α-Synuclein anti-aggregation (Parkinson's models) — PRECLINICAL, extract-level.** *S. pratensis* was one of four Mediterranean plants whose extract inhibited α-synuclein amyloid aggregation in yeast/neuroblastoma screens; note the compound isolated as the direct binder (acteoside) came from *Verbascum thapsus*, not from meadow sage. *Graded: preclinical, screening-level.*
- **Human clinical efficacy (any indication) — NULL.** No randomised or controlled human trials of *S. pratensis* were retrieved. *Graded: NULL / no human evidence.*
- **Memory, menopausal hot-flush and sore-throat efficacy — belongs to the CONGENER.** These are *S. officinalis* indications and are **not** established for *S. pratensis*.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

The Warm–Dry classification carried in the header is an **inference by genus analogy** to *S. officinalis*, whose Unani/Greek reading as warming, drying, nervine and styptic is well attested. No independent classical energetic grading of *S. pratensis* as a distinct drug survives; the meadow species was not the sage the humoral authors were describing. Recorded here as inferred, not attested.

### Contraindications

- **Thujone (genus/precautionary note, not measured for this species):** high-thujone sage essential oils are neurotoxic and abortifacient in excess and are restricted in food use. *S. pratensis* oil is reported as linalool-dominant and its thujone level is **not assumed**; nonetheless, until species-specific quantification is confirmed, avoid concentrated essential-oil ingestion and prolonged high-dose use, especially in pregnancy and epilepsy — the standard precaution applied across aromatic *Salvia*.
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** insufficient species-specific safety data; the sage genus carries traditional emmenagogue/abortifacient and milk-reducing cautions (officinalis). Avoid medicinal doses in pregnancy and while breastfeeding.
- **Allergy:** Lamiaceae/aromatic-plant sensitivity is possible though uncommon.
- **No established therapeutic use:** the absence of human trials means meadow sage should not be presented or used as a treatment for any condition; internal use should stay at food-herb / tisane level.
