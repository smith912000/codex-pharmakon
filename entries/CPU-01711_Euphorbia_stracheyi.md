# Euphorbia stracheyi — Tronbu, khron bu (rGyud-bzhi, Men-Tsee-Khang glossary, by column position)

## *Euphorbia stracheyi, as the binomial printed with the English "Spurge" in the scientific-name run of the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary's "T" block; the Tibetan name "Tronbu" is set against it by column position only, and Clark 1995 prints other names for khron.bu* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT), and under this binomial only by the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Sushruta texts on disk found no *Euphorbia stracheyi*; those texts name other *Euphorbia* species, which are not read as this one. Clark 1995 prints no line for the binomial and none for the spelling "Tronbu". Its entry for the same Tibetan name, *khron.bu*, prints other binomials (see below). This entry rests on one glossary line for the binomial and on running-text lines for the Tibetan name that the glossary does not print beside it. The extraction note reads: "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, line 379). The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (lines 5858 to 5982).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, the "T" block (the letter at line 5858). The glossary prints its columns in separate runs. The phonetic names (lines 5860 to 5924) include, in this order, "Thangtrom", "Tharam" (line 5887 to 5888), "Thamu" (line 5893), "Tikta", "Tiyangku Traagkang" and "Tronbu" (lines 5897 to 5899), then "Tsa tri" (line 5904). The transliterations that follow include "khron bu" for Tronbu (line 5902) and "rtsa mkhris" for Tsa tri (line 5906). The scientific names are in a separate run from line 5928. There, the lines read in turn "Swertia chirayta" (line 5968), "Dracocephalum tanguticum Dragon head" (line 5972), "Coptis teetordes chinensis Mamira" (line 5974), "Euphorbia stracheyi" (line 5976, with the English "Spurge" at line 5978) and "Cicerbita macrorhiza" (line 5980, "Sow thistle" at line 5982). Counting in order, the five phonetic names Tikta, Tiyangku, Traagkang, Tronbu and Tsa tri stand against the five scientific names in that order. Two of the pairs can be checked against Clark. "Traagkang" with "khrag rkang" (line 5901) is the name Clark gives as *ser.po.khrag.rkang* for the gold thread, *Coptis teetoides* (line 1072). "Tsa tri" with "rtsa mkhris" is Clark's *rtsa.mkhris* (line 1082), which prints "Cierhita macrorhiza" as its second binomial. The Codex therefore reads the glossary as pairing "Tronbu" with *Euphorbia stracheyi*, by column position only and not as a printed row. The count rests on order, and several lines hold more than one name. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text on "Tronbu".* The text gives no Latin name for the drug. In chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225), within the run headed "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (line 3428), line 3495 reads: "Ngonbu cleanses chhuser disorders, while Tronbu cleanses tripa disorders." In chapter 21, line 3661: "Arura, Dhenda, Dhong ga, Shri khenda, Dhurji, Thamu, Chum tsa, Ngonbu, Tronbu, Tsi takmo, Rechagpa and Chhu tsa are the group of medicines that are purgatives." The index lists "Tronbu 211,223" (line 7523). The sentence at line 3495 follows "Dhurji and Tharnu purge all forms of hot and cold disorders", so the three names Dhurji, Thamu and Tronbu stand close together in both passages. That the "Tronbu" of these passages is the glossary's "Tronbu" rests on the identical spelling. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, the same Tibetan name.* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, line 1136, in the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997): "khron.bu: Physalis peruviana: Clerodendron fortunatum: Euphorbia sieboldiana [has three names. It has a small, round green leaf and a taproot and] purges bile disorders." The entry stands two lines after *thar.nu* (line 1134). It prints *Euphorbia sieboldiana*, and not *Euphorbia stracheyi*, and the root-text clause "purges bile disorders" agrees in kind with the Men-Tsee-Khang "cleanses tripa disorders" and with the purgatives group. The two Tibetan texts therefore give different *Euphorbia* species for one Tibetan name, and the first binomial Clark prints is *Physalis peruviana*, which is not a *Euphorbia*. The Codex does not choose. Clark's preface (line 83) says: "Therefore more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance". [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *stracheyi*, *Tronbu* and *khron.bu* found these. The word *stracheyi* matched only entries on *Bergenia stracheyi*, a plant of another genus (I_1243, I_1242, I_1189, I_884 and I_308). I_1175 (*Rheum emodi*) quotes the same Men-Tsee-Khang purgatives list at line 3661, in which Tronbu is a neighbour. None is an entry on this drug. The spine has a row for *Physalis peruviana* (CPU-01769, from Clark) with no written file at the time of this entry, and none for *Euphorbia sieboldiana* or *Clerodendron fortunatum*. The sibling *Euphorbia* rows are *Euphorbia helioscopia* (CPU-01710, I_1274) and *Euphorbia wallichii* (CPU-01712, I_1276), written in this batch, and *Euphorbia pallasii* (CPU-01867, from Clark line 1134). Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Euphorbia* is a very large genus of the spurge family (Euphorbiaceae), that its species carry a milky latex, and that the latex of many species is a skin and eye irritant. Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained, and Clark's own list for the name includes two plants of other families (*Physalis*, a nightshade, and *Clerodendron*). [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Men-Tsee-Khang text records that "Tronbu cleanses tripa disorders" (line 3495) and lists Tronbu among the purgatives (line 3661), and Clark's neighbouring entry says the drug "purges bile disorders" (line 1136). These are the texts' claims, attached to this binomial only by a column position, and they are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no part used, no quantity, no frequency and no duration for Tronbu. Clark's commentary layer describes the leaf and the taproot and does not say which part is taken. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Tibetan terms ("tripa disorders", "bile disorders", "purgatives") are not modern diagnoses or measured outcomes, so no modern finding can be set beside them here. The identity of the drug rests on a column position and the other Tibetan text gives different species, so a study of *Euphorbia stracheyi* would not bear directly on it. It is a general, well-established fact that spurge latex can burn the skin and eyes.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** No taste, power or post-digestive taste is printed for Tronbu in the lines read. The name does not appear in the Men-Tsee-Khang taste-group lists of chapter 19 (which begin at line 3204) that were read for this entry, and Clark's commentary layer for *khron.bu* gives leaf and root, not taste. The one quality printed is the action on the bile or *tripa* disorders and the place in the purgatives group.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Men-Tsee-Khang text places Tronbu in its list of the efficacy of medicinal substances and in its group of purgatives, and Clark's *khron.bu* line says the same in kind. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this name places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the sentences cited above were read in full and none mentions one. Its framing is therapeutic only, and the link from this binomial to that framing rests on a column position.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a purgative for bile or *tripa* disorders in the Tibetan texts, with no preparation and no dose, and its Latin name rests on a glossary column position that the other Tibetan text does not confirm. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. The latex of *Euphorbia* species is generally known to be an irritant, a purgative drug can cause dehydration, and the identity is not settled, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Latex that touches the eyes needs urgent medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
