# Sushai — Sushavi (Sushruta Samhita; the row name "Sushai" is an OCR misreading)

## *Probably Sushavi, glossed Momordica charantia (bitter gourd) in Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 by the order of the two printed columns; the Latin is the translator's, the column pairing is ours, and the text itself lists Sushavi as an alternative to Karavellaka, which is also a gourd name; confidence low to medium* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row "Sushai" came from the 13 October extraction of Vol. I line 15372, where the OCR prints the name as "Susha^•i" in the Aragvadhadi group. The same name is printed *Sushavi* in the *Chikitsasthana* (ch. 5, lines 106 and 333, and, with OCR damage, "Sushazi" at line 317) and in Appendix 3 (Vol. III line 33042). The reading "Sushavi" is therefore the correction of the row name; this is an identification of a misread name and not a new plant. The row is kept under its spine name.

**Ayurveda (SS).**

*The Aragvadhadi group (Sutra ch. 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 26 to 41; Vol. I line 15372).* "The A'ragvadha'di Group: — The drugs known as Aragvadha, Madana, Gopaghonta, Kutaja, Patha, Kantaki, Patala, Murva, Indrayava, Saptaparna, Ximva [OCR], Kuruntaka, Dasi-kuruntaka, the two kinds of Karanja, Patola, Kiratttikua [OCR], Guduchi, Chitraka, Sh^ngshta [OCR], and Sushavi form the group known as the Aragvadhadi." (The OCR damage is left as printed in the three damaged names, marked [OCR].) The metrical text: "The group under discussion destroys the deranged Kaphani and the effects of poison and proves beneficial in cases of Meha (morbid discharges from the urethra), Kushtha, fever, vomiting and itching of the body and acts as a purifying (aseptic) agent in the case of an ulcer." These are group properties.

*Chikitsasthana ch. 5 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt`).* Three passages in the treatment that begins with the deranged Vayu name the drug. (1) At line 106: a potion of goat's milk "cooked with Syama, Rasna, Sushavi, Prisniparni, Pilu, Satavari, S'vadojnshtrd [OCR] and Das'a-fmda [OCR]" (diacritics normalised). (2) At lines 316 to 318: "A plaster composed of Saireyaka, Atarushaka, Bala, Ati-bala, Jivanti and Sushavi [printed Sushazi, OCR], pasted together with the milk of a she-goat, should be likewise applied (to the seat of the disease)." (3) At lines 330 to 334, in the Vata-rakta passage: old clarified butter boiled with the drugs of the Kakolyadi group, "or with those of the Jivaniya group, or with the decoction of Sushavi, or of Karavellaka". The "or" between Sushavi and Karavellaka argues that the text treats them as separate drugs, even if a later gloss pairs both with a gourd.

*Appendix 3 (Vol. III, "Names of drugs with Latin synonyms", `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` lines 33030 to 33060).* The Sanskrit names are printed in one column and the Latin glosses in another, and the OCR separates the columns. In the column order, Sushavi stands twelfth of fifteen names, between Surya-varta ("Cleome viscosa") and Suvarchika ("Carbonate of Soda"), and the twelfth gloss is "Momordica Charantia." The pairing was made by counting, with the one brace (Sura-daru and Sura-druma, "See Devadaru") accounted for; it is the Codex's reading and should be checked against the printed page. ### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. If the translator's gloss is right, the species is the bitter gourd, whose fruit is generally known to contain bitter triterpenoid glycosides (cucurbitacin-type compounds) and proteins; this is stated of the species only, and the species is itself a gloss. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a group member: Kapham, poison, Meha, Kushtha, fever, vomiting, itching, and purifying an ulcer. In Chikitsa ch. 5: an ingredient of a milk potion and of a goat's-milk plaster in the treatment of Vayu, and, as a decoction, an alternative to the Karavellaka decoction in Vata-rakta.
- **What the text does not say.** Any dose, any duration, or the plant's description.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not translate the Sushruta disease terms into modern diagnoses.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations in the lines read are the goat's-milk potion and plaster and the decoction named above. The potion at line 106 is introduced with "two Tola weight" of other drugs; no quantity for Sushavi is printed in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Momordica charantia*, if that is the species, is a food and folk-medicine gourd, and the fruit has been studied for its effect on blood sugar. The identification is not secure and nothing here transfers to the Sushruta drug. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Sushavi alone. The Aragvadhadi group is described in chapter 38 as destroying Kapha and the effects of poison (lines above). The Aragvadhadi group is also one of those that together form the bitter group in chapter 42 (the bitter group is introduced at lines 333 to 336 of `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch42.txt`, and begins with "the groups ... known as the Aragvadhadi-Gana and the Guduchyadi-Gana"); the group's membership of that class is read from that sentence and is not a statement about Sushavi singly.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Sushavi is named in the Sushruta Samhita as a member of a therapeutic group (*gana*) (the Aragvadhadi group, Sutra ch. 38) and in three prescriptions of *Chikitsasthana* ch. 5. A *gana* is a grouping by therapeutic action. Neither a group nor a prescription for Vayu is a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status, and the text read assigns Sushavi neither. The chapters of the *Chikitsasthana* on *rasayana* and *vajikarana* (ch. 26 to 31 on disk) were searched for the name and it does not occur in them. Nothing links it to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The status is unattested; only a therapeutic one is recorded.

**Practitioner's note.** A bitter gourd-type drug in a group for Kapha, skin and poison and in two goat's-milk preparations for Vayu. A cultivator has nothing to use from it, and the record carries no long-term or life-nourishing claim.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The gloss to the bitter gourd rests on a two-column OCR pairing and is unconfirmed; the text itself separates Sushavi from Karavellaka. Nothing may be gathered, bought or used on this entry. [USER: verify]
- **Bitter gourd.** If the species is *Momordica charantia*, the fruit and seeds are generally advised against in pregnancy and may lower blood sugar; no source for this entry states it. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the unwell.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Medical conditions named in the text** (fever, skin disease, Vata disorders) need proper medical care; the entry gives no protocol.
