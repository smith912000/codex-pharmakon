# Rhododendron anthopogonoides — ba.lu, Bhalu, da.li (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Rhododendron anthopogonoides, the first of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for ba.lu (then "Rhododendron affcephalanthum (white type)", as printed); the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints "Rhododendron sp." by position against Bhalu and no anthopogonoides* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi, read in two modern editions, and under this binomial on Clark's edition alone.** The editions are Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, both in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. They are two translations of one tradition's text and are not two independent traditions. A search of the Shennong (Yang 1998), Sushruta, Ebers and Assyrian texts on disk for *Rhododendron* found only Yang line 3387, "Yang Zhi Zhu (Flos Rhododendri Sinensis) is acrid, warm, and toxic", a Shennong drug of another species, which is not read as this one. The Dioscorides text prints "rhododendron" at line 8782 only as one of the names of the Greek drug rododaphne (index line 11411), which is not read as this plant. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (line 203) the row is keyed by the Tibetan name *ba.lu*, with the identification "Rhododendron anthopogonoides; Rhododendron affcephalanthum (white type)" and the locus "Clark 1995 (line 1121)". The spine row (CPU-01781) took the **first** binomial, not the later one. The second binomial has no spine row of its own as far as the files read show. Clark's Latin index prints both (line 1639).

**Identity doubt.** Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance" and (line 68) that "the data given in some of these is occasionally suspect". The second name is spelled "affcephalanthum" here and "affcephoelanthum" at line 1217, which looks like a transcription fault; the Codex prints each as it stands. The Men-Tsee-Khang glossary gives only the genus. The Tibetan name does not fix the species, and the Codex does not choose. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1121).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 ("The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines", line 792), within "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (heading at line 997). It reads: "ba.lu: Rhododendron anthopogonoides: Rhododendron affcephalanthum (white type): [has three names, for example (bdud.rtsi.)da.li (name of the flower of the white type) grows on high shady hills. It has a white stem, blue leaf and a white or dark flower. The white type has sweet, bitter and astringent tastes and smooth power]. da.li cures phlegm disorders, hot and cold reactive conditions, [lung diseases, first stage oedema and hoarseness caused by any of the three humors, and is a recommended ingredient in essence-extraction compounds]."

Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries, and that those in round brackets come from the oral instructions of Dr. Yeshe Donden and Dr. Pema Dorje. The three names, the description, the tastes and power, and the last part of the sentence (lung diseases, first stage oedema, hoarseness, essence-extraction) are therefore the commentary layer. "(bdud.rtsi.)" and "(name of the flower of the white type)" are oral instruction. Outside the brackets, as Clark's marks stand, are the Tibetan name, the two Latin names and "da.li cures phlegm disorders, hot and cold reactive conditions". Clark's index lists *ba.lu* (line 1722) and "bdud.rtsi. (da.li.)" (line 1708).

*Clark 1995, other lines naming the plant.* In chapter 21, "The Principles of Medicines: Compounding of Medicines" (heading at line 1205), line 1217 reads: "The category of medicines which cure cold phlegm conditions includes Pomegranate, Black Pepper, Longpeper, medicinal Ginger, white-flowered Leadwort, Greater Cardamon, Smaller Cardamon, Cinnamon, Indian Beech, Embelia (Bay breng), Rhododendron affcephoelanthum Franch, Black Cumin seed, Trachyspermum amni, (black) Clematis, Anemone (rivularis), Ranunculus pulchellus, white mineral salt, lake salt, rock salt, horn salt and ash salt." It prints the second binomial, with the authority "Franch", and not *R. anthopogonoides*. In the Root Tantra, under Clark's heading "5. Methods of Treatment" (line 171) and the sub-heading "MEDICINES" (line 179), line 188 reads: "Phlegm disorders [are pacified] by compounds (in the form of) pills [principally of black aconite or of various salts] and calcinated powders (tres.sam) e.g., of pomegranate, white flower of Rhododendron anthopogonoides, rgod.ma.kha [a pungent medicinal mixture, e.g., the 'sixfold compound of Indian beech'], or of calcinated salts or calcinated calcite." The "e.g." clause naming the white flower is Clark's own and is not enclosed in either bracket mark, so which layer it belongs to is not shown.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (read through the name, not the binomial).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225), line 3493: "Bhalu eases the confrontation of hot and cold baekan disorders." The index lists "Bhalu 211" (line 7267). "Baekan" is a Tibetan term that the editors keep untranslated (note at line 686). The glossary's "B" block (lines 5091 to 5100) prints three phonetic names, "Bhalu", "Bhampo" and "Bhashaka" (lines 5091 to 5093), the transliterations "ba lu", "'bam po" and "ba sha ka" (lines 5095 to 5097), and the scientific names "Rhododendron sp. Heracluem millefolium" (line 5099) and "Justicia Adhatoda" (line 5100), three in all. Counted in order, "Bhalu" is first against "Rhododendron sp.", with a genus and no species. The third, "Bhashaka", agrees with Clark's *ba.sha.ka* ("Malabar Nut Tree: Adhatoda vasica", line 975). The glossary does not print the pairing in one row. [USER: verify]

The two editions agree in kind: Clark's drug "cures phlegm disorders, hot and cold reactive conditions", and the Men-Tsee-Khang *Bhalu* "eases the confrontation of hot and cold baekan disorders", where Clark's "phlegm" and the editors' *baekan* are the two translators' words. The Codex reads *Bhalu* and *ba.lu* as one drug from the spelling and that agreement; neither text says so. The Men-Tsee-Khang text also names a *Dali* (lines 3623 and 4435) in a baekan context, and the glossary sets *Rhododendron laudandum* against it by position (entry I_1346). Clark gives *da.li* as a name of this drug's white-flowered type, so the two Men-Tsee-Khang names may be one drug or two. Neither text settles it. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Rhododendron anthopogonoides*, *ba.lu*, *Bhalu* and *affcephalanthum* found no entry on this plant. I_1346 (*Rhododendron laudandum*, CPU-01782) is the sibling row from the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary line 5215, written in the same batch. I_1224 (*Adhatoda vasica*) prints the neighbouring binomial of the glossary "B" block. I_1228 (*Anemone rivularis*) and I_1053 (*Clematis montana*) are entries on drugs in Clark's line 1217 list. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Rhododendron* is a genus of the heath family (Ericaceae), and that many species are known to contain grayanotoxins in leaves and flowers (the "mad honey" toxins). Which compounds this species or the Tibetan drug holds is not shown by the lines read, and nothing is stated here about its composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a flower, da.li, that "cures phlegm disorders, hot and cold reactive conditions" (Clark, line 1121), that appears among the cold-phlegm medicines (line 1217) and in calcinated powders for phlegm (line 188), and a *Bhalu* that "eases the confrontation of hot and cold baekan disorders" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3493). These are the texts' claims, attached to this binomial only through the name, and they are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose. Hoarseness, lung disease and oedema need medical care, and no plant should be used for them on the strength of this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources give one preparation form. Clark's Root Tantra line 188 names a "calcinated powder (tres.sam)" of the "white flower of Rhododendron anthopogonoides" among examples for phlegm disorders, and the Men-Tsee-Khang text (line 4435) names "Dali in a powdered form" in a decoction treatment for baekan disorders. Neither gives a quantity, a frequency, a duration or a method of calcination. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Clark's commentary layer (line 1121) prints, for the white type, "sweet, bitter and astringent tastes and smooth power". No post-digestive taste is printed. The Men-Tsee-Khang lines read (3493, 5091 to 5100) print no taste or power. Clark's chapter 21 puts the plant among the medicines "which cure cold phlegm conditions" (line 1217), which is a use group and not a taste.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica gives this drug by what it does: the flower cures phlegm disorders and hot and cold reactive conditions. One line connects it to essence extraction. Clark's bracketed commentary layer, taken from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries, says da.li "is a recommended ingredient in essence-extraction compounds" (line 1121). The cultivation spec locates *bcud len* inside the Four Tantras, and says that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. The statement here is from commentary and not from the root-text layer, and the Men-Tsee-Khang lines read (3493, 3623, 4435) and its rejuvenation chapter (chapter 23, lines 3906 to 3950) do not repeat it, so the Codex grades it (b) and does not state a cultivation status for the plant. Clark's preface (line 69) says that "certain flowers" have been renowned in essence-extraction compounds and names none. Of the herb entries of Clark's chapter 20 that mention essence extraction (lines 971, 1043, 1078, 1103, 1121 and 1144), this is one. Medicine is the third of four treatment branches in this tradition, supporting practice without reaching its root, and the plant's framing here is therapeutic with a commentary note about essence extraction.

**Practitioner's note.** The lines read give a cultivator no practice with this plant: no preparation of an essence-extraction compound, no dose and no instruction. The one note is that a later commentary lists da.li as a recommended ingredient of such compounds, and a compound of that kind is a matter for a trained physician of the tradition and not for a reader of this entry. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. It is a general fact that *Rhododendron* leaves and flowers of several species are poisonous and that honey from their nectar has caused poisoning; this is not shown for this species in the lines read. The identity is open, the calcination method is not given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Lung disease, hoarseness and oedema need medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
