# Bread-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus — as printed by Bryan 1930 (Ebers papyrus)

## *A bread or cake of the "Zizyphus Lotus", as Bryan's English prints the ingredient; the botanical reading (Ziziphus lotus, the lotus jujube) is Bryan's own printed name and is a modern hypothesis, since the Egyptian word is not given in the sources retrieved. Confidence: low as to what was actually baked, medium as to the plant named* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Ebers papyrus among the texts on disk, in Bryan's 1930 English.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from a list of Bryan's plant remedies; this entry replaces it. A search of the Ebers text on disk (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`) for the name found the lines below and no other.

**Egyptian Papyrus Ebers (EP).** Bryan's Chapter VI, "Plant Remedies", lists the ingredient under the heading "Zizyphus-Lotus", with four forms: Bread-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus, Resin-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus, Splinters-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus and Wood-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus (`PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` lines 1353 to 1355; names.csv row, Ch. VI, line 1353). The same bread also appears in Bryan's Chapter V list of the different breads (line 1147: "Bread, Bread-crumbs, Bread-dough, Bread-meal, Fragrant Bread, Fermenting Bread, Bread-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus"). The recipes that use it are these.

1. A plaster for the abdomen. Under the heading "Another to drive out the hardening [in the] abdomen" the ingredients are Bread-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus, Cat's dung and Red-lead; after a page break the list goes on with Watermelon, Sweet Beer and Wine, and the instruction reads "Make into one and apply as a plaster" (lines 1900 to 1913). Bryan comments that "a plaster that combines Cat's dung with Watermelon might be counted on to remove 'hardenings' anywhere and in anybody" (line 1914).
2. The same remedy recurs later under "To drive away the hardening in the abdomen": Bread-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus, Watermelon, Cat's dung (lines 3544 to 3556; the list is cut by the page break in the e-text).
3. An internal remedy. Bryan's commentary on the *met* (his printed term, not interpreted here) gives, for "the *met* of the left side of the Body", a remedy "compounded of Figs, Grapes, and Onions, Bread-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus, Blossoms-of-the-Cucumber, Incense, Wine, and Sweet Beer. It was taken for four days." (line 3199).
4. Bryan also uses "a poultice consisting of the Zizyphus Lotus, Watermelon, Cat's dung, Sweet Beer, and Wine" as an example of preparations that "confound all criticism" (line 1377). It is probably a loose summary of recipe 1 and is not counted as a separate recipe.

The other three forms of the plant (resin, splinters, wood) are used elsewhere in the papyrus, for instance "Wood-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus" mixed in a yeast drink and used to anoint the Phallus (line 2431), and are not separate entries here. Whether they belong to this entry or to a general lotus-jujube entry is a question for the spine.

**Overlap with the spine.** CPU-01043 (Lotus Jujube, *Ziziphus lotus*, which already carries an Ebers tag) is the same plant under Bryan's name. This row is the bread form only. It is likely to be a duplicate of that row, or a variety folded into it, and is left to the maintainer. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent and do not say how the bread was made. In general terms, jujube-type fruits (the genus *Ziziphus*) are sweet, with sugars and some mucilage, and have long been eaten fresh, dried or ground. The lotus jujube is a thorny shrub of the Mediterranean and Saharan margins. None of this comes from a source retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** A plaster to drive out a "hardening" of the abdomen, and one ingredient in a four-day internal remedy for a condition Bryan calls the *met* of the left side.
- **What the text does not say.** No amount of the bread, no way of making it, and no statement of why it was chosen.
- **The company it keeps.** In the plaster the bread sits with cat's dung and red lead. The plaster is a historical curiosity. The Codex does not recommend any part of it, and the lead-based ingredient is a poison.

The Codex gives no protocol for this material. Anything drawn from it would rest on a single translated line and on a modern guess at the plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those listed above: a plaster (with cat's dung, red lead, watermelon, sweet beer and wine) and an internal remedy (with figs, grapes, onions, cucumber blossoms, incense, wine and sweet beer) taken for four days (line 3199). No quantity is given for the bread. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any recipe from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Ziziphus fruits are edible and sweet. Nothing in modern pharmacology supports a plaster of fruit bread, cat's dung and red lead for an abdominal "hardening", and red lead (a lead oxide) is toxic. The Ebers terms *met* and "hardening" are not translated here into modern diagnoses. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

**Egyptian (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. **Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do with it. A fruit bread in a pair of abdominal recipes, with no recorded quantity, no preparation and no cultivation status. A cultivator reads it as a record of what Egyptian physicians put on and in a patient, not as something to take.

### Contraindications

- **Do not use the recipes.** The abdominal plaster contains cat's dung (an infection risk on skin) and red lead (a lead compound; poisonous).
- **Internal remedy.** It contains incense, wine and beer as well as the bread. No safety statement is made in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The bread is Bryan's English label. What was baked is not shown. Nobody should substitute a modern jujube product for it.
