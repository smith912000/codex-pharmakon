# Human excrement — "Human excreta" (Midruen, in the Men-Tsee-Khang transliteration; rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20, "Droppings")
## *Human faeces named in the Tibetan materia medica; a historical record, not a recommended practice* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The locus is Clark's 1995 translation, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, "the powers of medicines" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). Section (8), "Medicines of Animal Origin", lists "thirteen medicines of animal origin ... horn, bone, meat, blood, bile, fat, brain, skin, nails, hair, urine, droppings and whole body" (lines 1147-1148). Human material is therefore handled by this text inside the animal group. Under the sub-heading "Droppings" (line 1198) the text reads, at line 1199:

"Vulture's droppings: increase digestive heat, disperse tumors and suppurate swellings. Pig's stool: cures indigestion, septic disorders, infectious fevers and bile tumors. Human excreta: cure gall stones, neutralize poisons, reduce swellings [and subdue spirits]. Horse dung: is anthelmintic, anti-emetic, cures wind-bile combinations [and is beneficial against spirits]. Rabbit droppings: purge oedema [and are beneficial for wounds]. Dog stool: [is especially beneficial against spirits]. ..."

For human excreta the text gives three plain actions (it cures gall stones, neutralises poisons and reduces swellings) and a bracketed fourth (it subdues spirits). `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 377 lists "Midruen", animal, with the source "Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Efficacy of Medicinal Substances)". That is the Men-Tsee-Khang transliteration, and the glossary of the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` lines 4920 and 4936) lists *Mi drun* (*mi brun*) in a column of five *Mi* terms glossed "Human urine / Human excreta / Human brain / Human flesh / Human fat". The pairing rests on the column layout of the scanned glossary and is my reading. [USER: verify]

The Men-Tsee-Khang text (line 3568) has: "Midruen cure cholelithiasis, act as an antidote against poisons and reduces swelling." Cholelithiasis is the medical word for gallstones, so the two translations agree on all three plain actions, and the Men-Tsee-Khang line carries no "spirits" clause. Human urine is a separate item in the "Urine" group (Clark line 1197) and is not covered here.

Clark's preface says that "nowadays only very few animal products are used, although many are documented in the traditional texts" (line 57). He also says that he "interpolated liberally from the Blue Aquamarine commentary" (line 80); the square brackets in the lines above are not explained in the lines read, so which words belong to the root text is not established here. [USER: verify] The "spirits" clause is a statement about spirit-caused illness in the Tibetan frame and is recorded only as the text states it.

### Active Components

Human faeces are mainly water, bacteria (a large fraction of the dry mass is bacterial), undigested fibre, bile pigments and salts, and shed cells. They are a major carrier of intestinal pathogens. The text gives no preparation and no analysis was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. This entry records what the Tibetan text says about human excreta, and nothing else.

It is not a recommended practice. Human faeces are a source of serious infection, and taking them as a medicine is hazardous. The Codex's application layer offers no protocol, no preparation and no substitute.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation, dose or route is given for human excreta in the lines read in either translation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] Whether the material was fresh, dried or burnt, and whether taken or applied, is not stated.

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General facts: untreated human faeces transmit bacterial, viral and parasitic infections, including cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A and intestinal worms. No faecal material treats gallstones or neutralises poisons; gallstones are managed by diet, medication or surgery, and poisoning requires urgent medical care. The one clinical use of human stool today, faecal microbiota transplantation for certain recurrent gut infections, is carried out with screened donors and processed material under clinical control, and has no connection to the raw material in this entry.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT): the chapter 20 line gives actions only (cures gall stones, neutralises poisons, reduces swellings). It prints no taste, potency or post-digestive taste for human excreta, and none is assigned here.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Human excreta are attested in the Explanatory Tantra ch.20 animal section as a therapeutic substance, with three plain actions and one bracketed action against spirits. The frame it sits in is read from Clark's preface and root text: the three humors (wind, bile, phlegm) "are rooted in attachment, hatred and obscuration respectively" (line 51), and treatment relies on "the fourfold food, behavioral pattern, medicine and accessory therapy" (line 1299). Nothing in the lines read gives this substance a place in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or in any practice of the subtle body, and no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition. The text does not say why this material was chosen.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this. The text lists human excreta as a medicine in a group of droppings, with no cultivation claim and no method. It is recorded here as history only.

### Contraindications

- **Not a recommended practice.** This records a historical entry in a Tibetan materia medica. Nothing here is advice to take or apply human faeces.
- **Infection and parasites:** faeces carry bacteria, viruses and parasite eggs; ingestion or application to broken skin is dangerous.
- **Gallstones, poisoning and swellings:** these need ordinary medical assessment; poisoning is an emergency.
- **Unknown preparation and dose:** neither is given in the sources, so no safe use can be described.
- **Spirit claims:** recorded as the text states them and not a basis for treatment.
