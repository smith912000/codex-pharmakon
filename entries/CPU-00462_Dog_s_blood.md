# Dog's blood — "Dog's Blood" (Bryan's English rendering of the Ebers Papyrus)
## *The blood of the animal Bryan calls "Dog"; Bryan prints no Egyptian word and no binomial, so the spine row's "Canis lupus familiaris" is the list-maker's gloss and is not read from the text* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter VII "Organic Remedies (animals)". "Dog" stands in Bryan's list of the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1404, after "Hog" and before "Cat"; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 116, class animal, "no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). The parts-of-animals list (lines 1405-1415) includes "Blood" but ties it to no animal. The spine row reads "Dog's blood, Canis lupus familiaris" with the note "Ebers; Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus" and a marker that the identification is uncertain (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 86). Wikipedia was not used.

**The one prescription.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "dog", "bitch", "hound" and "blood". Dog's blood occurs once, in Chapter XVI "Diseases of the Eyes" (heading lines 2720-2721), in Bryan's account of ingrowing eyelashes (introduced at line 2858). The prescription is headed "TO PREVENT THE HAIR GROWING INTO THE EYE AFTER IT HAS BEEN PULLED OUT" (line 2882). The ingredients are printed in a column: "Incense-ground-in-Lizard's-dung" (line 2884), then "Cow's Blood, Ass's Blood, Pig's Blood, Dog's Blood, Stag's Blood" (lines 2885-2888), "Collyrium" (line 2888) and "Incense", with the direction (line 2894): "Crush, rub into one in the different kinds of Blood, and place on the part whence the Hair has been pulled out so that it may not grow again." The dog's blood is one of five bloods in one compound. The text read does not say whether the five bloods were mixed in equal parts or in what amount; Bryan's rendering "the different kinds of Blood" implies that they are used together.

**Related items, printed separately, not counted as the blood.** The dog supplies other things in Bryan's text, each a different item: "Dog's dung" (with a hog's tooth and cat's dung in a poultice, lines 2683 and 2688); "Toes-of-a-Dog" (with date refuse and an ass's hoof, the first hair remedy in the chapter, which Bryan says was prepared for a queen, lines 3994-3996); "Vulva-of-a-Bitch" (a hair-dye with a tapeworm and an ass's hoof, lines 1378 and 4057); and a rubric, "PUT A DOG'S HIDE ON HIM! DO NOT PRESS THE HAND ON HIM!" (line 2178; the remedy it belongs to was not analysed). None is the blood.

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say what kind of dog is meant, how the blood was obtained, or why a dog's blood was among the five. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. The Ghalioungui 1987 glossary was not opened and has no dog row in `_sources/ebers.csv`.

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it. In general terms, mammalian blood contains water, proteins and cells, and it clots and dries quickly. Nothing in the sources retrieved says that dog blood contains anything that acts on hair follicles. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show dog's blood as one of five bloods, with incense and collyrium, put on the lid after eyelashes had been pulled out. No quantity is legible. Raw animal blood near the eye is an infection risk, and the dog is a companion animal. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Contraindications

- **Eye safety:** animal blood near the eye risks infection; ingrowing lashes need medical care.
- **Hygiene and infection:** dog blood can carry pathogens shared with people, and the compound mixes the blood of five species.
- **Legal and ethical:** taking blood from or killing a dog for this purpose is cruelty in most jurisdictions; the compound should not be reproduced.
