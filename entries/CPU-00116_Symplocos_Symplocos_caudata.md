### Origin & Tradition

The genus *Symplocos* yields, from several species, the Ayurvedic drug **Lodhra** — the astringent bark of *Symplocos racemosa* and related species such as *S. caudata* of the Himalaya. (The genus is also famous as the "sweetleaf" group and as an aluminium-accumulator historically used in dyeing as a natural mordant.) The bark has a long and focused place in the Indian materia medica as a **cooling astringent** specifically for the female reproductive system and for fluxes and lax tissues.

In Ayurveda Lodhra is the classic **uterine astringent and haemostatic** — the premier remedy for *menorrhagia* (excessive menstrual bleeding) and *leucorrhoea* (vaginal discharge), and for other uterine and gynaecological complaints, where its astringent, cooling, *kapha-pitta*-reducing action binds, cools and staunches. Beyond gynaecology it serves the general astringent purposes: for diarrhoea, bleeding, relaxed and inflamed tissues, spongy gums and as a wound astringent, and (the bark powder) cosmetically for the skin. The Himalayan and folk traditions use the related species similarly.

The astringency derives from **tannins**, and the bark also contains characteristic **alkaloids (loturine and relatives)** and flavonoids/triterpenes, giving the traditional haemostatic and astringent uses a phytochemical basis.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS).** No cultivation status is attested for *Symplocos caudata*. The Sushruta Samhita names *Lodhra* only as an ingredient in wound plasters, medicated oils and powders (Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch. 36, Bhishagratna tr., Vol. I), a therapeutic use; the translation does not tie the name to this species, and Lodhra is conventionally assigned to *S. racemosa*. No *rasayana* or *vajikarana* assignment for Lodhra was found in the text read. The tag is therefore unconfirmed for this species: grade (c).

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Himalayan species has no recorded cultivation role; the Ayurvedic Lodhra astringent use belongs to the better-attested bark of the racemosa group.

### Contraindications

- **Constipation / very dry conditions.** The astringent action can over-bind.
- **Pregnancy — caution.** Uterine-acting astringent; medicinal use warrants caution. [USER: verify.]
- **Heavy/abnormal uterine bleeding.** Significant or unexplained bleeding requires medical assessment, not self-treatment.
- **Allergy.** Possible individual sensitivity.
