# Wang Sun 王孙 (Shennong bencao jing); Yang 1998: "Rhizoma Paridis Tetraphyllae"; alias Huang Sun 黄孙, Man Yan 蔓延, Gong Cao 公草, Hai Sun 海孙 (Wu Pu)

## *A creeping or climbing herb of the middle class of the Shennong bencao jing, whose root or rhizome is the drug wang sun; Yang 1998 labels it "Rhizoma Paridis Tetraphyllae", that is Paris tetraphylla A. Gray, a four-leaved species of the lily family. The identification is the translator's and ours by adoption, at low to medium confidence: the classic describes no plant, the Wu Pu layer's description ("red markings, stem and leaves corresponding") is not clearly that of a Paris, and the name wang sun has been applied to other plants.* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) is the second witness. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Rhizoma Paridis Tetraphyllae [SBJ]", taken from Yang's English (Yang line 3035; names file `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv`, line 139); this entry replaces it. It is a different drug from Zao Xiu (Paris rhizome, `I_1534_Paris_rhizome.md`, SBJ lower class). A search of the other texts on disk for the Chinese and pinyin names found nothing for this drug.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 王孙 in the middle classic (Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, `...daizhige.txt` lines 2305-2313; the name is also in the middle-class list of the preface at line 1677, between 女菀 and 蜀羊泉). The classic's own paragraph (line 2307) reads: 味苦，平。主五脏邪气，寒湿痹，四肢疼酸，膝冷痛。生川谷。 ("Bitter, balanced. Governs evil qi in the five viscera, cold-damp impediment, aching and soreness of the four limbs, cold pain in the knees. Grows in river valleys.") No part used is named, and there is no 久服 clause.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, line 2309) gives alternative names 黄孙, 王孙, 蔓延, 公草, 海孙, and records that "Shennong and Lei Gong: bitter, nontoxic; Huangdi: sweet, nontoxic", with a habitat of "the western sea mountain valleys and under city walls at Runan" and a description 蔓延，赤文，茎叶相当 (quoting the *Yulan*), which may be read as "spreading, with red markings, stem and leaves matching". The Mingyi bielu layer (line 2311) adds regional names (吴: 白功草; 楚: 王孙; 齐: 长孙), further aliases (黄昏, 海孙, 蔓延) and the habitat "west of the sea and under the walls of Runan". Sun's note (案, line 2313) quotes Tao Hongjing: "the prescription-masters now all call it 王昏, and also 壮蒙". These layers are variant and later readings; they identify the plant by name and habitat, and they do not fix a species. The plant they describe is a creeper, which does not clearly match a four-leaved Paris. [USER: verify]

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 3035): "Wang Sun (Rhizoma Paridis Tetraphyllae) is bitter and balanced. It is nontoxic, treating mainly evil qi in the five viscera, cold damp impediment, aching pain in the limbs, and cold pain in the knees. It grows in rivers and valleys." Yang's "nontoxic" is not in Sun's classic paragraph; Sun has the statement only in the Wu Pu layer (Shennong and Lei Gong, Huangdi: nontoxic). Yang's page is the middle class ("Herbs: Middle Class, PDF p.81"). Sun and Yang agree on the grade.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Paris* is a genus of rhizomatous herbs of the lily family, found in Asia and Europe, with whorled leaves; some species carry steroidal saponins. The classic's "cold-damp impediment" has no modern equivalent and is not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle classic, whose preface (Sun line 1677) reads 中药一百二十种为臣，主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经: the middle drugs are "ministers, mainly nourishing nature (养性) to correspond with man; nontoxic or toxic, weigh their suitability; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the emaciated". The drug's own paragraph names only complaints (evil qi, impediment, limb ache) and has no 久服 clause and no promise of lightening the body, of not ageing or of prolonging life. The register is therefore (b): framed by the middle class, with no cultivation status attested for this drug. The Wu Pu "nontoxic" is a variant layer and does not count toward a grade.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-class herb recorded for cold-damp pain in the joints and limbs. The classic's own grading places it between the life-nourishing upper class and the short-course lower class, and nothing in the record supports any long-term cultivation use. A cultivator has nothing to do with it, and the species is not settled.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The species is the translator's label adopted with low to medium confidence; the name wang sun has been applied to other plants, and the Wu Pu description is of a creeper. Do not gather or use a plant on this label. [USER: verify]
- **Toxicity.** The classic's paragraph states none; the Wu Pu layer and Yang say nontoxic, but the middle-class preface says the class may be either toxic or nontoxic, and *Paris* species are generally regarded as medicinal-toxic plants. Do not ingest. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Impediment, limb and knee pain** are medical conditions; use proper care.
