# Ganoderma, white — Bai Zhi 白芝 (Shennong bencao jing); alias Yu Zhi 玉芝 ("Jade Ganoderma")

## *One of the six colour-named "zhi" 芝 of the Shennong classic, a fungus of the zhi group; the species is not settled. The pre-filled guess "Ganoderma lucidum, white variety" is not supported by any text on disk (red zhi is the one filed under G. lucidum, I_110): Yang 1998 prints only the Latin-form colour label "Ganoderma Alba". Not to be confused with Bai Zhi 白芷, the root of Angelica dahurica, a different drug with the same pinyin. Identification: fungus of the zhi group, by the Chinese name and Yang's label; species unresolved; confidence low* | *(Volume IV — Fungal/Algal, shelved in Volume I)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** White zhi is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; Yang's 1998 English edition (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) and the plain e-text (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_plain_daizhige.txt`) are read as second and third witnesses. The placeholder row of 13 October cited two lines of `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` for "Bai Zhi". Only the first, "Herbs: Superior Class, PDF p.37 (line 1424)", is the ganoderma. The second, "Herbs: Middle Class, PDF p.76 (line 2835)", is 白芷, Radix Angelicae Dahuricae (Yang line 2835 onward: "Bai Zhi (Radix Angelicae Dahuricae) is acrid and warm..."), a plant drug of the middle class, which is a different drug. It is not used in this entry.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), Sun Xingyan edition.** The entry is 白芝 in the upper classic (卷一 上经), `Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 623 to 625. It reads 味辛，平 ("acrid, balanced"); 主咳逆上气 ("governs cough with counterflow qi ascent"); 益肺气 ("boosts lung qi"); 通利口鼻 ("frees and disinhibits the mouth and nose"); 强志意，勇悍 ("strengthens will and intent; courage and sturdiness"); 安魄 ("quiets the *po* soul"); 久食，轻身、不老、延年、神仙 ("taken for a long time: lightens the body, no ageing, prolongs years, immortal-spirit"); 一名玉芝 ("one name Yu zhi, Jade zhi"). The Mingyi bielu layer (line 643) says 白芝，生华山 ("white zhi grows on Mount Hua") and, of the six zhi together, 皆无毒，六月、八月采 ("all non-toxic, gathered in the sixth and eighth months"). Sun's note (案, line 645) quotes the *Baopuzi*, "Xian yao pian" (as Sun cites it), and for white it reads 白者，如截肪 ("the white is like cut fat"). Line 639 records that the old text had "six kinds" which Sun merged (旧作六种，今并).

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (lines 1424 to 1430, his page 37): "Bai Zhi (Ganoderma Alba) is acrid and balanced. It mainly treats cough and counterflow qi ascent, boosts the lung qi, disinhibits the mouth and nose, fortifies the will [to cultivate] bravery and undauntedness, and quiets the corporeal soul. Protracted taking may make the body light, prevent senility, and prolong life so as to make one an immortal. Its other name is Yu Zhi (Jade Ganoderma). It grows in mountains and valleys." The plain e-text, `Shennong_plain_daizhige.txt` line 109, carries the same paragraph. Yang's footnote 81 (line 1444 onward) is commentary and not the classic: "All the different kinds of Ganoderma except for Purple Ganoderma are explained according to five phase theory. Take White Ganoderma for example. White corresponds to metal which, in turn, corresponds to the lungs. Therefore, it supplements the lung qi, boosts the corporeal soul, and is able to disinhibit the qi track [i.e., the respiratory track], the nose, and mouth." Yang's footnote 79 (line 1400) says the book divides *Ganoderma* into six types by colour, calls it "Immortal Weed", and says it is "said to even cultivate virtues".

A search of the other texts on disk for the Chinese and pinyin names found nothing further for this drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Cough with counterflow qi ascent; it boosts lung qi, frees the mouth and nose, and strengthens will and courage, and quiets the *po* soul.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no manner of eating it, no species.

The Codex does not translate these terms into modern diagnoses and gives no protocol. Nobody should forage or buy an unidentified fungus because of a classical colour name.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph says 久食 ("taken for a long time") and gives no preparation. The only handling statement is the Mingyi layer's harvest note for all six zhi (sixth and eighth months, line 643). The upper-class preface (Sun line 43; Yang lines 352 to 358) says the upper drugs may be taken in large amounts and for a long time without harming people. That is a statement about the class, not a dose for this fungus. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. "Zhi" 芝 is a Chinese group name for certain woody fungi; the best known in modern use is *Ganoderma lucidum* (reishi), treated at I_110. Yang's "Ganoderma Alba" is a colour label, and the colour variants of the classic are not generally assigned to separate, published species. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Sun 1799).** Flavour and nature 味辛，平, "acrid, balanced" (line 625). Non-toxic, with the other five zhi (Mingyi layer, line 643).
- **SBJ (Yang 1998).** "Acrid and balanced" (line 1424).
- The paragraph ties the drug to the lung (益肺气) and to the 魄 soul. The lung and metal correspondence in the note is Yang's footnote 81 (line 1444 onward), which reads the paragraph in five-phase terms; it is a modern editor's gloss, and the classic's own text names the lung, not metal. ### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* White zhi stands in the upper classic (上经), whose preface (Sun line 43) reads 上药一百二十种，为君，主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经: the upper drugs are "sovereigns, mainly nourishing life (养命) to correspond with heaven, non-toxic; taken in large amounts and for a long time they do not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body (轻身), boost qi, not age (不老) and prolong years (延年), base [the effort] on the upper classic". The drug's own paragraph carries the promise: 久食，轻身、不老、延年、神仙 (Sun line 625; plain e-text line 109; Yang line 1424 onward). Sun and Yang place the drug in the same class, so no difference is to be stated. The paragraph also names qualities of will and courage (强志意，勇悍) and the quieting of the *po* soul (安魄); these are the classic's own terms and are not carried into any other tradition's vocabulary here. *Qing shen* 輕身 is not weight loss or any pharmacological effect. The attestation is for the white zhi named in the classic and does not establish that any mushroom now sold or foraged is that zhi. The Neidan reading of the zhi is an overlay that cultivators bring and is not a *bencao* category; it is not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade drug in the classic's own scheme, the kind taken long-term for nourishing life, but with no identified species, no dose and no preparation in the record. A cultivator can read it as the tradition's own account of an upper-grade fungus and should not act on it by eating any fungus.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The white zhi has no settled species. Do not gather or eat wild fungi on the strength of this entry; many are poisonous and some are lethal.
- **Not Bai Zhi 白芷.** Do not mix this drug up with the angelica root of the same pinyin, a different drug that the classic lists in the middle class.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, people on medicines.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Cough and counterflow qi.** Persistent cough is a medical condition; see a clinician.
