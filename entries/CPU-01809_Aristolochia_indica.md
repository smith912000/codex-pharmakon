# Aristolochia indica — Ahigandha, Arka-mula (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3)

## *Aristolochia indica, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints, with the English "Indian birthwort", against the Sanskrit name Ahigandha (line 31207), and prints again without English against Arka-mula (line 31331). The pairing of each Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and the only thing it holds is a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The appendix is in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. I searched the chapter files (Sutra, Chikitsa, Uttara, Kalpa, Nidana, Sharira) for both Sanskrit names and found neither: no "Ahigandha" (or "Ahi-gandha" or "Ahigand"), no "Arka-mula" (or "Arkamula" or "Arka-mool"), and no "Aristolochia" or "birthwort". The Latin is therefore the only evidence the Codex has that Sushruta names this plant, and it is a proposal from the appendix's layout. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Tibetan text folders found other *Aristolochia* species under their own chapters and entries, which this entry does not treat. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. Quotations keep the translator's words; the OCR's garbled diacritics are normalised. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** The row has two source rows in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, each keyed by a Sanskrit name as the OCR prints it: line 355, "AhigandbsL" (appendix line 31149; Latin line 31207, "Aristolochia Indica. Indian birthwort.", pairing "by position, block of 31"), and line 392, "Arka-mnla" (appendix line 31275; Latin line 31331, "Aristolochia Indica.", "proposed by a Sonnet agent ... checked by script"). The OCR spelling "AhigandbsL" is a damaged "Ahigandha".

**The pairing, checked by counting.** The first page of the appendix prints thirty-one Sanskrit names (31126 to 31181) and thirty-one Latin entries (31184 to 31235), in the same order. "Ahigandha" is the fourteenth Sanskrit name: Abhaya, Abhraka, Adhaki, Aditya-vaktra, Ardraka, Agairadhama, Agasti, Agastya, Agni, Agnika, Agni-mantha, Aguru, Ahifena, **Ahigandha**. The fourteenth Latin entry is "Aristolochia Indica. Indian birthwort." (31207). The neighbours fit (the thirteenth, "Papaver Somniferum. Opium.", with Ahifena, and the twelfth, "Aquilaria agallocha.", with Aguru). On the second page, the name run is Amlika, Amra, Amrataka, Amritaka, Ananta with Anantamula (bracketed), Ankotaka, Apamarga, Aparajita, Aragvadha, Aravinda, Arevata, Arimeda, two Arishtas, Arjaka, Arjuna, Arka, **Arka-mula**, Arka-pushpi (31244 to 31277); the Latin run (31312 to 31332) is "Tamatindus Indica", "Mangifera Indica", "Sapondias Mangifera", "See Guduchi", "Hemidesmus Indicus", "Alangium hexapetalum", "Achyranthis Aspera", "Clitoria Ternatia", "Cassia Fistula", "See Ambhoja", "See Aragvadha", "Accacia Farnesiana", "Sapindus trifoliatus", "See Nimba", "Ocimum Album", "Terminalia Arjuna", "Calotropis gigantia" (31330, Arka), **"Aristolochia Indica."** (31331, Arka-mula) and "Gynandropsis Pentaphylla" (31332, Arka-pushpi). The counts agree and the neighbours fit (*Terminalia arjuna* with Arjuna, *Calotropis* with Arka). [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* Nothing. Neither Sanskrit name is in any chapter file. The name "Arka" is (Sutra 14, 36, 38, 39, 45, 46 and others), but the appendix prints "Calotropis gigantia" against it (line 31330), and the drug named "Arka" is the subject of the Codex's I_895, not of this entry. "Arka-mula" is in the appendix only. The Sutra 38 group list headed "The Arkardi Group" (lines 102 to 114) names "the Arka, Alarka, the two kinds of Karanja, Nagadanti, Mayuraka, Bhargi, Rasna, Indrapushpi ..." and no "Arka-mula". A plain "Ahi-" prefix does occur in the chapters in other compounds (Ahi-putana, a disease, in Chikitsa 20 and Nidana 13; Ahi-pushpa in Chikitsa 17, line 215; Ahi-patika in Kalpa 4, line 229), but none is "Ahigandha", and the Codex does not read them as this plant.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Ahigandha*, *Arka-mula* and *Aristolochia indica* found I_895 (Crown Flower, Madar; tags CM, SS), which quotes the same appendix lines (`names.csv` lines 391 to 393) in an identification note on Arka, where Arka-mula and Arka-pushpi are the two names that follow Arka, and lists the chapter passages that name Arka. Other *Aristolochia* entries are of different species and mostly different traditions: I_035 (*A. clematitis*, tags EP, AT, DMM, CM, SS, SBJ), I_1011 (*A. clematitis*), I_1012 (*A. pistolochia*) and I_1234 (*A. contorta*). Nothing is merged. A data flag: I_035 carries SS on its tag line and its SS paragraph describes a snake-bite and wound use; the Sushruta recheck notes `Sources/SS_Sushruta/RECHECK_2026-10-07.md` (lines 115 to 117) found no name match for it in Volumes I to III or in `evidence.csv`. That is the parent's to decide.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, plants of the genus *Aristolochia* (family Aristolochiaceae) contain aristolochic acids, which are well-established kidney and urinary-tract toxins and carcinogens. That is a statement about the genus, not about what Sushruta used under an unnamed Sanskrit word. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

None. The Sushruta chapters retrieved say nothing of this drug under either Sanskrit name, so there is no tradition statement to record. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose, and it recommends none. Plants of this genus should not be taken internally (see the contraindications).

### Contraindications

Do not use any *Aristolochia* internally. Aristolochic acids are recognised as kidney-damaging and cancer-causing, and the genus is restricted or banned as a medicine in many countries. Nothing in the Sushruta text retrieved offsets that. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read, and the genus should be avoided in pregnancy. [USER: verify]
