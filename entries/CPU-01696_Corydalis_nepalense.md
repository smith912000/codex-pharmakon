# Corydalis nepalense — Rekon, re skon (rGyud-bzhi, Men-Tsee-Khang glossary, by column position)

## *Corydalis nepalense, as the binomial printed in the scientific-name run of the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary, set against "Rekon" / "re skon" by column position only; Clark 1995 prints re.skon under Corydalis edulis and two Potentilla species, and not under this binomial* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT), and under this binomial only by the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. Both Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The extraction note reads: "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, line 344). The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (lines 5667 to 5683).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, the "R" block. The glossary prints its columns in separate runs. The phonetic names are "Ranye Rechagpa / Rekon Rerel Risho Ru-ta" (lines 5667 and 5668), the Tibetan transliterations are "ra mnye / re lchag pa / re skon re ral ri sho / ru ta" (lines 5671 to 5674), and the scientific names are on one line (5676): "Polygonatum cirhifolium Stellera chamaejasme Corydalis nepalense Polystichum squarros Ligularia cymbulifera Saussurea lappa". The English column reads "Solomon’s seal", "-", "-", "Fern", "Sheathing groundel", "Costus" (lines 5678 to 5683). Six phonetic names pair with six scientific names. "Rekon" is the third in both runs, and the English column has "-" at the third place (line 5680). The count can be checked on neighbours: "Ru-ta" stands last against *Saussurea lappa* and "Costus", "Rerel" stands fourth against "Polystichum squarros" and "Fern", and "Ranye" stands first against *Polygonatum*, as Clark's *ra.mnye* line (1144) lists *Polygonatum* species. The Codex therefore reads the glossary as pairing "Rekon" with *Corydalis nepalense*, by column position only and not as a printed row. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text.* The text prints the name in three passages and gives no Latin name in any of them. In chapter 19 (which ends at line 3264) it is in the bitter group (line 3213): "Nimpa, Tikta, Bongnga karpo, Honglen, Sergyi metok, Dhungmo nyung, La tsi, Tripa, Kyerpa, Bhashaka, Drak shhun, Kyiche, Rekon and other similar substances are the group of medicinal substances having a bitter taste." In chapter 20 (line 3428), under "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines": "Rekon dries up blood, cures baekan mukpo and hot disorder of the channels." In chapter 21 (lines 3598 to 3600): "Tsendhen marpo, zhomo, Sengdeng, Honglen, Bhashaka, Kyurura, Rekon, Pang tsi dhowo, Tsod and Tshoe are the group of medicines that cure blood disorders." The index (line 7478) lists "Rekon 187,206,220". That the "Rekon" of these passages is the glossary's "Rekon" rests on the identical spelling. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, the same Tibetan name under other binomials (line 1002).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance". The entry reads:

"re.skon: Corydalis edulis: Potentilla tanacetifolia: Potentilla supina: [has seven names, for example, rtsi.dmar and phug.ron.rkang.bchas. It grows in rocky meadows, has spreading leaves like Nelumbium nucifer (‘utpala’) and a single root with eight tendrils. The flower is yellow and retains dewdrops. It is well-scented and has extremely bitter taste]. Corydalis is hemostatic and cures channel fever caused by brown phlegm [and dysentery with fever]."

Clark's preface (line 82) says: "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". Inside the brackets (the commentary layer) are the seven names, the habitat, the leaves, the root, the flower, the scent, the "extremely bitter taste" and the closing "and dysentery with fever". Outside the brackets (the root-text layer, as far as Clark's brackets mark it) are the Tibetan name, the three Latin names, which are Clark's identifications and not the root text's, and the clause "Corydalis is hemostatic and cures channel fever caused by brown phlegm". Clark prints no *Corydalis nepalense*, and the glossary prints no *Corydalis edulis*. The two Tibetan texts agree on the name, the bitter taste and the blood and channel actions, and differ on the species; the Codex does not choose. Two other Clark lines name *Corydalis edulis* in the bitter group (line 768) and in the class of medicines for blood disorders (line 1210), and are treated in I_1257.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Corydalis nepalense*, *re.skon* and *Rekon* found no entry on this plant. I_1257 (*Corydalis edulis*), written in this batch, is the Clark-binomial sibling row for the same Tibetan name, and the two entries share their Tibetan evidence. I_1225 (*Adiantum pedatum*) quotes the same "R" block of the glossary (including line 5676) for its neighbour "Rerel", and is not an entry on this plant. I_1258 (*Corydalis meifolia*) and I_1259 (*Corydalis melanochlora*) are sibling rows of the same genus on other Tibetan names. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Corydalis* is a genus of the poppy family (Papaveraceae, in its fumitory subfamily) and that the genus is generally known to contain isoquinoline alkaloids. Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained, because its Latin name is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Men-Tsee-Khang text records a bitter drug, "Rekon", that "dries up blood, cures baekan mukpo and hot disorder of the channels" (line 3428) and stands in the group of medicines for blood disorders (lines 3598 to 3600). Clark records the same name as "hemostatic" and curing "channel fever caused by brown phlegm" (line 1002). These are the texts' claims, attached to this binomial only by a column position, and they are not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation for this drug. Clark's commentary describes a single root with eight tendrils but does not say which part is taken, and no quantity, frequency or duration is given in either text. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Bitter, by the taste-group list (Men-Tsee-Khang line 3213; Clark line 768 for the Clark binomial) and, in Clark's commentary, "extremely bitter taste" (line 1002, inside the brackets). No power (hot or cool) and no post-digestive taste is printed for this drug in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Men-Tsee-Khang text classes the drug named Rekon by taste (bitter) and by what it does: it dries up blood, it cures hot disorder of the channels, and it stands in the group of medicines for blood disorders. Clark says the same under another binomial. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this name places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the passages cited above were read in full and none mentions one. Its framing is therapeutic only, and the link from this binomial to that framing rests on a column position.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a bitter blood and channel drug in the Tibetan texts, and the glossary pairs its name with this binomial only by column order while Clark gives another species. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. The identity rests on a column position and the genus is generally known to contain alkaloids, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Bleeding and fever need medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
