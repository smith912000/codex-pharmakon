### Origin & Tradition

Few herbs were more esteemed in the ancient and medieval European pharmacy than betony, a modest purple-flowered mint-family plant of woods and meadows. A whole treatise — attributed to **Antonius Musa**, physician to the Emperor Augustus — was devoted to its virtues, listing dozens of ailments it was said to cure, and the medieval esteem was such that the Italian proverb "sell your coat and buy betony" and the saying "he has as many virtues as betony" passed into common speech. Dioscorides records it, and through the Anglo-Saxon leechbooks (the *Lacnunga*, Bald's *Leechbook*) betony was a pre-eminent **amuletic and protective herb** — grown in churchyards, worn against the "elf-sickness," night-fears and bad dreams, and credited with driving off despair and ill influence.

Stripped of the magical accretions, betony's core medicinal identity is as a **head and nerve herb** and a **vulnerary**. Its most enduring use is for **headache** — especially the chronic, tension and "nervous" headache — and for vertigo, anxiety and frayed nerves, as a gentle calming "tonic of the head." Alongside this it was a wound herb (the bruised leaf to staunch and clean wounds), a mild astringent for loose bowels and relaxed tissues, a bitter digestive, and a remedy for coughs and catarrh. The plant is a member of the mint family (Lamiaceae) and shares that family's aromatic, mildly astringent, gently tonic character.

Modern phytochemistry finds betony rich in **phenylethanoid glycosides** (the verbascoside/acteoside group), bitter diterpenoids and tannins — constituents consistent with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, mild astringent and nervine activity, giving a measured footing to the gentler of its traditional uses while leaving the "thousand virtues" as the enthusiasm of an earlier pharmacy.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Head / nervine tonic.** The defining use — for chronic, tension and "nervous" headache, vertigo, anxiety and frayed nerves; a gentle calming tonic of the head.
2. **Vulnerary (external/internal).** Bruised leaf to clean and staunch wounds; the wound-herb tradition.
3. **Astringent.** Mild tannin astringency for loose bowels, relaxed tissues, and as a gargle for sore throat and spongy gums.
4. **Bitter digestive.** A gentle bitter-aromatic to support digestion.
5. **Amuletic / protective (historical-cultural).** The Anglo-Saxon use against night-fears, bad dreams and "elf-sickness" — recorded as cultural history, not a material claim.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM / Galenic humoral degree:** Hot and Dry (commonly hot and dry in the second degree) — a warming, drying, attenuating and astringent-aromatic herb; opening obstructions of the head and stomach.
- **Folk/Anglo-Saxon mapping:** the supreme protective/amuletic herb of the head and spirit; vulnerary and nervine.
- **Kenetica Qi/Shen tier:** ◉ **Qi** for the warming-drying, astringent-aromatic and digestive actions, with a ☿ **Shen** ascription for its head-and-nerve, anxiety- and dream-affecting register — a substance reaching from the gut to the head and the spirit.

- **Pregnancy — caution/avoid.** Traditionally regarded as a uterine stimulant/emmenagogue; medicinal use avoided in pregnancy. [USER: verify.]
