# Propolis (bee glue) — Dioscorides, 2-106 PROPOLIS ("Bee-glue")
## *Propolis, the resinous glue bees use in the hive; the translator's gloss for Dioscorides' chapter heading "PROPOLIS" is "Bee-glue"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` (line 5414 of the text file) and the chapter itself in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` (lines 5414-5416; the name index prints "PROPOLIS 228" and "Bee-glue 228", the page of this 2000 English edition). The chapter is 2-106, in Book Two, which opens with "Living creatures" (the chapters on animals and animal products, beginning at 2-1) and runs through honey (2-101), sugar (2-104) and wax (2-105). Propolis follows beeswax (2-105, "KEROS", suggested "Beeswax") and precedes wheat (2-107, "PUROI"). The whole chapter reads:

"The yellow bee-glue that has a sweet scent and resembles styrax [1-79] should be chosen, and which is soft, excessively dry, and easy to spread (like mastic [1-90]). It is extremely warm and attractive, and draws out thorns and splinters. The smoke from it (inhaled) helps old coughs, and it is applied to take away lichen [skin disease with red pustules]. It is found around the mouths of hives, being similar in nature to wax."

The chapter therefore gives: a test for choosing good material (colour yellow, sweet scent, likeness to styrax, softness, dryness, ease of spreading, likened to mastic); a quality ("extremely warm and attractive"; the translation does not explain "attractive", and the clause that follows, that it draws out thorns, may be its sense, which the lines read do not settle [USER: verify]); three uses (drawing out thorns and splinters, the inhaled smoke for old coughs, and applied to remove "lichen", which the translator glosses as a skin disease with red pustules); and where it is found ("around the mouths of hives"), and its likeness to wax. The cross-references in square brackets, [1-79] for styrax and [1-90] for mastic, are the translator's, pointing to Book One chapters on those resins. The chapter does not say how the glue is gathered from the hive, how it is to be applied or smoked, or for how long.

### Active Components

Propolis is a resinous mixture bees make from plant resins and buds mixed with wax and their own secretions. Its composition varies with the plants in the area and the season, but it generally contains resins and balsams, waxes, essential oils and pollen, with phenolic compounds including flavonoids and phenolic acids. No composition specific to Dioscorides' material was read, and no active principle is claimed by the sources. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced content is Dioscorides' short chapter: choose the yellow, sweet-smelling, soft glue; it draws out thorns and splinters, its smoke is inhaled for old coughs, and it is applied for "lichen". The Codex offers no protocol. Inhaling smoke is itself a harm to the airways, and propolis is a known cause of allergic contact reactions (see Contraindications). A persistent cough or a skin eruption needs clinical assessment. [USER: verify]

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations named in the chapter are: the glue applied directly ("is applied to take away lichen"; "draws out thorns and splinters") and its smoke inhaled for old coughs. No quantity, vehicle, frequency or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Propolis is used in many countries in topical and oral products, and it is a recognised cause of allergic contact dermatitis, especially in people sensitised to balsams, resins or bee products. Smoke from burning resins irritates the airways. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is supplied here. What he offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention (provenance, season of gathering, sensory assay, detection of adulteration), and this chapter has part of it: it gives a sensory assay for choosing good material (yellow, sweet-scented, like styrax, soft, "excessively dry", easy to spread like mastic), and a note on where the material is found (around the mouths of hives). It gives no season of gathering, no account of adulteration and no amuletic use, so there is nothing to distinguish between recording and endorsing. No cultivation status is attested; the chapter gives a therapeutic one only. The frame is recorded as the book's habit of attention and not as a practice for the vital constitution.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this beyond reading the chapter for its method. The method, to choose by colour, scent and feel what a material should be, is the real content; the uses are a physician's short list for splinters, cough and a skin eruption, and the cough remedy involves inhaling smoke. A person who keeps bees and handles propolis learns the same attentiveness at first hand, but the Codex offers no protocol.

### Contraindications

- **Allergy:** propolis commonly causes contact dermatitis and can cause reactions in people allergic to bee products, balsams, resins or poplar; avoid in anyone with such a history.
- **Smoke inhalation:** inhaling smoke from burning resin is an airway irritant and is not advised, especially in asthma or lung disease.
- **Not a treatment for cough or skin disease:** a cough lasting weeks or a spreading skin eruption needs clinical assessment.
- **Pregnancy, children and asthma:** safety is not established for these groups in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Source and purity:** raw propolis can contain contaminants from the environment and from hive treatments; do not collect or use it without knowing its source.
- **Conservation:** harvest does not require killing bees, but disturbing hives harms colonies and may need a beekeeper's licence in some places. [USER: verify]
