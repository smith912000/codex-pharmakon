# Champaka — Champaka, Champakam (Sushruta Samhita); Hemanga as the translator's bracket for the bark (Chikitsa ch.25)

## *Michelia champaca, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints against Champaka (OCR "Michelia Champaca.", line 31544); the Latin is the translator's, the pairing is positional from two OCR columns and was re-checked here by counting, and the chapters name the flower, flowers and fruits, and a bark under the name; confidence in the pairing is high, in the species it rests on the translator* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS) among the texts on disk.** This entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`; the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`). The row is CPU-02672 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 450, "Champaka", locus "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31508; id line 31544)"). The Latin comes from the appendix and not from the chapters.

**Pairing checked by counting.** On the page headed "68" the names run Ohakramarda (line 31506, OCR of Chakramarda), **Champaka (31508)**, Ohanaka (31510, Chanaka), Ohandana (31512, Chandana), OhaCageri (31514). The Latin lines run "Cassia Tora." (31542), **"Michelia Champaca." (31544)**, "Cicer Arietinum." (31546), "Santalum Album." (31547), "Oxalis Monadelpha ; O. corniculata." (31549). Chakramarda with Cassia, Chanaka with the chickpea and Chandana with sandalwood agree, so Champaka is the second name and *Michelia champaca* the second Latin line. The alignment is sound; the Latin is the translator's. [USER: verify against POWO]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** All chapter files were searched for Champaka and Champakam. The hits, with the part named:

- *Sutra ch.46 (the flowers), `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1813 to 1815:* "The Champakam is curative in cases of haemoptysis. It is both cooling and heat-making in its potency and subdues the deranged Kapham." This is the only place the text prints a property for the flower.
- *Sutra ch.45, line 186:* in the treatment of water, its smell "should be removed by perfuming it with the Nageshvara, Champaka, Utpala, or Patala flowers, etc."
- *Sutra ch.28, line 71:* a sweet smell in an ulcer, like that of "wine, or fragrant aloe wood (Aguru), clarified-butter, Jati flower, Champaka, sandal, lotus or any celestial flower (Divyagandha)", is a precursor sign in the prognostic passage.
- *Kalpa ch.7, line 126, and Uttara ch.39, line 1313:* "flowers and fruits of Champaka" in a list of materials (Kalpa 7); "Kalka of Sumanah, Champaka, As'oka and S'irisha flowers" cooked with milk and other things (Uttara 39).
- *Chikitsa ch.25, line 237:* "bark of Hemanga (Champaka)" in a list of drugs for a paste. The bracket is the translator's gloss and equates the two names there.

**Overlap.** Bhumi-champaka (CPU-02669, entry I_1852) is a different appendix row and is not folded in. No other spine row for *Michelia champaca* was found. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Michelia champaca* (family Magnoliaceae) bears strongly fragrant flowers that are used in perfumery. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The flower is said to be curative in haemoptysis and to subdue deranged Kapham (Sutra 46). Elsewhere it is a perfuming flower (Sutra 45), a smell in a prognostic sign (Sutra 28), and an ingredient in lists (Kalpa 7, Uttara 39, Chikitsa 25).
- **What the text does not say.** No quantity, no preparation of the flower for haemoptysis, no duration.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not translate the text's terms into modern diagnoses.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives no preparation of Champaka on its own. It appears as a perfuming flower, a Kalka (paste) with other flowers cooked in milk (Uttara 39, line 1313), and a bark in a paste (Chikitsa 25, line 237). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any quantity from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Michelia champaca* is a tree of South and Southeast Asia, cultivated for its fragrant flowers and for ornament. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** Sutra ch.46 prints "both cooling and heat-making in its potency" and that it "subdues the deranged Kapham" (lines 1813 to 1815). No *rasa* or *vipaka* is printed for it in the passage read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The flower is classified in the dietetic chapter and used in lists; no rasayana or vajikarana passage naming it as a drug was found in Chikitsa 26 to 30, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for the plant.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. A fragrant flower in the Sushruta text, recorded for haemoptysis and as a perfume; it gives a cultivator no practice.

### Contraindications

The text prints no warning for this flower. Haemoptysis (coughing blood) is a serious medical sign and needs proper care. No safety information for pregnancy or lactation was read. [USER: verify] The Latin is the translator's, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry.
