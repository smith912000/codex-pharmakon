# Rumex vesicarius — Amla-vetasa (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Rumex vesicarius, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Ruxem  vesicarius.", line 31235) against the Sanskrit name Amla-vetasa (OCR "Amla  Vetasa", line 31181), and prints a second time (OCR "Rnmex  Vesicarius.", line 32178) against another name by position. The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print Amla-vetasa (acid, "Amla-Vetash" in Sutra 42) in seven compounds and one list, and give no Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 373 gives the name as "Amla  Vetasa", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31181; id line 31235)" and the Latin as "Ruxem  vesicarius.", with the note that the pairing was positional in a block of 31. The Latin is printed in two spellings, "Ruxem vesicarius" (31235) and "Rnmex Vesicarius" (32178), which the spine row merges and the Codex reads as *Rumex vesicarius*; the OCR has garbled the first letters of the genus both times.

**The first pairing, Amla-vetasa, checked by counting.** The first page of the appendix prints thirty-one Sanskrit names (lines 31126 to 31181, with Amalaka and Amalaki bracketed as one) and thirty-one Latin entries (lines 31184 to 31235), in the same order. Amla-vetasa is the thirty-first and last Sanskrit name, and "Ruxem vesicarius." is the thirty-first and last Latin entry. The neighbours agree: Amla-lonika, the thirtieth, stands against "See Changeri." (line 31233), and Ambu against "Pavonia odorata." (31231); Ambashtha against "Stephania hernandifolia." (31229), Ambuja against "Nelumbium speciosum." (31227) and Amarapushpi against "Andropogon acicularis." (31225). Counted from the front, Ajamoda is the seventeenth on both sides and Akhu-parni the twentieth (the entries on *Pimpinella involucrata* and *Salvinia cucullata* count the same page). The same page is counted in the entries on *Pavonia odorata* (I_1376), *Oxalis monadelpha* (I_1409, which holds Amla-lonika), *Cleome viscosa* (I_1381), *Andropogon acicularis* (I_1390) and *Ocimum gratissimum* (I_1407). **A second appendix name, Likucha, by position.** On the page of the L names (Sanskrit lines 32126 to 32149) the Sanskrit run is fourteen entries, Lajjalu, Laksha, Lakucha, Lamajjaka, Langali (two OCR lines bracketed), Lasuna, Lata-kasturika (two lines bracketed), Lavanga, Lavali, Lavani, Likucha, Limpaka, Lodhra and Loni, and the Latin run (lines 32164 to 32181) is fourteen, from "Mimosa Pudica." to "Portulaca oleracea.". Likucha, the eleventh Sanskrit entry (OCR "Likucha", line 32144), stands against the eleventh Latin entry, "Rnmex Vesicarius." (32178), with "Citrus acida. Lemon." after it. The same block is counted in the entry on *Mimosa pudica* (I_1383). The chapters never print Likucha, so the pairing has no chapter behind it. They print Lakucha (a different appendix entry, against "Artocarpus Lakucha.") in the acid group of Sutra 42 line 313, the neighbour of Amla-Vetash. The Codex records this as a positional pairing only. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Amla-vetasa in its spellings ("Amla-vetasa", "Amla-Vetash", "Ainla-vetasa", "Amla-" at a line end with "vetasa" below), for Vetasa alone, and for Chukra and Chuchchu. Vetasa alone appears in other passages (for example the "Vetasa creeper", Chikitsa 18 line 96), and the chapters do not tie it to Amla-vetasa; Chuchchu is a potherb that the chapters also do not tie to it. The hits for Amla-vetasa are these.

*Sutra ch.42 (the specific properties of flavours).* `SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` lines 301 to 316, the acid group: "The fruits known as Dadima, Amalaka, Matulanga, Amrataka, Kapittha, Karamarda, Badara, Kola, Prachina-Amalaka, Tintidi, Koshamra, Bhavya, Paravata, Vetraphala, Lakucha, Amla-Vetash, Dantashatha and curd, whey, Sura, Shukta, Sauvira, Tushodaka and Dhanyamla, etc. are generally included within the acid group." That is the only place in the chapters where the plant is given a taste, and it is the taste of its group, *amla*, acid.

*Chikitsa ch.5 (Maha-Vata-Vyadhi).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt` lines 560 to 572: for Apatantraka, "The patient should be made to drink a potion consisting of Tumburu, Pushkara, Hingu, Amla-vetasa, Haritaki and the three (officinal) kinds of salts, with a decoction of barley." A footnote (lines 571 to 572) says: "Chakradatta quotes this in the chapter on the treatment of colic (sula), but does not read 'Amla-vetasa' there." The same chapter, lines 678 to 690, the Hingvadi-vati, lists "asafoetida, Trikatu, Vacha, Ajamoda, Dhanya, Ajagandha, Dadimba, Tintidi, Patha, Chitraka, Yava-kshara, Saindhava salt, Vid salt, Sauvarchala salt, Svarjika-kshara, Pippali-mula, Amla-vetasa, Shathi, Pushkara-mula, Hapusha, Chavya, Ajaji and Pathya" in boluses "each weighing an Aksha (two Tolas)".

*Chikitsa ch.10 (major cutaneous affections).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch10.txt` lines 46 to 55: a barley saktu given in a decoction of Khadira, Asana, Nimba, Raja-vriksha, Rohitaka or Guduchi, "sweetened with honey and sugar, and acidified with grapes, or the expressed juice of pomegranate and Amla-vetasa and then mixed with rock-salt. This is the method of preparing all kinds of Manthas."

*Uttara ch.39 (fever).* `SS_Uttara_ch39.txt` lines 1631 to 1641: "A plaster composed of Yashti-madhu, Rajani, Musta, Dadima, Amla-vetasa, Rasanjana, Tintidika (tamarind), Nalada (Mansi), Patra, Utpala, Tvak (cinnamon), Vyaghra-nakha, the expressed juice of Matulunga, honey and Madhu-shukta, if applied to the head, would alleviate heat in the head, delirium, vomiting, hic-cough, and shivering — concomitants in cases of fever."

*Uttara ch.42 (Gulma).* `SS_Uttara_ch42.txt` lines 185 to 192, the Hingvadi Ghrita: ghee "cooked with Hingu, Sauvarchala-salt, Ajaji, Vit-salt, Dadima, Dipyaka, Pushkara, Vyosha, Dhanyaka, Amla-vetasa, Yava-kshara, Chitraka, Shathi, Vacha, Ajagandha, Ela and Surasa as Kalka and with the admixture of curd as liquid", for Vataja Gulma, colic-pain and suppression of stool and urine. Lines 194 to 203, the Dadhika Ghrita: ghee cooked with a list of drugs (Vit-salt, Dadima, Saindhava, Chitraka, Vyosha, Jiraka, Hingu and others) including "Amla-vetasa as Kalka and with the expressed juice of Vija-pura and with curd weighing four times as much as of Ghrita", for Gulma, enlargement of the spleen and colic. Lines 433 to 441: three compounds, the first of "Pathya, the three kinds of salt (Saindhava, Sauvarchala and Vit), Yava-kshara, Hingu, Tumburu, Pushkara, Yamani, Haridra, Vidanga and Amla-vetasa", to be given "respectively" in the Vataja, Pittaja and Kaphaja kinds.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Amla-vetasa, Vetasa and Chukra found nothing.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Rumex* is a genus of the dock family (Polygonaceae), many of whose leaves are sour, a taste associated with oxalic acid. That is a statement about the genus and not a finding of the Sushruta text, and nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the name the chapters print, the plant (if it is this one) is a member of the acid group of drugs (Sutra 42) and an ingredient in a handful of compounds: a potion for Apatantraka, boluses for deranged *Vayu*, an acidifier for the barley mantha in the skin-disease chapter, a head plaster for fever, and ghees for Gulma. The text gives it no action by itself. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the chapters give are a potion with barley decoction (Chikitsa 5), boluses "each weighing an Aksha (two Tolas)" (Chikitsa 5), a juice in the barley mantha (Chikitsa 10), a plaster applied to the head (Uttara 39), ghees cooked with the drug as Kalka and with curd or fruit juice (Uttara 42) and a powder given by the three humoral types (Uttara 42). The only quantities are the weight of the finished bolus and the proportion "curd weighing four times as much as of Ghrita"; no quantity of Amla-vetasa itself is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** Sutra 42 places Amla-Vetash in the **acid** (*amla*) group, with the fruits of Dadima, Amalaka and Matulanga, curd and whey (lines 301 to 316). The chapters print no *virya* or *vipaka* for it, and no quality for it singly.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Amla-vetasa in the acid group of the classification of flavours and in compounds for named illnesses (Apatantraka, deranged *Vayu*, skin disease, fever, Gulma). Those are therapeutic and dietetic contexts. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The plant therefore carries no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status in the text as retrieved.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with Amla-vetasa: it is a sour ingredient in compounds for illness, and the Latin name is not the text's. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of it.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this plant. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Sour leaves rich in oxalic acid are a general hazard for people prone to kidney stones, which is a general fact about the dock family and not a finding of the text. Apoplectic convulsions, abdominal tumours and persistent fever need medical care. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
