# Naskaphthon — NASKAPHTHON (Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 1-22); Latin nascaphthum, also narcaphthon

## *A bark "like the rind of the mulberry tree" brought out of India and used as a perfume; the translator marks the heading "UNKNOWN" and gives no identification. Identification: unresolved, and a bark, so a plant material rather than a mineral. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The material is not identified. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should burn, inhale or gather a bark on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk, under this Greek name.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it. The chapter describes a bark, a plant material, so the Codex files it in Volume I (Plant-Based).

**Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter is 1-22, NASKAPHTHON, in Book One (Aromatics) (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 3751 to 3754; the name index prints "NASKAPHTHON 28"). The translator's line under the heading reads "UNKNOWN", and the next line, "nasceus — nascor — plants used by women", is the translator's note on the Latin name and is not part of Dioscorides' text. The text reads in full: "Nascaphthum some call narcaphthon and this is also brought out of India. It is a bark like the rind of the mulberry tree, used for a perfume for the sweet smell it has, and put into artificial perfumes. Taken as inhalations of fumes or smoke it is good for constriction of the vulva."

The chapter says where it comes from (India), what it is (a bark, compared in appearance to mulberry rind), and how it is used (as a perfume ingredient, and as fumes or smoke for the vulva). It gives no tree and no description beyond the simile. [USER: verify against the printed page]
