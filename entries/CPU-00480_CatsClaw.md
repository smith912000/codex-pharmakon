### Origin & Tradition

Uncaria tomentosa is a massive Amazonian vine named for its curved, cat-claw-shaped thorns that allow it to climb through the dense forest canopy. Indigenous peoples of the Peruvian Amazon — Asháninka, Shipibo, Awajún — have used the inner bark of this vine for at least 2000 years, as documented in the *Badianus Manuscript* tradition of Mesoamerican healing and in extensive ethnobotanical records from the Amazon basin.

The vine was called "Uña de Gato" by early Spanish missionaries who documented its use for treating inflammation, wounds, and what appeared to be autoimmune and infectious conditions — long before those categories existed in Western medicine. The Asháninka considered it a sacred vine of the forest's immune intelligence.

Cat's Claw represents the Amazon's contribution to the Codex — the forest's immune-modulating, connective-tissue-supporting medicine. Within the integrated pharmacological context of this Codex, it is classified as the primary *Lymphatic Qi* herb — clearing the lymphatic channels that run through and alongside the fascial tissue.

### Active Components

- **Oxindole alkaloids (pentacyclic — POAs):** mitraphylline, isomitraphylline, uncarine D, F — immunomodulatory; phagocytosis-enhancing
- **Oxindole alkaloids (tetracyclic — TOAs):** rhynchophylline, isorhynchophylline — vasodilatory; anti-platelet; antagonize POA activity (low-TOA preparations preferred)
- **Procyanidins, proanthocyanidins** — powerful antioxidants; DNA repair support
- **Quinovic acid glycosides** — anti-inflammatory; anti-viral
- **Plant sterols (β-sitosterol, stigmasterol, campesterol)** — anti-inflammatory; phytoestrogenic
- **Tannins (catechins, epicatechin)** — astringent; anti-inflammatory; wound healing

### Preparation & Dosing

**Root bark decoction:** 1–2g per cup, simmered 15 minutes. 2–3 cups daily.
**Standardized extract (3% alkaloids, low-TOA):** 250–500mg twice daily.
**Cycle:** 8–12 weeks, 2–4 weeks off.

**Critical:** Choose standardized extracts specifying POA alkaloid content and minimal TOAs — the tetracyclic alkaloids (TOAs) antagonize the immunomodulatory effects of the pentacyclic alkaloids (POAs).

### Scientific Correlation

- POA alkaloids increase phagocytic index by 50–70% in in vitro and animal studies
- DNA repair: catechin procyanidins enhance excision repair of oxidatively damaged DNA bases
- NF-κB inhibition: quinovic acid glycosides downregulate TNF-α production
- Anti-viral: active against HSV-1, HIV, and influenza A in in vitro studies
- Anti-arthritic: RCT in rheumatoid arthritis shows significant reduction in painful joint count vs. placebo (Mur 2002)
- Anti-tumor: inhibits tumor cell proliferation via AP-1 pathway; synergistic with standard chemotherapy in some models

### Contraindications

- **Autoimmune conditions on immunosuppressant therapy** — immune stimulation may worsen or interact
- **Pregnancy or breast feeding** — insufficient safety data
- **Anticoagulants** — TOA alkaloids have anti-platelet effects
- **Pre-surgery** — discontinue 2 weeks prior
- **Organ transplant immunosuppression** — contraindicated
