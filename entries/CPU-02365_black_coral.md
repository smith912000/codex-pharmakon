# black coral — Antipathes (Dioscorides, *De Materia Medica* 5-140)

## *Black coral, the Greek ANTIPATHES of Dioscorides: "coral with a specific difference: it is black in colour", tree-like and more branched than coral. The translator's suggested heading is "Black Coral". Identification: a black, branching, tree-like marine growth of the black-coral kind (the antipatharians, whose Greek-derived group name preserves the word), from the text's description and the translator's heading; no binomial is given by Dioscorides. **Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is the 2000 English edition of *De Materia Medica* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`). The chapter is 5-140, headed "ANTIPATHES" (line 10564), with the translator's suggestion "Black Coral" (line 10566). It stands in the closing "Metallic Stones" part of Book Five. In full (line 10567):

> "Antipathes is thought to be coral with a specific difference: it is black in colour. This is also like a tree only more branched, and it has the same strength as that mentioned above."

The text says three things: it is "thought to be" a kind of coral (the verb leaves the identification as opinion); its difference is that it is black; and it is tree-like and "more branched". It then refers to "the same strength as that mentioned above", meaning the chapter immediately before it.

**The chapter it refers to, 5-139 (KORALLION).** The "strength" is given there (lines 10557 to 10562). Coral "seems to be a sea plant which hardens when it is drawn out of the deep — taken out of the sea and as it were, dipped into the air flowing all around us". Great amounts are found at the promontory called Pachynum near Syracuse. "The best is red like sandarac [5-122] or sandyx [ancient red pigment], a very intense colour. This is easy to pound; even [textured] throughout its whole concretion, with a mossy smell like little seaweed, and with many branches, imitating cinnamon in the form of little shrubs." Worthless coral is that "hardened like stone, rough, without colour, hollow and loose". And then the strength: "By its strength it is therapeutic for the bowels, cools gently, represses abnormal growths, takes off scars on the eyes, fills up hollow sores and cracks, works effectively against spitting up blood, is good for frequent painful urination, and (taken as a drink with water) reduces the spleen. Some have called it lithodendron [stone tree]."

So, on Dioscorides's own words, black coral shares coral's strength: bowel-healing, gently cooling, repressing abnormal growths, clearing scars on the eyes, filling hollow sores and cracks, against spitting of blood, painful urination and (as a drink in water) the spleen. This is carried across by the single clause "it has the same strength as that mentioned above". The text gives no use specific to black coral, no source place, and no assay.

**A different spine row.** Red coral is on the spine as CPU-00292, "Coral" (*Corallium rubrum*), tagged Avicenna, with its entry at `III_295_Coral.md`. Dioscorides's 5-139 (Korallion) is not recorded as one of that row's attestations in the index. Black coral is here kept as its own row, for the reason that Dioscorides names it separately and by its own Greek name. Whether it should later be folded into the Coral entry as a variety is a decision for the editor and is not made here.

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, red coral (*Corallium*) is a skeleton of calcium carbonate with a small amount of magnesium, and black corals (antipatharians) have a skeleton built of a horny protein (chitin-like) material and not of a calcium carbonate. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** By reference to coral: a therapeutic effect on the bowels, scars on the eyes, hollow sores, spitting of blood, painful urination and the spleen, with a gentle cooling.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation specific to black coral, no source place.

Black corals are slow-growing animals of reef and deep-water habitats and are protected; the Codex does not recommend taking or using any coral.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives no preparation. For coral, 5-139 says that the good kind is "easy to pound" (line 10560) and that for the spleen it is taken "as a drink with water". It gives no quantity, and the water-drink is not said to apply to black coral. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Black corals (order Antipatharia) are colonial marine animals of the phylum Cnidaria, with a dark, branching skeleton; they live in deep and shaded reef habitats. Black corals are listed in the appendices of the CITES convention on trade in endangered species. ### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is supplied here. What he offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention: how a material is told apart from its neighbour (here, by colour and by branching), which grade is best, and what a counterfeit looks like. The chapter is two sentences long and does only the first of these. It records no amuletic or protective use of black coral, and no cultivation status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes black coral a cultivation substance. The chapter is a note that black coral is a dark, more branched relative of coral, and an ethical cultivator has no reason to harvest it.

### Contraindications

- **Conservation and law.** Black corals are slow-growing, protected animals; trade is regulated. Do not take or buy black coral for any purpose here. [USER: verify the current legal status in your country.]
