### Origin & Tradition

The narcissus is the fragrant spring bulb of Mediterranean myth — the flower of Narcissus, the youth who pined away gazing at his own reflection, and the bloom that drew Persephone to the spot where Hades seized her into the underworld. Its very name was anciently linked (whether truly or by folk etymology) to *narke*, "numbness/narcosis," the root also of "narcotic" — a memory of the bulb's stupefying, poisonous quality. Dioscorides places narcissus among the dangerous plants, recording the bulb's strong **emetic and purgative** action and its use, mashed into plasters, as a drawing **vulnerary** for wounds, burns, sprains and to extract thorns and splinters.

Across the European tradition the daffodil/narcissus bulb was handled as a **toxic emetic** — used cautiously (and dangerously) to provoke vomiting, and externally as a discutient/drawing poultice — while always carrying its reputation as a poison. The bulbs are genuinely toxic: containing irritant calcium-oxalate raphides and a family of **Amaryllidaceae alkaloids** (lycorine chief among them), they cause vomiting, salivation, diarrhoea and, in quantity, more serious toxicity. Daffodil bulbs are a recurrent cause of accidental poisoning when mistaken for onions, and the sap causes "daffodil itch" in florists.

The plant's most consequential modern chapter belongs to a sister species/genus of the same family: **galantamine**, an Amaryllidaceae alkaloid first obtained from snowdrop (*Galanthus*) and daffodil (*Narcissus*) species, is an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor now used as a licensed medicine for **Alzheimer's disease**. Certain *Narcissus* cultivars are grown commercially as a galantamine source. Thus the flower of numbness and self-regard yields, by an irony, a drug that supports failing memory — a fitting entry in a Codex attentive to the deep reach of plant alkaloids into the mind.

### Active Components

- **Lycorine** — the principal Amaryllidaceae alkaloid of the bulb; a potent **emetic** and toxic principle (also antiviral/cytotoxic in research); responsible for much of the poisoning syndrome.
- **Galantamine** — Amaryllidaceae alkaloid; a reversible **acetylcholinesterase inhibitor** (and allosteric nicotinic modulator); the licensed Alzheimer's drug; present in some *Narcissus*/*Galanthus* species and cultivars. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM / Galenic humoral degree:** Hot and Dry in a high degree, with a stupefying/poisonous quality — classed among the dangerous, "narcotic"-associated plants; a drawing discutient externally.
- **Folk-European mapping:** the toxic emetic bulb and drawing wound-plaster; the flower of myth.
- **Kenetica Qi/Shen tier:** ◉ **Qi** for the forceful emetic/drawing action, with a ☿ **Shen** ascription reflecting both the ancient *narke*/numbness association and the modern galantamine action on cholinergic signalling in the brain — a plant family that genuinely reaches the mind.

### Contraindications

- **Internal use — avoid.** Toxic emetic; serious poisoning risk; no casual use.
- **Bulb-for-onion confusion.** A leading cause of poisoning; never store/handle near food onions.
- **Children and pets.** Toxic bulbs; keep out of reach.
- **Daffodil itch (contact).** Sap causes dermatitis; florists/handlers should protect skin.
- **Pregnancy — avoid.** Toxic alkaloids; contraindicated.
- **No casual use of any kind.** Specialist/archival framing; galantamine use is purified-drug, medically supervised only.
