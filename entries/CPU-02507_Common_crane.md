# Common crane — لحم الكركي (laḥm al-kurkī), the flesh of the crane

## *The flesh of the crane,* Grus grus *(modern identification of Arabic* kurkī*)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** TBN

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Prophetic medicine (TBN).** The crane is named only by Ibn Qayyim's *al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī* among the texts on disk. This is a short entry because the text is short. It is read in the Arabic (`Sources/TBN_Prophetic/text/IbnQayyim_TibbNabawi_OpenITI_Shamela0023649.txt`, letter *lām*, entry *laḥm al-kurkī*, line 6665, two sentences).

The whole entry: "The flesh of the crane is dry and light, and there is disagreement about its heat and cold; it generates a black-bilious blood (*dam sawdāwī*) and suits people of toil and fatigue; it should be left a day or two after slaughter, then eaten" (لحم الكركي: يابس خفيف، وفي حره وبرده خلاف، يولد دما سوداويا، ويصلح لأصحاب الكد والتعب، وينبغي أن يترك بعد ذبحه يوما أو يومين، ثم يؤكل). No hadith is cited, so there is nothing to grade: the entry is purely Ibn Qayyim's medical statement, set beside the houbara entry (III_337), which does cite a report.

**Identity.** *Kurkī* is the crane; *Grus grus*, the common crane, is the modern default for a migrant crane in the Arabic lands, but the text gives no species. [USER: verify if a species is wanted.]

### Active Components

The flesh of a bird: protein and fat. Nothing specific is stated in the text and none is asserted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The text gives a food judgement: what the flesh does to the humours, whom it suits, and how to hang it. It is not offered as a recommended practice, because cranes are protected in many countries.

### Preparation & Dosing

One preparation rule: leave the bird for a day or two after slaughter before eating. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Hanging game before eating is a long-standing practice that lets the meat soften; it also carries a spoilage risk if not kept cold. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Ibn Qayyim (TBN).** Dry (*yābis*) and light (*khafīf*); heat and cold disputed, so no quality of heat or cold is assigned; generates a black-bilious blood (*dam sawdāwī*).

### Cultivation Register

**Prophetic medicine (TBN).** *(c) Absent.* No hadith, *sunna* report or cultivation framing is given for the crane. The entry is a food judgement in Ibn Qayyim's own voice and falls under his category of food and drink; it does not carry a life-lengthening or cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to cultivate. The only practical instruction in the entry is a hanging time, and the bird is now protected.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Crane flesh, in Ibn Qayyim's Prophetic medicine entry, is mapped to the humoral blood and to digestion. The flesh is described as dry (yabis) and light (khafif), with heat and cold disputed, so no quality of heat or cold is assigned. Its main action is on the blood: it generates a black-bilious blood (dam sawdawi), a humoral disadvantage in the tradition, which places the effect on blood formation in humoral terms, though no organ is named. The entry says it suits people of toil and fatigue (ashab al-kadd wa-l-taʿab), linking it to muscular exertion. The only practical instruction is to leave the bird a day or two after slaughter before eating, which the entry gives as a preparation rule and not as a body action. No cited report and no digestive claim beyond the flesh's lightness are given.

### Contraindications

Historical record only. The text notes the blood it generates is black-bilious (a humoral disadvantage in the tradition) and that it should be hung one or two days. Cranes are protected under law in much of their range and several species are endangered; hunting and eating them is restricted or banned. Do not treat any use here as medical advice.
