# Ground beetle — Zhe Chong 䗪虫 / Tu Bie Chong 土鳖虫 (Shennong bencao jing); alias Tu Bie 土鳖, "earth turtle"

## *Eupolyphaga sinensis Walker, the "earth turtle" insect of Chinese pharmacy, a flat, flightless, cockroach-like insect (not a true beetle, despite the English label "ground beetle" in this entry's title, which follows the file's name); the identification is ours, from the alias tu bie and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence for the drug, though the entry's own commentary does not support it, see below* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The only text read for this entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk for *Eupolyphaga* and *Steleophaga* found nothing. The cockroach entry III_31 (Dioscorides *silphe*, *Blatta orientalis*; rGyud-bzhi *bse.sbur*) is a different insect from different traditions and is not this drug.

**The name.** In the transcription the name line of this entry is a single character, 虫 ("insect"), with the rare character that should precede it missing; the batch's full name 䗪虫 (*zhe chong*) is the standard form and comes from the batch, not from the line read. The middle-classic roster of the preface (line 1677) likewise prints "虫" between 蜚廉 and 伏翼. The Codex therefore reads the name from the alias printed inside the paragraph itself, 土鳖.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry sits in the middle classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 虫 [䗪虫] *zhe chong*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2809). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味咸，寒 ("salty, cold"); 主心腹寒热洗洗 ("governs chills and fever, shivering, in the heart and abdomen"), 血积症瘕 ("blood accumulations and lumps"), 破坚 ("breaks hard masses"), 下血闭 ("brings down blocked blood") and 生子大良 (literally "bearing children, very good"; the line is ambiguous and the Codex does not read it further); alias 一名土鳖 ("also called tu bie"); habitat 生川泽 ("grows by rivers and marshes"). There is no 久服 clause.

The Mingyi bielu layer (line 2811) adds: 一名土鳖。生河东及沙中、人家墙壁下、土中湿处。十月，曝干, "also called tu bie; grows in Hedong and in sand, under the walls of people's houses, in damp places in the soil; dried in the sun in the tenth month".

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 2813) cites the Shuowen, the Guangya, the Erya, Guo Pu, the Mao Shi and its commentary, and Lu Ji, with several characters missing. As transcribed, it is about the "grass insect" (草虫), which the Erya glosses "fu [missing]", Guo Pu "changyang", and Lu Ji describes as "in size like a locust, with a strange cry, green, liking to be in thatch grass" (小大长短如蝗也。奇音，青色，好在茅草中). That is not the creature described by the alias tu bie and the Mingyi habitat (under house walls, in damp soil). The note as it stands therefore does not support the identification of this drug, whether because of the missing characters or because it concerns a different insect, and the Codex does not read it as support. The identification rests on the alias 土鳖 and the Mingyi habitat. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the dried insect body is mostly protein and chitin with fats. Nothing more specific can be said without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a salty, cold insect for chills and fever with shivering, blood accumulations and lumps, breaking hard masses and bringing down blocked blood. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose, and does not recommend the use of any animal body.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Mingyi layer gives the one preparation: it is dried in the sun in the tenth month (line 2811). The Shennong paragraph names no part, route or dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The dried insect is used in modern Chinese pharmacy. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The classic's categories ("blood accumulations", "blocked blood") are not modern diagnoses, so no modern finding can be set beside them.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味咸，寒, "salty, cold" (line 2809). No other quality is printed in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph reads 味咸，寒 and the drug is filed in the middle grade (中品). The classic's preface (line 1677) says the middle medicines "nourish the nature, to correspond to man; non-toxic and toxic, weigh what is fitting; for those who wish to check disease and mend weakness, base it on the middle classic" (主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经). The paragraph has no 久服 ("taken long") clause and no promise of lightening the body or of freedom from ageing; it is a list of conditions of the blood and abdomen and of actions that break and bring down. The register is therefore (b). The Mingyi bielu adds only the alias, habitat and drying. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 虫 [䗪虫] *zhe chong*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2809)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The paragraph is a list of conditions and of actions that break and bring down, without a long-use clause, and the Codex does not recommend taking animal bodies. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy:** the paragraph names breaking hard masses and bringing down blocked blood among its actions, which are not actions for pregnancy; no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Hygiene:** insects from walls, sand and damp soil carry bacteria and parasites; do not eat them.
- **Abdominal masses, blocked blood:** the text's uses are historical record only; abdominal lumps and abnormal bleeding need medical assessment.
- **Ethics:** the Codex does not recommend harming any animal for these uses.
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given.
