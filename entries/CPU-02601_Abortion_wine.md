# Abortion wine — Oinos Phthorios Enibruon (Dioscorides 5-77)

## *A poisonous wine made from grapes grown among planted veratrum, wild cucumber or scammony, said to destroy embryos; the identification is the chapter's own description, so confidence in "a wine tainted by plants grown among the vines" is medium, and no single species is the wine's identity: the chapter names veratrum (cross-referred to 4-150 and 4-151), wild cucumber and scammony, and the translator marks the heading POISONOUS* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** Dioscorides' translator marks this chapter POISONOUS. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, no method is given here, and nobody should make or drink such a wine. The substances in question can kill.

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 8 November 2026 as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it. The reading was made from the chapter and from the two hellebore chapters it cites.

**Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter is 5-77, "OINOS PHTHORIOS ENIBRUON", glossed "Abortion Wine", with the marker "POISONOUS" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, lines 10182 to 10184). Full text: "A wine is made that destroys embryos, for among the vines planted there is planted veratrum [4-150, 4-151], wild cucumber or scammony, from which the grapes take the strength; and the wine made from [the grapes growing near] them becomes destructive. Eight cups mixed with water is given to women fasting, having first vomited."

The cross-references lead to chapters 4-150 (ELLEBOROS, translated "white hellebore", marked POISONOUS, line 9345) and 4-151 (ELLEBOROS MELAS, "black hellebore", line 9367). The text of those chapters was not used to extend this entry. The wine's placement is in the book on wines, in the run of "Other Wines" (it is followed by thymelaea wine, 5-78, and dwarf-olive wine, 5-79, which are medicated wines of a more ordinary kind).

The statement of the preparation is not a method of making the wine; it is a claim about a belief, that the grapes "take the strength" of the neighbouring plant. Dioscorides records it in the same plain voice as the rest of the book. The chapter does not say that the physician recommends it, and it uses the word "destructive" of the wine.

### Active Components

The source names no constituent. In general terms, plants of the genus *Veratrum* contain steroidal alkaloids that are potent poisons of the heart and nerves; wild cucumber and scammony are purgative plants, with scammony a drastic resin purgative. Whether poisons could pass from a neighbouring plant into the grape is not established here, and it is a claim of the text, not a verified fact. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Poisonous.**

- **What the text says it does.** It "destroys embryos" and is "destructive".
- **What the text does not say.** It gives no safety, no outcome for the woman, and no advice beyond the dosing line below.

The Codex gives no protocol, and it does not recommend any attempt to end a pregnancy by poison. Anyone who needs pregnancy care should seek a qualified clinician or a trusted health service.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Veratrum alkaloids are toxic and have caused fatal poisonings and birth defects in grazing animals. Substances that harm an embryo equally harm the pregnant person, and poisonous plants are an unreliable and dangerous means of anything. There is no basis for the claim that a grape vine takes the properties of a neighbouring plant. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **POISONOUS.** Do not make, obtain or drink. Veratrum and scammony are dangerous; poisoning can be fatal.
- **Pregnancy.** Any attempt to end a pregnancy with poison risks the death of the pregnant person.
- **Alcohol.** The wine adds its own hazards.
- **Support.** For pregnancy options or poisoning, contact a clinician, emergency services or poison control.
- **Identity.** The wine is not a defined substance; no species is the identity.
