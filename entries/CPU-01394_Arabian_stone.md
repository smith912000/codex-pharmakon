# Arabian stone — Arabikos lithos (Arabicus stone), Dioscorides 5-149
## *A stone "like a piece of ivory" used for haemorrhoids and, burnt, as a tooth-powder; the translator gives no identification ("UNKNOWN") and the chapter names no mineral* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-149, *Arabikos lithos* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10610; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 922, translator identification "UNKNOWN", class guess mineral; list row `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 875, "Arabikos Lithos", mineral, flagged as an uncertain identification; the book index sends the reader to page 820). The whole chapter was read. It is two sentences long (line 10613):

"Arabicus stone is like a piece of ivory. Pounded into small pieces and smeared on it dries up haemorrhoids, and burnt it is a dentifrice for the teeth."

That is the entire text. The chapter gives a likeness (ivory), two treatments (pounded and smeared on, for haemorrhoids; burnt, as a tooth-powder), and one named effect ("dries up"). It gives no place of origin beyond the adjective in the name, no colour, hardness, weight or taste, no way of testing or choosing the stone, no adulterant, and no burning method. The words "Arabicus" and "Arabian" are the translator's rendering of the adjective in the Greek name; the chapter does not say that the stone is dug in Arabia. The word "Arabikos" occurs in no other mineral chapter of the Herbal (a search of the whole text found the stone only here, at line 10613, apart from the chapter heading, the contents list and the index).

The chapter is followed directly by 5-150 *Galaktites lithos* (line 10614); that chapter does not mention it.

**Identity.** The translator offers no identification ("UNKNOWN"), the Codex list marks the identification uncertain, and the only physical clue is the comparison with ivory. Which material Dioscorides meant is not settled by the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records an unidentified, ivory-like stone as a drying external application for haemorrhoids and, burnt, a tooth-powder. The material cannot be identified, no source, quantity or method is given, and haemorrhoids and dental disease are conditions for medical care. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

---

### Preparation & Dosing

Two preparations are given in the single sentence of 5-149: the stone is "pounded into small pieces and smeared on" for haemorrhoids, and "burnt" for use as "a dentifrice for the teeth". The chapter gives no burning method (compare 5-141, the Phrygian stone, where wine and coals are specified; that method is not stated to apply here), no vehicle for smearing, and no quantity or frequency. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Unidentified material:** the identification is uncertain; an unknown mineral powder should not be applied to the body or the mouth.
- **Abrasion and contamination:** powders used on the teeth wear them; those used on broken or inflamed skin may infect it.
- **Serious conditions:** rectal bleeding or persistent haemorrhoids need medical assessment, as they may signal other disease.
