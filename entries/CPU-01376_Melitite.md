# Melitite — Melitites Lithos (μελιτίτης λίθος), also called galactites and lethaeus (Dioscorides 5-151)
## *A stone "like galactites in all respects" that exudes a sweeter liquid; the translator prints "Melelite — Calcium Silicate, Aluminum Silicate"* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek Tradition (DMM).** The main locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-151 *MELITITES LITHOS* in the 2000 English translation (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 10618-10621; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 924; book index "MELITITES LITHOS 820", line 12313, and "Melitites 820", line 16537; list row `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 877, "Melitites Lithos, , mineral, DMM V, id_uncertain yes"). The translator's heading is "SUGGESTED: Melelite — Calcium Silicate, Aluminum Silicate."

Dioscorides' account, read in full, is:

"Melitites is like galactites [above] in all respects, differing only in that it exudes a sweeter liquid. It is effective for the same things as galactites. It is also called galactites and lethaeus because men carrying it and going to their own masters find them well disposed and forgetful of wrongs (often committed). When the milk of goats and sheep dry up it helps the little flock if it is beaten finely and mixed with brine. They say that pounded and taken as a drink it can do the same for women to produce milk. They say that bound around the neck of an infant it keeps him free from envy and diseases, and keeps the infant from treachery, and that both kings and judges revere anyone that wears this, and that they seem grandiose to all men." (line 10621)

**The galactites chapter.** Because melitites is defined by reference to it, the previous chapter was read. 5-150 *Galaktites Lithos* (marked "UNKNOWN" by the translator; line 10617): "This is called galactites because it exudes milky stuff. It is like ash in colour and sweet to the taste. It is good rubbed on for discharges and ulcers of the eyes. It is necessary that those who beat it in water put it in jars in a lead box because of its glueyness." So melitites is, in the text, a sweeter-exuding stone used as galactites is: externally for eye discharges and ulcers.

**Reading the chapter.** (1) Distinguishing mark: a sweeter exudate than galactites. (2) Uses given in Dioscorides' own voice: the eye uses (by reference to galactites); and, for livestock, when the milk of goats and sheep "dry up", the stone beaten fine and mixed with brine "helps the little flock". (3) Reported in the form "They say": taken as a drink it produces milk in women; bound around an infant's neck it keeps off envy, disease and treachery; kings and judges revere the wearer. (4) The name story: it is also called galactites and *lethaeus*, "forgetful of wrongs", from the effect said to be had on a master's disposition toward the one who carries it. The amuletic and social effects are reported, not vouched for. Recording is not endorsing.

**A different thing with the same name.** *Vinum melitites* (line 9847) is a wine, not a stone, and belongs to the wines chapter; the stone is not described there.

**Identity.** The translator prints "Melelite — Calcium Silicate, Aluminum Silicate"; the usual spelling of the mineral group would be melilite, but the printed form is kept here. The chapter gives only the sweetness of an exudate, the likeness to galactites (ash-coloured, sweet, glutinous), and the uses; it gives no place. The identification rests on the translator's suggestion. The list row marks the identity uncertain. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Stated generally. If the stone is a calcium aluminium silicate it is a mineral silicate and chemically stable. A stable silicate does not by itself explain the "sweet" taste and "milky" exudate the text describes. The text gives no composition, and none is asserted for melitites.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The chapter records an external eye application, a livestock remedy for failing milk, and reported amuletic and lactation uses. Putting an unidentified powdered stone in the eye or drinking it to increase milk is not supported by anything retrieved. The social and protective claims are reports, not practices. No modern protocol is supplied.

---

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations given are: for livestock, "beaten finely and mixed with brine" (line 10621); for women, "pounded and taken as a drink" (reported with "They say"); for infants, the stone bound around the neck (reported). For the eye uses the chapter refers back to galactites, whose chapter says the stone is beaten in water and kept in jars in a lead box because of its glueyness (line 10617). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM).** The only printed qualities are in the galactites chapter that melitites follows: "sweet to the taste", and "glueyness" (line 10617); melitites is said to exude a "sweeter" liquid (line 10621). No hot, cold, moist or dry degree is printed for melitites.

---

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. What he offers is a discipline of attention: the stone is told from its twin by taste (sweeter exudate), and its handling is specified in the neighbouring chapter (lead box, because of glueyness). He also records amuletic claims (protection of an infant from envy, the favour of masters, the reverence of kings and judges), but as report ("They say"), set apart from his own account; recording is not endorsing. The chapter is therapeutic and no cultivation status is assigned.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator to take or wear. What carries over is the habit of separating what an author states from what "they say", and of telling near-identical materials apart by a simple sensory mark. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Unidentified mineral:** the identity is a suggestion; an unidentified stone should not be ingested, and a powdered mineral should not be put in the eye.
- **Eye:** applying any mineral powder to ulcerated or discharging eyes risks injury and infection.
- **Lactation:** nothing retrieved supports swallowing a stone to increase milk.
- **Infants:** a stone bound round the neck of an infant is a choking and strangulation hazard.
- **Lead box:** the neighbouring chapter's lead container is a lead-exposure hazard if the stone is stored in it.
- **Legal status:** not retrieved. [USER: verify]
