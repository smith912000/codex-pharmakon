# Honey, Arghya (Arghyam Madhu) — Sushruta Samhita

## *Honey of the Argha bees, one of the eight honeys of Sushruta, found in ant-hills; the text and its translator give no species, and "Apis"-type honeybee honey is our general reading, of medium confidence; the name is printed "Arghyam" and "Argh3'am" in the damaged English* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta Samhita only, among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The English text is Bhishagratna's translation, Sutra ch.45, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`. The honey group opens (line 1076) "Honey is sweet, and leaves an astringent after-taste. It is parchifying, cold, stomachic, cosmetic, tonic, light, softening, palatable, liquefacient (Lekhanan), and fermenting (Sandhanam)." The metrical text then says (lines 1093-1102): "Eight different kinds of honey are commonly used such as, the Pauttikam, Bhramaram, Kshaudram, Makshikam, Chhatram, Arghyam, Auddalakam and Dalam." The translator's footnote (lines 1120-1142) names the bee or hive of each kind.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The translator's footnote (lines 1135-1136) gives the sixth kind: "The honey obtained from the hives of thin-mouthed bees of the Argha species often found in ant-hills is called Arghyam." The text describes it (lines 1154-1158): "Honey known as the Arghyam is possessed of properties highly beneficial to the eye. It is a greater subduer of Pittam and Kapham than any other kind of honey, is astringent in taste, and pungent in digestion. It is a bitter tonic and does not generate Vayu in the system." It is also the one kind that the chapter exempts from the rule on rain-water: "Atmospheric water (rain-water), like heat, serves to impart an injurious character to all kinds of honey (except the one known as the Arghyam Madhu)" (lines 1203-1206). The chapter's general rules apply to every kind. Honey "is not collected from the flowers of any particular species" but from flowers and plants that are incompatible in nature, taste, potency and effect; "for these reasons, and further from the fact of it being prepared by poisonous bees, honey becomes positively injurious after contact with heat or fire, and accordingly the use of hot or boiled honey is forbidden" (lines 1183-1192, with the metrical verses following). Fresh honey is "constructive and aphrodisiac" and old honey "astringent and liquefacient" (lines 1165-1169).

### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms honey is mostly the sugars fructose and glucose with water, and small amounts of enzymes, organic acids and pollen-derived material; the composition varies with the flowers and the bee. These are well-established general facts, not read in the source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The eye; the lessening of *Pittam* and *Kapham* more than any other honey. It is a bitter tonic and does not raise *Vayu*.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose and no source for the bees beyond the footnote. The text does not say what the "Argha species" is.

The Codex does not translate the Sanskrit terms into modern diagnoses, and gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation for this honey beyond the general rules on honey. The chapter's rules: do not heat or boil honey; honey with rain-water is injurious (all kinds, except Arghya, lines 1203-1206); and for emetic purposes honey may be given with a hot substance, since the aim is that it be thrown up (lines 1208-1213). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Honey is a sweet food made by bees from nectar; the Sushruta text's claim that it becomes injurious when heated is not a modern pharmacological finding, and heating honey is not generally regarded as poisonous. Honey must not be given to infants under twelve months (a general statement, not from the source). No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (ch.45).** Rasa: astringent. *Vipaka*: pungent in digestion ("pungent in digestion", line 1157). Also bitter, a tonic. Effect on the *doshas*: lowers *Pittam* and *Kapham* more than any other honey; does not generate *Vayu* (lines 1154-1158).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The Arghya honey is described in the dietetic chapter by taste, potency and effect on the *doshas*. It is not named in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter and no *ojas* claim is made for it. The honey group's own sentences call honey "tonic" and say that fresh honey is "constructive and aphrodisiac" (lines 1076-1088, 1165); these are group statements and are not carried over to a single kind. The one distinctive thing the text says that bears on practice is that rain-water does not spoil it, which is a statement about the honey's handling and not a cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** The tradition sets this honey apart from the other seven by its eye-benefit and its bitterness, which is the nearest the text comes to singling it out. A cultivator has nothing to act on beyond the group rules on honey, above all that honey is never heated.

### Contraindications

- **Heated honey.** The text forbids hot or boiled honey and says it can be fatal like poison (lines 1183-1200).
- **Infants.** Honey is not given to infants under twelve months; this is a general statement, not from the source.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The kind of bee is the translator's footnote and the English prints the Sanskrit names only; no species of bee is identified by us beyond *Apis*-type honeybees in general, which is itself ours.
