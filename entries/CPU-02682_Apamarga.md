### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Only the *Sushruta Samhita* (Bhishagratna's translation) names this plant among the texts on disk. The row began on 8 November 2026 as an unverified placeholder from the Appendix 3 glossary; this entry replaces it. The existing entry for *Achyranthes bidentata* (I_500) mentions apamarga only as a "genus-relative", and this entry stands on the Sushruta passages read here.

**Sushruta (SS), glossary.** Appendix No. 3 in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` is a list of Sanskrit names in one column with their identifications in another. The name column prints "Apamarga" at line 31253, between Ankotaka (line 31252) and Aparajita (line 31255). The identification column prints "Achyranthis Aspera." at line 31320, in the same order: Amra-Tamarindus/Mangifera, Amrataka-Spondias, Amritak "See Guduchi", Anantamula "Hemidesmus Indicus", Ankotaka "Alangium hexapetalum", Apamarga "Achyranthis Aspera", Aparajita "Clitoria Ternatia", Aragvadha "Cassia Fistula" (lines 31312-31324). The pairing is by order in two OCR columns and is therefore consistent but not printed on one line. [USER: verify against the printed page.]

**Sushruta (SS), uses read.** The plant is named in these places:
- **Sutra ch.39** (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` line 50): in the list of errhines (drugs for the nose), "Pippali, Vidanga, Apamarga, Shigru, Siddharthaka, Shirisha, Maricha, Karavira, Vimvi ..." (lines 49-52).
- **Sutra ch.42** (`SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` line 340): within the "Bitter Group" list (the Aragvadhadi and Guduchyadi gana members together with Mandukaparni ... "Sumanah, Sankha-pushpi Apamarga, Trayamana, Ashoka ..."), the group the text calls the bitter group.
- **Sutra ch.11** (`SS_Sutra_ch11.txt` line 126): in the list of woods, leaves, roots and fruits ("Arka, Snuhi, Apamarga, Patala, Naktamala, Vrisha ...") that are burnt to ashes for the preparation of alkali (lines 118-130).
- **Sutra ch.16** (`SS_Sutra_ch16.txt` line 79): "(sticks of Nimba or Apamarga, or rods of lead)" inserted into fissures for expansion; and line 315, Apamarga among drugs in a decoction used with an unguent for elongation (Kvatha of Arka, Alarka, Vala, Ativala, Ananta, Apamarga ...).
- **Chikitsa ch.18** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt` line 256, footnote): "Madhuka-sara mixed with tepid water and the expressed juice of Shigru-seeds and of Apamarga-seeds should be used."
- **Chikitsa ch.22** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch22.txt` line 390, footnote): alkaline water "prepared from the ashes of Mushkaka, Apamarga, etc." (the footnote credits Shivadasa).
- **Uttara ch.54** (`SS_Uttara_ch54.txt` line 150): "expressed juice of Shirisha and Kinihi (Apamarga) mixed together and sweetened with honey." The "(Apamarga)" is the translator's gloss for Kinihi.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text does with it.** Nasal drug (errhine), a member of the bitter group, a source of ash for alkali, rods for dilating fissures, seed juice with madhuka-sara, and a juice with honey in the Uttara passage.
- **What the text does not say.** The passages are lists and instructions, not a monograph. No single passage here states a disease it cures by itself.

The Codex does not recommend any of these procedures.

### Preparation & Dosing

Preparations read: the plant burned to ash in an alkali preparation (Sutra ch.11); sticks inserted into fissures (Sutra ch.16); juice of the seeds (Chikitsa ch.18 footnote); juice mixed with that of Shirisha and honey (Uttara ch.54). No dose is given in the passages read. **Do not use any figure from this entry.** [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Achyranthes aspera* is a common weedy herb of the tropics. Plant-ash lye is strongly alkaline and injures skin and mucous membranes. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

In the Sushruta passages read, apamarga (Achyranthes aspera) acts on the nasal passage, on fissures, and through the ash and alkali preparations the text describes. As an errhine, a drug given into the nose, it is used in the nasal passage, and the text counts it among the bitter-group drugs, the only quality the entry records; no rasa, virya or vipaka is printed. Its burnt ash is used to prepare an alkali, and sticks or rods are placed into fissures for expansion, which the entry classes as surgical use. Seed juice given with madhuka-sara appears in a Chikitsa footnote, and a juice mixed with shirisha and honey appears in Uttara ch. 54, with no organ named. The entry assigns no ojas, tejas or prana role and no rasayana use. Preparations are listed, but no dose is printed, and the entry warns that plant-ash lye is caustic and must not be used on the body.

### Contraindications

- **Alkali from ash.** Plant-ash lye is caustic; do not use it on the body.
- **Errhines.** Nasal drugs irritate the nose; the entry gives no safe method.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The glossary pairing rests on column order; the species differs from A. bidentata. [USER: verify]
