# Epipactis latifolia — Epipaktis (Dioscorides 4-109)

## *Epipactis latifolia (named on the SUGGESTED line and in the plate caption "Epipactis latifolia after FAGUET — 1891"; "Broad Helleborine", with Epipactis grandiflora and Cephalenthera ensifolia given as another reading)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) printed under the chapter heading, not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED block has two lines: "Epipactis grandiflora, Cephalenthera ensifolia — Epipactis, Helleborine" and "Epipactis helleborine, Epipactis latifolia — Broad Helleborine". So the translators offer a different genus (*Cephalenthera*, as printed) and a second *Epipactis* alongside the species the row takes. The plate caption, an illustration credited to Faguet, 1891, names *E. latifolia* as well. The row has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *4-109. EPIPAKTIS* at line 9009, caption at line 9011, SUGGESTED lines at 9013 and 9014, text at line 9015. The chapter stands in Book Four, "Other Herbs & Roots". The translators' name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 689. The next chapter, 4-110 *KAPNOS* (line 9016), is a different plant.

The whole chapter reads: "Epipactis is a small little shrub with very small little leaves. It is taken as a drink against poisons [antidote], and for disorders in the liver. It is also called elleborine, or borion."

That is the whole record. The plant is a small shrub with very small leaves. It is taken as a drink for two purposes, against poisons (the bracketed "[antidote]" is the translators' gloss) and for disorders in the liver. Two other names are printed, *elleborine* and *borion*. The chapter gives no habitat, no flower, no root, no season, no preparation and no quantity. A "small little shrub with very small little leaves" may not fit the orchids the translators suggest, since an orchid is not a shrub, and the entry records that tension without resolving it. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records a drink against poisons and for liver disorders, with no preparation or dose. The Codex gives no protocol, and a poison or a liver disorder needs medical care.
