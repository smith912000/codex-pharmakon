# Sida spinosa — Naga-vala; Naga-bala (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Sida spinosa, read from the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints as "Sida  spierosa.     Graveolens." (line 32422) against the Sanskrit name that the OCR gives as "Nj£ga-vaU" (line 32362), read here as Naga-vala. The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print Naga-vala (and Nagavala) as a drug beside Vala, and give no Latin name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed, the Latin is OCR-garbled ("spierosa", read as spinosa, followed by an unexplained "Graveolens."), and the Sanskrit name is an OCR reading, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Naga-vala, Nagavala, Naga-bala, Nagabala and OCR variants (the translator's "Ndga-vald", a hyphen or line break inside the word), and the hits were read. There are six. The name is listed beside Vala in four of them, and the translator never glosses it.

*The rasayana chapter (Chikitsa ch.27).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch27.txt` lines 4 to 9 open the chapter on "elixirs and rejuvinators of the human organism which will make it invulnerable to the inroads of any disease or of decay (Sarvopaghata-shamaniya-Rasayanam)", and its first metrical text (lines 11 to 22) says a physician should prescribe a tonic (*Rasayana*) in youth and middle age only after the system has been cleansed by a Sneha and by emetics and purgatives. In the *Vala-Kalpa* (lines 153 to 169) the roots of the Vala are taken in cow's milk and "Premature old age and senile decay would be arrested for a period of twelve years" by twelve days' continuous use. Then, lines 169 to 179: "Ati-vala, Naga-vala, Vidari, Satavari may be similarly taken for the same purposes, with this distinction that the (powders of) the Ati-vala should be taken with water, those of the Naga-vala, with honey, whereas Viddri and the Satavari powders should be taken with milk. The regimen of diet and conduct as well as the beneficial results produced therefrom should be the same (as from the use of Vala). The present remedies are recommended to persons seeking strength or suffering from an attack of Haematemesis or Haematochezia." (The OCR prints "Naga-valai" and "Ndga-vald".) A footnote signed "D. R." glosses the last two words as "Suffering from consumption and hoemoptysis".

*The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa ch.26) and the other rasayana chapters (ch.28 to 30).* A search found no Naga-vala in any of them.

*Other chapters, read for the line and its sentence.* `SS_Sutra_ch15.txt` line 664 lists "Vala, Ativala, Nagavala and such other drugs of sweet taste" in a medicated compound. `SS_Chikitsa_ch37.txt` lines 90 to 105, the "Fourth Taila" of the chapter on the Anuvasana and Uttara Vasti: a decoction of a long list of drugs including "Vala, Naga-vala, Murva, Asva-gandha" is cooked in oil with the Jivaniya group and milk, and "if employed in the manner of a Vasti, would prove beneficial in cases of diseases due to the action of the deranged Vayu localised in the regions of the thighs, legs ...". `SS_Uttara_ch41.txt` lines 348 to 352, on phthisis: "A Phthisical patient should likewise be made to take Rasona (garlic) or Naga-vala or Magadhika or Silajatu with milk, in the prescribed manner (laid down in connection with the medical treatment of Maha-vata-Vyadhi)." `SS_Uttara_ch47.txt` line 234, in the treatment of the alcohol-related disorders: a Painaka (draught) of "Karpasa-roots, Naga-vala and Suvarchala (Surjavarta) all taken in equal parts". `SS_Uttara_ch52.txt` lines 354 to 356, on cough: "The use of clarified butter duly cooked with Satavari, Naga-vala and Vala proves beneficial to a person afflicted with cough". The sentence at Sutra 15 line 663 reads, as printed, "A case of patent obesity should be checked with a medicated compound", and follows an account of emaciation; the Codex reports it as printed. ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Sida* is a genus of the mallow family (Malvaceae), and some species are reported to contain ephedrine-type alkaloids. That is a statement about the genus and not a finding of the Sushruta text, and which species the name Naga-vala stands for is open. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the name the chapters print, the drug (if it is this plant) is a root powder taken with honey in the Sushruta rasayana chapter (Chikitsa 27), a drug of sweet taste in a medicated compound (Sutra 15), and an ingredient taken with milk for phthisis, in a ghrita for cough, in an oil for a Vasti and in a draught after alcohol (Uttara 41, 52, 47, Chikitsa 37). The Codex gives no protocol and no dose, and none of these is offered as a recommendation.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparation the rasayana chapter gives for Naga-vala is the powder taken with honey, "similarly" to the Vala preparation (Chikitsa 27, lines 170 to 173), under the regimen of the Vala-Kalpa. The only quantity in that passage belongs to Vala: "A Pala or half a Pala weight of the (powdered) roots of the Vala" in cow's milk (lines 153 to 155). The text does not restate it for Naga-vala. Elsewhere it is taken with milk (Uttara 41), cooked in ghrita with Satavari and Vala (Uttara 52), in equal parts in a draught (Uttara 47) or in an oil with milk (Chikitsa 37), without a quantity of its own. No dose of Naga-vala's own is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(a) Attested for the Sanskrit name Naga-vala: named in the rasayana chapter as a drug with a stated use.* Chikitsa 27, the chapter on elixirs and rejuvenators (*Rasayana*), says (lines 169 to 179, page 519) that Naga-vala "may be similarly taken for the same purposes" as the Vala-Kalpa, with honey, under the same regimen of diet and conduct, and recommends it "to persons seeking strength". The status rests on the Sanskrit name in that chapter. It does not rest on the Latin name, which comes from Bhishagratna's appendix by position and is OCR-garbled. The chapter says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for the drug, and none is asserted here. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) does not name it. The text makes the course conditional on prior cleansing (Chikitsa 27, lines 11 to 22), and the Codex repeats that condition. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** In the Sushruta scheme the drug named Naga-vala is a rasayana taken under a physician, after cleansing, as a powder in honey and with the regimen of the Vala-Kalpa, which the text spells out only under Vala. The Latin identification is a translator's appendix pairing and the chapters do not confirm it; nothing here supports gathering or taking a plant on the strength of the Latin name.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for Naga-vala. The text itself makes the rasayana course conditional on cleansing by emetics and purgatives, and speaks of seclusion in a room for the Vala-Kalpa; these are not minor steps and should not be skipped on the strength of this entry. The identity is open, and some species of the genus *Sida* are reported to contain stimulant alkaloids, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Phthisis, cough and the disorders of alcohol need medical care. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
