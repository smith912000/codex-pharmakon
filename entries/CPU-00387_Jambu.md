### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** The spine names this tree *Syzygium cumini*. The Latin printed against the Sanskrit word in the text on disk is *Eugenia jambolana*. The two binomials are generally treated as names of one tree, but neither source read says so, so the link is partly an inference. [USER: verify] Several lookalike words occur in the same text and are different things: *Jambira* (a citrus fruit, Sutra ch.46, Appendix 3 "Citrus medica"), *Jambvoshtha* (a cautery instrument, Chikitsa ch.8, line 195), and *Bhumi-jambu* (a fungus, treated in `I_194_Poria_Wolfiporia_extensa.md`). Bhishagratna's own English "Jamboline fruit" (Sutra ch.31, line 19; Nidana ch.14, line 34) is taken here to mean the *Jambu* fruit, which is an inference. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

*The gana (Sutra ch.38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`).* In the Nyagrodhadi group (lines 344 to 351) the list reads "... Amra, Koshamra, Chorakapatra, the two sorts of Jamvu, Piyala, Madhuka (Maula), Rohini ..." (line 347), so Sushruta lists two kinds of Jamvu under the one name, and the text read does not say which two. The group's metrical text (lines 353 to 360) says it is beneficial in ulcer, cures disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones, is astringent (*Sangrahi*), is curative in haemoptysis and "assuages the burning sensation of the body". Membership of the group is a grouping by therapeutic action, grade (b) at most.

*Appendix 3 glossary (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`).* "Jambu" is printed at line 31793 in the Sanskrit column and the page's Latin column is a separate block. Counted down by position, "Jambira" (line 31791) pairs with "Citrus medica." (line 31841) and "Jambu" with "Eugenia gambolana." (line 31843), followed by "Jata'-ma'nsi" against "Valeriana Jatamansi." (line 31845). The count was checked back from the ends of the block and agrees. The pairing is by OCR column position, not by a printed link.

*Passages read, in the order of the book.* Diacritics are dropped in the quotations, and the OCR spelling is kept where a letter is damaged (for example "Jmnbu" at Uttara ch.40, line 476, which is read as Jambu).

- **Sutra ch.42** (the chapter on the specific properties of flavours; `SS_Sutra_ch42.txt`, lines 350 to 353), the astringent group: "Triphala, Shallaki, Jambu, Amra, Vakula, Timduka fruits, Katakha fruits, Shaka fruits ..." Jambu is listed as an astringent substance.
- **Sutra ch.45** (`SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`, lines 1493 to 1497): "The wine known as the Jamvava Sidhu (wine prepared from the expressed juice of the Jambalin fruit, a decoction of coriander seeds, treacle and Dhataki flowers, etc.) is anuretic, reduces the quantity of urine, has an astringent taste, and tends to enrage the bodily Vayu." The word "anuretic" is as printed.
- **Sutra ch.46** (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 1120 to 1129 and 1140 to 1142): the fruit "known as the Jamva" is named with the Kshira-Vrikshas among fruits that are "cooling, and astringent in their effect", subduing "the Pittam and Kapham" with "a sweet and astringent taste". The later sentence reads "The fruit known as the Jamvava generates the Vayu. It is astringent, and subdues the Pittam and Kapham". A footnote at line 2506 gives "the expressed juice of Jambuline fruits" among the makings of a dish called Rasala.
- **Chikitsa ch.18** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt`, lines 87 to 96): "Hot plasters, prepared by pasting together the bark of the Madhuka (flower) tree, and of the Jambu tree, Arjuna tree, and Vetasa creeper."
- **Chikitsa ch.19** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch19.txt`, lines 289 to 293), in the passage on Upadams'a: "A decoction of the tender leaves of Jambu, Amra, Sumanas, Nimba ... and the barks of S'allaki, Vadara, Vilva, Palas'a ... and of the Kshiri trees, as well as Triphala should be used by the physician for constantly washing the ulcer." (Ellipses mark OCR-garbled names.)
- **Chikitsa ch.25** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch25.txt`, lines 101 to 106, 109 to 115, 189 to 201), the miscellany chapter: the tender leaves of "Vala, Jambu and Amra (mango)" cooked in oil and *Dhanyamla* for *Utpaita*; a wash of "a decoction of the leaves of the As'mantaka, Jambu and Amra" for the affected lobes (*Unmantha*); and the flowers of "S'airiya, Jambu, Arjuna and of the Kas'mari", with sesamum, Bhringa-raja, mango-stones, Triphala and other drugs, pasted with a decoction of the pith of Vijaka, mixed with Vibhitaka-oil and kept in an iron pitcher. That last passage follows an oil that "arrests a premature greyness of the hair", and the sentence stating its own purpose was not found in the lines read.
- **Uttara ch.19** (`SS_Uttara_ch19.txt`, lines 99 to 101): "Decoction of the tender leaves of Jambu, Antra [OCR for Amra] and Amalaki should be used for washing and sprinkling purposes", with clarified butter dropped into the eye as an *As'chyotana*.
- **Uttara ch.21** (`SS_Uttara_ch21.txt`, lines 273 to 275): an oil cooked with "the tender sprouts of S'aivala, Maha-vriksha, Jambu and of Amra".
- **Uttara ch.40** (`SS_Uttara_ch40.txt`, lines 375 to 377 and 476 to 478), diarrhoea: "dried Vilva and Jambu fruits should be taken with the washings of rice mixed with honey", and "Barks of Badari, Arjuna, Jambu, Amra, S'allaki and Vetasa taken with honey and sugar would cure a case of Atisara".
- **Uttara ch.45** (`SS_Uttara_ch45.txt`, lines 242 to 246), bleeding disorders: "the decoction of Jambu, Arjuna and Amra — these three compounds prove curative in cases of Rakta-pitta."
- **Chikitsa ch.6** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch06.txt`, line 79) uses "a ripe Jambu fruit" as a colour comparison for a polyp after alkali application.

No rasayana or vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) was found to name Jambu, so no grade (a) status is claimed.

### Active Components

Fruits and barks of trees of this kind are generally known to contain tannins, which are astringent, and ripe dark fruits generally carry plant pigments such as anthocyanins. The composition of the material in the text was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Sushruta records Jambu as an astringent fruit and a wine, and as a leaf, bark and flower in washes, oils and potions for ulcers, diarrhoea, bleeding and eye complaints. These are the text's own claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol for this tree.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted above: leaf decoctions for washing ulcers and for eye washes (Chikitsa ch.19; Uttara ch.19), leaves cooked in oil (Chikitsa ch.25), hot plasters of bark (Chikitsa ch.18), bark or dried fruit taken with honey for diarrhoea (Uttara ch.40), and a wine (Sutra ch.45). The Uttara ch.40 line 375 passage fixes a weight for a different drug in the same sentence group, and none of the passages above gives a measure for Jambu. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** The text prints taste and effect, but no *virya* or *vipaka*, for Jambu in the lines read. Sutra ch.42 (lines 350 to 353) puts it in the astringent group. Sutra ch.46 (lines 1120 to 1129 and 1140 to 1142) prints "cooling, and astringent" with "a sweet and astringent taste" for the group that includes the Jamva, and "astringent, and subdues the Pittam and Kapham" while it "generates the Vayu" for the Jamvava fruit. Sutra ch.45 prints "astringent taste" and "tends to enrage the bodily Vayu" for the Jamvava wine.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Jambu is an astringent in the flavour chapters, a member of the Nyagrodhadi group, and a leaf, bark or flower in ulcer, eye, diarrhoea and bleeding remedies. The Sushruta frame for it is therapeutic. The ch.38 gana is a grouping by action, not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. Nothing read ties Jambu to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*, and none of the rasayana or vajikarana chapters searched (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) names it. The status is unattested, and the identification with *Syzygium cumini* rests on an OCR-paired appendix. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this tree. In Sushruta it is an astringent used for washes and for bowel and bleeding complaints. The abstentive answer is the correct one: any use is a matter for a qualified practitioner, and the text supplies no long-term regimen.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Astringent preparations may constipate, and Sushruta itself says the Jamvava fruit and wine "generate" or "enrage" the Vayu (Sutra ch.45 and ch.46). The eye, ulcer and bleeding uses above are conditions needing medical care, and none of them is advice. Do not put plant decoctions in the eye. Do not use in pregnancy. Seek a qualified practitioner, and ordinary medical care for diarrhoea with blood or for any wound that does not heal.
