# Ass's liver — Hepar Oneiron (ἧπαρ ὀνείου, Dioscorides)

## *The liver of the domestic ass, an animal organ used as a food-medicine; the identification is ours, from the printed headword "Ass's Liver" in the 2000 English translation of Dioscorides, and is of medium confidence as to the species (the translator's gloss names no binomial for this chapter; the ass is generally identified as Equus asinus, which is our reading and is not printed at this chapter)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 8 November 2026 as an unverified placeholder from an extraction residual (key "HEPAR ONEIRON [DMM]"); this entry replaces it. It was written from the chapter itself, read at the line cited, and from the neighbouring chapters of the same book, which show the setting.

**Dioscorides (DMM).** The substance is chapter 2-42 of *De Materia Medica* in the 2000 English translation on disk (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 5109), headed in transliterated Greek "2-42. HEPAR ONEIRON" and glossed "Ass's Liver". The whole chapter is one sentence: "An ass's liver eaten roasted is good for epilepsy but it should be taken while fasting." (line 5110). In the printed text the next chapter heading, 2-43 (testes of deer), follows on the same line, which is an artefact of the e-text and not a sign of a joined entry.

The chapter sits in the second book, in the run of "Living Creatures" that handles parts of animals one after another: the lungs of the sea (2-39), the lungs of swine, lamb or bear (2-40), and the lungs of the fox (2-41) come immediately before it (lines 5098 to 5108). The pattern is the one Dioscorides keeps throughout this group: the animal, the organ, the complaint, and sometimes a rule of how it is taken. For the ass's liver the rule of method is two-fold in the single sentence: it is eaten, and it is roasted, and it is taken fasting.

Nothing else is said. The chapter names no preparation beyond roasting, no quantity, no duration, no season, no source of the animal, and no second use. It does not describe the liver or the animal. The translator adds no suggested modern identification line to this chapter, unlike many plant and mineral chapters. A search of the other texts on disk for the ass's liver was not made beyond this text; the entry therefore rests on the one sentence above.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Liver is an organ food in many cuisines. Because the liver filters blood, it can hold high levels of vitamin A and, depending on what the animal has eaten, other compounds, so even in modern food guidance it is eaten in limited amounts. Epilepsy is a group of neurological conditions managed with anticonvulsant drugs and specialist care; eating a roasted liver is not an accepted treatment. ### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The Galenic three spirits are later than Dioscorides and are not used. The chapter assigns the liver no status other than its one indication. What the text offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention in the sense of the specification: the part of the animal is named exactly, the preparation is named (roasted), and the condition of the person taking it is named (fasting). Provenance, season and adulteration, which Dioscorides watches for in plants and minerals, are not discussed for this organ.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing for a cultivator to do. The record is a one-line food-medicine for a seizure disorder, with no cultivation framing, and it should not be used as one. If the line teaches anything, it is the habit of attending to how a thing is prepared and in what state the person takes it.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Little to map: the entry states that an ass's liver, eaten roasted and taken while fasting, is good for epilepsy. That is the only indication, and the text names no organ, humour, temperament or channel through which the liver would act. The one body system the chapter touches is the nervous system in epilepsy, but Dioscorides gives no location for it, such as head or heart. The fasting is stated as a condition of the person taking the food, not as an action on any organ. The chapter says nothing of the liver's own function, its vessels, digestion or any second use, and it describes neither the liver nor the animal. Only the indication and the two conditions of preparation and taking are recorded here, and no further body mapping is supported by the chapter.

### Contraindications

- **Epilepsy.** A medical condition. Do not replace treatment with any food.
- **Hygiene.** Organ meat must come from a known, inspected source and be cooked through; liver carries parasites and bacteria if raw or undercooked.
- **Vitamin A and pregnancy.** Liver is generally limited in pregnancy because of its vitamin A content. [USER: verify against current food-safety guidance.]
- **Ethics and law.** The sourcing of an ass is a matter of local animal-welfare and food law; the Codex gives no advice on obtaining it.
- **Identity.** The species is our identification; the chapter says only "ass".
