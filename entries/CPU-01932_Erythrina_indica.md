### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** The seeds and bark of Erythrina species contain alkaloids and are generally regarded as unsafe to eat. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take this plant on the strength of it.


**Named in the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) only through the translator's Appendix No. 3, "Names of drugs with Latin synonyms".** This is a single-text entry. The appendix is printed in two columns, Sanskrit names on the left and Bhishagratna's Latin or English glosses on the right, and the e-text `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` splits the two columns into separate runs of lines. In that file the Sanskrit name stands at line 32482 as "Pstribhadra \ Patrijata (grouped)" (the OCR garbles diacritics) and the Latin gloss "Erythrina  Indica." at line 32553. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Seeds are toxic.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The wood, leaves, roots and fruit are among the vegetable matter burnt to make alkali (Sutra 11), and the wood is among the woods of a Ksharagada, an alkaline antivenom preparation (Kalpa 7).
- **What the text does not say.** No internal use, no dose, and no statement that the tree in these lines is the species glossed in the Appendix.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this tree.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations read are burning the parts to ashes for alkali (Sutra 11, lines 124 to 130) and the Ksharagada of Kalpa 7. **No dose is given in the sources retrieved.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Erythrina variegata* is a tropical tree widely planted for shade, as a support for vines and as an ornamental; its seeds are not considered food. Many Fabaceae ashes are used as plant alkali. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC.** Do not eat the seeds or take the bark. Do not gather the plant for ingestion.
- **Alkali.** Alkali preparations are caustic; the Codex gives no procedure.
