# Whey — ORROS GALAKTOS, "whey" (Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 2-76)
## *Whey, the watery part of milk that separates from the curd; the translation's heading gloss is simply "Whey"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Chapter 2-76 of *De Materia Medica*, headed ORROS GALAKTOS, stands in Book 2 among the "Living Creatures", in the run of chapters on milk and its products that begins with 2-75, milk (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 5241-5243; `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 266, translator's identification "Whey"). I read the whole chapter and the chapters on either side. The chapter reads:

"All milk has whey contained within it. Separated out, this is fitter for purging and is given to those whom we would purge without distress, as well as for depression, epilepsy, leprosy, elephantiasis, and pustules that break out over the whole body."

The method is not in 2-76 but in the next chapter, 2-77 (GALA SCHISTON, headed "Junket" by the translator; lines 5244-5250): "All milk is separated by boiling it in a new ceramic jar and stirring it with a freshly cut down fig tree branch. After it has boiled two or three times a wine-cup of vinegar and honey for every half-pint of milk is poured into it: thus the whey is parted from the cheesy matter. So that the milk does not boil over you must continually rub the brim of the jar with a sponge dipped in cold water during boiling, and let down a silver pint jar full of cold water into it. Whey is given to drink (at intervals) in amounts of from one half-pint to five, and let those who drink it walk around during that time." The same chapter then turns to milk itself: new milk is good for ulcers and for inflammation caused by deadly medicines (cantharis, pine caterpillar, salamander, buprestis, hyoscyamus, aconite and others), and cow's milk is said to help best (line 5246ff).

Three other places in the milk run bear on whey. The chapter on milk (2-75, line 5235ff) gives a sensory assay of the raw material: "The best milk is white, has an equal thickness, and 'beads' when it is dropped on a fingernail", and notes that spring milk is more watery than summer milk and that milk from green pasture is gentler on the bowels. The chapter on woman's milk (2-78, lines 5251-5253) says that "all milk is worthless for the splenetic and hepatic, vertigo, epilepsy, and those troubled in their tendons, those who have fevers or whose heads ache, unless at any time one gives them whey for purgation as was formerly shown". And the chapter on new cheese (2-79, lines 5254-5260) says that "the whey that is made along with cheese is very good nourishment for dogs". So whey is recorded in four ways: as a purgative given "without distress" (2-76), as the product of a stated method (2-77), as the form in which milk may be given to people forbidden milk (2-78), and as feed for dogs (2-79).

*Other texts on disk, not folded in.* The Sushruta Samhita (Bhishagratna) has a "Takra Group" in the food chapter on milk and its products: "The Takra (whey) is sweet and acid in taste, and leaves an astringent after-taste. It is light, appetising and heat-making in its potency", and it is listed against poisoning, oedema, dysentery, jaundice, piles and other conditions (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 668-685; the next verses define *takram* as curd and water churned until the butter is skimmed off, and *gholam* as waterless curd churned with its butter, "a kind of whey", lines 686-695). In that text "whey" is the translator's English for *takra*, which on the verse's own description is a churned curd-and-water drink; whether it is the same material as Dioscorides' whey I cannot say from these texts. In the Tibetan Explanatory Tantra Clark's English has "after the essence (butter) has been extracted from the milk the whey that remains cures (infectious fever) imbalances, influenza, scattered fever, disturbed fever and diarrhoea" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 709) and "fresh whey is astringent, sour, and light in power, increases digestive heat and removes tumors, spleen disorders, hemorrhoids and undigested butter" (line 710); the Men-Tsee-Khang translation reads "Whey treats baekan problems without aggravating loong and tripa disorders" and "buttermilk" where Clark has "whey" in places (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` lines 3064-3065). Sushruta and the Tibetan texts are not on this entry's tag line; these loci are reported to the editor as candidates for added tags and not applied.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, whey is the liquid left when milk is curdled and the curd (casein and fat) is removed. It is mostly water, with the milk sugar lactose, the whey proteins (such as lactalbumin and lactoglobulin), some minerals and small amounts of fat and B vitamins. Dioscorides' method curdles the milk with heat and an acid (vinegar), and adds honey, which would add sugars. Nothing in the text links a constituent to the purging he reports.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records whey as a gentle purgative and as a drink for several chronic complaints, and the Codex does not recommend whey as a purge or a treatment for any of them. Whey is an ordinary dairy food. The point of the record is that Dioscorides treats it as a medicine of its own, separate from milk, with a stated method, and that he allows it to people from whom he withholds milk. People with a milk allergy or lactose intolerance should avoid it, and a complaint such as epilepsy, leprosy or a widespread skin eruption needs a clinician, not a drink. The entry is a record of the text.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparation is that of 2-77, quoted above: milk boiled in a new ceramic jar, stirred with a freshly cut fig branch, curdled after two or three boilings with a wine-cup of vinegar and honey for each half-pint of milk, with the brim wiped by a cold wet sponge and a silver pint jar of cold water lowered in to prevent boiling over. The dose is the one he gives: "from one half-pint to five" (a translator's rendering of the measure), drunk "at intervals", with the drinker walking about. The number of intervals and the length of a course are not given. The translator's units are not converted here. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Whey contains lactose, and in people who cannot digest lactose a large drink can cause loose stools, bloating and cramps, which is a plain reason a whey drink can act as a mild laxative. Whey is also a source of milk protein and calcium. Milk allergy is a separate condition, and people with it must avoid whey. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM):** The chapter prints one quality for whey: it is "fitter for purging" than milk in general. It prints no taste and no warming or cooling. For all milk the neighbouring chapter prints "nourishing, softening to the intestines, and causing the stomach and bowels to be inflated with gas" (2-75), and says that boiled milk "becomes astringent to the intestines", which is a statement about milk and not whey.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. The Galenic three spirits are later than Dioscorides. What he offers a practitioner is a discipline of attention (provenance, season of gathering, sensory assay, adulteration detection), and the milk chapters show it clearly: milk is assayed by colour, thickness and the bead on the fingernail (2-75); the season and the pasture are noted; and the method of making whey is a controlled process with a stated vessel, a stated stirrer, a stated acid and a stated way to stop the boil going over (2-77). He also records a rule for who may be given what: milk is withheld from the splenetic, the hepatic, the feverish and the epileptic, unless it is given as whey (2-78). That is a practice of observation and restraint. None of the whey chapters assigns a cultivation status; the uses are purgative and therapeutic.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator beyond ordinary food. The sourced lesson is attention: choose the milk by its look and body, notice the season and the pasture, and observe the process while making the whey. If a person is told to avoid milk, the Greek text would have them leave it alone or take only the whey, which is a statement about the text and not a recommendation. The Codex does not recommend whey as a purge. A dose is given only in the translator's "half-pint to five". [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Milk allergy and lactose intolerance:** avoid whey; it contains milk proteins and lactose.
- **Purging:** do not use any drink as a purge for epilepsy, leprosy, depression or a skin eruption; these need clinical care.
- **Dehydration and electrolyte loss:** repeated purging in the very young, old or ill can be dangerous.
- **Hygiene:** unpasteurised milk and whey can carry pathogens, including those of tuberculosis and brucellosis; heat-treated products are safer.
