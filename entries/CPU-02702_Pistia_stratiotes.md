# Pistia stratiotes — Kumbhika (printed "Kmubhik^" in the Appendix of Bhishagratna's Sushruta Samhita)

## *Pistia stratiotes L., the water lettuce, a floating aquatic plant of the arum family (Araceae), given as the Latin equivalent of the Sanskrit name Kumbhika in Bhishagratna's Appendix glossary; the pairing is by column position in a damaged e-text and is of medium confidence, and whether the drug of the same name in the Priyangvadi group of Sutra ch. 38 is this plant is unresolved* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 8 November 2026 as an unverified placeholder from a residual extraction (key "Pistia Stratiotes [SS]"); this entry replaces it.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The Latin name is printed in the Appendix glossary (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, "APPENDIX", line 32105: ", Pistia Stratiotes."). As in the whole glossary, the Sanskrit names stand in one column and the Latin names in another, so the pairing is by position. The Sanskrit run reads "Kulattha ... Kuma'ri ... Kmubhik^ ... Eamkama ... Eumuda" (lines about 32068 to 32076; the e-text misprints the third as "Kmubhik^", the fourth as "Eamkama" and the fifth as "Eumuda"), and the Latin run reads "Dolichos Biflorus; D. uniflorus. Aloe Barbadensis. Indica. Pistia Stratiotes. Crocus Sativus. Saffron." (lines 32102 to 32106). The first three Latin names fall against *Kulattha*, *Kuma'ri* and *Kumbhika*, and the fourth against *Kunkuma*; the third place therefore falls to *Pistia stratiotes*. The pairing of the neighbours (*Kulattha* with horse gram, *Kuma'ri* with aloe, *Kunkuma* with saffron) is consistent with it.

The name *Kumbhika* is also printed in the main text. It is the thirteen-drug **Priyangvadi group** of Sutra-sthana ch. 38 ("Priyangu, Samanga, Dhataki, Naga-pushpa, Chandana, Kuchandana, Mocharasa, Rasanjana, Kumbhika, Srotoanjana, Padma-keshara, Jojanvalli, and Dirghamula"; `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 320 to 324, the name on line 323; Vol. I line 15664). The Codex already holds an entry for that mention under the name "Kumbhika [SS]" (CPU-02174, `I_1705_Kumbhika_SS.md`), which treats the plant as unresolved because the chapter text prints no Latin name. The Appendix supplies a Latin name for the same Sanskrit word, but the Appendix does not say which of the several uses of the word in the work it refers to, and *Kumbhika* is also a disease name in the Uttara-tantra (`SS_Uttara_ch03.txt` line 17). This entry therefore stands on the Appendix identification alone and does not claim that it is the Priyangvadi drug. [USER: verify whether CPU-02174 and this row should be merged.]

No other tradition was searched for the Sanskrit name. The Dioscorides English text prints "Pistia stratiodes — Water Lettuce, Tropical Duckweed" as one of the translator's suggestions under 4-102, *Stratiotes* (line 8935), the chapter held by Codex row CPU-01640 (*Stratiotes aloides*). That is the translator's gloss on a Greek plant, not an attestation of this Sanskrit drug, and the two are not equated here.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Pistia stratiotes* is a floating aquatic arum, and plants of the arum family commonly contain calcium oxalate crystals that irritate the mouth and skin when the fresh plant is eaten or handled. ### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Little to map: the entry states no organ, tissue, action or body system for this drug. The Appendix gives only the Latin name Pistia stratiotes against the Sanskrit Kumbhika, by column position in a damaged glossary, so the identification itself is medium confidence. The word appears in the Priyangvadi group of Sutra ch. 38, a thirteen-drug group whose actions the chapter gives for the group as a whole, and the entry does not assign those actions to this plant. No taste, potency, post-digestive effect, preparation or part used is given. The entry therefore maps nothing to the body, and the Codex does not recommend the plant.
