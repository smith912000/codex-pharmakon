# Fragaria nubicola — Drita sazin, "Wild strawberry" (rGyud-bzhi, Men-Tsee-Khang glossary)

## *Fragaria nubicola, as the scientific name the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints (line 5259) in the position that column order gives to the Tibetan name "Drita sazin"; the English column prints "Wild strawberry" (line 5261)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT), and under this binomial only by the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Sushruta texts on disk (including the three Bhishagratna volumes) for the genus name *Fragaria* found no occurrence. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, line 5259.* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. The glossary is printed in three columns that the text file has split into separate runs of lines: the phonetic names, the Tibetan transliterations and the scientific names, then the English names. In the "D" block (the letter at line 5179) the phonetic names run from "Dali" (line 5181) to "Drita sazin" (line 5196), twenty-two names in all, and the transliterations end with "gesar ’bri ta sa ’dzin" (line 5213). The scientific names begin at line 5215 and end with a line that reads "Fragaria nubicola" (line 5259), followed by the English "Wild strawberry" (line 5261). Counting in order, "Drita sazin" is the last phonetic name and *Fragaria nubicola* is the last scientific name, and the English fits it. The glossary therefore names *Fragaria nubicola* for this drug by column position; it does not print the pairing in one row. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text.* In chapter 20 (the run headed "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines", line 3428), line 3446 reads: "Dhambu kara cures hot disorder of the lungs, liver, and channels. Drita sazin drain pus, blood and chhuser. Jhapo tsitsi stops menstrual bleeding." The Codex does not translate *chhuser* into another word. In chapter 21, lines 3659 to 3660: "Soenchha, Ri sho, Chang tsher, Shudag, Serphue, Drita sazin, Kyi dru, Yakyima and white mustard seed are the group of medicines that are emetics." The running text gives no Latin name in either passage.

*Clark 1995.* The same Tibetan drug appears in Clark's materia medica as *hbri.ta.sa.hdzin* (line 1058), "a type of Wild Strawberry", where the first of four binomials is *Fragaria nilgeerensis* Schlecht, a different species; the entry's sentence reads "Fragaria is an emetic in pus, blood and lymph disorders [as well as diseases in the upper body]." Clark's chapter 21 (line 1224) lists "Fragaria nilgeerensis (a type of mock strawberry)" among "the class of emetics". Clark does not print *Fragaria nubicola*. The two Tibetan texts agree on the Tibetan name and on the genus and differ on the species; the Codex does not choose.

**Overlap.** The same Tibetan drug is the subject of I_1280 (*Fragaria nilgeerensis*, from Clark 1995), a sibling entry. This file records the glossary's species; nothing is merged. I_810 (Wild Strawberry, *Fragaria vesca*) is a different species from the European tradition.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Fragaria* is a genus of the rose family (Rosaceae) of low, creeping plants that spread by runners and bear small fruit. Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained or which part was used.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Men-Tsee-Khang text records a drug named "Drita sazin" that "drain[s] pus, blood and chhuser" (line 3446) and stands in the group of emetics (lines 3659 to 3660). The link from that name to this binomial rests on column position in the glossary. These are the text's claims and not Codex protocols. Inducing vomiting is a hazard and not a practice here. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no part used, no quantity, no frequency and no duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] The glossary and the running text name the drug and its action only.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** No taste, power or post-digestive taste is printed for this drug in the Men-Tsee-Khang lines read (3446, 3659 to 3660). The one action printed is the draining of "pus, blood and chhuser" and membership of the emetic group. Clark's commentary layer for the same Tibetan name (line 1058) gives the fruit a "slightly sweet-tasting" quality, but that is Clark's entry for another species and is not carried to this one.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Men-Tsee-Khang text gives this drug by what it does: it drains pus, blood and *chhuser*, and it stands in the group of emetics. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this name places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the sentences cited above were read in full and none mentions one. Its framing is therapeutic only, and the link from the binomial to that framing rests on column position.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is an emetic in the Tibetan texts, with no preparation and no dose, and its Latin name differs between the two Tibetan texts. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. Emesis can cause dehydration and, in some conditions, harm; the identity is open between *Fragaria* species; and no preparation or dose is given. No plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Pus, blood and upper-body disorders need medical care. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
