# Rhinoceros milk — milk of a cow-rhinoceros (Sushruta Samhita, translator's footnote)
## *The milk of a female rhinoceros (Bhishagratna's "cow-rhinoceros"), which his footnote adds to Sushruta's list of milks on the strength of the construction of the original sentence; the species is not named, and the identification as milk of a rhinoceros is the text's plain meaning, confidence medium* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry, and the attestation is a translator's footnote.** On the project's spine (CPU-02390) only the *Sushruta Samhita* is attested for the milk of a cow-rhinoceros. The row began on 13 October 2026 as an unverified placeholder from `_nonplant/nonplant_master.csv` (curated 8 October, confidence probable); this entry replaces it. The locus is Bhishagratna's English.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The locus is *Sutrasthana* ch.45 (the chapter on liquids), `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`, footnote at lines 441-444 to the milk group. The milk group opens (lines 413-416, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`): "The milk of a cow, she-goat, she-camel, ewe, she-buffalo, mare, she-elephant, or of a woman, is what generally comes to the use of man." The text then describes milk in general (lines 419-462) as "the white fluid essence of drugs and cereals ... the best of all nutritive substances", "heavy, sweet, slimy, cold, glossy, emollient, laxative and mild", and, among a long list of benefits, "rejuvenating and aphrodisiac" and one that "increases the duration of life" (lines 450-457). Those lines belong to milk as a group and are not specific to any one animal. The footnote, which is attached to the line "The milk of a cow, she-goat, she-camel, ewe, she-buffalo, mare, she-elephant, or of a woman" (line 415) but printed on the page with the discussion of the benefits of milk, reads: "From the construction of the present sentence in the original texts, we are warranted to include the milk of a doe, or of a she-mule, or of a cow-rhinoceros in the list, as they sometimes prove beneficial for external applications."

**What that means for the entry.** The footnote is the translator's own, and gives his reading of the construction of the Sanskrit sentence. The substance is named only there. The footnote does not say which rhinoceros is meant. The footnote says only that such milk "sometimes prove[s] beneficial for external applications"; it names no disease, gives no property of taste or potency, and no dose. The Sushruta chapters on disease and treatment (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`) were not searched for further mention of this milk. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The English is "a cow-rhinoceros", that is a female rhinoceros. The species is not given. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The Sushruta text names no constituent. Milk is, in general, an emulsion of fat in a water solution of lactose with proteins, minerals and vitamins; composition varies by species. ### Contraindications

- **Hygiene.** Raw milk of any animal can carry infection.
