# Ningpo Figwort — Xuan Shen (玄参, "dark/mysterious root"), Scrophulariae Radix, Yuan Shen (元参); Western congeners: Common Figwort / Throatwort / *herba scrophulariae* / *galeopsis* of the Greeks
## *Scrophularia ningpoensis* Hemsl. | Family: Scrophulariaceae
**Tradition Tags:** CM, DMM, SBJ
**Energetic Classification:** TCM — Cold, sweet-bitter-salty; Kidney, Stomach, Lung channels. Galenic/Unani (of the European figworts) — cool and drying; a *muḥallil* (resolvent) of hard cold swellings.
**Training Phase:** Cooling / Resolving — a yin-nourishing, nodule-dispersing agent used where heat has dried the fluids and matter has hardened.

---

### Origin & Tradition

A tall perennial of eastern China (Zhejiang, Sichuan, Hubei), whose thick, dark, fleshy root is one of the standard heat-clearing, yin-nourishing drugs of Chinese medicine. Its Western relatives — *Scrophularia nodosa* and kin — carry a Latin name that is itself a diagnosis. The convergence between the two ends of Eurasia is the reason this entry exists.

**Shennong / Chinese tradition (*Shennong Bencao Jing* lineage; the drug is a canonical item of Chinese materia medica and appears in the *Shennong Bencao Jing* as **玄参**).** Xuan Shen — "the dark root" — is filed among the **heat-clearing, blood-cooling (清热凉血 *qīng rè liáng xuè*)** drugs. Its three canonical functions are stated with unusual consistency across the tradition and are repeated verbatim in the modern pharmacognostic literature:

1. **Clear heat and cool the blood** (for heat entering the blood level: fever, crimson tongue, eruptions).
2. **Nourish yin and reduce fire** (for yin-deficient heat: dry throat, night sweats, thirst, sore red throat).
3. **Detoxify and DISPERSE NODULES** (解毒散结 *jiě dú sàn jié*) — for hard lumps and swellings in the neck, sores, and specifically for **瘰疬 *luǒlì*: scrofula**, the chain of hard, matted, chronically suppurating cervical lymph nodes.

That third function is the hinge of this entry. A 2021 review states the classical indications in exactly these terms: Scrophulariae Radix is used "for clearing away heat and cooling blood, nourishing Yin and reducing fire, detoxicating and resolving a mass," and has been in continuous East Asian use for over two thousand years (Zhang et al. 2021, *J Pharm Pharmacol*, [DOI 10.1093/jpp/rgaa036](https://doi.org/10.1093/jpp/rgaa036)).

**Dioscorides / Greek and the European figwort tradition.** The Greek herbal knows a knobbly-rooted plant of this affinity (the *galeopsis*/figwort group) used against hard swellings, scrofulous sores, haemorrhoids and skin ulceration. The European tradition then hardens this into a name: the root of the common figwort is covered in **knobs, tubercles and glandular swellings**, and this was read as a **signature** — the plant that *looks like* swollen glands is the plant that *cures* swollen glands. The result is the botanical name we still use: ***Scrophularia*, "the scrofula plant"** — scrofula being the tuberculous cervical lymphadenitis known in England as the King's Evil. English folk names preserve the doctrine intact: **throatwort**, **kernelwort** ("kernels" = the palpable neck nodes). The Western figworts were poulticed and decocted for exactly this indication for well over a millennium.

**Avicenna / Unani.** The Arabic-Persian tradition inherits the Greek knobby-root drug within the class of *muḥallil* (resolvent) and *muḥallil al-awrām al-ṣulba* — resolvers of **hard swellings** — the category into which scrofulous glands (Arabic *khanāzīr*, "the swine-disease", the direct semantic parallel of Latin *scrofula* from *scrofa*, "sow") were classified. The Unani reading is again structural rather than botanical: **the drug class is "cool resolvents of hard glandular swelling", and the figworts sit in it.** The specific identification of Avicenna's item with any single *Scrophularia* species is **not secure** and this Codex does not assert one. **Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:**

This is the most striking convergence in Volume I, and it deserves to be stated precisely, because the temptation to over-read it is enormous.

Two medical civilisations, with no meaningful pharmacological contact, at opposite ends of Eurasia, took two different species of the **same genus** and used them for **the same disease** — hard, chronic, suppurating swellings of the neck lymph nodes — and each arrived there by a completely different route.

**Europe arrived by signature.** The reasoning is visible and, by modern lights, invalid: the root is knobbly like a diseased gland → therefore it treats diseased glands. This is the doctrine of signatures in its purest form. It is the kind of reasoning that produces walnut-for-brain and lungwort-for-lung, and it is *usually wrong*.

**China arrived by system.** Xuan Shen was not selected because it looks like a node. It was placed in the *qing re liang xue* category by its taste, its temperature, its effect on the tongue and pulse, and its observed action on the fluids — and *disperse nodules* is a function that follows from the theory: heat has consumed the fluids, the residue has congealed into a mass (痰核 phlegm-nodule), and a cold, moistening, softening (salty-taste) drug will soften and disperse it. The Chinese route is theory-driven and the European route is resemblance-driven, and **they land on the same clinical target using different species of the same botanical genus.**

That double convergence is what makes the case interesting rather than merely charming. If it were one tradition, it would be coincidence. If it were the same species, it might be trade. It is neither.

**Now the chemistry, and the honesty this entry demands.**

The genus is defined pharmacologically by **iridoid glycosides** — principally **harpagoside** and **aucubin** (and harpagide, catalpol-type iridoids) — together with **phenylpropanoid glycosides** (angoroside C, acteoside/verbascoside-type), **iridoid-rich** in the root, plus organic acids and sterols (Zhang et al. 2021, [DOI 10.1093/jpp/rgaa036](https://doi.org/10.1093/jpp/rgaa036); new iridoids continue to be isolated — Ma et al. 2017, *Chem Pharm Bull*, [DOI 10.1248/cpb.c17-00163](https://doi.org/10.1248/cpb.c17-00163)).

Harpagoside is the same iridoid that carries the anti-inflammatory reputation of Devil's Claw (*Harpagophytum*), and it is a genuinely characterised anti-inflammatory agent. It is the molecule the convergence has to be mapped onto — carefully:

- **"Disperse nodules / resolve hard swellings" ↔ anti-inflammatory iridoid pharmacology.** Scrofula is a *chronically inflamed, granulomatous, suppurating* lymph node. A drug that damps NF-κB-driven inflammatory signalling and reduces macrophage nitric-oxide output will reduce the *inflammatory bulk and the pain and the discharge* of such a node. Ma et al. (2017) isolated new iridoids from *S. ningpoensis* rhizomes and evaluated anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, antifungal and cytotoxic activity, with one compound significantly inhibiting **LPS-induced nitric oxide production in RAW264.7 macrophages** ([DOI 10.1248/cpb.c17-00163](https://doi.org/10.1248/cpb.c17-00163)). That is a real, measured correlate of "resolve the mass" — but it is an *anti-inflammatory* correlate, not a *curative* one.
- **"Clear heat, cool the blood" ↔ suppression of the inflammatory febrile cascade.** The Chinese heat-clearing category and the modern anti-inflammatory/antipyretic category are not the same concept, but they overlap on a large shared clinical territory: the drugs that damp cytokine-driven fever, redness and inflammatory pain. Xuan Shen's placement is coherent with its chemistry.
- **"Nourish yin, moisten dryness" ↔ mucilage and polysaccharide fraction, plus reduced inflammatory fluid loss.** This is the softest mapping and the Codex marks it as **speculative**.

**And here is the honest limit, which must not be blurred:** scrofula is *tuberculosis*. It is caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (or, in children, atypical mycobacteria). Neither tradition cured it, because **no iridoid glycoside has been shown to cure mycobacterial lymphadenitis**, and this Codex retrieved no evidence that any *Scrophularia* preparation does. What both traditions could genuinely do was **reduce the inflammation, the heat, the pain, and the discharge** of a chronic granulomatous node. Both traditions then interpreted symptomatic relief as resolution — which is exactly the error a symptom-relieving anti-inflammatory invites.

So the convergence is real, and its explanation is deflationary and *more* interesting than the mystical reading: **two traditions independently found a genuinely anti-inflammatory root and applied it to the most conspicuous chronic inflammatory swelling in their clinical experience.** The signature doctrine did not detect a cure. It detected an anti-inflammatory — and then over-claimed. The Chinese system did the same thing from the other direction. The pharmacology is the same in both, and so is the limit.

### Active Components

- **Iridoid glycosides:** **harpagoside** (the flagship; also the marker for quality control), **harpagide**, **aucubin**, catalpol-type iridoids, plus continuing novel isolations (Ma et al. 2017, [DOI 10.1248/cpb.c17-00163](https://doi.org/10.1248/cpb.c17-00163)).
- **Phenylpropanoid glycosides:** angoroside C, acteoside (verbascoside)-type caffeoyl phenylethanoids — antioxidant and anti-inflammatory.
- **Organic acids, sterols, saccharides/mucilage, essential-oil traces, alkaloid traces.** The full phytochemical, pharmacological, quality-control and pharmacokinetic picture is reviewed in Zhang et al. 2021, *J Pharm Pharmacol* ([DOI 10.1093/jpp/rgaa036](https://doi.org/10.1093/jpp/rgaa036)).
- Note: harpagoside content varies by provenance and processing; Chinese pharmacopoeial QC for Scrophulariae Radix uses harpagoside (and related markers) precisely because potency is not stable across sources.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Xuan Shen sits in the **Cooling / Resolving phase**. Its Kenetica role:

- **Dry, hot, depleted states:** where heat has burned off the fluids — dry irritated throat, thirst, night restlessness, a red dry tongue. This is the yin-nourishing indication and it is the one Xuan Shen is most reliably used for in practice, almost always **in formula, never alone**.
- **Chronic inflammatory swelling and hardened tissue:** as the classical "disperse nodules" agent — with the explicit, non-negotiable caveat below that any persistent neck lump is a *diagnostic emergency*, not a herbal indication.
- **Not** a first-line agent, not a daily tonic, and not appropriate for cold, damp, or hypofunctional presentations — its Cold nature will worsen them.

### Scientific Correlation

*Attribution: the following studies were retrieved from PubMed.*

1. **Comprehensive phytochemistry/pharmacology review, with the classical indications stated.** Zhang, Wang et al. (2021) systematically review *S. ningpoensis* — phytochemistry, pharmacology, quality control, pharmacokinetics — and record the traditional indications (clear heat, cool blood, nourish yin, reduce fire, detoxify, resolve a mass) alongside the modern data. *J Pharm Pharmacol* 2021. [DOI 10.1093/jpp/rgaa036](https://doi.org/10.1093/jpp/rgaa036). **Grade: strong as a secondary source; the primary literature it summarises is overwhelmingly preclinical.**

2. **Anti-inflammatory activity of *S. ningpoensis* iridoids — measured.** Ma et al. (2017) isolated five new iridoids plus six known compounds from *S. ningpoensis* rhizomes, structures determined by NMR/IR/MS, and assayed anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, antifungal and cytotoxic activity; one compound significantly inhibited LPS-induced nitric oxide production in RAW264.7 macrophages. *Chem Pharm Bull (Tokyo)* 2017. [DOI 10.1248/cpb.c17-00163](https://doi.org/10.1248/cpb.c17-00163). **Grade: in vitro.** This is the closest thing to a direct molecular correlate of "disperse nodules" that exists — and it is a cell assay, not a patient.

3. **Harpagoside — neuroprotection (a use *outside* both traditions).** Li et al. (2015) showed harpagoside, an iridoid purified from *S. ningpoensis*, protects against β-amyloid-induced cognitive impairment in rats, up-regulating BDNF and acting via MAPK/PI3K pathways. *Neuroscience* 2015. [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroscience.2015.06.042](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2015.06.042). **Grade: animal model only.** Recorded because it is real and because it is *not* a traditional claim — the tradition did not predict this, and honesty requires saying when the molecule outruns the doctrine as well as when the doctrine outruns the molecule.

4. **Harpagoside — uric-acid lowering and renal protection.** Fu et al. (2024) report that harpagoside lowers uric acid and protects against hyperuricaemia-induced renal injury in mice, with mechanistic work on urate-producing and urate-transporting proteins. *Biochem Biophys Res Commun* 2024. [DOI 10.1016/j.bbrc.2024.150038](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2024.150038). **Grade: animal model only.**

5. **⚠️ THE NEGATIVE FINDING — harpagoside causes anaphylaxis.** Che et al. (2018) demonstrated that harpagoside — the *principal active constituent* — induces an **anaphylactic reaction in an IgE-independent manner, both in vitro and in vivo**, via mast-cell activation, and explicitly connect this to the frequently reported allergic responses to clinical *Scrophularia ningpoensis* injection preparations in China. *Immunopharmacol Immunotoxicol* 2018. [DOI 10.1080/08923973.2018.1424900](https://doi.org/10.1080/08923973.2018.1424900). **Grade: strong preclinical, with clinical pharmacovigilance signal.** This is not a footnote. The molecule that carries the anti-inflammatory reputation also carries a pseudo-allergic (MRGPRX2-type, IgE-independent) mast-cell activation liability, which means a reaction can occur **on first exposure with no prior sensitisation**.

**Honest evidence grade for the entry as a whole:** The chemistry is well characterised. The anti-inflammatory activity is real but **preclinical** — cell assays and rodent models. **No randomised controlled human trial of *Scrophularia ningpoensis* for scrofula, lymphadenitis, nodules, sore throat or yin-deficiency was retrieved in this search.** The Eurasian convergence on the scrofula indication is historically and doctrinally striking and **should not be mistaken for clinical validation**: neither tradition cured tuberculous lymphadenitis, and the modern evidence does not show that this plant does either. Grade the traditional indications **traditional, with a plausible anti-inflammatory mechanism and no clinical proof** — and grade the safety signal **stronger than the efficacy signal**, which is an uncomfortable but accurate summary.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM (attested, canonical):** Taste — sweet, bitter, salty (甘苦咸). Temperature — **Cold** (寒). Channels — Kidney, Stomach, Lung. Category — clear heat / cool blood (清热凉血). ### Cultivation Register

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The European figworts enter the Arabic-Persian corpus as *muḥallil* — resolvents of hard, cold swellings (*awrām ṣulba*), the *muḥallil al-awrām al-ṣulba* class into which *khanāzīr* ("the swine-disease", the semantic twin of Latin *scrofula*) was filed — and are read as cool and drying. The tradition's governing cultivation idea is the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture, the finite vital endowment whose depletion is ageing; but that is a frame of regimen, and a cooling resolvent of glandular swelling is placed by *quality and target*, not assigned a status for tending vitality. No *ruh*-strengthening or *mufarrih* role is claimed, and — as the entry states — the identification of Avicenna's item with any single *Scrophularia* species is not secure, so even the therapeutic attestation is by class rather than by confirmed binomial. Cultivation status: **(b) framed** — attested within the resolvent class and the innate-heat frame, with no cultivation status assigned and species identity itself unsettled.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The knobbly-rooted figwort / *galeopsis* group is used against hard swellings, scrofulous sores, haemorrhoids and ulcers. The famous reasoning attached to this plant — the root that *looks like* a swollen gland *cures* swollen glands, which hardened into the genus name *Scrophularia* and the folk-names throatwort and kernelwort — is the European doctrine of signatures, a resemblance logic that post-dates Dioscorides and is not a subtle-body doctrine at all; Dioscorides himself carries no vital-body system into which a cultivation status could be entered. What the Greek tradition genuinely offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention: the knobby root recognised and assayed by its form, its cool drying quality judged on the tongue and the tissue. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status — the signature reads a resemblance, not a status for building vitality.

**Chinese — Shennong stratum (SBJ).** Xuan Shen 玄参, "the dark root", is a canonical item of the classical materia medica, filed among the heat-clearing, blood-cooling drugs (清热凉血) with the three consistent functions of clearing heat and cooling blood, nourishing yin and reducing fire (滋阴降火), and detoxifying and dispersing nodules (解毒散结, for 瘰疬 *luǒlì*, scrofula). Its project sits inside *yang sheng* 養生, nourishing life — yet *yang sheng* is conservational, and Xuan Shen is a **Cold**, fluid-restoring drug worked against heat that has burned off the yin, closer to correcting a depletion than to a long-term nourishing regimen taken to lengthen life. No *san pin* 三品 grade for this species could be traced to named scholarship (Unschuld, Stanley-Baker, or a scholarly translation) rather than to conflicting wellness sources, so no upper/middle/lower cultivation grade is asserted here; and the yin it nourishes is yin-fluid, not a *jing* 精 claim, which the materia medica itself rarely makes explicitly. **(b) framed** — canonical and yin-nourishing in function, but with no *san pin* cultivation grade citable and therefore none recorded.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator uses Xuan Shen only in formula, never alone, for dry, hot, depleted states — the parched throat, the night restlessness, the red dry tongue of burned-off fluids — and its Cold nature makes it wrong for cold, damp, hypofunctional constitutions. It is not a daily tonic or a longevity substance. The historical "disperse nodules" indication is the one place a cultivator must *stop*: a persistent neck lump is a diagnostic emergency (tuberculosis, lymphoma, carcinoma), not a nodule to be dispersed, and the harpagoside anaphylaxis signal means the safety warning outweighs any cultivation ambition here.

### Contraindications

- **⚠️ ANY PERSISTENT NECK LUMP OR SWOLLEN LYMPH NODE REQUIRES MEDICAL DIAGNOSIS BEFORE ANY HERBAL TREATMENT.** The historical indication for this plant — a hard, chronic, matted cervical mass — is, in modern terms, the presenting sign of **tuberculosis, lymphoma, or metastatic carcinoma**. Treating it as a "nodule to be dispersed" delays diagnosis of conditions that are curable when caught and lethal when missed. **This is the single most important line in this entry.**
- **⚠️ ANAPHYLAXIS RISK — IgE-independent.** Harpagoside induces anaphylactic reaction via non-IgE mast-cell activation in vitro and in vivo, and allergic reactions to clinical *S. ningpoensis* injection preparations are frequently reported (Che et al. 2018, [DOI 10.1080/08923973.2018.1424900](https://doi.org/10.1080/08923973.2018.1424900)). Because the mechanism is IgE-independent, a **first-ever exposure can produce a reaction**. **Injectable Scrophularia preparations are not endorsed by this Codex under any circumstances.** Discontinue immediately on any rash, wheeze, swelling, or flushing.
- **Cold/deficient patterns:** contraindicated in spleen/stomach cold-damp, poor appetite, loose stools, chronic diarrhoea. Xuan Shen is Cold and will deepen a cold, hypofunctional picture. This is a standard and well-founded TCM interdiction.
- **Classical incompatibility:** do not combine with *Veratrum* (Li Lu, 藜芦) per the Eighteen Incompatibilities. (Recorded as tradition; *Veratrum* is independently a cardiotoxic plant and should not be used regardless.)
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** insufficient data. Avoid.
- **Anticoagulants:** exercise caution — iridoid/phenylpropanoid-rich extracts and platelet/coagulation interactions are not adequately characterised for this species. [USER: verify before printing a specific interaction claim.]
- **Autoimmune and immunomodulatory contexts, and mast-cell disorders (mastocytosis, chronic urticaria):** avoid, given the documented direct mast-cell activation.
- **Do not self-prescribe.** Xuan Shen is a formula herb in a diagnostic system. Used solo, on the strength of a doctrine, against a lump, without diagnosis, it is close to the worst use case in this Codex.
