# Muscle fat (vasa) — Vasa (also printed Vasd, Vassi), the fat of the muscles, Sushruta Samhita
## *The animal "muscle-fat" Bhishagratna prints as Vasa and renders "myosin" in Sutrasthana ch.45, "muscle-fat" in Sharirasthana ch.4, and "lard", "grease" or "essence of the flesh" elsewhere; the source animal varies by passage* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Vasa is first met as a body substance. *Sharirasthana* ch.4 defines it among the fats: "Marrow is found inside large bones, whereas a substance similar in appearance and found inside other bony structures (cartilages) should be considered as Meda, mixed with blood. The fats, present in purely muscular structures, go by the name of Vassi (muscle-fat)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sharira/SS_Sharira_ch04.txt`, lines 119-125). The translator's Vol. 3 index has "Vasa (muscle-fat)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, line 27628).

As an animal product it stands in the Oil Group of *Sutrasthana* ch.45, the chapter on liquid substances (*Drava-Dravya-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`; the Codex's cited locus is line 1051): "The oil, myosin (Vasa), fat, marrow, and Ghritam obtained from animals, which live in villages (Gramya), or frequent the marshy swamps (Anupa), or are aquatic (Audoka) in their habits, are heavy, heat-making in their potency, and sweet in taste. They subdue the bodily Vayu, while those obtained from Jangala (such as deer, etc.) or carnivorous animals, or from those possessed of unbifurcated hoofs, are light, cool in their potency, astringent in taste, and prove curative in cases of haemoptysis. The fat, marrow, etc. of animals of the Pratuda and Vishkira species (doves, pigeons, etc.) reduce the bodily Kapham. Of clarified butter, oil, myosin (Vasa), fat and marrow of animals each is heavier in digestion, and possesses a greater power of subduing the bodily Vayu than the one immediately preceding it in the order of enumeration" (lines 1051-1074). The sentence thus sets Vasa third in a series of five, after ghee and oil and before fat and marrow.

Other places, where Vasa is an ingredient or a diseased substance. As an ingredient: lukewarm plaster of pasted *Murangi* roots with ghee, oil and lard (Vasa) for *Vataja Vidradhi* (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch16.txt`, lines 17-21); "fresh Vasa (lard) of Jangala animals" as an errhine and, cooked with the *Utpaladi* drugs, in the manner of an *Asthapana* enema, in the chapter on head diseases (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch26.txt`, lines 83-86); curd, ghee, oil, lard (Vasa) and marrow of marsh-dwelling and aquatic animals as an *Anuvasana* enema in *Vataja Gulma* (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch42.txt`, lines 148-152); "The Vasa (essence of the flesh) of aquatic animals or of those which frequent swampy grounds", mixed with powdered *Sunthi* and *Saindhava* salt, applied to the eyes in the non-secreting type of conjunctivitis (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch09.txt`, lines 138-142); the *Snehana* eye-pack (*puta-paka*) prepared with flesh "abounding in Sneha", with Vasa, marrow and fat and the *Madhura* group drugs (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch18.txt`, lines 145-150); and a plaster of *Shirisha*, *Katuka*, *Kushtha*, *Vacha*, *Rajani*, *Saindhava*, milk, marrow, lard (Vasa), ghee, *Sunthi*, *Pippali* and *Devadaru* as a fomentation for an insect bite (`kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch08.txt`, lines 274-278). As a diseased substance: *Vasa-meha* is one of four *Vataja* types of *Prameha*, with *Sarpi-meha*, *Kshoudra-meha* and *Hasti-meha*, "most incurable", and the aggravated *Vayu* in union with *Kapham*, *Pittam*, fat, marrow and Vasa engenders them (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/nidana/SS_Nidana_ch06.txt`, lines 70-77 and 90-93; also `uttara/SS_Uttara_ch65.txt`, lines 196-203). No Dioscorides, Tibetan or other text is attached to this entry; the tag is SS only.

**Identity and wording.** "Myosin" is the translator's rendering in ch.45; in current usage the word names a muscle protein, which the text cannot mean, and the other places render the same word "lard", "grease", "muscle-fat" and "essence of the flesh". The source animal is not fixed: ch.45 distributes it by the animal's habitat, and the other passages name "Jangala animals" or "aquatic animals". The Codex's tag and the Sushruta text agree on the substance, a fatty animal product; they do not give a single species. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Muscle fat of an animal is mostly triglycerides with some phospholipids and cholesterol, and fat-soluble vitamins in small amounts. Its fatty-acid make-up depends on the species and diet. No analysis specific to this entry was read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Sushruta uses animal fat chiefly as a vehicle in enemas, errhines, plasters and eye-packs, and as a dietary fat. The Codex does not recommend rendering or taking animal fats for any of these uses and gives no protocol. Animal fat intake is a general dietary and cardiovascular question, and the source animal in the text is unspecified. A reader who avoids animal products for ethical, religious or health reasons has no need of it on the strength of this text.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the text gives are all compounds in which Vasa is one ingredient: the plaster for *Vataja Vidradhi* (*Chikitsasthana* ch.16), the errhine and enema (*Uttaratantra* ch.26 and ch.42), the eye-salve and eye-pack (ch.9, ch.18), and the bite plaster (*Kalpasthana* ch.8). The passages read do not state how the Vasa itself is obtained or rendered. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Animal fats are dense energy sources and are rich in saturated fatty acids; rendered fat can go rancid and become a source of contamination if stored warm. Any animal fat applied to the eye or taken by enema should be regarded as a hygiene hazard for sterility reasons. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.45 (lines 1051-1074). From village, marsh or aquatic animals: heavy; heat-making in potency; sweet in taste; subdues *Vayu*. From *Jangala* or carnivorous animals, or those with unbifurcated hoofs: light; cool in potency; astringent in taste; curative in haemoptysis. From the *Pratuda* and *Vishkira* birds: reduces *Kapham*. In the series ghee, oil, Vasa, fat, marrow, each is heavier and subdues *Vayu* more than the one before it. No post-digestive taste (*vipaka*) is printed. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Vasa belongs to the Oil Group of the chapter on liquids (ch.45). The text states its qualities and its place in the series of fats, and uses it as a vehicle and an ingredient. The text defines it as a body substance (the fat of the muscles) and as a diseased substance (*Vasa-meha*); neither is a cultivation claim, and the *ojas* of the *dhatus* is not tied to it in any line read. No *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter read names it. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta, Vasa is an animal fat of stated weight and potency, used as a vehicle and a dietary fat, and a substance whose derangement gives one of the incurable *Prameha* types. A cultivator has nothing to take from it; the one cautionary reading is that the text ranks it heavier than ghee and oil.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** animal fat of unknown origin applied to the eye or given by enema is a contamination risk; the text's eye uses are historical and are not advised.
- **Heavy and heat-making:** from village, marsh and aquatic animals, according to ch.45; the text itself ranks it heavier than ghee and oil.
- **Identity:** the source animal is not fixed by the text, and "myosin" is not a protein claim. [USER: verify]
- **Cardiovascular and dietary:** animal fat is high in saturated fat; no dose or safe amount is given.
- **Ethics and religion:** animal fat comes from slaughtered or hunted animals, a moral question for many traditions; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
