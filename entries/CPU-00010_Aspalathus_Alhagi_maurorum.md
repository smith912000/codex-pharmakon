### Origin & Tradition

Dioscorides lists *aspalathos* among the aromatic woods of Book I — a hard, fragrant, somewhat rose-scented wood used in perfumery and as an astringent. The trouble is that **the botanical identity of the classical *aspalathos* is genuinely uncertain**, and has been disputed for centuries: candidates include thorny legumes such as *Alhagi maurorum* (camel-thorn) and *Convolvulus dorycnium*, among others. The Codex follows the canonical index in tentatively assigning *Alhagi maurorum*, **while flagging the identification as unresolved** — a model case of the "id-uncertain" entries that pervade the early Dioscoridean material. (This name is unrelated to the modern *Aspalathus linearis*, rooibos, a South African plant given the Linnaean genus name much later.)

Whatever the exact classical referent, *Alhagi maurorum* itself has a rich and well-attested medicinal life across the Near East and Central Asia. It is a thorny, deep-rooted desert legume, and its great traditional value is as the source of a sweet exudate — **tarangabin** (Persian) / **Persian manna** — a sugary substance that collects on the plant, gathered and used as a gentle laxative, demulcent and cooling sweet, particularly for children and in fevers. This "manna" is one of several plant mannas of the Old World pharmacy (alongside the manna of *Fraxinus ornus* and the tamarisk manna), all valued as mild, food-like aperient sweets.

In Unani and Persian medicine *Alhagi* (yantak/aaqul) is used for its cooling, diuretic and laxative qualities — for "hot" conditions, urinary complaints, and as a febrifuge and liver-soother. The thorny aerial parts and roots are decocted; the manna is taken as the gentle purgative-demulcent. The split character of the plant — astringent-aromatic wood in the Greek tradition, cooling-laxative manna in the Persian — is exactly what one expects of a desert legume whose different parts and exudates serve different ends.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM / Galenic humoral degree:** the classical *aspalathos* wood was reckoned astringent and warming-drying (binding); **identity caveat applies.** The Persian *manna*, by contrast, is treated as cooling and moistening — illustrating how a single canonical name can sit across opposite qualities depending on which substance and tradition is meant.
- **Unani/Persian mapping:** *Alhagi* (yantak) — cold and dry temperament in the aerial parts (cooling, diuretic, astringent); the manna sweet and gently laxative. Used for hot dystemperaments, urinary and hepatic complaints.
- **Kenetica Qi/Shen tier:** ◉ **Qi** — operating on circulation and elimination (gentle purgation, diuresis, cooling), with the manna on the cooling-moistening side and the wood/aerial parts on the astringent-drying side.

### Contraindications

- **Identity uncertainty.** Because the classical *aspalathos* is unresolved, practical use should rest on the well-characterised *Alhagi maurorum*, not on an assumed equivalence.
- **Pregnancy — caution.** Diuretic and laxative desert legumes warrant caution in pregnancy. [USER: verify.]
- **Diarrhoea / loose stool.** The manna's laxative action is inappropriate where stools are already loose.
- **Diabetes.** Tarangabin is a sugar; relevant for glycaemic control if taken in quantity.
- **Fluid/electrolyte balance.** Strong or prolonged diuretic decoctions warrant the usual cautions in those on diuretics or with renal compromise.
