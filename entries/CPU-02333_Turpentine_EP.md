# Turpentine — Turpentine, Turpentine-nuts (Ebers Papyrus, Bryan 1930)

## *Bryan's English rendering of a plant substance in the Ebers Papyrus, "Turpentine" and "Turpentine-nuts"; no Egyptian word and no tree is given in the sources read, and the 1930 word covers resin from the terebinth and from conifers; the identification is therefore a modern hypothesis only, of low confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Ebers Papyrus (EP), in Bryan's 1930 translation.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder with the locus "EP Bryan 1930, Ch. VI Plant Remedies (line 1339)"; this entry replaces it. The text is `Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`. The spine already has a terebinth row (CPU-00016, *Pistacia terebinthus*) and a pine-resin row (CPU-00029); this entry does not decide between them or fold into either, because no text read shows which tree the Egyptian word means.

**Egyptian, Ebers Papyrus (EP).**

*The list (Bryan, Ch. VI, "Plant Remedies").* In Bryan's alphabetical list of the plants whose identity he takes to be recognisable, the lines read "Turpentine" and "Turpentine-nuts" (line 1339 and line 1340), between "Sycamore-tree" with its forms and "Watermelon". The extract of these lists (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/README.md`) records that they give only Bryan's English: "no Egyptian word and no binomial".

*The prescriptions found.* (1) Under "Diseases of the Skin" (page 93 in Bryan's printing), the "REMEDY TO FREE THE FLESH FROM DISEASE" is listed (line 2701 to 2706) as Ass's Milk, Resin-of-Acanthus, Indigo, duat-plant, Turpentine-nuts and Honey, with the instruction "Cook, strain, and take for four days." (2) In Bryan's commentary on the *uxedu*-disease (line 3278): "A mixture of Onions, Turpentine, and Cow's Milk, drunk for four days, knocked the spots off. It would probably have the same effect if it were rubbed on." The commentary is Bryan's and the mixture is described by him in his own words. The two prescriptions are both for skin conditions, both drunk for four days, and both were found by searching the whole translation for "Turpentine" and "Turpentine-nuts", which gave only lines 1339, 1340, 2704 and 3278.

**What the sources do not give.** The Egyptian word, the tree, the part used (beyond "nuts" in one remedy), and a quantity. A rendering "turpentine-nuts" suggests a fruit, and the terebinth bears small nut-like fruits; that would fit the terebinth, but it is an inference from the English and is not stated by Bryan in the lines read. The word "turpentine" alone could be resin from a conifer. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, turpentine as a modern term is the volatile fraction of conifer resin (rich in monoterpenes such as pinene), while terebinth resin is a different oleoresin from a *Pistacia*; which of the two the Egyptian text means is unknown. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Toxic if misused.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Skin disease: freeing the flesh from disease, and removing spots (*uxedu*), in remedies drunk for four days.
- **What the text does not say.** A dose, the preparation of the turpentine, or the Egyptian name.

The Codex gives no protocol. Turpentine as sold today is a solvent and is poisonous if drunk. The Ebers remedies are not a reason to take it.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the lines read is "Cook, strain, and take for four days" (the first remedy, with honey) and "drunk for four days" (Bryan's description of the second, with onions and cow's milk). No quantity is printed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Turpentine (the conifer solvent) is toxic if swallowed and irritant to skin and lungs; terebinth resin has been used as a flavouring and a traditional remedy. The identity of the Ebers material is not established, so neither fact is tied to it. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC.** Modern turpentine is poisonous if swallowed or inhaled and irritates the skin; do not drink it. The Ebers remedies are historical.
- **Identity.** The substance is a modern hypothesis from Bryan's English. Do not gather or use on this entry. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved; avoid. [USER: verify]
