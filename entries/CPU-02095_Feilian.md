# Feilian — 飞廉 *fei lian*, "Flying Lightness" (also 飞轻 *fei qing*)

## *Carduus crispus* L., welted thistle; the identification is the modern standard one for the drug name 飞廉 and is of medium confidence, because the early commentators themselves disagreed on what the name meant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** A search of the other traditions' texts in `Sources/` for *Carduus crispus* found nothing. (The blessed thistle of Dioscorides and Avicenna, *Carduus benedictus*, has its own entry, I_789, and is a different plant.) The entry rests on one tradition, in Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction and Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 translation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from Yang under the name *Flos Et Radix Carduntis Crispi*; this entry replaces it.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), the Shennong text proper.** The entry sits in the upper classic (上经). Sun's 味 paragraph reads: "味苦，平。主骨节热，胫重酸疼。久服，令人身轻。一名飞轻" ("Bitter, balanced. It governs heat in the bone joints and heaviness, soreness and pain of the shins. Taken long, it makes a person's body light. Another name: Flying-Light [飞轻]."). A parenthesis follows the alias in Sun's file: "已以上四字，原本黑字" (literally "the four characters up to here are, in the original, black characters"). Its meaning is not explained in the lines read and the Codex does not interpret it. The paragraph ends "生川泽" ("it grows in rivers and marshes", line 875). (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 飞廉 *feilian*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 873-875)

**Second witness, Yang 1998 (attributed to Yang).** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, lines 3459-3462) gives "bitter and balanced… heat in the bone joints and heaviness and aching pain in the lower legs. Protracted taking makes the body light. Its other name is Fei Qing (Lightly Flying). It grows in rivers and swamps." This agrees with Sun and is a translation of the same text. In Yang the entry stands among the lower-class herbs of the file, at "PDF p.93", which differs from Sun's placement in the upper classic; the two reconstructions disagree on the grade. The Codex follows Sun's placement because the job and Sun's volume heading (卷一 上经, line 883) place it there, and notes the difference. [USER: verify]

**Mingyi bielu layer (later additions, quoted by Sun).** Line 877 adds the aliases 伏兔 ("hidden hare"), 飞雉 ("flying pheasant") and 木禾 ("wood grain"), the habitat Henei, the root gathered in the first month and the flower in the seventh and eighth months, then "阴干" (dried in the shade, line 879). The Shennong text proper names no part used; the Mingyi layer names root and flower.

**Sun's own note (案).** Line 881 reads: "《广雅》云：伏猪，木禾也。飞廉，漏芦也。陶弘景云：今既别有漏芦，则非。此别名耳。" ("The *Guangya* says: *fuzhu* [伏猪] is *muhe*. *Feilian* is *loulu* [漏芦]. Tao Hongjing says: since there is now a separate *loulu*, this is not so; these are only other names.") That is a real dispute in the early record: the *Guangya* equates 飞廉 with 漏芦, which has its own entry (line 801) in the upper classic; Tao Hongjing rejects the equation because the drug 漏芦 was by then a distinct item. Sun's own entry for 漏芦 (line 807) repeats the *Guangya* line. The Codex does not resolve it.

**Identity.** Yang's note 213 (Yang 1998, line 3482) says the drug "is rarely if ever used, and its identity is problematic", that another likely plant is *Cirsium ovalifolium* (Yang's spelling "Crisium Ovalifolium"), and that "descriptions of its actions differ from scholar to scholar depending upon what actual species is used". The text read describes no leaf, flower or habit, so it cannot separate the candidates; *Carduus crispus* is the modern standard assignment. Confidence: medium. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. As a general fact, *Carduus* is a genus of thistles in the daisy family (Asteraceae), plants with spiny leaves and composite flower heads. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry, and nothing about its chemistry is asserted. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Shennong text lists a bitter, balanced drug for heat in the joints and heavy, sore shins, with a long-use promise (below). These are the text's own terms; the Codex does not turn "heat in the bone joints" into a modern diagnosis and gives no protocol. Joint pain and leg weakness need medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text proper gives no preparation. The Mingyi layer says the root is gathered in the first month, the flower in the seventh and eighth, and the drug is dried in the shade (lines 877-879). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

*Carduus crispus* is used in current Chinese pharmacy under the name *fei lian*, which is a general fact and not a finding. Because the early identity is disputed (above), a study of any one thistle would not bear directly on the Shennong drug. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ.** The Shennong text prints "味苦，平" (bitter, balanced). The Wu Pu layer has no entry here and the Mingyi layer adds no flavour or temperature. The text proper prints no toxicity word, and the entry's class (upper) is described as non-toxic in the preface (line 43).

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* In Sun's reconstruction the entry stands in the upper classic (上经), whose preface says upper drugs "nourish life to correspond with heaven, are non-toxic, and many or long doses do not harm people" (line 43). The entry's own clause, 久服，令人身轻 ("taken long, it makes a person's body light"), is a long-use promise of the *qing shen* 轻身 kind; *qing shen* is "lighten the body" and has no pharmacological equivalent, and it is not weight loss. Unlike many upper-grade entries this one has no promise of "not aging" or "extending the years". The claim is thin but it is the Shennong text's own, so the grade is (a). The flower and root of the Mingyi layer, and the alias "Flying-Light", are not additional status claims. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 飞廉 *feilian*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 873)

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator has a single clause to go on: an upper-grade drug said to lighten the body when taken long. The identity of the plant was in doubt even to the early commentators, no dose is given, and Yang's own commentary says it is rarely used. Without a verified plant and a dose, the honest practice is to read the entry and leave the thistle alone.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. Thistles are generally not poisonous, but that is not stated by the sources and the plant behind the name is uncertain, so nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of this entry. Joint heat and leg pain need medical assessment. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
