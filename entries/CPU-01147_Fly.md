# Fly — "Fly", "Fly-dirt-which-is-on-the-Wall" (Bryan 1930, Ebers Papyrus; no Egyptian word printed in the list)
## *The fly, and its droppings ("fly-dirt"), as Bryan's English renders the Ebers material; his list gives no Egyptian word and no binomial for the fly* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The only text on disk for this tradition is C. P. Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). In his chapter VII, "Organic Remedies", Bryan lists the animals "whose identity is not in doubt, and who were called upon to sacrifice themselves in the cause of the Healing Art" (lines 1385-1392). The list ends "... Locust Tarantula Beetle Bat Wasp Fly Tapeworm" (line 1404; row 131 of `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, "Bryan 1930, Ch. VII Organic Remedies (animals) (line 1404)", which notes "no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). A second list, `_sources/ebers.csv` line 116, has "Fly" with the source "Ebers Papyrus; Ghalioungui 1987 trans., Glossary I (Medicaments)" and the identification-uncertain flag set. Ghalioungui's translation is not among the texts on disk and I have not read it. What the Ebers papyrus has in Egyptian for "fly", and which insect it names, is not stated in anything I read; identification is a modern hypothesis in this corpus, since the papyrus is organised by complaint, not by substance.

The material that Bryan prints is one recipe, which he gives twice. In his chapter XXIV, "Domestic Hints", the first remedy "to stop the crying of a child" is (lines 4160-4162):

"REMEDY TO STOP THE CRYING OF A CHILD / Pods-of-the-Poppy-plant (Opium) Fly-dirt-which-is-on-the-Wall / Make into one, strain, and take for four days. It acts at once!"

The words "(Opium)" are Bryan's own gloss on "Pods-of-the-Poppy-plant". Bryan comments that "the means employed to quell the squalling infant five thousand years ago are identically the same as many a modern mother employs to-day", and sets beside it a "new style" version, "Powder-which-contains-Opium" with "Fly-dirt-which-is-on-the-Dummy" (lines 4164-4165), which is Bryan's own modern comparison and not an Egyptian text. The same recipe is mentioned earlier, in his survey of strange ingredients, as "a mixture of Opium and Fly-dirt-which-is-on-the-Wall, to soothe a crying child" (lines 1378-1380). The ingredient is thus the fly's droppings, scraped from a wall, in a mixture with poppy pods; it is not the fly's body.

I searched Bryan's text for "fly", "flies" and "fly-dirt". The only remedies that name them are those above. Other lines with the word "fly" are verbs (for example, line 1119, "the Evil will fly", and a spell at line 4249 in which a hawk is told to "Fly hither"). Bryan's chapter on domestic remedies also contains charms and remedies against fleas, lice, mice, wasps, scorpions and serpents (lines 4172-4245), which do not use the fly. Bryan's text is a summary with selected quotations, not a full translation, so the absence of other fly recipes in it proves little about the papyrus. [USER: verify]

The only text accompanying the recipe is Bryan's comment above. No spell is reported with it, no deity is named, and no dose beyond "take for four days" is given.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, opium poppy pods contain the alkaloids morphine and codeine and others, which are sedative and can stop breathing, especially in infants. "Fly-dirt" is the excrement and regurgitated matter of flies; it consists of partly digested food from whatever the flies fed on and carries bacteria picked up on that food and on the fly's body. The recipe's effect, if it had one, would be the poppy's.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice, and a dangerous one. The recipe gives opium by mouth to a crying infant, with a dirty ingredient. The Codex does not recommend any part of it. Opium products can suppress an infant's breathing and have killed babies; a crying infant needs examination for the cause. Fly droppings from a wall carry bacteria. The honest use of this entry is as a record that the fly appears in the Ebers material as a source of an ingredient used in one remedy.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation is the one quoted: the two ingredients "Make into one, strain, and take for four days." Bryan's text gives no quantity of either ingredient and no amount per day. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Flies are mechanical carriers of disease-causing bacteria and other agents on their bodies and droppings. Opium-derived drugs depress breathing and are dangerous in infants; colic or crying in an infant has many causes and is evaluated, not sedated. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Egyptian (EP):** None. Bryan's text gives no quality (hot, cold, moist, dry) for the fly or fly-dirt. The Ebers corpus is organised by complaint and records what was done for a condition, not a classification of the substance.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. The one recipe is for a crying child. The Egyptian practice of treating spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*) is relevant in general, and Bryan prints "words to be spoken" elsewhere in the papyrus; none is reported with this recipe, and since Bryan summarises, that is no proof that the papyrus gives none. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The fly is the source of one ingredient in one infant-soothing recipe, and the recipe is dangerous. The Codex does not recommend it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Infants and children:** opium and its derivatives must never be given to a crying infant; they can stop breathing. Seek medical care for a baby who cries persistently.
- **Hygiene:** fly droppings carry bacteria; do not take or apply them.
- **Legal:** opium and its products are controlled substances in most jurisdictions.
- **Ethics:** the Codex does not recommend harming any animal for these uses.
