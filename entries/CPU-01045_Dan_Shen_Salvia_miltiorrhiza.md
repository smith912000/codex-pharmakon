### Origin & Tradition

**CM (Classical Chinese Medicine — Shennong Bencao Jing through Song Dynasty consolidation):** Dān Shēn (丹参) is recorded as an upper-class (*shàng pǐn*, 上品) herb in the *Shennong Bencao Jing* (神農本草經, compiled approximately 1st–3rd century CE, attributed to the mythological agriculturalist-emperor Shennong), the founding document of the entire Chinese pharmacopoeia. Upper-class designation in the Shennong classification signified herbs suitable for long-term tonic use without toxicity — the highest grade in the three-tier classical system (*shàng*, *zhōng*, *xià*). The *Shennong Bencao Jing* records Dān Shēn's actions as: "governs pain in the heart and abdomen, accumulation of masses, breaks up blood stagnation, promotes new blood production, calms the spirit and stops restlessness." These classical indications — cardiovascular pain, internal masses from blood stasis, and neurological agitation — anticipate with remarkable precision the modern pharmacological applications of its diterpenoid and polyphenolic fractions.

The herb's name, *Dān Shēn* (丹参), carries significant classical symbolism: *Dān* (丹) means "cinnabar" or "vermilion," referring to the vivid red color of the dried root cortex and, more profoundly, to the cosmological association between cinnabar (mercury sulfide — the alchemical prima materia) and the Heart organ in Daoist medical philosophy. In Chinese five-phase theory (五行, *wǔ xíng*), red corresponds to Fire (*huǒ*, 火), and Fire governs the Heart — the root's crimson color thus classified it immediately within the Heart organ system even before clinical documentation. The *Shen* (参) character links it to the great tonics (ginseng, *rén shēn*; codonopsis, *dǎng shēn*; adenophora, *nán shā shēn*), denoting a substance of substantial medicinal power. The herb enters the Heart (心, *Xīn*) and Liver (肝, *Gān*) channels, possessing bitter taste (*kǔ*, 苦) and a slightly cold thermal nature (*wēi hán*, 微寒) — a combination that in TCM theory produces blood-cooling, blood-moving, and fire-clearing actions simultaneously, explaining its use across both hot blood stasis conditions and Heart-fire agitation patterns.

**SBJ (Bencao Gangmu, Li Shizhen, 1596 CE):** Li Shizhen's treatment of Dān Shēn in the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目, "Compendium of Materia Medica") represents one of the most consequential assessments in the history of Chinese pharmacology. After documenting the herb's classical indications, he appended his own clinical commentary — one of the most famous assertions in the entire SBJ corpus: **"一味丹参，功同四物" (*yī wèi dān shēn, gōng tóng sì wù*) — "One single herb, Dān Shēn, has the function equal to Sì Wù Tāng."** Sì Wù Tāng (四物湯, Four Substance Decoction) is the foundational blood-nourishing formula in all of classical Chinese medicine, combining Rehmannia (*Shú Dì Huáng* — nourishes Yin and Blood), Angelica sinensis (*Dāng Guī* — nourishes and invigorates Blood), Paeonia lactiflora (*Bái Sháo* — nourishes Blood and astringes Yin), and Ligusticum chuanxiong (*Chuān Xiōng* — moves Blood and Qi). Li Shizhen's assertion that a single herb could subsume the combined clinical action of these four exemplary blood medicines was an extraordinary claim — and it testifies to the centuries of clinical observation behind the statement. Modern pharmacology vindicates the claim: Dān Shēn simultaneously nourishes blood (antioxidant protection of erythrocytes via salvianolic acids), moves blood (antiplatelet danshensu), warms channels (tanshinone-mediated improved circulation), and calms the spirit (miltirone's GABA-A modulation).

Li Shizhen further documented Dān Shēn's ability to "manage 23 Heart disorders" — an enumeration spanning what would today be classified as angina, palpitation, arrhythmia, heart failure-associated edema, and anxiety — a breadth of cardiovascular indications that remained clinically unverifiable until the pharmacological era. He documented compound preparations including Dān Shēn combined with Sān Qī (*Panax notoginseng*) and aromatic agents (borneol), forming the predecessor to the modern compound Danshen dripping pills. He noted its properties for "activating blood and removing stasis, cooling blood and reducing carbuncles, nourishing blood and calming the spirit, calming fetal restlessness" — the full spectrum of TCM Blood-level therapeutic actions attributed to a single root.

**FMT (Tibetan Medicine / Sowa Rigpa):** While Dān Shēn does not occupy the same canonical prominence in Tibetan medicine that it commands in the Chinese tradition, its pharmacologically relevant equivalent compounds appear in numerous Tibetan compound preparations aimed at *khrag* (blood) disorders and *snying nad* (heart disease). The Tibetan medical tradition, particularly in the *Gyushi* (Four Medical Tantras, rGyud bzhi, 12th century CE) and its extensive commentarial literature, classifies blood-moving and heart-stabilizing medicines within the broader category of *gNyer-pa* (medicines for heat diseases of the solid organs). Tibetan physicians working in the Amdo and Kham regions where *Salvia miltiorrhiza* grows at altitude incorporated it into local compound preparations. The *Sangye Gyatso* commentary (*Vaidurya sNgon-po*, 1688 CE) documents red-rooted sage-type plants within the broader Tibetan herb-classification framework as cooling, blood-purifying medicines with cardiovascular applications. The FMT influence on this entry is minor compared to the CM and SBJ traditions but acknowledges the herb's established place in the greater Himalayan pharmacological continuum.

### Active Components

- **Tanshinone IIA** — Abietane diterpene quinone; the most extensively studied lipophilic bioactive in *Salvia miltiorrhiza*. Mechanisms: (1) Mitochondrial protection — preserves mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) and prevents pathological opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) during ischemia-reperfusion conditions, reducing cytochrome c release and downstream caspase activation; (2) NF-κB inhibition — blocks IκB kinase (IKK) phosphorylation, preventing IκB degradation and NF-κB nuclear translocation, reducing TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and COX-2 transcription; (3) Vascular smooth muscle anti-proliferation — inhibits PDGF-stimulated smooth muscle cell proliferation relevant to atherosclerotic plaque stabilization; (4) Apoptosis induction in tumor cells via Bax/Bcl-2 ratio alteration. Hepatic CYP1A2 and CYP3A4 inhibitor — clinically significant drug interaction potential. Fat-soluble; requires dietary fat for optimal absorption.

- **Tanshinone IIB and Tanshinone I** — Structural congeners of Tanshinone IIA; overlapping cardiovascular and anti-inflammatory activities. Tanshinone IIB shows particular cerebrovascular protection via endothelial eNOS upregulation; present in meaningful concentrations in whole root preparations that may be absent or reduced in standardized extracts optimized only for IIA.

- **Cryptotanshinone** — Abietane diterpene quinone; direct inhibitor of STAT3 (Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3), a transcription factor driving both chronic inflammatory gene expression and oncogenic pathways. STAT3 hyperactivation is associated with sarcopenic inflammation and anabolic resistance in chronically overtrained athletes. Cryptotanshinone additionally demonstrates androgen receptor modulation and anti-adipogenic activity (inhibiting PPARγ-driven adipocyte differentiation). Volatile; partially lost during high-temperature decoction — best preserved in standardized extracts or low-temperature ethanolic preparations.

- **Salvianolic Acid B (Sal B, Lithospermic Acid B)** — Water-soluble polyphenol; the most pharmacologically potent antioxidant in this class on a per-mole basis. Scavenges superoxide anion (O₂⁻), hydroxyl radical (•OH), and peroxynitrite (ONOO⁻) with rate constants exceeding those of resveratrol by approximately 5-fold in comparative free radical scavenging assays. Primary anti-fibrotic mechanism: inhibits TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling, the master pathway driving hepatic stellate cell (HSC) activation, proliferation, and collagen deposition (type I and III collagen) in hepatic fibrosis; reduces α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) expression in activated HSCs. Protects the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) independently of Tanshinone IIA. Oral bioavailability is low (~5%) due to first-pass glucuronidation, but bioactive sulfated and glucuronidated metabolites retain antioxidant activity; Sal B is substantially more bioavailable via intravenous routes (used in Chinese clinical practice).

- **Salvianolic Acid A** — Water-soluble polyphenol; potent inhibitor of LDL oxidation (IC₅₀ ~2 μM), directly relevant to anti-atherosclerotic protection; neuroprotective via reduction of β-amyloid oligomer aggregation and ROS-mediated neuronal injury; synergizes with Sal B in hepatoprotective applications.

- **Danshensu (3,4-Dihydroxyphenyllactic Acid, DSS)** — The primary water-soluble cardiovascular active and the compound most directly responsible for Dān Shēn's classical blood-activating reputation. Dual mechanism: (1) Inhibits TXA₂ synthase in platelets — reduces thromboxane A₂ production, thereby reducing ADP-stimulated platelet aggregation; (2) Upregulates prostacyclin (PGI₂) synthesis in vascular endothelium via COX-1 pathway — PGI₂ is the endogenous vasodilator and platelet aggregation inhibitor; the net effect of DSS is to shift the TXA₂/PGI₂ ratio decisively toward vasodilation, anti-aggregation, and improved endothelial function. DSS additionally upregulates endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) expression, increasing bioavailable NO. Renally cleared with relatively short half-life (~2 hours); most relevant in decoction and aqueous preparations; reduced in tanshinone-standardized lipophilic extracts.

- **Protocatechuic Aldehyde (Protocatechualdehyde, PAD)** — Phenolic aldehyde; anti-fibrotic via inhibition of HSC proliferation and reduction of hepatic collagen deposition; anti-atherogenic; contributes to the hepatoprotective spectrum of the water-soluble fraction alongside Sal B.

- **Rosmarinic Acid** — Caffeic acid ester; antioxidant and anti-inflammatory shared with *Salvia rosmarinus* (rosemary) and other Lamiaceae; inhibits COX-2 and 5-LOX; rapidly absorbed (plasma Tmax ~30 min); contributes to early-onset anti-inflammatory effect following administration; bioavailable intact across the intestinal epithelium.

- **Miltirone** — Minor abietane diterpene; documented GABA-A receptor positive allosteric modulator (benzodiazepine site binding); mechanistic substrate for the classical TCM indication of Dān Shēn to "calm the spirit and eliminate vexation" (*ān shén chú fán*, 安神除煩); contributes anxiolytic and mild sedative dimension most relevant to [CYCLE-OFF] and [RESET] recovery phases.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. `[BUILD]` **Cardiovascular protection during high-volume anabolic loading** — High-intensity resistance training combined with caloric surplus generates substantial myocardial oxidative and inflammatory stress: catecholamine surges increase myocardial oxygen demand, training-induced hypoxia-reperfusion dynamics create ROS bursts, and systemic inflammatory mediators (TNF-α, IL-6) exert myocardial stress. Tanshinone IIA's mPTP protection preserves cardiac mitochondrial integrity during these episodic stress events, while DSS's TXA₂ inhibition and PGI₂ upregulation optimize coronary microcirculation. Dose: 500–900 mg standardized extract (≥3% tanshinones, ≥5% salvianolic acids) taken 60 minutes before training sessions; co-administer with a small fat-containing meal to maximize tanshinone absorption. The NMPA-approved compound Danshen Dripping Pills (復方丹參滴丸) represent the most clinically validated preparation for cardiovascular protection and may be used at the labeled dosage.

2. `[RESET]` **Post-training microcirculatory recovery and DOMS mitigation** — In the 24–72 hours following heavy resistance sessions, local muscle blood flow optimization is a rate-limiting factor in recovery; congested microcirculation from training-induced platelet hyperreactivity and endothelial inflammation impairs oxygen and nutrient delivery to recovering tissue. DSS's dual antiplatelet/vasodilatory mechanism and rosmarinic acid's COX-2/5-LOX anti-inflammatory activity together improve microvascular perfusion in recovering muscle. Tanshinone IIA NF-κB inhibition reduces systemic inflammatory mediators without impairing satellite cell activation (the mTOR/Akt anabolic cascade remains unaffected). Synergizes with nitric oxide precursors (citrulline 6g, beetroot concentrate) for additive vasodilatory effect. Dose: 600 mg standardized extract twice daily during RESET phases.

3. `[CYCLE-OFF]` **Hepatic fibrosis prevention and liver restoration after supplementation cycles** — Salvianolic Acid B's inhibition of TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling prevents the activation of hepatic stellate cells that drives hepatic fibrosis — the primary pathological process initiated by prolonged hepatotoxic exposure. Sal B reduces α-SMA expression (a marker of HSC activation), inhibits type I and III collagen synthesis, and promotes endogenous matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-mediated fibrous matrix remodeling. Simultaneously, protocatechuic aldehyde inhibits HSC proliferation. For CYCLE-OFF hepatic restoration protocols: 400–600 mg Sal B-standardized extract twice daily for a minimum 8-week course; compound with Milk Thistle (silymarin — TGF-β/Smad pathway synergy) for enhanced anti-fibrotic coverage. This application has the strongest evidence base in the entry and is the primary reason Dān Shēn belongs in the CYCLE-OFF protocol stack.

4. `[RESET]` **NF-κB-mediated systemic anti-inflammation without anabolic suppression** — Post-training NF-κB activation drives both beneficial (satellite cell signaling) and detrimental (inflammatory catabolism, delayed recovery) downstream effects. NSAID use to suppress this pathway carries well-documented inhibitory effects on muscle protein synthesis and satellite cell activity. Tanshinone IIA's NF-κB inhibition at the IKK level reduces the detrimental inflammatory cascade without interfering with mTORC1/Akt/p70S6K anabolic signaling — making it a mechanistically superior choice to NSAIDs for athletes requiring anti-inflammatory management during training blocks. Targets the TNF-α/IL-1β/IL-6 cytokine storm while preserving the anabolic environment.

5. `[BUILD]` **Blood viscosity and hypercoagulability management at altitude and in heat** — Altitude exposure (>2,000 m) induces compensatory polycythemia (elevated hematocrit), reducing blood viscosity tolerance and creating a pro-thrombotic hemodynamic state. Dehydration and high ambient temperature training amplifies platelet hyperreactivity. DSS and the salvianolic acid fraction collectively reduce plasma fibrinogen cross-linking, inhibit ADP-induced platelet aggregation, and shift the TXA₂/PGI₂ balance toward vascular dilation — directly addressing altitude- and heat-induced hypercoagulability that is a meaningful cardiovascular risk factor in elite altitude-training athletes. Dose: 500 mg standardized extract twice daily during altitude training camps.

6. `[CYCLE-OFF]` **Mitochondrial biogenesis maintenance during detraining** — The detraining-induced decline in mitochondrial density (mitochondrial biogenesis requires sustained training stimulus via PGC-1α upregulation) is a major contributor to the physiological deconditioning that occurs during planned off-cycle periods. Tanshinone IIA and Sal B together maintain PGC-1α expression and upstream AMPK activation even in the absence of training stimulus — preserving a portion of the mitochondrial quality and density acquired during the preceding training block. This allows faster re-training re-adaptation when the BUILD phase resumes.

### Preparation & Dosing

**Standardized dry extract (≥3–5% tanshinones, ≥5% salvianolic acids):** 500–1,000 mg/day in two divided doses. Clinical gold standard for ensuring consistent delivery of both lipophilic (tanshinones) and water-soluble (danshensu, salvianolic acids, rosmarinic acid) bioactive fractions. Take with food containing fat to maximize tanshinone absorption (lipophilic compounds require micellar solubilization for intestinal uptake). The dual-fraction standardization is critical — single-fraction extracts (tanshinone-only or water-extract-only) fail to replicate the full pharmacological profile of the whole root.

**Traditional aqueous decoction (whole dried root):** 9–30 g dried root in 500 mL water, decocted 30–45 minutes, strained, consumed in two portions across the day. This preparation delivers primarily water-soluble fractions (DSS, salvianolic acids, rosmarinic acid); lipophilic tanshinones are poorly soluble in water and are only partially extracted by aqueous decoction. The classical standard preparation for blood stasis in Chinese clinical medicine. Suitable for cardiovascular and hepatoprotective applications where the water-soluble fraction is sufficient.

**Ethanolic tincture (1:4, 50–60% ethanol):** 4–6 mL three times daily. More complete dual-fraction extraction than aqueous decoction alone; the 50–60% ethanol solvent extracts both polar salvianolic acids and less-polar tanshinones into a single preparation. Allow 4–6 weeks for cardiovascular consolidation of effects.

**Compound Danshen Dripping Pills (復方丹參滴丸 — Fùfāng Dānshen Dīwán):** The NMPA (formerly SFDA)-approved compound preparation containing Dān Shēn, Sān Qī (*Panax notoginseng*), and borneol (as penetration enhancer and aromatic opener of the Heart orifices). A Phase III clinical trial was completed and submitted as an NDA to the US FDA in 2018 for stable angina, representing the most rigorously validated herbal cardiovascular preparation to have undergone full Phase III regulatory evaluation in a Western regulatory context. Standard labeled dose: 10 pills (270 mg total), 3× daily. Sublingual administration of the dripping pills enhances bioavailability by bypassing hepatic first-pass metabolism.

**Topical application (bruising and local blood stasis):** Dān Shēn tincture (60% ethanol extract) applied 2–3× daily to ecchymotic tissue; transdermal delivery of salvianolic acids accelerates resolution of local blood stasis. Traditional application for blunt trauma and post-exercise bruising.

**Duration of use:** CYCLE-OFF hepatoprotective course: minimum 8 weeks; cardiovascular protective BUILD/RESET: continuous throughout phase; detraining mitochondrial preservation: entire CYCLE-OFF duration.

### Scientific Correlation

Ji XY, Tan BKH, Zhu YC (2000) "Salvia miltiorrhiza and ischemic diseases" *Acta Pharmacologica Sinica* 21(12):1089–1094 — Mechanistic investigation of danshensu cardiovascular activity; demonstrated that danshensu at physiologically relevant concentrations (10–100 μM) inhibits ADP-induced platelet aggregation by 42–67% via TXA₂ synthase inhibition in isolated platelet preparations, and concurrently increases prostacyclin (PGI₂) synthesis in cultured human umbilical vein endothelial cells by 2.3-fold compared to control; established the TXA₂/PGI₂ ratio correction as the primary antiplatelet mechanism of the water-soluble Dān Shēn fraction, biochemically distinct from aspirin's irreversible COX-1 acetylation and identifying danshensu as the pharmacological basis for the classical "blood-activating" clinical action.

Gao S, Liu Z, Li H, Little PJ, Liu P, Xu S (2012) "Cardiovascular actions and therapeutic potential of tanshinone IIA" *Atherosclerosis* 220(1):3–10 — Comprehensive review synthesizing in vitro, in vivo, and clinical evidence for tanshinone IIA cardiovascular activity; documents mitochondrial protection mechanism (mPTP inhibition, ΔΨm preservation, cytochrome c release prevention) in ischemia-reperfusion models; quantifies NF-κB inhibition at IC₅₀ ~8 μM in macrophage preparations; documents vascular smooth muscle anti-proliferative activity via PDGF receptor signaling modulation; reviews available clinical pharmacokinetic data showing peak plasma concentrations of 5–12 μM achievable with oral standardized extract at 500 mg doses — within the mechanistically active range for cardiovascular protection.

Zhou L, Zuo Z, Chow MSS (2005) "Danshen: an overview of its chemistry, pharmacology, pharmacokinetics, and clinical use" *Journal of Clinical Pharmacology* 45(12):1345–1359 — Definitive pharmacokinetic characterization establishing the dual lipophilic/hydrophilic framework; quantifies distinct pharmacokinetic profiles: tanshinones (high volume of distribution due to lipophilicity, Vd ~10–20 L/kg, hepatic CYP1A2 and CYP3A4 metabolism with t½ ~2–4 hours) versus water-soluble phenolics (lower Vd, rapid renal clearance, t½ ~1–2 hours for danshensu); documents CYP1A2/3A4 inhibition by tanshinones with clinical drug interaction potential; documents P-glycoprotein-mediated competition with digoxin requiring dose adjustment; establishes the pharmacokinetic rationale for twice-daily standardized extract dosing.

Lam FF, Ng ES, Lau CB, et al. (2007) *Journal of Cardiovascular Pharmacology* 50:434–441 — vasorelaxant mechanisms in isolated rat aortic rings: endothelium-dependent relaxation (abolished by L-NAME, confirming eNOS/NO mediation) plus endothelium-independent calcium-channel-blocking relaxation; the aqueous fraction (danshensu, salvianolic acids) accounted for >80% of the vasodilatory response.

Wang BQ (2010) "Salvia miltiorrhiza: Chemical and pharmacological review of a medicinal plant" *Journal of Medicinal Plants Research* 4(25):2813–2820 — Systematic pharmacological review covering salvianolic acid B's TGF-β/Smad3 anti-fibrotic mechanism in hepatic stellate cells; documents Sal B reduction of α-SMA expression by 65–78% in TGF-β1-stimulated human LX-2 stellate cells at 50 μM; quantifies collagen type I mRNA suppression of 71% vs control; reviews hepatoprotective in vivo data showing 40–60% reduction in serum ALT/AST in CCl₄-induced hepatic fibrosis rat models treated with Sal B at 20 mg/kg; establishes the molecular basis for the CYCLE-OFF hepatoprotective application.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Primary Tier:** ◉ Qi — Dān Shēn operates fundamentally at the Qi level within the Blood: in TCM theory, Qi and Blood are inseparable (氣為血帥, *qì wèi xuè shuài* — "Qi is the commander of Blood"; 血為氣母, *xuè wèi qì mǔ* — "Blood is the mother of Qi"), and Dān Shēn's activation of blood circulation is understood as the expression of its Qi-moving action through the medium of the Blood vessels. All cardiovascular, anti-platelet, and vasodilatory actions correspond to Qi-level pharmacology.

**Secondary Tier:** ◈ Jing — Salvianolic Acid B's protection of mitochondrial membrane integrity and inhibition of fibrotic structural remodeling in the liver aligns with the Jing tier's governance of constitutional tissue substance and structural integrity; the capacity to prevent fibrosis (pathological replacement of functional tissue with inert collagen) corresponds to Jing-preservation — maintaining the structural Essence against degenerative transformation.

**TCM Channel:** Heart (心, *Xīn*) primary · Liver (肝, *Gān*) secondary

**Energetic Action:**
- 活血化瘀 (*Huó xuè huà yū*) — Activates Blood, transforms Blood stasis: primary action; mechanistic basis: DSS antiplatelet, salvianolic acid vasodilatory, tanshinone IIA microcirculatory improvement
- 涼血消癰 (*Liáng xuè xiāo yōng*) — Cools Blood, reduces carbuncles: the anti-inflammatory, heat-clearing dimension; mechanistic basis: salvianolic acids' antioxidant quenching of ROS-driven inflammation, tanshinone IIA NF-κB inhibition
- 清心除煩 (*Qīng xīn chú fán*) — Clears Heart fire, eliminates vexation: addresses palpitations and anxiety arising from Heart blood stasis with heat; mechanistic basis: miltirone GABA-A modulation, cardiac anti-inflammatory
- 安神 (*Ān shén*) — Calms the spirit: settles insomnia and agitation arising from Blood heat; mechanistic basis: miltirone's mild sedative action, secondary cardiac stabilization reducing reflex anxiety

**Ayurvedic (functional correspondence):** Not formally in the classical Ayurvedic pharmacopoeia; functional correspondence — *Rasa*: tikta (bitter), kashaya (astringent); *Virya*: sheeta (cooling); *Vipaka*: katu (pungent); *Dosha*: reduces Pitta and Kapha, mild Vata increase in excess; *Karma*: raktashodhana (blood purifying), hridya (cardiac tonic), jwaraghna (anti-pyretic/anti-inflammatory), yakrtuttejaka (liver stimulating).

**Tibetan (FMT):** *Khrag nad* (blood disease) therapeutic agent; cooling class; *snying nad* (heart disease) adjunct; subsumed under the *gNyer-po* category of solid organ heat-clearing medicines in the *Gyushi* system.

### Contraindications

- **MAJOR — Anticoagulants and antiplatelets:** DSS's TXA₂/PGI₂ mechanism combined with salvianolic acid antiplatelet activity produces additive anticoagulant effect with warfarin, heparin, rivaroxaban, apixaban, clopidogrel, and aspirin. Clinical case reports document INR elevation to 3× therapeutic range in patients initiating Dān Shēn on stable warfarin. Absolute contraindication without specialist supervision and serial INR monitoring.

- **CYP1A2 and CYP3A4 inhibition:** Tanshinone IIA and cryptotanshinone are established CYP1A2 inhibitors (relevant to theophylline, caffeine at pharmacological doses, clozapine, tacrine) and CYP3A4 inhibitors (relevant to statins, benzodiazepines, cyclosporine, tacrolimus, many cardiovascular drugs). Can elevate co-administered drug plasma concentrations to toxic levels. Full medication review required before initiating tanshinone-standardized extracts.

- **Digoxin (P-glycoprotein competition):** *Salvia miltiorrhiza* components compete with digoxin for P-glycoprotein efflux transport, elevating digoxin plasma concentrations. Digoxin's narrow therapeutic index makes even modest elevation dangerous (toxicity: bradycardia, AV block, nausea, visual disturbances). Contraindicated with digoxin.

- **Hypotension risk with antihypertensive medications:** DSS-mediated eNOS upregulation and PGI₂-driven vasodilation potentiate ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and calcium channel blockers, potentially causing symptomatic hypotension. Monitor blood pressure upon initiating in patients on antihypertensive therapy.

- **Pre-operative period:** Discontinue a minimum of 2 weeks before elective surgery due to antiplatelet and anticoagulant mechanisms. Inform surgical and anaesthetic teams.

- **Pregnancy:** Strong blood-moving herb; danshensu and tanshinones cross the placental barrier in animal models. Classical TCM contraindication in pregnancy is well-founded. Avoid.

- **Cold and Yang-deficient constitution:** Bitter and slightly cold nature; prolonged use in individuals with Spleen Qi deficiency, chronic fatigue, cold extremities, or Yang deficiency may aggravate digestive weakness and cold symptoms. Balance with warming Qi-tonics (Astragalus, dried ginger) or reserve use for RESET/CYCLE-OFF phases only.

- **Renal impairment:** Danshensu and salvianolic acids are primarily renally excreted; impaired renal clearance increases plasma exposure. Proportionally reduce dose with declining eGFR; avoid at eGFR <30 mL/min without specialist guidance.
