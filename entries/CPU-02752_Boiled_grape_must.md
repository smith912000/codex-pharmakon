# Boiled grape must — ميبختج (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *Mībukhtaj (Persian-derived name): grape juice that has been cooked, i.e. boiled-down must. The identification is the Canon's own ("the cooked juice of the grape") and the vine, Vitis vinifera, is the spine row's; confidence high* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *mībukhtaj* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 15464-15466), two sentences.

*What it is.* هو عصير العنب المطبوخ (15465): "it is the juice of the grape, cooked".

*Chest (أعضاء النفس).* يعين على النفث ويقع في شراب الخشخاش المعروف بدياقوذا لذلك (15465-15466): "it helps expectoration, and enters the syrup of the poppy known as *diyāqūdhā* for that reason". *Diyāqūdhā* is the Arabic form of the Greek *diakōdion*, the poppy syrup; the gloss is ours, and the Canon text on disk does not give the Greek. *Excretory organs.* نافع لوجع الكلى والمثانة (15466): "useful for pain of the kidneys and the bladder". No temperament, no source author, no preparation method and no dose are printed.

### Active Components

The passage names no constituent. In general terms boiled-down grape juice is a concentrated solution of sugars (glucose and fructose) with fruit acids. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Canon records cooked grape juice as an expectorant helper, an ingredient of the poppy syrup, and a remedy for kidney and bladder pain. The Codex gives no protocol. The poppy syrup it enters is an opium-bearing preparation and is not recommended here.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparation is the boiling of grape juice (15465). The text gives no boiling-down ratio and no dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Boiled grape must is a sugar-rich syrup used as a sweetener and food. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

The Canon places cooked grape juice on the chest and on the kidneys and bladder, and names no temperament. For the chest, under the heading for the organs of breath, it is said to help expectoration, and it enters the poppy syrup diyaqudha for that reason. For the excretory organs, it is said to be useful for pain of the kidneys and of the bladder. No degree of heat, cold, dryness or moisture is given, and no digestive, head or skin action is stated. The passage names no constituent. Because the poppy syrup it enters is an opium-bearing preparation, the Codex does not attribute that compound's action to the grape juice. The Codex records these statements as the Canon's claims and gives no protocol, no boiling ratio and no dose.

### Contraindications

- **Sugar load.** A concentrated sugar syrup; take care in diabetes. [USER: verify]
- **Poppy syrup.** The compound it enters contains opium; not recommended.
- **Kidney or bladder pain.** A medical condition; use proper care.
