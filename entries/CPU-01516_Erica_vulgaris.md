# Erica vulgaris — Ereika (Dioscorides 1-117)

## *Erica vulgaris, as the form "Erica vulgaris glabra [Bauhin]" on the SUGGESTED line; the same line gives "Calluna vulgaris [in Sprague]" and the translators' English name "Heath"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) printed under the chapter heading, not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED line reads "Erice [Fuchs], Erica vulgaris glabra [Bauhin], Calluna vulgaris [in Sprague] — Heath". The first two forms are pre-Linnaean names credited to Fuchs and Bauhin, and the row's "Erica vulgaris" shortens Bauhin's. The last is a modern genus, *Calluna*, credited to a later source. So the row's name and the translators' modern suggestion are not the same, and which of them the entry is "really" about is not settled here. The row has not been verified against POWO. **Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading *1-117. EREIKA* at line 4410, SUGGESTED line at 4411, text at line 4412. The chapter stands in Book One, "Aromatics", directly after 1-116 *MURIKE, MURRIS* (line 4395), the tamarisk chapter to which it refers. The translators' name index lists it at `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, line 119. The next chapter, 1-118 *AKAKALIS* (line 4413), is a different substance.

The whole chapter reads: "Erica is a shrubby tree like myrica [1-116] (but a great deal smaller) the flowers of which the bees use, but they make honey with it that is not good. The leaves and flowers applied as a poultice heal snakebites."

So the chapter has three statements. The plant is a shrubby tree like *myrica* but much smaller. Bees use its flowers but make honey of it that "is not good". Its leaves and flowers applied as a poultice "heal snakebites". No other name is printed, and there is no habitat, taste, season or preparation beyond the poultice.

*The chapter it refers to (1-116, lines 4395 to 4409).* *Myrica* is glossed by the translators as *Tamarix* ("Tamarisk"), with *Myrica gale* ("Sweet Gale") as another usage. Dioscorides says it is "a well-known tree, growing in marshy grounds and standing waters", bearing "a fruit as a flower, of a mossy consistency", with a gall-like fruit "unequally astringent to the taste" used for the mouth, eyes and spitting of blood, and that a decoction of the leaves in wine "melts the spleen". The comparison gives only the build of the plant, a shrubby tree. The entry does not carry the tamarisk's uses to *erica*.

### Active Components

The constituents of the plant Dioscorides describes were not read. The heath family (Ericaceae), to which *Calluna* and *Erica* belong, is known for woody, evergreen shrubs of acid ground. Nothing is stated here about the composition of this plant, or of the honey the text calls poor. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records one medical use, a poultice for snakebite, and one remark on honey. The Codex gives no protocol. Snakebite is an emergency and needs urgent medical care, and a poultice of any plant is no substitute.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives one preparation: "The leaves and flowers applied as a poultice." No quantity, frequency or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints no hot, cold, moist or dry quality and no taste. The only quality printed is of the honey the bees make from it: "not good".

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What the chapter gives toward the discipline of attention that the spec describes for this tradition is a note on provenance: the flowers bees use, and the honey they make of them, which Dioscorides calls not good. That is a statement about where a product comes from and how it is judged, which is the kind of attention the spec names, and it is not a cultivation status. The identification is the translators'. **Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The one transferable point is a modest one, that Dioscorides notes which flowers a honey comes from and judges it by that. The text offers nothing more.

### Contraindications

The chapter prints no warning. Snakebite is a medical emergency and a poultice must not delay care. The identification is only the translators' suggestion, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No safety information for pregnancy was read.
