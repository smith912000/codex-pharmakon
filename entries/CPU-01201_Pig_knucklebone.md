# Pig knucklebone — ASTRAGALOS UOS, "Knucklebone of Pig" (Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 2-62)
## *The anklebone (astragalus) of the domestic pig, burnt to white and powdered; a Dioscoridean animal remedy* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is the 2000 English edition of *De Materia Medica* in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is 2-62, headed in the file "ASTRAGALOS UOS Knucklebone of Pig" (line 5182; the heading is printed inline, mid-line, after the end of 2-61, as the names.csv note records). The full text of the chapter (line 5183) is: "The anklebone of a swine (burnt until it becomes white, then pounded to powder and taken in a drink) heals gas from colic, and griping that has endured for a long time."

The chapter sits in Book 2 among the animal materials. The chapter before it (2-61, line 5180) is elephant's tooth: "The scraping of elephant's tooth when applied cures whitlows of the finger or toenails. It is astringent." The chapter after it (2-63, line 5184) is the burnt horn of a hart, which is also burnt and drunk. The Greek heading *astragalos* is the word for the ankle bone; the translator's English is "Knucklebone of Pig", and the text itself says "anklebone of a swine". Which bone is meant is therefore given two ways in the file. The chapter gives one indication (colic gas and long-standing griping), one preparation (burn to white, pound to powder, drink), and no quantity. It gives no qualities such as warming or drying, no cautions, no season or source, and no alternative. Compare the lung chapter 2-40 in this batch, which is externally applied and not burnt.

### Active Components

Burnt bone is mostly mineral: calcined bone is chiefly calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite) with some calcium carbonate, since the heat removes the organic matrix, which is why the bone turns white. The text names no active compound. As a general fact, calcined bone ash is an alkaline, insoluble mineral powder.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides says the burnt, powdered anklebone, taken in a drink, "heals gas from colic, and griping that has endured for a long time". The Codex does not recommend it, and gives no protocol. Long-standing abdominal pain needs diagnosis; it can have causes that a powder will not touch and that delay would worsen. The line is kept as a record of what the text documents.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives one preparation: the anklebone is "burnt until it becomes white, then pounded to powder and taken in a drink". It names no drink, so the vehicle (wine, water or other) is not stated, and it gives no quantity. Compare the neighbouring hart's horn chapter (2-63), which does give "two spoonfuls"; I did not copy that to this chapter. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Greek: the chapter prints an action only ("heals gas from colic, and griping that has endured for a long time"), with no quality such as warming, cooling, drying or astringent. Dioscorides does print "astringent" for the preceding elephant's-tooth chapter (2-61), but not here, and I do not carry it over. No other tradition is tagged.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is supplied here. What the chapter offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention: a sensory endpoint for the preparation ("burnt until it becomes white"), and a plain statement of what is claimed and for what complaint. The chapter does not record an amulet use, a ritual, or a protective function for the bone; the neighbouring chapter 2-60 (swallow stones tied "to the arm or neck" for epilepsy, lines 5173-5179) shows that the text does record such uses where they exist, and none is recorded for the pig's anklebone.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. A cultivator does not take burnt pig bone. The text records it against colic, with a preparation but no dose, and the Codex keeps the record, including the one practical lesson in it, the observable endpoint (white), without carrying it into practice.

### Contraindications

Bone burnt in an open fire or an unlined pot can carry soot, residues and pathogens, and uncalcined or partly burnt bone is infectious material. Calcined animal bone powder from uncontrolled sources may contain lead or other toxic metals. Do not treat persistent abdominal pain with a powder; seek medical care, since the cause may be urgent. Pig products are prohibited in some religious traditions; respect them. This entry gives no medical advice.
