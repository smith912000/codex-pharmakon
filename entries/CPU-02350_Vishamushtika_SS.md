### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** Nothing in the sources read says this drug is poisonous, but the first element of its name (visha) ordinarily means poison, and the drug is unidentified. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should prepare or take an unidentified plant.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Vishamushtika [SS]", taken from an extraction of Bhishagratna's translation; this entry replaces it. The locus given was "SS Sutra 38, Surasa'di group (Vol. I line 15465)".

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Vishamushtika is printed in the Sutrasthana, chapter 38 (locus: Surasa'di group, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 124; the same text is in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, line 15465). Chapter 38 is "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3-6), and says that "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (lines 8-10). The name is given by the translator without a gloss, a botanical name or a description.

In the Surasa'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 119-125 (the list), lines 126-130 (the metrical text)), Vishamushtika is one of the drugs listed. The group's metrical text reads: "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough." That is a property of the group as a whole, not of Vishamushtika alone.

**Other places the name appears.** None. A search of all chapter files on disk for "mushtika" found only this line in the drug groups (the one other hit, Chikitsa ch.30 line 61, is the gloss "Sanakha-mushtika" for a measure, and is not this drug). The name is printed in the Surasa'di list as "Kakamachi and Vishamushtika" (lines 124-125). The first element of the name, visha, ordinarily means poison, but the text does not say that this drug is poisonous, and the Codex does not infer toxicity from a name.

The chapter closes (lines 554-566) by saying that the groups "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", and that plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, Ghritas or potions are to be prepared "according to the exigencies of each individual case". That is a general rule of the chapter and is not a recipe for this drug.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Vishamushtika stands in the dravya-group classification of Sutrasthana ch.38, a classification by therapeutic action. The Surasa'di group is a therapeutic group, not a rasayana or vajikarana group. A search of the chapter files on disk for the spellings printed here found no mention of Vishamushtika in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters, and no text read calls it an elixir or a drug of vitality. The register is therefore (b): framed by its therapeutic group, with no cultivation status attested. The Codex does not assign *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* effects to it, because no source read does.

**Practitioner's note.** Vishamushtika is a name in a list. The tradition's text gives the drug a place among therapeutic groups and little more, and nothing in the record supports a cultivation use. A cultivator can learn from it only how Sushruta organises drugs into groups by action, and that some names in that classification are no longer certainly identified. Nobody should try to find a plant to match the name.
