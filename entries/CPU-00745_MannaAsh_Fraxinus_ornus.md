# Manna Ash — Flowering Ash

## *Fraxinus ornus* L. | Family: Oleaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Cool · Sweet · Moistening · Gently Descending

**Training Phase:** Phase I — Foundational (one of the safest agents in the codex)

---

### Origin & Tradition

Cut the bark of a manna ash in the Sicilian summer, in July or August, in the dry weeks when the tree is under water stress, and a colourless sap wells out and hardens on the trunk into pale, brittle, faintly sweet stalactites. Scrape them off and you have **manna** — *cannoli* if the tears hang whole from the incision, *manna in sorte* if they fall and pool. It tastes like a poor, sandy honey. It is one of the very few substances in this codex that a child can safely be given.

**Ancient Traditions (AT) — Mediterranean and Levantine**

Manna's name and its shadow are biblical: the bread from heaven that fell on the wilderness. That the Sicilian ash exudate got the name is a piece of theological branding — the Exodus manna and the *Fraxinus* exudate are almost certainly unrelated substances, and no serious identification links them. [USER: verify — the tradition of identifying biblical manna with tamarisk exudate, ash manna, or a lichen is contested and should not be asserted as settled.] What is not in dispute is that the classical and medieval Mediterranean world traded manna as a *gentle* medicine and that the Sicilian Madonie mountains have carried the tapping craft into the present as a living, endangered agricultural tradition (Abbate et al., *Plants*, 2020).

**Dravyaguna / Materia Medica of the Pharmacopoeias (DMM) — *Manna*, Manna Cannellata**

Manna held pharmacopoeial status across Europe for centuries with an indication that barely varied: **a mild laxative, safe for children, the elderly, and pregnancy.** It is described in the modern literature in exactly those inherited terms — manna "owing its content in mannitol, is used in medicine as a mild laxative" (Restivo et al., *Molecules*, 2020), and "has long been known for its mild laxative and emollient properties" (Attanzio et al., *Antioxidants*, 2019). This is the entire classical claim. Not a purgative. Not a drastic. A *mild* laxative, and the mildness is the point — manna occupied the pharmacopoeial slot where nothing violent could be risked.

**Chinese Medicine**

*Fraxinus* enters the Chinese materia medica as **秦皮** (*Qín Pí*, ash bark) — bitter, astringent, cold, for damp-heat dysentery and red eyes. **This is a different drug from a different part of a different species and it does the opposite thing:** Qin Pi is astringent and *binds* the bowel; manna is osmotic and *loosens* it. The tag is retained here because the genus is shared and because the contrast is instructive — bark binds, sap looses. Do not let the shared Latin genus collapse them. [USER: verify — *Qin Pi* is sourced from *F. rhynchophylla* and related species, not *F. ornus*; confirm the species list before any cross-prescription reasoning.]

**Distinguish from *Fraxinus excelsior*** (European Ash — see its own entry): *F. excelsior* is the tall timber ash of northern Europe, used for its leaves (diuretic, "poor man's tea") and bark. It does **not** yield commercial manna. *F. ornus* is a smaller southern tree with showy white flower panicles — hence "flowering ash" — and it is the manna tree. Both are threatened by the invasive ascomycete *Hymenoscyphus fraxineus*, the ash dieback pathogen, which puts *F. excelsior*, *F. angustifolia*, and *F. ornus* all "at risk" (Lutz et al., *Fungal Biol Biotechnol*, 2023). The supply chain for this entry has a biological expiry date on it.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

Most of this codex is an exercise in watching a doctrine strain against the chemistry. Manna is the opposite case, and it is here as the **control specimen**.

The doctrine says: *manna is sweet, and its sweetness draws water into the bowel, and the softened stool passes without force.* The tradition classified it as sweet and moistening, and prescribed it precisely where force was contraindicated — the child, the pregnant woman, the frail elder, the patient with haemorrhoids or a fresh anal fissure for whom straining is the actual injury.

Now open the molecule. Manna is **~40–60% mannitol** — a six-carbon sugar alcohol. [USER: verify the exact percentage range against an analytical source; the figure is widely quoted but I did not retrieve a primary assay this run.] Mannitol is poorly absorbed from the small intestine. Being poorly absorbed and osmotically active, it retains water in the lumen. Retained water softens stool and increases bulk. Softened bulk triggers peristalsis. The stool passes without force.

Read those two paragraphs again. **They are the same paragraph.** "Sweet" is a taste description of a polyol. "Moistening" is a folk statement of osmotic water retention. "Passes without force" is a description of an osmotic rather than stimulant mechanism. The doctrine is not a metaphor that happens to gesture at the chemistry — the doctrine is a *correct low-resolution description* of the chemistry, arrived at by tasting and watching.

And the identity goes one step further than the tradition could have known. Mannitol is not merely *like* a drug. Mannitol **is** a drug — a current, listed, hospital-stocked osmotic agent, given intravenously to draw water out of the brain in raised intracranial pressure and used as a renal osmotic diuretic. The same physical principle the Sicilian bark-tapper exploited in the colon, the neurosurgeon exploits across the blood–brain barrier. One mechanism, two organs, two millennia apart. In the whole codex there is no cleaner doctrine↔molecule identity.

**Where it must be deflated.** The clean identity covers *mannitol*, and mannitol only. The rest of manna — the phenolic phytocomplex — has attracted a modern literature making much larger claims (antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-cancer) that are laboratory-only. And critically: the doctrinal claim of *safety in pregnancy and childhood* rests on centuries of use, not on trial data. I retrieved no randomised controlled trial of manna in any population. Traditional safety is real evidence; it is not the same evidence as a trial, and the distinction must be held.

### Active Components

- **Mannitol (D-mannitol)** — the principal constituent, ~40–60% of dried manna [USER: verify]; a sugar alcohol; poorly absorbed; osmotically active. **The active drug.**
- **Mannotriose, stachyose, glucose, fructose** — accessory sugars; the residual sweetness. [USER: verify proportions.]
- **Fraxetin** — a coumarin; identified by HPLC-DAD in Sicilian manna (Attanzio et al., 2019). The genus's signature coumarin.
- **Hydroxytyrosol, tyrosol, elenolic acid** — the Oleaceae phenolic fingerprint, shared with olive; manna's phenolic identity is an *olive-family* identity.
- **Verbascoside, catechin, gallic acid, procyanidin-B1, luteolin-3,7-glucoside** — the remaining identified phenolics, in descending abundance after elenolic acid and tyrosol (Attanzio et al., 2019).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Manna is the codex's **force-free evacuant** and its **paediatric-safe entry point**.

**The indication is narrow and it is a mechanism indication, not a symptom indication.** Manna is for constipation where the problem is *hard, dry stool* and where *straining is contraindicated or dangerous*. That population is specific and it is exactly the classical one:

- the child, where a stimulant laxative is inappropriate and dependence is a real risk
- the frail elder, where cramping and fluid shifts are poorly tolerated
- the post-partum or haemorrhoidal or fissured patient, where the straining itself is the pathology
- the patient in whom a stimulant has already failed or been abandoned

**It is not indicated** for slow-transit constipation where the stool is already soft, for opioid-induced constipation as a first-line agent, or as a general "cleansing" practice. Manna does one physical thing — it holds water in the lumen — and if water is not the missing variable, manna will do nothing except make the patient flatulent.

**Phase I placement** is deliberate and it is a teaching placement. Manna is where the practitioner learns that *gentleness is a mechanism, not a mood*. Manna is mild because it is osmotic rather than secretagogue or motility-stimulant — not because it is "natural," not because it is sweet, not because the tradition says so. The pharmacological reason for the safety is available and checkable. Every subsequent entry in the codex should be interrogated the way manna can be.

Its **secondary Kenetic function** is as the doctrinal control: when a practitioner claims a traditional doctrine maps onto a molecule, manna is the standard the claim is measured against. Most claims will not survive the comparison. That is the intended outcome.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

**Mannitol as an osmotic agent in the gut — Grade: STRONG mechanistically, and the identity is the finding.**
- Mannitol added to oral water measurably improved small-bowel distension in MR imaging, by "addition of osmotic and nonosmotic substances that lead to decreased water resorption" — Lauenstein et al., *Radiology* 2003 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.2281020161) (PMID 12750457). This is a *human* study, in adults, demonstrating exactly the physical mechanism the doctrine describes: mannitol in the lumen holds water in the lumen. It was not run as a constipation trial — it was run to fill bowel for imaging — which is, if anything, a cleaner demonstration of the mechanism uncontaminated by symptom endpoints.
- Mannitol's poor mucosal permeability is so reliable that it is used as the **probe molecule** for measuring intestinal permeability — ³H-mannitol flux as a barrier-function readout (Moeser et al., *World J Gastroenterol* 2008 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.14.6012), PMID 18932279), and the lactulose–mannitol ratio as the standard clinical permeability endpoint (Kessoku et al., *Contemp Clin Trials* 2018 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cct.2018.04.002), PMID 29627620). **Read the significance:** mannitol is the molecule science uses *as its definition* of "does not get absorbed." The doctrinal requirement — that the sweetness stay in the gut and hold water — is not merely satisfied. It is the property mannitol is metrologically famous for.

**Manna as a laxative in humans — Grade: WEAK. This is the honest null.**
- I searched and retrieved **no randomised controlled trial of manna** for constipation in any population, and **no RCT of oral mannitol** as a laxative. The retrieved literature repeats the laxative indication as *received tradition* — Restivo et al. (2020) and Attanzio et al. (2019) both state the mild-laxative use as background, not as a finding they tested.
- **So the correct grading is uncomfortable and must be stated plainly:** the *mechanism* is strongly evidenced in humans; the *clinical claim* is evidenced by centuries of use and by inference from mechanism, not by a trial. The inference is a good one — the same osmotic class (lactulose, PEG, magnesium salts) is trial-supported — but manna itself has not been tested. Do not upgrade a mechanism to an outcome.

**The phenolic phytocomplex — Grade: WEAK. In vitro only, and note the species problem.**
- A hydrophilic manna extract showed time- and concentration-dependent anti-proliferative activity against Caco-2, HCT-116 and HT-29 colon cancer cells (IC50 8.51–28.92 mg manna equivalents/mL), via mitochondrial-pathway apoptosis and G1/S arrest, with **no toxicity to normally differentiated Caco-2 intestinal cells** — Restivo et al., *Molecules* 2020 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules25215055) (PMID 33143282). Notably, hydroxytyrosol at its concentration in the extract was *ineffective* — a useful negative finding: the effect is not attributable to the fashionable constituent.
- Manna extract reduced oxidants across FRAP/ABTS/DPPH assays, acted as a chain-breaking antioxidant comparable to vitamin E against lipoperoxyl radicals, and reduced IL-6 and IL-8 release in an IL-1β-activated intestinal cell model — Attanzio et al., *Antioxidants* 2019 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox8100494) (PMID 31635261).
- **Deflate this hard.** Both are in vitro. IC50 values in the *milligrams per millilitre* range are not achievable luminal concentrations by any plausible dose, let alone systemic ones. Antioxidant assay results are not clinical antioxidant effects. **Manna is not a cancer preventive and nothing retrieved suggests it is.** These papers are chemistry, and they should be read as chemistry.

**⚠️ A source-material correction the practitioner needs.** Both bioactivity papers above studied manna from ***Fraxinus angustifolia*** Vahl, not *F. ornus*. And a genetic study of the Sicilian Madonie germplasm concluded that, "contrary to popular belief that manna ash is *F. ornus* L., the germplasm currently used for manna production belongs to *F. angustifolia* Vahl" — Abbate et al., *Plants* 2020 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants9081035) (PMID 32824084). **This entry is filed under *F. ornus* because that is the canonical botanical attribution of manna ash in the pharmacopoeial and horticultural literature, but the commercial Sicilian material may not be that species.** The mannitol identity is unaffected — mannitol is mannitol. The phenolic profile is not. [USER: this is a live taxonomic dispute and the canonical binomial for this entry may need revisiting.]

**Supply risk — Grade: ESTABLISHED.** Ash dieback (*Hymenoscyphus fraxineus*) places *F. excelsior*, *F. angustifolia*, and *F. ornus* at risk across Europe — Lutz et al., *Fungal Biol Biotechnol* 2023 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40694-023-00149-y) (PMID 36639657). The Sicilian collection is described as endangered and of conservation value (Abbate et al., 2020). This is a materia medica whose material is disappearing.

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian (AT).** The tag places manna in an ancient Near-Eastern frame, but the material attested here is a Sicilian ash exudate whose "ancient" resonance is biblical and Levantine — the wilderness bread of Exodus — not the cuneiform medical corpus of the *asû* and *āšipu*. No Nineveh tablet identification for *Fraxinus* manna is claimed, and under the standing blocking warning for Assyrian substance identifications nothing is asserted here. Even were an attestation secure, that corpus classifies a substance partly by the causal agency it opposes (divine hand, ghost, oath), not by a vital-cultivation status. No cultivation framing is attested. **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides' *manna* (μάννα) in *De Materia Medica* denotes the granular siftings of frankincense — *manna thuris* — not the sweet exudate of the flowering ash; ash-manna as a mild laxative enters the European pharmacopoeias later, by way of the Arabic-into-medieval materia medica. Dioscorides in any case carries no subtle-body system to which a cultivation status could attach. What his tradition offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention — grading the tears (*cannoli* whole on the incision versus *manna in sorte* fallen and pooled), assaying purity, guarding against adulteration and the substituted species. That is a real practice of provenance and sensory judgement, not a vital-body doctrine. No cultivation status. **(c) absent.**

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The content carried under this tag in the entry is in fact Chinese — *Qín Pí* 秦皮, the astringent bark of a different *Fraxinus* species that *binds* the bowel rather than loosening it — and documents no Unani source. Manna itself (as *mann* / *taranjabīn*) is broadly known to the Arabic-Persian materia medica as a cool, gentle laxative, but no cultivation status attaches to it: the strongest Unani longevity idea — conservation of innate heat and radical moisture, ageing understood as their depletion — is a frame for the whole regimen, not a standing the tradition assigns to an osmotic purgative, and no *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed. No cultivation framing is attested. **(c) absent.**

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a cultivation substance. Across all three tags manna is one thing — a force-free osmotic laxative for the narrow case where straining is the danger — taken for a defined complaint and then stopped. There is no longevity, tonic, or vital-building use to build a practice around. The discipline manna teaches a cultivator is diagnostic rather than tonic: that gentleness is a mechanism (osmosis, mannitol holding water in the lumen) and not a virtue or a mood, and that a clean doctrine-to-molecule fit — sweet and moistening answering to mannitol — is a thing to verify, not to venerate.

### Contraindications

Manna is among the safest agents in this codex. That is not a licence to skip this section.

- **⚠️ Osmotic GI effects — the expected, dose-limiting adverse effect.** Unabsorbed polyols reaching the colon are fermented by gut bacteria. The product is gas. **Bloating, flatulence, borborygmi, and abdominal cramping are the predictable consequence of the mechanism working**, not a sign of intolerance or of a bad batch. Excess dose produces osmotic diarrhoea. Start low.
- **Polyol intolerance / IBS:** mannitol is a **FODMAP** — the "P" in the acronym is polyols, and mannitol is a named member. Patients with IBS or established polyol intolerance may react disproportionately. This is not an allergy; it is the same mechanism at an unwelcome intensity.
- **Inadequate fluid intake:** an osmotic laxative pulls water into the lumen. If the patient is not drinking, that water comes from a body that needs it. **Fluid intake is part of the dose.**
- **Bowel obstruction, suspected obstruction, ileus, acute abdomen:** absolutely contraindicated. Adding luminal water proximal to an obstruction is dangerous. This applies to every osmotic agent and manna is not exempt because it is gentle.
- **Diabetes:** mannitol is poorly absorbed and has minimal glycaemic impact, but manna is **not pure mannitol** — it carries glucose, fructose, and other sugars. Monitor. Do not treat manna as a sugar-free product.
- **Dehydration, significant electrolyte derangement, renal impairment:** caution. The margin is wide but the mechanism is a fluid-shift mechanism.
- **Pregnancy and paediatric use:** these are the *classical* indications and the traditional safety record is long and reassuring. **It is not trial data.** I retrieved none. Both populations require a clinician, not a codex entry.
- **Chronic use / dependence:** osmotic laxatives do not produce the cathartic-colon dependence associated with chronic stimulant use, but **chronic constipation is a diagnosis, not a symptom to manage indefinitely.** Persistent constipation, any change in bowel habit, blood in stool, or unexplained weight loss is a referral.
- **Species uncertainty:** see the correction above. If the phenolic content matters to a given claim, the species matters. If only the laxative action matters, it does not.
