# Verdigris — zinjār (زنجار)

## *Green copper corrosion product, basic copper acetate (and, in the text, also the corrosion of iron); the identification is the modern chemical one, matched to the text's account of making it with copper and vinegar* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** The spine records one other verdigris in the Codex: the "worm" verdigris (*scolecia*) of the Dioscorides tradition, filed inside the copper entry II_96_Copper_Tong. Avicenna's *zinjār* is a separate entry, Book II, entry *zinjār*, Arabic text lines 12647 to 12668. The next entry, *zahrat al-nuḥās* ("flower of copper"), is a different drug and is not used here.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** What it is: "known", and Avicenna gives the ways of making it. (1) Copper is treated (*takrīj*, a word the edition leaves unclear) in the dregs of vinegar and its filings are sprinkled with vinegar and buried in the dew; (2) copper vessels are inverted over vessels holding vinegar and left until they turn to verdigris, which is scraped off; (3) it is mixed with sal ammoniac (*nushādir*) and buried in the dew. A "very fine" kind is made from distilled vinegar ground in a copper mortar with a copper pestle in the burning sun, with alum and salt added, then vinegar and the urine of boys, ground again and left in the dew and dried. It is also taken from what forms on rock and in copper mines, and, he says, "it is sometimes taken from the stomach" (lines 12648 to 12657). The last phrase is not explained, and the Arabic word printed (*al-miʿda*, stomach) may be a misprint for *al-maʿdin* (mine). [USER: verify against another edition.]
Temperament: "hot and dry to the fourth [degree]" (line 12657). Actions: "a polisher (*jallāʾ*), eating (*akkāl*) hard and soft flesh alike, sharp; the cerate (*qayrūṭī*) corrects it and makes it drying without stinging" (lines 12658 to 12659). Wounds and ulcers: it stops spreading ulcers, heals with cerate, cleans dirty ulcers, and with Nabataean mastic and natron "is the treatment of ulcerating mange (*jarab*), leucoderma (*barāṣ*) and vitiligo (*bahaq*)" (lines 12659 to 12661). Head: the verdigris made with sal ammoniac, alum and vinegar, ground and blown into the nose "while the mouth is filled with water so that it does not reach the throat," benefits foul smell of the nose and bad ulcers in it; iron verdigris with vinegar "tightens the gums," and a cerate of it, as of copper verdigris, is for swellings of the gums (lines 12661 to 12664). Eye: it benefits thickness and roughness of the lids, polishes the eye, enters eye-ulcer medicines and "makes the tears flow greatly"; when used in kohls "it is right to foment the eye with a sponge dipped in hot water" (lines 12664 to 12666). Excretory organs: it enters medicines for haemorrhoids, and wicks (*fatāʾil*) are made of it with ammoniacum (*ashshaq*) and packed into the haemorrhoids (lines 12667 to 12668). No dose is given. No other author is quoted.

### Active Components

Verdigris is a mixture of basic copper acetates, formed when copper meets acetic acid and air. The text names copper and vinegar as its materials, and iron verdigris as a second kind with vinegar. Alum, salt and sal ammoniac are named as additions.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The text records a caustic copper salt used on skin, gums, nose, eye lids and haemorrhoids. Copper salts are irritant and toxic if swallowed or applied to the eye, and the Codex does not recommend this material.

### Preparation & Dosing

Preparations are those in the Origin section, with the locus. Cerate is the corrective for the drug's bite (line 12659). No dose or quantity is given beyond "a measure" of alum and salt for the fine kind (line 12654). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

Copper salts are caustic and have antimicrobial properties, and the polishing, flesh-eating action the text describes is the action of a corrosive. The older uses (eye, nose, haemorrhoids) involve toxic doses of a heavy metal salt and are not supported as treatments. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Avicenna: hot and dry to the fourth degree; a polisher and flesh-eater, sharp; corrected by cerate (lines 12657 to 12659). No other tradition is tagged.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The entry is a caustic and a recipe for making it, with uses for ulcers, gums and eye. It has no word on the vital faculties. **(c) Absent: no cultivation framing is attested for verdigris.**

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here to practise. A cultivator's use of the entry is as a lesson in how the Canon grades a corrosive drug and in what it asks to be done to blunt it.

### Contraindications

- Toxic if swallowed; irritant to skin, eye and mucous membranes; a heavy-metal salt.
- Blowing it into the nose or placing it in the eye or rectum is dangerous.
- Not for any use without medical advice.
