# Silver ore — dngul.rdo (Clark 1995); Ngul dho (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011), rGyud-bzhi Explanatory Tantra ch.20

## *The ore from which silver is smelted, which Clark describes in a bracketed commentary as "of three names", of no fixed shape, "brown externally and ... a shiny silver hue on the inside"; the species of ore is not named in either text, so the identification is only "a silver-bearing ore" (high confidence for that much, none for any mineral name)* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The Codex's list row is `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 138, "Ngul dho, , mineral, rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Efficacy of Medicinal Substances); Four Tantras of Tibetan Medicine (English trans.)", flagged as an uncertain identification. Two texts were read.

*Clark 1995* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). Chapter 20 has a section on the powers of individual natural substances in eight parts: precious metals, stones, earths, trees, resins, plants, herbs and animal products (line 802). Silver ore stands in part (2), "The Powers of Stone Medicines" (line 816), directly after gold ore (line 828). The entry (line 829) reads:

"dngul.rdo: Silver Ore: [has three names and is of (indeterminate) shape. It is brown externally and has a shiny silver hue on the inside. Silver is obtained by smelting it]. Both of the above types of ore drain lymph [accumulations]."

Clark's introduction says that "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary, whilst those in parentheses ( ) are derived from the oral instructions" of two named teachers (line 82). On that reading the tantra's own words about silver ore are only the unbracketed ones: "Both of the above types of ore drain lymph". "The above types" are gold ore (gser.rdo, line 828, whose entry has no action of its own) and this ore, so the action is a joint one. The colour, the shape and the smelting are commentary. That bracket convention is as stated in Clark's introduction and was not checked against the Tibetan. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`). In the stone-medicine summary of the Explanatory Tantra (page 199; text line 3356) the passage reads "Ser dho and Ngul dho drain out chhuser." (Ser dho is gold ore, Ngul dho silver ore, by the pairing with Clark's gold ore and silver ore. [USER: verify]) The same summary places "Tangzil, Serzil and Ngulzil" next, which "give color to bones". The index lists "Ngul dho 199" (line 7453). *Chhuser* is a Tibetan term left untranslated by this edition; Clark renders the same action as draining "lymph [accumulations]".

*The metal.* The entry for the metal silver (line 805) is a different entry. Its bracket says silver "is known to be smelted from ore" and that it "dries lymph disorders, pus and blood". That is the metal, not the ore, and is cited here only to show how the same bracket places the ore as the source of the metal.

**What is not given.** In the passages read, neither text gives a taste, potency or post-digestive taste for silver ore by itself, a dose, a preparation (beyond the commentary's smelting), or a formula that contains it. The commentary says "three names" but the names are not printed in the English. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The mineral is not named. Silver is commonly won from several ores (native silver, silver sulphides, silver-bearing lead and copper ores), and the description "brown outside, shiny silver inside" does not settle which. The entry is therefore filed as "silver-bearing ore, species unresolved". [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None is stated in the texts. As a general fact, a silver ore contains silver compounds, usually sulphides or native silver together with other metal minerals, and it is commonly mixed with lead, copper or iron minerals. Ores from the ground can also carry arsenic, antimony and lead. No composition is asserted for the ore the tantra means.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The tantra names the ore, with gold ore, as draining *chhuser* (lymph accumulations). An unprocessed metal ore of unknown composition should not be swallowed. The tradition itself treats metallic and mineral medicines as materials needing specialist processing, which the sources retrieved do not describe. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation for silver ore as a medicine. The only process named is the smelting of the ore to obtain silver (Clark's bracket, line 829), which produces the metal and not a medicine. No compound, formula, route, measure or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] As general facts: silver salts and colloidal silver have antimicrobial properties, and chronic exposure to silver compounds can cause argyria, a permanent grey-blue discolouration of the skin; ores may also carry toxic heavy metals. Lymph accumulations (swellings of the lymph nodes and similar) are conditions that need medical assessment.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT).** For silver ore by itself no taste, potency or post-digestive taste is printed in either text read. For the metal silver, Clark's bracket says it "has the same (astringent) taste and post-digestive taste as gold" (line 805). That line is about the metal and is not carried across to the ore. No wind, bile or phlegm (*rlung*, *mkhris pa*, *bad kan*) action is printed for the ore in the passages read. No classification is imported from the Chinese or Indian systems.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The tantra places the ore among the stone medicines of Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Clark line 816) and gives it one therapeutic action, shared with gold ore. In the same stone section, one other substance, *brag.zhun* (Clark line 870), is called "unsurpassed as an essence-extraction substance"; nothing like this is said of silver ore, and no *bcud len* use of it was found. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation. It is a raw ore the tantra lists for draining lymph, with no preparation given, and a raw metal ore is not a practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Raw metal ore:** may contain lead, arsenic, antimony and other toxic metals; do not swallow, grind or inhale dust.
- **Silver compounds:** repeated or prolonged exposure can cause permanent skin discolouration (argyria).
- **Lymph swellings:** need medical assessment, not self-treatment.
- **Preparation unknown:** Tibetan metal and mineral medicines are traditionally processed by specialists; this entry states no processing. [USER: verify]
