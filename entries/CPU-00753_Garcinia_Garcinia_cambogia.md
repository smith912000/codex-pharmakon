# Gamboge / Malabar Tamarind — Kudam Puli (Malayalam), Vrikshamla (Sanskrit), Brindleberry, Goraka

## *Garcinia gummi-gutta* (syn. *Garcinia cambogia*) | Family: Clusiaceae (Guttiferae)

**Tradition Tags:** DMM, CM, SS
**Energetic Classification:** Cool and dry (third degree drying) — a sharp astringent-sour that binds and scrapes
**Training Phase:** Refinement / Descent — a corrective agent for surplus, never a foundational tonic

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda (Sushruta lineage):** In the classical South Indian and Sushruta-inflected materia medica, *Garcinia* fruit rind — *vrikshamla*, literally "tree-sour" — is a *dipana* (kindler of digestive fire) and *pachana* (digestant). The sun-dried, blackened rind (*kudam puli*) is a staple souring agent in Keralan fish curries, valued for cutting the heaviness (*guru*) and oiliness (*snigdha*) of rich food. Traditional use frames it as an agent that lightens the body and eases the sensation of over-fullness — a symbolic and practical thinning of accumulation.

**Chinese / Shennong stratum:** *Garcinia* species enter the broader East and Southeast Asian sour-astringent tradition as digestives and preservatives. The rind's ability to sour and to check spoilage aligns it, in the Shennong-descended logic of correspondences, with substances that "gather inward" and restrain leakage — a drying, consolidating action rather than a warming, dispersing one.

**Unani / Avicennan reception:** Sour *Garcinia*-type fruits sit in the Unani framework among the cold-dry correctives — astringents that temper an excess of phlegmatic or sanguine repletion, sharpen appetite by their acidity, and bind loose bowels. The Avicennan reading treats such sours as tools of *tanqiya* (corrective cleansing) to be used briefly and with judgement, not as daily nourishment.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Across all three receptions the fruit reads as the *thinner of surplus* — sour, cool, and scraping where the body has become heavy, oily, and over-full. That symbolism maps with unusual precision onto the modern pharmacology: the rind's hydroxycitric acid (HCA) competitively inhibits ATP-citrate lyase, the enzyme that channels dietary carbohydrate carbon into fatty-acid synthesis. The traditional image of a substance that "cuts the grease" and the biochemical fact of a de-novo-lipogenesis brake are the same gesture seen from two eras. The Kenetica reading keeps the symbol but refuses the marketing: a corrective for surplus is not a licence for excess, and — as the Contraindications make plain — the concentrated modern extract carries a real and documented liver hazard the whole fruit in a curry pot never did.

### Active Components

- **(−)-Hydroxycitric acid (HCA)** — the principal rind acid; a competitive, reversible inhibitor of ATP-citrate lyase, the committed step of de-novo fatty-acid synthesis. Marketed as calcium, potassium, magnesium and mixed salts, which differ in solubility and bioavailability.
- **Garcinol and isogarcinol** — polyisoprenylated benzophenones with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and histone-acetyltransferase-inhibitory activity in laboratory models.
- **Xanthones** (e.g. camboginol-type constituents) and **organic acids** (citric, tartaric) contributing to the sharp souring profile.
- **Flavonoids and pectin** in the rind, contributing astringency and bulk.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Within Kenetica, *Garcinia* is filed strictly as a **surplus-corrective of the Refinement phase** — invoked where the pattern is heaviness, oily repletion and blunted digestive fire, and only in the form the tradition actually used: the dried culinary rind. Its legitimate role is the souring, appetite-sharpening, grease-cutting action recognised for centuries in South Indian cookery. The Kenetica frame explicitly **de-couples the plant from the weight-loss-supplement claims** attached to concentrated HCA: the training use is culinary and digestive, short-arc and corrective, never a standalone slimming protocol. Practitioners are directed away from high-dose standalone HCA capsules toward whole-rind culinary preparation, precisely because the safety signal (below) tracks the concentrated extract, not the pot.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the clinical weight-loss evidence for *Garcinia*/HCA is **modest and mixed**, and the safety literature is the more important story. A hot-water *Garcinia cambogia* extract with HCA is widely sold for weight control, but human trials show small and inconsistent effects, and mechanistic disputes remain unresolved.

- A defence of HCA safety argues that a large body of animal and clinical work found no consistent inflammation or hepatotoxicity from HCA per se, and even reported hepatoprotective effects against ethanol and dexamethasone (Clouatre & Preuss, *World J Gastroenterol* 2013 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v19.i44.8160)). This is presented here for balance, and its industry affiliation is noted.
- Against that, the pharmacovigilance record is serious. A review tracing food-safety alerts from the 2009 FDA warning through European alerts of 2020–2021 concludes that multiple cases demonstrate a relationship between HCA-containing supplements and acute liver injury (Zovi et al., *J Integr Med* 2022 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joim.2022.05.003)).
- Most starkly, a documented case of **fulminant hepatic failure requiring liver transplantation** was associated with an HCA-containing weight-loss supplement (Lunsford et al., *World J Gastroenterol* 2016 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v22.i45.10071)).

**Evidence grade:** *Moderate for the hepatotoxicity signal* (converging case reports, transplant-level events, and regulatory alerts); *weak/mixed for weight-loss efficacy*. Traditional culinary and digestive use is well-attested but is a different exposure from concentrated extract.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Ayurveda:** *Rasa* — amla (sour), with kashaya (astringent) secondary; *Guna* — laghu (light), ruksha (dry); *Virya* — cooling to neutral (sour rasa is conventionally heating, but the rind's drying-scraping action is used to reduce heaviness); *Vipaka* — amla. Actions: *dipana*, *pachana*, *lekhana* (scraping/reducing).
- **Chinese/Shennong stratum:** sour and astringent flavour, cool nature; gathering-inward, restraining action.
- **Unani/Avicennan:** cold and dry; astringent corrective of repletion, appetite-sharpening by acidity, bowel-binding.

### Contraindications

**⚠️ HEPATOTOXICITY — PRIMARY SAFETY CONCERN.** Concentrated HCA weight-loss supplements derived from *Garcinia cambogia* are documented in the peer-reviewed literature as a cause of **herb-induced liver injury**, ranging from reversible hepatitis to **acute/fulminant hepatic failure requiring transplantation** (Lunsford et al. 2016 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v22.i45.10071); Zovi et al. 2022 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joim.2022.05.003)). The signal prompted a 2009 FDA warning and repeated European food-safety alerts. Anyone with existing liver disease, or taking other hepatotoxic drugs or alcohol, must avoid concentrated HCA products. Discontinue immediately and seek medical care for jaundice, dark urine, right-upper-quadrant pain, or unexplained fatigue.

**⚠️ SEROTONIN-TOXICITY INTERACTION.** Case reports link *Garcinia cambogia* supplements to **serotonin toxicity** when combined with serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, and related agents). Do not combine HCA supplements with antidepressants or other serotonergic medication without medical supervision.

**Other cautions:** avoid in pregnancy and lactation (inadequate safety data for concentrated extract); use caution in diabetes and with hypoglycaemic drugs, as HCA products may lower blood glucose; the astringent-sour rind may aggravate hyperacidity/reflux in sensitive individuals. Culinary rind use in normal food quantities has a long safety record, but this does **not** transfer to high-dose capsules. **This entry does not endorse standalone HCA weight-loss supplementation.**
