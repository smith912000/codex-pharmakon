# Donkey (ass) flesh — "asses" (Bhishagratna), in the Gramya (domestic animal) group of the Sushruta meat chapter; Gardabha (the Sanskrit word in the source list)
## *The meat of the ass or donkey. Bhishagratna's English prints "asses" in a list of domestic animals; the source list `_sources/sushruta.csv` (line 233) gives the Sanskrit as "Gardabha (ass)". No binomial is printed in the lines read* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The English is Bhishagratna's translation of the *Sushruta Samhita*. The ass is named in the meat group of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink (Annapana-Vidhi-madhyayam)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 4-6). The chapter lists the kinds of flesh by habitat, "those which are aquatic in their habits (Jaleshaya), or frequent marshy lands (Anupa), or dwell in villages (Gramya), or are carnivorous in their habits (Kravyabhuja), or are possessed of unbifurcated hoofs (Ekashapha), or dwell on high ground (Jangala)", and says that "each succeeding kind is superior to the one immediately preceding it in the order of enumeration" (lines 387-394). A second classification (lines 396-409) divides the *Jangala* class into eight sub-species, the *Gramya* (domestic animals) being the last.

The ass is named in one line, inside the definition of the domestic group (lines 614-619): "Animals such as horses, mules, cows, bullocks, asses, camels, goats, sheep, and Medapuchhas (fat tailed or Turkish sheep) etc., belong to the group of domestic animals (Gramyas)." The group statement follows (lines 621-625): "The flesh of domestic animals is possessed of constructive, tonic and appetising properties, is sweet in taste and digestion. It destroys the deranged Vayu and produces the Kapham and Pittam." The lines after it give separate statements for the goat, the sheep, the Medapuchcha, beef, and "an animal with unbifurcated hoofs (Ekashapha such as, the horse and the mule etc.)" (lines 625-650). **The English prints no separate statement for the ass, and does not name the ass among the Ekashapha examples.** What the chapter says of ass flesh is therefore the group statement and nothing more; whether it is meant to hold for each member alike is not said.

One statement in the same passage bears on habitat, not on the ass by name: "the flesh of an animal living in a village or near a reservoir of water, excessively increases that secretion", the secretion of the internal organs (lines 653-657). The ass is a domestic animal in this chapter's list, so the sentence may be meant to include it, but the text does not apply it to the ass in so many words. [USER: verify]

Ass flesh is named once more as food, in the *Uttaratantra* chapter on phthisis (*Sosha-Pratishedha*, ch.41; `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch41.txt` lines 222-224): "The diet may also consist of Mudga-soup or of Adhaki-soup or of the flesh of an ass, camel, elephant, mule or horse variously prepared and well cooked." Here the flesh is a diet for a wasting disease, in a list with the flesh of the camel, elephant, mule and horse. The preceding sentence (lines 216-221) is the recipe of other meats, including a crow, an owl and an earthworm (*Gandupada*), fried with rock salt in mustard oil.

Other places where Sushruta names the ass, which I opened. None of them is ass flesh; they are listed so that the ass's appearances in the book are not lost:

- *Urine.* In the chapter on liquids (*Sutrasthana* ch.45, lines 1755-1758): "The urine of an ass tends to neutralise the effects of poison generated through the chemical action of two different substances in the organism. It is strong and proves curative in cases of chronic dysentery. It is a vermifuge and subdues the Vayu and Kapham, and is appetising." The she-ass's urine is also one of five urines used with ashes to make an alkaline preparation (*Uttaratantra* ch.42, line 273).
- *Dung.* "The powdered dung of a camel, a mule, or an ass should be administered to him in food" (*Chikitsasthana* ch.11, the chapter on *Prameha*, lines 222-223); "the expressed liquid of the dung of a horse or of an ass should be taken" in retention of urine (*Uttaratantra* ch.55, line 141); "The dung of an ass or of a horse should be squeezed through a piece of linen" and a measure of the liquid taken for painful urination (*Uttaratantra* ch.58, lines 179-181). In the phthisis chapter, ghee is cooked with "the milk, blood, meat-essence and the expressed liquid of the dungs of a cow, a mare, a ewe, a she-goat, a cow-elephant, a hind, a she-ass and a she-camel" with other drugs (ch.41, lines 297-302).
- *Bones.* "The bones of the Krauncha, camel and ass", pounded with named drugs and given in wine or hot water, are said to disintegrate gravel (*Chikitsasthana* ch.7, lines 98-100). The ashes of the bones of "an elephant, horse, hog, ass or camel" are used for an alkaline water (*Kshara*) in *Hasti-meha* (*Chikitsasthana* ch.11, lines 177-179).
- *Semen.* In the chapter on increasing strength and virile power (*Kshina-Valiyam Vaji-Karana*, *Chikitsasthana* ch.26, line 4), "the semen of a male buffalo, of a he-ass, or of a he-goat" is among the materials taken "for the same purpose" as the testes of the he-goat and the eggs of the tortoise and crab (lines 130-136). That is a statement about the semen of the he-ass in a *vajikarana* chapter, not about the flesh; it is not carried over to the flesh.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms donkey meat is skeletal muscle: protein, with a variable and usually low fat content, iron and B vitamins. Nothing more specific can be said without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text records ass flesh as one of the domestic meats: constructive, tonic, appetising, sweet, subduing deranged *Vayu*, and producing *Kapham* and *Pittam*, and as one meat named for the diet in phthisis. It gives the ass no use of its own as a drug in the chapter on food. Donkey meat is an ordinary food in some regions and is forbidden or avoided in others; the Codex gives no regimen built on it, and does not recommend the non-meat uses above (the urine, dung, bones or semen).

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives no preparation for ass flesh in the food chapter. In the phthisis chapter it says only that the flesh is to be "variously prepared and well cooked" (ch.41, line 224). The preparations that involve other ass materials are those listed above. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Donkey meat is a source of protein and iron. Raw or undercooked meat of any animal carries bacterial and parasitic risk. Horse and donkey meat can carry the parasite *Trichinella* and other infections if not properly cooked. Ayurvedic recommendations of meat for wasting diseases are not an established treatment for tuberculosis, the disease most often meant by phthisis today, which needs medical treatment. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** ch.46 lines 621-625 (domestic group): constructive, tonic, appetising; sweet in taste and in digestion; destroys deranged *Vayu*; produces *Kapham* and *Pittam*. The English prints no separate *virya* (potency) or *vipaka* for the ass, and no line specific to it. The group statement belongs to the group; there is no ass line to set beside it.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Ass flesh is attested in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 only as one member of the domestic group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 616), whose flesh is called "constructive, tonic and appetising", and in *Uttaratantra* ch.41 as a meat in the diet of a patient with phthisis (line 223). "Tonic" is a property listed for a food group in a chapter on food and drink; it is not a placement in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and it is not read as a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. The he-ass's semen is named in the *vajikarana* chapter (ch.26), and that is a different material from the flesh; the flesh is not named there. No passage read puts ass flesh in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and the English prints no claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The popular *sattvic*/*rajasic*/*tamasic* food lists belong to *Samkhya*-era and later material, not to Sushruta, and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes ass flesh a cultivation substance. Sushruta files it with the domestic meats, ranks that kind below the others, and gives the ass no line of its own; the one place it is named for a purpose is a diet for a wasting disease. For a cultivator the honest use is ordinary diet where donkey is an ordinary food, and nothing more. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** raw or undercooked meat carries bacterial and parasitic risk; cook thoroughly.
- **Wasting disease:** a person with long-lasting weight loss, cough or fever needs medical assessment, not a meat diet.
- **Allergy:** some people react to red meat and to other mammalian meat proteins.
- **Non-meat uses:** the urine, dung, bone-ash and semen uses recorded by Sushruta are not recommended.
- **Ethics and law:** donkeys are working animals in many regions, and the slaughter of donkeys for meat or parts is restricted or illegal in some places; some communities have food rules about equids. The Codex does not recommend harming any animal for these uses.
