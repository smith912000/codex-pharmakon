# Geranium pratense — (li.)ga.dur, Gadur (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Geranium pratense, Meadow Cranesbill, the first of four Latin names Clark 1995 prints for (li.)ga.dur (then Erodium stephanium, Bergenia crassifolia and Rhodiola wallichiana); also the third name of zhim.thig.le in Clark, and named by binomial in two of Clark's chapter 21 class lists. The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text prints the Tibetan name but no Geranium* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); the binomial rests on one text.** This entry rests on one tradition's texts, in two modern editions. Only Clark 1995 prints *Geranium pratense* (lines 977, 1054, 1211, 1213, and the index of Latin names at line 1590). A search of Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 for "Geranium" found nothing. The Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. No other text read for this entry names the plant: a search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Sushruta texts on disk (including the three Bhishagratna volumes) for the genus name *Geranium* found only Dioscorides 3-131 GERANION, GERANION ETERON (line 7700). Its translator's list of suggested identifications (lines 7701 to 7703) includes *Geranium robertianum* and, on the last line, the spelling "Geranium praetense" with "Crane's Bill". That list is the translator's and not Dioscorides' own identification, the chapter itself says of the plant it describes that "there is no use for it in medicine" (line 7704), and the Codex does not attribute the chapter to this row. Whether the spelling "praetense" in that list makes Dioscorides a second tradition for this species is a decision for the Codex editor. [USER: verify] Clark's preface (line 83) says that more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (row 110) the Tibetan name *(li.)ga.dur* is recorded at Clark line 977 with the identification "Meadow Cranesbill; Geranium pratense; Erodium stephanium; Bergenia crassifolia; Rhodiola wallichiana" and the note "more than one Latin name given; key uses the first". The key, *Geranium pratense*, is therefore the first binomial Clark prints (after the English name). The line is shared with the existing entries on its other binomials (see Overlap).

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 977).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 (heading at line 792), in the stretch of the materia medica that precedes the heading "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). It reads:

"(li.)ga.dur: Meadow Cranesbill: Geranium pratense: Erodium stephanium: Bergenia crassifolia: Rhodiola wallichiana: [The male type has seven names and the female type has six. It has balanced hot and cold power, hot and sweet tastes, cures lung disorders and reduces swellings. It has thick leaves similar to rice shoots, and the root resembles a withered gland. The flower has a pink interior and resembles a bird's beak. The common variety grows in Tibet and has pink petioles. All types] cure infectious fevers, lung fevers and channel fevers."

Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries. Inside the brackets (commentary layer) are the types and number of names, the power, the tastes, the lung and swelling indications, the description and the words "All types". Outside them (root-text layer, as far as Clark's brackets mark it) are the Tibetan name, the English and Latin names, which are Clark's own identifications and not the root text's, and the clause "cure infectious fevers, lung fevers and channel fevers". The four Latin names are of four different genera; the Codex does not say which of them the commentary's description fits.

*Clark 1995, the same Latin name elsewhere.* In the entry for *zhim.thig.le* (line 1054) *Geranium pratense* is the third of five names; that line is the subject of I_1295 (*Geranium dahuricum*) and reads that all three main types "dispel cataract". In chapter 21 ("The Principles of Medicines: Compounding of Medicines", heading at line 1205), line 1211 reads: "The categories of medicines which cure infectious fevers includes Bile of elephant and so forth, Bitter Cucumber, White Aconite, Meadow Cranesbill (Geranium pratense), Fagonia cretica, Populus alba (tree), Primula sibirica (herb), Common Fumitoty and Gold Thread." Line 1213 reads: "The category of medicines for lung disorders includes Bamboo concretion, Liquorice, Raisin, Hippophae rhamnoides, Costus root, Meadow Cranesbill (Geranium pratense), Gypsophila dahurica (Artemesia), Cochlearia scapiflora and Solms-Laubachia." In both lists Clark prints the English name and the binomial together, so the lists agree with line 977 in the actions (infectious fevers; lung) without giving a Tibetan name.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011.* The glossary prints "Gadur" (line 5276), "ga dur" (line 5280) and, counted in order against the scientific names (line 5282), "Bergenia stracheyi"; the English column leaves a blank at that place (line 5290). The pairing is by position and is the subject of I_1243. The running text spells the name "Gadhur": line 3220 (page 188) puts it in the group having an astringent taste; line 3423 (page 205) reads "Gadhur cures contagious diseases and hot disorders of the lungs and blood vessels"; line 3601 (page 220) lists it among the medicines "that cure contagious disorders"; line 3611 (page 221) lists it among those "that cure disorders of the lungs". The index lists "Gadhur 188,205,220,221" (line 7370). The two Tibetan texts agree that the name is a drug for lung disorders and for contagious or infectious fevers. They differ on the taste: Clark's commentary prints "hot and sweet tastes", while Men-Tsee-Khang lists the name under the astringent taste (line 3220). They also differ on the species: Men-Tsee-Khang's glossary, by position, gives *Bergenia stracheyi*. The Codex does not choose.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder found no entry on *Geranium pratense* as the subject. The same Clark line 977 is the locus of I_884 (*Bergenia crassifolia*, Elephant Ears), I_1243 (*Bergenia stracheyi*), I_1242 and I_308 (other *Bergenia* entries) and I_318 (*Rhodiola crenulata*), which quote or name it for their own binomials. I_1277 (*Fagonia cretica*) quotes the chapter 21 line 1211. I_1295 (*Geranium dahuricum*, this batch) covers Clark line 1054. I_814 (*Geranium robertianum*, tagged DMM and EF) is a different species from other traditions. This entry covers the *Geranium pratense* reading of the line and adds no source those entries lack, apart from the Men-Tsee-Khang running-text spelling "Gadhur". Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Geranium* is a genus of the geranium family (Geraniaceae) and that tannins are common in the genus. Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained or which part was used; the root is described in the commentary as resembling "a withered gland".

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a drug that "cure[s] infectious fevers, lung fevers and channel fevers" (Clark, line 977) and that stands in Clark's lists of medicines for infectious fevers (line 1211) and for lung disorders (line 1213); Men-Tsee-Khang has "Gadhur cures contagious diseases and hot disorders of the lungs and blood vessels" (line 3423). These are the texts' claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no quantity, no frequency and no duration. Clark's commentary describes the leaves, root, flower and petioles and does not say which part is taken. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Clark's commentary layer (line 977) prints "balanced hot and cold power, hot and sweet tastes". No post-digestive taste is printed. Men-Tsee-Khang lists the name "Gadhur" among the medicines having an astringent taste (line 3220); the two lists are not the same. The actions printed are for infectious, lung and channel fevers (Clark) and for contagious diseases and hot disorders of the lungs and blood vessels (Men-Tsee-Khang).

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica gives this drug by what it does: it cures infectious, lung and channel fevers, and Clark's chapter 21 puts it in the lists for infectious fevers and for lung disorders. Men-Tsee-Khang says it cures contagious diseases and hot disorders of the lungs and blood vessels. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this name places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the sentences cited above were read in full and none mentions one. Its framing is therapeutic only, and it attaches to the Tibetan name, which Clark glosses with four genera.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a drug for fevers and lung disorders in the Tibetan texts, with no preparation and no dose, and the Tibetan name is not securely a *Geranium*. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. Fevers and lung disorders need medical assessment, no preparation or dose is given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No conservation information was read.
