### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese Medicine (Qín Jiāo).** As a Qín Jiāo source, *G. straminea* shares the drug's identity: a bitter, acrid, cooling-and-moistening wind-damp herb that also clears heat, relaxes the sinews, unblocks the channels, clears deficiency heat, and treats damp-heat jaundice — the wind-damp herb of choice for **hot, inflamed** joint conditions (see I_315).

**Tibetan (rGyud-bzhi / FMT; Ji-ma).** *G. straminea* is a plateau gentian with its own standing in Tibetan medicine, where the *Gentiana* group (Tibetan **Ji-ma / Jia-ma**) is used as a bitter, cooling drug for **liver and gallbladder heat, fever, inflammation and jaundice-type conditions** — consistent with the gentian bitters' cross-tradition association with the liver and with the clearing of heat. Its abundance on the Qinghai–Tibet plateau makes it one of the region's own gentian medicines rather than a purely imported drug.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the plateau bitter of the liver-heat.** Like its sister Qín Jiāo gentians, *G. straminea* embodies "cool bitterness for hot obstruction" (see I_315); its distinctive note is its **dual TCM/Tibetan liver-heat identity** — a high-altitude bitter that both opens hot joints and cools the inflamed liver/gallbladder, its intense secoiridoid bitterness being the sensory signature of that heat-clearing action.

### Active Components

Essentially the Qín Jiāo secoiridoid profile (gentiopicroside as the marker; loganic acid, swertiamarin, sweroside). Distinctively, *G. straminea* root has yielded a characterised set of **secoiridoids** studied for anti-inflammatory action, including gentiopicroside (GTP), sweroside, swertiamarin, and the less-common **6′-O-β-D-glucosylgentiopicroside** and **6′-O-(2-hydroxy-3-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl-benzoyl)-sweroside (HGBS)**. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Same **`[RESET]`-phase cooling anti-inflammatory / anti-obstruction** positioning as Qín Jiāo (see I_315): damp-heat joint obstruction, relaxing the sinews, clearing deficiency heat, damp-heat jaundice. ### Scientific Correlation

Modern evidence is **preclinical** (cell/animal models); no efficacy-establishing human clinical trials specific to *G. straminea*. According to PubMed:

- **Secoiridoids — anti-inflammatory mechanism (specific to *G. straminea*).** Wei et al. (2011, *Phytother Res*) isolated five secoiridoids from *G. straminea* roots — gentiopicroside, HGBS, 6′-O-β-D-glucosylgentiopicroside, sweroside and swertiamarin — and showed they suppressed stimulus-induced superoxide generation in human neutrophils (with gentiopicroside and HGBS also inhibiting PMA- and arachidonic-acid-induced superoxide), via suppression of protein phosphorylation and cytosolic-to-membrane translocation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.3496)
- **Group-level pharmacology review.** Zhang et al. (2018, *Phytomedicine*) reviewed the Qín Jiāo complex including *G. straminea*, documenting iridoid-glycoside chemistry and anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, hepatoprotective and related activities, while calling for proper toxicity and mechanistic evaluation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phymed.2018.04.020)

Evidence grade: **preclinical.** The neutrophil/anti-inflammatory data give a mechanistic basis for the cooling anti-inflammatory tradition, but clinical efficacy in humans is not established.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Chinese Medicine:** Qín Jiāo — bitter, acrid, neutral-to-cool; Stomach, Liver, Gallbladder channels; dispels wind-damp, clears heat, relaxes sinews (see I_315).
- **Tibetan (rGyud-bzhi; Ji-ma):** a cold-natured bitter for liver/gallbladder heat, fever, inflammation and jaundice.
- **Dantian affiliation:** **middle** (Liver/Gallbladder bitter) with joint/sinew channel action.
- **Kenetica Qi/Shen tier:** ◐ **Yin-cool** — cooling, bitter, heat-clearing, damp-resolving.

### Contraindications

As for Qín Jiāo (see I_315): avoid in cold-type / deficiency-cold *bi* without heat and in Spleen/Stomach deficiency-cold with loose stools; caution with marked qi/blood deficiency and in pregnancy [USER: verify]. Isolated secoiridoids are experimental constituents with no established standalone human safety profile.
