# Gizzards of birds — قوانص (*qawāniṣ*)

## *The gizzards (muscular stomachs) of birds, with hens' and cocks' gizzards singled out; a food-and-drug entry (confidence high for the material)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. Source file: `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`.

**Locus (CM).** *Canon* Book II, entry *qawāniṣ* (قوانص), heading at Arabic text line 17736; entry to line 17739 (the next heading is *qūqī*). The Canon uses the plural; the singular is *qāniṣa*.

**What the text says (CM).** Properties: 'Gizzards of birds are much nourishing, and those of hens are not digested quickly' (الخواص: قوانص الطير كثيرة الغذاء والتي للدجاج لا تنهضم بسرعة; lines 17737-17738). Digestive: 'They claim that the inner layer of the gizzard is drying, and benefits the mouth of the stomach and its pain', attributed to **Ibn Māsawayh**, 'especially the gizzards of cocks' (lines 17738-17739). The attribution to Ibn Māsawayh is the Canon's; the work is not on disk. The text is short and gives no temperament.

### Active Components

General biology: the gizzard is a thick muscular organ, mostly protein; the inner lining (the koilin layer) is a hardened protein layer. That is general knowledge, not from the Canon.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The entry records a food and a stomach-mouth remedy from the inner lining. It is not a recommended practice.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation and no dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General fact: poultry gizzard is eaten as food in many cuisines.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No degree of heat or dryness is printed. The only quality is that the inner layer is 'drying' (line 17738).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(b) Framed.* The entry is a drug-by-drug list of qualities and uses inside the *Canon*'s materia medica. It assigns no cultivation status and says nothing of innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruh*. Food, as the text says, is to be treated in the register of nourishment, not of the cultivation faculties.

**Practitioner's note.** A food chapter: the gizzard as nourishing and slow to digest, with a drying inner layer for the stomach mouth. Nothing supports a cultivation use.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Little to map: the entry states that the inner layer of a bird's gizzard is drying and benefits the mouth of the stomach and its pain, a report the Canon attributes to Ibn Masawayh and says is especially true of cocks' gizzards. The text also calls bird gizzards much nourishing, and says hens' gizzards are not digested quickly. The organs named are the mouth of the stomach and the stomach through its pain. The only quality given is the drying of the inner layer, and no temperament, degree, humour, faculty or subtle-body status is named. No preparation or dose is given. The claim is reported on the authority of Ibn Masawayh, whose work is not on disk, so the Codex records it as reported and makes no further body mapping from it.

### Contraindications

Meat-hygiene cautions apply (cook poultry thoroughly). The text states no contraindication; it only says that hens' gizzards digest slowly. [USER: verify]
