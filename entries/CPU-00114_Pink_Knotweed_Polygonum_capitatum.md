### Origin & Tradition

Pink knotweed is a low, creeping knotweed (Polygonaceae) of the Himalaya and southwest China, recognisable by its rounded heads of small pink flowers and often reddish, V-marked leaves. It is an important herb of southwest-Chinese ethnomedicine (notably the Miao tradition) and of Himalayan folk medicine, used chiefly as a **clearer of damp-heat from the urinary tract** — for urinary infection, painful/difficult urination, urinary stones and "lin" (dysuria) syndromes. It is the basis of marketed Chinese herbal urological preparations for these conditions.

In the Chinese medical framework Pink knotweed (**Tou Hua Liao** / the patent-drug herb) is bitter and cooling, used to **clear heat, drain damp-heat, promote urination, resolve stones, cool the blood and resolve toxicity** — applied to urinary tract infection and inflammation, urolithiasis, and topically to sores and inflammatory skin lesions. Its focused urological reputation has made it the subject of modern pharmacological and clinical study for urinary-tract conditions.

The herb is rich in **flavonoids and phenolic acids** (the antioxidant, anti-inflammatory Polygonaceae constituents), consistent with its anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial and diuretic traditional use.
