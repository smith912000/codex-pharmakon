# Carbonate of soda — Suvarchika (Bhishagratna: "Suvarchika", Appendix 3 OCR "Snvarcliika")
## *"Carbonate of Soda", the equation printed in Bhishagratna's Appendix 3; in Sushruta a member of the Saline group and an ingredient of several compounds* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurvedic Tradition (SS).** The tag rests on the Sanskrit name *Suvarchika* in Bhishagratna's English translation of the *Sushruta Samhita*, and on the Appendix 3 equation of that name with "Carbonate of Soda." The Appendix 3 row is `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` line 624 ("Snvarcliika", `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` name line 33044, Latin-column line 33062); its notes field says the pairing was "proposed by a Sonnet agent from the two OCR columns" and checked by script. I read the surrounding columns: the Appendix prints braces for names sharing one entry, and the sequence of names *Sthulaila ... Sushavi*, *Suvarchika*, *Suvarnakshiri*, *Swarna-makshika* lines up with the Latin column "..., Momordica Charantia, Carbonate of Soda, Cleome felina, Iron Pyrites" (lines 33030-33075 of the Vol. III text), so the pairing is supported by position, though not by an explicit printed line. The same Appendix has a second "Carbonate of Soda" row at line 32855, near the name *Sarjikshara* (line 32778); that is a separate name and is not treated here. [USER: verify]

Where the word occurs in Sushruta (Bhishagratna's spelling, OCR variants *Suvarchika* and *Suvarchikd*):

- **Saline group.** *Sutrasthana* ch.42, the chapter on the specific properties of flavours (*Rasa-vishesha-vijnaniya*): "Saline Group: The different kinds of salt such as, Saindhaba, Sauvarchala, Vida, Fakya, Romaka, Samudraka, Paktrima, Yavakshara (nitrate of potash), Ushara and Suvarchika collectively form the Saline group" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` lines 318-322; the same passage is Vol. I line 17015). This is the clearest placement: Suvarchika is one of ten named in the group.
- **Strong alkali.** *Sutrasthana* ch.11, on the preparation of the alkali (*kshara*): alkali "prepared with the addition of the powders of the drugs known as Danti, Dravanti, Chitraka, Langulaki, Putika-Pravala, Talpatri, Vidha, Suvarchika, Kanaka-Kshiri, Hingu, Vacha, and Visha, or with as many of them as are available, each weighing four tolas, is called the strong Kshara (extremely irritating alkali)" (`SS_Sutra_ch11.txt` lines 185-190).
- **In compounds.** *Chikitsasthana* ch.6, a compound of "Danti, Chitraka, Suvarchika and Langali pounded together and made into a paste with cow's bile" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch06.txt` lines 254-256); ch.17, "Oil duly cooked with Suvarchika, Saindhava, Chitraka, Nikumbha, Tali, Nala, Rupika and Apamarga-seeds and with cow's urine" for healing (`SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt` lines 200-201); ch.18, "a paste, composed of sesamum, Suvarchika, Haritala and rock-salt, pounded together and mixed with honey, clarified butter and an abundant quantity of Yava-kshara", applied to a wound for purifying (`SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt` lines 180-183); *Kalpasthana* ch.2, the *Vishari-agada*, with Pippali, Dhyamaka and others "taken with honey", for slow chemical poisoning (`kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch02.txt` lines 358-364); *Kalpasthana* ch.5, the *Tarkshyagada*, among a long list taken "in equal parts and pasted with honey" (`kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch05.txt` lines 395-402).

Whether *Suvarchika* means the same substance in every compound was not stated by the passages read. In the compounds it stands among plant drugs (Danti, Chitraka, Langali), so the lists do not by themselves show that the mineral is meant. In the ch.42 Saline group and the ch.11 alkali context the sense is that of an alkali or salt. [USER: verify]

**Related, not duplicated.** The Codex already holds a separate entry for the alkali *Ushara* (II_50), also named in the ch.42 Saline group; and *Sauvarchala*, black salt, is a different word and belongs under Salt. They are cross-references only.

### Active Components

Stated generally. "Carbonate of soda" is sodium carbonate (Na2CO3), a water-soluble alkaline salt; natural deposits (natron, trona, and salt-lake and efflorescent earths) are mixtures of sodium carbonate, bicarbonate and sodium chloride and sulphate. A solution of sodium carbonate is strongly alkaline, which is why such materials are described as caustic or "irritating". The text read does not say what Sushruta's *Suvarchika* contained, and no composition is asserted. No figures were read in the sources retrieved.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. In Sushruta *Suvarchika* is a salt in the Saline group, an alkali ingredient strengthening *kshara* to the "strong" (extremely irritating) grade, and a component of pastes and oils for ulcers and of antidotal compounds. These are surgical, wound-care and antidotal uses of a caustic material. No modern protocol is supplied, and the strong-alkali use in particular is a clinical procedure and not a self-care practice.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurvedic (SS).** Sushruta's ch.42 places *Suvarchika* in the Saline group (lines 318-322). What the same chapter says of the saline taste in general (lines 216-226) is that it "is possessed of corrective (purgative and emetic) virtues, favours the processes of suppuration and spontaneous bursting of swellings, brings about the looseness or resolution of any affected part (ulcer), is heat-engendering in its property and proves incompatible with all other tastes", and that, if largely partaken of to the exclusion of other tastes, it may bring on scabies, urticaria, oedematous swellings, loss of complexion, loss of virile potency and similar troubles. That is a statement about the saline taste, and it is applied to this salt only by virtue of the group membership. No separate *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for *Suvarchika* in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, with an abstentive caution.* The Sushrutan frame is the tastes and the groups (*rasa*, with the Saline group), and *kshara* as one of the surgeon's tools. *Suvarchika* appears in the group of salts and in compounds, and nowhere in the passages read does it appear in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. The text's own account of the saline taste points toward restraint: taken to excess to the exclusion of other tastes it brings on a list of ills including loss of virile potency (ch.42, lines 224-231). The sattvic, rajasic and tamasic food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here supports taking carbonate of soda for cultivation. It is a salt and alkali of the surgeon's and physician's workshop, graded among the salts, and the tradition's own account of the saline taste is a warning against excess. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Caustic:** sodium carbonate solutions and strong alkalis burn skin and eyes and injure the gullet and stomach if swallowed; the "strong kshara" is described by Sushruta himself as "extremely irritating".
- **Internal use:** not for ingestion except as ordinary food-grade sodium bicarbonate, which is a different compound.
- **Sodium load and acid-base disturbance:** large oral doses of alkaline sodium salts upset blood pH and electrolytes, with particular risk in heart, kidney and liver disease and in pregnancy.
- **Excess of the saline taste:** Sushruta's own list of ills of excess (scabies, urticaria, oedematous swellings, loss of virile potency, mouth and eye inflammation, haemoptysis) is given in ch.42.
- **Identity:** Sushruta's *Suvarchika* may not be a pure sodium carbonate, and several compounds use the word among plant drugs (see Origin & Tradition).
