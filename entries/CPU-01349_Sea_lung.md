# Sea lung (jellyfish) — Pneumon Thalassios, Pulmo marinus, "the lungs of the sea", Dioscorides
## *The material Dioscorides' chapter 2-39 calls the lung of the sea; the translator's gloss reads "Scopelidae, genus Plagyodus or Alepisaurus — Jellyfish family", which names fish genera beside a jellyfish label, and the spine list repeats "Jellyfish family"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* Book 2, the book that opens with animal products, has a short chapter on the sea lung (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 5096-5099; `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` row 229). In full: "2-39. PNEUMON THALASSIOS. SUGGESTED: Scopelidae, genus Plagyodus or Alepisaurus — Jellyfish family. translates as the lungs of the sea. Pulmo marinus pounded into small pieces (whilst it is fresh), and applied, helps those troubled with ulcerated chilblains and other chilblains, and those with gout." The chapter is one sentence. It names one preparation (the fresh substance, pounded and applied), and two complaints (chilblains, ulcerated or not, and gout). It does not describe the animal, give its habitat, say where it is found, or give a dose. The translator's bracket "SUGGESTED:" and the gloss are the translator's, not Dioscorides'.

Other places. The same material appears in the discussion of pompholyx (a zinc oxide product of smelting), 5-85, where Dioscorides lists what it is counterfeited with: "it is counterfeited with bulls' glue, or lambs' lungs, with lungs of the sea [jellyfish], or with burnt green figs and some other similar things. These are easily perceived for none of the previously mentioned tests work on them" (line 10234; the bracket is the translator's). The chapter neighbours 2-40 to 2-42 on the lungs of swine, lamb or bear, the fox, and the liver of an ass (lines 5100-5110), which set the sea lung among the "lungs" in the order of the animal-products book.

**Identity.** The chapter title, "the lungs of the sea", and the Latin *Pulmo marinus* describe something that looks like a lung, and the translator's gloss gives two things at once: a fish family (Scopelidae, with the genera *Plagyodus* and *Alepisaurus*) and "Jellyfish family". Those are not the same kind of animal, and the lines read do not resolve it; the sea-lung label in the pompholyx chapter is glossed "[jellyfish]" by the same translator. The spine list (`_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 220) reads "Jellyfish family" and classes it "plant", which is a source-data error for an animal product. The identity is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

If the material is a jellyfish, its body is mostly water with collagen-type proteins and a small amount of salts, and many species carry stinging cells (nematocysts) that can cause painful skin reactions. If it is a fish of the families named in the gloss, it is muscle protein and fat. No analysis specific to this entry was read, and the identity is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text records the fresh sea lung, pounded and applied, for chilblains and gout. Nothing in the sources supports using any marine animal for these complaints, the animal is not identified, and many jellyfish sting. The Codex does not recommend the material for any complaint and gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives one preparation: the fresh sea lung "pounded into small pieces (whilst it is fresh), and applied" (line 5099). No quantity, frequency or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Many jellyfish deliver painful, sometimes dangerous, stings through nematocysts even when dead or beached. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Dioscorides (DMM).** No quality is printed for the sea lung in 2-39: the chapter says what it helps and nothing about warming, cooling, astringent or other qualities. None is supplied here. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. The sea lung has a single therapeutic chapter and no cultivation status. What the two chapters do show is the discipline of attention that makes up his method: the substance is to be applied "whilst it is fresh" (2-39), and in 5-85 the sea lung is named among the things pompholyx is counterfeited with, a fraud the author says is "easily perceived" because the tests he has just given do not work on it. The chapters are therapeutic and about trade, and neither assigns a cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The sea lung is a one-line remedy for chilblains and gout and a counterfeit in a smelting product, and the animal cannot be identified. The honest use of the entry is to read it as a record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** the animal is not settled (a fish family and "jellyfish" in the same gloss); do not substitute a species on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Stings:** many jellyfish sting and dead or fragmented specimens can still sting; handling an unidentified marine animal is a risk.
- **Hygiene:** a raw marine animal applied to broken skin, such as ulcerated chilblains, can introduce infection.
- **Gout and chilblains:** both need medical assessment; the passage is not a treatment for either.
- **Conservation and ethics:** protected status depends on the species, which is unidentified; the entry takes no position beyond recording the text. [USER: verify]
