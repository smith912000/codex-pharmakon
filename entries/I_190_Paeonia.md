# I-190 · Paeonia (White Peony)
**Paeonia lactiflora** Pall. | Paeoniaceae

---

## Section 1 — Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Accepted name:** *Paeonia lactiflora* Pall. (1776)  
**Pharmacopoeial drug:** *Bai Shao* (白芍, White Peony Root); *Radix Paeoniae Alba*  
**Related pharmacopoeial species:**
- *P. lactiflora* → Bai Shao (白芍): peeled, white root; blood-nourishing, spasmolytic
- *P. lactiflora* + *P. veitchii* → Chi Shao (赤芍): unpeeled, red root; blood-cooling, blood-moving
- *P. suffruticosa* → Mu Dan Pi (牡丹皮): tree peony root bark; blood-cooling, heat-clearing

**These three "Paeonia medicines" are non-interchangeable in TCM despite sharing genus.**

**Common names:** Chinese peony, white peony, lactiflora peony  
**Traditional names:**
- **Bai Shao (白芍):** "White Peony" — from root processing: peeled → white; cooling
- **Chi Shao (赤芍):** "Red Peony" — unpeeled root is red-brown; hotter, blood-moving

**Etymology:**
- *Paeonia* = after Paeon, physician to the Greek gods (mythology); who reportedly used peony to treat Pluto's wounds
- *lactiflora* = Latin *lac* (milk) + *flos* (flower) = milk-flowered; large, creamy white flowers
- Paeoniaceae is its own family (once placed in Ranunculaceae); monogeneric

**Historical name — Paeon's plant:**

The Greek mythological association — Paeon using peony root to treat Pluto's arrow wounds — is encoded in the genus name. This mythological use for wounds and pain is consistent with the actual pharmacology (anti-inflammatory, anti-spasmodic). The painkilling attribution was encoded in the plant's naming at the moment it entered European medical literature.

**Botanical morphology:**
- Perennial herbaceous plant to 90cm; large showy flowers (white to pink to crimson, cultivated forms); 2-ternate compound leaves; 3–5 follicle fruit capsules; massive thickened root system (tuberous, multiple branching roots)
- Root: 5–18cm long cylindrical/tapered pieces; peeled (Bai Shao) = cream-white; unpeeled (Chi Shao) = red-brown; characteristic "eye" markings on cross-section

**Part used:** Dried root; peeled for Bai Shao, unpeeled for Chi Shao; sun-dried after boiling

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**The anti-convulsant plant across civilizations:**

The consistency of peony's anti-convulsant/anti-spasmodic attribution across Greek (Dioscorides), Chinese (*Shennong Bencao Jing*), Arabic (Ibn Sīnā), and Tibetan traditions is pharmacologically supported by paeoniflorin's GABA-A PAM activity and adenosine A1 receptor agonism — both anticonvulsant mechanisms. The traditional "prevents epileptic convulsions" claim, made independently across all four traditions, now has mechanistic pharmacological explanation.

**Paeoniflorin as the most cross-culturally identified terpene glycoside:**

The specific compound paeoniflorin (a monoterpene glycoside unique to Paeonia) is the primary active in all Paeonia species across all traditional applications. This compound was independently associated with: Greek anti-convulsant + uterine, Chinese blood-nourishing + liver-soothing + anti-spasmodic, Arabic anti-spasmodic + menstrual. The convergence of four traditions on the same plant species (or genus) with the same primary compound class and the same primary indications (convulsions, pain, uterus) represents the strongest convergence signal in the Codex for this tier of documentation.

**Liver-soothing — the TCM innovation:**

The TCM-specific addition to the cross-traditional record is "liver-soothing" (*shu gan*, 疏肝) — the concept that peony reduces hepatic qi constraint, caloric frustration, and tension. Modern pharmacology found: paeoniflorin reduces dopamine D2 receptor hypersensitivity (relevant to emotional regulation), lowers cortisol in HPA-stressed animals, and modulates serotonergic signaling. The "liver constraint → emotional tension/frustration" attribution has partial neuroendocrine correlate.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Primary active compounds:**

**1. Monoterpene Glycosides (PRIMARY actives)**
- **Paeoniflorin:** 2–6% dry weight (Bai Shao); PRIMARY pharmacological compound
  - Monoterpene lactone glycoside; unique to Paeoniaceae
  - Multi-target CNS-active: adenosine A1 agonism, GABA-A PAM, NMDA antagonism, Ca²⁺ channel inhibition
  - Peripheral: smooth muscle relaxation; anti-inflammatory; nociception reduction
- **Albiflorin:** Second major monoterpene; 0.3–1.0%; smooth muscle relaxant
- **Oxypaeoniflorin, benzoylpaeoniflorin, galloylpaeoniflorin:** Minor monoterpenes

**2. Phenolics and Gallotannins**
- **Pentagalloylglucose (PGG):** Potent antioxidant; anti-inflammatory; antiviral (inhibits influenza neuraminidase); α-amylase inhibitor
- **Gallic acid, methyl gallate:** Antioxidant; antimicrobial
- 3–7% total tannins

**3. Triterpenoids**
- Oleanolic acid, ursolic acid: anti-inflammatory (same as in Ligustrum)

**4. Benzoic Acid Derivatives**
- Benzoic acid: weak antimicrobial; characteristic smell of peony root

**5. Polysaccharides**
- Bai Shao polysaccharides: immunomodulatory; minor contribution

**Processing chemistry (Bai Shao vs. Chi Shao):**
- Boiling + peeling removes: surface tannins, changes monoterpene profile
- Result: Bai Shao = higher paeoniflorin, lower astringent tannins, more nourishing
- Chi Shao = higher galloylpaeoniflorin, more blood-moving, stronger anti-inflammatory
- Heat processing also converts some paeoniflorin → peonol (different activity)

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**1. Anti-spasmodic / Muscle Relaxant (Paeoniflorin — Primary)**
- Ca²⁺ channel (VGCC) inhibition → reduced smooth muscle Ca²⁺ → relaxation
- Adenosine A1 receptor agonism → inhibition of excitatory neurotransmission → reduced neural stimulation of smooth muscle
- cAMP elevation: inhibition of phosphodiesterase → sustained relaxation signaling
- Targets: uterine smooth muscle, intestinal smooth muscle, vascular smooth muscle

**2. Anticonvulsant / Neuroprotective (Paeoniflorin)**
- GABA-A PAM: positive allosteric modulation → increased chloride influx → neuronal inhibition
- Adenosine A1 agonism: inhibits synaptic glutamate release → reduced excitatory tone
- NMDA receptor antagonism (partial): reduces excitotoxicity
- BDNF upregulation: neuroprotective neurotrophin support
- In animal models: reduces electroshock seizure threshold, pentylenetetrazol convulsions

**3. Analgesic (Central and Peripheral)**
- Central: opioid receptor modulation (mild µ and κ agonism via adenosine pathway)
- Peripheral: PGE₂ synthesis inhibition (COX-2 inhibition)
- Reduction of substance P release from sensory neurons
- Spinal cord: inhibition of wide-dynamic-range neuron activation

**4. Hepatoprotective and "Liver-Soothing" (Paeoniflorin)**
- Cortisol normalization: HPA axis modulation → reduced stress-induced cortisol excess
- Dopamine D2 receptor normalization: relevant to frustration/emotional dysregulation
- Hepatic stellate cell inhibition: anti-fibrotic
- ALT reduction in hepatitis animal models
- NF-κB pathway inhibition in hepatocytes

**5. Anti-inflammatory**
- PGG: strong COX-1/COX-2 inhibition; NF-κB inhibition
- Paeoniflorin: reduces TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 production
- Anti-T-cell proliferation in autoimmune contexts (TH17 reduction)

**6. Immunomodulatory (Autoimmune Context)**
- Paeoniflorin: inhibits T-cell activation in autoimmune conditions
- Reduces Th17 cells, anti-inflammatory; increases Treg (regulatory T cells)
- Research applications: rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, Sjögren's syndrome

**7. Phytoestrogenic (Paeoniflorin + Glycosides)**
- Weak ERβ modulation; relevant for menopausal formula use
- Does NOT produce significant estrogenic uterine stimulation at standard doses

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Autoimmune / Inflammatory Diseases (best evidence):**
- He DY & Dai SM (2011) *Evid-Based Complement Alternat Med* 2011:480890: Review; total glucosides of peony (TGP) — a standardized Bai Shao extract — in autoimmune diseases; 11 RCTs in RA, SLE, Sjögren's syndrome; consistent significant improvements in disease activity scores, reduced inflammatory markers; methodological quality moderate
- TGP (Total Glucosides of Peony): registered pharmaceutical drug in China for RA and autoimmune conditions; 600mg 3× daily; primary active: paeoniflorin + albiflorin + related glucosides
- Chen LX et al (2013) *Int J Mol Sci* 14:7580–603: TGP in lupus (SLE); significant reduction in anti-dsDNA antibodies, improved disease activity

**Dysmenorrhea:**
- Combined with Dang Gui in Si Wu Tang: Zhu X et al (2008) Cochrane; significant dysmenorrhea reduction — joint attribution with other formula herbs

**Liver protection:**
- TGP in autoimmune hepatitis: clinical studies in China; ALT reduction, anti-inflammatory; moderate evidence

**Cognitive / Neuroprotective:**
- Cho J et al (2015): Paeoniflorin + memory models; BDNF upregulation; animal data; no human RCT

**Regulatory:**
- TGP (Total Glucosides of Peony, "Zhengqing Fengtongning"): registered Chinese pharmaceutical drug for autoimmune joint disease — one of the clearest herb → pharmaceutical translations in TCM; paeoniflorin as measurable standardization marker
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020: Bai Shao and Chi Shao both official
- Commission E and EMA: not separately assessed; traditional use classification in EU

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**Paeon — physician to the gods:**

The mythological naming of the peony genus (Paeon, who healed Pluto's wounds from Heracles) places peony in the most exalted medical context of Greek mythology — herb of divine healing. Dioscorides adopted this name, embedding peony in Greek pharmacognosy as a herb for "black nightmare, the illness of the womb, and spasms." The 2,500-year consistency from divine wound-healing myth to modern anti-spasmodic pharmaceutical represents an unusually coherent narrative arc.

**The Shaoyao — ancient Chinese beauty:**

In Chinese poetics, peony (*shaoyao*, the herbaceous species = *P. lactiflora*; and *mudan*, tree peony = *P. suffruticosa*) represents feminine beauty and spring abundance. The Tang dynasty (618–907 CE) elevated tree peony (*mudan*) to the national flower of China; Emperor Xuanzong's love for Yang Guifei was compared to his admiration for peony. The medicinal and aesthetic traditions interweave: the same plant used to "nourish blood and calm the liver" was the symbol of Chinese feminine ideal for a millennium.

**Total Glucosides of Peony (TGP) — the pharmaceutical**:

In the 1980s–1990s, Chinese pharmacologists at the Institute of Materia Medica developed TGP (Zhengqing Fengtongning) as a standardized extract from Bai Shao for rheumatoid arthritis — one of the first registered Chinese herbal pharmaceutical drugs. The development pathway: traditional formula containing Bai Shao → isolation of active fraction (total glucosides) → clinical trials → registration → clinical use in hospital rheumatology. TGP remains in current Chinese clinical practice for RA, SLE, and Sjögren's syndrome as a registered medicine. The paeoniflorin-based drug class represents the most successful modern pharmacological development from a single TCM herb (comparable to artesunate from Artemisia for malaria).

**Paeonia, Greek anti-convulsant:**

Dioscorides prescribes peony root hung around children's necks to prevent epilepsy — a sympathetic-magical use (plant amulet preventing seizure). The modern understanding that paeoniflorin's GABA-A PAM + adenosine A1 agonism does have anticonvulsant properties makes this not merely magical thinking: a child wearing dried peony root would have limited dermal absorption of paeoniflorin, but the tradition preserved an accurate therapeutic attribution through a magically-reasoned delivery system.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

**Primary indications:**
- **Bai Shao (White):** Blood deficiency patterns; dysmenorrhea; spasmodic abdominal pain; night sweats; headache from liver-blood deficiency; muscle cramping; emotional tension ("liver constraint")
- **Chi Shao (Red):** Blood stasis; inflammatory conditions; skin eruptions with heat; chest pain; coagulation/thrombosis contexts

**Standard dosing:**
- **Bai Shao:** 6–30g dried root as decoction daily; most formulas use 9–15g
- **TGP (pharmaceutical):** 600mg 3× daily (0.6g tabs × 3) for autoimmune indications
- **Duration:** 4–8 weeks minimum for blood deficiency; 3–6 months for autoimmune conditions

⚠️ **DRUG INTERACTIONS:**
- **Anticoagulants (warfarin):** Pentagalloylglucose has antiplatelet/anticoagulant activity; monitor INR
- **Anticonvulsant drugs:** GABA-A/adenosine mechanism → additive with benzodiazepines, valproate; may require dose adjustment
- **Muscle relaxants:** Additive smooth muscle relaxation; caution with baclofen, cyclobenzaprine
- **Immunosuppressants:** TGP reduces T-cell activity; additive with cyclosporine, methotrexate in autoimmune use; monitor
- **Aspirin:** Pentagalloylglucose has anti-platelet activity; additive

⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATIONS:**
- **Deficiency cold (TCM: cold extremities, diarrhea, aversion to cold, weak pulse):** Bai Shao is cooling; contraindicated alone; combine with warming herbs if used
- **Full Yang (acute yang excess conditions):** Same reason
- **Pregnancy (Chi Shao specifically):** Blood-moving action → caution/avoid; Bai Shao used in some pregnancy formulas under supervision
- **Aconite (Fu Zi) incompatibility:** Classical "eighteen antagonisms" (十八反) in TCM — Bai Shao is traditionally incompatible with aconite (Fu Zi/Wu Tou); DO NOT combine in formulas (potentiates aconite cardiac glycoside toxicity)

**Standardization:**
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020: minimum 1.6% paeoniflorin (Bai Shao) on dry basis
- TGP (pharmaceutical): paeoniflorin 30–44% of total glucosides
- Quality: Hangzhou Bai Shao (杭白芍, Hangzhou origin) considered finest; daodi material

**In formula context:**
- Si Wu Tang: Dang Gui + Shu Di Huang + Chuan Xiong + Bai Shao (equal parts) — foundational blood tonic formula
- Xiao Yao San (逍遥散): Bai Shao + Chai Hu + Dang Gui + Bai Zhu + Fu Ling + Bo He + Gan Cao — liver-qi constraint formula with emotional-tension/PMS applications; widely used
- Shao Yao Gan Cao Tang (芍药甘草汤): Bai Shao + Gan Cao (licorice); classical formula specifically for muscle cramping; used by Zhongjing Zhang (c.220 CE) — among the oldest documented for spasm

**Energetic tier:** ◈ Jing (structural/constitutional) + ☿ Shen (neuromodulatory) — dual tier; addresses blood/yin constitutional substrate (Jing) while simultaneously modulating neuromuscular function, emotional tension, and convulsive states (Shen)
