### Origin & Tradition

Embelia is a large climbing shrub of the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia whose small dried berries resemble black peppercorns — hence "false black pepper," and the frequent historical adulteration of pepper with them. In Ayurveda the berry is **Vidanga**, and it holds an unrivalled and specific reputation: it is *the* premier **anthelmintic** (worm-expelling) drug of the entire materia medica, the herb named first and foremost whenever intestinal worms and parasites are to be expelled (*krimighna*, "worm-destroying").

Beyond its defining antiparasitic role, Vidanga is a **pungent, bitter, warming, *kapha-vata*-reducing** drug used as a digestive stimulant and carminative (for sluggish digestion, bloating, "ama"/undigested stagnation), for skin diseases, as a mild laxative-detoxifier accompanying worm treatment, and as a component of rejuvenative and metabolic formulations. It is classically given with honey or buttermilk for deworming, often followed by a purgative to expel the dislodged parasites. The Unani tradition (*baobarang*) uses it for the same antiparasitic purpose.

The activity is attributed chiefly to **embelin**, a benzoquinone that is the berry's principal constituent and a documented anthelmintic and antibacterial agent of significant pharmacological interest (including studied antifertility, anti-inflammatory and antitumour activities).
