# I-477 | Motherwort (*Leonurus cardiaca* L.)

## 1. Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Accepted name:** *Leonurus cardiaca* L.  
**Family:** Lamiaceae (Mint family)  
**Common names:** Motherwort, lion's tail, throw-wort, heartwort  
**Synonyms:** *Leonurus cardiaca* subsp. *villosus* (Desf.) Hyl. — the more woolly-leaved European subspecies; *L. artemisia* (Lour.) S.Y.Hu (*yi mu cao*) — Chinese motherwort, closely related species used in TCM

**Etymology:** *Leonurus* from Greek *leon* ("lion") + *oura* ("tail") — the flower spike resembles a lion's tail. *cardiaca* from Greek *kardia* ("heart") — directly encoding the primary cardiovascular application. "Motherwort" — mother's herb, encoding the uterine/gynecological application alongside the cardiac. The genus name and species epithet together encode the plant's two primary therapeutic domains in classical Latin-Greek botanical nomenclature: cardiac + uterine (cardiac = *cardiaca*; uterine = *mater*/mother = motherwort).

**Chinese motherwort (*yi mu cao*, 益母草):** *Leonurus japonicus* Houtt. (= *L. artemisia*) is the primary TCM species. *Yi mu cao* = "beneficial herb for mothers" — directly encoding the gynecological application. The different species (*L. cardiaca* European; *L. japonicus* Asian) share the core alkaloid leonurine and cardiac glycoside framework, but differ in secondary compounds.

**Botanical description:** Erect perennial to 1.2 m; square stems (characteristic Lamiaceae); leaves opposite, palmately lobed (3–7 lobes) with deep sinuses; upper leaves progressively smaller and less divided; white to pale pink or purple 2-lipped flowers in dense axillary whorls (verticillasters); persistent spiny calyx after flowering. Naturalized globally from Eurasian origin; grows on disturbed ground, walls, roadsides.

**Part used:** Aerial parts, harvested at flowering (before seed set); entire flowering tops  
**Preparation:** Tincture (preferred — alkaloids soluble in alcohol); fresh plant juice; infusion (bitter); dried herb capsules

## 3. Convergence Analysis

**Cardiac + uterine dual application — consistent cross-traditional encoding:** The two applications encoded in *Leonurus cardiaca* — cardiac and uterine — appear consistently across CM and SBJ despite the use of different species (*L. cardiaca* in Western Eurasia; *L. japonicus* in East Asia). Both traditions identify the same dual therapeutic domain from independent clinical observation. The shared chemistry (leonurine alkaloid active in both cardiac and uterine smooth muscle) provides mechanistic coherence for this convergence.

**TCM *yi mu cao* priority — gynecology:** In TCM, *yi mu cao* is primarily a gynecological herb — menstrual irregularity, dysmenorrhea, amenorrhea, postpartum blood stasis, lochia. Its cardiac use is secondary. In European tradition, the cardiac application is primary and the uterine application secondary. This priority inversion across traditions may reflect different patient populations (China: female practitioners and female medicine more culturally developed; Europe: cardiac disease more prevalent in documented patients) rather than pharmacological difference.

**The "beneficial herb for mothers" — three traditions' consensus on women's medicine:** CM, SBJ, and European folk medicine all encode motherwort as specifically indicated for women's reproductive health. This cross-continental convergence on a gynecological herb predates endocrinology, uterine physiology, or any understanding of smooth muscle pharmacology — suggesting careful empirical observation of clinical outcomes across independent cultural contexts.

## 4. Phytochemical Composition

### Alkaloids (primary cardiovascular and uterine actives)
- **Leonurine** (principal alkaloid; guanidine derivative; 0.01–0.15% aerial parts; positive chronotropic + inotropic cardiac effect at low dose; uterotonic at higher dose; vasodilatory via endothelial NO release)
- **Stachydrine** (proline betaine; principal alkaloid by concentration; anti-platelet; anti-apoptotic in cardiomyocytes; uterine stimulant; ACE inhibitor-like activity)
- **Betonicine** (betaine alkaloid; minor)
- **Turicine** (betaine; minor)

**Leonurine pharmacological significance:** Unique to *Leonurus* genus; guanidine structure; cardiac effects at low dose (positive chronotropy, inotropy); uterine smooth muscle stimulation at higher dose; the dose-response curve makes precise dosing important — low dose: cardiac tonic; high dose: uterotonic/abortifacient.

### Iridoids
- **Leonuride** (iridoid glycoside; cardiac protective; anti-inflammatory)
- **8-Epiajugoside** (Lamiaceae-characteristic iridoid)

### Diterpenes
- **Leocardin** (labdane diterpene; anti-platelet; anti-inflammatory)
- **Preleoheterin** (labdane; smooth muscle relaxant at specific concentrations)

### Flavonoids
- **Quercetin** and glycosides (anti-inflammatory; cardioprotective)
- **Rutin** (quercetin-3-rutinoside; capillary protective; anti-inflammatory)
- **Hyperoside** (quercetin-3-galactoside; cardiac protective)
- **Luteolin** (anti-inflammatory; vasodilatory)
- **Genkwanin** (5-hydroxy-7-methoxyflavone; anti-inflammatory)

### Phenolic Acids
- **Caffeic acid derivatives** (antioxidant; anti-inflammatory)
- **Chlorogenic acid** (antioxidant)
- **Rosmarinic acid** (present; anti-inflammatory; characteristic of Lamiaceae)

### Tannins
- **Lamiaceae tannins** (moderate; astringent)

## 5. Mechanisms of Action

### Cardiovascular (Leonurine + Stachydrine + Flavonoids)
**Cardioprotective — multiple convergent pathways:**
- **Leonurine:** at low concentrations (10⁻⁹–10⁻⁷ M), positive chronotropic (increases HR) + positive inotropic (increases contractile force) → cardiac tonic effect
- **Leonurine:** at higher concentrations, calcium channel antagonism → vasodilation → BP reduction → negative chronotropy (dose-biphasic response critical)
- **Stachydrine:** ACE-like inhibition → angiotensin II reduction → vasodilation; anti-ischemic (protective in myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury models)
- **Stachydrine:** anti-apoptotic in cardiomyocytes (PI3K/Akt pathway; Bcl-2 upregulation)
- **Flavonoids (quercetin, rutin):** Antioxidant protection of cardiac mitochondria; COX inhibition reduces inflammatory cytokines damaging cardiac tissue
- **Rosmarinic acid:** NF-κB inhibition → ↓cardiac inflammatory cytokines

**Anti-platelet / anti-thrombotic:**
- Stachydrine: inhibits ADP-induced platelet aggregation (IC₅₀ ~0.1 mM)
- Leocardin diterpene: additional anti-platelet mechanism
- Relevant for cardiovascular risk reduction

### Uterine Stimulation (Leonurine + Stachydrine)
- Leonurine: direct uterine smooth muscle contraction (oxytocin receptor sensitization; voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channel activation → myometrial contraction)
- Stachydrine: uterotonic at higher concentrations
- Traditional postpartum use: promotes uterine involution, clears lochia; mechanistically coherent with oxytocin receptor sensitization

### Anxiolytic / Sedative (Minor)
- Iridoid and flavonoid fraction: mild GABA-A modulation (weaker than Skullcap)
- Traditional use as mild sedative for anxiety with palpitations ("nervous heart")
- The anxiolytic + cardiac effect combination is appropriate for "cardiac neurosis" (anxiety manifesting as palpitations)

### Anti-inflammatory
- Rosmarinic acid (characteristic Lamiaceae compound): potent NF-κB inhibitor; 5-LOX inhibitor; cyclooxygenase inhibitor
- Quercetin, luteolin: COX-1/2, 5-LOX inhibition
- Relevant for cardiac inflammation context

## 6. Clinical Evidence

**Cardiac / palpitations:**
- Chinese clinical trials (RCTs) for *yi mu cao* in cardiac arrhythmia, angina, and heart failure: positive results in multiple small RCTs — Level B (quality concerns: blinding, allocation concealment)
- European Commission E (Germany) approval: **nervous cardiac complaints** — palpitations with anxiety; Level C
- Xu et al. 2015 meta-analysis: *yi mu cao* in angina — 16 RCTs; significant improvement in clinical symptoms and ECG changes vs control — Level B (meta-analysis of Chinese trials)

**Gynecological:**
- Chinese RCTs for *yi mu cao* in dysmenorrhea, postpartum uterine contraction: multiple positive trials — Level B
- Traditional gynecological use extremely well-documented; Commission E monograph recognizes menstrual complaints

**Hypertension:**
- Small RCTs (Chinese literature): *yi mu cao* extract in mild hypertension — modest BP reduction — Level B
- Stachydrine ACE-like activity provides mechanistic basis

**Summary by evidence level:**
- Level A (high-quality RCT/meta-analysis): Limited from Western perspective; Chinese meta-analyses (quality variable)
- Level B: Multiple Chinese RCTs (cardiac, gynecological, hypertension); meta-analysis (cardiac)
- Level C: Commission E Germany — nervous cardiac complaints; gynecological (menstrual irregularity)
- Level D: Leonurine and stachydrine mechanism studies; extensive pre-clinical

## 7. Phenomenological Record

**Nervous heart — the mind-body cardiac archetype:** Motherwort's traditional indication — "nervous heart complaints" (Commission E) — describes a clinical syndrome familiar in pre-20th-century medicine: anxiety-driven palpitations, the physical manifestation of mental distress in the cardiovascular system. Before beta-blockers and SSRIs, motherwort was the standard European remedy for this syndrome. The dual mechanism (anxiolytic flavonoid/iridoid + cardiac regulatory alkaloid leonurine) creates a pharmacologically coherent treatment for a mind-body condition — the exact pharmacological combination needed for anxiety-palpitation syndrome.

**The postpartum herb across Eurasia:** *Yi mu cao* (益母草) is synonymous in Chinese medical tradition with postpartum recovery — it is taken in the first weeks after childbirth to promote uterine involution, clear retained blood (lochia), and restore menstrual regularity. European motherwort serves the same function in a parallel but independently developed tradition. The convergence of this very specific postpartum indication (not just "uterine herb" generally) suggests both precision of traditional observation and genuine pharmacological specificity — the uterotonic leonurine and stachydrine have exactly the activity pattern needed for postpartum uterine involution support.

**Lion's tail and the heraldic herb:** The *Leonurus* genus name — lion's tail — combined with the plant's use for the "lion-hearted" (the brave or the anxious with the pounding heart) creates a metaphorical coherence in medieval European herbalism. The plant's association with courage (lion), heart (cardiaca), and mothers (motherwort) gave it a culturally rich identity that may have amplified its use. Culpeper associated it with Leo the lion and the heart.

**The bitterest Lamiaceae:** Motherwort's taste is intensely bitter — more bitter than most Lamiaceae members, which tend to be aromatic and mildly bitter at most. This bitterness (from alkaloids and iridoids) created a barrier to culinary use, keeping motherwort firmly in the medicinal domain. It was never used as a culinary herb or in food, only as medicine — distinguishing it from most aromatic Lamiaceae (mint, rosemary, thyme, lavender) that cross the food-medicine boundary.

## 8. Practical Application

**Energetic tier:** ◉ Qi (cardiovascular functional; smooth muscle regulatory) + ☿ Shen (anxiolytic; nervous cardiac)

### Preparation Methods and Dosing

**Tincture (preferred for alkaloid extraction):**
- Fresh plant or dried: 1:5, 60% ethanol
- 2–4 mL, 3× daily
- For palpitations with anxiety: 2 mL acute dose

**Dried herb infusion:**
- 2 g dried herb per 250 mL water; steep 10 min (bitter; often combined with honey or peppermint)
- 1–3 cups daily
- Commission E dose: 4.5 g herb per day

**Standardized extract (stachydrine ≥2%):**
- 300–500 mg, 3× daily for cardiac indication

**TCM *yi mu cao* decoction:**
- 10–15 g dried aerial parts per 500 mL; simmer 20 min
- For gynecological indications: 3 cups per day; may be used from end of menstrual period through cycle

### Drug Interactions

- **Cardiac medications:** Additive effects with anti-arrhythmics, cardiac glycosides (digoxin), and calcium channel blockers — monitor cardiac rate and rhythm
- **Anticoagulants (warfarin, heparin):** Stachydrine anti-platelet + possible anticoagulant effects; INR monitoring; 2-week discontinuation before surgery
- **Antihypertensives:** Additive BP reduction
- **Oxytocin:** Additive uterine stimulation — do not combine

### Contraindications

**ABSOLUTE:**
- **Pregnancy** — leonurine and stachydrine uterotonic/abortifacient; documented risk of miscarriage at medicinal doses; ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION
- **Active bleeding disorders** — antiplatelet effect

**Relative:**
- **Bradycardia, heart block** — leonurine dose-dependent cardiac effects; monitor in existing bradycardia
- **Hypothyroidism** — stachydrine may alter thyroid hormone metabolism (theoretical; monitor)
- **Nursing infants** — stachydrine passes into breast milk; theoretical infant effects

### Quality Markers

- Leonurine content ≥0.05% (aerial parts dry weight; HPLC)
- Stachydrine content ≥0.5% (primary alkaloid by concentration)
- Characteristic bitter taste; square-stemmed with whorled flowers (botanical authentication)
- Chinese species (*L. japonicus*): verify for *yi mu cao* applications
