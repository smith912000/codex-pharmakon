# Medicago lupulina — Busuhang, 'bu su hang; Clark hbu.su.hang (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Medicago lupulina L., the binomial the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints directly beside Busuhang (English name "Blue clover"); Clark 1995 prints other names for hbu.su.hang (Lotus corniculata, Medicago sativa, Trigonella pubescens) and not this one* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi, read in two modern editions, and on a binomial that only one of the two editions prints.** The editions are Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, both in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. They are two translations of one tradition's text and are not two independent traditions. A search of the Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Sushruta texts on disk for *lupulina* found nothing. The Dioscorides translation has the name once, in a translator's gloss: the chapter 4-113, *Kutisos* (heading at line 9043 of `Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`), has SUGGESTED lines (9047 to 9049) that include "Medicago lupulina [Linnaeus] — Black Medick, Hop Clover, Yellow Trefoil", and the chapter's own text on the shrub *kutisos* follows at line 9050. That is Dioscorides' drug under a modern suggestion and is not read here as a mention of the Tibetan drug (see Overlap). The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (line 246) the key *Medicago lupulina* is taken from the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary line 5126 with the note "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)". The key is the only binomial the glossary prints for the name, and it is **not among the names Clark prints** for the corresponding line. Clark's row for *hbu.su.hang* (names.csv line 145, Clark line 1034) is keyed to the first of three names, *Lotus corniculata* (printed so, and repaired to *Lotus corniculatus* for the spine), and the second is *Medicago sativa*, a different species of the same genus. This entry therefore follows the glossary against Clark.

**Identity doubt.** The two editions disagree on the plant. Men-Tsee-Khang prints *Medicago lupulina* L.; Clark prints a *Lotus*, a *Medicago* of another species and a *Trigonella*, three genera that the Codex understands to be legumes (not checked against a flora). Clark's commentary says both the female and male types have "yellow flowers", and the glossary's English name for the place is "Blue clover"; the Codex records the difference and does not resolve it. The Codex cannot say which plant the Tibetan texts meant, and the entry stands for the Tibetan drug. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (the "B" block, lines 5057 to 5160).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients. The last row of the "B" run prints its three columns in consecutive lines: "Busuhang" (line 5122), "'bu su hang" (line 5124) and "Medicago lupulina L." (line 5126). The pairing is therefore not by counting a separate column; it is printed in sequence for this row, though the glossary does not print it as one line. The English names of the block are one line (5157), "Indian aconite Yellow wolfsbane Blue clover", and "Blue clover" is the last English name of the block, as Busuhang is its last row; the glossary does not print an English name for every row of the block, so this is the closing place and not a count. The transliteration "'bu su hang" is the spelling of Clark's *hbu.su.hang*, in a different transliteration scheme. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text.* Chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225), within the run headed "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (line 3428). Line 3433 ends a run of short sentences: "Busuhang heals wounds and cures lung disorders." The page number 207 is printed at line 3434, and the index lists "Busuhang 207" (line 7283). The text gives no Latin name, no taste and no part used.

*Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1034).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20 ("The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines", line 792), within "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (heading at line 997). It reads:

"hbu.su.hang: Lotus corniculata: Medicago sativa: Trigonella pubescens: [has three names and grows in fields. It has a glossy seed pod and cures fevers of the kidneys, lungs and heart. The female type has a rough leaf and stalk, and yellow flowers and seed pods. The male type has slender spreading petioles, small round green leaves, yellow flowers and is without pods. Both types] heal wounds and cure lung diseases."

Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries. Inside the brackets (the commentary layer) are the three names, the field habitat, the seed pod, the fevers of the kidneys, lungs and heart, and the descriptions of the female and male types. Outside them (the root-text layer, as far as Clark's brackets mark it) are the Tibetan name, the three identifications, which are Clark's and not the root text's, and the clause "heal wounds and cure lung diseases". Clark's name index lists *hbu.su.hang* (line 1725) and the Latin index lists *Medicago sativa* (line 1614). Clark's "heal wounds and cure lung diseases" and Men-Tsee-Khang's "heals wounds and cures lung disorders" agree in kind and nearly in words; the two translations are of one tradition's text. The Codex reads "Busuhang" as *hbu.su.hang* from the spelling, the transliteration and the agreeing actions. The line gives no part used and no preparation.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Medicago lupulina*, *Busuhang* and *hbu.su.hang* found no entry on this drug. I_599 (Alfalfa, *Medicago sativa*, tags AT, CM, DMM, SBJ, SS) and I_593 (Bird's-foot trefoil, *Lotus corniculatus*, tags AT, CM, DMM) are the entries of Clark's second and first binomials for the same Tibetan line (1034); neither carries the FMT tag or cites Clark line 1034 or the Men-Tsee-Khang sentence, so the Tibetan attestation of both rests on this passage and is a candidate tag addition, not applied here. The Dioscorides gloss for 4-113 *Kutisos* (lines 9047 to 9049) gives *Medicago lupulina* as a modern suggestion for Dioscorides' *kutisos*, a different drug in a different text; it bears on the Codex's spine (a possible DMM attestation for this binomial) and is flagged, not applied. I_1121 (*Lotus tetragonolobus*) is a different species. *Trigonella pubescens* has no entry. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Medicago*, *Lotus* and *Trigonella* are genera of the legume family (Fabaceae), that *Medicago lupulina* is a small clover-like plant with yellow flower heads and kidney-shaped coiled pods (the English name black medick), and that legumes characteristically fix nitrogen through root nodules. Clark's commentary describes the Tibetan plant's leaf, petiole, flowers and pod and not its chemistry. Which plant the Tibetan texts mean is not settled by the lines read, so no statement about this drug's constituents is made.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a drug that "heal[s] wounds and cure[s] lung diseases" (Clark, line 1034) and "heals wounds and cures lung disorders" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3433); Clark's commentary adds "fevers of the kidneys, lungs and heart". These are the texts' claims in their own terms and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose, and does not translate "lung diseases" or "fevers" into modern diagnoses. Wounds and lung disease need medical assessment.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica gives this drug by what it does: it heals wounds and cures lung diseases, and the Men-Tsee-Khang text says it heals wounds and cures lung disorders. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. A search of both Tibetan texts for the drug's names found it only in the chapter 20 lines cited above, the glossary and the indexes, and not in the rejuvenation chapter (chapter 23 in both editions); no line read places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice. Its framing is therapeutic only, and it attaches to the Tibetan name, not securely to this binomial.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a wound and lung drug in the Tibetan texts, with no preparation and no dose, and the plant behind the Tibetan name differs between the two editions. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. Wounds and lung disease need medical assessment; no preparation or dose is given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Because the identity is uncertain, a plant could not be matched to the Tibetan name from this entry in any case. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read.
