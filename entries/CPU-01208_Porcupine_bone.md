# Porcupine bone — rGyud-bzhi, zer.mo, "Porcupine bone" (bone, one of the thirteen animal-derived medicines)
## *Bone of the porcupine; Clark prints the Tibetan name zer.mo and the binomial Pucrasia macrolopha, which does not obviously fit a porcupine* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Porcupine bone is listed in the Explanatory Tantra, chapter 20, the chapter on the efficacy of medicinal substances, in Clark 1995 (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). It falls in section "(8) Medicines of Animal Origin" (line 1147), whose thirteen headings are "horn, bone, meat, blood, bile, fat, brain, skin, nails, hair, urine, droppings and whole body" (line 1148). Under "Bone" (heading line 1159) the long line 1160 reads, for the relevant clause: "zer.mo: Porcupine bone: Pucrasia macrolopha: [has six names and] is hemostatic." The clause gives the Tibetan word *zer.mo*, a name count of six, and one action, hemostatic (it stops bleeding). It stands between "Pig bone: ... It cures brown phlegm. Sheep bone: cures (all types of) wind disorders. Bone from the head of a sheep's femur (thigh bone) dispels urinary retention" and "spre.hu: Monkey bone: [has seven names. It is ecbolic [and abortifacient]". The bracketed words in the list are Clark's interpolation; he says in his introduction (line 80) that he has "interpolated liberally from the Blue Aquamarine commentary", but the lines read do not state the bracket convention outright. [USER: verify]

The binomial Clark prints, *Pucrasia macrolopha*, is the scientific name of the koklass pheasant, a bird, not a porcupine (a rodent of the family Hystricidae or Erethizontidae). The text gives both the English "Porcupine" and that binomial for *zer.mo*, and the lines read do not explain the mismatch; it may be a misprint, a transposition from a neighbouring entry, or the translator's identification of the Tibetan word. [USER: verify] The substance is entered here as the Tantra's "Porcupine bone" and no identification beyond the printed English is claimed.

The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, line 3523) agrees on the action: "Porcupine bone stops bleeding." It prints no Tibetan name or binomial for it in the line read. The Tibetan wording is not in the files read.

The porcupine also appears in the Tantra's classification of meats, as one of the "burrowing animals" with "the marmot (Marmota bobak), porcupine, frog, snake, Tibetan badger, lizard (Eremias argus), gecko, and scorpion" (Clark, line 672; Men-Tsee-Khang, line 2918, "The marmot, porcupine, frog, snake, badger, lizard, mountain-dwelling lizard and scorpion are the group of animals that live in burrows"). That is a statement about the animal in the meat classification, not about its bone, and no property of porcupine flesh is printed in the lines read. The Men-Tsee-Khang index lists "Porcupine 213" (line 7469). Its "Glossary on Animals" (running heads at lines 6808-7114) prints "Porcupine" with the binomial "Hystrix hodqsoni" (lines 7041-7047, an OCR form of *Hystrix hodgsoni*, a porcupine), which supports the animal being a true porcupine and not the bird of Clark's binomial; the glossary columns are scrambled in the file, so the pairing of name and binomial is probable, not certain. ### Active Components

Bone is a mineralised connective tissue made of calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite) in a collagen framework, with marrow spaces and a small amount of other minerals and proteins. No active principle is claimed by the sources, and no composition specific to this entry was read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced content is one action in a materia medica list: porcupine bone is hemostatic (Clark) or "stops bleeding" (Men-Tsee-Khang). The Codex offers no protocol. Bleeding that does not stop is an emergency for a clinician, and applying animal bone or powder to a wound is an infection risk (see Contraindications). The Tantra gives no preparation, dose or route in these lines. The identification is also unsettled because of the binomial. [USER: verify]

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation, route or dose is given in the sources retrieved: the Clark and Men-Tsee-Khang lines state the action only. [USER: verify] No compound containing porcupine bone was read.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Bleeding is stopped by direct pressure, compression, clotting and, where needed, surgical or medical measures; bone powder is not a recognised haemostatic. Powdered animal bone can carry bacteria and spores, including those of tetanus and anthrax in some settings, and should not be put on an open wound. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(c) Absent.* Neither translation read assigns porcupine bone any cultivation framing. It is a short action in a list of animal bones. The frame around the list is the tradition's own: the project's cultivation spec records that the three *nyes pa* (*rlung*, *mkhris pa*, *bad kan*) are derived from the three poisons and that medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root. That point comes from the spec and not from any line about porcupine bone. No source read attaches porcupine bone to *bcud len* (essence extraction), and there is no formal Tibetan category of substances for subtle-body practice. Clark's remark (line 57) that "only very few animal products are used" nowadays is a note on usage, not on status.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this. It is a one-line action in a list of animal bones, the identification is uncertain, and the substance carries no cultivation claim. Its use today is as a record of what the Tantra says.

### Contraindications

- **Infection:** powdered or raw bone applied to a wound can introduce bacteria and spores; do not apply it.
- **Not a treatment for bleeding:** significant or persistent bleeding is an emergency; do not rely on this line.
- **Legal and conservation status:** porcupines are protected or regulated in some countries, and trade in their parts may be unlawful. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** killing a wild animal for a substance with no supported benefit is hard to justify.
