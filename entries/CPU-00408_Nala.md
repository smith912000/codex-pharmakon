### Origin & Tradition

**Identity note.** The spine names this reed *Phragmites karka*, and the Latin printed in the glossary agrees. The Sushruta word is short and has lookalikes, which is where doubt lies. *Nalada* (Anjanadi group, Sutra ch.38, line 302) is a different drug, which the glossary pairs with "See 'Us'ira.'" (line 32434) and not with this reed. *Nala-setu* (Chikitsa ch.30, `SS_Chikitsa_ch30.txt`, line 230: "the Oshadhi of the Vegavati species grows on the Malaya hills and on the Nala-setu") is a place. A footnote at Chikitsa ch.17 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt`, lines 221 to 223) records that the commentator Sivadasa quotes the line with "Nili-Nala" in place of "Tala-Nala", so the reading of the word is not fixed in every manuscript. The finished entry `I_619_CommonReed_Phragmites_australis.md` (tagged SBJ, AT, DMM, EP, CM) covers a different species of the same genus and carries no SS tag. This entry does not edit it. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).**

*The ganas (Sutra ch.38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`).* Nala stands in two groups.

- The Viratarvadi group (lines 57 to 64): "The drugs known as Virataru, the two kinds of Sahachara, Darbha, Vrikshadani, Gundra, I\'ala [OCR, read as Nala], Kusha, Kasha, Ashmabhedaka, Agnimantha, Morata Vasuka, Vasira, Bhalluka, Kuruntaka, Indivara, Kapotavanka, and Shvadanstra enter into the composition of the group known as the Viratarvadi." The metrical text (lines 66 to 73) says the group "subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles." The name here is damaged in the OCR and read as Nala by position and by its place beside Kusha and Kasha, which is an inference. [USER: verify]
- The Pancha-Trina group (lines 531 to 539): "The group consisting of the five medicinal herbs (of the grass species) and known as Kusha, Kasha, Nala, Darbha, Kandekshuka, is called the Pancha-Trina." The metrical text reads "Cases of Haemoptysis, renal defects or of uninary diseases are found to speedily yield to the curative efficacy of the compound internally administered through the medium of cow's milk." The summary at the end of the chapter (lines 545 to 552) says that the Trina-Panchamula, "standing in the bottom of the list", "is endued with the property of killing the deranged Pittam".

Membership of these groups is a grouping by therapeutic action, grade (b) at most.

*Appendix 3 glossary (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`).* "Nala" is printed at line 32372 on the page of N-names. The names on that page run on from "Nala" through "Nalada", "Nandi", "Narikela", "Nichula", "Nidigdhika", "Nikumbha" and onward, and the page's Latin block begins at line 32408. Counted down from the end of the block, "Nyagrodha" pairs with "Ficus Bengalensis" (line 32456), and counted back through the names to "Nala" (line 32372) the pairings are "Nandi" with "Ficus Cingalensis." (line 32436), "Nalada" with 'See "Us\'ira."' (line 32434) and "Nala" with "Phragmites Karka." (line 32432). The count was checked at several names along the way and agrees. The pairing is by OCR column position, not by a printed link.

*Passages read, in the order of the book.*

- **Sutra ch.46** (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 2874 to 2878), on drinks taken after foods: "The wine of Durva, Nala, or of Vetra should be taken after pungent fruits."
- **Chikitsa ch.5** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt`, lines 203 to 208), in the chapter on the great Vata disorders: "Pradeha (plaster) composed of Shali, Shashtika, Nala, Vanjula, Talisha, Shringataka, Galodya, Haridra, Gairika, Shaivala, Padma-kashtha, leaves of padma (lotus), pasted with Dhanyamla and mixed with clarified butter, should be applied to the affected part. This plaster (Pradeha) may be applied lukewarm even in cases of Vata-Rakta, marked by a preponderance of the aggravated Vayu."
- **Chikitsa ch.17** (`SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt`, lines 183 to 203, with the footnote at 221 to 223), in the treatment of the Kaphaja type of *Nadi* (a sinus), after it has been poulticed, opened by the surgeon and washed: "Oil duly cooked with Suvarchika, Saindhava, Chitraka, Nikumbha, Tali, Nala, Rupika and Apamarga-seeds and with cow's urine should be used for healing purposes."
- **Kalpa ch.8** (`SS_Kalpa_ch08.txt`, lines 630 to 634), in the chapter on insect bites, for the bite of a *Svetaka* (white) spider: "An Agada consisting of Chandana, Rasna, Ela, Harenu, Nala, Vanjula, Kushtha, Lamajjaka, Chakra and Nalada is efficacious in such a case." The signs that the text gives for this bite are white pustules with itching, burning, fits, fever, erysipelas and pain.
- **Uttara ch.26** (`SS_Uttara_ch26.txt`, lines 64 to 68), in the chapter on head diseases (*Siro-roga*), for the Pittaja and Raktaja types: "Plaster for the head should be prepared with Nala, Vetasa, Kahlara (red lotus), Chandana, Utpala, Shankha (conch-shell), Shaivala, Yashti-madhu, Musta and lotus taken together and mixed with clarified butter."
- **Uttara ch.39** (`SS_Uttara_ch39.txt`, lines 1158 to 1161), on fever: "A potion of the decoction duly prepared with the roots of Nala and of Vetasa (cane) and Murva and Devadaru would prove remedial to this form of fever" (the *Tridoshaja* type).
- **Uttara ch.58** (`SS_Uttara_ch58.txt`, lines 228 to 233), on suppression of urine: "a paste of Nala, Ikshu, Darbha, Ashmabheda and the seeds of Trapusha and Ervaruka, should be duly washed in milk and filtered and taken in combination with clarified butter."

No rasayana or vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) was found to name Nala as a plant, so no grade (a) status is claimed.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Sushruta records Nala as one of the Trina-Panchamula grasses, a wine, a plaster ingredient, an oil ingredient for sinuses, an ingredient of a spider-bite antidote, a head plaster, a fever decoction (the root) and a paste for urinary retention. These are the text's own claims and not a Codex protocol. The Codex gives no protocol for this reed.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted above: a wine (Sutra ch.46), plasters mixed with ghee (Chikitsa ch.5; Uttara ch.26), an oil cooked with cow's urine (Chikitsa ch.17), an agada (Kalpa ch.8), a root decoction (Uttara ch.39) and a paste washed in milk and taken with ghee (Uttara ch.58). No quantity is given for Nala in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** The text prints no *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* for Nala in the lines read. Sutra ch.38 (lines 545 to 552) says the Pancha-Trina group, to which it belongs, "kills the deranged Pittam", and the Viratarvadi group (lines 66 to 73) "subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata". Uttara ch.26 uses it in a cooling head plaster with lotus, Utpala and sandal for Pittaja and Raktaja head disease.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Nala is a named reed in the Viratarvadi and Pancha-Trina groups, a wine in the drinks chapter, and an ingredient in plasters, oils, an agada, a fever decoction and a urinary paste. The Sushruta frame for it is therapeutic. The ch.38 gana is a grouping by action, not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. Nothing read ties Nala to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*, and none of the rasayana or vajikarana chapters searched (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) names it as a plant. The status is unattested, and the identification with *Phragmites karka* rests on an OCR-paired appendix. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this reed. In Sushruta it is a grass of a Pitta-quieting group and an ingredient in a physician's plasters, oils and decoctions. The abstentive answer is the correct one: any use is a matter for a qualified practitioner, and the text supplies no long-term regimen.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. The sinus, spider-bite, fever and urinary-retention uses above are conditions needing medical care, and none of them is advice. A spider bite with fits, fever or spreading rash needs urgent medical care, and anyone who cannot pass urine needs it at once. Do not put reed material into a sinus or wound. Reed beds are wildlife habitat and are protected in some places, so check local rules before harvesting. Do not use in pregnancy.
