# Gentiana triflora — spang.rgyan.dkar.po, Pang gyen karpo (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Gentiana triflora, the second of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for spang.rgyan.dkar.po (the first is Gentiana algida Pall, white type); the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text prints Gentiana algida for the same Tibetan name and does not print Gentiana triflora* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one printed source for the binomial.** Of the two Tibetan texts on disk (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`), only Clark 1995 prints *Gentiana triflora* (lines 1018 and, in the index of Latin names, 1590). A search of Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 for "triflora" found nothing. No other text read for this entry names the plant: a search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Sushruta texts on disk (including the three Bhishagratna volumes) for the genus name *Gentiana* found Dioscorides 3-3 GENTIANE (line 6541, glosses *Gentiana lutea* and Yellow Gentian) and Yang 1998's Long Dan (Radix Gentianae Scabrae, line 1936) and Qin Jiao (Radix Gentianae Macrophyllae, line 2494). These are other species and are not attributed to this row. Clark's preface (line 83) says that more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (row 137) the Tibetan name *spang.rgyan.dkar.po* is recorded at Clark line 1018 with the identification "Gentiana algida Pall (white type); Gentiana triflora". The key for this spine row, *Gentiana triflora*, is therefore the second of the two names, not the first. The first name is the key of the existing entry I_1286 (*Gentiana algida*). Both entries rest on the same single Clark line.

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1018).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, within "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (heading at line 997). It reads:

"spang.rgyan.dkar.po: Gentiana algida Pall (white type): Gentiana triflora: [has a bitter taste, small elongated green leaves and a white, blue or striped flower]. It cures throat disorders, fever caused by poisoning [and infectious fever]."

Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries. Inside the brackets (commentary layer) are the bitter taste, the leaves, the flower and the clause "and infectious fever". Outside them (root-text layer, as far as Clark's brackets mark it) are the Tibetan name, the two Latin names, which are Clark's identifications and not the root text's, and the sentence "It cures throat disorders, fever caused by poisoning". Clark prints no separate description for *Gentiana triflora*; the line gives it only as the second name of a drug already identified as *Gentiana algida* (white type). The flower description "white, blue or striped" is the only place the line allows more than one flower colour, and the Codex does not read it as a statement about *G. triflora* in particular.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011.* The Tibetan name appears there as "Pang gyen karpo": line 3432 reads "Pang gyen karpo cures laryngitis and hot disorder associated with poisoning." Line 3605 lists "Pang gyen" among "the group of medicines that cure poisoning". The glossary prints, on one line (5584), "Pang gyen karpo spang rgyan dkar po Gentiana algida". The Men-Tsee-Khang text therefore supports the Tibetan name and its action, and gives *Gentiana algida*, not *Gentiana triflora*. The Codex does not treat *laryngitis* and *throat disorders* as one term.

**Overlap.** I_1286 (*Gentiana algida*) is the entry on this Tibetan name and quotes the same Clark line 1018 and the Men-Tsee-Khang lines 3432, 3605 and 5584; this entry covers the other binomial on that line and adds no source that I_1286 lacks. It is not merged. I_113 (Gentian, *Gentiana*) is a general entry from other traditions. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Gentiana* is a genus of the gentian family (Gentianaceae), and that bitter secoiridoid glycosides, of which gentiopicroside is an example, are characteristic of the family; Clark's commentary does record a "bitter taste". Nothing here says what the Tibetan drug contained or which part was used.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a drug that "cures throat disorders, fever caused by poisoning" (Clark, line 1018) and "cures laryngitis and hot disorder associated with poisoning" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3432). These are the texts' claims and not Codex protocols. Poisoning is an emergency and the Codex offers no advice for it. Because the binomial *Gentiana triflora* rests on one translator's identification, the Codex does not recommend any plant under this name.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation, no part used, no quantity, no frequency and no duration. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] Clark's commentary describes taste, leaves and flower and does not say which part is taken.

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Tibetan terms ("throat disorders", "fever caused by poisoning", "infectious fever") are not modern diagnoses or measured outcomes, so no modern finding can be set beside them. The bitterness of gentians is a general, well-established property of the genus.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Clark's commentary layer (line 1018) prints "a bitter taste". No power or post-digestive taste is printed. The name "Pang gyen" stands in the Men-Tsee-Khang group of medicines that cure poisoning (line 3605). The one action printed is the curing of throat disorders and of fever caused by poisoning.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica gives this drug by what it does: it cures throat disorders and fever caused by poisoning, and the Men-Tsee-Khang text says the same in kind. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. No line read for this name places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice: the sentences cited above were read in full and none mentions one. Its framing is therapeutic only. The link from the binomial *Gentiana triflora* to that framing is weaker than for *Gentiana algida*, because only Clark prints it, and as the second name on a line.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a bitter drug for throat disorders and poisoning fever in the Tibetan texts, with no preparation and no dose. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. Poisoning needs emergency medical care, no preparation or dose is given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
