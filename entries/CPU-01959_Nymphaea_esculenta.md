**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The name in the chapters.* Kumuda is a water-lily named in the lists of the lotus family. The passages below were read from the chapter files for this entry.

*The Utpaladi group (Sutra 38, `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 375–378).* "The Utpala'di Group:— The drugs known as Utpala, Raktotpala, Kumuda Saugondhika, Kuvalaya, Pundarika and Madhuka constitute the group known as the Utpaladi Gana." The text prints no comma between Kumuda and Saugondhika, so whether they are two names or one is unclear. [USER: verify]

*The flowers (Sutra 46, `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 1799–1803).* "The Padma has a bitter and sweet taste, is cooling in its potency, and subdues the deranged Pittam and Kapham. The Kumuda has a sweet taste, and is slimy, demulcent, pleasing and cooling in its potency." The Kuvalayam and the Utpalam are said to differ slightly in their properties. At lines 1996–1999 the "bulbs of such aquatic plants as the Kumuda, Utpala and Padma" are given an astringent taste and a sweet after-taste in digestion, with a cooling potency.

*The leeches (Sutra 13, line 133).* The origin of non-venomous leeches is ascribed to decomposed vegetable matter, among it the stems of "Padma, Utpalam, Nalina, Kumuda, Pundarika" and zoophytes of clear waters. Kumuda is named as an aquatic plant here, and nothing is said of its use.

*Recipes.* Chikitsa 17 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt`, line 85): Kumuda is one of the drugs in the Kalka of a medicated ghee for erysipelas, among "Kakoli, Meda, Kumuda, Utpala, Chandana, Madhu-Sharkara ...". Kalpa 7 (`SS_Kalpa_ch07.txt`, line 125): "salts, Kumuda, Utpala, Padma, flower of Arka" appear in an antivenom compound. Uttara 18 (`SS_Uttara_ch18.txt`, line 243): "the leaves of Kadali, Kasmari, Eranda, Kumuda or of Padma plant" cover a ball of meat and drugs in a cooking method. In each, Kumuda is one item among many and no separate role is stated for it. The passages above are the ones this entry re-read; I_913 quotes a longer set, including Sutra 39, which should be consulted for the full record.

**Overlap.** `I_913_Kumuda.md` is the same Sanskrit name with the spine's *Nymphaea pubescens*; the Codex also holds `I_425_Prapaundarika_Nymphaea_lotus.md`, `I_479_Utpala_Nymphaea_stellata.md`, `I_462_WhiteWaterLily_Nymphaea_alba.md` and `I_494_BlueLotus_Nymphaea_caerulea.md`, which are other water-lilies. ### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Nymphaea* is a genus of water-lilies (Nymphaeaceae) whose members grow rooted in water with floating leaves; nothing is stated here about the composition of the Sushruta drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Kumuda as a sweet, slimy, demulcent, cooling flower, as astringent, sweet-after-digestion bulbs, as a member of the Utpaladi group, and as an ingredient in a ghee, an antivenom compound and a cooking method. These are the text's statements about a Sanskrit name; the Latin name is the translator's. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives the recipes above, each with its locus. The measures in them belong to the compounds, and no dose, part-quantity or duration is printed for Kumuda itself in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Water-lily flowers and rhizomes are used as foods and ornamentals in parts of Asia. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Sushruta terms (Pittam, Kapham, slimy, demulcent) are not modern diagnoses, and the identity rests on a positional pairing, so a study of *Nymphaea esculenta* would not bear directly on the Sushruta drug.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Sutra 46):** flower: "a sweet taste, and is slimy, demulcent, pleasing and cooling in its potency" (line 1800); bulbs: astringent, sweet in digestion, cooling in potency (lines 1996–1999). Sutra 38 places it in the Utpaladi group (line 376). The metrical text of the group, as quoted in I_913, gives group actions only.
- No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text frames Kumuda as a cooling flower and bulb of the water-lily family and as a member of the Utpaladi group. A search of the Chikitsa chapters 26 to 30 (the vajikarana and rasayana chapters) for "Kumuda" found nothing, so the text assigns it no rejuvenative or generative status in the lines retrieved, and the Codex gives it none. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for this drug.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. In the Sushruta scheme it is a cooling water-lily in a list of its family, and an ingredient in recipes a physician prescribes. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of a Latin name that the text does not itself give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read print no warning for the drug itself. The identity rests on a translator's appendix and a positional pairing, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry.
