# Elephant flesh — Gaja (as listed in Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch.46); "the flesh of the Elephant" (Bhishagratna)
## *The flesh of the elephant, named in Bhishagratna's English among the marsh-dwelling shore animals (Kulacharas) of the Anupa group of meats; the translation gives no Sanskrit word for it in the passage* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The text is Bhishagratna's 1907 English *Sushruta Samhita*, Sutrasthana ch.46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink (Annapana-Vidhi-madhyayam)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`). The list row is `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 238, "Gaja (elephant)", with the source "Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1". The Sanskrit word *gaja* is the row's, not the translation's; the English of the passage prints only "elephant". The row confirms the animal and chapter. Hence the attestation is a food-and-drug listing in the meat group.

The chapter opens with the claim that food "determines the origin of beings and forms the chief source of their bodily strength and complexion, as well as of the albuminous (Ojas) principle in their organisms" (lines 9-12). That is said of food in general, in Sushruta's question that begins the chapter, and not of any particular meat. The meat section begins at line 385: "Now I shall describe the properties of the different species of edible meats", and says the flesh of animals that are aquatic (Jaleshaya), marsh-dwelling (Anupa), village-dwelling (Gramya), carnivorous (Kravyabhuja), unbifurcated-hoofed (Ekashapha) or high-ground (Jangala) "is generally used as food" (lines 386-393).

The elephant appears in the Anupa group (heading at line 659). The Anupa animals "may be divided into five groups, such as, the Kulacharas (frequenting the shores of pools and lakes), Plavas (divers or swimmers), Koshastha (conchiferous aquatic animals such as, the molluscs etc.), the Fadinas [sic, as printed in the OCR text] and the (piscatory) Matsya", and the text places the elephant first in the first: "Of these, the elephant, the Gavaya, buffalo, Ruru ... Chamara, Srimara ..., Rohita (red deer), boar, rhinoceros, Gokarna, Kalapuchchaka, together with the Nynku ... and the wild cow, etc., frequent the cool shores of swamps and lakes, and are accordingly included within the group of Kulacharas (shore-dwellers)" (lines 671-680). The group's general properties follow, in the "Metrical Texts" (from line 682): "The flesh of an animal of this group is spermatopoietic and destroys the deranged Vayu and Kapham. It is sweet in taste and digestion, cooling, tonic, demulcent and diuretic, and increases the quantity of Kapham."

The passage then singles out the elephant (lines 688-693):

"The flesh of the Elephant tends to produce a state of extreme parchedness in the system, and is liquefacient and heat-making in its potency. It vitiates the Pittam and has a palatable acid and saline taste, and destroys the Vayu and Kapham."

So the elephant's flesh differs from its group's general statement. The group is "cooling" and "sweet in taste and digestion"; the elephant's flesh is "heat-making in its potency" with a "palatable acid and saline taste", and it "vitiates" *pittam*. Bhishagratna does not reconcile this, and neither does the passage. Neither does the text say how the flesh is cooked, who may eat it, or in which illness.

I searched all the chapter files for the word "elephant" and found other occurrences, none of them a use of elephant flesh: riding, elephantiasis, elephant's-trunk-shaped instruments, the poisoning of elephants, and "well burnt ashes of the dung of an elephant" mixed with elephant's urine in an alkaline wash, in the treatment of *svitra* (*Chikitsasthana* ch.9, lines 291-304). The she-elephant's milk is graded in *Sutrasthana* ch.45 (lines 518-521, 615-617, 840-842); it is a separate substance and not this entry.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, elephant muscle, like other mammalian meat, is mainly water and protein with some fat and iron; no more specific statement can be made without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Sushruta lists the elephant's flesh among edible meats and assigns it a set of qualities. The Codex does not recommend eating elephant: elephants are protected by law across much of their range and are threatened as species, and wild-animal meat carries zoonotic risks. Nothing in the passage describes it as a medicine for a named complaint, only as a food with effects on the *doṣas*. The honest use of this entry is as a record of how the text classifies the meat.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The *doṣa* terms (*vayu*, *pittam*, *kapham*) and the qualities "heat-making" and "parchedness" belong to the Ayurvedic frame and have no direct counterpart in modern physiology. Meat from wild animals can carry bacteria, parasites and diseases that spread from animals to people, and no statement is made here about particular agents in elephants. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** From Sutrasthana ch.46 (lines 688-693), for the flesh of the elephant: taste, "palatable acid and saline"; potency (*virya*), "heat-making"; effect on the humours, it "vitiates the Pittam" and "destroys the Vayu and Kapham"; general effect, "extreme parchedness in the system" and "liquefacient". No *vipaka* (taste after digestion) is printed for it. For its group of Kulacharas (from line 682): sweet in taste and digestion, cooling, tonic, demulcent, diuretic, spermatopoietic, destroying deranged *vayu* and *kapham* and increasing *kapham*. The two statements differ, as noted under Origin & Tradition.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The elephant's flesh is described in a food-and-drug chapter (*Sutrasthana* ch.46), whose opening says that food is the chief source of strength, complexion and *ojas* in beings (lines 9-12). That is a statement about food in general. No passage read assigns the elephant's flesh to a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, or says that it builds *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The group it belongs to is called "spermatopoietic" in the text, which is a property listed for the group, not a placement in a *vajikarana* chapter, and it is not read as one; the elephant's own sentence does not use the word. The elephant appears as a measure of strength in two *rasayana* chapters ("an elephantine strength", *Chikitsasthana* ch.27, line 131; "as strong as an elephant", ch.28, line 301), but as a simile for the result of other compounds, not as an ingredient. The sattvic and rajasic food lists are *Samkhya*-era material and not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The text classifies elephant flesh as a food with heating, parching effects, and the two *rasayana* mentions of elephant strength are similes, not recipes. The Codex does not recommend the meat. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Conservation and law:** elephants are protected in law in most of their range, and trade in their parts is restricted internationally; do not obtain any elephant product.
- **Zoonosis and hygiene:** wild-animal meat can carry bacteria, parasites and viruses.
- **Ayurvedic caution:** the text itself says the flesh "vitiates the Pittam" and is parching; the text does not say who should avoid it.
- **Ethics:** the Codex does not recommend harming any animal for these uses, and certainly not an endangered one.
