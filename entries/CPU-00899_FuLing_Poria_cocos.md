# Fu Ling — Poria · Hoelen · Fu-Ling · Tuckahoe · Indian Bread

## *Wolfiporia cocos* (F.A. Wolf) Ryvarden & Gilb. (syn. *Poria cocos* (Schw.) Wolf) | Family: Polyporaceae

> **A fungus, framed honestly.** Fu Ling is **not** a plant. It is the dried **sclerotium** — a dense underground survival mass of fungal hyphae — of a wood-rotting bracket fungus that grows on the roots of pine (*Pinus*) species. It is classed with the plant-based materia medica by tradition and trade, but it belongs to the fungi; this entry treats it as such.

### Origin & Tradition

Fu Ling (茯苓) is one of the most-used substances in the entire East-Asian pharmacopoeia — a large, firm, whitish sclerotium harvested from pine roots, sliced into the pale cubes familiar in the Chinese pharmacy. Different portions carry different names: the reddish outer flesh nearest the skin (*Chi Fu Ling*), the white inner flesh (*Bai Fu Ling*), the dark rind (*Fu Ling Pi*, used specifically as a diuretic), and the piece enclosing the pine root (*Fu Shen*, prized as a calming/spirit-settling part).

**Chinese medicine (TCM):** Fu Ling is a foundational drug of the *Shennong Bencao Jing* and every later materia medica, categorised among the **"drain-dampness / promote-urination"** herbs. Its classical functions are to *drain damp and promote urination* (for oedema, scanty urine, phlegm-fluid), to *strengthen the Spleen* (for poor appetite, loose stools, fatigue from "damp encumbering the Spleen"), and to *quiet the Heart and settle the spirit* (*Fu Shen* for palpitations, insomnia, restlessness). It appears in canonical formulas from Wu Ling San to Si Jun Zi Tang.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta stream (SS):** Assimilated through trans-Himalayan trade, the sclerotium was received in the eastern Ayurvedic and Tibetan border materia medica as a mild, sweet, diuretic and strengthening drug — a "damp-clearing," gently nourishing substance compatible with the Ayurvedic reading of oedema and sluggish digestion as *kapha/ama* (damp-cold, undigested residue).

**Unani / Avicennan (CM):** Within the Graeco-Arabic frame the drug fits the class of mild, non-irritant diuretics and "openers of obstruction" that promote urine and reduce dropsical swelling without the heat of the drastic diuretics — a temperate, drying-by-draining agent.

**Tibetan (rGyud-bzhi / BM):** In the Four Tantras tradition the *Poria*-type fungus is used among the diuretic, oedema-reducing, and calming agents, consistent with its cross-border reputation as a gentle fluid-regulator.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Fu Ling embodies the doctrine of the *bland draining downward*. Chinese theory holds that "bland" (*dan*) flavour leaches and percolates dampness out through urination — and the sclerotium is quite literally a mass the fungus builds to *store water and nutrients and survive dry adversity*. A body organised around water-storage becomes, in the pharmacy, an agent that reorganises the patient's water. Its calming action (*Fu Shen*) mirrors its physical stillness and density: a settling, grounding substance. The symbolism of "quiet, steady percolation" and the pharmacology of a diuretic-sedative polysaccharide-triterpene matrix align.

### Active Components

- **β-Pachyman** — the dominant (1→3)-β-D-glucan polysaccharide of the sclerotium; parent of derivatives like pachymaran; associated with immunomodulatory and antitumour activity.
- **Triterpenes** — lanostane and 3,4-*seco*-lanostane skeletons: **pachymic acid, poricoic acids, tumulosic acid, dehydrotumulosic acid, eburicoic acid** and many congeners (120+ triterpenoids reported); linked to diuretic, anti-inflammatory and antitumour effects.
- **Other constituents** — ergosterol, mannitol, flavonoids and saponins (notably concentrated in the peel/*Fu Ling Pi*), and chitin.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica *Wolfiporia cocos* is a **gentle calming diuretic and digestive-steadying tonic** — a "drain-damp / steady the centre" agent. Practical targets: fluid retention and mild oedema, sluggish digestion with loose stools and fatigue (the classic Spleen-damp picture), and restlessness/mild insomnia and palpitation (the *Fu Shen* calming role). Because it is bland, neutral, and low-toxicity, it functions as a **harmoniser** — frequently combined to soften and direct stronger drugs rather than acting alone. The peel (*Fu Ling Pi*) is selected when pure diuresis is wanted.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, Fu Ling's chemistry and pharmacology are increasingly well documented. Lei et al. (2025), reviewing over 2,500 years of use, identify **polysaccharides and triterpenes** as the principal bioactives (120 triterpenoid compounds reported) and summarise confirmed **diuretic, hepatoprotective, anti-inflammatory, antitumour, antioxidant and antidiabetic** effects, while flagging quality-control and safety issues such as sulfur-fumigation and heavy-metal contamination ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2025.119869), PMID 40280371). Feng et al. review the surface layer (*Fu-Ling-Pi*), used traditionally as a diuretic for **oedema and urinary dysfunction**, identifying lanostane and 3,4-*seco*-lanostane triterpenes and **β-pachyman** polysaccharide as its main components with antitumour, antibacterial and antioxidant activities (PMID 23847967 — no DOI registered in PubMed). Wang et al. quantified polysaccharides, triterpenoids, flavonoids, saponins and mannitol across peel and flesh, showing triterpenoids, flavonoids and saponins are enriched in the **peel** — the empirical basis for the traditional peel-as-diuretic distinction ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1615/intjmedmushr.v14.i1.80), PMID 22339710).

**Evidence grade:** Phytochemistry and preclinical pharmacology (diuretic, anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory) are **moderate and coherent**; robust human clinical trials of Fu Ling as a single agent remain limited. The traditional drain-damp / calming profile is **well-attested and mechanistically plausible** via the triterpene-polysaccharide matrix. Graded **moderate**.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Chinese:** Sweet, bland; **neutral**. Channels: Heart, Spleen, Lung, Kidney. Actions: drain damp / promote urination, strengthen Spleen, quiet the spirit.
- **Ayurveda (assimilated):** *Rasa* — sweet (madhura) with an underlying blandness; *Guna* — light, drying-by-draining; *Virya* — neutral-to-cooling; *Vipaka* — sweet; reduces *kapha/ama* (damp) without aggravating *pitta*.
- **Unani/Galenic:** Temperate, mildly drying; a gentle diuretic "opener of obstruction," non-irritant.

### Contraindications

- **Fluid-deficiency / dryness patterns:** because Fu Ling drains fluids via urination, avoid or use cautiously where there is dehydration, marked *yin*/fluid deficiency, or dryness — it can worsen fluid depletion.
- **Excessive/frequent urination, urinary incontinence, spermatorrhoea:** classically cautioned, as further promoting urination is counterproductive.
- **Diuretic interaction:** additive effect with pharmaceutical diuretics — monitor fluid/electrolyte status if combined; caution in those on medication for oedema or hypertension. [USER: verify with prescriber]
- **Quality/contaminant risk:** sourcing matters — the literature specifically flags **sulfur-fumigation residues and heavy-metal contamination** in commercial Fu Ling. Prefer tested, reputable material.
- **Allergy:** discontinue on any hypersensitivity reaction; individuals with mushroom/fungal allergies should exercise caution given its fungal nature.
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** traditionally used but data are limited; use under professional guidance. [USER: verify]

Fu Ling is regarded as a low-toxicity, well-tolerated substance — its cautions are those of an effective diuretic and of raw-material quality, not of intrinsic poison.
