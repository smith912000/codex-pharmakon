# Zarnab — زرنب (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *Thin, round, dark-to-yellowish rods with a faint citron-like scent, said to be a substitute for cinnamon; the plant is not named in the lines read. The identification is ours, from the Arabic description, and is of low confidence, with no species offered* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk, under this Arabic name.** This is a single-text entry. The Arabic text is `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; the heading is زرنب (*zarnab*), Arabic text line 12579. A search of the other texts on disk for the Arabic name found nothing for this drug. The row is a heading found in the Canon's Book II alphabetical materia medica and replaces an unverified placeholder.

**Avicenna, *Canon* of Medicine (CM).** (Canon Book II, entry *Zarnab*, Arabic text line 12579–12586). *What it is:* "thin round rods, between the thickness of an awl and the thickness of pens, black verging on yellow, with not much taste or smell; the little scent it has is aromatic, citron-like, and its strength is the strength of *jawz buwwā* but a little finer, and it may stand in place of cinnamon, it is said" (جوزبوا is usually glossed as nutmeg [USER: verify]). *Temperament:* hot and dry in the second degree. *Actions:* astringency and dispersing of winds. *Head:* snuffed with water and rose oil for cold headache. *Digestive:* of clear benefit to a cold liver and stomach. *Excretion:* it binds the belly, "it is said". The word *zarnab* also occurs in the hadith of Umm Zarʿ as a scent (Muslim, `Sources/TBN_Prophetic/text/Muslim_Sahih_OpenITI_JK000109.txt` line 33167; Bukhari, same folder, line 34413, *Bukhari_Sahih_OpenITI_JK000110.txt*); it is not discussed there as a drug, and the link to this entry is ours. No authority is named.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms aromatic barks and twigs carry volatile oils, which fits the citron-like scent and the warming actions. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Cold headache (snuffed), cold liver and stomach, winds, and binding the belly.
- **What the text does not say.** The plant, and any dose.

The Codex does not translate the Canon's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this material.

### Preparation & Dosing

Snuffed with water and rose oil for headache. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
