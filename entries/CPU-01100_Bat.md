# Bat — Bat, Bat's Blood (Bryan's Ebers)
## *The bat as an animal source of medicine; Bryan gives only "Bat", and the Egyptian word and the species are not printed* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

This entry is the Egyptian bat. The Tibetan "bat flesh" of Clark's translation of the rGyud-bzhi is a separate spine row, `III_44_Bat_flesh.md`, and is not merged here; the two entries cross-refer and nothing in either text links them.

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The English text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 *The Papyrus Ebers*, a translation with summaries; the Egyptian words are not printed. In Chapter VII, "Organic Remedies", Bryan lists the animals "whose identity is not in doubt, and who were called upon to sacrifice themselves in the cause of the Healing Art", and "Bat" stands in that list between "Beetle" and "Wasp" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1404; `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` row "Bat", line 129, marked "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). The list row `_sources/ebers.csv` line 84, "Bat's blood", gives the identification "Chiroptera sp." with the source field "Ebers; Wikipedia/Ebers Papyrus"; the order name and the second source are not in Bryan's text and were not relied on. The prescriptions found that name the bat:

- *Trichiasis.* "TO DRIVE OUT TRICHIASIS: Myrrh, Lizard's Blood, Bat's Blood. Tear out the Hairs and put thereon in order to make him well." (line 2874, in the eye section).
- *Hair pulled out of the eye.* Under "TO PREVENT THE HAIR GROWING INTO THE EYE AFTER IT HAS BEEN PULLED OUT", the first compound is incense with the blood of the cow, ass, pig, dog and stag (see III_23 for the pig's blood); the next, "ANOTHER", is "Bat's Blood, Rim-of-a-new-hennu-vessel, Honey. Powder and place where the Hair has been pulled out" (lines 2886-2899).
- *Itch of the skin.* In Bryan's chapter on diseases of the skin: "For the common Itch a mixture composed of Onion, crushed in Honey and taken in Beer, was prescribed. If the Itch was confined to the Neck, a Chopped-up Bat applied to it as a poultice healed it 'at once'." (line 2608). Here it is the whole animal, chopped, and not the blood.

The bat's blood is thus an eye-section remedy for trichiasis and for the place where a hair has been pulled; the whole bat is a poultice for itch. Bryan reports no recitation with these compounds. The papyrus joins spell and drug in one intervention elsewhere, but Bryan summarises, so his silence is no proof that none is given. No dose, quantity or duration is printed in the passages read. No bat remedy was found in the other chapters read (the grep of the text for "bat" returns only these lines).

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, blood is plasma proteins and haemoglobin in water; the whole animal is muscle, skin and bone. Bats are reservoir hosts of several zoonotic viruses, among them rabies and related lyssaviruses, and coronaviruses, Nipah and Hendra viruses; that is a hazard, not a constituent.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Ebers prescriptions use the bat's blood, in the eye section, for trichiasis and for the place where a lash has been pulled and a chopped bat as a poultice for neck itch; the sources give no outcomes. The Codex gives no protocol. Handling or applying bat blood or tissue near the eye or on the skin carries a real infection hazard, and many bat species are protected.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparations the text gives: blood mixed with myrrh and lizard's blood and put on the place after the hairs are torn out (line 2874); blood powdered with the rim of a new vessel and honey, put where the hair was pulled (line 2899); and the animal chopped and applied as a poultice (line 2608). "Powder" in the second is Bryan's wording for a dry preparation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Bats are mammals, the only group of mammals capable of powered flight. They carry rabies and other zoonotic viruses, and bat bites, scratches and contact with saliva or blood are an indication for rabies assessment. Pulling lashes and applying animal blood to the eye region is not a treatment for trichiasis; the modern treatment is epilation or surgery. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* The bat is named in the Ebers list of animal sources and in prescriptions for trichiasis, misgrown lashes and neck itch, which record what was done for a complaint and not what the animal does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); Bryan reports no recitation with the bat compounds read, and since he summarises, that does not show the papyrus gives none. Substance identity in an indication-organised corpus is a modern hypothesis, and the species of "Bat" is not given. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The only sourced uses are an eye-section remedy and a poultice, and the animal is a recognised carrier of lethal viruses. A cultivator takes no practice from this entry. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Infection:** bats carry rabies and other zoonotic viruses; contact with a bat's blood, saliva or tissue calls for medical advice, and blood in the eye is an entry route for infection.
- **Eye safety:** applying any animal blood to the eye or its margin risks infection.
- **Conservation and law:** many bat species are protected, and disturbing or killing bats or their roosts is illegal in many places. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** killing a protected, insect-eating animal for a remedy of no demonstrated benefit cannot be defended.
