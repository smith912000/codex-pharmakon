# Dipsacus root — Xu Duan 续断 (Shennong bencao jing); aliases Long Dou 龙豆, "dragon bean", and Shu Zhe 属折, "joining the broken"

## *Dipsacus asper Wall. ex DC., the teasel whose root is the Chinese drug xu duan; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence for the modern drug but only moderate for what the classic itself meant, since the classic describes no plant, and Yang 1998's commentary notes that more than one plant has gone by the name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** The principal text read is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Its other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), also has the drug as "Xu Duan (Radix Dypsaci)" (line 2429), and is read here as a second witness, attributed to Yang. The row began as an unverified placeholder ("Radix Dypsaci [SBJ]").

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 续断 in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 续断 *xu duan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 795). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，微温 ("bitter, slightly warm"); 主伤寒 ("governs cold damage"), 补不足 ("supplements insufficiency"), 金创痈伤 ("metal-inflicted wounds and abscess injuries"), 折跌 ("breaks and falls"), 续筋骨 ("joins sinews and bones"), 妇人乳难 ("difficulty with milk in women"); 久服，益气力 ("taken long: boosts qi and strength"); aliases 一名龙豆 ("dragon bean"), 一名属折 ("joining the broken"); habitat 生山, the following character (谷, "valley") lost in the digitisation, where the Mingyi layer begins without a break. Sun notes that the *Yulan* reads 乳痈 ("breast abscess") for 乳难 and adds "flooding and spotting" (崩中、漏血), and that the 大观 edition prints these in "black characters"; I read this as marking them as later additions. [USER: verify]

The Wu Pu layer is absent for this drug. The Mingyi bielu layer (《名医》曰, as printed in line 795) adds the aliases 接骨 ("joint-joiner"), 南草 and 槐, the habitat 生常山 ("grows at Changshan"), and the harvest 七月、八月采，阴干 ("gathered in the seventh and eighth months, dried in the shade"; the part is not named in the line as printed).

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 797) gives three older sources, all the commentators' and not the Shennong text's. The *Guangya* equates a (lost) character with xu duan. The *Fanzi jiran* says xu duan comes from the Sanfu region. The *Tongjun yaolu* is quoted for the plant: 生蔓延，叶细，茎如荏大，根本黄白，有汁。七月、八月采根 ("it grows trailing, the leaves are fine, the stalk is as large as perilla, the root is yellow-white and has juice; the root is gathered in the seventh and eighth months"). This is the only description of the plant in the entry, and it is a third-party quotation.

Yang 1998 (line 2429) renders the paragraph as "Xu Duan (Radix Dypsaci) is bitter and slightly warm. It mainly treats cold damage, supplements insufficiency, [treats] incised wounds and welling abscesses, joins broken sinews and bones, and [resolves] difficult lactation in females. Protracted taking may boost the qi and [physical] force." His footnote 146 (line 2433 onward) says that "there are three varieties of the plant" under this name, and names *Lamium album*, *Sonchus asper* and *Dipsacus japonicus* (the OCR reads "Dispsaclls Japoniclls"). That is Yang's commentary and the Codex cannot check it against the classic; it is why the identity above is held with caution.

**Grade difference between the two editions.** Sun lists xu duan among the upper medicines (contents, line 45; entry under 卷一 上经). In Yang the nearest class heading above the paragraph reads "Herbs: Middle Class" (line 2389). The Codex follows Sun, as for all SBJ entries, and records the difference without resolving it. [USER: verify]

**A shared name.** Another upper-class entry, 石龙刍 (*shi long chu*, Sun line 1017), carries the alias 草续断 ("herb xu duan") in the Shennong paragraph itself, and its Wu Pu layer (line 1019) lists 续断 among its names. The name xu duan was therefore also attached to that plant in the sources; this entry is for the drug that is entered under 续断 itself.

**Appendix.** An appendix of drug relations, later than the main text, says of it: 续断 地黄为使；恶雷丸 ("xu duan: di huang is its envoy; it dislikes lei wan", line 4333), and lists xu duan as an envoy of sang gen bai pi (line 4517).

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the roots of *Dipsacus* species contain iridoid glycosides and triterpenoid saponins; which compounds predominate varies with the species. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, slightly warm root for cold damage, insufficiency, wounds and abscesses, breaks and falls, joining the sinews and bones, and difficulty with milk; and, when taken long, for boosting qi and strength. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation in the sources read is the harvest: the Mingyi layer gives the seventh and eighth months with drying in the shade, and the *Tongjun yaolu* quotation in Sun's note gives the root in those months (line 797). The preface (line 4121) says that drugs differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Dipsacus asper* (teasel family, Caprifoliaceae/Dipsacaceae by older reckoning) is a perennial whose root is used in modern Chinese pharmacy; the genus is unrelated to the European teasels discussed by Dioscorides (see I_1070). The classic's claims about joining sinews and bones are not translated into a modern pharmacological claim. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味苦，微温, "bitter, slightly warm" (line 795). The Wu Pu layer, which elsewhere lists the authorities' differing flavours, is absent for this drug, so no disagreement is recorded.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The paragraph for 续断 stands in the upper classic (卷一 上经), whose preface (line 43) says the upper medicines "nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic; taken much or long they do not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body and benefit qi, not age and extend the years, base it on the upper classic" (主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经). On the grading rule used for this series, placement in the upper classic is itself the ground for (a). Its own 久服 clause reads only 久服，益气力, "taken long: boosts qi and strength". That matches the preface's "benefit qi" (益气) but contains none of the terms for lightening the body, not ageing or extending the years, so the grade rests on the placement and the drug's own long-use promise is modest. In Yang's edition the same drug is under a middle heading (see above); the Codex follows Sun, and the Yang placement does not count. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 续断 *xu duan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 795)

**Practitioner's note.** The classic frames this root as an upper-class drug whose long use is said to boost qi and strength, and mends sinews and bones after breaks and falls. That is a record of the classic's wording, not a practice. No dose or course is given, and the plant behind the name is not settled by the text. A cultivator should take from it only the grade and the wording.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** commentary on the name (Yang's note 146) allows more than one plant; do not treat products sold as xu duan as interchangeable.
- **Injuries:** breaks, falls, deep wounds and abscesses need medical and surgical care; the entry is not a treatment.
- **Pregnancy and nursing:** the classic uses the drug for flooding and milk difficulty (Sun's note, line 795), but no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Appendix cautions:** the appendix says xu duan "dislikes lei wan" (line 4333); this is a historical pairing and not a modern interaction list.
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given; "taken long" is not a basis for a regimen.
