### Origin & Tradition

**Shennong Ben Cao Jing (SBJ):** Hòu Pò — "thick bark," named for the thick, aromatic stem/root bark that is the medicinal part — is one of the classical Qi-regulating, damp-transforming agents of Chinese medicine, present in the early materia medica. Its defining action is to *move and descend* stagnant Qi: it relieves fullness, distension and bloating in the chest and abdomen, resolves phlegm-damp accumulation, and "descends rebellious Qi" — the upward-surging pattern of cough, wheeze, hiccup, nausea, and the constricted feeling in chest and throat. It is a keystone ingredient of *Ban Xia Hou Po Tang* ("Pinellia and Magnolia Bark Decoction"), the classical formula for **"plum-pit qi" (梅核氣)** — the sensation of a lump in the throat that will not swallow or spit out, a description that maps remarkably onto what modern medicine frames as globus and somatic anxiety. It also anchors *Ping Wei San* ("Calm the Stomach Powder") for damp-cold stagnation of the middle.

**Regional spread:** As *Kōboku* in Japanese Kampo it retains its Qi-moving, chest-and-abdomen-opening role and appears in anxiety- and digestion-oriented prescriptions.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — Descending the Rebellious:** The classical language of Hòu Pò is directional — it takes what is stuck and rising (Qi that rebels upward as tightness, nausea, a throat-lump, restless unease) and moves it down and through. That downward, "un-sticking" motion is the doctrinal bridge between its two clinical faces: digestive (bloating, stagnation) and psycho-somatic (the anxious throat-constriction of plum-pit qi). Modern pharmacology gives an honest correlate in the two lignans **magnolol and honokiol**, which cross the blood–brain barrier and show anxiolytic, GABAergic-modulating and anti-inflammatory activity in animal models — the "descending the rebellious Qi" of an over-tight, anxious system rendered as central neuromodulation. The tradition treated tension in the gut and tension in the mind as one moving obstruction; the chemistry does not contradict that.

### Active Components

- **Magnolol** and **honokiol** — the two signature biphenolic lignans; both cross the BBB and drive most of the studied neuropharmacology (anxiolytic, anti-inflammatory, neuroprotective).
- **Volatile oil** (β-eudesmol and other sesquiterpenes) — contributes the aromatic, Qi-moving and digestive actions.
- **Alkaloids** (e.g. magnocurarine — relevant to a curare-like caution, see contraindications).
- **Additional phenolics and lignans.**

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Hòu Pò sits at the **`[RESET]`** node as the *un-stick-and-descend* agent — the fit is stagnation felt as fullness, bloating, sluggish digestion after heavy load, or the tight, held tension of a system that will not release. Its **`[SLEEP]`** role is the anxiolytic wind-down: the magnolol/honokiol axis makes it a rational choice where the barrier to rest is somatic anxiety, chest/throat constriction, or a "wired-tight" state — the plum-pit-qi profile. It is a moving, aromatic herb, not a heavy sedative; it releases rather than suppresses.

### Scientific Correlation

*Attribution: the following findings are from PubMed.*

The neuropharmacology of magnolol and honokiol is **well developed preclinically**; robust human RCTs of standardised Magnolia officinalis extract for anxiety/sleep remain limited.

- Dai et al. (2023), *Current Molecular Pharmacology*, reviewing the neuropharmacology of magnolol and honokiol, report that both lignans cross the blood–brain barrier and exert neuroprotective effects through multiple mechanisms, with documented activity across cerebral ischaemia, neuroinflammation, Alzheimer's disease and brain tumours, as well as psychiatric disorders including **anxiety and depression** — while explicitly noting that the clinical potential remains largely untapped and that bioavailability and human trials are the key gaps. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/1874467215666220223141101)

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical evidence is strong and mechanistically coherent (GABAergic modulation, anti-inflammatory, neuroprotective); human clinical efficacy for anxiety/sleep is plausible but not yet established by high-quality trials of the isolated bark extract. Keep anxiolytic claims traditional/forward-looking.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM tier:** Aromatic damp-transforming / Qi-regulating herb. Flavour: bitter + acrid; nature: warm, aromatic. Channels: Spleen, Stomach, Lung, Large Intestine.
- **Core actions (classical):** promotes the movement of Qi and reduces distension/fullness (行氣消脹); transforms damp and resolves phlegm (燥濕化痰); descends rebellious Qi to calm wheezing and the throat-lump of plum-pit qi.
- **Ayurvedic:** Not attested for this species.
- **Tibetan / Badianus:** Not attested.
- **Dantian correspondence:** Middle–Lower descending — moves and lowers stuck Qi through the middle burner; an un-blocking, releasing agent.

### Contraindications

- **Qi/Yin deficiency without stagnation:** As a warm, drying, Qi-moving herb, it can further deplete in true deficiency states or dryness — classically contraindicated where there is no real stagnation to move.
- **Pregnancy:** Traditionally cautioned/contraindicated in pregnancy (a Qi-moving, descending herb); avoid absent professional supervision — `[USER: verify]`.
- **Curare-like alkaloid caution:** Magnolia bark contains **magnocurarine**, a curare-type alkaloid with potential neuromuscular-blocking activity at high exposure — a reason to avoid excessive doses and to exercise caution around anaesthesia/neuromuscular agents.
- **Sedative / CNS-depressant interactions:** Given magnolol/honokiol's GABAergic and anxiolytic actions, expect additive effects with alcohol, benzodiazepines, and other sedatives — use caution when combining.
- **Anticoagulant / bleeding:** Honokiol has shown anti-platelet activity in models; caution with anticoagulants. `[USER: verify]`.
- **Toxicity note:** Low toxicity at classical doses; principal concerns are the alkaloid at high exposure, sedative interactions, and pregnancy.
