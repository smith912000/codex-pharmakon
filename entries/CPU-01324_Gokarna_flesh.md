# Gokarna flesh — Gokarna, flesh of a shore-dweller (*Kulachara*) of the marsh (*Anupa*) group, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the animal Bhishagratna prints as "Gokarna", with no gloss in the chapter; his Vol. 3 glossary reads "An animal frequenting a marshy place; (the Nilgai?)" by column order; no binomial is printed* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Gokarna is named in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 247 lists "Gokarna", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", flagged identity-uncertain). The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The marsh-dwellers (*Anupa*) "may be divided into five groups", of which the first is the *Kulacharas*, "frequenting the shores of pools and lakes" (lines 659-669). The Gokarna belongs to that first group: "the elephant, the Gavaya, buffalo, Ruru ... Chamara, Srimara ... Rohita (red deer), boar, rhinoceros, Gokarna, Kalapuchchaka, together with the Nynku (a species of antlered deer) and the wild cow, etc., frequent the cool shores of swamps and lakes, and are accordingly included within the group of Kulacharas (shore-dwellers)" (lines 671-680; the translator glosses Ruru, Srimara and Nynku but not Gokarna or Kalapuchchaka).

The group text reads: "The flesh of an animal of this group is spermatopoietic and destroys the deranged Vayu and Kapham. It is sweet in taste and digestion, cooling, tonic, demulcent and diuretic, and increases the quantity of Kapham" (lines 682-686). Of the Gokarna by name: "The flesh of the Gokarna is sweet, demulcent, mild (soft), sweet in digestion and proves curative in cases of haemoptysis, and generates Kapham in the system" (lines 727-730). Searches of every Sushruta chapter file for the name returned only ch.46, so there is no second passage, no preparation, no cautionary use and no omen for the Gokarna in the text on disk.

**Identity.** Ch.46 does not say what animal the Gokarna is. The glossary of Bhishagratna's Vol. 3 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, name at line 30549) gives, in a separate OCR column, "An animal frequenting a marshy place; (the Nilgai?)", with its own question mark; the pairing is by column order. The identity is open. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Animal flesh is mostly water and muscle protein, with fat that varies by species, and supplies iron, zinc and B vitamins. No analysis of the animal Sushruta calls Gokarna was read, and its species is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced record is a single dietary description in a food chapter: the flesh of a shore-dwelling animal, with stated qualities. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no use for haemoptysis or any other condition. The animal cannot be named with confidence, so no one could follow the text even if they wished to, and large wild herbivores are protected by wildlife law in many countries. A reader who eats no meat has nothing to adopt.

### Preparation & Dosing

None is given for the Gokarna. The ch.46 lines state qualities and indications only. Ch.46 elsewhere describes ways of preparing meat in general, but none is attributed to the Gokarna. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Wild game can carry zoonotic infections and parasites, and thorough cooking with hygienic handling is the standard precaution. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 682-686, 727-730). For the *Kulachara* group generally: sweet in taste and in digestion; cooling; tonic; demulcent; diuretic; spermatopoietic; destroys deranged *Vayu* and *Kapham*; increases *Kapham*. For the Gokarna by name: sweet; demulcent; mild (soft); sweet in digestion; curative in haemoptysis; generates *Kapham*. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Gokarna is named only in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food, not about this flesh. The group text calls the flesh of the shore-dwellers "spermatopoietic" and "tonic", which are properties listed among foods and not a placement in a *vajikarana* or *rasayana* chapter; no line read puts the Gokarna in either. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta the Gokarna is a food with a few stated qualities and one indication, and nothing in the sources read tells a cultivator what to do with it. With the animal unidentified, the honest answer is nothing.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** wild game can carry bacteria and parasites; cook through.
- **Identity:** the animal is not identified in the text; no species should be substituted on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **The text's own caution:** the flesh "generates Kapham", which the text treats as a quality to weigh, not a prohibition.
- **Legal and conservation status:** wild herbivores are protected in many countries; status for any candidate species was not checked. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food or medicine is a moral question for many traditions; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
