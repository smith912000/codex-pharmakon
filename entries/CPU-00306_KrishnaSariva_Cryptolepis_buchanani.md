# Krishna Sariva — Sanskrit: Kṛṣṇa-sāriva, Kāla-sāriva; Black Sarsaparilla, Karanta

## *Cryptolepis buchanani* Roem. & Schult. | Family: Apocynaceae (formerly Asclepiadaceae)

**Tags (previous line, retained for reference):** Suśruta Saṃhitā (as the "black" (*kṛṣṇa*) counterpart of Sāriva; raktaprasādana / sārivādi context); Ayurvedic nighaṇṭus (Kṛṣṇa-sāriva as recognised Sāriva-varga substitute); Thai & Southeast-Asian folk medicine (anti-inflammatory, arthritis); tribal medicine of India (galactagogue, bone-setting, wounds)

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta & the Sāriva pair:** Classical dravyaguṇa recognises two Sārivas — the white (*śveta*, *Hemidesmus indicus*) and the black (*kṛṣṇa*, most commonly identified with *Cryptolepis buchanani*). They share the core indication set of the sārivādi complex: cooling the blood, calming Pitta heat, clearing the skin, and settling burning and eruptive conditions. Kṛṣṇa-sāriva is treated as *raktaprasādana* and *raktaśodhana* like its white partner, and the two are frequently used interchangeably or together, the black form regarded as somewhat more drying (its bitter-astringent taste more pronounced) and thus useful where damp/Kapha accompanies the heat.

**Nighaṇṭu tradition:** Later lexicons (e.g. the Bhāvaprakāśa nighaṇṭu tradition) distinguish the two Sārivas by colour and describe overlapping *guṇa* — cooling, blood-clarifying, complexion-restoring, antipyretic, and diuretic — with Kṛṣṇa-sāriva additionally valued for chronic skin disease and as a supportive lactation drug.

**Folk & regional medicine:** Across India and Southeast Asia the plant is a climbing shrub whose stem and root are widely used. In Thai and Lao folk practice the stem treats inflammation, arthritis, and muscle-and-joint pain; Indian tribal medicine uses it for wounds, bone-setting, general debility, and to promote lactation. This convergent folk emphasis on inflammation, joints, and connective tissue extends the classical cooling logic toward the musculoskeletal.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Dark Twin:** Where white Sāriva is *śveta* — the fragrant, sweet, complexion-brightening face of blood-clarification — Kṛṣṇa-sāriva is its darker, more astringent shadow. In Ayurvedic pair-logic (a recurrent structuring device, as in the Aṣṭavarga couples) the two colours are not redundant but complementary poles of a single doctrine: both draw down to the blood, but the white cools-and-graces while the black cools-and-binds. *Kṛṣṇa* (black/dark) in the plant lexicon often signals a deeper, more consolidating reach — a drug that not only clears heat but tightens and mends the tissue left slack or weeping by inflammation, which is why folk practice pushes it toward wounds and joints. The pairing itself is the symbol: complexion and consolidation, brightness and binding, held as two aspects of *raktaprasādana* — settling the blood so that it becomes, again, clear.

### Active Components
The genus is characterised by **cryptolepine-type and related alkaloids** (indoloquinoline and pregnane chemistry across *Cryptolepis*), **triterpenoids** and **phytosterols**, **cardenolide/pregnane glycosides**, phenolics, and a proteolytic latex enzyme. From *C. buchanani* latex a novel serine protease, **cryptolepain** (~50.5 kDa), has been purified and characterised. According to PubMed, cryptolepain is described by Pande et al. (2006), *J Agric Food Chem* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/jf062206a). Note: precise root/stem phytochemistry of *C. buchanani* is less fully mapped than that of *Hemidesmus*.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica
Kṛṣṇa-sāriva serves Kenetica as the **drying-cooling** member of the raktaśodhana pair — chosen over white Sāriva when the inflammatory heat is accompanied by damp, weeping, or joint/connective-tissue involvement. Its documented anti-inflammatory and chondroprotective actions make it the phase-appropriate agent for Recovery and for Structural Repair where hot, painful joints and soft-tissue inflammation follow heavy loading. Use alongside or in place of white Sāriva when a more astringent, tissue-binding cooling is wanted.

### Scientific Correlation
According to PubMed:
- **Anti-inflammatory (in vitro & in vivo)** — Laupattarakasem et al. (2006), *J Ethnopharmacol*, show a hydro-ethanolic stem extract reduces carrageenan paw oedema, eicosanoid release, granuloma formation, and TNF-α release from stimulated human monocytes, with low oral toxicity — supporting the traditional anti-inflammatory use [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2006.05.029).
- **Analgesic, anti-inflammatory & chondroprotective** — Hanprasertpong et al. (2014), *Biomed Res Int*, report analgesic and anti-oedema activity plus protection of cartilage matrix (reduced glycosaminoglycan/hyaluronan loss, suppressed MMP-2) in porcine cartilage explants, suggesting osteoarthritis relevance [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/978582).
- **Latex enzyme** — Pande et al. (2006), *J Agric Food Chem*, purify and characterise cryptolepain, a stable serine protease from the latex [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/jf062206a).

**Evidence grade:** Preclinical (in vitro, rodent, cartilage-explant). Anti-inflammatory/chondroprotective claims are mechanistically supported; no human clinical trials. Classical raktaprasādana use rests on tradition plus mechanistic corroboration.

### Traditional Energetic Classification
- **Rasa (taste):** Tikta (bitter), Kaṣāya (astringent), Madhura (sweet)
- **Guṇa (quality):** Laghu–Guru (light to heavy per part), Snigdha to Rūkṣa (the astringency lends dryness)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Madhura (sweet)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** *Raktaprasādana* — blood-clarifying like white Sāriva, with a more consolidating/tissue-binding character
- **Doṣa action:** Pacifies Pitta and Rakta; the bitter-astringent-dry facet also restrains Kapha; may increase Vāta if used dry and long
- **Karma (actions):** Raktaśodhana, dāhapraśamana, kaṇḍūghna, jvaraghna, śothahara (anti-inflammatory), vraṇaropaṇa (wound-healing, folk), stanya (galactagogue, folk), sandhi-supportive (joint, folk/preclinical)
- **Gaṇa:** Functionally within the Sāriva-varga / sārivādi context as the "black" Sāriva

### Contraindications
Cooling and, in its astringent aspect, drying — may aggravate Vāta-cold and Kapha-cold digestive sluggishness with prolonged use. The genus carries alkaloid and cardenolide/pregnane chemistry, so avoid high or prolonged dosing without supervision; safety in pregnancy/lactation not established `[USER: verify]`. Human safety data limited. Confirm botanical identity: multiple plants are traded as "Sāriva," and *Cryptolepis buchanani* must not be confused with white Sāriva (*Hemidesmus indicus*) or with the African *Cryptolepis sanguinolenta*.
