# Panicum frumentaceuin — Shyamaka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3); "frumentaceuin" is an OCR misprint

## *Panicum frumentaceum Roxb., a millet, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints as OCR "Panicum Frumentaceuin." (line 33003), the "in" being a misreading of "um" ("frumentaceum"). The Sanskrit name it stands against is, by counting down the OCR columns, Shyamaka (OCR "dya'ma'ka", line 32944), a minor grain of the Sushruta Kudhanya group. In current botany the name is usually treated as a synonym of the little millet, Panicum sumatrense; confidence medium for the Latin as the translator's, medium for the pairing, low for the modern species* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was opened on 13 October from the extraction (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`), where the Latin line is kept as "not paired to a Sanskrit name (OCR column split)". **The pairing, by counting.** On the page that carries this Latin line, the Sanskrit names run "Srivasa", "Sriveshtaka", "Suka-simbi" (line 32929), "Suka-nasa" (32931), "Sukla-dugdha" (32933), "Sukla-mudga" (32935), "Sunthi" (32937), "Surana" (OCR "Parana", 32939), "Sveta-chandana" (32941), "Syama" (OCR "riya'ma'", 32942), "**Shyamaka**" (OCR "dya'ma'ka", 32944), "Shyamalata" (32946), "Siriva", "Syonaka" and on to "Soma". The Latin run, a page of lines (32960–33025), goes through "Mucuna pruriens" (Suka-simbi), "Oroxylum Indicum" (Suka-nasa), "See S'ringitaka" (Sukla-dugdha), "Phaseolus Subloleatus" (Sukla-mudga, see I_1599), "Gingiber officinal" (Sunthi), "Amorphophallus Campanulatus" (Surana), "Santalum Album" (Sveta-chandana), "See Siriva and Trivrit. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The name in the chapters.* "Shyamaka" occurs in four places, all read from the chapter files. The translator gives no Latin name in them.

*The Kudhanya genus (Sutra 46, `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, lines 158–190).* The chapter, on foods, passes from the Shali rice group to the inferior grains: "The Genus Kudhariya (Kudhanya):— The several varieties of Kudhanyam are known as the Koradushaka, Shyamaka, Nivara, Shantanu, Varaka, Uddalaka, Priyangu, Madhulika, Nandimukhi, Kuravinda, Gavedhuka, Varuka, Todaparni, Mukundaka, and Venu-yava, etc." The metrical text follows: "They generate heat and have a sweet and astringent taste. They are parchifying and pungent of digestion, subdue the Kapham and tend to produce retention of the urine, and enrage the bodily Vayu and the Pittam. Of these the species known as the Kodrava, Nivara, Shyamaka and Shantanu have a sweet and astringent taste and prove curative in Sheeta pitta (urticaria)." The translator's gloss in parentheses is "(urticaria)".

*Against excess fat (Sutra 15, `SS_Sutra_ch15.txt`, lines 612–622).* In the chapter on the humours and the body, after the avoidance of conditions that foster abnormal fat, "medicated compositions, consisting of such drugs and substances as Shilajatu, Guggulu, Go-Mutram, Triphala, Loharaja, Rasanjanam, Madhu, Yava, Mudga, Koradusha, Shyamaka and Uddalaka which are anti-fat in their properties, or of remedial agents possessing the efficacy of cleansing the internal channels ... should be prescribed" (the text continues with enemas and exercise).

*As an aggravator of Vayu (Sutra 21, `SS_Sutra_ch21.txt`, lines 266–270).* Among the factors that aggravate the bodily Vayu is food of pungent, astringent, bitter, light or parchifying articles, and "Diets consisting of dried pot-herbs, Vallura, Varaka, Uddalaka, Karadusha, Shyamaka, Nivara, Mudga, Masura, Adhaki, Harenu, Kalaya, and Nishpava tend to aggravate the bodily Vayu."

The name is not in the Sutra 38 group lists read. Shyamaka is a grain, and the passages treat it as food and as a diet to avoid or to prescribe for a purpose.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, millets are cereal grains (Poaceae) rich in starch, with some protein and fibre. Nothing more is stated here about the composition of the Sushruta grain.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Shyamaka as a minor grain of the Kudhanya group that is heating, sweet and astringent, retains urine and aggravates Vayu and Pittam as a class, is curative in urticaria (Sheeta pitta), and is named in an anti-fat list and in a Vayu-aggravating diet. These are the text's statements about a Sanskrit name; the Latin name is the translator's. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Sutra 46, lines 158–190):** as one of the Kudhanyas it is heating (it "generates heat"), "sweet and astringent", "parchifying and pungent of digestion"; it subdues Kapham, tends to produce retention of urine, and enrages Vayu and Pittam. Shyamaka is named with Kodrava, Nivara and Shantanu as sweet and astringent and curative in urticaria. No *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Shyamaka alone in the lines read.
- No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text frames Shyamaka as a minor grain: a food, an item in an anti-fat list (Sutra 15) and in a Vayu-aggravating diet (Sutra 21). A search of the Chikitsa chapters 26 to 30 (the vajikarana and rasayana chapters) for "Shyamaka" found nothing, so the text assigns it no rejuvenative or generative status in the lines retrieved, and the Codex gives it none. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for this grain.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice with this grain. In the Sushruta scheme it is an inferior grain whose class is heating, parching and Vayu-aggravating, and it is named in a list of anti-fat agents. Nothing should be gathered or eaten in order to follow this entry.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read warn that the Kudhanya class retains urine and aggravates Vayu and Pittam, and that Shyamaka is in a diet that aggravates Vayu. The identity rests on a translator's appendix and a positional pairing; millet is a food, and no medicinal use should be taken from this entry. Millets can carry mould toxins if stored damp.
