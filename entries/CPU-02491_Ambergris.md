# Ambergris — عنبر (*ʿanbar*, as printed)

## *Ambergris: the waxy, aromatic mass found floating or washed ashore, which the Canon calls a spring in the sea and modern knowledge attributes to the sperm whale* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry for the tag line.** Named as a medicine only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. The locus is *Canon* Book II, entry *ʿanbar* (عنبر), Arabic text line 16529 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; the entry runs to line 16544. (Ibn Qayyim's *al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī* also discusses ambergris, at lines 5969-5990 on disk, as a food-law question and a perfume; that is not a medicinal entry and this entry is not tagged TBN. See report.)

**Origin as Avicenna thought of it (CM).** "Ambergris is, as is supposed, a spring welling up in the sea; and what is said, that it is the foam of the sea or the dung of an animal, is far-fetched" (العنبر فيما يظن نبع عين في البحر والذي يقال من أنه زبد البحر أو روث دابة بعيد; lines 16530-16531). He gives the testimony of a trusted man who found it in pieces of various colours on the shore of a sea-town he names *Bakhākh* (بخاخ), where whoever came first took it (lines 16531-16538). Neither the whale nor the intestinal origin is named in this passage, so the identification of ambergris with the sperm-whale product is modern (high confidence), not the text's. The text also says that "the black, bad kind" is "often taken from the bellies of the fish that eat it and die" (lines 16540-16541); the grammar leaves open whether that sentence is about *mandah* or about the black ambergris, and no conclusion is drawn here.

**Choice and adulteration (CM).** "Its best is the grey (*ashhab*), strong, *salāhiṭī*; then the blue, then the yellow; and the black is last" (lines 16538-16540). *Salāhiṭī* is printed as such and is not translated here. It is adulterated with gypsum, wax, labdanum (*lādhan*) and *mandah*; *mandah* is the last adulterant named (line 16540).

**Actions (CM).** "Hot and dry; it is likely that its heat is in the second degree and its dryness in the first" (line 16541). "It benefits old men (*mashāyikh*) by the gentleness of its warming" (ينفع المشايخ بلطف تسخينه; line 16542). Cosmetic: one kind of *mandah* dyes the hand and is suitable to follow the henna (lines 16542-16543). Head: "it benefits the brain and the senses" (ينفع الدماغ والحواس). Chest: "it benefits the heart greatly" (ينفع القلب جدا; line 16544).

### Active Components

Ambergris is a waxy mass formed in the gut of the sperm whale; it contains ambrein, a triterpenoid alcohol, which oxidises on ageing to the odorants prized in perfumery. This is general knowledge; the Canon names no constituent.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The entry is a perfume and a gentle warming tonic for the old, the head, the senses and the heart. It is not a recommended practice. Natural ambergris is rare and costly, and adulterated material is common (the Canon already says so).

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Hot in the second degree and dry in the first, "it is likely" (*Canon* Book II, entry *ʿanbar*, line 16541).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(b) Framed.* The *Canon* records "it benefits old men by the gentleness of its warming" (line 16542) and benefit to the brain, senses and heart (lines 16543-16544). These are tonic actions in Avicenna's terms. No cultivation status (no ranking as a life-lengthening drug) is attested, and the text does not speak of innate heat, radical moisture or *ruh*, so none is added.

**Practitioner's note.** A rare aromatic in the text, warming and gentle, for the old and for the head and heart. What a cultivator does with it today is mostly nothing: the supply is scarce, adulterated and legally awkward in places, and the text asks for no more than a small warming aromatic.

### Contraindications

No contraindication is given in the text. Trade in natural ambergris is regulated or restricted in some countries because it comes from a protected animal; legal status varies by place. [USER: verify] Fragrance sensitisation is possible. Adulterated or synthetic material is common.
