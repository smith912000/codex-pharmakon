### Origin & Tradition

The blue poppies of the genus *Meconopsis* are among the iconic wildflowers of the high Himalaya and Tibetan plateau — poppy-family plants (Papaveraceae) bearing, in many species, luminous blue flowers at extreme altitude. Several *Meconopsis* species are important in Tibetan medicine, where they belong to groups recorded in the *rGyud-bzhi*; *Meconopsis torquata* is one of the high-plateau species so used. (Unlike the opium poppy, *Papaver somniferum*, the *Meconopsis* poppies are not sources of opium; their chemistry and use are quite distinct.)

In the Tibetan tradition the blue poppies are used as **cooling herbs to clear heat and inflammation** — for "hot" disorders of the **liver and lungs**, for fever, for inflammatory and infectious conditions, for pain and (in some species) for bone/traumatic conditions, and for headache. The whole flowering plant is gathered at altitude and dried for decoction or compound formulae. The genus carries the Papaveraceae family's characteristic **alkaloid** chemistry (distinct from the morphinan opiates of *Papaver*), and modern phytochemical study of *Meconopsis* has found a range of alkaloids and flavonoids consistent with anti-inflammatory, analgesic and hepatoprotective traditional uses.

These are high-altitude, regionally specific medicines, documented here from the Tibetan *rGyud-bzhi* tradition that the canonical index draws upon.
