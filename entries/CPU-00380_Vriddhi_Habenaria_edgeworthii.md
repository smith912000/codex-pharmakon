# Vriddhi — Vṛddhi, the paired Aṣṭavarga orchid ("Ṛddhi-Vṛddhi"); "Growth/Increase"
## *Habenaria edgeworthii* Hook.f. ex Collett | Family: Orchidaceae
**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā); Aṣṭavarga group; classical rasāyana / Jīvanīya tradition
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Rasa — madhura (sweet); Vīrya — śīta (cooling); Vipāka — madhura (sweet); primarily Vātapittahara, Jīvanīya (life-promoting), Rasāyana.
**Training Phase:** Deep Restorative (Rasāyana) · Ojas-building / Jīvanīya · Reproductive & tissue nourishment

> ⚠️ **Conservation & substitution flag:** *Habenaria edgeworthii* is a rare, wild-harvested high-Himalayan terrestrial orchid and, as one of the eight *Aṣṭavarga* drugs, is **endangered / commercially scarce and habitually substituted** in the market. Vṛddhi is the twin of Ṛddhi (*H. intermedia*, I_426); the two are named and used together. Authentic Vṛddhi is frequently unavailable and replaced by approved *pratinidhi dravya* (substitutes). Treat any commercial "Vriddhi" as **likely substituted unless provenance is verified**, and **do not source wild-harvested tubers** — wild collection is a serious conservation and legal concern.

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS) & the Aṣṭavarga:** Vṛddhi belongs to the **Aṣṭavarga** — the eight rare, sweet, cooling, unctuous, life-promoting (Jīvanīya) drugs at the summit of the rasāyana pharmacopoeia (Jīvaka, Ṛṣabhaka, Meda, Mahāmeda, Kākolī, Kṣīrakākolī, Ṛddhi, Vṛddhi). Within this group Vṛddhi is the constant companion of Ṛddhi; the pair is treated almost as a single dravya ("Ṛddhi-Vṛddhi") in the classical restorative formulations — most famously **Cyavanaprāśa** and the Aṣṭavarga ghees and rejuvenative confections. Classical actions are *jīvanīya* (vitalising), *bṛṃhaṇa* (bulk/tissue-building), *balya* (strengthening), *śukrala* (reproductive-promoting), *vayaḥsthāpana* (age-sustaining), with indications in *kṣaya* (wasting), debility, seminal depletion and rakta-pitta.

**Rasāyana tradition:** As a sweet, cooling, heavy, nutritive tuber, Vṛddhi is a paradigmatic *ojas*- and *śukra*-building substance, employed where deep vitality and tissue essence are depleted and require rebuilding over time.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Second Goddess of Increase:** If Ṛddhi is "prosperity," *Vṛddhi* is quite literally **"growth, increase, augmentation, flourishing"** — the word used for the waxing moon, for the swelling of prosperity, for anything that grows fuller. The Aṣṭavarga tradition's decision to name a *pair* of near-identical orchids after two goddesses of abundance is doctrinally deliberate: it doubles and emphasises the single therapeutic idea these drugs embody — *increase itself*. Where most medicines subtract (drain heat, purge, dry, cut), Ṛddhi-Vṛddhi *add*: they are the pharmacology of filling, of building the wasted body back toward fullness. The near-indistinguishability of the twin orchids — same genus, same cold high-altitude habitat, same swollen sweet tuber — mirrors the near-indistinguishability of the two goddesses, and the tradition's insistence on using them *together* enacts a symbolic completeness: prosperity (ṛddhi) and growth (vṛddhi) as the two faces of restoration. In Kenetica's frame Vṛddhi is the *completion of the increase-pair* — the second half of the deep anabolic principle that rebuilds substance, vitality and reproductive essence in the truly depleted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica, Vṛddhi functions as the **paired deep nutritive rasāyana** to Ṛddhi — a sweet, cooling, *bṛṃhaṇa* (anabolic) restorative for genuine depletion: wasting, convalescent debility, reproductive/tissue depletion, and dry, over-fired, run-down constitutions that need building rather than clearing. Its role and signature are effectively identical to Ṛddhi's — nourishing, moistening, ojas- and śukra-supporting, immunomodulatory in the rasāyana sense — and classical practice uses the two together to complete the "increase" action. **Given the same scarcity, Kenetica treats Vṛddhi chiefly as a doctrinal/formulary member ordinarily filled by an approved substitute**, not as a routinely available single drug.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, species-specific research on *Habenaria edgeworthii* / Vṛddhi is **effectively absent** — a targeted search this run for "Habenaria edgeworthii Vriddhi Astavarga" returned **zero** indexed articles. This is an honest and expected gap for a rare, substituted Aṣṭavarga orchid, and no efficacy claim can be made from primary trials of this species.

The closest available evidence is from its **pair-species and near-taxonomic twin**, *Habenaria intermedia* (Ṛddhi), on which a 2023 study isolated seven tuber constituents and reported **immunomodulatory and anti-NLRP3-inflammasome activity** (spleen-cell proliferation; IL-2/TNF-α/IFN-γ secretion; reversal of cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression), explicitly validating the plant's "prominent Rasāyana" reputation (Kumar C et al., 2023, *ACS Omega* — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.3c03071)). Because Ṛddhi and Vṛddhi are the same genus, closely related, and used as one paired drug, this is offered as **adjacent context only — not as direct evidence for *H. edgeworthii*.**

**Evidence grade: VERY LOW / traditional-only for this species.** No *H. edgeworthii*-specific studies retrieved; the single supporting paper concerns the paired species. Jīvanīya/ojas/śukra claims are classical.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** madhura (sweet)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** guru (heavy), snigdha (unctuous), often picchila (slimy/mucilaginous)
- **Vīrya (potency):** śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive taste):** madhura (sweet)
- **Doṣa action:** Vātapittahara (pacifies Vāta and Pitta); increases Kapha
- **Karma (actions):** jīvanīya (life-promoting), bṛṃhaṇa (anabolic/tissue-building), balya (strengthening), rasāyana (rejuvenative), śukrala/vājīkara-supportive (reproductive), vayaḥsthāpana (age-sustaining), immunomodulatory (classical bala/ojas), raktapittahara
- **Srotas / systems:** rasavaha & raktavaha (plasma/blood), śukravaha (reproductive), ojas/immunity, mucosa

### Contraindications

Heavy, sweet, unctuous and Kapha-increasing — use with care or avoid in obesity, diabetes/medas disorders, congestion, high Kapha and weak digestion (mandāgni); can cause heaviness and sluggishness. Not for acute infection or high-āma states. The dominant practical hazard is **adulteration/misidentification**, since authentic material is rare and routinely substituted.
