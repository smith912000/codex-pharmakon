# Common Yew — *smilax/milos* (Greek, Dioscorides' deadly tree); *taxus* (Latin); the churchyard yew, the "tree of death"

## *Taxus baccata* L. | Family: Taxaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, CM, DMM
**Energetic Classification:** Cold & dry in the extreme — classed among the *deadly poisons* (Galenic *venenum frigidum*), not a graded therapeutic hot/cold drug
**Training Phase:** Threshold (the death-adjacent teaching entry — the poison that became a cure; studied, never self-administered)

---

> ☠️ **DEADLY POISON — ONE OF THE MOST TOXIC TREES IN THE TEMPERATE WORLD.** Every part of *Taxus baccata* except the fleshy red aril is lethally poisonous. The **taxine alkaloids** cause cardiac conduction collapse and fatal arrhythmia; **there is no specific antidote.** Ingestion of a small quantity of needles can kill. **This entry is historical, doctrinal and pharmacological. Nothing in it is a preparation instruction. Do not ingest any part of the yew.**

---

### Origin & Tradition

Few plants carry a doctrinal arc as complete as the yew's: it is the archetypal **Tree of Death** of the classical and Northern European imagination — and, in the twentieth century, the source of **paclitaxel (Taxol)** and **docetaxel**, among the most important anticancer drugs ever discovered. The poison and the cure are the same molecule-family's cytotoxicity, pointed at different cells. The Codex files the yew as the volume's supreme *poison-into-cure* teaching.

**Assyrian / Mesopotamian (SS).** The yew is not native to Mesopotamia, and no secure cuneiform identification of *Taxus* should be assumed; the deadly-tree/poison category, however, is well represented in the tablet corpus. **[USER: verify — do not assign a specific Akkadian plant-name to yew.]** The tradition's relevance here is the general one: the recognition of a class of trees whose substance kills, handled with ritual caution rather than as ordinary medicine.

**Avicenna / Unani (AT).** In the Galenic-Unani framework transmitted through Ibn Sīnā, the yew belongs not to the graded therapeutic drugs but to the **lethal cold poisons (sumūm)** — substances that extinguish the innate heat and stop the pulse. Where used at all it was as an external agent or a poison to be guarded against and antidoted, never a routine internal remedy. The classification "cold and dry in the extreme, deadly" is the tradition's accurate pharmacological verdict.

**Dioscorides / Graeco-Roman (DMM).** Dioscorides' *De Materia Medica* is explicit and unusually blunt: the yew (Greek *smilax*, the tree of Narbonne especially) is **deadly** — he warns that people who sleep or eat beneath it sicken or die, and that its fruit/foliage is fatal. Roman authors (Caesar, Pliny) record yew as a suicide and arrow-poison. The tree is sacred to the underworld, associated with Hecate and the Furies, and by the medieval period planted in **churchyards and graveyards** — the living emblem of death, mourning and (through its extreme longevity and evergreen habit) paradoxically of resurrection.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** The yew doctrine is the Codex's crown example of a symbolic reading that turns out to be *literally, mechanistically true — and then reverses into its opposite.*

The classical doctrine says: *the yew is the tree of death; its substance stops life.* The chemistry confirms it exactly. The **taxine alkaloids (taxine A, taxine B, isotaxine B)** are cardiotoxins that block cardiac sodium and calcium channels, collapsing conduction and producing the "chaotic," unshockable arrhythmias that kill yew-poisoning victims. The graveyard planting, the underworld sacredness, the "do not sleep beneath it" warning — these are folk-encodings of a genuinely lethal cardiotoxic pharmacology. The doctrine was *right*.

Then modernity completes the arc. The same defining property — **cytotoxicity, the capacity to arrest a living process** — was turned, via a different constituent (the diterpenoid **paclitaxel** from the bark and needles), against **cancer cells**. Paclitaxel does not poison the heart's rhythm; it **stabilises microtubules**, freezing the mitotic spindle so that dividing cells cannot complete mitosis and die by cell-cycle arrest in G2/M. The "tree of death" kills *dividing* cells preferentially — which is precisely what a tumour is. The underworld symbol becomes a cancer therapeutic without ever ceasing to be a poison: the doctrine of death was not overturned, it was *redirected*. **This is the exemplary poison-into-cure mapping in the plant volume — the symbolism and the pharmacology are the same fact seen from two sides.** The Codex is careful, though, to keep the two chemistries distinct: taxine (the graveyard cardiotoxin) and paclitaxel (the chemotherapeutic) are different compounds from the same tree; the redemption is a triumph of *isolation and dose*, not of the whole-plant poison becoming safe.

### Active Components

- **Taxine alkaloids — taxine A, taxine B, isotaxine B** — the principal **cardiotoxic** constituents (all parts except the aril flesh); block cardiac Na⁺/Ca²⁺ conduction. **3,5-dimethoxyphenol** is a forensic marker of *Taxus* ingestion.
- **Paclitaxel (Taxol)** and related taxane diterpenoids — originally isolated from *Taxus brevifolia* bark; present in *T. baccata* needles; the **microtubule-stabilising anticancer** pharmacophore.
- **10-deacetylbaccatin III (10-DAB)** — the *T. baccata* needle precursor from which **docetaxel** and semi-synthetic paclitaxel are manufactured (sparing the slow-growing bark).
- **Cyanogenic glycoside (taxiphyllin), ecdysteroids, lignans.**
- The **red aril** (fleshy seed-cup) flesh is the sole non-toxic part — but the **seed within it is lethal**.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

The yew is a **Threshold** entry: a poison studied for what its danger teaches and for the drug it became, never a self-administered remedy. Its two pharmacological identities:
1. **Taxine cardiotoxin** — of forensic and toxicological interest only; the basis of its "tree of death" identity and of every contraindication below.
2. **Taxane chemotherapeutics** — paclitaxel and docetaxel, mainstays of oncology (ovarian, breast, non-small-cell lung, and other cancers), acting by microtubule stabilisation and mitotic arrest. These are administered **only** as purified, dose-controlled pharmaceuticals under oncological supervision — the entire clinical point is that the isolated molecule at a controlled dose severs cure from the whole-plant poison.

### Preparation & Dosing

| Preparation | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Any internal whole-plant preparation | **None — lethal** | All parts except aril flesh are deadly; no safe internal dose exists. |
| Taxane chemotherapy (paclitaxel/docetaxel) | Pharmaceutical dosing, IV, oncologist-directed | Purified drug only; **not derivable from home preparation**. Out of scope for self-use. |
| Aril (red flesh) | Not recommended | Flesh is non-toxic but the enclosed seed is lethal; separating them is not worth the risk. |

There is **no traditional therapeutic internal dose** for yew — its internal history is one of poisoning, suicide and homicide, not of graded medicine.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- **Froldi et al. (2010), *Journal of Analytical Toxicology*** — a fatal *T. baccata* poisoning confirmed forensically; **taxine A and taxine B** identified as the cardiotoxic alkaloids, with **3,5-dimethoxyphenol** as the marker of ingestion. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jat/34.1.53). Documents lethality and mechanism-class.
- **Zutter et al. (2019), *Pediatric Emergency Care*** — an adolescent who ingested yew needles suffered out-of-hospital cardiac arrest and required **>6 hours of CPR**, with ventricular tachycardia and broad-complex bradycardia; survived with intensive supportive care. Notes **no specific therapy exists**; taxine cardiotoxicity is prolonged. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001196). Confirms the "chaotic arrhythmia, no antidote" hazard.
- **Buetler et al. (2023), *Clinical Toxicology*** — case series of 55 ED presentations (11 patients) after yew exposure; symptoms ranged from GI disturbance to life-threatening cardiotoxicity (tachycardia, hypotension); management by gastroscopic leaf extraction and activated charcoal. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2022.2158097). Establishes the clinical spectrum and dose-dependence.
- **Ahmed Khalil et al. (2022), *Current Pharmaceutical Design*** — review of **paclitaxel (Taxol)**: isolated from *Taxus* species, it **selectively targets microtubules and causes cell-cycle arrest in the G2/M phase**, producing concentration- and time-dependent cytotoxicity; total synthesis is commercially non-viable, so plant-cell-culture and semisynthesis (from 10-DAB) are used. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/1381612829666221102155212). The mechanistic basis of the "redemption" arc.
- **Maeda et al. (2023), *Mutation Research*** — Taxol inhibits microtubule disassembly (G2/M arrest) **and** generates reactive oxygen species causing oxidative DNA damage; PARP-deficient cells are hypersensitive, and PARP inhibition (olaparib) potentiates Taxol cytotoxicity. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mrfmmm.2023.111826). Adds a second cytotoxic mechanism (oxidative DNA damage) beyond pure microtubule stabilisation.

**Evidence grade: HIGH.** Both halves of the arc are strongly documented: taxine cardiotoxicity (multiple forensic/clinical reports, no antidote) and paclitaxel's microtubule-stabilising, mitosis-arresting anticancer mechanism (established pharmacology, decades of clinical oncology use). **Honest boundary:** the whole-plant poison is *not* a cancer remedy — only the isolated, dose-controlled taxanes are therapeutic. Folk "yew tea" cancer cures are dangerous fabrications and are explicitly rejected here.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic / Unani (attested):** cold & dry in the extreme; classed among the deadly cold poisons (*sumūm*), not graded therapeutics.
- **TCM:** *Taxus* spp. are not a classical Chinese materia medica staple in this role; modern Chinese use is of isolated taxanes as chemotherapy, not traditional. Marked **not attested** as a classical drug.
- **Ayurveda:** *Taxus wallichiana* (Himalayan yew, "sthauneya/talispatra"-associated) has some regional use, but *T. baccata* as such is **not a classical dravya** — marked **not attested** rather than invented; do not conflate the species.

### Contraindications

**☠️ DEADLY — NO SELF-USE OF ANY KIND.**

- **Taxine cardiotoxicity:** all parts except the aril flesh contain cardiotoxic taxine alkaloids; ingestion causes bradyarrhythmia, ventricular tachycardia, widening QRS, hypotension and **cardiac arrest**. **No specific antidote exists**; management is prolonged supportive resuscitation only. Small quantities of needles can be fatal.
- **All parts deadly except the red aril flesh — and the seed inside the aril is lethal.** Never rely on distinguishing them.
- **No home/traditional internal preparation is safe.** "Yew tea," tinctures, or folk anticancer preparations are potentially fatal and must never be attempted.
- **Taxane chemotherapy** (paclitaxel/docetaxel) is a separate, pharmaceutical-only reality with its own serious toxicity profile (myelosuppression, neuropathy, hypersensitivity) and is administered exclusively under oncological supervision — it is **not** obtainable or derivable from the plant by any non-industrial means.
- Handle horticulturally with care; keep clippings away from livestock (yew is a common cause of fatal animal poisoning) and children.
