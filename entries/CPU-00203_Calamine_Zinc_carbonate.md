### Origin & Tradition

Calamine is a **zinc-carbonate ore** (smithsonite; historically the term also covered zinc silicate, hemimorphite, and the furnace-sublimate zinc oxide known as *tutty* or *cadmia*/*pompholyx*). It is one of the oldest topical medicines in continuous use on earth — the pink, mild, astringent "calamine" of the modern medicine cabinet is the direct descendant of the zinc-ore preparations of antiquity. In the Chinese materia medica it is **Lú Gān Shí** ("furnace sweet stone"), the premier mineral for **eye and skin** disorders, and it is essentially never taken internally: calamine is the archetypal *external* stone-medicine.

**Chinese Tradition (Lú Gān Shí):** processed (calcined and often quenched in herbal decoctions such as coptis water), Lú Gān Shí is the great topical agent that **clears heat, dries damp, astringes, generates flesh, and — above all — brightens the eyes**: it is a classic ingredient of eye powders and washes for red, swollen, weeping, or filmed eyes, and of powders for weeping eczema, ulcers, and non-healing damp sores. Its action is drying, protective, mildly antiseptic, and tissue-supporting.

**Egyptian and Greco-Roman Traditions (EP, and the cadmia tradition):** zinc-bearing eye and skin remedies are ancient — Egyptian eye-paints and medicines used mineral cosmetics/medicaments, and the Greco-Roman pharmacists knew the furnace zinc-sublimates **cadmia**, **pompholyx**, and **spodos** (Dioscorides, Galen) as prized ingredients of eye-salves (collyria) and wound powders — the same zinc-oxide/carbonate chemistry, drying and healing, that underlies calamine.

**Tibetan Medicine (FMT):** zinc and the astringent-cooling stone-medicines are used in the topical, eye, and wound-healing categories of Tibetan practice.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the mild stone that clears the eye:**
Calamine's doctrine is **gentle astringent protection of the body's most delicate surfaces** — the eye and the weeping skin. Where gypsum (I_343) is the heavy cold stone that quenches deep fever and salt (I_345) the essential mineral of the inner sea, calamine is the *soft, mild, surface* stone: it does not drain or purge but *dries, soothes, protects, and closes*. The recurring cross-cultural focus on the **eye** — "brightening the eyes" in China, the collyria of Greece and Egypt — reflects the mineral's genuine mild astringent-antiseptic gentleness, safe enough for the most sensitive tissue. The Codex reads calamine as the emblem of the **mild protective surface**: the pink dust that soothes the inflamed boundary without harming it — the tenderest of the stone-medicines.

### Active Components

- **Zinc carbonate / zinc oxide (the zinc content)** — zinc is the pharmacologically active element: mildly astringent, protective, drying, and supportive of epithelial/wound healing (zinc is a genuine cofactor in tissue repair). The processed mineral provides a fine, insoluble, adherent powder that coats and protects the surface.
- **(Modern pharmaceutical "calamine")** — officinally a mixture of **zinc oxide with ~0.5% ferric oxide** (which gives the characteristic pink colour), formulated as calamine lotion — a long-established over-the-counter **antipruritic/protective** skin preparation.
- **Trace iron and processing residues** — the ferric oxide (colour) and any herb-decoction residues from traditional quenching (e.g. coptis, adding a berberine antimicrobial layer).

**Primary domain: RESET — topical only. Weeping/itching skin, minor wounds, and (traditionally) inflamed eyes.**

1. **Itch and weeping skin (RESET):** calamine's classic modern use — an antipruritic, drying, protective lotion for heat rash, contact/allergic dermatitis, insect bites, chafing, and the sweat-and-friction skin problems of training. Genuinely useful and safe.

### Scientific Correlation

*Attribution: calamine's status rests on established zinc chemistry and long pharmacopoeial use rather than on modern efficacy trials; stated honestly.*

The active principle is **zinc** (as carbonate/oxide), a mildly astringent, protective element with a genuine role in epithelial and wound healing. **Calamine lotion** (zinc oxide with ferric oxide) is a **longstanding officinal (pharmacopoeial) topical antipruritic and skin protectant** — its standing is that of an established, safe, mild OTC preparation rather than a trial-proven active drug, and controlled efficacy trials are limited; its value is soothing/protective/drying. The traditional Chinese eye and sore uses rest on the same mild astringent-antiseptic zinc action plus (in coptis-quenched preparations) an added berberine antimicrobial layer. This entry grades calamine as **chemically well-defined, pharmacopoeially established for mild topical skin use, and safe — but not a trial-validated therapeutic beyond its protective/antipruritic role.**

### Contraindications

- **Internal use — do not ingest:** calamine/Lú Gān Shí is an **external** medicine; it is not for internal consumption.
- **Deep/heavily-exuding or infected wounds:** the drying protective powder is for minor, superficial, weeping lesions; deep or seriously infected wounds need proper medical care.
- **Eyes:** modern eye complaints (infection, injury) require ophthalmic evaluation and sterile ophthalmic products, not improvised mineral powders.
- **Allergy/sensitisation:** uncommon but possible contact reaction to lotion components.
- **Evidence honesty:** calamine's standing is pharmacopoeial/traditional (mild protective/antipruritic), not trial-validated beyond that role.
