# Field Pennycress — Xi Ming (菥蓂), Pennycress, Stinkweed, Mithridate Mustard

## *Thlaspi arvense* L. | Family: Brassicaceae

**Tradition Tags:** BM, CM, DMM, SBJ
**Energetic Classification:** Hot & Dry (2nd degree) — pungent-bitter depurative (Galenic/Unani)
**Training Phase:** Purification & Clearing

### Origin & Tradition

Field pennycress is a cosmopolitan weed of the mustard family, recognisable by its round, notched, coin-shaped seed-pods (the *Thlaspi* of the Greeks, from *thlaein*, "to crush," referencing the flattened pod). Across the manuscript traditions it belongs squarely to the great class of "sharp seeds" — the pungent cresses and mustards prescribed to cut phlegm, open obstructions, and drive out corrupt humours.

**Assyrian / Mesopotamian (Babylonian Medicine).** The cuneiform medical corpus catalogues numerous *Brassica*-type "sharp" and "bitter" seeds used in poultices for swellings and in purgative draughts. A specific, secure binomial identification of pennycress in the Akkadian herbal is not established, so any native Akkadian name must be treated as conjectural [USER: verify against Assyrian herbal concordance]. What is genuine and general is the Mesopotamian doctrinal placement of acrid mustard-family seeds among the drugs that "burn" and "scour" — the same category later systematised by the Greeks.

**Dioscorides (*De Materia Medica*).** Dioscorides describes *thlaspi* explicitly, noting its flat pods and biting seed, and prescribes it as a sharp, heating drug: taken in quantity it purges bile and phlegm upward and downward, provokes urine and menses, and — crushed — is applied to sciatica and inflammatory swellings. This is the earliest unambiguous attestation and fixes the plant's classical profile as a **depurative, diuretic, and counter-irritant**.

**Avicenna (*Canon of Medicine*).** In the Arabic-Galenic synthesis the *thlaspi*-type cresses are classed *ḥārr yābis* (hot and dry, ~2nd degree), attenuating (*mulaṭṭif*) and deobstruent (*mufattiḥ*). Such seeds were held to thin thick humours, open blockages of the liver and spleen, provoke urine, and resolve cold inflammatory masses by their penetrating heat — a rationalised extension of Dioscorides' empirical picture.

**Chinese Materia Medica (Xi Ming / Xi Ming Zi 菥蓂子).** *Xi Ming* is an old entry in the Chinese herbal tradition, listed among the seeds that "brighten the eyes" and clear heat; the seed (*Xi Ming Zi*) has been used for red, swollen, painful eyes and, in later folk practice, for intestinal inflammation of the appendicitis type. It is classed as pungent-bitter and cooling-draining in effect — a near-mirror of the Western "sharp seed that clears obstruction," reached independently.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Every tradition converges on one image: a *seed that bites*. The doctrine — "sharp, penetrating, scours out corruption, opens what is blocked" — maps with unusual directness onto the plant's real chemistry. Pennycress, like all Brassicaceae, stores the glucosinolate **sinigrin** in inert form alongside the enzyme **myrosinase** in separate compartments. Crushing the seed (exactly the *thlaein*, the "crushing," embedded in the plant's name) ruptures those compartments and detonates the enzyme onto the substrate, releasing volatile **allyl isothiocyanate (AITC)** — the pungent, lachrymatory, tissue-biting principle of mustard and wasabi. The ancient sensory test *was* the assay: the burn on the tongue is the isothiocyanate, and the doctrinal category "hot, dry, penetrating" is a faithful description of an electrophilic volatile that reacts with and irritates living tissue. The symbolic "opening of obstruction" (deobstruent, diuretic, emmenagogue) corresponds to genuine counter-irritant and mucosal-stimulant actions of AITC; the "clearing of corrupt humour" corresponds to its antimicrobial reactivity. The doctrine is not mysticism dressed up — it is a pre-molecular account of an enzyme-triggered chemical weapon.

### Active Components

- **Glucosinolates — chiefly sinigrin (allyl glucosinolate)**: the dominant defensive thioglucoside of *T. arvense* seed; enzymatically converted on tissue damage.
- **Myrosinase (thioglucosidase)**: compartmentalised β-thioglucosidase that hydrolyses sinigrin to the aglucone, which rearranges to **allyl isothiocyanate** (AITC). Specifier proteins in *T. arvense* can divert this to nitriles and thiocyanate instead (the thiocyanate-forming protein TaTFP has been biochemically characterised).
- **Allyl isothiocyanate (AITC)**: the pungent volatile end-product — electrophilic, antimicrobial, a TRPA1 agonist, and the molecular basis of the "sharpness."
- **Flavonoids** (kaempferol/quercetin glycosides typical of the family) and **phenolic acids**: antioxidant, mildly anti-inflammatory.
- **Seed fixed oil** (erucic-acid-rich, ~30%+): the basis of modern interest in pennycress as a low-input oilseed/biofuel cover crop ("pennycress/Covercress").

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Pennycress sits in the **Purification & Clearing** phase as a minor, sharp depurative — a "scouring" agent rather than a tonic. Its Kenetica role is didactic and secondary: it demonstrates the enzyme-activated pungent principle (the mustard-oil bomb) in a humble weed, and stands as a counter-irritant/diuretic in the same functional family as mustard and cress. It is **not** a front-line clinical remedy in the modern programme; it is retained for tradition-continuity, for its eye/heat-clearing lineage (Xi Ming Zi), and as an entry point to the wider glucosinolate pharmacology.

### Scientific Correlation

The chemistry underlying the traditional "sharp, scouring" doctrine is well established, but plant-specific clinical data for *Thlaspi arvense* are sparse — the evidence is largely at the level of the shared active molecule (AITC) and the genus, not the herb in humans.

- **Glucosinolate→isothiocyanate activation** is mechanistically resolved: myrosinase hydrolysis of the thioglucoside yields the aglucone, which rearranges to the isothiocyanate, with specifier proteins diverting product to nitriles/thiocyanate. *According to PubMed:* Eisenschmidt-Bönn et al., *Plant J.* 2019 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/tpj.14327); and the *T. arvense* thiocyanate-forming protein (TaTFP) has been characterised by EPR — Gumataw/… *Biol. Chem.* 2023 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/hsz-2023-0187). This directly validates the "crush-to-activate" empirical practice embedded in the plant's name.
- **Anti-inflammatory / antimicrobial action of the released AITC** is demonstrated, though in other model systems: AITC suppressed periodontal tissue destruction in mice with both bacteriostatic and anti-inflammatory activity against *Porphyromonas gingivalis* — Yasunaga et al., *Arch. Oral Biol.* 2024 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.archoralbio.2024.106118). Brassica-seed isothiocyanates (allyl-ITC among them) suppressed the virulence of the plant pathogen *Ralstonia solanacearum* — Peng et al., *Pest Manag. Sci.* 2021 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ps.6552). These support the doctrinal "clears corruption / resolves inflammation" claim *at the molecule level*.
- **Genus-level ecology**: *Thlaspi/Noccaea* species are model metal-hyperaccumulators, a reminder that field-collected pennycress can concentrate soil metals (Zn, Cd, Ni) — a real safety caveat for medicinal harvest — Kolbas et al., *Int. J. Mol. Sci.* 2025 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms26178748).
- **Modern non-medicinal interest** centres on pennycress as an oilseed/biofuel cover crop, not as a drug.

**Evidence grade: LOW / genus-level.** The doctrine↔chemistry mapping (pungency = isothiocyanate) is strong and mechanistically sound; direct clinical evidence for *T. arvense* as an anti-inflammatory or depurative in humans is essentially absent. Honest verdict: the tradition correctly identified a real reactive antimicrobial/counter-irritant chemistry, but the specific therapeutic claims (appendicitis, eye disease) remain **unproven** and should not be presented as validated.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Chinese (Xi Ming Zi):** pungent-bitter, cooling/draining; "clears heat, brightens the eyes." (Attested in the Chinese materia medica lineage.)
- **Ayurvedic (rasa/guna/virya/vipaka):** *not attested* as a classical Ayurvedic dravya — do not assign invented rasa values.
- **Galenic/Unani (Avicenna):** hot & dry ~2nd degree; attenuant (*mulaṭṭif*), deobstruent (*mufattiḥ*), diuretic, emmenagogue.

### Contraindications

Pregnancy (traditional emmenagogue action; isothiocyanate/uterine-stimulant caution) — avoid. Not for prolonged or high-dose internal use: AITC is a mucosal and skin irritant, and large seed quantities may cause gastric burning. Topical poultices can blister sensitive skin (intended counter-irritant effect — limit contact time). **Do not harvest from contaminated ground:** *Thlaspi* is a known heavy-metal hyperaccumulator. The seed oil is erucic-acid-rich and unsuitable as a food oil unless specifically low-erucic cultivars. Seek professional guidance before internal use; doses above are unverified.
