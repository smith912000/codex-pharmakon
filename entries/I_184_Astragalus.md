# I-184 · Astragalus
**Astragalus membranaceus** (Fisch. ex Link) Bunge | Fabaceae (Leguminosae)

---

## Section 1 — Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Accepted name:** *Astragalus membranaceus* (Fisch. ex Link) Bunge (1868)  
**Pharmacopoeial synonym:** *A. membranaceus* var. *mongholicus* (Bunge) Hsiao = "Huang Qi" variant; both treated as equivalent in most pharmacopoeias  
**Common names (English):** Astragalus, milkvetch, Chinese milkvetch, huang qi root  
**Traditional names:**
- **Huang Qi (黄芪 / 黄耆):** "Yellow elder/senior" — yellow from root color, *qi* = elder/superior → "yellow superior one"; reflects highest-tier tonic classification in TCM
- Japanese: *Ōgi* (黄耆)
- Korean: *Hwanggi* (황기)
- Arabic/Unani: *Katira* (different species context); *Unshul al-sana*

**Critical taxonomic note:** *Astragalus* is the LARGEST GENUS of vascular plants — approximately 3,000 species worldwide. The name "astragalus" without species specification is ambiguous:
- *A. membranaceus*: Chinese pharmacopoeial herb (Huang Qi); SAFE — primary subject of this entry
- *A. gummifer* and others: gum tragacanth producers (food industry)
- *A. lentiginosus*, *A. mollissimus*, *A. bisulcatus*: **TOXIC LOCOWEEDS** (North American) — selenium accumulation + swainsonine (Golgi mannosidase inhibitor → "locoweed" neurological syndrome in cattle)
- Species identification ESSENTIAL — the genus spans from premier tonic herb to toxic livestock poison

**Identifying morphology of *A. membranaceus*:**
- Perennial herb to 1m; pinnately compound leaves with 12–18 pairs of leaflets; yellowish-white pea-family flowers; seed pod characteristic legume
- **Root (medicinal part):** Taproot to 60cm, 1–3cm diameter; yellow-brown exterior, yellow interior; slightly sweet taste; fibrous, not friable
- 4-year-old roots preferred; older roots have higher polysaccharide and saponin content

**Part used:** Dried root (*Radix Astragali*); harvested in autumn from 4-year-old plants

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**Single-tradition depth vs. multi-traditional breadth:**

Astragalus presents the inverse of horse chestnut's situation: where horse chestnut has low convergence but high modern evidence, astragalus has moderate convergence (within Sino-Tibetan) but is also one of the most intensively studied immunomodulatory herbs in contemporary pharmacology.

**The "Wei Qi" concept and immunology:**

The TCM concept of *Wei Qi* (衛氣) — defensive qi, the body's protective energy at the surface — maps conceptually onto aspects of innate immunity: first-line defensive response, protection against external pathogens, maintenance of functional integrity at the body's interfaces. Huang Qi's primary traditional action was tonifying Wei Qi. The modern finding that astragalus polysaccharides modulate innate immune signaling (TLR pathways, macrophage activation, NK cell activity) represents a convergence of traditional theoretical framework with molecular pharmacology.

**Superior tonic classification:**

The *Shennong Bencao Jing* "three-tier" classification of herbs (superior/upper class = tonic, non-toxic, taken long-term; middle class = therapeutic, some toxicity; lower class = toxic, limited use) placed Huang Qi in the superior/upper class alongside Ginseng and Reishi. This classification implies: designed for extended use, no significant side effects, for maintaining health rather than treating acute disease. Modern pharmacological profile is consistent: the drug-like adverse effects are minimal; the immunomodulatory effects are cumulative.

**Telomere biology discovery (21st century):**

Cycloastragenol (the aglycone of astragaloside IV) activates telomerase — extending telomere length in human cells. This represents a genuinely novel pharmacological action not predicted by any traditional theory. Neither TCM theory nor Western pharmacology anticipated that a plant could activate telomerase; this was discovered through high-throughput screening programs. The discovery launched TA-65 (cycloastragenol supplement) and significant ongoing anti-aging research. Whether this in vitro mechanism translates to meaningful human anti-aging effects remains actively debated.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Primary active compound classes:**

**1. Polysaccharides (Astragalus Polysaccharides — APS)**
- Heterogeneous mixture: APS-I, APS-II, APS-III (molecular weight 36–160 kDa)
- Primary immunomodulatory active compounds
- β-glycosidic linkages; branched structures; toll-like receptor agonists
- Water-soluble; good oral bioavailability (partial intestinal fermentation)
- 40–70% total polysaccharide content of dried root
- Major mechanism: TLR4 agonism → NF-κB pathway → macrophage activation, cytokine production

**2. Saponins (Cycloartane-type triterpene glycosides)**
- Astragaloside I, II, III, IV (primary saponins)
- **Astragaloside IV** = primary saponin; cycloartane-type triterpene
- **Cycloastragenol** = aglycone of astragaloside IV (enzymatic/acid hydrolysis product)
- Cycloastragenol activates telomerase (hTERT) — anti-aging mechanism
- 0.02–0.2% total saponin content (low relative to polysaccharides)
- Bioavailability challenge: astragaloside IV → gut microbiome metabolism → cycloastragenol → systemically absorbed; interindividual variation significant

**3. Flavonoids**
- Calycosin (calycosin-7-glucoside primary): isoflavone; phytoestrogenic
- Formononetin: isoflavone; weak ER-β agonist
- Ononin, astragalin (kaempferol glycoside)
- Isoflavone content relevant for: hormonal balance, cardiovascular effects, bone density
- Provide antioxidant and mild anti-inflammatory scaffold

**4. Amino Acids and Minerals**
- L-arginine: NO precursor → vasorelaxation; significant content
- Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA): minor
- Selenium: variable; soil-dependent
- Zinc, manganese: present

**Phytoestrogenic note (calycosin/formononetin):**
- Astragalus contains isoflavone phytoestrogens, though at lower concentration than red clover or soy
- Relevant for: hormone-sensitive conditions, elderly women, estrogen-receptor positive malignancies
- See Section 8 for clinical guidance on ER+ breast cancer (theoretical caution, clinically not established as risk)

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**1. Innate Immune Activation (APS — Primary Immunomodulatory Mechanism)**
- APS → TLR4 receptor agonism → MyD88 pathway → NF-κB activation
- Result: macrophage phagocytic activation; NK cell cytotoxicity enhancement
- Increases pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-12) in immunosuppressed contexts
- Also increases anti-inflammatory IL-10 in inflammatory contexts (bidirectional modulation)
- **Immunomodulatory, not strictly immunostimulant** — direction depends on basal immune state
- Key: not simply "boosting" immune system but restoring appropriate immune tone

**2. T-cell and Adaptive Immunity Enhancement**
- Increases CD4+/CD8+ T-cell ratio (shifts toward helper T-cell dominance)
- Enhances Th1/Th2 balance toward Th1 (cellular immunity, anti-tumor surveillance)
- Promotes dendritic cell maturation → enhanced antigen presentation
- NK cell activation → direct cytotoxicity against tumor cells and virally-infected cells

**3. Telomerase Activation (Cycloastragenol)**
- Cycloastragenol → hTERT upregulation → telomerase assembly → telomere extension
- In vitro: measurably extends telomere length in human immune cells and fibroblasts
- Mechanism: direct hTERT protein interaction; transcriptional upregulation
- Clinical relevance: pilot data show telomere length extension in PBMCs of older adults taking cycloastragenol
- Theoretical concern: telomerase is also activated in cancer cells; risk of promoting tumor growth? Current consensus: no increase in cancer cell lines in standard doses; caution in active malignancy

**4. Cardioprotective Mechanisms**
- Astragaloside IV → PI3K/Akt pathway → cardiomyocyte protection from apoptosis
- L-arginine → endothelial NOS → NO → vasorelaxation and blood pressure reduction
- Saponins → reduction of cardiac fibrosis (TGF-β1 inhibition)
- Clinical: potential in heart failure as adjunct

**5. Antiviral**
- APS: induces type I interferon (IFN-α, IFN-β) production → antiviral innate defense
- Astragaloside IV: inhibits viral replication in animal models (influenza, coxsackievirus)
- Mechanism: TLR7/TLR9 agonism → IRF3 → IFN pathway activation

**6. Glucose and Lipid Metabolism**
- AMPK activation (APS) → glucose uptake in muscle
- Hepatic glucose production reduction
- LDL reduction, HDL enhancement (moderate effect)

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Cancer supportive care (best-established human evidence):**

- McCulloch M et al (2006) *Journal of Clinical Oncology* 24(3):419–430: systematic review of 34 RCTs (N=2,815 patients); astragalus-based Chinese herbal formulas + chemotherapy for non-small cell lung cancer; significant improvement in 1-year survival (RR 1.33, P<0.0001) vs. chemotherapy alone; improved tumor response and quality of life metrics
- NOTE: Confounded by multi-herb formulas; attribution to astragalus specifically uncertain
- Qi F et al (2010) *J Ethnopharmacol* 130(3):522–529: meta-analysis astragalus in NSCLC; consistent findings

**Immune function in healthy volunteers:**
- Limited RCT data; Chinese literature predominantly; methodological quality variable
- Cho WCS & Leung KN (2007) *Int Immunopharmacol* 7(8):1024–1031: Astragalus extract; 8 weeks; significant increase in NK cell activity vs. baseline; small N

**Heart failure:**
- Qi W et al (2011) *Phytomedicine* 18(2–3):145–150: IV astragaloside IV (clinical trial China); reduction in LVESV and improvement in EF; P<0.01; limitations: small N, IV route not oral herb
- Zhang JG et al (2012) *J Huazhong Univ Sci Tech* 32(4):568–573: similar positive finding for chronic heart failure

**Chronic kidney disease:**
- Mao XJ et al (2004): oral astragalus in CKD stages 1–3; reduced serum creatinine and proteinuria; controlled but not randomized
- Systematic reviews (Li M et al 2011): positive but low-quality evidence

**Telomere length:**
- Harley CB et al (2011) *Rejuvenation Research* 14(1):45–56: TA-65 (cycloastragenol); N=114; significant increase in proportion of short telomeres in PBMCs vs. placebo; methodologically interesting but small

**Overall evidence quality:**
- Best quality: cancer supportive care (multiple RCTs, systematic reviews)
- Moderate quality: immune function, cardiovascular
- Emerging/pilot: telomere biology, antiviral
- Traditional use: extensive documentation warranting THMP classification

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**2,000 years of tonic culture:**

Huang Qi's appearance in the Shennong Bencao Jing — the earliest extant Chinese pharmacopoeia (c. 200 CE, though sources older) — as a "superior" herb represents the highest classification in that system. The text states it "makes the body light, prolongs life, and can be used over many years" — language used exclusively for the highest-tier adaptogens. This category of herb (superior/jun — sovereign) was meant for cultivation of long-term vitality, taken preventively across decades rather than used curatively in illness.

**The Yellow Emperor's herb:**

The Yellow Emperor (*Huangdi*) from whom the *Huangdi Neijing* (Yellow Emperor's Classic of Medicine, c. 2600 BCE attributed, compiled c. 200 BCE actual) takes its name was later mythologically associated with Huang Qi — "the yellow elder" — through color correspondence (yellow = Earth element = spleen/stomach in Five Elements theory). Whether this connection is historically authentic or later mythological retroactive assignment is uncertain; the association is culturally entrenched.

**Wei Qi and epidemics:**

Traditional Chinese Medical theory attributed epidemic vulnerability to depleted Wei Qi — the protective defensive energy. During epidemic periods (plague, influenza, pox outbreaks), high-ranking patients with access to tonics including Huang Qi were prescribed preventive decoctions. The modern finding that APS enhances interferon production and NK cell activity partially validates this traditional risk management strategy: enhanced innate immune tone does reduce susceptibility to viral infections.

**The TA-65 controversy:**

In 2011, a small company began marketing cycloastragenol as "TA-65" — the first commercially available telomerase activator — at prices ranging from $200 to $600/month. The scientific basis (real telomerase activation, real telomere extension in pilot data) was entangled with commercial marketing claims that exceeded evidence. This triggered one of the largest modern debates in anti-aging medicine: is telomere extension beneficial (extending cellular replicative lifespan, restoring immune aging) or potentially harmful (reducing cellular checkpoints against malignancy)? The debate remains open; the compound is a genuine scientific first.

**Locoweed and the genus shadow:**

Every livestock manager in the American West knows *Astragalus* as the locoweed genus — the plants that cause "locoism" in cattle and horses: bizarre circling behavior, incoordination, progressive neurological decline, and death. This reputation creates a dual identity problem for the genus: the same name covers both one of the most studied tonic herbs in traditional medicine and one of the most widespread livestock neurotoxins in North America. The resolution is species identity: *A. membranaceus* is the tonic; *A. mollissimus*, *A. lentiginosus*, *A. bisulcatus* are the poisons. The name alone is insufficient.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

**Primary indications (evidence-supported):**
- Immune support and reconstitution (post-chemotherapy, chronic infection, recurrent URTI)
- Cancer supportive care (adjunct to chemotherapy; do not replace conventional treatment)
- Chronic fatigue with immune dysfunction
- Long-term tonic use for vitality maintenance (traditional indication with biological plausibility)
- Cardiovascular support in heart failure (emerging; adjunct)

**Dosing:**
- **Standard tonic dose:** 9–15g dried root as decoction daily (traditional dose); or 500–1,000mg standardized 10:1 extract daily
- **Immune reconstitution (adjunct to cancer therapy):** 15–30g dried root equivalent daily (under oncology supervision)
- **TA-65 / cycloastragenol:** 5–25mg/day purified; not standard herb dosing
- **Duration:** Designed for extended continuous use (months to years for tonic applications)
- **Traditional preparation:** Decoction (simmer root 30–45 minutes); root slices can be cooked in soups

⚠️ **DRUG INTERACTIONS:**
- **Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus, corticosteroids):** APS may oppose immunosuppression → organ transplant patients: AVOID without consultation; theoretically reduces efficacy of immunosuppressant therapy
- **Cyclophosphamide:** Animal data suggest astragalus reduces immunosuppressive effect; clinical significance unclear; use with oncology team approval
- **Warfarin:** Potential additive or opposing effects (variable reports); monitor INR
- **Antihypertensives:** L-arginine → vasorelaxation → additive hypotensive effect; monitor blood pressure
- **Antidiabetic agents:** AMPK activation → glucose lowering → additive; monitor blood glucose

⚠️ **CAUTIONS:**
- **Active autoimmune disease (lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, MS):** Immunomodulatory effect may flare autoimmune activity — avoid or use only under specialist supervision
- **Acute phase infections (fever, acute inflammation):** Defer to resolution; not for acute treatment
- **Hormone-sensitive malignancies (ER+ breast cancer, prostate cancer):** Isoflavone content (calycosin) → theoretical ER agonism; clinical risk unestablished but caution warranted; discuss with oncologist
- **Pre-operative:** Discontinue 2 weeks before surgery (immune/coagulation interactions)

⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATIONS:**
- Organ transplant recipients (immune rejection risk from immunostimulation)
- Acute illness with fever — defer until resolved
- Children: insufficient pediatric data

**Standardization:**
- For APS activity: ≥40% polysaccharides
- For saponin content: ≥0.5% astragaloside IV (if saponin-focused product)
- Ensure species: *A. membranaceus* or *A. membranaceus* var. *mongholicus* — not generic *Astragalus* spp.

**Quality note:**
- 4-year-old roots (minimum) contain significantly higher APS and saponin than younger roots
- Growing region matters: traditional Mongolian/northern Chinese cultivation produces different chemotype than southern varieties
- Certification for species identity critical given genus-level toxicity confusion

**Energetic tier:** ⊕ Yuan (constitutional) — acts at the deepest adaptive and constitutional level; long-term immune architecture, cellular aging, foundational vitality maintenance
