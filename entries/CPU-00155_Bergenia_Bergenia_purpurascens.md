### Origin & Tradition

*Bergenia purpurascens* is a hardy, thick-rhizomed rock plant of the high Himalaya and the Tibetan plateau, growing in crevices and on cliff-faces up to ~4,500 m, its leathery leaves flushing deep purple-red in the cold — the "winter-purple rockfoil." Its thick creeping rhizome is the medicine, and across the Himalayan traditions it carries one of the most evocative of all plant-names: **pashanbhed** (Sanskrit *pāṣāṇabheda*, "stone-breaker"/"rock-render") — a name shared among several unrelated Himalayan crevice-dwelling plants believed to dissolve urinary stones, the plant's habit of splitting rocks read as a signature of its lithotriptic power.

**Tibetan / Himalayan Tradition (FMT):**
In Tibetan medicine bergenia rhizome (*ga-dur* and related names) is a cooling, astringent medicine used for fevers and "hot" disorders, for diarrhoea and dysentery (the astringent action), for coughs and pulmonary complaints, and as a wound and mucosal remedy. In Himalayan Ayurvedic and folk practice (as *pashanbhed*) its signature use is for **urinary and kidney stones** and urinary-tract complaints, alongside its use for diarrhoea, fever, and as a tonic.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the stone-breaker:**
Bergenia is the paradigm case of the **doctrine of signatures** operating in the high Himalaya: a plant whose roots visibly prise apart the living rock is taken as nature's sign that it will likewise break the "stone" within the body. The *pashanbhed* name encodes a whole medical cosmology — that the plant's *behaviour in the world* discloses its action in the body. The Codex records this honestly as symbolic reasoning, and notes where the chemistry does and does not support it (bergenin and the plant's phenolics have real antiurolithiatic, diuretic, and antioxidant activity in preclinical models, giving the ancient signature a partial modern vindication — though not a demonstrated clinical stone-dissolving effect). It is filed to RESET as a cooling astringent-resolvent of the lower tract.

### Active Components

- **Bergenin** — the signature C-glycoside of trihydroxybenzoic acid (first isolated from *Bergenia* in 1880, and now known from at least 112 plant species across 34 families). Bergenin is the plant's most-studied constituent, with well-documented **antioxidant and anti-inflammatory** activity and a broad reported preclinical spectrum (antinociceptive, antiulcerogenic, antiarrhythmic, hepatoprotective, neuroprotective, antidiabetic and more), with no cytotoxicity to healthy cells reported (Salimo et al., 2023).
- **Arbutin and gallic acid / gallotannins** — arbutin (a hydroquinone glycoside) contributes the traditional urinary-antiseptic reputation; the gallotannins provide the strong **astringent** action underlying the antidiarrhoeal, haemostatic, and wound-drying uses.
- **Catechins, β-sitosterol, and phenolic acids** — antioxidant and anti-inflammatory supporting constituents.

**Primary domain: RESET — cooling astringent for gut and urinary tract, and antioxidant support. A modest, traditional-tier herb.**

### Scientific Correlation

*Attribution: findings below are from a review retrieved from PubMed; DOI linked.*

The plant's most-studied constituent, **bergenin**, is reviewed comprehensively by Salimo et al. (2023, *Biomolecules*): a glycosidic trihydroxybenzoic-acid derivative distributed across ≥112 species, whose principal well-established activities are **antioxidant and anti-inflammatory**, with a wide additional preclinical spectrum (antinociceptive, antiulcerogenic, hepatoprotective, neuroprotective, antidiabetic/antiobesity, antiarrhythmic, antimicrobial, anticancer) and no reported cytotoxicity to healthy cells ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biom13030403)). The astringent (antidiarrhoeal, haemostatic) actions rest on the gallotannin fraction, and the urinary uses on arbutin and bergenin — giving the traditional *pashanbhed* stone-breaker reputation a plausible but not clinically-proven antiurolithiatic/diuretic basis.

*Evidence-grade note: bergenin's antioxidant/anti-inflammatory activity is well supported preclinically; the specific stone-dissolving claim of the "stone-breaker" tradition remains preclinical/traditional and is not established in controlled human trials. Presented as such.*

### Contraindications

- **Constipation / cold-dry patterns:** the strong astringency can aggravate constipation and dryness; not for cold-deficient bowel patterns.
- **Arbutin/hydroquinone caution:** arbutin-containing urinary herbs are generally used short-term; prolonged high-dose use of hydroquinone-yielding botanicals is cautioned.
- **Pregnancy:** insufficient safety data; avoid medicinal doses.
- **Actual obstructing urinary calculi:** the "stone-breaker" tradition notwithstanding, symptomatic or obstructing stones are a medical matter — bergenia is not a substitute for urological evaluation.
- **Evidence honesty:** the lithotriptic claim is traditional/preclinical, not clinically demonstrated.
