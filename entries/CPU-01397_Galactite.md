# Galactite (milk stone) — Galaktites lithos, Dioscorides 5-150
## *An ash-coloured, sweet-tasting stone so called "because it exudes milky stuff", rubbed on for discharges and ulcers of the eyes and beaten in water in a lead box because of its glueyness; the translator gives no identification ("UNKNOWN")* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-150, *Galaktites lithos* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10614; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 923, translator identification "UNKNOWN", class guess mineral; list row `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 876, "Galaktites Lithos", mineral, flagged as an uncertain identification; the book index sends the reader to page 820). The whole chapter was read. It reads (line 10617):

"This is called galactites because it exudes milky stuff. It is like ash in colour and sweet to the taste. It is good rubbed on for discharges and ulcers of the eyes. It is necessary that those who beat it in water put it in jars in a lead box because of its glueyness."

The chapter thus gives an etymology of the name (the stone "exudes milky stuff"), a colour (ash), a taste (sweet), a use (rubbed on for eye discharges and ulcers), and one handling instruction: the stone is beaten in water and then kept in jars placed in a lead box, "because of its glueyness". It names no place of origin, no season, no assay and no adulterant, and gives no burning method.

**The next chapter.** Chapter 5-151, *Melitites lithos* (line 10618), refers back to it: "Melitites is like galactites [above] in all respects, differing only in that it exudes a sweeter liquid. It is effective for the same things as galactites. It is also called galactites and lethaeus because men carrying it and going to their own masters find them well disposed and forgetful of wrongs (often committed)." The same chapter adds, in the voice of reported tradition: "When the milk of goats and sheep dry up it helps the little flock if it is beaten finely and mixed with brine. They say that pounded and taken as a drink it can do the same for women to produce milk. They say that bound around the neck of an infant it keeps him free from envy and diseases, and keeps the infant from treachery, and that both kings and judges revere anyone that wears this, and that they seem grandiose to all men." (line 10621). Dioscorides records these claims with "they say"; recording is not endorsing, and they are claims of 5-151, not of 5-150. The translator proposes an identification for 5-151 ("Melelite — Calcium Silicate, Aluminum Silicate"), but gives none for 5-150, and no identification is carried across from one to the other here. Melitites is a separate chapter and a separate spine question; it is not folded into this entry.

**A different stone with a similar name.** Chapter 5-152 says that the stone it treats, *morochthos*, is one "which some call galaxia or leucographis" (line 10625) and is used by linen-makers for whitening cloth. That is a different chapter, a different stone and a different use, and it is mentioned only so that the shared milk-name is not mistaken for identity.

**Identity.** The translator gives no identification ("UNKNOWN"), and the Codex list marks the identification uncertain. The text gives colour, taste, a "milky" exudate and glueyness but names no mineral. Which material Dioscorides meant, and how it relates to *melitites* (5-151), is not settled by the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated from the chapter. The stone is unidentified, so no composition is asserted. The chapter's own clues (ash-grey, sweet, a milky exudate, sticky when wet) describe properties but do not identify a mineral, and no composition is inferred from them.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records an unidentified ash-grey, sweet stone as an external rub for eye discharges and eye ulcers. The material cannot be identified, the chapter says it must be kept in a lead box, and the eye is a site where contamination or toxic metals cause serious harm. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

---

### Preparation & Dosing

Two steps are given in 5-150: the stone is "rubbed on" (for eye discharges and ulcers) and is "beat in water", the result being put "in jars in a lead box". The text does not say what the stone is rubbed on to or in what vehicle, nor how much water is used, nor how the jars are closed; and it does not say whether the stone is applied to the eye directly. The lead box is a storage container, and the chapter does not say that any lead enters the preparation. No quantity, frequency or duration is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What the herbal offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention (provenance, season of gathering, sensory assay, adulteration detection), and this chapter shows a little of it: a colour, a taste and a handling note based on the stone's behaviour in water. It gives no place, season, test for quality or adulterant. The chapter is therapeutic and carries no cultivation status. The milk-name connects, in 5-151, to a reported amulet use; that belongs to another chapter, is reported with "they say", and is not a cultivation claim.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation beyond the habit of noting a material's colour, taste and how it behaves when wetted. The stone itself is unidentified, and the chapter's use is on the eye. It is not a recommended practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Unidentified material:** the identification is uncertain; an unknown mineral should not be put in or near the eye.
- **Lead:** the text calls for storage in a lead box; lead contamination is a poisoning risk.
- **The eye:** discharge and ulcers of the eye can threaten sight and need medical assessment.
- **Breastfeeding and infants:** the text of the neighbouring chapter 5-151 describes drinks for women to bring milk and amulets for infants; nothing from that chapter is recommended here, and an unidentified mineral should not be given to a nursing mother or tied to an infant.
