## Section 1 — Nomenclature

### Primary Scientific Name
*Uncaria tomentosa* (Willd. ex Schult.) DC. — Cat's Claw (Rubiaceae)

### Botanical Identity
Woody liana/vine to 20–30m; distinctive J-shaped recurved thorns resembling cat claws (at every stem node — the morphological naming source); white to cream flowers in spherical heads; native to Amazon rainforest basin (Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, Brazil, Colombia); grows at 200–800m altitude on forest margins and secondary growth. Two pharmacologically distinct chemotypes: **pentacyclic oxindole alkaloid (POA)** type and **tetracyclic oxindole alkaloid (TOA)** type — the POA type is considered pharmacologically superior for immune applications; the TOA type may antagonise POA effects. Commercial products should specify POA-dominant.

## Section 2 — Cross-Traditional Presence

### 2.1 South American Indigenous Traditions (SA)

**Asháninka (primary users)**: The Asháninka of the Peruvian Amazon have used *samento* (inner bark decoction) for at least 2,000 years (archaeological plant macrofossils) for:
- Arthritis and joint inflammation ("swollen joints")
- Tumours and cancers ("hard swellings that grow inside")
- Stomach ulcers and digestive conditions
- Wound healing
- Contraception (high-dose root preparations)
- Deep infections and "internal diseases"

The Asháninka tradition distinguishes between inner bark (immunostimulatory, anti-inflammatory — preferred) and root bark (contraceptive, abortifacient — restricted use).

**Aguaruna/Awajún**: *Paraguayo* bark decoction for arthritis, gastric ulcers, and "internal cleansing."

**Shipibo**: For cancer-type presentations and immune weakness.

### 2.2 Badianus Manuscript / Mesoamerican (BM — loose classification)

While cat's claw is specifically Amazonian and not present in the Badianus Manuscript proper (which covers Mexican highland plants), it belongs within the broader Mesoamerican/South American indigenous pharmacopoeia tradition that the Badianus Manuscript represents. The *uña de gato* name indicates pre-Colombian use documented in colonial-era Spanish botanical surveys.

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

### 5.1 Immunostimulatory / Immunomodulatory (POA)

**Pentacyclic oxindole alkaloids (isomitraphylline, pteropodine)**: Stimulate macrophage phagocytosis → enhance innate immune response. Modulate cytokine production (increase TNF-α and IL-1 at physiological doses → anti-tumour; reduce NF-κB at anti-inflammatory doses → anti-arthritic). This bidirectional immunomodulation — stimulating when suppressed, moderating when overactive — is the hallmark of the adaptogenic immune herbs.

### 5.2 NF-κB Inhibition / Anti-Inflammatory

**Quinovic acid glycosides + OPCs + ursolic acid**: Multiple converging NF-κB inhibition pathways → reduced TNF-α, IL-1β, COX-2. Active in both acute and chronic inflammatory contexts.

**Articular cartilage**: Cat's claw preparations demonstrate MMP (matrix metalloproteinase) inhibition in synovial tissue → reduces cartilage degradation in OA and RA → one of few plants with direct cartilage-protective mechanism.

### 5.3 DNA Repair / Anti-Mutagenic

**OPCs + alkaloids**: Stimulate DNA repair enzymes (particularly in response to UV and oxidative damage). Sheng et al. (2000) demonstrated enhanced DNA repair capacity in lymphocytes exposed to cat's claw extract — mechanistically relevant to both cancer prevention and immune function.

### 5.4 Antiviral

**Quinovic acid glycosides**: Inhibit RSV, rhinovirus, VSV replication in vitro. Plausibly relevant to traditional use for "internal infections."

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Rheumatoid arthritis**: Mur et al. (2002, *Journal of Rheumatology*): Cat's claw extract (60mg/day × 24 weeks) vs placebo — significant reduction in RA joint pain (tender joint count −29% vs −8% placebo). One of the more rigorous botanical RA RCTs.

**Osteoarthritis (knee)**: Piscoya et al. (2001, *Phytomedicine*): Freeze-dried cat's claw vs placebo — significantly reduced pain and disability index in OA knee; maintained at 4 weeks post-treatment.

**Gastric ulcer**: Case series and pilot studies: cat's claw bark decoction reduces *Helicobacter pylori* density (in vitro antimicrobial confirmed) and improves ulcer healing. No RCT data but mechanistically credible.

**HIV (adjunctive)**: Early studies (Keplinger 1989): TOA-free POA extract improved immune cell counts in HIV patients (small series). Not established as antiviral; may support immune function as adjunct.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**2,000 years of Asháninka medicine**: Archaeological evidence for *samento* use in Peruvian Amazon sites dates to approximately 0 CE. The Asháninka have used cat's claw continuously through to the present — making it one of the longest unbroken medicinal traditions documented for a single plant and people. When Austrian scientist Klaus Keplinger brought cat's claw to European attention in the 1970s–80s, he was not discovering a new plant but amplifying a tradition with 2,000 years of empirical validation.

**The TOA controversy**: The commercial category of "TOA-free" cat's claw (pioneered by Keplinger and marketed as Samento brand) reflects a real pharmacological discovery — TOAs appear to inhibit the CNS effects of POAs in vitro. However, the traditional Asháninka preparation uses whole inner bark (containing both alkaloid types) with consistent outcomes. Whether TOA-free preparations are meaningfully superior to traditional preparations in clinical practice remains unresolved. This is a case where isolationist reductionism may not improve on the traditional whole-plant preparation.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### 8.1 Preparation

| Form | Dose | Application |
|------|------|-------------|
| POA-standardised bark extract | 250–350mg 2–3× daily | RA; OA; immune support; anti-inflammatory |
| TOA-free extract (Samento) | 1–3 drops to 1ml/day (titrate) | Same; preferred by some practitioners |
| Traditional bark decoction | 5–10g inner bark per 1L, simmer 20 min | Anti-inflammatory; gastric; traditional |
| Root bark (caution — higher alkaloid) | Traditional use only | Contraceptive (ethnomedical — not for clinical use) |

**Titration**: Start low (60–120mg/day) and increase — some individuals sensitive to alkaloid load. Traditional Asháninka practice uses gradual introduction over several weeks.

### 8.2 Energetic Positioning

**TCM (cross-referencing)**: Bitter, astringent; neutral to slightly warm. Would be classified as: enters Liver (肝), Kidney (腎), Large Intestine (大腸). Dispels Bi syndrome (painful obstruction), activates Blood.

**Ayurvedic (cross-referencing)**: Bitter, astringent; neutral. *Śothahara* (anti-inflammatory), *Krimighna*, *Rasāyana* qualities.

### 8.3 Contraindications

- **Pregnancy**: Uterotonic/contraceptive at high doses — absolute contraindication
- **Immunosuppressives / post-transplant**: Immunostimulatory — contraindicated with cyclosporine, tacrolimus
- **Anticoagulants**: Anti-platelet activity — additive with warfarin, aspirin
- **Antihypertensives**: TOA-containing preparations → additive hypotensive
- **Skin graft / organ transplant**: Immune activation contraindicated
- **Autoimmune conditions**: Bidirectional modulation may unpredictably alter disease; caution in SLE, MS
