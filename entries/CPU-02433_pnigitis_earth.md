# Pnigitis earth — Pnigitis (PNIGITIS), Dioscorides 5-177

## *An earth coloured much like Eretrian earth, thickly crusted, cool to the hand and so adhesive to the tongue that it hangs on it, with the properties of fuller's earth except in strength; the translator gives no identification ("UNKNOWN") and glosses only the comparison. The identification is unresolved and of low confidence* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, marked "uncertain". A search of `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` for "pnigitis" and "Pnigitis" found this chapter and the index entries, and nothing in another tradition on disk.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-177, *Pnigitis* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10750; name-index row in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` at row 950, translator identification "UNKNOWN", class guess mineral; list row in `_sources/dioscorides.csv` at line 902, "Pnigitis", mineral). The whole chapter was read. It reads (line 10753):

"Pnigitis earth in a way resembles Eretrian earth [5-171] in colour. It is thickly crusted, cooling if touched by the hands, and adheres to the tongue very much so that it hangs on it. It has the same properties as fuller's earth [ammonium silicate] except for its strength. Some sell this as Eretrian earth."

The chapter thus gives: a colour (somewhat like Eretrian earth); a feel and a texture (thickly crusted, cooling to the touch); a tongue test (it adheres to the tongue so strongly that it "hangs on it"); a comparison in properties (the same as fuller's earth, but weaker); and an adulteration note (some sell it as Eretrian earth). It gives no place, no use of its own and no preparation.

**The comparison earths.** Eretrian earth (5-171, line 10718) is "considerably white" or ashy; "the best is that which resembles ash in colour, and is very soft, and when drawn along brass yields a violet line"; it is washed like cerussa or by pounding, settling, drying in the sun and forming into tablets (lines 10721-10725). A search of the text for "fuller" found no chapter on fuller's earth, so the reference is to a material not described in this book; the translator's bracket "[ammonium silicate]" is the translator's gloss and is not Dioscorides' own. The properties that "the same as fuller's earth" refers to are not stated in this chapter. [USER: verify]

**The adhesion test.** Samian earth (5-172) "on touching the tongue holds it fast like glue" (line 10726), so the tongue test is used for more than one earth in this book. The pnigitis chapter, like the Samian chapter, thus gives a sensory test for the earth and a warning that it is sold under a better name.

**Identity.** The translator marks it unknown and offers only the comparison to fuller's earth. The chapter's marks (crusty, cool, adhesive to the tongue) are those of a fine clay but do not name a deposit or a mineral. The name is not explained by the text. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated from the chapter. The material is described as a clay-like earth with the properties of fuller's earth. In general terms, fuller's earth is an absorbent clay (a hydrous aluminium silicate); the translator's bracket "[ammonium silicate]" is not a standard description of it. This is a general fact and is not asserted of the pnigitis earth.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The chapter gives no medical use of its own. It says only that pnigitis earth has the properties of fuller's earth but weaker, and that it is sometimes sold as Eretrian earth. The value of the chapter to the Codex is as a record of an earth, a sensory test and an adulteration. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given in 5-177. Eretrian earth, which it resembles and is sold for, is washed and tableted (5-171), but that is that earth's method and is not carried to pnigitis. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM).** The chapter prints one quality: "cooling if touched by the hands" (line 10753). It also says that it adheres strongly to the tongue and is thickly crusted. No warming, drying or moistening quality is printed, and no degree.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What the herbal offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention (provenance, season of gathering, sensory assay, adulteration detection), and this short chapter shows two of the four: a sensory assay (colour, crust, coolness to the hand, adhesion to the tongue) and an adulteration warning (it is sold as Eretrian earth). It carries no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation beyond the model of assay by touch and tongue and the warning that a lesser earth is sold under a better name. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
