# Bahu-vara — Bahu-vara (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation, Appendix No. 3)

## *Cordia myxa L. (Cordia dichotoma is also used under the name), a tree of the family Boraginaceae with a sticky, edible fruit; the identification is ours, taken from Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3, which prints "G)rdi Myxa" against the name by position in the OCR'd columns, and is of medium confidence, since the appendix is the only place the name was found and the body text of the translation gives no Latin name and describes no plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* index among the texts on disk, in one place.** This is a single-text entry and an index-only attestation. The row began on 8 November 2026 as an unverified residual, "G)rdi Myxa [SS]", read from the translation's Appendix; this entry replaces it. A search of the three Bhishagratna volumes and the chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` for the spellings Bahu-vara, Bahuvara and Bahu vara found it only in the appendix. A search of the other texts on disk was not extended beyond that.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS), the index.** In Bhishagratna's Vol. III, Appendix No. 3, the Sanskrit name is printed "Bahu-vara" in the B column (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 31372). The Latin names stand in a separate block below it (id line 31437), where the second entry reads ".  G)rdi Myxa." The OCR is damaged (the first letters read "G)rdi", almost certainly "Gordi" or "Cordi"; the standard form is *Cordia myxa*). The pairing is by position: the first name on the page, Bahu-mulak, carries "See S'atavati" (line 31436), Bahu-vara the Cordia entry, and the third, Bahu-vija, "Citrus Medica" (line 31438), after which Baka carries "See Agasti" and Bakuchi "Psoralea Corylifolia". The Latin name is Bhishagratna's and not Sushruta's.

**What the text on disk does not give.** The sources retrieved contain no chapter passage naming Bahu-vara: no taste, no part used, no preparation, no disease.
