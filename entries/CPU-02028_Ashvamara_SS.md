# Ashvamara — Ashvamara, Ashvamaraka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation, 1907)

## *Ashvamara, a plant drug of the Lakshadi group of Sutrasthana ch. 38, also printed Ashvamaraka and used in alkali-making. Identification: Nerium odorum (the oleander, now Nerium oleander) by a positional pairing of "As'va-maraka" in the botanical appendix of Bhishagratna's Vol. III; confidence medium-low, because the group list prints "Ashvamara" and the appendix "As'va-maraka" and no passage states that they are one drug; the first-pass note "identity unverified" is improved, not contradicted* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** If the identification holds, this is the oleander, every part of which is a recognised poison. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take this plant or burn it and breathe the smoke.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk. This is a single-text entry.** Spellings in quotations are normalised silently from the OCR where the damage is plain (for example Nandi for the printed Xandi); the line numbers cited give the text as printed. "Ashvamara" and "Ashvamaraka" were searched in all three volumes of Bhishagratna and the chapter files.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Sutrasthana ch. 38, the Lakshadi group (`Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15793; chapter file `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`): "The drugs known as the Laksha, Arevata, Kutaja, Ashvamara, Katphalam, Haridra, Daru-Haridra, Nimba, Saptachchhada, Malati, and Trayamana form the Lakshadi Gana." The metrical text reads: "This consists of astringent, bitter and sweet taste (Rasa) and acts as a good vermifuge and a purifying (aseptic) agent in cases of bad, malignant or indolent ulcers. Diseases due to the deranged Kapham and Pittam prove amenable to its curative properties, which extend to cases of cutaneous affections (Kushtham) as well." The OCR prints "Ximva" for "Nimba".

In Sutra ch. 11, the chapter on the preparation of alkalis (Kshara), the form "Ashvamaraka" is in the list of woods burnt to ash: "the wood as well as the leaves, roots and fruits of Kutaja, Palasha, Ashvakarna, Paribhadra, Vibhitaka, Aragvadha, Tilvaka, Arka, Snuhi, Apamarga, Patala, Naktamala, Vrisha, Kadali, Chitraka, Putika, Indra-Vriksha, Asphota, Ashvamaraka, Saptachchhada, Agnimantha, Gunja, and the four species of Koshataki, should be burnt down to ashes" (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch11.txt` lines 123 to 130; Vol. I line 6571). In Chikitsa ch. 6, the form is "Asvamaraka" among the ingredients of the oil used as an unguent that "leads to the falling off of the polypus" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch06.txt` line 261; Vol. II line 14697).

**Identification.** The group-list row gives none. In Vol. III Appendix 3, "As'va-maraka" (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 31297) is paired by position with "Nerium Odorum" (line 31344), with the neighbours holding (As'va-karna with a cross-reference in the Latin column, Asvattha with Ficus religiosa). The Lakshadi group contains Saptachchhada, which also appears next to Ashvamaraka in Sutra ch. 11 and in Chikitsa ch. 6; this supports reading the group name and the alkali-list name as one drug, but is not proof. ### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent for this drug. In general terms, the oleander (Nerium oleander) contains cardiac glycosides, such as oleandrin, and all parts of the plant are poisonous. Whether this applies to the drug as Sushruta used it was not checked. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Toxic if the identification holds.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Lakshadi group: bad and indolent ulcers, worms, and Kapha and Pitta diseases including skin disease (Sutra ch. 38). As an alkali source (Sutra ch. 11) and in a polypus unguent (Chikitsa ch. 6).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used for the group drug.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations given are burning the wood, leaves, roots and fruits to ash for alkali (Sutra ch. 11) and an oil cooked with it as an unguent (Chikitsa ch. 6). Chapter 38 closes with a rule for all the groups: "Only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group, or a similar number of drugs chosen from the different groups, or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety, or in combination with another, should be used according to the indications of any particular case, as the physician, in his discretion, would determine" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, near the end; Vol. I, ch. XXXVIII). It also says that the groups "will be more elaborately dealt with later on in the chapter on Therapeutics". This is a rule of method for the groups, not a dose for this drug. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The oleander is a widely grown shrub, and its poisoning by ingestion, including of the leaves, is well recorded. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** The Lakshadi group is said to "consist of astringent, bitter and sweet taste (Rasa)" (Sutra ch. 38). No rasa, virya or vipaka is printed for Ashvamara alone.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is attested in the Sushruta Samhita, in the therapeutic groups of Sutrasthana ch. 38 and the other passages cited above, and its role there is therapeutic: groups are said to subdue Kapha, Vata or Pitta, to act as vermifuge or aseptic agents, and so on. Chikitsa ch. 26 (the vajikarana chapter, "increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons") and chs. 27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) were searched under the spellings printed here (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` to `ch30.txt`, and ch. 31) and the name was not found in them. The drug therefore carries no rasayana or vajikarana status in the sources retrieved, and nothing is said of ojas for it. The register is (b): framed by the therapeutic gana, with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A group drug for malignant ulcers and skin disease, and an alkali source, which is probably a poisonous plant. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use. A cultivator should not use it.

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC, probably.** Do not ingest it, and do not burn it and inhale the smoke.
- **Ulcers, polyp, skin disease.** Medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Identity.** Positional OCR pairing and two spellings; do not substitute any plant.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved; given the probable toxicity, avoid. [USER: verify]
