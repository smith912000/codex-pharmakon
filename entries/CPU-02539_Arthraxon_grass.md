# Arthraxon grass — Jin Cao 荩草 (Shennong bencao jing); Wu Pu aliases Wang Chu 王刍, Huang Cao 黄草

## *Arthraxon hispidus (Thunb.) Makino, a small grass used in China as a yellow dye; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence, supported by the Mingyi bielu note that it "can dye yellow" and by Guo Pu's gloss as a grass of the sedge-like kind* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The only text read for this entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk for *Arthraxon* found nothing.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 荩草 in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 荩草 *jin cao*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3555). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，平 ("bitter, even"); 主久咳上气、喘逆 ("governs long cough with rising qi, wheezing counterflow"), 久寒 ("long cold"), 惊悸 ("fright palpitation"), 痂疥 ("scab and itch"), 白秃 ("white baldness", a scalp disease), 疡气 ("sore qi") and 杀皮肤小虫 ("kills small worms of the skin"). The paragraph prints no alias and no habitat. There is no 久服 clause.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 3557) says: 王刍，一名黄草, "wang chu, also called huang cao ('yellow grass')"; and "Shennong and Leigong say: it grows in the mountain valleys of Mount Tai; it treats heat of the body and evil qi, and children's body-heat qi" (神农、雷公曰：生太山山谷。治身热邪气，小儿身热气), quoted from the Yulan. The Wu Pu layer is attributing words to Shennong; those words are not in the paragraph above and are recorded here as that layer's report, not as the Shennong text.

The Mingyi bielu layer (line 3559) adds: 可以染黄，作金公，生青衣。九月、十月采, "can be used to dye yellow; [it is made into] 'jin gong' [the sense of this phrase is not clear to the Codex]; grows at Qingyi; gathered in the ninth and tenth months".

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 3561) cites the Shuowen (荩, a grass; and a second character, rendered "KT" in the file because it is missing, "wang chu"), the Erya (the same, "wang chu"), Guo Pu ("[that grass] is a mat or bedding [蓐]; today called owl-foot sedge, 鸱脚莎"), the Mao Shi (绿竹猗猗) and its commentary ("wang chu"), and the Tang bencao note, which says jin cao is commonly called "[missing] ru cao" (mat grass), the "wang chu" of the Erya. These are the commentators' and not the Shennong text's. Together they identify a small grass used for matting and as a yellow dye, and they give no description of the plant's form.

An appendix of drug pairings at the end of the volume has the line 荩草 畏鼠妇, "jin cao fears shu fu (pill bugs)" (line 4465). It is the compiler's later appendix and gives no further statement. Shu fu is the drug of the separate entry III_348 (Pill bug), another monograph of this batch.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, a plant used to dye yellow is likely to contain yellow flavonoid pigments; the grass is reported to contain flavonoids such as luteolin. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, even grass for long cough with wheezing, long cold, fright palpitation, scab and itch, scalp disease and small skin worms. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Mingyi layer gives the harvest months (ninth and tenth) and the dyeing use (line 3559). The Shennong paragraph names no part and no preparation. For lower-grade drugs generally the classic's preface gives the rule that, with a toxic drug, one begins with a dose like a grain of millet; if the disease leaves, one stops; if not, doubles it; if not, increases tenfold, "taking the departure of the disease as the measure" (若用毒药疗病，先起如黍粟，病去，即止。不去，倍之；不去，十之。取去为度) (line 4125). That rule is general to toxic drugs, the paragraph does not say this grass is toxic, and the Codex does not use the rule as a dose for it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The grass has been used as a yellow dye. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The classic's categories ("rising qi", "sore qi", "fright palpitation") are not modern diagnoses, so no modern finding can be set beside them.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味苦，平, "bitter, even" (line 3555). No other quality is printed in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph for 荩草 reads 味苦，平 and files the grass in the lower grade (下品). The classic's preface (line 2935) says the lower medicines "are for treating disease, to correspond to earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to clear cold-and-heat evil qi, break accumulations and heal illness, base it on the lower classic" (主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经). The paragraph has no 久服 clause and is an indication list for cough, cold, fright and skin disease. The register is therefore (b): a lower-grade drug whose grade is the tradition's own warning against long use. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 荩草 *jin cao*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3555)

**Practitioner's note.** A lower-grade drug in the Chinese scheme, to be taken, if at all, for a defined complaint and then stopped. Nothing here supports long use, and the tradition's own grading is the warning.

### Contraindications

- **Lower-grade drug:** the classic's preface calls the lower medicines mostly toxic and not for long use (line 2935); the paragraph does not say this grass is toxic, and no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Cough, wheeze and skin disease:** the uses are historical record only and need medical assessment.
- **Pregnancy and nursing:** no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Identity:** grasses of similar form are not interchangeable.
