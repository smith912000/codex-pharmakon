# Stalactite (white frog's-back stone) — dkar.po.sbal.rgyab (Clark 1995); Karpo balgyab (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011), rGyud-bzhi Explanatory Tantra ch.20

## *"The white frog's back stone", which Clark's heading glosses "Stalactite: Hematite" (two different minerals) and the commentary compares to white and brown pyrolusite; a hard, heavy, pointed stone whose male type bears a stupa design and female type a lotus design; its powers are those of the brown frog's-back stone; the identification is unresolved (low to medium confidence)* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The Codex's names row (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`) gives the locus "Clark 1995 (line 818)", translator identification "Stalactite" (the class guess in that extract is "plant", which is wrong for a stone); no separate row in `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` was cited for this entry. Two texts were read.

*Clark 1995* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). Chapter 20 lists the stone medicines in part (2) (line 816). The white frog's-back stone is the second of a pair; the brown one stands directly before it (line 817):

"smug.po.sbal.rgyab (lit. 'the brown frog's back stone'): Hematite: is of two types, The 'male frog' [type is brown, hard, heavy and similar to a frog's back. The superior type bears a stupa design and has large bumps, whilst the common type has small bumps only. The superior quality of] the 'female frog' [type bears a lotus design and has no bumps, whilst the common type is just without bumps. Both types] expel, extract and dry lymph, maintain bone resin, heal fractures and sustain the brain."

and then (line 818, continuing at line 820):

"dkar.po.sbal.rgyab (lit. 'the white frog's back stone'): Stalactite: Hematite: Is similar to white chhig.thub (white pyrolusite) and brown chhig.thub (brown pyrolusite). [It is hard, heavy and pointed. The male type bears a stupa design, whilst the female type has a lotus design. These correspond in quality to the two types of brown stone above. With regard to white and brown pyrolusite, the superior type is ice-like, soft, pointed and breaks into needle-like pieces. Some people used to call the brown type 'stone camphor' because of its strong odor. It is dark brown and shaped like slate]. The white type has the same powers as the brown 'frog's back' stone."

By the bracket convention of Clark's introduction (line 82), the tantra's own words about the white stone are the unbracketed ones: that it is similar to white and brown pyrolusite (*chhig.thub*), and that it has the same powers as the brown frog's-back stone. The description (hard, heavy, pointed, male with stupa, female with lotus) is commentary. The convention was not checked against the Tibetan. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`). In the stone-medicine summary of the Explanatory Tantra (page 198, line 3352): "Mukpo balgyab is of two types: male and female. They dry up chhuser, protects spongy bone, join fractured bones and stop cerebro-spinal fluid discharge. Karpo balgyab, Karpo chhikthub and Mukpo chhikthub and etc. have potencies similar to the Mukpo balgyab." The index lists "Karpo balgyab 198" and "Mukpo balgyab 198" (lines 7405, 7445). In the Men-Tsee-Khang wording *mukpo* is the brown stone and *karpo* the white one, matching Clark's *smug.po* and *dkar.po*. *Chhuser* is left untranslated in that edition; Clark renders the same action as "lymph".

**What is not given.** In the passages read, neither text gives a taste, potency or post-digestive taste for the white stone by itself, a dose, a preparation or a formula containing it.

**Identity.** Clark's heading gives two English glosses for the white stone, "Stalactite" and "Hematite", and the same "Hematite" for the brown stone. Stalactite (a cave deposit of calcium carbonate), hematite (iron oxide) and pyrolusite (manganese dioxide), the stone the commentary compares it with, are three different minerals. The commentary's "hard, heavy and pointed" and "ice-like, soft, pointed" for the pyrolusite do not fit one mineral. The Codex name "stalactite (white frog's-back stone)" is Clark's first gloss only. The entry is therefore filed as "white frog's-back stone, unresolved". [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None is stated in the texts. As a general fact, the glosses name calcium carbonate (stalactite), iron oxide (hematite) and manganese dioxide (pyrolusite). Which, if any, is the stone the tantra means is not settled; no composition is asserted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The texts give the white stone the powers of the brown: drying and extracting lymph (*chhuser*), maintaining bone resin, healing fractures and sustaining the brain (Clark, line 817), or protecting spongy bone, joining fractures and stopping cerebro-spinal fluid discharge (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3352). Fractures and fluid leaking from the head are emergencies. A stone of uncertain identity should not be swallowed, and the Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no preparation for the white frog's-back stone. No form, route, measure or duration is given. The male/female distinction (stupa or lotus design) is a classification of the stone's appearance, not a preparation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] As general facts: calcium carbonate is an antacid and a calcium source; iron oxide is largely inert; manganese dioxide dust is hazardous to inhale and chronic manganese exposure is neurotoxic. No mineral is known to heal bone fractures when swallowed.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Tibetan (FMT).** For the white frog's-back stone by itself no taste, potency or post-digestive taste is printed in either text read. Its powers are stated to be those of the brown stone (Clark line 820) and, in Men-Tsee-Khang, its "potencies" to be "similar to" the brown (line 3352). No wind, bile or phlegm (*rlung*, *mkhris pa*, *bad kan*) action is printed in the passages read. No classification is carried across from the Chinese or Indian systems.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The tantra places the stone among the stone medicines (Clark line 816) and gives it only the brown stone's therapeutic powers. The commentary's stupa and lotus designs of the male and female types are descriptions of the stone's markings and are not a cultivation status. The one substance of the section called "unsurpassed as an essence-extraction substance" is *brag.zhun* (Clark line 870); nothing like this is said of this stone, and no *bcud len* use was found. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation. It is a stone of uncertain identity that the tantra lists for fractures and lymph; no preparation is given. It is not a recommended practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Unsettled identity:** the glosses stalactite, hematite and pyrolusite name different minerals; if manganese-bearing, dust is hazardous to inhale.
- **Fractures, cerebro-spinal fluid leaks:** medical emergencies; see a doctor.
- **Preparation unknown:** Tibetan mineral medicines are traditionally processed by specialists; this entry states no processing. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Legal and environmental status** (cave formations are often protected): not retrieved. [USER: verify]
