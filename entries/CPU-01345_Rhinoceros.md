# Rhinoceros — bse.ru (Clark: "Rhino"; rhino horn and rhino hide), and the rhinoceros as a game animal, rGyud-bzhi
## *The rhinoceros and its horn and hide; Clark prints "bse.ru: Rhino" with no Latin name, the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary prints "Black Rhinoceros" and "Rhinoceros" with the binomials Rhinoceros bicornis L. and Rhinoceros imus Burchell* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The rhinoceros heads the *Horn* section of the Explanatory Tantra's materia medica (ch.20) in Clark 1995 (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 1152; `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` row 222): "bse.ru: Rhino: [has four names and is of three colors, white, variegated and black in descending order of quality]. It dries [accumulations of] pus, blood and lymph in the stomach [and is beneficial in cases of poisoning]." The bracketed words are Clark's commentary on the root verse. Rhino horn is then the standard against which two deer horns are measured: "The horns [of these two types of deer have] similar [action] to Rhino horn" (line 1154). Under *Skin*: "Rhino hide and Ox hide: cure smallpox" (line 1185).

The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation has the same entries: "Rhino horn dries up pus, blood and chhuser of the torso. The therapeutic effect of roe deer horn and common deer horn are similar to that of rhino horn" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` lines 3506-3508), and "The hide of the rhinoceros and the ox cure small pox" (lines 3554-3555). Its index lists "rhinoceros 161, 216" and "black rhinoceros 161" (lines 7274 and 7478).

As food the rhinoceros is a larger game animal. Clark's meat classification puts "rhinoceros" in group (d), the larger hill and game animals, with spotted deer, wild boar, buffalo and wild yak (line 672). The Men-Tsee-Khang list reads "The goral, spotted deer, Himalayan tahr, boar, buffalo, black rhinoceros, wild ass, wild yak and wild Zho are the group of larger herbivorous wild animals" (lines 2907-2911). The glossary of animals has the Tibetan names "Semuk Seru" against the English "Black Rhinoceros Rhinoceros" and the binomials "Rhinoceros bicornis L. Rhinoceros imus Burchell" (lines 7118-7130); the table is OCR-scrambled, which of the two Tibetan names goes with which English name is by column order only, and `names.csv` rows 422-423 record that the Tibetan name was not paired. Whether "Seru" is the Clark "bse.ru" is a sound match and no line states it. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** Clark gives no Latin name for "bse.ru". The two binomials in the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary are printed as given here and were not checked against current taxonomy or against the range of the Tibetan trade. Row 222 of `names.csv` classes the entry "plant"; it is an animal horn, and the class is a source-data error. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Rhinoceros horn is a mass of compacted keratin fibres, the protein of hair and nails, and is not bone; it has no known pharmacologically active constituent. Hide is mostly collagen with keratin in the epidermis. No analysis specific to this entry was read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources record rhino horn as a drying remedy for pus, blood and lymph and for poisoning, and rhino hide for smallpox. Every rhinoceros species is threatened, and trade in rhino horn is prohibited under international law; the demand for it has driven poaching. The Codex does not recommend rhino horn, hide or meat for any purpose and gives no protocol. The Tibetan text itself says the horns of two deer act similarly (Clark line 1154); that too is a record and not a recommendation.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation of the horn or hide is described in the lines read; the entries give only the part and its action, and Clark's note on colour grades the horn white, variegated and black "in descending order of quality" (line 1152). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Rhino horn is keratin; no clinical evidence supports medicinal effects from it, and it is chemically similar to hair and fingernail. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** For the horn the lines read give an action (drying pus, blood and lymph in the stomach; poisoning) and a grade by colour (white, variegated, black, in descending order of quality); no taste or potency is printed (Clark line 1152). For the hide, an action only (smallpox; Clark line 1185). As meat the rhinoceros is a dry-land game animal: all meats "are sweet tasting and have a sweet post-digestive taste", and dry-land flesh "has cool, light and coarse powers which eliminate wind and phlegm fevers" (Clark line 673; Men-Tsee-Khang lines 2923-2925). No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Rhino horn and hide are substances of the Explanatory Tantra's materia medica, with stated actions and no taste group. The tradition's frame is given in Clark's introduction, which roots the three humors in attachment, hatred and obscuration (line 51); the animal materials here treat branch conditions (pus, poisoning, smallpox). No line read connects the rhinoceros with *bcud len* (essence extraction) or with any practice, and a search of Clark for "bcud" found nothing. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition, and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. Rhino horn is a historical remedy, and in the present day using it harms an endangered animal. The honest use of the entry is to read the record and leave the material alone.

### Contraindications

- **Conservation and law:** all rhinoceros species are threatened, and trade in horn and other parts is banned or tightly restricted internationally and in most countries. Possession can be a criminal offence. [USER: verify]
- **No benefit:** horn is keratin, and nothing in the sources retrieved supports a modern use.
- **Quality and fakes:** horn offered for sale may not be rhino horn at all; the text's colour grades (Clark line 1152) are no test.
- **Identity:** the Tibetan, Latin and English names are paired through OCR-scrambled tables; do not rely on them. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** the products require killing an animal; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
