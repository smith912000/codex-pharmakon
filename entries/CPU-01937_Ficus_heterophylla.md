# Ficus heterophylla — Trayanti and Trayamana (Tra'yanti, Tra'yama'na), Sushruta Samhita Appendix 3

## *Ficus heterophylla L.f., a creeping or climbing fig of South Asia, as the translator's Latin gloss to the two Sanskrit names Trayanti and Trayamana, which are printed together under one brace; the Latin is Bhishagratna's and is printed on the line beside the names, and the identification of the Sanskrit names is of medium confidence (the glossary is the only identification read)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named in the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) only. This is a single-text entry.** In Appendix No. 3, "Names of drugs with Latin synonyms" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`), the Sanskrit names stand at line 33145 as "Tra'yanti ^" and the next line as "Tra'yama'na' j", a brace joining them to one gloss, and the Latin at line 33148 as "Ficus Heterophylla." In this stretch the OCR prints each name with its gloss close by (as in "Tinisa ... Dalbergia Oujeinensis" and "Tintida ... Tamarindus Indica" just above), so the pairing needs no column counting. The extraction file `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` lists the Latin line as unpaired ("OCR column split").

**The name in the main text.** The name Trayamana occurs in two group lists of the Sutrasthana, and the lines were opened and read. In the Lakshadi Gana: "The drugs known as the Laksha, Arevata, Kutaja, Ashvamara, Katphalam, Haridra, Daru-Haridra, Nimva, Saptachchhada, Malati, and Trayamana form the Lakshadi Gana" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 451 to 454). The metrical text which follows reads: "This consists of astringent, bitter and sweet taste (Rasa) and acts as a good vermifuge and a purifying (aseptic) agent in cases of bad, malignant or indolent ulcers. Diseases due to the deranged Kapham and Pittam prove amenable to its curative properties, which extend to cases of cutaneous affections (Kushtham) as well" (lines 456 to 462). In the classification of drugs by taste, Trayamana stands in the bitter group: "... Sankha-pushpi, Apamarga, Trayamana, Ashoka, Rohini, Vaijayanti, Suvarchala, Punarnava, Vrishikali and Jyotishmati, etc. collectively constitute the bitter group" (`SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` lines 333 to 347).

The related form "Trayantika" stands in a list of drugs for medicated wines, gruels and similar preparations in the Chikitsasthana: "... Padma (Bhargi), Trayantika, Lohitika, Amlashtha, bark of pomegranate ..." (`SS_Chikitsa_ch11.txt` lines 189 to 191; the condition treated was not read). [USER: verify] Whether the Trayantika of that list is the Trayanti of the appendix is not stated. Whether the group-list drug Trayamana is correctly glossed as a fig is also open: the appendix is the only identification read. [USER: verify]

**No other text.** A search of the other source texts on disk for *Ficus heterophylla* found nothing.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Lakshadi group: against worms, bad ulcers, Kapha and Pitta disorders and skin disease, as quoted above; and as a bitter-group drug.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation of the single drug, and no statement that the fig of the glossary is the plant meant.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

None for the single drug. The Lakshadi Gana is a group, and the Trayantika of Chikitsa 11 is an ingredient in a compound whose quantities were not read. **No dose is given in the sources retrieved.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Ficus heterophylla* is a small creeping fig of the Indian region, and the milky latex of figs can irritate skin. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Sutra ch.38 and ch.42).** As a member of the Lakshadi Gana: astringent, bitter and sweet in taste, vermifuge and purifying in ulcers (ch.38, lines 456 to 462); as a bitter-group drug (ch.42, lines 333 to 347). The ascription depends on Trayamana being the name glossed Ficus heterophylla. [USER: verify]
- **SS (Appendix 3).** The glossary gives no qualities.
- No other tradition is tagged, and none is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name Trayamana is a member of the Lakshadi group and of the bitter group of the Sutrasthana. No chapter of the Chikitsa on rasayana or vajikarana (chapters 26 to 31 of the Chikitsasthana on disk) contains this name in a search of `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/`, so no cultivation status is attested for it. The register is therefore (b): framed by the dravya-guna groups, with no *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status attested for it.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to cultivate here. A group-list drug for ulcers, worms and skin disease, with no cultivation status in the sources read. A cultivator has no sourced use for it.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The glossary's fig may not be the Trayamana of the group lists. [USER: verify]
- **Latex.** Fig latex can irritate skin (general note, not from the source).
- **Ulcers, worms, skin disease.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
