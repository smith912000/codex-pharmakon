# Salt froth (sea froth) — Alos Achne (ALOS ACHNE; *spuma salis*), Dioscorides 5-127

## *"Fragments of the foaming sea found on the little rocks" (*spuma salis*, "froth of salt"), which Dioscorides says have the same properties as salt; the translator's gloss is "Sea Froth". Our reading is a crust of sea salt left on shore rocks by dried spray, of medium confidence, because the chapter does not describe the material beyond this one phrase* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder. A search of `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` for "sea froth", "spuma salis" and "Alos Achne" found this chapter and the index entries, and nothing in another tradition on disk.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-127, *Alos Achne* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10493; name-index row in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` at row 900, translator gloss "Sea Froth", class guess mineral; list row in `_sources/dioscorides.csv` at line 853, "Alos Achne", mineral; the book index lists "Sea Froth 809" and "Spuma salis 809"). The whole chapter was read. It is one sentence (line 10495):

"Spuma salis are fragments of the foaming sea found on the little rocks, and it has the same properties as salt."

The chapter thus gives: a Latin name (*spuma salis*, "froth of salt"), a description ("fragments of the foaming sea found on the little rocks") and a comparison ("the same properties as salt"). It gives no use of its own, no method and no dose.

**What it sends the reader to.** The properties of salt are in the two chapters before, 5-125 (*Kisseris*, pumice, line 10483, which is not salt) and 5-126 (*Ales*, "Salts — Sodium Chloride, Ammonium Salts", line 10486). The salt chapter reads: "Mineral salt is the most useful — usually that which is without stones, white, transparent, thick and equally compacted ... These salts all have similar properties — useful as an astringent, and to clean and dissolve, as well as repress, reduce the intensity of symptoms, and form scabs — only differing slightly" (lines 10487-10488). It goes on to give a long list of external uses: against rotten ulcers, psoriasis, growths in the eye and pterygium, dropsy, itching, lichen and leprosy, throat and tonsil complaints, scorpion and viper stings, bruises on the face and more, and as an antidote with vinegar to poppy juice and mushrooms (lines 10489-10492). All of these reach the froth only through "the same properties". The chapter does not say the froth is used for the same complaints; the reader is told only that its properties are those of salt.

**Identity.** The translator's gloss is "Sea Froth". The Latin *spuma salis* is "froth of salt". The phrase "fragments of the foaming sea found on the little rocks" describes a material left on rocks by the sea; our reading is the salty crust that sea spray deposits on shore rocks when it dries, but the chapter does not say that, and a mineral identification is not offered by the translator. ### Active Components

None can be stated from the chapter beyond the comparison to salt. In general terms, the dried crust left by sea spray is mainly sodium chloride with the other salts of seawater (magnesium, calcium and potassium salts, sulphates); this is a general fact and is not drawn from Dioscorides.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** Dioscorides records sea froth as having the same properties as salt, which in his salt chapter are astringent, cleansing and dissolving, with many external uses. The chapter gives none for the froth itself. The Codex supplies no modern protocol. Salt crust from shore rocks can be contaminated by whatever is in the sea or on the shore.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given in 5-127. The salt chapter (5-126) gives forms for salt (for example "warm packs ... in little bags", roasted with honey, applied burnt with polenta, taken in vinegar as an antidote; lines 10488-10492), but these are salt's methods and are not carried to the froth by the text. ### Scientific Correlation

### Contraindications

- **Salt on ulcers or the eye:** the salt chapter's uses are not safe to copy; salt irritates ulcers and the eye.
- **Salt load:** large amounts of salt are dangerous, especially for children and people with heart, kidney or blood-pressure disease.
- **Poisoning:** salt is not an antidote to mushroom or poppy poisoning; seek emergency care.
- **Shore contamination:** shore crusts can carry pollution and microbes.
