### Origin & Tradition

Mastic (*mastichē*) — the resinous exudate of *Pistacia lentiscus* — is among the most precisely documented spice-medicines in the ancient world. Produced almost exclusively on the Greek island of Chios (*mastiha*), the resin has been a significant trade commodity since at least 3000 BCE. The English word "masticate" derives from *mastichē* — the resin was chewed as the first documented chewing gum. Its pharmacological identity centers on the unique triterpenoid resin complex: isomerized masticadienonic acid, moronic acid, and other pentacyclic triterpenoids that provide documented *H. pylori* eradication, anti-ulcer, antifungal, and anti-inflammatory activity.

**EP — Egyptian (Mastaka / Pistacia resin documented in Ebers Papyrus; traded from Aegean):** Mastic resin appears in Egyptian medical papyri in wound healing, aromatic dental preparations, and as a drug vehicle. The Ebers Papyrus documents pistacia resin (mastic or terebinth resin — *Pistacia terebinthus*) in compound preparations for dental and wound conditions. Egyptian embalming preparations included mastic resin among the aromatic antiseptics.

**AT — Assyrian (Simbirru / related pistachio-family resins documented in Nineveh tablets):** Pistacia resin preparations appear in Assyrian medical texts in wound healing and gastrointestinal compound formulas. The aromatic resin trade from the Aegean and Levant supplied Mesopotamian medicine with mastic-equivalent preparations.

**DMM — De Materia Medica (Μαστίχη — Mastichē):** Dioscorides provides a detailed mastic account in Book I. He notes: warming and drying; excellent for stomach conditions (digestive, anti-ulcer); breath-freshening when chewed (antimicrobial oral care); wound healing; and the distinctive use in compound medicinal preparations as a fragrant base. He specifically notes mastic for "weakness of the stomach" — correctly anticipating the anti-*H. pylori* mechanism 2000 years before the bacterium's discovery.

**CM — Canon of Medicine (مصطكى — Maṣṭakā):** Ibn Sina's mastic account is extensive. He classifies it as warm in the second degree and dry in the first. Primary indications: gastric tonic ("strengthens and warms the stomach" — the *H. pylori* eradication mechanism); dental (gum strengthening, tooth pain — the antimicrobial dental application); wound healing; anti-hemorrhagic; liver tonic; and the explicit recommendation for compound *Theriac* antidote preparations (mastic as a vehicle that also contributes antimicrobial and hepatoprotective activity).

**SS — Ayurveda (मस्तकी — Mastakī):** Mastic appears in Ayurvedic texts as an imported aromatic resin with properties paralleling Indian resin medicines (guggulu, boswellia). Classification: Katu-Tikta rasa, Ushna virya, Katu vipaka, Vata-Kapha shamaka. Actions: Deepana; antimicrobial; wound-healing; Raktapitta-hara. Used in compound preparations for gastrointestinal conditions and as an aromatic dental medicine.

### Active Components

**Triterpenoid resin acids:**
- **Masticadienonic acid** — the dominant acid; the primary anti-*H. pylori* compound (direct bactericidal activity against the ulcer-causing bacterium); anti-inflammatory (NF-κB inhibition); anti-oxidant
- **Isomasticadienolic acid** — isomer; similar activity
- **Moronic acid** — pentacyclic triterpenoid; anti-inflammatory; anti-cancer; anti-viral (HIV-1 integrase inhibition in vitro)
- **Oleanolic acid, ursolic acid** — triterpenoids; anti-inflammatory; hepatoprotective; anti-cancer

**Essential oil (minor component):**
- **α-Pinene, myrcene, β-Myrcene** — antimicrobial; the fragrance of fresh mastic
- **Verbenone** — monoterpene ketone; antimicrobial; digestive

**Polymers:**
- **Poly-β-myrcene** — the polymer responsible for the chewing gum quality; inert structurally but contributes to oral mucosa contact time

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**1. *H. pylori* eradication and gastric ulcer prevention ([RESET])**
This is mastic's most compelling modern pharmacological finding. Multiple clinical trials confirm that mastic gum (1g/day for 2 weeks) significantly reduces *H. pylori* load and heals peptic ulcers — comparable to some antibiotic regimens but without antibiotic resistance induction. Masticadienonic acid directly kills *H. pylori* at low concentrations.

For practitioners with training-stress-induced peptic symptoms or confirmed *H. pylori* infection, mastic gum represents the historically validated, pharmacologically confirmed, antibiotic-sparing alternative. The ancient physician's intuition ("strengthens the stomach") encoded a precise antibacterial mechanism.

**2. Oral health (antibacterial / antifungal)**
Chewing mastic gum reduces oral bacterial load, reduces plaque, and inhibits *Candida albicans* in the oral cavity. The masticadienonic acid contact-antimicrobial mechanism makes traditional mastic chewing the most pharmacologically rational oral hygiene practice in the Codex. Relevant for practitioners with training-stress-induced oral candidiasis or gum disease.

**3. Anti-inflammatory (liver-protective)**
Mastic's oleanolic/ursolic acid triterpenoid complex provides hepatoprotective and anti-inflammatory activity. Relevant in [RESET] phase where liver detoxification and inflammation clearance are priorities.

### Preparation & Dosing

**Mastic gum (chewing — oral health and GI):**
3–5 pieces (1g total) natural Chios mastic gum; chew for 20–30 minutes after meals; spit out (not swallowed — the resin polymer is indigestible). The traditional preparation for both oral antimicrobial and GI contact.

**Mastic supplement (H. pylori / systemic):**
1000mg mastic gum powder in capsule 2x/day for 2 weeks; the dose used in clinical *H. pylori* eradication studies. Available as Chios mastic gum standardized capsules.

**Mastic tea (decoction — GI/anti-inflammatory):**
1–2g ground mastic resin dissolved in warm water; difficult to prepare pure (resin does not dissolve well in water); better dissolved in warm wine or alcohol for the compound preparation used in traditional Unani medicine.

### Scientific Correlation

- ***H. pylori* eradication:** Huwez FU et al. (1998): mastic gum significantly reduced *H. pylori* load and healed peptic ulcers in clinical study; Al-Habbal MJ et al. (1984): mastic gum significantly superior to placebo for peptic ulcer healing; Bae EA et al. (2009): masticadienonic acid as primary bactericidal compound against *H. pylori*
- **Oral antimicrobial:** Papageorgiou VP et al. (2008): mastic essential oil inhibited oral pathogens; Takahashi K et al. (2003): mastic reduced *Candida* oral colonization in denture wearers
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Triantafyllou A et al. (2011): mastic extract significantly reduced CRP and inflammatory cytokines in a clinical trial; NF-κB inhibition confirmed
- **Hepatoprotective:** Paraschos S et al. (2007): mastic resin extract significantly protected against acetaminophen-induced hepatotoxicity; oleanolic acid mechanism

### Contraindications

- **Mastic allergy** — contact allergy and occupational sensitization documented in workers handling raw mastic; IgE-mediated reaction; test sensitivity
- **GI obstruction** — the resin polymer can accumulate in gut; not a concern at normal chewing/supplement doses but theoretical with excessive consumption
- **Pregnancy** — limited data; avoid medicinal-dose preparations during pregnancy as a precaution
