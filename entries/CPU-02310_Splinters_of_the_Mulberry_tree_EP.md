# Splinters-of-the-Mulberry-tree — as printed in Bryan's English of the Papyrus Ebers (1930)

## *Slivers or splinters of wood of a tree Bryan prints as "Mulberry-tree"; used as one ingredient of a poultice in the nervous-system chapter. The Egyptian name is not given in the text on disk, and whether the tree is a true mulberry or the sycomore fig (listed separately by Bryan as "Sycamore-tree") is not settled by the sources read. Identification: Bryan's English only. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** EP

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Papyrus Ebers among the texts on disk, in Bryan's English (1930).** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it. The text on disk is `Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`, an OCR of Bryan's introduction; the identification of every substance is the translator's.

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The name occurs twice in the text on disk.

1. **The list of plant remedies.** Bryan's list of plant remedies, "Plant Remedies" (text line 1306; `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 72, "Bryan 1930, Ch. VI Plant Remedies (line 1306)"), gives it as "Splinters-of-the-Mulberry-tree", in the run "Mint-of-the-Mountains, Splinters-of-the-Mulberry-tree". The names.csv row says "Bryan's English identification". Bryan's list also has "Sycamore-tree" with "Splinters-of-the-Sycamore" (line 1337), and similar splinters of the cedar, willow and Zizyphus lotus (lines 49, 89 and 91 of names.csv). So the Mulberry-tree and the Sycamore-tree are two items in his list.
2. **A poultice in the nervous-system chapter.** Chapter XVIII, "The Nervous System" (text lines 3140 to 3197). Bryan explains that the word *met* "undoubtedly refers to the nerves of the body; but there, it would appear just as undoubtedly to apply to the blood-vessels", and he describes remedies to refresh, strengthen and make supple the *met*. For the last, he writes that "still more complicated and imposing was another poultice which boasted thirty-seven ingredients ... which numbered Hog's dung, Watermelon, and Goose-oil among those ingredients" (line 3170 and the page break after it); his introduction says that one prescription "on Plate 83 consists of thirty-seven ingredients" (line 1106). The list headed "REMEDY TO MAKE THE MET SUPPLE" (line 3172) contains Hog's dung, Watermelon and Goose Oil, so it is very probably that recipe. In it (line 3182) stand "Splinters-of-the-Cedar-tree, Splinters-of-the-Mulberry-tree, Splinters-of-the-Willow-tree, Splinters-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus, Splinters-of-the-Sycamore, Splinters-of-the-uan-tree, Resin-of-Acanthus ...", and the directions end "Make into one and poultice therewith" (line 3197). The splinters are one of six kinds of wood splinters in a very long recipe.

The text on disk does not give an Egyptian name or a hieroglyphic reading, and it does not say which of the thirty-seven ingredients does what.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

The Codex gives no protocol. The poultice also contains hog's dung and other ingredients that are unsafe on skin that may be broken.
