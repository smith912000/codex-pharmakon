# Human sweat — عرق (*ʿaraq*, as printed; the text speaks of wrestlers' sweat)

## *Human sweat: the skin's watery excretion, here that collected from wrestlers; the Canon's entry covers sweat generally and the sweat of wrestlers in use* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Named as a drug only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. The locus is *Canon* Book II, entry *ʿaraq* (عرق), Arabic text line 16777 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; the entry is lines 16778-16784. It names no other authority.

**What sweat is (CM).** "Sweat is the wateriness of the blood mixed with a bilious sanies (*ṣadīd*); one must use of it what has not yet dried, that in which there is still moisture" (العرق مائية الدم خالطها صديد مراري يجب أن يستعمل منه ما لم يجف بعد بل ما فيه رطوبة; lines 16778-16779). "It is riper (*anḍaj*) than urine, for it is from the surplus of softness and moisture after the final digestion, while urine is from the surplus of the second digestion" (lines 16779-16781).

**Action (CM).** "It is riper than urine and differs according to the animal, and in it is a dissolving that is not slight" (line 16781). Swellings: "the sweat of wrestlers with henna oil benefits swelling of the groin, indeed dissolves it" (عرق المصارعين مع دهن الحناء ينفع ورم الأربية بل يحللها; lines 16782-16783). Chest: "the dried sweat of wrestlers with henna oil is placed on swellings of the breast and dissolves them, and with rose oil for the clotting of milk in the breast" (lines 16783-16784).

**Identification.** There is nothing to identify beyond the substance: human sweat, and especially wrestlers' sweat. The text says it "differs according to the animal", so the entry is not limited to the human.

### Active Components

Sweat is mostly water with sodium chloride and small amounts of urea, lactate and other solutes. This is general physiology; the Canon states none of it.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The entry reports a topical dissolving application. It is not a recommended practice. Using another person's body fluid as a plaster is unhygienic, and no protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation is the Canon's: wrestlers' sweat, fresh or dried, with henna oil or rose oil, applied to a swelling (lines 16782-16784). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Described by digestion rather than degree: riper than urine, with a dissolving power (*Canon* Book II, entry *ʿaraq*, lines 16779-16781). No degree of hot or dry is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* Sweat is a topical dissolving agent in the text. The entry assigns no cultivation status and nothing about the vital faculty.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The text treats a body excretion as a poultice for swellings; it is not a practice for cultivation and not one to repeat.

### Contraindications

Hygiene: applying another person's sweat to the skin or to a swelling risks infection and is not advised. The text's use is historical record. Sweat from an unwell person can carry skin organisms.
