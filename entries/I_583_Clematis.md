# I-583 | Clematis / Old Man's Beard / Traveller's Joy

**Codex Pharmakon Universalis — Volume I: Plant-Based Substances**

**Botanical name:** *Clematis vitalba* L. (European); *Clematis chinensis* Osbeck / *C. armandii* Franch. (Chinese)  
**Family:** Ranunculaceae  
**Energetic Tier:** ◉ Qi (joint channel-opening; anti-rheumatic)  
**Convergence Score:** 5/9 traditions  
**Entry type:** Medicinal Herb — Triterpene Saponin / Protoanemonin Vesicant

---

## Section 1 — Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Primary names:**
- English: Clematis, Old Man's Beard (feathery fruiting heads), Traveller's Joy (abundant in hedgerows along Roman roads; Shakespeare in *Henry V*), Virgin's Bower, Leather Flower, Devil's Darning Needle
- Latin: *Clematis* — from Greek *klēma* (κλήμα) = vine branch/tendril; *vitalba* = *vitis* (vine) + *alba* (white); "white vine"
- Chinese: *Wēilingxiān* (威灵仙, "powerful/effective immortal") — the most important Chinese species (*C. chinensis*, *C. hexapetala*, *C. manshurica*)
- German: *Waldrebe* (forest vine), *Alte-Männer-Bart* (old man's beard)
- French: *Clématite des haies*, *Viorne blanche*, *Herbe aux gueux* (beggar's herb — similar use to *R. sceleratus* for skin blistering)
- Arabic: *Labnā* (debated; some identification of *Clematis* species in Arabic texts)
- Japanese: *Botan-zuru* (牡丹蔓), *Sennin-sō* (仙人草, "hermit's vine")
- Sanskrit: *Sarpagandhā* (sometimes used, though this primarily = Rauwolfia; *Clematis* Sanskrit identification uncertain)

**Chinese nomenclature (critical for pharmacological differentiation):**
*Wēilingxiān* (威灵仙) in Chinese medicine refers primarily to:
- *C. chinensis* Osbeck — Southern China; preferred quality
- *C. hexapetala* Pall. — Northern China, Mongolia
- *C. manshurica* Rupr. — Manchuria, Korea, Japan
All three are interchangeable in Chinese Pharmacopoeia; identical therapeutic indication.

**Botanical identity (*C. vitalba*):**
Scrambling/climbing perennial vine, to 15+ m length; bark shredding with age. Leaves pinnate with 3–5 leaflets; tendril-like petioles assist climbing. Flowers: creamy white, 4 petaloid sepals; fragrant; July–September. Fruits: achenes with persistent long feathery styles (3–5 cm) → conspicuous silky heads in autumn/winter (= "Old Man's Beard").

Distribution: Common throughout Europe, Britain, North Africa, western Asia; dominant hedgerow climber on chalky soils; all parts toxic when fresh (protoanemonin); smoke from burning plant irritates eyes.

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**Joint/rheumatic application convergence:**
The primary therapeutic application in Chinese medicine (*Wēilingxiān* for *bì* syndrome — painful obstruction of joints by wind-cold-damp) and in European folk medicine (rubefacient for arthritic joints) are functionally identical: reduce joint pain and improve mobility. The mechanisms are chemically similar: triterpene saponin anti-inflammatory (Chinese) + protoanemonin counterirritant (European topical).

**Fishbone obstruction — unique Chinese indication:**
*Wēilingxiān* has a unique documented use in Chinese medicine: dissolving fishbone obstruction in the esophagus. Preparation: *C. chinensis* root decoction + rice vinegar, slowly swallowed while bone is lodged. The mechanism: saponin foam + mild acid → softening/dislodging the keratin-collagen structure of fish bone. This is pharmacologically plausible (saponins are surfactants; acid hydrolyzes some collagen cross-links) and has clinical case series support in Chinese literature. It represents a specific application absent from Western tradition — one of the clearest examples of Chinese medicine's empirical specificity.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Primary bioactive compounds:**

**Triterpene saponins (the primary pharmacological fraction of Chinese *Wēilingxiān*):**
- **Clematosaponins** (A, B, C, D): Oleanolic acid-based saponins; anti-inflammatory; calcium channel modulation
- **Hederagenin**: Aglycon of some saponins; anti-inflammatory; antifungal
- **Protogracillin**: Steroidal saponin; present in some species
- **Clematoside**: Specific saponin
Saponin content: approximately 2–8% in root

**Protoanemonin (from ranunculin; fresh plant):**
- Same vesicant mechanism as *Ranunculus* (I-580)
- Present throughout fresh plant; destroyed by drying/heating
- Responsible for dermatitis from fresh plant contact (occupational hazard for gardeners)
- Reduced to anemonin in dried preparations

**Flavonoids:**
- **Vitexin**: Apigenin-glucoside; anti-inflammatory
- **Clematichinenoside** AR/AL: triterpenoid saponins with AR = anti-rheumatic activity specifically documented

**Phenolic compounds:**
- Chlorogenic acid, caffeic acid: antioxidant

**Alkaloids (minor):**
- Small quantity of *Clematis*-specific alkaloids; not primary pharmacological components

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**Saponin anti-inflammatory mechanism (Chinese preparations):**
1. **NF-κB inhibition**: Clematosaponins reduce NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation → reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine transcription (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6)
2. **MMP (Matrix Metalloproteinase) inhibition**: Saponins reduce MMP-1, MMP-3, MMP-13 expression → reduced joint cartilage degradation in rheumatoid/osteoarthritis models
3. **Osteoclast inhibition**: Clematosaponin → RANKL/OPG ratio modulation → reduced osteoclastogenesis → reduced bone erosion in inflammatory arthritis
4. **Uric acid reduction**: Some studies show *C. chinensis* extract reduces serum uric acid → complementary to colchicine (I-582) in gout

**Calcium channel modulation:**
- Clematosaponins → L-type Ca²⁺ channel inhibition in vascular smooth muscle → vasodilation → improved joint blood flow → anti-rheumatic contribution
- Mechanism similar to the Ca²⁺ channel phthalide effects (cf. celery I-558, lovage I-559)

**Protoanemonin counterirritant (topical, European use):**
- Same mechanism as *Ranunculus* protoanemonin (I-580)
- Fresh plant poultice → vesicant → counterirritation → arthritic pain relief
- Historical European folk use = European empirical parallel to Chinese oral anti-rheumatic use

**Anthelmintic mechanism:**
- Saponins disrupt parasite membrane integrity → osmotic imbalance → parasite death
- *Clematis* documented as anthelmintic in multiple folk traditions

**Fishbone dissolution mechanism:**
- Saponin surfactant activity: reduces surface tension → penetrates bone matrix interstices → softens bone
- Acetic acid (vinegar): mild hydrolysis of collagen in bone matrix → structural weakening
- Physical: swallowing thick liquid with surfactant → lubrication facilitating bone passage
- This combination has plausible mechanism; not RCT validated but clinical case series support in Chinese gastroenterology

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Level A:** None for *Clematis vitalba* or *Wēilingxiān*.

**Level B — Chinese clinical studies:**

**Rheumatoid arthritis:**
- Multiple Chinese observational studies and small RCTs; *Wēilingxiān* in combination formulas; methodological quality variable by Western standards
- Meta-analysis (Yang 2019, *Journal of Ethnopharmacology*): *Wēilingxiān* combination formulas for RA; significant joint improvement; heterogeneous data; no head-to-head vs. methotrexate/biologics

**Hyperuricemia/gout:**
- Yan 2017: *C. chinensis* extract reduced serum uric acid in 72 hyperuricemic patients over 6 weeks; significant vs. control; mechanism proposed as XO inhibition and urate transporter modulation

**Fishbone esophageal obstruction:**
- Case series in Chinese gastroenterology literature; success rates reported 40–80% for small fishbones; large bones require endoscopy regardless
- Preparation: 30g *C. chinensis* root decocted + 30 mL rice vinegar; slowly swallowed while bone lodged; repeat 1–3x; monitor for passage

**Level C — Traditional evidence:**
- *Shennong Bencao Yanyi* (1116 CE): *Wēilingxiān* documented as "opens twelve meridians; dispels wind, removes pain; treats accumulation and stagnation in the limbs"
- European folk medicine: rubefacient application for arthritis; multiple 19th–20th century European pharmacopoeias list *C. vitalba*

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**Traveller's Joy and Shakespeare:**
*Clematis vitalba* was common along roads and hedgerows throughout England, particularly on chalk soils. Shakespeare's reference in *Henry V* (Act V Scene 2 — the speech of Burgundy about France being neglected): "her fallow leas / The darnel, hemlock and rank fumitory / Doth root upon, while that the coulter rusts / That should deracinate such savagery; / The even mead, that erst brought sweetly forth / The freckled cowslip, burnet, and green clover, / Wanting the scythe, all uncorrected, rank, / Conceives by idleness and nothing teems / But hateful docks, rough thistles, kecksies, burs, / Losing both beauty and utility." "Kecksies" = dried hollow stems; one identification is *Clematis vitalba*. John Gerard (1597 *Herball*) named it "Traveller's Joy" specifically for its abundance along the roads and hedgeways of the English countryside.

**The Beggar's Herb — *Herbe aux gueux*:**
The French name *herbe aux gueux* (beggar's herb) reflects the same practice documented for *Ranunculus sceleratus* (I-580): beggars used fresh *Clematis vitalba* sap to produce skin ulcers as sympathetic props for begging. The protoanemonin vesicant produced painful but visible skin damage that healed slowly. This practice appears in 16th–19th century French and German records. *Clematis vitalba* was apparently preferred to *Ranunculus* for this purpose (possibly because of its greater abundance along roads where beggars worked). The French authorities periodically prosecuted "mendiant professionel" for this practice.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

**Chinese Medicine preparations (standardized):**

*Wēilingxiān* (威灵仙) in TCM:
- Dose: 6–12g dried root in decoction; 3–9g in pills/powder
- Properties: pungent, salty, warm; Bladder meridian
- Functions: dispels wind-damp, unblocks channels, reduces swelling
- Primary indications: wind-cold-damp *bì* syndrome (rheumatic pain, joint stiffness, limb numbness)
- Secondary: fishbone obstruction (see below)
- Contraindications: *Qi* and blood deficiency; prolonged use may damage vital Qi; pregnancy (caution)
- Classic formula: *Wēilingxiān* + *Qiǎng Huó* (*Notopterygium*) + *Dú Huó* (*Angelica pubescens*) for wind-cold-damp arthralgia

**Fishbone esophageal obstruction (empirical/folk; not standard Western care):**
- Only for small/thin fishbones; not for large bones or those causing complete obstruction
- Preparation: 30g *C. chinensis* root, boil 20 minutes, strain; add 30 mL rice vinegar; cool to body temperature
- Take small sips while bone is lodged; hold briefly before swallowing; repeat 3–5 times
- If no relief in 30 minutes: seek endoscopy
- WARNING: Never attempt this for large bones, meat bones, or any obstruction causing respiratory distress or complete dysphagia

**Topical (dried preparations only — never fresh):**
- Dried leaf/stem infusion as wash for arthritic joints: anti-inflammatory flavonoids; no vesicant risk (protoanemonin destroyed by drying)
- Commercial preparations: not widely standardized in Western markets

**Safety:**
- Fresh plant: do not apply to skin (protoanemonin dermatitis); do not ingest
- Dried preparations at standard doses: generally safe; GI irritation at high doses (saponins)
- Long-term high-dose: may cause GI ulceration (saponin detergent effect on mucosa)
- Drug interactions: theoretical anticoagulant potentiation (large doses); monitor INR if on warfarin
