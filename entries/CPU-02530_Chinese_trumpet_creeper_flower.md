# Chinese trumpet creeper flower — 紫葳 *Ziwei* (Shennong bencao jing), with Wu Pu's aliases 武威, 瞿麦, 陵居腹, 鬼目, 茏华
## *Campsis grandiflora (Thunb.) Loisel. (syn. C. chinensis (L.) Voss), the Chinese trumpet creeper, whose flowers are the modern drug 凌霄花; our identification, of high confidence for the modern drug, but note that Sun Xingyan, in his own note, does NOT accept that identification for the classic's 紫葳* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The identification is ours and is the modern one. The text contradicts it at one point, and the Codex states that plainly below.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the middle (中) classic, reads 味酸（《御览》作咸），微寒: "sour (the Yulan has salty), slightly cold". It governs 妇人产乳余疾 ("the lingering illnesses of women after childbirth and nursing"), 崩中 ("flooding", heavy uterine bleeding), 症瘕血闭 ("concretions, masses and blocked blood"), 寒热羸瘦 ("cold and heat with emaciation") and 养胎 ("nourishes the fetus"). The habitat is 生川谷, "stream valleys". There is no 久服 clause, and the paragraph names no part of the plant. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 紫葳 *ziwei*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2473)

**Wu Pu (3rd century).** 《吴普》曰 gives the aliases 武威, 瞿麦, 陵居腹, 鬼目 and 茏华, and sets out a chorus of authorities on the flavour: "Shennong and Leigong: sour; Qibo: acrid; Bian Que: bitter, salty; Huangdi: sweet, non-toxic". It describes the plant as 如麦根黑 ("like the root of wheat, black"), gives the gathering months as the first and the eighth, and the origin "or it grows at Zhending" (Yulan). The alias 瞿麦 here is the name of the pink 瞿麦 (*Dianthus*) in later usage.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 adds the aliases 陵苕 and 茏华 and the habitat 生西海及山阳, "West Sea and the sunny side of mountains".

**Sun's note (案).** Sun cites the Guangya (茈葳 = 陵苕 = 蘧麦, where 蘧麦 is the name of the pink), the Erya (苕, 陵苕), Guo Pu (an alias 陵时), a quotation from the "Bencao" noting yellow-flowered and white-flowered forms, and Guo Pu again, who says the colours and names differ, the Shijing line 苕之华 ("the flowers of the *tiao*") and the Fanzi jiran (紫葳 from the Sanfu region). He then gives the key judgement: **Li Dangzhi says it is "the root of 瞿麦"**, which agrees with the Guangya; but the Tang Materia Medica (《唐本》) note, citing a gloss to the Erya, has the four characters 一名凌霄 ("also called *lingxiao*"), which identifies it with the trumpet creeper (凌霄花); and Lu Ji takes it for 鼠尾. Sun writes 疑皆非，故不采之: "I suspect that all of these are wrong, and so I do not adopt them."

**Identity.** The modern drug 紫葳 / 凌霄花 is the flower of *Campsis*. That identification comes from the Tang commentary, which Sun explicitly declines to adopt; the evidence in his own note (Li Dangzhi, the Guangya, Wu Pu's alias 瞿麦 and the "black root like wheat") points instead to a pink (瞿麦). So the text does not support the trumpet creeper for the classic's plant. The Codex files the entry under the modern name because that is how the spine names it, and writes only what the paragraph says. ### Active Components

The text prints no constituent. Because the identity of the classic's plant is open, no statement about its constituents is made. In general terms the modern drug 凌霄花 (the *Campsis* flower) is a flower of the Bignoniaceae. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records a sour, slightly cold drug for women's illnesses after childbirth, heavy bleeding, masses and blocked blood, and "nourishing the fetus". The Codex gives no protocol: heavy uterine bleeding and pregnancy need medical care. The "nourishes the fetus" claim in particular conflicts with the modern practice of caution about the flower in pregnancy (see Contraindications), and the Codex does not recommend any use.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives no part, preparation or quantity. Wu Pu gives gathering in the first and the eighth months. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

In general knowledge the trumpet creeper flower is an article of Chinese pharmacy that is traditionally avoided in pregnancy. The classic's claim of "nourishing the fetus" is its own. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong text proper).** 味酸 (the Yulan has 咸), 微寒: sour (or salty); slightly cold. No toxicity statement in the Shennong paragraph. Wu Pu reports different flavours from different authorities and, for Huangdi, "non-toxic".
- No organ or channel affinity is printed. No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 紫葳 in the middle grade (中品); its preface says middle-grade drugs nourish the nature, and some are toxic. The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no promise to lighten the body or extend life; it lists women's complaints and "nourishes the fetus". The Mingyi bielu and Wu Pu, not the Shennong text, give the aliases, habitat and months. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 紫葳 *ziwei*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2473)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A middle-grade drug for a defined set of women's complaints, with an identity that Sun himself leaves open. Do not use a plant on the strength of this entry.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy.** The classic says "nourishes the fetus"; the modern flower is, in general knowledge, avoided in pregnancy. The two statements conflict, and the Codex advises no use in pregnancy. [USER: verify]
- **Bleeding.** Heavy uterine bleeding needs medical assessment.
- **Identification.** The classic's plant may not be the trumpet creeper.
