# European Ephedra / Sea Grape

## *Ephedra distachya* L. (syn. *E. vulgaris* Rich.) | Family: Ephedraceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Acrid, slightly bitter; warm to hot; dispersing and ascending. A wind-opening, surface-releasing agent whose action is centrifugal — it drives from the core toward the skin and the airway.

**Training Phase:** Restricted. Not a training substance in Kenetica. Held in the codex as a **cautionary reference** for the sympathomimetic class and as a taxonomic and historical anchor. No practitioner protocol is issued.

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Ephedra distachya* is the western member of a genus that has shaped medicine on three continents. It is a low, jointed, rush-like shrub — leafless to the eye, its photosynthesis carried by green stems — bearing scarlet fleshy cones that earn it the folk name **sea grape**. It runs from the Atlantic coasts of France and Iberia across the Mediterranean littoral, into the Pontic steppe, the Caucasus, and Central Asia. Its eastern cousin *E. sinica* (Ma Huang, 麻黄) is already established in this codex; the two are near relatives and easily conflated, but they are **not interchangeable**, and the difference is chemical, not merely nomenclatural.

**Attested Traditions**

**Ancient Mediterranean / Graeco-Roman (AT).** Classical materia medica records a jointed, leafless shrub used as an astringent and for cough, chest complaints, and the staunching of flux. The identification of that classical drug with modern *Ephedra distachya* is a reconstruction by later botanists rather than a claim the ancient text itself makes, and this codex marks it as such. [USER: verify the specific classical passage and its attribution before publishing any named-author citation.] What can be said with confidence is that the European plant entered the herbal tradition as a **drying, binding, chest-clearing** drug — a doctrine of *tightening what leaks and opening what is stopped*.

**Zoroastrian / Avestan — the Haoma question (AT).** The Avestan ritual plant *haoma* (Vedic cognate *soma*) is pressed, strained, and drunk in the Yasna liturgy, and is praised as **strength-giving, wakefulness-giving, and death-averting**. Which botanical it originally denoted is one of the genuinely open questions in Iranian philology. Ephedra is a leading candidate: living Zoroastrian practice in Iran and among the Parsis uses *hōm* twigs identified as Ephedra; the plant is jointed and stemmy in a way that fits ritual descriptions of pressing stalks; and Ephedra remains have been reported from Central Asian ritual contexts. Competing candidates — including *Peganum harmala* and various fungi — have their own advocates. This codex records the Ephedra-as-haoma identification as **a live scholarly debate, not a settled fact**, and asserts nothing beyond that. [USER: verify preferred citations from the Iranological literature before this section is published outward.]

The doctrinal content is worth isolating from the botanical dispute. *Haoma* is not praised as a visionary agent in the Yasna; it is praised as an agent of **wakefulness, vigour, and the holding-off of death**. That is a stimulant's résumé, not a hallucinogen's. Whatever the plant was, the doctrine attached to it describes arousal.

**Doctrine of Materia Medica / Herbal Lineage (DMM).** Within the European herbal inheritance the plant sits in the *astringent-and-pectoral* class: given for cough, for weak or sluggish circulation, for cold extremities, and for the sense of a chest that will not open. The two doctrines — binding and opening — look contradictory until one notices they are the same vasoconstrictive act read at two sites: tissue that is squeezed stops weeping, and an airway whose smooth muscle relaxes stops closing.

**Chinese Medicine — as contrast (CM).** Ma Huang (*E. sinica*) is classed as an acrid-warm exterior-releasing herb: it opens the exterior, disperses cold, diffuses Lung qi to stop wheezing, and promotes urination. It is never given casually — the classical tradition itself warns against it in deficiency, in spontaneous sweating, and in the debilitated. *E. distachya* is included here **for contrast**, not to inherit Ma Huang's indications. According to PubMed, *E. sinica* has been treated taxonomically as reducible to a subspecies of *E. distachya* on DNA-sequence grounds ([Kakiuchi 2017, *Yakugaku Zasshi*](https://doi.org/10.1248/yakushi.17-00129)) — which makes the pharmacological divergence below more, not less, important.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

Three doctrines converge, and all three map onto one molecule class.

*The wakefulness doctrine (haoma).* Ephedrine is a sympathomimetic: it releases endogenous noradrenaline and acts directly at adrenergic receptors, and unlike catecholamines it crosses into the CNS. Alertness, suppressed appetite, suppressed fatigue, raised heart rate — the physiological signature of *not-sleeping and not-tiring*. A liturgy praising a pressed stalk for wakefulness and strength is, if the plant was Ephedra, describing a β-adrenergic and CNS-stimulant effect in the only vocabulary available.

*The opening doctrine (cough, chest).* β₂-adrenoceptors on bronchial smooth muscle relax it. That is a real, mechanistically specific bronchodilation — ephedrine and its congeners are the historical seed of the entire β-agonist asthma drug class. The doctrine "it opens the chest" is not metaphor. It is the correct empirical observation of a receptor event.

*The binding doctrine (astringency, flux).* α₁-adrenoceptors on vascular smooth muscle constrict it. Pseudoephedrine's decongestant action is exactly this: constrict the mucosal vessels, the swollen membrane shrinks, the passage opens, the running stops. "Tightening what leaks" is α₁ agonism named phenomenologically.

And the same three receptor populations elsewhere in the body produce hypertension, arrhythmia, and stroke. **The doctrine is correct and the plant is still dangerous.** Those are not in tension. That is the entry's whole lesson.

The critical divergence: according to PubMed, in Uzbek field material almost all *E. distachya* sampled contained **only pseudoephedrine** (1.25–1.59% dry weight) with ephedrine not separately reported, whereas *E. equisetina* carried both ([Minami et al. 2020, *J Nat Med*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11418-020-01460-3)). Metabolomic profiling likewise separates *E. sinica*, *E. intermedia*, and *E. distachya* var. *distachya* chiefly by benzoic-acid analogues and ephedrine-type alkaloids ([Kim et al. 2005, *Chem Pharm Bull*](https://doi.org/10.1248/cpb.53.105)). **Chemotype is provenance-dependent.** The doctrine of one Ephedra does not transfer to another.

### Active Components

- **Pseudoephedrine** — the dominant alkaloid in sampled *E. distachya*; α₁-agonist decongestant, weaker CNS penetration than ephedrine.
- **Ephedrine** — mixed direct/indirect sympathomimetic; α and β agonist plus noradrenaline releaser; CNS-active. Present in the genus; **species- and provenance-variable**, and by the Uzbek dataset not reliably the *distachya* signature.
- **Norephedrine / norpseudoephedrine (cathine)** — minor congeners; controlled substances in several jurisdictions.
- **Benzoic acid analogues** — a discriminating class in NMR metabolomics of the genus.
- **Proanthocyanidins / tannins** — the plausible chemical basis of the astringent, flux-staunching doctrine, distinct from the alkaloids.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**None. This substance is not administered in Kenetica in any phase.**

It is filed as a **class exemplar**: the case where an ancient doctrine is pharmacologically *vindicated* and the substance is nevertheless withdrawn from use. Ephedra teaches the practitioner that traditional accuracy is not a safety argument. A tradition can correctly identify a receptor effect and be entirely silent on the population-level death rate, because a tradition has no denominator.

Practitioner-facing use is limited to: recognising sympathomimetic load in a person's stack; recognising that "herbal" pre-workout and weight-loss products may carry banned ephedrine alkaloids under unfamiliar botanical names; and interpreting the haoma-wakefulness doctrine as evidence that ritual arousal traditions were often pharmacological.

### Scientific Correlation

**Bronchodilation / decongestion — GRADED: STRONG mechanistically, and superseded clinically.** The β₂ and α₁ pharmacology underlying the "opens the chest / stops the flux" doctrine is textbook and is the origin of a real drug class. But the modern β₂-agonists and topical decongestants that descend from it are more selective and safer, and the crude plant offers no advantage. The doctrine won; the plant lost.

**Weight loss — GRADED: REAL BUT TRIVIAL, AND NOT WORTH THE RISK.** According to PubMed, the definitive meta-analysis found ephedrine/ephedra produced roughly **0.9 kg/month more weight loss than placebo**, with no data beyond six months, and with **2.2- to 3.6-fold increased odds of psychiatric, autonomic, or gastrointestinal symptoms and heart palpitations** ([Shekelle et al. 2003, *JAMA*](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.289.12.1537); companion AHRQ report [PMID 12647510](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12647510/)).

**Athletic performance — GRADED: NULL / INSUFFICIENT.** The same meta-analysis found **no trials of ephedra for athletic performance at all**, and the seven ephedrine trials were too heterogeneous to pool ([Shekelle et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.289.12.1537)). The haoma doctrine of strength-giving is *unsupported by controlled evidence in this specific sense* — subjective arousal is not demonstrated ergogenic benefit. **This deflates the most attractive reading of the ritual claim, and the deflation is the honest finding.** Ephedrine is additionally a WADA-regulated substance in competition [USER: verify current WADA threshold and status before citing].

**Cardiovascular / cerebrovascular toxicity — GRADED: STRONG SIGNAL, SERIOUS.** According to PubMed, FDA adverse-reaction review found ma huang use temporally related to **stroke (16), myocardial infarction (10), and sudden death (11)** among 37 patients, with underlying heart disease *not* a prerequisite and events **not limited to massive doses** — 36 of 37 were within manufacturer dosing ([Samenuk et al. 2002, *Mayo Clin Proc*](https://doi.org/10.4065/77.1.12)). Five ischaemic infarctions after OTC ephedra products were reported from a single centre over two years ([Chen et al. 2004, *J Neurol Sci*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jns.2003.08.012)). Dilated cardiomyopathy with acute liver injury has been reported where ephedra was combined with anabolic steroids and GHB ([Clark & Schofield 2005, *Pharmacotherapy*](https://doi.org/10.1592/phco.25.5.756.63592)). Ephedrine alkaloids continue to appear in OTC herbal supplements post-ban and to precipitate arrhythmia — a recent case describes atrial tachycardia traced to a supplement containing a banned botanical ephedrine alkaloid, resolving on discontinuation ([Cheng et al. 2022, *Eur Heart J Case Rep*](https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjcr/ytac023)).

**Honest counterweight.** A 14-day randomised trial of a commercial ephedra-caffeine product in 27 healthy overweight adults found **no significant cardiovascular changes** and no serious adverse events ([Kalman et al. 2002, *Int J Obes*](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.ijo.0802061)). This is real and must be reported. It is also small, short, in a healthy screened population, and precisely the study design that cannot detect events at a rate under 1 per thousand — a limitation the Shekelle analysis states explicitly. A null in an underpowered trial does not cancel a stroke series. Both go in the record.

**Net grade: the doctrines are pharmacologically vindicated; the plant is withdrawn.** Ephedra weight-loss supplements were prohibited by the US FDA and by the European Commission.

### Contraindications

**⚠️ HIGH-RISK SUBSTANCE. Kenetica issues no protocol for this plant.**

- **Absolute:** hypertension; any arrhythmia or history of arrhythmia; coronary artery disease; cardiomyopathy; prior stroke or TIA; aneurysm; phaeochromocytoma; hyperthyroidism; narrow-angle glaucoma; prostatic obstruction with retention; pregnancy and lactation.
- **MAOI interaction — potentially fatal.** Indirect sympathomimetics with monoamine oxidase inhibitors (including moclobemide, and including *Peganum harmala* / harmala-alkaloid preparations, which is a real and dangerous adjacency given the shared haoma literature) risk hypertensive crisis. Do not combine.
- **Stimulant stacking.** Caffeine, other sympathomimetics, anabolic steroids, thyroid hormone. Combination products drove much of the adverse-event record.
- **Do not assume an event needs a large dose or a diseased heart.** The FDA series shows neither is required.
- **Regulatory:** ephedra alkaloid supplements are prohibited in the EU and USA; ephedrine and pseudoephedrine are precursor-controlled; ephedrine is doping-regulated in competition.
- **Asteraceae-style cross-reactivity does not apply**, but Ephedra pollen is an allergen in some regions [USER: verify].
- **Identification:** *E. distachya* is not *E. sinica*, and neither is chemically uniform across provenance. Any material of unverified species and origin should be treated as of unknown alkaloid content.
