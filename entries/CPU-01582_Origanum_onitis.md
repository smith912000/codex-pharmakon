# Origanum onitis — Origanos Onitis, the onitis origanum (Dioscorides 3-33)

## *Origanum onitis — "Pot Marjoram", the single name on the translators' SUGGESTED line for 3-33* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** The row was added because one text on disk, Dioscorides, names the plant under this chapter. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED line (line 6812) prints the binomial without an authority and with the English "Pot Marjoram". The row has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is *3-33. ORIGANOS ONITIS*, heading at line 6811, text at line 6813, in Book Three among the roots of the *akanthoda*. The whole chapter reads: "That which is called onitis is paler in the leaves, resembles hyssop [3-30] more, and has seed like berries hanging together. It can do the same things as the Heracleotic [3-32]. Yet it is not altogether as effective."

It is a chapter of four sentences, and it is a comparison. The plant is given only by contrast with the Heracleotic origanum of the chapter before (3-32): "paler in the leaves", "resembles hyssop [3-30] more", with "seed like berries hanging together". Its uses are the same as the Heracleotic's, and it is "not altogether as effective". The chapter prints no use of its own, no habitat, no season, no preparation and no other name.

**The chapter before it.** 3-32 *ORIGANOS ERAKLEOTIKE* (heading at line 6808) is the chapter whose uses this one refers to: a warming herb, with antidotes for poisonous bites and poisons, a purge, and many external uses. The Codex does not state those uses for 3-33 beyond the text's "the same things". The chapter after it, 3-34 *AGRIORIGANOS* (the translators' wild marjoram), is a separate chapter and is not read into this row.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for the chapter id 3-33 and for the names *onitis* and *origanos onitis* found no existing entry on this chapter. I_1145 (*Origanum heracleoticum*, chapter 3-32), written in the same batch, treats the chapter this one refers to. I_433 and I_177 (*Origanum vulgare*) and I_464 (*Origanum majorana*) cite no Dioscorides chapter. No entry is duplicated.

### Active Components

The composition of the plant Dioscorides describes was not read, and nothing is stated here about the particular species. As a general fact, plants of the mint family (Lamiaceae) that are strongly aromatic, among them the marjorams and oreganos, owe their smell to volatile terpenoid oils. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The chapter records a pale-leaved origanum rated as a weaker equal of another. The Codex gives no protocol for it. The identification is the translators', and the chapter says almost nothing about the plant on its own.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives no preparation. It refers to what the Heracleotic origanum does and prints no quantity of its own. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints no taste and no warming, cooling, drying, moistening or astringent quality for 3-33. It rates the plant as "not altogether as effective" as the Heracleotic origanum, which 3-32 calls "warming".

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter offers a small instance of the discipline of attention that the cultivation spec describes for this tradition: it tells a plant by degree (paler in the leaf, closer to hyssop, seed in hanging berry-like clusters) and rates it against its better kin. The plant has no status in the chapter beyond that of a weaker equal. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The useful lesson is that Dioscorides sets a minor plant beside a major one and says plainly which is the lesser, and the abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The chapter prints no warning. The identification rests on a translators' suggestion, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. None of the traditional uses above is advice. [USER: verify]
