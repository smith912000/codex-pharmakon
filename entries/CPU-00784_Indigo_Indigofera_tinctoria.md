# Indigo — Neel (नील), Nila, True Indigo, Qir (قرمز/نيل), Dian-qing (靛青)

## *Indigofera tinctoria* L. | Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)

### Origin & Tradition

Indigo is the great blue of the ancient world — the dye that coloured the robes of empires and the fingers of the dyers, and, everywhere it was grown, a medicine as well as a pigment. *Indigofera tinctoria*, the "true indigo" of the Indian subcontinent, is a leguminous shrub whose leaves yield, through fermentation, the insoluble blue *indigotin*. But the medical traditions did not separate colour from cure: the same leaf that dyed the cloth cooled the blood, cleared the liver, blackened the hair, and countered poisons. Across India, the Arab world, China (through the parallel indigo-yielding species), West Africa and Europe, indigo is read as a **cold, Heat-clearing, toxin-resolving, liver-and-skin agent** — the blue that quenches heat and draws out poison. Its cross-tradition convergence is unusually tight for a plant defined first by craft.

**Ayurveda & Siddha (AT / SS):** In the Indian systems indigo is *Nili* / *Neel* (नील), a premier agent of the *keshya* (hair-benefiting) and *kushtha* (skin-disease) pharmacology. Classical Ayurveda uses *Nili* leaf and root for liver and spleen disorders (*yakrit-pliha*), for enlarged liver and jaundice, for chronic skin conditions, for epilepsy and nervous conditions (*apasmara*), and famously as the base of hair-blackening and hair-growth preparations. It is *pitta*- and *kapha*-reducing, cooling and detoxifying. The Siddha tradition of Tamil Nadu holds *Avuri* (indigo) in nearly the same office — a cooling liver-and-skin drug and a hair tonic, integrated into the great Siddha pharmacy of cooling *pitta*-agents. The consistency between Ayurveda and Siddha here is one of shared subcontinental lineage.

**Graeco-Arabic / Unani (DMM):** Indigo (*nil* / *wasma*) enters the Unani materia medica as a cold, dry agent, used externally on skin lesions, ulcers and inflammations, and as a hair colourant and scalp application (often paired with henna). Its cold-and-dry humoral grade placed it among the refrigerant-desiccant drugs for "hot" skin and liver conditions and for cooling inflamed swellings.

**Chinese Medicine (CM):** The indigo-yielding plants of the Chinese materia medica — processed into *qing-dai* (青黛, "indigo naturalis") and the related *ban-lan-gen* / *da-qing-ye* complex — constitute one of the most important cold, toxin-resolving classes in the pharmacopoeia. *Qing-dai* is cold and salty, clears Heat, cools Blood, resolves toxin (*jie-du*) and reduces swelling; it is used for febrile eruptions, mouth sores, "hot" bleeding and, in modern practice, inflammatory skin and gut conditions. *Indigofera tinctoria* stands as the South-Asian member of this broad indigo-medicine family, doctrinally aligned with the Chinese *qing* (blue-green cooling) principle.

**Sub-Saharan Ethnobotany (SBJ):** In the great indigo-dyeing cultures of West Africa (the Yoruba *adire*, the Tuareg blue, the Hausa dye-pits of Kano), indigo is a craft, an economy, and a folk pharmacy at once. Leaf preparations appear in local practice for skin complaints, wounds and as cooling washes, and the dye-pit itself carried ritual and medicinal associations. The plant's identity as *both* the blue of cloth and a remedy for the skin is a stable Pan-African reading.

**European Phytotherapy / Dyer's Pharmacopoeia (EP) & Folk Medicine (FMT):** European herbalism knew indigo chiefly through the woad-and-indigo dyeing trades, and adopted it (and the native *Isatis tinctoria*, woad, its doctrinal cousin) as a cooling, astringent agent for skin ulcers and inflammations. Folk domestic use continued its role as a hair colourant and an external application for "hot" sores.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Blue That Cools and Draws:**
Indigo is the Codex's exemplar of the *cold blue principle* — the doctrine that the blue-green end of the colour spectrum belongs, symbolically and functionally, to cooling, Heat-clearing and toxin-drawing. It is the chromatic opposite of the warm reds (cotton's Blood-moving warmth) and the crimson refrigerant of roselle: where roselle is red-against-red (cooling the hot blood with its own colour), indigo is *blue-against-heat* — the cool colour laid on inflamed, reddened, poisoned tissue to quench and to extract. This is the *qing* principle of Chinese doctrine and the *sheeta-pitta-hara* principle of Ayurveda meeting at the same shrub.

Indigo carries a second, deeper doctrinal charge: it is the plant of *transformation through fermentation and darkness*. The leaf is green; only through steeping, fermentation and oxidation does the hidden blue emerge — a visible enactment of the alchemical doctrine that the true medicine (and the true colour) is *latent*, released only by controlled decay and exposure to air. The dyer's vat, which appears dead-yellow-green and blooms blue only on contact with the air, is one of the oldest working images of *solve et coagula* in the material world. In Kenetica indigo therefore teaches both the *cold-blue cooling axis* and the *doctrine of the latent virtue* — the medicine that must be worked out of the plant, never simply taken from it.

### Active Components

- **Indican** — the colourless leaf glucoside (indoxyl-β-glucoside); the water-soluble precursor that, on hydrolysis and oxidation, yields the blue dye. The true "latent virtue" of the doctrine.
- **Indigotin (indigo)** — the insoluble blue pigment formed by oxidation of indoxyl; the finished dye and a bioactive indole.
- **Indirubin** — the red isomeric companion pigment (the active principle behind indigo-class anticancer/anti-inflammatory research in the *qing-dai* tradition).
- **Trans-tetracos-15-enoic acid (TCA)** — a long-chain monounsaturated fatty acid isolated from the aerial parts, identified as a hepatoprotective principle (see *Scientific Correlation*).
- **Flavonoids, rotenoids and glycosides** — contributing to the antioxidant and skin/liver activity; note the Fabaceae family also carries potentially toxic constituents (see *Contraindications*).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Indigo is taught as a **dual craft-and-medicine materia** and handled with graded caution:

1. **Foundational materia:** the indican→indigotin transformation is the Codex's canonical demonstration of the *latent virtue* doctrine — the medicine (and colour) released only through fermentation and oxidation.
2. **Skin & scalp axis (Foundational→Intermediate, external):** the cooling, toxin-drawing external application for "hot" skin and the traditional hair-darkening use (paired with henna) — external use is the safest register.
3. **Liver / cooling axis (Advanced, internal restricted):** the hepatoprotective and *pitta*-clearing internal use is real in tradition and partly supported by isolate research, but concentrated internal indigo preparations are handled at Advanced phase only, given Fabaceae toxicity concerns and the potency of the *qing-dai* class.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the traditional hepatoprotective ("liver-cooling") use has genuine isolate-level support:

- Singh, Chandan, Sharma et al. (2006), *Phytotherapy Research*, isolated *trans*-tetracos-15-enoic acid (TCA) from *Indigofera tinctoria* aerial parts and demonstrated significant, dose-dependent hepatoprotection against carbon-tetrachloride- and paracetamol-induced liver damage in rats and mice, comparable to silymarin, with oral LD50 > 2000 mg/kg (a favourable safety margin for the isolate). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.1856)
- Singh, Saxena, Chandan et al. (2001), *Phytotherapy Research*, showed the bioactive fraction "indigtone" from *I. tinctoria* gave significant dose-related hepatoprotective activity against CCl₄-induced liver injury in rats and mice, reversing raised transaminases, bilirubin and prolonged hexobarbitone sleep-time — corroborating the classical liver indication. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.760)

**Evidence grade:** *Low-to-moderate (preclinical).* The hepatoprotective tradition is supported by reproducible animal and isolate studies (two independent *Phytotherapy Research* papers), but human clinical trials are lacking. The broader *qing-dai*/indirubin anti-inflammatory and antineoplastic literature strengthens the cooling/toxin-resolving reading at the class level, though not specifically for *I. tinctoria* whole-leaf internal use.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM (indigo class):** Cold, salty; clears Heat, cools Blood, resolves toxin (*jie-du*), reduces swelling; Liver, Lung and Stomach channels (*qing-dai* profile).
- **Ayurveda / Siddha:** *Sheeta* (cooling) *virya*, bitter/astringent *rasa*, pungent *vipaka*; *pitta*- and *kapha*-reducing; *yakrit-uttejaka* (liver-acting), *keshya* (hair-benefiting), *kushtha-ghna* (skin-disease-clearing).
- **Galenic / Unani:** Cold & dry (grade II–III), refrigerant-desiccant; external for hot skin, ulcers and as hair colourant.

### Cultivation Register

**The short answer, before the detail.** Indigo is first a **dye plant** and secondarily a medicine. The register is therefore heavily **(b)/(c)**, and one distinction governs the whole section: **colour symbolism, ritual weight and craft mystique are not a cultivation status.** The blue is doctrinally loaded (see *Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer*) and that loading is real, but it says nothing about what the substance does to a person's vital constitution, and it is not permitted to stand in for a status here.

**Ebers Papyrus / ancient Egyptian (EP).** No cultivation framing is attested. Egyptian textile and pigment practice is a **craft record**, and craft use of a colourant is not a therapeutic attestation, still less a constitutional one. The Ebers corpus is moreover **indication-organised** — it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person — so substance identity within it is a modern hypothesis and no identification of *Indigofera tinctoria* there is claimed. No *heka* framing, no *ka*/*ba*/*akh*/*ib* claim, no deity attribution. Cultivation status: **absent**.

**Assyrian / Mesopotamian (AT).** No cultivation framing is attested, and the Codex's **blocking warning on Assyrian substance identifications, deity or demon attributions and tablet references is in force** — the default here is (c) and it is taken. Mesopotamian illness is classified partly by **causal agency** (divine hand, ghost, oath); no agency-opposing role is recorded for indigo in anything retrieved, and none is supplied. Cultivation status: **absent**.

**Dioscorides / Greek (DMM).** Greek and late-antique writers knew *indikon* as an imported blue pigment, and pigment knowledge is what the tradition principally records. **Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is constructed for him**; the Galenic three spirits are later. What the tradition does offer a cultivator is its **discipline of attention** — provenance, part, sensory assay, adulteration detection — and indigo is an unusually apt object for it, since a traded, processed, high-value pigment is precisely the class of material where adulteration is expected and identity is hardest to secure. That is a real practice and it points the right way here: toward doubt about identity, not toward a status. Cultivation status: **absent**.

**Avicenna / Unani (CM).** The Unani cultivation idea is the conservation of **innate heat and radical moisture** — a finite vital endowment whose depletion *is* ageing, so longevity practice in this system is conservation rather than acquisition. Indigo (*nīl* / *wasma*) is placed among the cold-and-dry refrigerant-desiccant drugs (grade II–III) and used externally on hot skin, ulcers and swellings, and as a hair colourant often paired with henna. That is a **humoral-therapeutic and cosmetic placement, not a conservation practice**: no strengthening of the *ruh* and no *mufarrih* attribution is attested for this substance in the sources retrieved, and no *Canon* chapter is cited, per house policy. Cultivation status: **unattested — a humoral standing only**.

**Shennong / Chinese (SBJ).** The Chinese cultivation hierarchy is the *san pin* 三品 grading, serving *yang sheng* 養生 — conservational, not curative. **No *san pin* grade for *Indigofera tinctoria* is attested in the sources retrieved**, and the barrier is a species barrier: the graded indigo-yielding material of the Chinese tradition (the *qing-dai* 青黛 / *da-qing-ye* / *ban-lan-gen* complex) derives from **other species**, and per the Codex's non-transfer rule a status belonging to those cannot be carried across to this plant. Note too that the *qing-dai* class is a cold, toxin-resolving, Heat-clearing material — the **short-course, disease-attacking register**, doctrinally opposite to the long-term nourishing-life register — so even the transferred status, were transfer permitted, would not be a cultivation status. No *jing*/*qi*/*shen* action is asserted. Cultivation status: **absent**.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS).** ⚠️ **The identification problem blocks any confident assignment here and must be stated first.** ***nīla* / *nīlī* in the Sanskrit materia medica does not denote a single plant**; the terms are colour-derived and multi-referent, and nothing retrieved establishes that classical *Nīlī* is *Indigofera tinctoria* throughout. Any status assigned to classical *Nīlī* therefore cannot be assigned to this species with confidence, and none is. Even setting identity aside, the indications the entry records — *yakrit-pliha* (liver and spleen), *kushtha-ghna* (skin-disease-clearing), *keshya* (hair-benefiting) — are **therapeutic and cosmetic, not constitutional**. Ayurveda's formal cultivation disciplines are *rasāyana* and *vājīkaraṇa* and its depletable vital distillate is *ojas*: **no *rasāyana* or *vājīkaraṇa* status, and no relation to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*, is attested.** The *keshya* office is worth a specific caution — hair-blackening reads to a modern eye as rejuvenation, and *rasāyana* is the rejuvenation discipline, so the two invite conflation. Darkening hair is a cosmetic act on a tissue; *rasāyana* is a constitutional discipline. They are not the same claim. Cultivation status: **unattested, and blocked at the identification step**.

**Practitioner's note.** For a cultivator, indigo is a plant to **work with rather than to take**. Nothing in any tradition tagged here supports long-term internal use for the sake of the constitution, and the cold, short-course grading of the indigo-medicine class is itself the warning. The external and hair applications are the honest register, patch-tested first, and the internal preparations stay at Advanced phase under supervision. What indigo genuinely teaches is a **discipline of preparation** — the leaf is green, and the blue must be fermented and oxidised out of it — and a discipline of **identity**, since neither the Sanskrit *nīlī* nor the Chinese indigo material reliably denotes this species. Both are craft attentions of the highest order. Neither is a cultivation status, and this register keeps them apart.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy & lactation:** insufficient safety data; traditional cooling/emmenagogue associations in the Fabaceae warrant avoidance of medicinal internal doses `[USER: verify]`.
- **Fabaceae toxicity:** the genus *Indigofera* contains species with hepatotoxic/neurotoxic nitro-compounds and the amino acid indospicine in some members; species identity and preparation matter greatly — never substitute uncharacterised *Indigofera* material for internal use.
- **Internal use:** concentrated indigo/*qing-dai*-type internal preparations can cause GI upset and, in the *qing-dai* clinical literature, rare serious effects (e.g. intussusception, hepatic and pulmonary events reported with high-dose indigo naturalis) — internal use only under qualified supervision.
- **Contact/allergy:** dye and fermented-leaf pastes can cause skin sensitisation in susceptible individuals; patch-test hair/skin applications.
