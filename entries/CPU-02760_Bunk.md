# Bunk — بنك (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *Bank (*bunk*, "bunchum"): a material carried from India and Yemen, which the Canon says some call the shavings of the roots of *umm ghaylān* (an acacia-type tree, our gloss). It is not identified here; the coffee reading proposed in the first-pass note is not supported by the passage (confidence low)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *bunk* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 11047-11055).

*What it is.* الماهية: هو شيء يحمل من الهند ومن اليمن. قال بعضهم: إنه من أصول أم غيلان إذا نجر فتساقط (11048-11049): "it is a thing carried from India and from Yemen. Some said: it is from the roots of *umm ghaylān* when planed and it falls off". Nothing in the passage names coffee or a drink.

*Choice.* الاختيار: أجوده الأصفر الخفيف العذب الرائحة والأبيض الرزين رديء (11051): "the best is the yellow, light, sweet of smell; the white, heavy kind is bad".

*Temperament and actions.* الطبع: حار يابس في الأولى وعند بعضهم بارد في الأولى (11052): "hot and dry in the first, and with some cold in the first". الأفعال والخواص: يقوي الأعضاء (11052-11053): "it strengthens the organs".

*Cosmetic.* It cleans the skin, dries the moistures beneath it, perfumes the body and cuts the smell of lime (*nūra*, the depilatory) (11053-11054). *Digestive organs:* good for the stomach (11054). *Head:* يشوش الدهن والعقل (11054-11055): "it disturbs the mind and the intellect". No preparation or dose is given.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Canon records a fragrant yellow imported material used on the skin and body and said to strengthen the stomach, with a warning that it disturbs the mind. Its source is unsettled. The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this material.

### Preparation & Dosing

None is given in the passage beyond choosing the yellow, light, sweet-smelling kind. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
