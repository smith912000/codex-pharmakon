# Phanji [SS] — Phanji (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna tr.)

## *A plant named in two of the Sutrasthana drug groups and used for its leaves; the translator glosses it "(Bhargi)" in one place and "a kind of Patha" in another, so the identification is unresolved; the standard modern identification is not given by the text and is not asserted here. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Phanji [SS]"; this entry replaces it. The text is Bhishagratna's English, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, with Vols. 2 and 3 for the clinical uses.

**Ayurveda (SS).** Two groups. Sutrasthana ch. 38, the Rodhradi group (Vol. I line 15431): "Rodhra, Savararodhra, Palasha, Kutannata, Ashoka, Phanji, Katphala, Elabaluka, Sallaki, Jingini, Kadamva, Sala and Kadali." Its stated effect (line 15437): "The group is antidotal to the deranged Kapham and fat, is astringent in its properties, removes vaginal and uterine disorders, neutralises the effects of poison (anti toxic) and acts as a styptic and purifying agent in a case of ulcer and arrests all secretions and excretions of the body." The Surasadi group (line 15460): "Surasa, white Surasa, Fanjjhaka, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika." Its effect (line 15470): "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough."

The leaf. Sutrasthana ch. 46, the leaf-vegetables passage (Vol. I line 21246 and following): Phanji is named among leaves "which have an astringent, sweet and bitter taste, and prove curative in haemoptysis. They subdue the Kapham, generate the Vayu and are astringent in their action and light of digestion"; and then (line 21259): "Phanji leaves are slightly tonic."

Clinical uses and the translator's glosses. In the diet for Atisara (Vol. III line 9706) the translator writes "Phanji (a kind of Patha)"; in the plaster for a poisoned-unguent case (Vol. II line 29215) he writes "Phanji, (Bhargi)". These glosses are different plants in the Ayurvedic literature, which is why the identity is left open. [USER: verify against a commentary (Dalhana) before any botanical name is given.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Rodhradi group, astringent and styptic, for vaginal and uterine disorders, ulcers and poison; as a member of the Surasadi group, a vermifuge for Kapha, catarrh, asthma and cough; as a leaf, for haemoptysis and "slightly tonic".
- **What the text does not say.** The plant is not identified, and no dose is given. "Slightly tonic" is the translator's phrase and is not a rasayana status.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** Leaves astringent, sweet and bitter in taste, subduing Kapha, generating Vayu, light of digestion (Vol. I line 21246 and following, as a group with Chuchchu, Jivanti and others). "Slightly tonic" (line 21259). The Rodhradi group is astringent (line 15437).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug sits in the Rodhradi and Surasadi gana of Sutrasthana ch. 38, with a therapeutic account. The only strengthening word is "slightly tonic" for the leaves (line 21259); it is not a rasayana or vajikarana assignment, and no *ojas* claim is made for the drug. The status is unattested.

**Practitioner's note.** An unidentified leaf and group-member in the Sushruta lists, astringent and used for ulcers, flux, catarrh and cough. Nothing here supports any cultivation use, and without an identification nothing should be gathered or taken.
