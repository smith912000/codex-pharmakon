# Pollia — 杜若 (Du Ruo); also Du Heng (杜衡) in the text

## **Pollia japonica* Thunb. (Commelinaceae), the root or rhizome; the identification is the modern one for 杜若 and is of medium confidence only, because the old texts themselves disagree on what 杜若 is (see Origin)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Du ruo* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The placeholder row it replaces was named "Radix Polliae Japonicae" from Yang's Latin.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 杜若 in the upper classic (卷一 上经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 971, 味 paragraph; name line 969). The Shennong text proper: "acrid, slightly warm" (味辛，微温). It "mainly treats" "counterflow qi below the chest and ribs; warms the centre; wind entering the brain door, swelling and pain of the head, copious snivel and tears" (主胸胁下逆气，温中，风入脑户，头肿痛，多涕泪出). Then: "Taken long, it benefits the essence (益精), brightens the eyes (明目), lightens the body (轻身)." Sun notes that the *Yiwen leiju* quotation reads 益气 ("benefits qi") where the text has 益精 ("benefits the essence"). "One name: Du Heng" 杜衡 (Sun adds that the *Yiwen leiju* writes 蘅 and that this is wrong). "It grows in rivers and marshes" (生川泽). There is no toxicity statement in the paragraph.

*Mingyi bielu layer* (line 973). Aliases 杜连, 白连, 白苓, 若芝. Habitat: Wuling and Yuanju. The root is gathered in the second and eighth months and dried in the sun. There is no Wu Pu layer for this entry.

*Sun's note* (案, line 975) is long, and it matters, because it shows the identity is contested in the sources themselves:
- *Shuowen*: "ruo, du ruo, a fragrant plant."
- *Guangya*: "Chu heng is du heng."
- *Shanhaijing*, Western Mountains (as Sun quotes it): above the Emperor's place is a plant whose form is like the mallow (葵) and whose smell is like *miwu*, called *du heng*; and *Erya* with Guo Pu: *du* is *tu lu*, "du heng, like the mallow and fragrant."
- *Chuci*: "gather du ruo on the fragrant islet."
- *Fanzi jiran*: du ruo "grows in Nanjun and Hanzhong"; and separately "Qin heng comes from Longxi, Tianshui."
- Shen Kuo (*Bu bitan*), as Sun quotes: "du ruo is today's *gao liang jiang*" (高良姜), and later people, not knowing this, "listed gao liang jiang separately." Sun then argues that, since the classic itself says the alias is *du heng*, the Mingyi's separate *du heng* entry is wrong.

So three identifications are in play within Sun's own note: a mallow-like fragrant herb called *du heng*, Shen Kuo's *gao liang jiang*, and the modern *Pollia*. The text does not decide among them. Nothing in the 味 paragraph describes the plant.

*Drug-relations table* (line 4339): du ruo "does well (得…良) with 辛夷 and 细辛; dislikes (恶) 柴胡 and 前胡."

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 2841) renders "Du Ruo (Radix Polliae Japonicae) is acrid and slightly warm... Protracted taking may boost the essence, brighten the eyes, and make the body light. Its other name is Du Heng (Horizontal Peartree)." Yang has no note on the identification. The page is under a running head that reads "Middle Class" in the text file (line 2801), but the class labels of this e-text are unreliable; Sun files it in the upper classic and the 久服 clause fits that, so the upper grade is used.

### Active Components

*Pollia japonica* is a monocot of the family Commelinaceae. No phytochemical source was retrieved for this plant, and none of the other candidate plants (a ginger-family rhizome in Shen Kuo's reading) is analysed here either. No constituent is asserted. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

This is a historical record of one text, not a recommended practice.

- **What the text says it is for.** Counterflow qi under the ribs and chest, a "cold" centre, and a head complaint with swelling, pain, runny nose and tears.
- **What the text says it does to the person.** "Taken long, benefits the essence, brightens the eyes, lightens the body." The *jing* claim rests on one reading: the *Yiwen leiju* has "qi" instead. *Qing shen* is a classical category and not weight loss.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no length of "long" taking.

Because the plant itself is disputed, nothing here supports taking any one modern plant as "du ruo". No Kenetica protocol is derived from this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest: the root, second and eighth months, dried in the sun (line 973). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the upper classic (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 971), and its own 久服 clause names three effects for taking it long: "benefits the essence, brightens the eyes, lightens the body." The preface (line 43) defines the upper drugs as those that "nourish life," nontoxic and harmless when taken long. Two cautions. First, the word *jing* ("essence") is the reading of Sun's text; the *Yiwen leiju* quotation reads *qi* ("benefits qi"), so the classic's claim about *jing* is not secure, and the *jing* gradient of the cultivation frame is not asserted here. Second, *qing shen* ("lighten the body") is a classical category and is not translated into a pharmacological action.

**Practitioner's note.** The classic gives this root the standing of a long-term drug, but its own commentary tradition cannot say with certainty which plant it is. A cultivator who wanted to act on the entry would first have to settle the identity, and the sources retrieved cannot do that. Nothing here supports taking any plant on the strength of the name.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** Do not take any plant on the basis of the name *du ruo* or *du heng*; the name has been applied to different plants (see the note by Sun Xingyan). [USER: verify]
- **Dose and form unknown.** The text gives none.
- **Pregnancy and lactation.** No statement is made in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Head and eye complaints.** The indications include head swelling and pain and eye complaints; these need medical assessment.
