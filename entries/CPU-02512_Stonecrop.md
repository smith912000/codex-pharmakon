# Stonecrop — 景天 *jingtian* (Shennong bencao jing)

## *Stonecrop, our identification (medium confidence) is Hylotelephium erythrostictum, a fleshy-leaved perennial of the stonecrop family; other Sedum and Hylotelephium species are candidates, and the Shennong text itself gives no description of the plant from which a species could be fixed* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Shennong bencao jing (SBJ).** Stonecrop is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The identification of *jingtian* with *Hylotelephium erythrostictum* is ours, made from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification; it is of medium confidence, and the text does not decide between it and other stonecrops. The source is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). Sun's edition sorts the drugs into three classes, and *jingtian* stands in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 景天 *jingtian*, same file, line 947). The preface says what the upper class is: upper drugs "mainly nourish life, to correspond to heaven; they are non-toxic; taken much or long they do not harm people; for one who wishes to lighten the body and benefit qi, not to age and to extend the years, take from the upper classic" (上药… 主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经; same file, line 43).

The paragraph that is the Shennong text proper reads: "Flavour bitter, balanced. It governs great heat, fire sores, heat of the body with vexation, and evil, bad qi. The flower governs women's 'leaking below', red and white, and lightens the body and brightens the eyes. One name, 'guard against fire' (戒火); one name, 'be careful of fire' (慎火). It grows in river valleys." (味苦，平。主大热、火创、身热烦，邪恶气。花，主女人漏下赤白，轻身、明目。一名戒火，一名慎火。生川谷。) Three points follow from the wording.

- The two names point to fire. They are given as aliases and the paragraph does not explain them.
- The *qing shen* ("lighten the body") clause belongs to the flower alone and is not tied to long taking. There is no *jiu fu* ("taken for long") clause anywhere in the paragraph.
- Sun adds a bracketed note that the *Taiping yulan* quotes a further name, "water mother" (水母), and that the Daguan edition prints that passage in black characters with 火 for 水. I do not know from the file what Sun meant by the colour of the characters beyond marking the clause as differing between witnesses. [USER: verify]

There is no Wu Pu or Mingyi paragraph for this entry. The only other layer is Sun's own note (案), which quotes Tao Hongjing (about 500 CE): "people today all raise it in basins on the roof, saying it wards off fire" (今人皆盆养之于屋上，云以辟火). That is a report of a household practice in Tao's day and is not a statement of the Shennong text. The Wu Pu paragraph of a different entry, firefly (荧火, same file, line 4009), lists *jingtian* among that insect's aliases; the two entries share a name there and nothing else, and no text read says why.

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, the stonecrop family (Crassulaceae) stores water in fleshy leaves and many of its members use a water-saving form of photosynthesis. Stonecrops contain organic acids, flavonoids and the usual plant sugars. No analytical source was retrieved for *H. erythrostictum* in particular, and none is asserted here. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Shennong paragraph records a bitter, balanced herb for "great heat", "fire sores", bodily heat with vexation, and for the flower, women's red and white discharge, with the flower said to lighten the body and brighten the eyes. The Codex does not translate these into present-day diagnoses and gives no protocol. Abnormal vaginal discharge or bleeding needs medical assessment, and "fire sores" cannot be matched to a condition from this text alone. The one use that is easy to repeat is Tao Hongjing's: a fleshy ornamental on a roof or window ledge. That is a horticultural note and has no medicinal weight.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph gives no preparation (no decoction, powder, wine or poultice) and no dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Stonecrops are succulent garden plants, widely grown for their late-summer flower heads, and are tough and drought-tolerant. Whether the herb relieves any condition the text names has not been tested in the sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong).** The paragraph prints one flavour and one nature: bitter (苦) and balanced (平). The class is upper (上经), by the placement of the entry. The text gives no further quality, and none is added here.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the upper classic, the class the preface (line 43) describes as nourishing life, corresponding to heaven, non-toxic and fit to be taken long (主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人). On the grading rule used for this series, placement in the upper classic is itself the ground for (a). In addition, the paragraph carries its own life-nourishing wording, "lightens the body and brightens the eyes" (轻身、明目), but this is limited to the flower (花) and is stated for the flower on its own; the whole-herb clause has no 久服 clause. The grade is therefore (a) by placement for the herb and also by the flower clause for that part. The household practice Tao Hongjing records, of keeping the plant on a roof against fire, is a protective custom reported by a later writer, not a cultivation practice of the classic.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator has nothing to do with this plant on the strength of the text beyond growing it, as Tao Hongjing reports people did. The text does not say how the flower is taken, in what amount or for how long, so there is no course to follow. Do not read the "lighten the body" clause as a promise.

### Contraindications

- **Identification.** The species is not fixed by the text. Stonecrop species differ and some *Sedum* plants are acrid. Do not eat or apply a plant on a guess. [USER: verify]
- **Women's bleeding and discharge.** The historical use is for "leaking below"; modern bleeding or discharge needs medical assessment, not a herb from a text with no dose.
- **Pregnancy and children.** The text says nothing on either. No use in pregnancy is supported. [USER: verify]
- **Sedum acre.** The spine has separate entries for the Dioscoridean *Sedum acre* (I_1186) and *Sedum rupestre* (I_1187); they are different plants and nothing from them is transferred here.
