### Origin & Tradition

Śālaparṇī is a small undershrub of the Indian subcontinent's plains and lower hills. Its root is one of the most doctrinally important roots in classical Ayurveda because it heads a named *gaṇa* (drug-group) in Sushruta and is a component of the two most-used Ayurvedic root-formulae, *Laghupañcamūla* and (through it) *Daśamūla*.

**Ayurvedic (Sushruta & Caraka):** In the *Sushruta Saṃhitā*, Śālaparṇī (under the synonym *Vidārigandhā* — "smelling of Vidārī") is the **head/eponym of the Vidārigandhādi gaṇa**, a large group of predominantly sweet, cooling, Vāta-Pitta-pacifying, strength-and-tissue-building drugs. It is one of the five roots of the **Laghupañcamūla** ("lesser five roots"), which together with the Bṛhatpañcamūla ("greater five roots") constitutes the classical **Daśamūla** ("ten roots") — the decoction that is arguably the single most important anti-Vāta, anti-inflammatory, febrifuge, and post-partum formula in the entire pharmacopoeia. Caraka places it among *balya* (strengthening), *śvayathuhara* (anti-oedema), and *saṃgrāhaka*-adjacent groups. Classical indications: *jvara* (fever), *śvāsa-kāsa* (respiratory conditions), *śotha* (oedema/inflammation), *atisāra* (diarrhoea/dysentery), general debility, and as a *rasāyana* nutritive-restorative.

Its Sanskrit names encode its doctrine: **Sthirā** ("the stable/firm one") — because it steadies and grounds aggravated Vāta; **Aṃśumatī** ("the rayed/fibrous one") — for its foliage; **Vidārigandhā** — signalling its classification alongside the sweet nutritive Vidārī group.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the Grounding Root that heads its gaṇa:** Śālaparṇī's dravyaguṇa logic is coherent and central. It is *madhura* (sweet) in rasa and *madhura* in vipāka, *śīta* (cooling) in vīrya, and *guru-snigdha* (heavy, unctuous) in guṇa — the textbook profile of a Vāta-Pitta pacifier and *balya/bṛṃhaṇa* (bulk- and strength-building) agent. Yet it is also *grāhī*/*saṃgrāhaka* (astringent, binding — hence its use in atisāra) and, in Daśamūla, functions as an anti-inflammatory and febrifuge. This apparent doubleness — nourishing yet febrifuge, building yet drying diarrhoea — is exactly what earns it its place at the *head* of the Vidārigandhādi gaṇa: it is the balancing exemplar of a whole class. The name **Sthirā**, "stability," is the doctrinal signature: this is a root that anchors and steadies a system blown about by aggravated Vāta (mobility, dryness, tremor, wasting), returning it to firm ground — the physiological correlate of its later-demonstrated neuroprotective/nootropic and cardioprotective actions.

### Active Components

- **Indole-3-alkaloids / β-phenylethylamine derivatives** — including gangetin and related pterocarpanoids/alkaloids reported from the roots.
- **Pterocarpans and isoflavonoids** (Fabaceae-typical).
- **Sterols** and triterpenoids.
- **Tryptamine-type and other minor alkaloids** historically noted in *Desmodium* species.
- Flavonoids and phenolics contributing antioxidant activity.

### Scientific Correlation

Attribution: findings from PubMed.

- **Nootropic / anti-amnesic (preclinical):** Joshi & Parle (2006, *Yakugaku Zasshi*) reported that aqueous extract of *D. gangeticum* (50–200 mg/kg p.o., 7 days) significantly improved learning and memory in mice, reversed scopolamine-induced and ageing-induced amnesia, and decreased whole-brain acetylcholinesterase activity — concluding it a promising candidate for memory disorders and dementia. This aligns with the traditional *medhya*-adjacent, *Sthirā* (steadying) doctrine. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1248/yakushi.126.795)
- **Anticancer (preclinical, in vitro):** Chen, Lu & Tsai (2022, *BioMedicine (Taipei)*) found a water crude extract of *D. gangeticum* inhibited A549 human lung carcinoma cells in a concentration- and time-dependent manner, arresting the cell cycle in G1 via modulation of p21, p27, cyclin D1/E and Cdc2/cyclin A/B1 expression. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.37796/2211-8039.1362)

**Evidence grade:** **Preclinical** (rodent behavioural and in-vitro cell-line studies). The traditional anti-inflammatory, febrifuge, cardioprotective and balya/rasāyana uses are longstanding and biologically plausible but not established by human clinical trials — treat as **traditional with supporting preclinical data**.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** *Madhura* (sweet), *Tikta* (bitter — secondary); *Kaṣāya* (astringent, in its grāhī aspect)
- **Guṇa (quality):** *Guru* (heavy), *Snigdha* (unctuous)
- **Vīrya (potency):** *Śīta* (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** *Madhura* (sweet)
- **Prabhāva (specific effect):** Its role as the **eponymous head of the Vidārigandhādi gaṇa** and its steadying (*Sthirā*) grounding of Vāta may be regarded as its prabhāva — a balancing action broader than its rasa/vīrya/vipāka predict.
- **Doṣa action:** *Vāta-śāmaka* and *Pitta-śāmaka* (primary — sweet, cooling, unctuous), Kapha-neutral (not markedly aggravating despite sweetness, owing to its bitter/astringent secondary rasa)
- **Karma (actions):** *Balya* (strengthening), *bṛṃhaṇa* (nourishing/bulk-building), *rasāyana* (rejuvenative), *jvaraghna* (febrifuge — in Daśamūla), *śothahara / śvayathuhara* (anti-oedema/anti-inflammatory), *grāhī* (astringent, anti-diarrhoeal), *śvāsahara / kāsahara* (anti-dyspnoeic/antitussive), *hṛdya*-adjacent (cardioprotective in modern data), *medhya*-adjacent (memory-supporting)

**Gaṇa membership (Sushruta):** Head of **Vidārigandhādi gaṇa**; component of **Laghupañcamūla** → **Daśamūla**.

### Contraindications

- Generally well-tolerated in classical doses. As a heavy, sweet, unctuous, building root, use cautiously in significant Kapha excess, marked *āma* (undigested metabolic toxin) / low agni, or where a strongly cleansing rather than nourishing action is required.
- Data in pregnancy/lactation are traditional (it features in post-partum Daśamūla formulae) rather than trial-based — use under qualified guidance. `[USER: verify]`
- No robust human safety/interaction data; the nootropic finding involves cholinergic (AChE) modulation, so theoretical caution alongside cholinergic drugs. `[USER: verify]`
- Botanical authentication matters — *Desmodium* species and market substitutes (e.g. *Pseudarthria*, *Uraria*) are sometimes traded under overlapping vernacular names.
