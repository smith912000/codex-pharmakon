# Self-Heal — Heal-All, Woundwort, Xia Ku Cao (夏枯草), Brunella

## *Prunella vulgaris* L. | Family: Lamiaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, CM, DMM, SBJ
**Energetic Classification:** Cold and dry (Galenic, second degree); bitter–pungent, cooling
**Training Phase:** Clarification — the cooling, clearing herbs that quiet inflammatory heat and restore clear perception before deeper work.

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Shennong / Chinese Materia Medica (夏枯草, Xia Ku Cao).** In the Chinese canon the herb is known by its dried flowering spike, *Xia Ku Cao* — literally "summer-withering grass," named for the spike that browns and dries at the height of summer. It is classed among the herbs that **clear liver heat and drain fire**, and is the classical remedy for red, painful, swollen eyes, headache with dizziness, and above all for **scrofula and goitre** — the "phlegm-fire nodules" of the neck that modern eyes read as lymphatic and thyroid swellings. Its reputation for dispersing nodules and detumescence is one of the most stable indications in the whole pharmacopoeia and is still standard clinical usage in China today.

**Dioscorides & the Greek stream.** The Lamiaceae woundworts were prized across the Mediterranean tradition for closing wounds and easing sore throat. The European common name *Prunella* / *Brunella* is traditionally tied to *die Bräune*, the quinsy or throat inflammation the herb was gathered to treat — a doctrine of the throat that survives in the modern binomial.

**Assyrian & Near-Eastern herbal record.** Woundworts and self-heals belong to the deep Mesopotamian tradition of gathered field herbs used as wound-washes and febrifuges; the throat-and-fever indication runs consistently through the Near-Eastern and later Arabic streams into European folk practice, where the plant earned its most famous name — **Heal-All** — for the breadth of its reputed uses.

**European folk medicine (EP).** By the early-modern herbals *Prunella vulgaris* was a first-reach remedy for **sore throat, fever, and wound-healing**, applied as a gargle, a poultice, and an infusion. A 1997 pharmacognostic review explicitly frames it as a "rediscovered medicinal plant," noting its historical use "alleviating pains in the throat, fevers and accelerating wound healing" (Marková, Sousek & Ulrichová, *Ceska Slov Farm* 1997, PMID 9244565).

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** The name *Heal-All* is a doctrine of universality — a humble field mint credited with mending almost anything — and the Chinese name is a doctrine of *timing*: the herb that withers as summer peaks was read as carrying summer's fire out of the body. Both doctrines point at the same real pharmacology: a plant unusually rich in **rosmarinic acid** and pentacyclic triterpene acids, giving it broad antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial reach that plausibly underwrites both the "heals everything" and the "drains fire" reputations.

### Active Components

- **Rosmarinic acid** — the signature phenolic; the total extract runs to roughly a quarter rosmarinic acid by weight in the organic fraction studied by Psotová et al. (2003). Potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory.
- **Ursolic and oleanolic acids** — pentacyclic triterpenoids on the spike, associated with anti-inflammatory and antitumour activity.
- **Prunellin / prunelline** — an anionic polysaccharide fraction credited with immunomodulatory and antiviral activity.
- **Caffeic acid** and **3-(3,4-dihydroxyphenyl)lactic acid** — supporting phenolic acids.
- **Mangiferin, salviaflaside, flavonoids** — additional bioactives identified by plasma pharmacochemistry.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Self-Heal is a **Clarification-phase cooling herb**: used to settle inflammatory heat, soothe the throat, calm red irritated eyes, and support the lymphatic "nodule-dispersing" action the Chinese tradition emphasises. In the Kenetica framework it is a gentle, high-safety opener — the herb you reach for when heat, irritation and low-grade swelling cloud the system before clearer work can begin. Its documented anti-insomnia and calming actions also make it a candidate for quieting an over-heated, restless mind.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the pharmacology broadly supports the traditional profile, though most evidence is preclinical.

- Psotová et al. characterised the organic fraction (25.7% w/w rosmarinic acid) and found **DPPH radical-scavenging, inhibition of LDL oxidation, hepatocyte and mitochondrial protection, 5-lipoxygenase/LTB4 inhibition, antiproliferative and antimicrobial activity**, concluding the "antioxidative, antimicrobial, together with antiviral effects offer good prospects" for the plant (*Phytother Res* 2003, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.1324)).
- Lin et al. confirmed **anti-insomnia activity** in mice, isolating mangiferin, rosmarinic acid and salviaflaside as the active constituents that shortened sleep latency and prolonged sleep, while restating the classical indications — "clearing the liver and expelling fire, improving eyesight, dispersing nodules" — and modern antiviral/antibacterial/immunomodulatory activity (*J Ethnopharmacol* 2021, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2021.114373)).
- The 1997 review documents rosmarinic acid content, immunomodulation by the polysaccharide **prunelline**, and antiviral activity of several constituents (PMID 9244565).

**Evidence grade:** Moderate mechanistic / preclinical; strong traditional. Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial and antiviral activities are well-supported in vitro; robust human clinical trials remain limited. Antiviral claims (including for enveloped viruses) rest on cell-culture and animal data, not confirmed clinical endpoints.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Bitter, pungent, cold. Enters Liver and Gallbladder channels. Clears liver fire, brightens the eyes, disperses phlegm-fire nodules and reduces swelling.
- **Ayurveda:** Not a classical dravya, but by profile it reads as *tikta* (bitter) / *kashaya* (astringent) rasa, *shita* (cooling) virya — pitta-pacifying.
- **Galenic / Unani:** Cold and dry, repercussive and desiccant — appropriate to hot, moist inflammatory conditions of the throat and eyes.

### Contraindications

- Cooling and draining by nature; caution in cold, deficient, or spleen-cold constitutions (may aggravate poor digestion) per TCM doctrine.
- Theoretical additive effects with antihypertensive, anticoagulant, and immunomodulatory drugs — supervise in patients on such medication.
- Thyroid: traditionally used *for* goitre, but self-treatment of any thyroid or neck swelling is inappropriate; such swellings require medical diagnosis first.
- Pregnancy and lactation: insufficient safety data — avoid without professional guidance.
- Possible Lamiaceae contact sensitivity in susceptible individuals.
