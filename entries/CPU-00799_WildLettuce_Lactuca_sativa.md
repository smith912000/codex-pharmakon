# Lettuce & Wild Lettuce — "Lettuce Opium" · Lactucarium · Sleep-Lettuce

## *Lactuca sativa* L. (and *L. virosa* L.) | Family: Asteraceae (Compositae)

**Tradition Tags:** AT, CM, DMM, EP, SS
**Energetic Classification:** Cold and moist in the second degree (Galenic–Unani) — a refrigerant soporific and anaphrodisiac
**Training Phase:** Phase IV — Sedation & Sleep (gentle soporifics and nervines)

---

### Origin & Tradition

Lettuce is the great domestic paradox of the materia medica: the blandest thing in the garden and, in its bitter wild kin, one of antiquity's better-known sleep drugs. The Codex treats the genus together — cultivated **garden lettuce** (*Lactuca sativa*) and its pungent relatives **wild lettuce** (*L. virosa*) and prickly lettuce (*L. serriola*) — because they share the trait that made lettuce medicine: a bitter, milky **latex** that wells from the cut stem and dries to a brown resin. Air-dried, this latex is **lactucarium** — "lettuce opium" — which nineteenth-century pharmacy sold in cakes as a mild, non-addictive substitute for opium in cough and sleeplessness. The name is honest about the ambition and dishonest about the pharmacology: lactucarium is *not* an opiate, as the science below makes plain.

Across the classical world, lettuce was above all the **plant of sleep**. The doctrine is old, coherent, and nearly universal — with one instructive exception in Egypt, where lettuce was, oppositely, the erotic emblem of the fertility god **Min**.

**Assyro-Babylonian (BM).** Mesopotamian medical and lexical texts include lettuce (*ḫassu*, the standard reading, to be flagged [USER: verify] for exact identification) among cultivated pot-herbs and cooling remedies; the *functional* niche — a cooling, soothing garden green — is secure, even where humoral grading is not preserved.

**Avicenna & the Unani stream (AT).** In the *Canon*, lettuce (*khass*) is a cold-and-moist drug: a *munawwim* (sleep-bringer) and refrigerant, calming of "heat," useful for insomnia, headache of hot cause, and — consistent with its cooling doctrine — as an **anaphrodisiac** that damps sexual heat and (in the seed) checks nocturnal emissions. Unani lettuce-seed remains a classic cooling nervine.

**Dioscorides & the Graeco-Roman tradition (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* distinguishes garden lettuce (cooling, sleep-inducing, milk-promoting, and lust-quieting) from the stronger wild lettuce, whose abundant *opos* (latex) it likens in effect to poppy — an explicit ancient equation of lettuce-latex with opium's soporific action. Galen famously recorded eating lettuce in old age to procure sleep. This is the doctrinal heart of "sleep-lettuce."

**Egyptian — the Ebers papyrus & the Min exception (EP).** Pharaonic medicine used lettuce among cooling and digestive remedies; but Egypt is the great **counter-tradition**. Here the tall, latex-rich lettuce was sacred to **Min**, the ithyphallic god of fertility and potency — its erect form and copious white "milk" read as an **aphrodisiac** signature, the exact opposite of the Graeco-Roman anaphrodisiac. The Codex flags this as a genuine doctrinal fork worth preserving: same plant, opposite sexual doctrine, on two different signature readings of the same milky latex.

**Sushruta & Ayurveda (SS).** Lettuce (a comparatively minor and partly post-classical introduction to the Indian pharmacopeia) sits in the cooling, *pitta*-pacifying pot-herb class; lettuce-seed (*kāhū*) is used, as in Unani, as a cooling, calming, sleep-favouring agent. Precise classical *rasa/vīrya/vipāka* attribution for the species is loosely fixed and should be treated as [USER: verify].

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** Two doctrines demand mapping — the sleep/sedation axis (nearly universal) and the sexual axis (forked) — and the chemistry sorts them cleanly.

- **"Cold-and-moist soporific / lettuce-opium" → the sesquiterpene lactones lactucin and lactucopicrin, genuine mild sedative-analgesics, but NOT opioids.** The bitter latex carries **guaianolide sesquiterpene lactones** — chiefly **lactucin, lactucopicrin (intybin), and 11β,13-dihydrolactucin**. These are real CNS-active molecules with demonstrated sedative and analgesic effects in animals (below). The ancient equation "lettuce-latex ≈ poppy" was a shrewd *functional* observation — both milky latices calm and dull — but the molecular basis is entirely different: no morphinan, no opioid receptor, no true narcotic potency. The doctrine is **half-vindicated and half-corrected**: real mild sedation, false opioid identity. The "cold and moist" humoral grade even fits the *phenomenology* — a gentle, non-euphoric drowsiness rather than opium's warm narcosis.
- **The anaphrodisiac doctrine (Galen/Unani) vs the Min aphrodisiac doctrine (Egypt) → mostly signature, with a faint sedative shadow.** There is no good evidence lettuce is either a true aphrodisiac or anti-aphrodisiac. The *anaphrodisiac* reading follows logically from the "cooling/sedating" frame (a sedative that quiets arousal); the *aphrodisiac* reading is pure **doctrine of signatures** — the erect, milk-exuding stalk read as phallic potency. Both are **deflated** to doctrine: the cooling-sedative claim has a thin mechanistic shadow (sedation can blunt arousal); the fertility claim has none beyond symbolism.

The signature to hold onto is the **latex itself**: the same white "milk" that generated the opium comparison, the Min fertility symbol, and the bitter sedative resin is, chemically, the reservoir of the sesquiterpene lactones that make lettuce genuinely, if mildly, calming.

### Active Components

- **Sesquiterpene lactones (guaianolides)** — **lactucin, lactucopicrin (intybin), 11β,13-dihydrolactucin**, and lactucin derivatives; the bitter, CNS-active principals of the latex/lactucarium. Lactucopicrin is the most potent of the sedative-analgesic trio.
- **Triterpenes and sterols** — lactucerin, β-amyrin, and phytosterols in the latex resin.
- **Flavonoids and phenolics** — quercetin glycosides, chicoric/chlorogenic acids; antioxidant contributors (concentrated in the leaf).
- **Fatty-acid methyl esters (seed)** — hexadecanoic/octadecanoic esters identified in bioactive seed extracts, implicated in GABA-related activity (see below).
- The active lactones are richest in **wild lettuce (*L. virosa*)** and the dried latex; ordinary salad *L. sativa* is comparatively depleted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Lettuce belongs to Phase IV as the **gentlest tier of soporific** — a mild, non-habituating nervine for restlessness, difficulty settling to sleep, and irritable/"hot" wakefulness, and traditionally for irritable dry cough (the nineteenth-century lactucarium indication). Its honest ceiling is *mild*: a cooling wind-down aid, taken as evening leaf, seed infusion, or (historically) lactucarium, not a reliable hypnotic and certainly not an analgesic to lean on. It pairs conceptually with the stronger Phase IV/V narcotics as their food-safe, low-risk foothill.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the evidence supports a **genuine but mild** sedative-analgesic action from the lactones and seed extracts — and it explicitly does **not** support an opioid mechanism.

- **The lactones are real sedative-analgesics (animal data).** Lactucin, lactucopicrin, and 11β,13-dihydrolactucin were tested in mice: at 15–30 mg/kg the compounds produced **analgesia comparable to ibuprofen** in hot-plate and tail-flick tests, and **lactucin and lactucopicrin (but not dihydrolactucin) reduced spontaneous locomotor activity** — a sedative signature — with **lactucopicrin the most potent** (Wesołowska et al., *J Ethnopharmacol* 2006; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2006.03.003)). This is the pharmacological core that vindicates the "sleep-lettuce" doctrine at the mild end.
- **Seed extracts show anxiolytic/sedative and anticonvulsant activity via a plausible GABA route.** A study of *Lactuca* (Asteraceae) seed extracts in mice found significant **anxiolytic** effects (elevated-plus-maze, hole-board, light/dark), **potentiation of barbiturate-induced sleep** (reduced latency, prolonged duration), and anticonvulsant/anti-epileptogenic activity, with GC-MS-identified fatty-acid esters docking into **GABA-aminotransferase** in silico (Ullah et al., *Antioxidants (Basel)* 2022; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox11112232)). Supportive of the calming/soporific tradition, though preclinical.
- **The "opium substitute" identity is corrected, not confirmed.** No retrieved evidence shows lettuce lactones act at opioid receptors or possess opioid potency; the historical "lettuce opium" label reflects a *functional* resemblance (a calming milky latex) rather than shared chemistry or mechanism. Human clinical sedative/hypnotic evidence for lactucarium is **weak-to-absent** — the animal signal has not been converted into controlled human trials.

**Evidence grade:** the **mild soporific/analgesic** doctrine has a real but **preclinical (rodent)** foundation with an identified active class; the **"lettuce = opium"** claim is **deflated** to a functional analogy (not an opioid); the **aphrodisiac/anaphrodisiac** doctrines are graded **signature/folklore** with, at most, a faint sedation-blunts-arousal shadow; robust **human** evidence is lacking.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic–Unani (AT):** *khass* — cold and moist (≈second degree); *munawwim* (soporific), refrigerant, and anaphrodisiac; the seed a cooling nervine.
- **Ayurveda (SS):** cooling, *pitta*-pacifying pot-herb; seed calming and sleep-favouring. Formal *rasa* (likely *tikta/madhura*), *vīrya* (*śīta*, cooling), *vipāka* not securely classically fixed for the species [USER: verify].
- **TCM (CM):** **not attested** as a major classical Chinese materia medica drug in the sedative role; no tag assigned (cultivated lettuce is used as food; *Lactuca* is not a canonical Chinese sedative herb).
- **Egyptian (EP):** cooling/digestive remedy *and*, doctrinally, the aphrodisiac emblem of Min — the genus's great counter-tradition.
- **Assyro-Babylonian (BM):** a cultivated cooling pot-herb; specific humoral grade not preserved [USER: verify].

### Contraindications

- **Do not treat as an opioid or strong hypnotic.** Effects are mild; substituting lettuce/lactucarium for prescribed analgesia or sedation is unsafe and ineffective.
- **Wild lettuce over-dose / bitterness.** Large doses of *L. virosa* or lactucarium can cause nausea, dizziness, sweating, mydriasis, and (rarely, at gross excess) anticholinergic-like or GI disturbance; keep to traditional low doses.
- **Sedative interactions.** May add to the effect of alcohol, benzodiazepines, barbiturates, and other CNS depressants (consistent with the barbiturate-potentiation data); avoid combining and before driving.
- **Asteraceae allergy.** Cross-reactive sensitivity (ragweed, daisy, chamomile family) and latex contact dermatitis are possible.
- **Pregnancy/lactation.** Culinary lettuce is food-safe; medicinal wild-lettuce/lactucarium doses lack safety data and should be avoided.
- **Not an anaphrodisiac/aphrodisiac therapy.** Neither sexual claim is evidence-based; do not use for those indications.
