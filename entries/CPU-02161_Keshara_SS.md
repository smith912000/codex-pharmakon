**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Keshara is printed in the Elardi (Eladi) group, Sutrasthana chapter 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15505 to 15512 (the name is on line 15512). The translator's sentence reads: "The group of medicinal drugs known as the Eladi-Gana consists of Ela, Tagara, Kushtha, Mansi, Dhyamaka, Tvaka, Patra, Nagapushpa, Priyangu, Harenuka, Vyaghranakha, Shukti, Chhanda, Sthauneyaka, Shriveshtaka, Chocha, Choraka, Valaka, Guggulu, Sarjarasa, Turushka, Kunduruka, Aguru, Sprikka, Ushira, Bhadradaru, Kumkuma, Punnaga and Keshara." The metrical text (lines 15520 to 15527) reads: "The therapeutic virtue of the group consists in subduing the action of Vayu and Kapham and in neutralising the effects of poison. It is a cosmetic and arrests the eruption of pimples and other vegetations on the skin such as rash, urticaria etc. and checks the itching sensation incidental thereto."

**Second appearance, Anjanadi group.** "The group known as the Anjanadi Gana consists of Anjana, Rasanjana, Nagapushpa, Priyangu, Nilotpala, Nalada, Nalina, Keshara and Madhuka" (lines 15641 to 15644). Its metrical text (lines 15645 to 15649) reads: "An attack of haemoptysis readily yields to the curative virtue of the group under discussion. It is anti-toxic in its character and allays the internal burning sensation of the body."

**What the text gives about the plant.** Nothing. Related compound names occur in the same lists ("Nagakeshara", "Padma keshara"), and the word *keshara* seems in the context to name a part, filament or flower, but the translation does not say which plant or part is meant. ### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is named inside a therapeutic drug group (*gana*) of Sutrasthana chapter 38, the Eladi and Anjanadi groups. The group's metrical text speaks of curing or subduing disorders. It does not call the group, or any member, a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) drug, and it does not mention *ojas*. The sources retrieved therefore attest a therapeutic framing only, and no cultivation status. The *sattva/rajas/tamas* lists of later popular literature are not from Sushruta and are not applied.
