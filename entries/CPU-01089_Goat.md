# Goat — Goat's Fat, Goat's dung, Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat (Bryan's Ebers) / goat meat, goat's milk, goat blood, goat brain, billy-goat pubic hair (Clark 1995, Men-Tsee-Khang 2011)
## *The domestic goat, its flesh, milk, blood, brain, hair and fat; the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary prints* Capra hircus *Linnaeus; the Ebers names are Bryan's English renderings* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP, FMT

### Origin & Tradition

This entry covers the goat's other parts and products. Goat liver, the Tibetan eye medicine, has its own entry (`III_21_Goat_liver.md`) and is not repeated; cattle are in `III_17_Cattle.md`.

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The English text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 *The Papyrus Ebers*, a translation with summaries; the Egyptian words are not printed. Bryan's list of the animals drawn on for remedies ends "Stag Gazelle Ram Goat" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1398, page 34 of the book); the spine's list row is `_sources/ebers.csv` line 119, "Goat", source field "Ebers Papyrus; Ghalioungui 1987 trans., Glossary I (Medicaments)", a glossary not on disk and not read. Items in the pages read:

- *Goat's fat.* In a drying poultice for a wound: "To dry a wound it was plastered with Incense, Onions, and Cow Fat; or if the only animal available was a goat, then some Goat's Fat was beaten up with Onions and Wax and used as a poultice" (line 2371). In two salves "to make everything possible supple": one of writing-fluid, vermilion and goat's fat with honey, another of ass's dung, yeast of the opium-drink, goat's fat, lettuce, onions, beans and white oil (lines 2183-2188; Bryan's remark that the first has "more claim to colour than cure" is his).
- *Goat's dung.* In a burns series, the second-day dressing: "Goat's dung. Burn, crush, rub in Yeast-that-is-fermenting, and put thereto" (line 2253).
- *Fat of the "Egyptian goat".* One of six fats (lion, hippopotamus, crocodile, cat, serpent, Egyptian goat) "make into one and rub the head of the Bald One therewith" in a remedy for the growth of hair (lines 4020-4028).

No recitation is reported with any goat compound in the lines read; since Bryan summarises, that is not proof the papyrus gives none. No deity is attributed.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The goat is the most widely mentioned domestic animal in the Tibetan lines read.

- *Flesh.* "Goat meat is heavy and cool, raises the three humors and is beneficial in syphilis, smallpox and burns" (Clark 1995, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 674); "Goat meat is heavy and cool in quality; it activates all three nyepa and helps against venereal disease, small pox and burns" (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 2931). It is also a food for the *tripa* (bile) disorder diet (Clark line 175; Men-Tsee-Khang line 1105) and one of the foods that arouse the *loong* (wind) disorder: "eating frail meat of goat or kompo" (Men-Tsee-Khang line 2106; Clark line 482, "goat meat").
- *Milk and dairy.* "Goat's milk cures asthmatic conditions" (Clark line 708; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3059, "Goat milk is good for treating asthma"). The dairy products of the cow and goat "are cool and light" (Clark line 711; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3067); butter of cow or goat milk "is cool and pacifies hot disorder associated with *loong* disorders" in Men-Tsee-Khang (line 2948) and "the cooling butter of cow and goat eliminates wind fevers" in Clark (line 686). Curd and buttermilk of goat or cow milk are for *tripa* disorders (Men-Tsee-Khang line 4428), while cold goat milk, curd and buttermilk are among the *loong* arousing foods (line 2112; Clark line 482, "fresh goat's milk").
- *Blood.* "Goat blood: cures venereal diseases, smallpox, [brown phlegm and leprosy]" (Clark line 1173, brackets being commentary interpolations per Clark's note at line 82); "Goat blood cures venereal diseases and smallpox" (Men-Tsee-Khang line 3539). The spine's list row is `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 358, "Goat blood", marked identification uncertain.
- *Brain.* "Goat brain: unites severed ligaments and tendons" (Clark line 1182; Men-Tsee-Khang line 3548, "heals torn ligaments and tendons"). List row 364.
- *Pubic hair of the billy-goat.* "ra.thug.rmongs.spus: Pubic hair of billy-goat cures infectious tissue degeneration" (Clark line 1195); "cures inflammation of muscle tissues" (Men-Tsee-Khang lines 3559-3560). The two translations render the disease differently and I did not check the Tibetan.
- *Group and names.* The goat is in the domestic-animals group (Clark line 672; Men-Tsee-Khang line 2917); "wild goat" is among the larger hill and game animals (Clark line 672). The Men-Tsee-Khang animal glossary prints "Goat — *Capra hircus* Linnaeus", "Himalayan Tahr — *Hemitragus jemlahicus*" and "Billy Goat" (lines 7094-7102); the spine's locus is that glossary.

**Other texts that treat the goat but are not on this entry's tag line.** Dioscorides (DMM, `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`): goat's milk "disturbs the intestines less" and "is also good for the stomach" (2-75, line 5234ff); goat milk as a source of butter (2-81, line 5266); goat suet prepared (2-88, line 5306); goat fat "more astringent ... given boiled with polenta, rhoe and cheese for dysentery" and "has the most dissolving nature and helps the gouty" (2-94, line 5344ff); blood of the he-goat and she-goat fried and eaten for dysentery (2-97, line 5363); and goat dung (2-98, line 5372). Sushruta (SS, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 614-627): goats and sheep belong to the group of domestic animals, whose flesh is "constructive, tonic and appetising ... sweet in taste and digestion", and "the flesh of the goat is moderately cooling in its potency". ### Active Components

The sources name none. In general: goat meat is muscle protein with fat; goat's milk fat is in small globules and contains medium-chain fatty acids; goat blood is a fluid tissue of cells in plasma; brain is rich in lipids; goat fat is mostly triglycerides. No quantities are given here because none were read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice beyond goat meat and milk as ordinary food. The Egyptian goat items are fat poultices and salves and a burn dressing of dung; the Tibetan ones are the flesh, milk, blood, brain and pubic hair, with actions from asthma to venereal disease to torn ligaments. The Codex has no protocol that depends on these uses. The reasons are specific: raw goat blood, brain and dung are infection routes; the Tibetan blood and brain uses concern venereal disease, smallpox and tendon injury, which are medical matters; the sources give no dose; and the goat's dung and burns dressing in Ebers is no guidance for a burn.

### Preparation & Dosing

Bryan gives goat's fat "beaten up with Onions and Wax" as a poultice, goat's fat in honey- or bean-based salves, and goat's dung burnt, crushed and rubbed in fermenting yeast. The Tibetan texts give actions only, with no method in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Goats are reservoirs of *Brucella melitensis* (brucellosis), *Coxiella burnetii* (Q fever), *Salmonella*, *Campylobacter* and *Listeria*; raw goat's milk and fresh cheese are a recognised route for brucellosis, and pasteurisation prevents it. Goat's milk is a source of protein, fat and calcium; it contains lactose and is not suitable for people with cow's-milk-protein allergy, as its proteins are similar. Raw blood, brain and dung can carry infection; goat brain also carries a theoretical risk of prion disease (scrapie occurs in goats). No effect of goat's fat, dung, blood, brain or hair on wounds, burns, venereal disease or ligament injury is established. No clinical studies of the traditional uses were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** None is printed; Bryan's translation gives the compounds by ingredient and complaint.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Goat meat is "heavy and cool" and activates all three *nyes pa* / *nyepa* (Clark: "raises the three humors"); goat's milk and its dairy products are "cool and light"; the butter of goat and cow "is cool" and pacifies hot disorder with *loong* (Men-Tsee-Khang). Goat blood, brain and pubic hair are given by action only, with no taste or potency assigned.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* Every goat item in Bryan is a remedy for a complaint: a wound to be dried, stiffness, a burn, baldness. None says what the goat does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); Bryan reports no recitation with the goat compounds, and since he summarises, that is not proof the papyrus gives none. No deity is attributed. Because Ebers identifications are modern hypotheses, the goat items are Bryan's English renderings, and the spine row (`_sources/ebers.csv` line 119) rests on a glossary not read. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The goat's flesh, milk, blood, brain and pubic hair are placed in the dietary and animal-medicine passages, with qualities for flesh and dairy and actions for the rest. Notably, goat meat and fresh goat's milk are placed among foods that arouse *loong* disorders, and are also foods for *tripa*; the tradition treats the same animal as a food to prescribe and a food to withhold according to the disorder. No essence-extraction (*bcud len*) use of any goat part is attested in the lines read, and no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition; none is asserted. Where the same chapter calls peacock flesh good "for rejuvenation" (Clark line 678), that is a claim for the peacock, not the goat.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do for cultivation. The Egyptian goat items are fat and dung for dressings and hair, and the Tibetan goat is a food to be chosen by the disorder: cooling and heavy meat and dairy for hot or *tripa* conditions, avoided in *loong*. What the Tibetan text offers a cultivator is that rule of fit, not a claim about vitality. Goat meat and pasteurised goat's milk are ordinary foods.

### Contraindications

- **Infection.** Raw goat's milk and fresh cheese can transmit brucellosis, Q fever, *Listeria* and *Salmonella*; use pasteurised or well-cooked products. Never apply dung or raw blood to wounds or burns, and do not eat raw brain.
- **Burns, wounds, venereal disease and smallpox.** These need medical care; the traditional uses are not guidance.
- **Allergy.** Goat's milk is cross-reactive with cow's milk allergy.
- **Tibetan dietary caution.** The text itself lists goat meat and fresh goat's milk among foods that aggravate *loong* disorders.
- **Dose unknown.** No dose is given in the sources read.
