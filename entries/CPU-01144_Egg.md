# Egg (yolk) — Oon (ΩΟΝ), "Eggs and their Yolk" (Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 2-54); white of egg, Leukon tou oou (2-55)
## *The egg and its yolk, as Dioscorides' chapter 2-54 is headed in the Osbaldeston English ("Eggs and their Yolk"); the edition prints no bird and no binomial for it* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The text on disk is the 2000 English edition of Dioscorides' *De Materia Medica* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`). The chapter is 2-54, headed "OON" with the English "Eggs and their Yolk" (line 5152; row 244 of `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, "2-54 (line 5152)"). It sits in Book Two, "living creatures and of honey and of milk and of animal fat", directly after the two chapters on parts of poultry (2-52 hens, 2-53 brains and broth of poultry) and before the grasshoppers (2-56). The edition prints no "SUGGESTED" identification for it, and the English does not say whose egg is meant; the nearby poultry chapters make a hen's egg the natural reading, but that is an inference, not a statement of the text. [USER: verify]

The whole of 2-54 reads (lines 5153-5154):

"A soft-cooked egg nourishes more than an uncooked one and a hard-cooked egg more than a soft. The yolk roasted with saffron and rosaceum [1-53] is good for sores in the eyes. It is good with melilot [3-48] for inflammation around the perineum and the joints. Fried in a pan with the seed of sumach or galls [oak galls] and eaten (or else given alone) it stops discharges of the bowels."

So the chapter gives one statement about food value (nourishment rises with cooking: raw, soft, hard) and three uses of the yolk: roasted with saffron and rosaceum for eye sores; with melilot for inflammation around the perineum and the joints; fried with sumach seed or oak galls, or alone, and eaten to stop bowel discharges. The bracketed glosses ("[oak galls]", the cross-references) are the edition's.

Beyond the chapter, eggs appear in Dioscorides as a vehicle or companion in other recipes I read: a thorn's gum "daubed on with an egg" so that burns do not break into pustules (line 4512); fig juice "applied with the yolk of an egg" to bring on the menses (line 4873); an edible bulb "alone or with the yolk of an egg" for bruises under the eyes (line 6303); opium with "a roasted egg yolk and saffron" for inflammation of the eyes (line 8586); sulphur "taken with an undercooked egg" for jaundice (line 10479). These belong to the chapters of those other substances and are only noted here.

### Varieties

**White of egg** (DMM; 2-55, "LEUKON TOU OOU", line 5155; names.csv row 245). This is the white as a separate chapter, headed "White of Egg" in the edition (lines 5156-5157). It says the white of an egg "used raw cools and closes the pores of the skin", soothes inflamed eyes when dropped in, prevents burns from breaking out into pustules, protects the face from sunburn, and "constipates those troubled with discharges". It is laid on the forehead with frankincense to repress discharges, applied with wool and with rosaceum, wine and honey for inflammation of the eyes, and, sipped raw, "helps bites of the snake called haemorrhois [a fable]"; warmed a little it is "good for bladder distress, ulcerated kidneys, rough arteries, and the throwing up of blood, mucus and fluids in the chest". The bracketed "[a fable]" is the edition's. The chapter gives no quantity.

*Synthesis of the varieties.* Dioscorides separates the egg by part: the yolk (2-54) and the white (2-55), each with its own chapter. The yolk chapter is the one that states food value and uses the yolk warmed (roasted, fried); the white chapter uses the white raw or only a little warmed, and states cooling and closing of the pores. Both chapters meet in the eye, which each treats, the yolk for "sores" and the white for "inflamed" eyes. The chapter on the yolk is the entry's parent; the white is folded under it. Neither chapter ranks one above the other.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, an egg yolk is rich in fat, protein, cholesterol, lecithin (phospholipids), fat-soluble vitamins and iron; the white is mostly water and protein (albumins). A raw egg may carry *Salmonella*, and raw white contains avidin, which binds the vitamin biotin until the egg is cooked. Nothing more specific can be said without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice as medicine. Dioscorides records the yolk for eye sores, for inflammation around the perineum and joints, and for bowel discharges; and the white for the eyes, burns, sunburn and discharges. The Codex recommends none of these as treatment: eye sores and inflamed eyes need examination, bowel discharge may be infection, and raw egg is a food-safety risk. The one point in the chapter that stands as ordinary food knowledge is its first sentence, that a cooked egg is more nourishing than a raw one. Eggs as a food are covered by ordinary nutrition, not by this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those quoted. For the yolk (2-54): eaten soft-cooked or hard-cooked; roasted with saffron and rosaceum for the eyes; with melilot; fried in a pan with sumach seed or oak galls, and eaten, or given alone. For the white (2-55): raw and dropped into the eyes; rubbed on burns afterwards; applied with wool and rosaceum, wine and honey; laid on the forehead with frankincense; sipped raw; or warmed a little. The chapters give no number of eggs, no quantity of the other ingredients, and no length of course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Egg is a protein-rich food, and cooking increases the digestibility of its protein and inactivates avidin; that is consistent with the chapter's first sentence about cooked eggs nourishing more. Raw or undercooked egg can transmit *Salmonella*. Egg white on a burn is not a treatment; burns are cooled with clean running water and assessed. Egg allergy is among the commonest food allergies in children. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM):** The yolk chapter 2-54 prints no quality (warming, cooling, drying) for the yolk; it gives only that a cooked egg "nourishes more" than a raw one. The white chapter 2-55 prints that the white raw "cools and closes the pores of the skin" and "constipates", which are the only qualities given for either part. Other chapters print qualities for other materials ("All fat is warming, softening and purifying", line 5344), but that statement is about fat and is not carried to the egg.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is supplied here. The two chapters are a statement about food value and a set of remedies for eyes, burns and discharges. They assign the egg no cultivation status, and no passage read connects it to *pneuma* or to any practice. The only discipline of attention in them is small: raw, soft or hard cooking matters, because they differ in how much the egg nourishes. He gives no provenance, season, assay or adulteration test for the egg, as he does for many plant and mineral materials.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator beyond what a cook already knows: a cooked egg feeds more than a raw one, on Dioscorides' own first sentence. The medical uses are a record and the Codex does not recommend them. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Raw or undercooked egg:** a risk of *Salmonella*; pregnant women, young children, older people and the immunocompromised should avoid it. The raw-white drink in 2-55 is not recommended.
- **Egg allergy:** a common food allergy; avoid in anyone who is allergic.
- **Eyes:** do not put raw egg, or anything unsterile, into inflamed or sore eyes; seek examination.
- **Burns:** cool with clean running water and seek assessment; do not apply egg white.
- **Bowel discharge:** persistent diarrhoea, particularly with blood or fever, needs medical assessment.
- **Ethics:** not an issue for the egg itself in ordinary food use; the Codex recommends no use of animals beyond food.
