# Argyreita speciosa — Vriddhadaraka, "Briddha daraka" (Sushruta Samhita, Appendix as scanned); the translator's gloss to Shyama in Sutrasthana ch.44

## *Argyreia speciosa (Burm.f.) Sweet, the elephant creeper, a large climbing plant of the morning-glory family; "Argyreita speciosa" is the Appendix's misprint of the genus name; the identification is Bhishagratna's, printed in his Appendix against a name the scan gives as "Briddha daraka", and is of medium confidence, since the pairing is read from a damaged two-column scan and the main text uses the name only as a gloss to Shyama* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, under the Sanskrit name Vriddhadaraka.** This is a single-text entry. The row was added on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the Appendix to Bhishagratna's Volume III; this entry replaces it. The attestation was confirmed on 30 October by opening the Appendix and then reading the chapters where the name occurs.

**Sushruta Samhita, Appendix (SS).** The Appendix to Bhishagratna's Volume III (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`) is a two-column glossary that the scan has split into a column of Sanskrit names and a column of identifications. The names column of the "B" page ends with "Brahmi" and "Briddha daraka" (line 31433); the identifications column of the same page ends with "Mimosa pudica", "Clerodendron Siphonanthus", "Siphonanthus Indica; Herpestis Mouniera" and "Argyreita speciosa." (line 31497). The last name on the page and the last identification line up, and the identification is the one the editor printed against the name. The page's other columns do not align cleanly, because the scan has dropped and merged lines. [USER: verify against the printed page.] The genus is printed "Argyreita" in the Appendix; it is a misprint for *Argyreia*, and the entry name keeps the printed spelling.

**Sushruta Samhita, main text (SS).** Bhishagratna uses the name inside the text as a translator's gloss to the drug Shyama. The glosses are in *Sutrasthana* ch.44, the chapter on the choice of purgatives (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch44.txt`):

- Line 161: "A compound consisting of equal parts of powdered Trivrit, Shyama (Vriddha-Daraka), Yavakshara, Shunti and Pippali, and taken with honey, acts as one of the most effective purgatives in diseases due to the action of the deranged Kapham."
- Lines 194-199: "A compound, consisting of equal parts of powdered Trivrit, Shyama (Vriddhadaraka), Pippali and Triphala and made into a confection (Modaka) (with the addition of honey and sugar), should be regarded as one of the most potent cures (purgatives) for Sannipata (simultaneous derangement of the three vital humours), haemoptysis and fever."
- Lines 220-225: "A purgative compound consisting of Shyama (Vriddhadaraka), Trivrit, Nili, Katvi, Musta, Duralabha, Chavya, Indrayava and Triphala, administered through the vehicle of clarified butter, essence of meat, or water, is commended to persons of dry temperament." The sentence before it, "the present remedy (purgative) is one of the most harmless purgative compounds (of our pharmacopoeia)", belongs to the compound ahead of it (Trivrit, Triphala, Yavakshara, Krishna and Vidanga), not to this one.

The name Shyama is itself a group-name in the text. In *Sutrasthana* ch.39, the list of purgative drugs begins "Trivrita, Shyama, Danti, Dravanti, Saptala, Shankhini..." (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` lines 22-24), with the rule that the roots of the plants that come before Tilvaka in the list are used for purging and the barks from Tilvaka to Patala. In ch.38 the *Shyamadi* group (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 204-212) is "Shyama, Maha-Shyama, Trivrit, Danti, Shankhini, Tilvaka, Kampillaka, Ramyaka, Kramuka, Putrashroni, Gavakshi, Rajavriksha, the two kinds of Karanja, Guduchi, Saptala, Chhagalantri, Sudha and Suvarnakhiri", said to cure abdominal glands and to act "as an anti-toxic". Those chapters name Shyama, not Vriddhadaraka, and the Appendix does not say in the lines read that the Shyama of the group is the plant the editor glosses as Vriddhadaraka. The present entry rests on the editor's gloss and on the Appendix pairing, and does not claim that every Shyama in the text is this plant. [USER: verify]

A search of the chapter files for the spellings "Vridha", "Briddha", "Vriddhadaraka" and "Argyreia" found no other hit. A search of the other texts on disk found nothing for this plant.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Argyreia* belongs to the morning-glory family (Convolvulaceae), whose seeds in several genera carry ergoline alkaloids, and the elephant creeper is one such plant. The text speaks of a purgative compound, and this entry draws no connection between them. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a gloss to Shyama, one ingredient of purgative powders and confections, given in diseases of deranged Kapham, in Sannipata, haemoptysis and fever, and to persons of dry temperament.
- **What the text does not say.** It names no use for Vriddhadaraka apart from Shyama, it describes no plant, and the compounds give proportions ("equal parts") without a dose.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are powders taken in honey (ch.44, line 161), a confection (Modaka) with honey and sugar (lines 194-199), and a powder given in ghee, meat essence or water (lines 220-225). The only quantity is the ratio "equal parts" for the first two. That is a proportion, not a dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Argyreia speciosa* is a perennial climber of the Indian subcontinent in the morning-glory family, and plants in that family are known to contain active alkaloids. The classic's purgative use is not assessed here. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* The name appears as a gloss in recipes for purgatives. It is not named in the tonic-remedy chapter read (Chikitsa ch.30, `chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch30.txt`), and no rasayana or vajikarana status is attested for it in the sources retrieved. No ojas, tejas or prana claim is made. The context is *virechana*, purging, which is a treatment procedure; *shodhana* in the spec's sense is the purification of a drug, which is not what these passages describe.

**Practitioner's note.** A purgative ingredient glossed in three recipes. The record does not support any cultivation use, and a cultivator has no business with it.

### Contraindications

- **Purgation.** These are purgative compounds used under the direction of a physician in disease. Do not take a purgative on the strength of this entry.
- **Seeds and alkaloids.** Plants of this family can contain active alkaloids; do not eat any part of the plant. [USER: verify]
