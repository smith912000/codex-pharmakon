# Pentapetes phoenicea — Bandhujiva, Bandhuka, Vandhujiva (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3)

## *Pentapetes phoenicea, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Pentapetes phoenicea." and "Pentapetas phoenicea.") against the Sanskrit names Bandhu-jiva and Bandhuka (lines 31448, 31449) and, on a later page, against Vandhujiva (OCR "Pentapetes Pboenica.", line 33247). The pairing of each Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The chapters name the drug only by Sanskrit name. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Tibetan text folders for *Pentapetes* found one hit that is not this plant: the Greek synonym "pentapetes" printed at the end of Dioscorides 4-42 *PENTAPHULLON* (line 8383, "It is also called pentapetes, pentatomon, pentadactylon ..."), the cinquefoil, which is a different plant and a coincidence of sound. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. Quotations keep the translator's words; the OCR's garbled diacritics are normalised. [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The names in the chapters.* The chapters print "Vandhujivaka" once (Sutra 39), "Vandhujiva" once (Kalpa 1), "Bandhujiva" once (Uttara 6), "Vandhuka" once (Sutra 6) and "Bandhuka" once (Kalpa 1); the appendix prints "Bandhuka" with a *B* and "Vandhujiva" with a *V*, so the *B*/*V* spellings are the translator's variants. A search of the chapter files found no other occurrence. The five passages are read below.

*The emetic list (Sutra 39, lines 9 to 19).* In the chapter on drugs of cleansing and soothing effect: "Emetic Drugs: The drugs known as Madana fruits, Kutaja, Jimutaka, Ikshvaku, Dhamagarba, Krita-vedhana, Sarshapa, Vidanga, Pippali, Karanjaka, Prapunnada, Kovidara, Korvudara, Arishta, Ashvagandha, Vidula, Vandhujivaka, Shveta, Shanapushpi, Vimvi, Vacha, Mrigervaru and Chitra, etc. are possessed of emetic properties." The text adds that "the fruits (seeds) of plants preceding Kovidara in the abovesaid list ... and the roots of plants from Kovidara to its close, should be used." Vandhujivaka is after Kovidara, so its part is the root.

*A plaster against poisoned cosmetics (Kalpa 1, lines 322 to 333).* In the chapter on preserving food and drink from poison, in a case of poisoning through cosmetics applied to the face, "the remedy in such a case consists in the application of a plaster composed of (white) sandal wood, clarified butter, Payasya, Yashti madhu, Phanji, (Bhargi), Vandhujiva and Punarnava. A potion of honey and clarified butter is also beneficial in this case."

*A collyrium remedy (Kalpa 1, lines 388 to 397).* In the case of a poisoned *Anjana* (collyrium), the patient is given a potion of fresh clarified butter alone or with pasted Pippali, and an *Anjana* "prepared with the expressed juice of Mesha-s'ringi, Varuna-bark, Mushkaka or Ajakama or with Samudra-phena, mixed with the bile of a cow, should be applied to the eyes, or the one prepared with the (expressed juice of the) flower of Kapittha, Mesha-s'ringi, Bhallataka, Bandhuka and Amkotha separately." The flower of each plant in the list is named, "separately".

*A colour simile (Uttara 6, lines 101 to 106).* In the chapter on the diseases of the eye as a whole, the symptoms of *Raktaja Adhimantha* include "a pricking pain in and a blood-streaked secretion from the affected organ which looks (bright red) like a Bandhujiva flower". The passage uses the flower only to describe a colour.

*The autumn flowers (Sutra 6, lines 368 to 376).* In the chapter on the seasons: in autumn "the level plains are covered with shrubs and undergrowths, and plants and trees such as, Vana, Saptahva, Vandhuka, Kasha and Asana, flower in abundance."

*What the chapters do not say.* The text gives no taste, potency or group for Vandhujiva, and does not say that the Vandhuka of Sutra 6 and the Bandhuka of Kalpa 1 are the same plant as Vandhujiva. The Codex treats the five spellings as one drug only on the appendix's pairing. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Pentapetes*, *Bandhujiva*, *Bandhuka* and *Vandhujiva* found no entry on this plant. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Pentapetes* is a genus of the mallow family (Malvaceae). Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Vandhujivaka as an emetic whose root is used, Vandhujiva in a plaster against poisoned cosmetics, Bandhuka flower juice in a collyrium remedy, and the red flower as a colour comparison. These are the text's statements about Sanskrit names, and the Latin name that the appendix attaches to them is the translator's, by position. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives these preparations, each with its locus. Sutra 39, lines 15 to 19: the root, as an emetic. Kalpa 1, lines 329 to 333: a plaster of the drug with sandal, ghee, Payasya, liquorice, Phanji and Punarnava, with a drink of honey and ghee. Kalpa 1, lines 393 to 397: the expressed juice of the flower, separately, as an *Anjana*. No dose, part (other than the root in Sutra 39 and the flower in Kalpa 1) or quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Sushruta terms (poisoned cosmetics, *Raktaja Adhimantha*, emetic properties) are not modern diagnoses, so no modern finding can be set beside them. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** the chapters read print for the drug only its place in the emetic list (Sutra 39) with the instruction that its root is the part used. No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for Vandhujiva or Bandhuka in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text names Vandhujivaka among emetic drugs, Vandhujiva in a plaster against poison, Bandhuka in a collyrium, and the flower as a seasonal and colour sign. A search of the chapter files found none of its names in the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) or the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30), so the text assigns it no rejuvenative or generative status in the lines retrieved. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The Latin identification is the appendix's. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. In the Sushruta scheme the name is an emetic root and an antidote-plaster ingredient given by a physician, and a flower that tells the season and a colour. Nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of a Latin name that the chapters do not give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read print no warning for the drug itself. The one oral use is as an emetic (Sutra 39), which is a physician's procedure and unsuitable for the weak, the pregnant and the very young without supervision. The uses against poison and in the eye describe emergencies that need medical care, not a household remedy. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry.
