# Dioscorea rhizome — 萆薢 *bi xie* (also 赤节 *chi jie*, "Red Joint")

## *Dioscorea hypoglauca* Palib. (the species Yang 1998 prints), fishpoison yam; the identification is medium-confidence, because the Shennong text prints the name in a truncated form (萆) and several yams have been sold as the drug* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** A search of the other traditions' texts in `Sources/` for *Dioscorea hypoglauca* found nothing; the Codex's other yam entries (I_038 black bryony, I_092, I_290 and I_562 Chinese yam, I_565 American wild yam) are different drugs and species. The entry rests on one tradition, in Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction and Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 translation. The row began on 13 October as an unverified genus-level placeholder, *Dioscorea* sp., from Yang's line; this entry replaces it.

**The name is cut in the file.** Sun's entry heading reads only 萆, and the 案 writes the second character as a blank (萆 followed by a gap). The Codex uses the full drug name 萆薢 (*bi xie*) for the heading, as the job directs, and treats the printed form as a transcription gap and not a different drug. Yang prints "Bei Xie" (line 2782), an apparent misspelling of *Bi Xie*. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), the Shennong text proper.** The entry sits in the middle classic (中经). Sun's 味 paragraph reads: "味苦，平。主腰背痛，强骨节，风寒湿、周痹，恶创不瘳，热气。生山谷。" ("Bitter, balanced. It governs pain in the lumbus and back; it strengthens the bones and joints; wind, cold and damp, generalized impediment [周痹], malign sores that do not heal, and heat qi. It grows in mountains and valleys.") The text proper gives no alias. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 萆[薢] *bi[xie]*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2165)

**Second witness, Yang 1998 (attributed to Yang).** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, lines 2782-2785) gives: "bitter and balanced. It mainly treats pain and rigidity of the upper and lower back and the joints, wind cold damp limb joint and generalized impediment, intractable malign sores, and heat qi. It grows in mountains and valleys." This agrees with Sun in substance. Yang's translation prints "limb joint" where Sun's text shows no limb clause, and "rigidity" where Sun shows 强骨节 "strengthen the bones and joints"; the Codex reports the difference and does not resolve it. Yang's note 163 (line 2795) adds, as later commentary, that the drug "is specifically capable of expelling wind dampness and normalizing urination… a good medicinal for generalized impediment and rigidity and pain of the back".

**Mingyi bielu layer (later additions, quoted by Sun).** Line 2167 reads: "一名赤节。生真定。八月采根，曝干。" ("Another name: Red Joint. It grows at Zhending. The root is gathered in the eighth month and dried in the sun.") The part used is the root.

**Sun's own note (案).** Line 2169 reads "《博物志》云：菝[…]，与萆[…]相乱" ("The *Bowuzhi* says: *ba* [菝 + a missing character] and *bi* [萆 + a missing character] are confused with each other"). The missing character after 菝 is read here as 葜, so the note says the drug is easily confused with the greenbrier-type plant 菝葜; the Codex notes that this is a reading and not what the file prints. Sun's note under 狗脊 (line 2059) gives a related statement: "《广雅》云：菝[…]，狗脊也… 《名医》别出菝[…]条，非" ("the *Guangya* says *ba…* is *gouji* [狗脊]… the *Mingyi* lists a separate *ba…* entry, wrongly"). The point of both is that early writers knew this root alongside two lookalikes, and already confused them.

**Identity.** The modern pharmacy accepts several *Dioscorea* species as 萆薢, among them *D. hypoglauca*, *D. tokoro* and *D. septemloba*; this is a general fact and not from the sources read. Yang chooses *D. hypoglauca*. The early text gives no description of the plant, so it cannot choose among them. The genus assignment is secure, the species is not. Confidence: medium. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. As a general fact, many yams of the genus *Dioscorea* contain steroidal saponins in their rhizomes (dioscin is the best-known example), and several are used as sources for steroid chemistry. This is a general statement and not drawn from the Codex's sources. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Shennong text lists a bitter, balanced drug for lumbar and joint pain, impediment of wind, cold and damp, and sores that do not heal. These are the text's terms; the Codex does not translate "generalized impediment" or "heat qi" into modern diagnoses and gives no protocol. Persistent back or joint pain and sores that do not heal need medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text proper gives no preparation. The Mingyi layer says the root is gathered in the eighth month and dried in the sun (line 2167). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Rhizomes of several *Dioscorea* species sold as *bi xie* are used in current Chinese pharmacy, which is a general fact and not a finding. Because the species used varies, a study of one would not bear directly on the Shennong drug. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ.** The Shennong text prints "味苦，平" (bitter, balanced). The Mingyi layer adds no flavour or temperature, and there is no Wu Pu layer for this entry. The text proper prints no toxicity word. Sun's appendix of drug relations (附：诸药制使, from line 4191; a later compilation, with Tang Shenwei's remark at line 4193) lists "萆 薏苡为使；畏葵根、大黄、柴胡、牡蛎、前胡" (line 4383; "[Coix seed] as its envoy; fears …"). It is not the Shennong text proper.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle classic (中经), whose preface (line 1677) says middle drugs "nourish the nature to correspond with man; non-toxic or toxic, weigh what is fit; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the thin [羸], take the middle classic". The entry has no 久服 (taken long) clause and no promise of lightening the body or lengthening life; "strengthens the bones and joints" (强骨节) is one action among the indications, not a status. The class is a frame; the drug carries no cultivation status of its own. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 萆[薢], `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2165)

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade drug for back and joint complaints, in the text's own frame. Nothing in the entry supports a long-term practice, and the species behind the name varies in practice. If anything is done, it is to read the entry for its indications and leave the rhizome where it grows.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. Several *Dioscorea* species contain active saponins, and wild-gathered tubers can be mistaken for edible yams or for other roots (the *Bowuzhi* note above records an early example of confusion), so identification matters. Persistent back pain and non-healing sores need medical assessment. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
