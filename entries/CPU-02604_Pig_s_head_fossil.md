**rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** In Clark's English *The Quintessence Tantras of Tibetan Medicine* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`) the materia medica chapter has a section headed "(2) The Powers of Stone Medicines" (line 816). In its run of stones, line 826 reads: "phag.mgo: Pig's Head Fossil: [bears the shape of a pig's head and is orange in color]. It heals bone disorders and drains lymph [accumulations]."

Reading of the line. In Clark's typography, the text in square brackets is added commentary, from the commentaries Clark follows, and the unbracketed sentence is the root text's statement of the power. So the colour and the shape ("bears the shape of a pig's head and is orange in color") are from the commentary and the power ("heals bone disorders and drains lymph [accumulations]") is the root text's. The neighbours show the setting. The next line (827) is the "Sparrow's Head Fossil", described as spiriferoides (and identified by Clark as *Cyrtiospirifer sinensis*), which "promote flesh growth". The stones before it include "man(y)jira", glossed fossil muscovite or ophicalcite, which cure bone fevers. The pig's head fossil is thus one of a pair of fossils named for animal heads.
