# Tudri — تودري (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *Tūdarī: a herb that Avicenna describes after Dioscorides, with leaves like those of *farāsiyūn* (horehound-type, our gloss), a square root, and black oblong seed in calyx-heads. The plant is not identified; the description is confirmed, the identification is not (confidence low)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *tūdarī* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 18460-18472).

*What it is.* الماهية: قال ديسقوريدوس: عشبة شبيهة الورق بورق الفراسيون مربع الجذر (18461): "Dioscorides said: a herb whose leaf resembles the leaf of *farāsiyūn*, with a square root". Avicenna names Dioscorides for this description only; the later clauses are not attributed. The root is "about half a cubit" and has calyxes (*aqmāʿ*) holding an oblong black seed, "and this is what is used of the *tūdarī*"; the wild kind has a rolling (*mudaḥraj*) seed (18462-18463).

*Temperament and actions.* الطبع: حار في الثانية رطب في الأولى (18463): "hot in the second, moist in the first". الخواص: له حرافة كحرافة الحرف وفيه تقريج (18464): "it has a pungency like that of *ḥirf* (garden cress, our gloss) and in it is *taqrīj*". The sense of *taqrīj* is not settled here. [USER: verify]

*Swellings.* ينفع من السرطانات التي ليست بمتقرحة طلاء بماء وعسل (18465): "it benefits the non-ulcerated cancers (*saraṭānāt*) as a paint with water and honey"; it benefits all hard swellings and is applied as a poultice to inflammation (18465-18466). *Joints and nerves:* a poultice for the hardness of gout (18468). *Head:* it benefits swellings at the roots of the ears (18468-18469). *Eye:* with honey as a salve it cleans eye ulcers (18469). *Chest:* in linctuses it helps the spitting out of humours, after being soaked, boiled in water, put in a pouch, coated in dough and roasted (18470-18471). *Excretory organs:* it benefits in *al-bāh* (sexual vigour), especially when cooked in wine (18471-18472).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The Canon records a hot, pungent seed used mainly as a paste or salve for hard swellings, gout, ear swellings and eye ulcers. The plant cannot be identified from the description. The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this material.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only what the passage gives: a paint with water and honey for swellings; a salve with honey for the eye; a soaked, boiled, pouched and dough-roasted form for linctuses; a form cooked in wine (lines 18465-18472). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
