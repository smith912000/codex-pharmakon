# Patala — पाटला (Pāṭalā), Amoghā, Kāleru, Padri, Trumpet-flower

## *Stereospermum suaveolens* (Roxb.) DC. (≡ *Stereospermum chelonoides* (L.f.) DC.) | Family: Bignoniaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SS
**Tags (previous line, retained for reference):** Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS — Daśamūla) · Caraka Saṃhitā (CS) · Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya · Bhāvaprakāśa Nighaṇṭu

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta — one of the Great Ten Roots.** Pāṭalā's supreme distinction is its membership in the **Daśamūla** — the "ten roots," Ayurveda's foundational anti-vāta and restorative root-complex, split into the five great roots (Bṛhat Pañcamūla) and five lesser (Laghu/Kaṇṭaka Pañcamūla). Pāṭalā is one of the *Bṛhat Pañcamūla* — the five great roots (with Bilva, Agnimantha, Śyonāka and Gambhārī) whose decoction, *Daśamūla-kvātha*, is arguably the single most-prescribed grounding, vāta-pacifying, anti-inflammatory and convalescent formula in the entire classical corpus. It is the backbone of post-partum care, of *jvara* (fever) management, of respiratory disease, and of the depleted, wind-disordered, inflamed states that follow illness and childbirth. To be in the Daśamūla is to be certified by the tradition as a *foundational* medicine — not a specialist's tool but a load-bearing pillar.

**Individual profile.** Beyond the Daśamūla, Suśruta and the nighaṇṭus give Pāṭalā's root and bark their own indications: *jvarahara* (antipyretic), *śvāsa-kāsahara* (anti-asthmatic/anti-cough), *śothahara* (anti-inflammatory), *dāhaprāśamana* (relieving burning sensation), a corrective in *raktapitta* (bleeding disorders) and *hikkā* (hiccough), and a *dīpana* digestive. Its fragrant trumpet flowers (whence *suaveolens*, "sweet-smelling") are themselves used as a cooling, cardiac-soothing hṛdya and in eye and thirst complaints. The plant thus works across the vāta-kapha axis with a cooling-yet-kindling balance rare enough to earn tridoṣa-hara status.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Ground Beneath the Ten:** If Madana is the emptier and Pāṭhā the flow-governor, Pāṭalā is the **ground** — the root among the roots. The Daśamūla is Ayurveda's image of *foundation*: ten roots bound into one decoction, no single one sovereign, the strength emerging from the collective grip on the earth. Pāṭalā's contribution is stability against wind (*vāta*) — the doṣa of dryness, depletion, anxiety, and dispersal. Symbolically it belongs to the pharmaka of *return and re-grounding*: what is given after the storm, after the birth, after the fever, when the body must be re-anchored. Its sweet-scented trumpet flowers, opening downward, are a fitting emblem — beauty and fragrance rooted in a deep, cooling, load-bearing root. In Kenetica it is filed as a **foundation-tier restorative**: the substance you build *on*, not the one you strike *with*.

### Active Components

- **Lignans** — cycloolivil (isolated from the roots), and related dibenzylbutyrolactone lignans; associated antioxidant/neuroprotective activity.
- **Naphthoquinones** — lapachol, dehydro-α-lapachone, sterospermone and related quinones (Bignoniaceae-characteristic) with anti-inflammatory/antimicrobial and antioxidant properties.
- **Iridoid glycosides** and phenolic acids.
- **Sterols** (β-sitosterol), triterpenes; aromatic constituents in the fragrant flowers.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Patala is filed as a **foundation-tier grounding restorative** — the anti-vāta, anti-inflammatory, convalescent pillar, deployed chiefly *as part of* the Daśamūla complex. Its Kenetica role is stabilisation and re-grounding: pacifying dispersed, dry, anxious, depleted vāta; cooling and settling inflammation and fever without over-cooling digestion; supporting the respiratory and post-illness/post-partum recovery arc. Indicated in the depleted, wind-disordered, low-grade-inflamed, convalescent picture — the aftermath state. It is a *building/settling* agent, the counter-pole to the shock-purifiers of this volume, and is characteristically used in sustained, gentle courses rather than sharp interventions.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, preclinical (rodent/in-vitro) studies support several traditional claims; no human trials:

- Balasubramanian T et al., 2010, *Pharmaceutical Biology* — an ethanol extract of *Stereospermum suaveolens* showed significant anti-inflammatory effect in rats, corroborating the classical *śothahara* use. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3109/13880200903127383)
- Shalavadi MH et al., 2012, *Indian Journal of Pharmacology* — *Stereospermum suaveolens* DC. demonstrated neuroprotective activity in a 6-OHDA-induced Parkinson's disease model in rats. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0253-7613.103275)
- Wahab Sab BA et al., 2015, *Pharmacognosy Research* — isolation and characterisation of the lignan **cycloolivil** from the roots of *Stereospermum suaveolens*, identifying an antioxidant constituent underlying the plant's activity. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-8490.147198)

Together these give a mechanistic (anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, neuroprotective) basis consistent with the grounding/anti-vāta and śothahara reputation. **Evidence grade: LOW** — animal and phytochemical studies only, no clinical data; the plant is used clinically almost entirely within the multi-herb Daśamūla, which has not been deconvoluted in trials. Attribution: PubMed.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Tikta (bitter), Kaṣāya (astringent), Madhura (sweet — root/flower)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry) — mild; some texts note Snigdha tendency in the sweet fraction
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (mildly heating) — regarded by some as anuṣṇa/near-neutral, hence tridoṣa suitability
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** *Vāta-śamana within the Daśamūla synergy* — the grounding, anti-inflammatory anchoring effect for which the root is prized as one of the five great roots.
- **Doṣa action:** ↓ Vāta (chief), ↓ Kapha, ↓ Pitta (via cooling bitter/astringent and dāha-praśamana) — classically **tridoṣa-śāmaka**, leaning vāta-kapha-hara.
- **Karma (actions):** Vāta-śamana/Balya (grounding-strengthening), Jvarahara (antipyretic), Śvāsa-kāsahara (anti-asthmatic/anti-cough), Śothahara (anti-inflammatory), Dāhaprāśamana (relieving burning), Raktapitta-hara, Hikkā-hara (anti-hiccough), Dīpana (kindling), Śukrala (in some texts).
- **Suśruta gaṇa membership:** One of the **Bṛhat Pañcamūla** (five great roots) constituting, with the lesser five, the **Daśamūla** — its cardinal classificatory home in the Suśruta and wider Ayurvedic corpus.

### Contraindications

Generally well-tolerated and mild (its Daśamūla membership reflects a broad safety profile), but: pregnancy and lactation — use only within recognised classical formulae under supervision. Naphthoquinone (lapachol-type) content warrants caution in high/prolonged single-drug doses and possibly in pregnancy and with anticoagulants (quinone chemistry) — `[USER: verify]`. Not primarily indicated for high-Pitta acute inflammatory heat as a solo heating drug; its balance is best expressed in compound. As with all Daśamūla work, match to a genuinely depleted/vāta-disordered picture, not to robust excess states.
