# Virataru — Virataru (as printed in Bhishagratna's Sushruta Samhita)

## *Virataru, the first-named drug of the Viratarva'di group of Sutrasthana ch.38; the translator prints no gloss, no botanical name is given in any source read, and the identification is unresolved (confidence low)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Virataru [SS]", taken from an extraction of Bhishagratna's translation; this entry replaces it. The locus given was "SS Sutra 38, Viratarva'di group (Vol. I line 15400)".

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Virataru is printed in the Sutrasthana, chapter 38 (locus: Viratarva'di group, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 59; the same text is in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, line 15400). Chapter 38 is "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3-6), and says that "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (lines 8-10). The name is given by the translator without a gloss, a botanical name or a description.

In the Viratarva'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 57-64 (the list), lines 65-72 (the metrical text)), Virataru is one of the drugs listed. The group's metrical text reads: "The group subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and ... proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles." (the sentence runs across a page break after "Vata and") That is a property of the group as a whole, not of Virataru alone.

**Other places the name appears.** Sutrasthana ch.39 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt`, line 89), in the Vata-Samshamana-Varga: "... Kuverakshi, Virataru, Sahachara, Agnimantha ...". Chikitsasthana ch.15 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch15.txt`, lines 302-304): after the account of the Vala-Kalpa oil, whose claims are that it improves "strength and complexion" and "adds a century (of years) to the duration of one's life" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch15.txt` lines 295-297), the text says: "Oils may similarly be prepared with each of Ativisha, Guduchi, Aditya-parni, Saireyaka, Virataru, S'atavari, Tri-kantaka, Madhuka and Prasarani, and may be prescribed by an experienced and erudite physician." Uttarasthana ch.40 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch40.txt`, lines 233-234): the twelfth Amatisara recipe is "Brikshadani, Virataru, the two kinds of Vrihati and the two kinds of Saha (Mudga-parni and Masha-parni)".

The chapter closes (lines 554-566) by saying that the groups "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", and that plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, Ghritas or potions are to be prepared "according to the exigencies of each individual case". That is a general rule of the chapter and is not a recipe for this drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The text uses it in the Viratarva'di group (said to subdue Vata disorders and help stone, gravel and urinary troubles), in the Vata-soothing list of ch.39, as one of the oils "similarly" prepared in Chikitsa ch.15, and in the twelfth Amatisara recipe of Uttara ch.40.
- What the text does not say: what Virataru is, which part is used, or its own dose.

The Codex does not translate the text's terms (Vayu, Pitta, Kapham, Gulma, Shosha, Asmari and the rest) into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend a plant it cannot identify.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation printed for Virataru alone is the medicated oil of Chikitsa ch.15: an oil "prepared with" Virataru in the same manner as the Vala-Kalpa oil (sesamum seeds soaked repeatedly in a decoction of the root, the pressed oil cooked a hundred times with the decoction; lines 274-292 of that file). Whether the Virataru oil follows exactly that method is not stated beyond "similarly". The Vala-Kalpa dose, "A Drona measure of the oil", is that oil's and is not a dose for Virataru. ### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Virataru stands in the dravya-group classification of Sutrasthana ch.38, a classification by therapeutic action. The one place where a longevity claim stands near the name is Chikitsasthana ch.15, and the claim there belongs to the Vala-Kalpa oil; the text says only that oils may "similarly" be prepared with Virataru among nine other drugs. That is a frame, and it is not an attribution to Virataru. The chapter is not one of the rasayana chapters and does not call the oils rasayana. The register is therefore (b): framed by its therapeutic group, with no cultivation status attested. The Codex does not assign *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* effects to it, because no source read does.

**Practitioner's note.** Virataru is a name in a list. The tradition's text gives the drug a place among therapeutic groups and little more, and nothing in the record supports a cultivation use. A cultivator can learn from it only how Sushruta organises drugs into groups by action, and that some names in that classification are no longer certainly identified. Nobody should try to find a plant to match the name.
