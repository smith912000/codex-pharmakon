# Vrishadansha flesh — Vrishadansha (as printed in Bhishagratna's English, Sutrasthana ch.46)
## *The flesh of an animal that Sushruta's meat chapter lists by the Sanskrit name Vrishadansha among the hole-dwellers (Vileshaya). The translation gives no English gloss for the name, and the list row marks the identification uncertain, so what the animal is cannot be said from the sources read* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The text is Bhishagratna's 1907 English *Sushruta Samhita*, Sutrasthana ch.46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink (Annapana-Vidhi-madhyayam)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`). The list row is `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 221, "Vrishadansha", with no botanical or English column filled, the source "Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", and the identification flag set to "yes" (uncertain). The row was matched to the ch.46 text by the name alone. The chapter opens (lines 9-12) with the claim that food "determines the origin of beings and forms the chief source of their bodily strength and complexion, as well as of the albuminous (Ojas) principle in their organisms". That is said of food in general and not of any one meat.

The meat section begins at line 385: "Now I shall describe the properties of the different species of edible meats". The high-ground (Janghala) animals are divided into eight sub-species (lines 403-410), of which the Vileshaya is the seventh. Lines 574-578: "The Vileshaya Group :— Animals such as, the Shvavit, Shalyaka, Godha, Shasha, Vrishadansha, Lopaka, Lomasha-Karna, Kadali, Mriga-Priyaka, Ajagara, Sarpa, Mushika, Nakula and Maha-Vabhru belong to the group of Vileshaya (hole-dwellers)." The translator glosses only the group, as "hole-dwellers". The animals in the list, other than the ones that have a separate sentence below, are not identified in the passage.

The group's properties follow (lines 580-587): "The general properties of the flesh of animals belonging to this species are to increase the consistency of stool and urine. They are heat-making in potency, sweet in digestion as those of the preceding group. They subdue the Vayu and generate the Pittam and Kapham. They are demulcent and beneficial in cough, dyspnoea and cachexia." After this the text gives individual sentences for the Shasha (lines 587-590), the Godha, the Shalyaka, the Mriga-Priyaka, the Ajagara and the Sarpa (lines 590-612). There is none for the Vrishadansha, so the group statement is all that the chapter says of its flesh. The Shasha, named beside it in the list, has its own entry (Shasha flesh).

I searched all the chapter files and the three Bhishagratna volume files for the name and for close spellings ("Vrishadamsha", "Vrishadans"), and the only occurrence is the list at line 575 (and its copy in the volume file). The name is not in a recipe, a diet or a list of drugs.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** From *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 580-587), for the flesh of the Vileshaya group to which the Vrishadansha belongs: potency (*virya*), heat-making; taste after digestion (*vipaka*), sweet "as those of the preceding group"; it increases the consistency of stool and urine, subdues *vayu*, generates *pittam* and *kapham*, and is demulcent and beneficial in cough, dyspnoea and cachexia. The passage prints no taste (*rasa*) for the group, and no individual statement for the Vrishadansha.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The flesh is named in a food-and-drug chapter (*Sutrasthana* ch.46), which opens by saying that food is the chief source of strength, complexion and *ojas* in beings (lines 9-12). That is a statement about food in general. The name occurs nowhere else in the text, so it is in no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter. The Vileshaya group's text never uses the word "spermatopoietic". No passage read says that this flesh builds *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The sattvic and rajasic food lists are *Samkhya*-era material and not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The text names the Vrishadansha once, in a list, and gives only the properties of its group. The Codex does not recommend the meat. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** the animal is not identified in the sources read; do not substitute any animal.
- **Hygiene and infection:** meat from wild burrowing animals can carry bacteria, parasites and viruses.
- **Ayurvedic caution:** the text ranks the group's flesh as heat-making and as generating *pittam* and *kapham*; it names no one who should avoid it.
- **Legal and ethics:** hunting and trade in wild animals are regulated, the status of this animal is unknown, and the Codex does not recommend taking an animal's life for these uses.
