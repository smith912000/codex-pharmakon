# Herba Ligustici Wallichii — Mi Wu (Yang 1998); in the classic 蘼芜 *mi wu*, alias 薇芜 *Wei Wu* "Fine Weed"

## *The herbage (苗, seedling or foliage) of the plant whose rhizome the classic calls 芎䓖 (Xiong Qiong); Yang 1998 prints "Herba Ligustici Wallichii", Ligusticum wallichii Franch., and the Mingyi layer of Sun's text states that 蘼芜 is "the 芎䓖 seedling", so the identification is textual and of medium confidence, not independent* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry: named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The row was filed from Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 English translation, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, "Herbs: Middle Class, PDF p.63 (line 2398)". The principal text for the Chinese is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; Yang is read as a second witness and is attributed to Yang.

**Probable relation to an existing entry.** The Codex's Chuan Xiong entry (`I_067_Chuan_Xiong_Ligusticum_chuanxiong.md`, tagged CM, SBJ, SS) covers the rhizome drug, which Yang prints directly above this one as "Xiong Qiong (Radix Ligustici Wallichii)", line 2393. This entry is the herbage monograph of the same plant in the classic and may be merged with that entry by the editor. [USER: verify whether to merge.]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun's entry is 蘼芜 (line 687), and it stands in the upper classic (卷一 上经, line 697; in the upper-class list at line 45). Line 689 reads 味辛，温。主咳逆，定惊气，辟邪恶，除蛊毒鬼注，去三虫，久服通神。一名薇芜。生川泽 ("acrid, warm; governs cough and counterflow, settles fright qi, wards off evil and malignancy, removes gu toxin and 鬼注, expels the three kinds of worms. Taken long, it 通神. One name 薇芜. Grows in rivers and marshes").

The Wu Pu layer (line 691) gives the variant name 麋芜 with the alias 芎䓖. The Mingyi layer (line 693) reads 一名江蓠，芎䓖苗也，生雍州及冤句，四月、五月采叶，曝干 ("one name 江蓠; it is the seedling of 芎䓖; grows in Yong Province and Yuanju; the leaf is gathered in the fourth and fifth months and dried in the sun"). That layer is the source of the identification with Xiong Qiong, and the leaf is the part named. Sun's note (line 695) collects philological citations for the name and is not read further here.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 2398): "Mi Wu (Herba Ligustici Wallichii) is acrid and warm. It mainly treats cough and counterflow, settles fright qi, keeps off evils and malignancy, eliminates gu toxins and demonic influx, and removes the three [kinds of] worms. Protracted taking may enable one to communicate with spirits. Its other name is Wei Wu (Fine Weed). It grows in rivers and swamps." Yang places it in the middle class (p.63, under the heading "Herbs: Middle Class"), while Sun places it in the upper classic. The grade differs between the two editions; Sun's decides under the project rule.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Cough and counterflow, fright qi, warding off evil and malignancy, gu toxin and "demonic influx", and the three worms; on long taking it "communicates with spirits" (Yang) or 通神 (Sun).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation beyond drying the leaf, no duration.

The Codex does not translate these terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and recommends no use.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given in the classic. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest: the leaf, gathered in the fourth and fifth months and dried in the sun (line 693). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The genus *Ligusticum* is in the carrot family, and species of the genus are aromatic perennial herbs. The classic's "gu toxin", "demonic influx" and "fright qi" have no modern equivalents and are not translated into any. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* Sun's reconstruction places 蘼芜 in the upper classic (list at line 45; entry under 卷一 上经, line 697), whose preface (line 43) reads 上药…主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经: the upper drugs are "mainly nourishing life to correspond with heaven, non-toxic; taken in quantity or long they do not harm". The drug's own paragraph also carries 久服通神 ("taken long, it communicates with the spirits"), which is the 通神 promise in the project's grading rule (Spec, SBJ grading rule of 2026-10-30). Yang places it in the middle class, so the two editions differ; under the rule Sun's grade decides and the difference is stated here. This (a) is a text-internal grade and does not make a claim about what the plant does. Neidan is not a bencao category and is not claimed here.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade drug in Sun's reconstruction, with a long-taking promise that is spirit-related and not a bodily one, and a middle-grade drug in Yang's edition. The record gives no dose, no preparation and no duration, and nothing here supports acting on it. A cultivator reads it as a record of grading.
