### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta & the classical Ayurvedic corpus.** Mūrvā is one of the older *dravyas* of the Sanskrit materia medica, named repeatedly across the *Suśruta Saṃhitā* and *Caraka Saṃhitā*. It appears as a component of important therapeutic clusters — classically grouped among the bitter, deobstruent, *prameha*- (urinary-disorder / diabetes-spectrum) and *jvara*- (fever) relevant herbs — and its fibrous stem yielded the celebrated **maurvī**, the bowstring of the *Dhanurveda* tradition, so that the plant sits at the intersection of medicine, ritual and martial culture. The name **Mūrvā** is itself the root of *maurvī* ("that which is made of mūrvā"), and the sacred thread (*yajñopavīta*) of the Kṣatriya was in some prescriptions to be strung from mūrvā fibre — a detail that fixes the plant firmly in the Vedic ritual imagination.

**The source-plant controversy — flagged honestly.** Mūrvā is one of the most *vivādāspada* (disputed) drugs in Ayurvedic pharmacognosy. The classical texts describe a strong-fibred climbing plant, but no single modern botanical species maps cleanly onto every classical description, and regional *vaidyas* have supplied "Mūrvā" from **several different plants**: *Marsdenia tenacissima* (the identification adopted for this entry, and the one with the strongest fibre-tradition and Himalayan-to-Deccan distribution match); *Sansevieria roxburghiana* (bowstring hemp — supplied as Mūrvā across much of South India and Bengal, and arguably the dominant market substitute); *Chonemorpha fragrans / macrophylla*; and *Clematis* and *Helicteres* species in various local traditions. This is not a failure to identify a plant so much as a genuine **poly-botanical drug-name** — "Mūrvā" behaved historically as a functional-and-fibrous category. The reader should treat any single Latin binomial for Mūrvā as *provisional*, and any clinical or energetic claim as belonging to the classical drug-concept unless a specific chemotype is named. This entry leads on *M. tenacissima* while marking the ambiguity explicitly.

**TCM and Yunnan folk tradition.** Independently of the Sanskrit lineage, *M. tenacissima* is a well-established drug of southern China, known in the Dai ethnomedicine of Yunnan and formalised in modern Chinese oncology as **Xiao'aiping (消癌平)** — a stem extract used adjunctively in cancer care — and historically for "heat-clearing and detoxifying," cough and asthma. That this same species independently attracted respiratory and deobstruent uses in two unconnected traditions is one of the quiet cross-tradition convergences the Codex exists to record.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Bowstring and the Bitter Thread:** Mūrvā is the plant of *tension held to purpose*. Its fibre becomes the bowstring — the line drawn taut between two points, storing potential, releasing it as directed force — and its taste is the bitter that scrapes, clears and disillusions. In the symbolic grammar of Kenetica this is the same gesture in two registers: the *string* is discipline made physical (a boundary under load that does not snap), and the *bitter rasa* is discipline made metabolic (the palate that has stopped chasing sweetness). The mūrvā-fibre sacred thread worn across the body marks the initiate who has bound himself to a vow; the bitter decoction taken into the body marks the practitioner who has bound his appetites. Mūrvā therefore reads, in the doctrinal layer, as **the substance of committed restraint** — that which draws the loose into line and holds it there without fraying.

### Active Components

Attribution of chemistry must track the source-plant caveat above. For *Marsdenia tenacissima* specifically, the characterised constituents are a family of **C21 pregnane (polyoxypregnane) glycosides** — the **tenacissosides** (notably Tenacissoside G and H) and related **marsdenosides / tenacigenins** — together with steroidal aglycones. These pregnane glycosides are the compounds carrying the anticancer and anti-inflammatory signals in the modern literature. For Mūrvā supplied as *Sansevieria roxburghiana*, the relevant chemistry is instead steroidal saponins and the fibre itself — a reminder that "Mūrvā's actives" are not a single well-defined set.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Within the Kenetica training frame Murva is a **bitter-deobstruent grounding agent** — deployed where the practitioner is congested, heavy, damp (Kapha-laden) or metabolically sluggish and needs the channels *scraped* rather than fed. Its classical *prameha* and *jvara* associations place it in the purification and bitter-tonic conditioning phases, not in restorative or rasāyana work. In the symbolic training arc it is the herb of the *taut line*: taken during periods where the discipline is slackening, it corresponds to the re-tensioning of a resolve. It is explicitly **not** a beginner's daily tonic — its bitterness, dryness and heat make it a corrective, used in defined courses.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the modern evidence base concerns *Marsdenia tenacissima* pregnane glycosides, and is **preclinical (in-vitro and animal)** — there is no basis here for human clinical claims:

- **Tenacissoside H** repressed glioblastoma progression in vitro and in an orthotopic model by inhibiting the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway (Dong et al., 2024, *European Journal of Pharmacology*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejphar.2024.176401).
- **Tenacissoside G** synergistically potentiated 5-fluorouracil against human colorectal cancer cells and in a xenograft model, via p53-mediated apoptosis (Wang et al., 2021, *Phytomedicine*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phymed.2021.153553).
- **Tenacissoside H** showed anti-inflammatory activity through NF-κB and p38 pathway modulation in zebrafish models (Li et al., 2018, *Fish & Shellfish Immunology*) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fsi.2018.09.032).

**Evidence grade: LOW / preclinical.** The anticancer and anti-inflammatory signals are mechanistically coherent and reproducible across labs, but confined to cell and animal models; the classical *Ayurvedic* Mūrvā indications (prameha, jvara, deobstruent) are **not** the endpoints these studies tested, and the source-plant ambiguity means this chemistry may not describe the Mūrvā a given practitioner actually holds. Science attributed to PubMed.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Tikta (bitter), Kaṣāya (astringent) — the scraping, drying pair.
- **Guṇa (quality):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry).
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating).
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent).
- **Prabhāva (specific effect):** Deobstruent / channel-clearing action attributed classically to the plant beyond what rasa-guṇa-vīrya predict; the fibre's *tension-bearing* nature is read symbolically as its prabhāva.
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha-Pitta śāmaka (pacifies Kapha and Pitta through bitterness and dryness); may aggravate Vāta if overused owing to its light-dry-heating profile.
- **Karma (actions):** Dīpana (kindling), Chedana / Lekhana (scraping, reducing), Jvaraghna (antipyretic), Pramehaghna (anti-prameha), Kṛmighna and Viṣaghna in some sources.
- **Gaṇa membership:** Classically enumerated among bitter/deobstruent and prameha-relevant groupings in the Sanskrit corpus; exact *gaṇa* placement varies by recension and, as with all Mūrvā data, is entangled with the source-plant question.

### Contraindications

- Source-plant identity uncertain — do not dose without confirming the actual botanical supplied.
- Heating, drying and light qualities make it unsuitable in Vāta excess, dryness disorders, and (as a general caution for bitter-deobstruent drugs) in pregnancy without expert supervision.
- The *M. tenacissima* anticancer chemistry is investigational; nothing here supports self-treatment of cancer, and the extract interacts with the context of chemotherapy — a clinical, not self-care, setting.
