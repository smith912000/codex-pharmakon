# Kashamarda — Kashamarda (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907); Kasamardaka (Sutra 46)

## *Kashamarda, in the Sushruta Samhita's Surasadi group; identified by us as Senna occidentalis (L.) Link (Cassia occidentalis, coffee senna) from the standard modern Ayurvedic identification of kasamarda; the translation gives no botanical name; confidence medium. It is not Cassia tora, the species of CPU-00165* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** This entry is identified by us as *Senna occidentalis*, a plant whose seeds are generally regarded as poisonous when eaten in quantity. The entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take this plant on its strength.

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk (Bhishagratna's 1907 English translation).** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from a name list (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, which records "group list (ch.38); no identification given here"). This entry replaces it. The text was opened at the line given and the neighbouring lines read. Searches of the other texts on disk (Dioscorides, Shennong, rGyud-bzhi, Ebers) were not made under a Sanskrit spelling, so absence elsewhere is not claimed.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Kashamarda is printed in the Surasa'di group, Sutrasthana chapter 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15461 to 15463 (the name is on line 15462). The translator's sentence reads: "The drugs known as Surasa, white Surasa, Fainjjhaka, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika form the group known as the Surasadi Gana." The metrical text (lines 15470 to 15474) reads: "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough."

**A second spelling, in Sutra 46.** In the chapter on the qualities of food and drugs the translation prints "The herb called the Kasamardaka has a bitter and sweet taste. It subdues the Vayu and Kapham, is a digestant, cleanses the throat and specially subdues the Pittam" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 21163 to 21166; also `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 1503). The two spellings are very probably one herb, since Sutra 46 treats a neighbouring herb of the Surasadi list (Surasa, Arjaka, Bhustrina) just before it, but the translation does not say so. **Identification.** The text gives no botanical name. Kasamarda is conventionally *Senna occidentalis* (formerly *Cassia occidentalis*). That is our identification from the Sanskrit name, not from the translation. It is distinct from *Cassia tora*, which the spine holds as CPU-00165 (Shennong and rGyud-bzhi), so this row is not a duplicate of that entry.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Senna occidentalis* belongs to the legume family (Fabaceae), and species of *Senna* characteristically contain anthraquinone glycosides, which are laxative. Its seeds are reported in the toxicology literature to be poisonous to livestock when eaten in quantity. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Senna occidentalis* is a weedy legume of the tropics. Related *Senna* species are used as stimulant laxatives, and the seeds of this species are generally regarded as toxic if eaten in quantity. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS, Sutra 46 (Bhishagratna 1907).** For *Kasamardaka*: taste "bitter and sweet"; it "subdues the Vayu and Kapham", is a digestant, cleanses the throat and "specially subdues the Pittam" (lines 21163 to 21166). This is printed for the Sutra 46 spelling; its identity with the Surasadi *Kashamarda* is probable but not stated.
- **SS, Sutra 38.** The group's properties are given above (acts on *Kapham*, vermifuge, aseptic).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is named inside a therapeutic drug group (*gana*) of Sutrasthana chapter 38, the Surasa'di group. The group's metrical text speaks of curing or subduing disorders. It does not call the group, or any member, a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) drug, and it does not mention *ojas*. The sources retrieved therefore attest a therapeutic framing only, and no cultivation status. The *sattva/rajas/tamas* lists of later popular literature are not from Sushruta and are not applied. The Sutra 46 passage for *Kasamardaka* is likewise a statement of taste and of the humours subdued, with no cultivation category.

**Practitioner's note.** A bitter-sweet herb used, with its group, against *Kapham* disorders of the chest and a poor appetite. Nothing in the record supports a cultivation use, and the plant's seeds are generally regarded as poisonous. A cultivator does nothing with this entry.

### Contraindications

- **Seeds and raw plant.** Generally regarded as toxic if eaten in quantity. Do not ingest any part of a plant gathered or bought under this name. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. *Senna* laxatives are generally avoided in pregnancy. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The species is our identification, of medium confidence; several plants carry the name *kasamarda* in trade. [USER: verify]
- **The conditions named** (catarrh, asthma, cough) need proper medical care.
