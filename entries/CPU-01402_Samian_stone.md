# Samian stone — Lithos Samios (stone "found in Samia"), Dioscorides 5-173
## *A white, heavy stone that goldsmiths use to polish gold, called astringent and cooling, drunk for stomach disorders, applied with milk to the eyes, and tied on women to hasten delivery or prevent miscarriage; the translator gives no identification ("UNKNOWN")* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-173, *Lithos Samios* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10732; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 946, translator identification "UNKNOWN", class guess mineral; list row `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 898, "Lithos Samios", mineral, flagged as an uncertain identification; the book index sends the reader to page 827). The whole chapter was read. It reads (line 10734):

"A certain stone is found in Samia that the goldsmiths use to polish and make the gold glistening. The best is white and heavy. It is astringent and cooling, good for stomach disorders in drinks, dulling the senses. It is good with milk for discharges and ulcers in the eyes, and it is thought to hasten delivery [childbirth], and prevent miscarriage in women that have conceived, tied around them."

The chapter thus gives a place ("Samia"), a trade use (goldsmiths polish gold with it), a rule for the best (white and heavy), two qualities (astringent and cooling), and four uses: in drinks for stomach disorders, with a mention that it "dull[s] the senses"; with milk for discharges and ulcers of the eyes; and, "thought" to work, to hasten childbirth and to prevent miscarriage when tied around a pregnant woman. The last claim is introduced with "it is thought", so Dioscorides records it as reputation and does not vouch for it; it is recorded, not endorsed. The chapter gives no preparation, no burning or washing method, no sensory assay and no adulterant.

**The place name.** The chapter says "Samia". The translation's own gazetteer defines SAMIA as "a town of Elis in the district Triphylia, south of Olympia, between Lepreum and the Alpheus" (line 3523). The Codex name "Samian stone" and the spelling of the Greek heading (*Samios*) leave open whether the stone is named for that town or for the island of Samos; the chapter does not say, and no choice is made here.

**A different chapter with a similar name.** Chapter 5-172, *Samia* (Samian earth), directly precedes this one (line 10722). It is a different substance: "Samian earth is preferred which is very white and light, and on touching the tongue holds it fast like glue. Additionally it is full of liquid, soft and brittle ... There are two kinds; besides that previously mentioned there is one called aster, crusty and thick like whetstone." It stops spitting of blood, is given for menstrual flows, soothes inflammation of the breasts and "from stones [urinary, kidney]", and in water "represses sweating" (lines 10726-10731). It is not folded into this entry, and the Samian-stone chapter does not refer to it. The chapter after, 5-174 (*Chia*, Chian earth), says that Chian earth "which is white and almost an ashy colour like the Samian [above] should be used" and that "It has the same properties as Samian earth" (line 10738); "the Samian [above]" is ambiguous between 5-172 and 5-173, and the wording "Samian earth" points to 5-172. The text does not settle whether Dioscorides meant the stone and the earth to be related.

**Identity.** The translator gives no identification ("UNKNOWN"), and the Codex list marks the identification uncertain. The chapter's clues (white, heavy, from Samia, a polish for gold, astringent, cooling) do not name a mineral. Which material Dioscorides meant is not settled by the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records an unidentified white heavy stone as an astringent, cooling drink for stomach disorders, an eye application with milk, and an amulet worn by pregnant women to hasten birth or prevent miscarriage. The stone cannot be identified; swallowing a mineral polish is unsafe; the eye is a site where contamination causes serious harm; and pregnancy loss and difficult labour are medical emergencies for which an amulet is no substitute. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given in 5-173 beyond the vehicles named with the uses: "in drinks" (for stomach disorders), "with milk" (for eye discharges and ulcers), and "tied around" the pregnant woman (as an amulet). The chapter does not say what is done to the stone before use (grinding, washing, burning), what the drink is made with, or how much of the stone is used. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM).** The chapter prints two qualities for the stone: "It is astringent and cooling". It also says it is "dulling the senses". No degree is given, and no warming, drying or moistening quality is printed. The qualities printed for the neighbouring Samian earth (5-172) and Chian earth (5-174) are not carried across to this stone.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What the herbal offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention (provenance, season of gathering, sensory assay, adulteration detection), and this chapter shows part of it: a place (Samia), the trade that uses the material (goldsmiths) and a rule for the best (white and heavy). It gives no season, assay or adulterant. The amulet use is recorded with "it is thought" and is not endorsed by the author; it is a reputation, not a cultivation status. The chapter is therapeutic and carries no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation beyond noting how the herbal ties a material to a trade and a place. The stone itself is unidentified, and the chapter's pregnancy uses are not a recommended practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy:** the chapter records an amulet "thought" to hasten delivery or prevent miscarriage; nothing here supports either claim, and a threatened miscarriage or difficult labour needs urgent medical care. An unidentified mineral should not be swallowed in pregnancy.
- **Unidentified material:** the identification is uncertain; an unknown stone should not be drunk or placed in the eye.
- **The eye:** eye discharge and ulcers can threaten sight and need medical assessment.
- **Stomach disorders:** persistent or severe stomach complaints need medical assessment.
