### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (the surgical-Ayurvedic root text):** Agnimantha earns its place in the Ayurvedic pharmacopoeia not as a solo remedy but as structural timber in the single most important compound of classical Indian medicine — the **Daśamūla**, the "ten roots." Suśruta and the later nighaṇṭus divide these ten into the *bṛhat-pañcamūla* (five great/large roots, from trees) and the *laghu-pañcamūla* (five lesser roots, from shrubs and herbs). Agnimantha sits among the *bṛhat-pañcamūla* alongside Bilva, Śyonāka, Gambhārī (Kashmari), and Pāṭalā — the five arboreal roots whose combined action is the archetypal Vāta-Kapha–reducing, *śothahara* (anti-inflammatory / anti-oedema) and *balya* (strengthening) decoction. Daśamūla-kvātha remains, to this day, the backbone of post-partum care, respiratory formulae, and convalescent tonics in living Ayurvedic practice.

The name itself is a doctrine in miniature: *agni-mantha* means "fire-churning." The wood of Agnimantha was, by tradition, one of the woods used as the *araṇi* — the fire-drill churned to kindle the sacrificial flame. The plant that births ritual fire by friction is, in the physician's hands, the plant that rekindles the *jāṭharāgni*, the digestive fire, and burns through the cold, heavy, obstructive quality of accumulated Kapha and stagnant Vāta. Suśruta's therapeutic logic is isomorphic with the ritual logic — the same substance that makes fire outside makes fire inside.

**Caraka Saṃhitā & the later synthesists:** Caraka lists Agnimantha among *śothahara* (anti-oedema) and *śvayathuhara* dravyas. Vāgbhaṭa (Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya) and the nighaṇṭu tradition (Bhāvaprakāśa, Rāja Nighaṇṭu, Dhanvantari Nighaṇṭu) consistently attribute to it *dīpana* (digestive-fire kindling), *śothahara*, *āmapācana* (metabolising undigested residue), and *mūtrala* (diuretic) actions, and prescribe it in *āmavāta* (a rheumatic-inflammatory disorder), *pāṇḍu* (anaemia), *arśas* (haemorrhoids), and *medoroga* (disorders of fat-tissue).

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Kindling Wood:** No entry in this Codex better illustrates the Ayurvedic principle that a plant's *action* and its *cultural meaning* are read from the same root. Agnimantha is fire made vegetal. In the Kenetica frame it is a **kindling** substance — not a stimulant that borrows tomorrow's energy, but a friction-igniter that restarts a stalled internal fire. Where a stimulant is a match struck against an already-dry surface, Agnimantha is the drill that generates heat *in* a cold, damp, congested tissue-bed until it catches. This is why it belongs to the great-roots and not to the pungent single-herbs: its heat is structural and slow, meant to be built into a foundation, not flashed.

**Botanical & nomenclatural note:** The Ayurvedic Formulary of India historically carries genuine disagreement over the botanical source of Agnimantha — *Premna integrifolia* / *P. serratifolia* is the dominant and most-accepted identification, though *Clerodendrum phlomidis* is cited as Agnimantha in some regional traditions. This Codex follows the *Premna* identification (the more widely attested for the Daśamūla member). According to PubMed, this nomenclatural divergence within the AFIs is documented by Mali (2015) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0257-7941.165624)).

### Active Components

According to PubMed, phytochemical surveys of *Premna integrifolia* report: the flavone **luteolin** and its glycosides; the iridoid and iridoid-glycoside fraction; **premnazole** (an isoxazole alkaloid associated with anti-inflammatory activity); the alkaloids **premnine, ganiarine, ganikarine, and aphelandrine**; sterols (β-sitosterol); *p*-methoxycinnamic acid; the monoterpene **linalool**; linoleic acid; the pentacyclic triterpene **betulin**; and diterpenoid constituents (clerodendrin-A, premnaspirodiene) (Mali 2015 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0257-7941.165624); Mali 2016 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0257-7941.179864)). The root — the medicinally used part in the Daśamūla — is the target of most Ayurvedic preparation.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Agnimantha is filed under `[BUILD]` because its role is *foundational reconstruction*, not acute intervention. It is deployed when the picture is cold, heavy, waterlogged, and stalled: sluggish digestion after a period of undertraining or illness, soft oedematous tissue, a metabolism that has "gone out." Its classical *dīpana–pācana* (kindle-and-digest) and *śothahara* (de-swell) profile makes it a rebuilding-phase root — clearing the *āma* (metabolic residue) that blocks the assimilation of everything else you feed the system, then relighting the digestive fire so the build can actually take. In practice it is almost never used alone in the Kenetica frame; like its classical home, it is a **team root**, best expressed inside a Daśamūla-type synergy with Bilva, Gambhārī, Śyonāka, and Pāṭalā.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the evidence base for *Premna integrifolia* is **preclinical and traditional**, not clinical. Two review articles by Mali collate in-vitro and in-vivo (animal/cell) reports of anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, hepatoprotective, and related activities across various extracts and isolates, while explicitly noting that the traditional claims still require validation by modern clinical technique and that active-constituent isolation and clinical safety work remain to be done:

- Mali PY. *Pharmacological potentials of Premna integrifolia L.* Anc Sci Life. 2016;35(3):132–142. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0257-7941.179864)
- Mali PY. *Premna integrifolia L.: A review of its biodiversity, traditional uses and phytochemistry.* Anc Sci Life. 2015;35(1):4–11. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/0257-7941.165624)

**Evidence grade: Preclinical / traditional.** No human randomised clinical-trial evidence is cited here for *Premna integrifolia* as a single agent. Claims of therapeutic effect in humans should be treated as traditionally-grounded and scientifically unconfirmed.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṭu (pungent), Tikta (bitter), Kaṣāya (astringent)
- **Guṇa (quality):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive effect):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** *Agni-dīpana* by friction — the "fire-churning" kindling action encoded in its name
- **Doṣa effect:** Kapha–Vāta-śāmaka (pacifies Kapha and Vāta); Pitta-vardhaka in excess
- **Karma (actions):** Dīpana (kindles digestive fire), Pācana (metabolises āma), Śothahara (anti-oedema), Mūtrala (diuretic), Balya (strengthening, in the Daśamūla context)
- **Gaṇa membership:** Bṛhat-pañcamūla → Daśamūla (Suśruta); śothahara-varga

### Contraindications

Uṣṇa-vīrya and pungent–bitter–astringent — potentially aggravating in high Pitta states, active inflammation with heat, or Pitta-predominant constitutions. Dryness (rūkṣa guṇa) may not suit already-depleted, dry, or Vāta-vitiated tissue if used unbalanced. Pregnancy, lactation, and paediatric use: defer to a qualified practitioner — no clinical safety data cited. As with all Daśamūla members, intended for combined/formulaic use rather than high-dose single-herb use.
