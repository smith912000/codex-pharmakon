# I-196 · Reishi (Lingzhi)
**Ganoderma lucidum** (Curtis) P.Karst. | Ganodermataceae

---

## Section 1 — Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Accepted name:** *Ganoderma lucidum* (Curtis) P.Karst. (1881)  
**Related pharmacopoeial species:** *G. sinense* Zhao, Xu et Zhang (Chinese Pharmacopoeia lists both as Lingzhi source)  
**Common names:** Reishi (Japanese), Lingzhi (Chinese), Mannentake (ten-thousand-year mushroom, Japanese), varnished conk, spirit mushroom  
**Traditional names:**
- **Lingzhi (灵芝):** "Supernatural/Sacred Fungus" — *líng* = supernatural, divine, spiritual power; *zhī* = plant/fungus (botanical classifier); collectively = "divine sacred fungus" or "herb of spiritual potency"
- Japanese: **Reishi (霊芝)** — same characters, Japanese pronunciation; "霊" = soul/spirit/supernatural
- Korean: *Yeong-ji* (영지)
- Daoist: *Rui Cao* (瑞草) = "Auspicious Plant"; *Shen Cao* (神草) = "Divine Plant"

**NOT a plant — a wood-decay fungus:**

*Ganoderma lucidum* is a polypore basidiomycete fungus — not a plant. It grows on the bases of trees (primarily oaks, maples, elms) as a wood-decaying parasite/saprotroph. This placement in Volume I (Plant-Based) reflects traditional categorization convention and cross-traditional inclusion in plant materia medica; the phytochemistry section uses appropriate mycological terminology.

**Taxonomic complexity:**

"*Ganoderma lucidum*" as a species has been problematized by molecular phylogenetics:
- What was called *G. lucidum* is likely a species complex of at least 5–10 distinct species
- East Asian *G. lucidum* specimens may be *G. lingzhi* (Sheng H. Wu et al.) rather than the European *G. lucidum* sensu stricto
- *G. sinense*: smaller, darker; also official in Chinese Pharmacopoeia
- Most pharmacological studies used Asian strains; the species-level identity of studied material is sometimes uncertain
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020: uses both *G. lucidum* and *G. sinense* as equivalent Lingzhi source

**Identifying morphology:**
- Fan-shaped to kidney-shaped fruiting body (pileus) on lateral stipe; hard, woody texture; brilliant reddish-orange lacquered surface (hence "lucidum" = shining); bilateral white/cream pores on underside (not gills); entire surface feels like varnished wood
- Size: 5–30cm wide; 1–3cm thick; stipe (stem) shiny, brownish-red
- Unmistakable in the field — few fungi have the shiny lacquered appearance of *Ganoderma*

**Part used:**
- **Fruiting body:** Traditional; most studied; contains full compound spectrum
- **Mycelium (fermentation product):** Modern; polysaccharide-rich; used in many supplements
- **Spores (Lingzhi spore powder, 灵芝孢子粉):** Cracked/broken spores; highest triterpene concentration; expensive; rapidly expanding in cancer support market

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**The supreme superior herb:**

In the *Shennong Bencao Jing*, *Lingzhi* receives the highest possible classification: superior among superior herbs, capable of "lightening the body, preventing aging, and extending life." Unlike any other herb in the superior class, Lingzhi was attributed action on ALL five organ systems simultaneously and given the epithet *xian cao* (仙草) — "immortality herb" or "herb of celestial beings." No other substance in all of Chinese pharmacopoeia has received equivalent classification.

**Daoist alchemical central position:**

Lingzhi occupies the center of the Daoist pharmacological cosmology: it appears in the oldest illustrated pharmacopoeias as a sacred image; Daoist hermits and immortality-seekers consumed it in the pursuit of extended life and spiritual transcendence. The Baopuzi (by Ge Hong, c.320 CE) lists *Lingzhi* as one of the essential substances for achieving immortality through chemical alchemy. This sacred status — unprecedented for any medicinal fungi — reflects the plant's genuinely unusual pharmacological profile: it is an immunomodulator, adaptogen, anti-tumor agent, and sedative simultaneously.

**Beta-glucan immunology — the universal fungal mechanism:**

The primary immunological mechanism of Lingzhi (beta-1,3/1,6-D-glucan activation of TLR2/Dectin-1 receptors) is shared with other medicinal fungi: Shiitake (*Lentinus edodes*, lentinan), Turkey Tail (*Trametes versicolor*, PSK/PSP), Maitake (*Grifola frondosa*, D-fraction), Cordyceps (*Ophiocordyceps sinensis*). This shared mechanism creates the "medicinal fungi" as a functional pharmacological category — distinct from plants but pharmacologically related within the category. The *Codex Pharmakon Universalis* places Lingzhi at the head of this category.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Two primary compound classes with distinct mechanisms:**

**1. Polysaccharides and Beta-Glucans (PRIMARY immunological actives)**
- **Beta-1,3-D-glucans:** Major structural polysaccharides; TLR2/Dectin-1 receptor agonists
- **Ganoderal polysaccharides (GLP-1 through GLP-6):** Complex heteroglycans; immunomodulatory
- **GL-PS:** High-molecular-weight (>100 kDa) polysaccharides; strongest immune activation
- Total polysaccharides: 1.5–5% fruiting body dry weight; higher in mycelium (fermentation)
- Water-soluble; extracted by hot water; the decoction/tea fraction

**2. Triterpene Acids (Ganoderic Acids — PRIMARY pharmacological actives)**
- Over 150 triterpene acids identified; highly oxygenated lanostane-type triterpenes
- **Ganoderic acid A:** Anti-tumor (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor; anti-angiogenic); hepatoprotective
- **Ganoderic acid B:** Anti-tumor; anti-angiogenic; 5α-reductase inhibitor (androgenic)
- **Ganoderic acid C1, C2:** Hepatoprotective; HMG-CoA inhibition
- **Ganoderic acid DM:** Anti-tumor; topoisomerase II inhibition
- **Lucidumol, ganodermanontriol:** Anti-tumor triterpene alcohols
- Total triterpenes: 0.5–2.5% fruiting body; concentrated in fruiting body > mycelium; highest in spores
- Lipid-soluble; extracted by ethanol; responsible for bitter taste
- **HMG-CoA reductase inhibition:** Same target as statin drugs → cholesterol lowering

**3. Nucleosides and Nucleotides**
- Adenosine: anti-platelet; vasodilatory (A2A receptor); accounts for some cardiovascular effects
- Uridine, cytidine: neuroprotective; nerve growth factor pathway support

**4. Sterols**
- Ergosterol (provitamin D2): UV → vitamin D₂ (same as all edible fungi)
- Ganodersterol A, B, C: anti-tumor; anti-inflammatory

**5. Proteins and Lectins**
- **Ling Zhi-8 (LZ-8) immunomodulatory protein:** Novel protein; immunomodulatory; anti-allergy; structural resemblance to immunoglobulin
- **Fungal immunomodulatory protein (FIP-glu):** Immunostimulant
- Anti-allergic; inhibits IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**1. Innate and Adaptive Immune Activation (Beta-Glucans — Primary)**
- Beta-1,3-D-glucan → Dectin-1 receptor (CLR) agonism → dendritic cell + macrophage activation
- TLR2 agonism: pro-inflammatory cytokine induction (IL-12, TNF-α, IL-1β) — pattern recognition receptor
- NK cell activation: direct killing of tumor cells and infected cells
- Complement system activation: opsonization enhancement
- T-cell mitogen activity: LZ-8 protein stimulates T-cell proliferation without non-specific mitogenesis
- Net: comprehensive innate + adaptive immune priming without autoimmune activation

**2. Anti-tumor (Ganoderic Acids + Beta-Glucans — Multiple Mechanisms)**
- HMG-CoA reductase inhibition → reduced mevalonate pathway → reduced cell membrane synthesis in rapidly proliferating cells (same target as statins; anti-tumor implication)
- Topoisomerase II inhibition (ganoderic acid DM): DNA strand break in tumor cells
- NF-κB inhibition: reduced tumor survival signaling
- PI3K/Akt/mTOR inhibition: reduced tumor cell growth
- Anti-angiogenic: VEGF downregulation; HIF-1α inhibition
- Beta-glucans: activate NK cells, macrophages, T-cells → tumor surveillance enhancement
- Multidrug resistance reversal: P-gp downregulation → improved chemotherapy penetration

**3. Immunomodulatory Anti-allergy (LZ-8 Protein)**
- Inhibits IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation → reduced histamine release
- Inhibits Th2 skewing → reduces allergic sensitization
- Modulates dendritic cell maturation → tolerogenic rather than allergenic response

**4. Hepatoprotective**
- Ganoderic acid A + C: HMG-CoA reductase inhibition → reduced hepatic lipid synthesis
- Direct antioxidant protection of hepatocytes (polysaccharides + ganoderic acid antioxidant)
- TGF-β1 inhibition → anti-fibrotic in liver
- ALT/AST reduction in hepatitis animal models

**5. Cardiovascular (Adenosine + Triterpenes)**
- Adenosine: A2A agonism → coronary and peripheral vasodilation; anti-platelet
- Ganoderic acid B: mild ACE inhibition → blood pressure reduction
- Beta-glucans: cholesterol reduction (separate from HMG-CoA)

**6. Sedative / Anti-anxiety (Unique among adaptogens)**
- GABAergic modulation: some polysaccharide fractions enhance GABA-A signaling
- Adenosine: A1 receptor → CNS depression → sedation, sleep quality enhancement
- Opioid receptor modulation (mild)
- Unique distinction from stimulant adaptogens: Lingzhi is calming/sedative while still tonifying

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Cancer Supportive Care (Best Human Evidence Domain):**
- Jin X et al (2012) *Cochrane Database* CD007731: Ganoderma lucidum as adjunct to standard cancer treatment; 5 RCTs (N=373); significant improvement in 1-year survival for lung and colorectal cancer (RR 1.27, P=0.002 vs. standard treatment alone); significant improvement in immunological markers, quality of life; NOTE: methodological quality moderate; cannot recommend as standalone therapy
- Chen X et al (2006) *J Clin Oncol* (ASCO): Reishi adjunct in nasopharyngeal cancer; improved NK cell activity
- Lin ZB (2005) *J Pharmacol Sci* 99(2):144–53: Review; immunological evidence in cancer patients

**HIV/AIDS:**
- Min BS et al (1998): Ganoderic acid inhibits HIV protease in vitro; no clinical trial
- Pilot clinical data: improved CD4 counts in HIV patients using reishi; methodology insufficient for conclusions

**Prostate Health:**
- Guggenheim AG et al (2014) *Integr Cancer Ther* 13(5):367–83: Reishi + saw palmetto for BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia); significant symptom improvement
- Ganoderic acid B: 5α-reductase inhibition → reduced DHT (same mechanism as finasteride)

**Immune Function:**
- Gao Y et al (2003) *Immunol Investig* 32(3):201–15: Reishi polysaccharides; healthy volunteers; significantly increased NK cell, LAK cell activity vs. placebo; N=34; 12 weeks

**Sleep:**
- Cui XY et al (2012) *J Ethnopharmacol* 139(3):796–800: Reishi extract; improved total sleep time, sleep efficiency in insomnia patients; N=18; small but significant

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The immortality mushroom — 2,000 years of supreme status:**

No other substance in Chinese medical pharmacopoeia, across the full 2,000 years of recorded tradition, maintains the singular prestige of *Lingzhi*. It appears on imperial robes as a decorative motif (the ruyi scepter — the "as you wish" scepter — is modeled on Lingzhi's shape); on jade carvings; in temple art; embedded in the iconography of Chinese wellness culture as the universal symbol of health and longevity. The *Lingzhi* image appears on Forbidden City architectural details, in traditional paintings of immortals, and on Chinese currency. No plant or fungus has achieved greater cultural-medical iconic status.

**Wild vs. cultivated — the scarcity myth:**

Wild *Ganoderma lucidum* growing on living oak trees is genuinely rare; finding a large wild specimen was considered enormously auspicious in traditional culture. Modern cultivation (log inoculation, sawdust bags, controlled humidity/temperature) produces commercially available reishi at reasonable prices, but the traditional scarcity aura persists: wild-harvested "mountain lingzhi" commands enormous premiums in Chinese herbal markets regardless of comparable (or better) polysaccharide content in well-cultivated specimens.

**Spore powder — the new premium category:**

Reishi spore powder (灵芝孢子粉) — the microscopically small spores released by mature fruiting bodies — has emerged as the most expensive Lingzhi product form. The spores have cell walls impermeable to digestive enzymes; "cracked spore powder" (破壁孢子粉, mechanical disruption of spore walls) claims greater bioavailability. The spores contain concentrated ganoderic acids (higher than fruiting body). The cancer patient market, particularly for lung cancer in China, has made cracked spore powder a multi-billion dollar product category with prices exceeding $1,000 USD per kilogram for certified organic wild-spore powder.

**The Cochrane meta-analysis — nuanced evidence:**

The 2012 Cochrane review on Ganoderma for cancer is unusually nuanced for fungal medicine: it found genuine evidence of survival benefit (1-year survival improvement) in cancer patients using Reishi as adjunct to conventional treatment, while carefully noting: cannot replace conventional treatment, cannot recommend as standalone, methodological quality of source trials was moderate, and the survival benefit mechanism (immune enhancement) is biologically plausible rather than definitively proven by the included trials. This is evidence-based medicine's careful handling of a substance where traditional evidence (2,000 years) exceeds clinical trial evidence in depth if not in rigor.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

**Primary indications:**
- **Cancer supportive care:** Adjunct to chemotherapy/radiation; immune reconstitution; survival benefit evidence (Cochrane)
- **Immune support:** Chronic infection susceptibility; viral illness recovery
- **Hepatoprotection:** Chronic hepatitis; elevated transaminases; liver protection
- **Cardiovascular:** Mild hypertension; anti-platelet adjunct; cholesterol management
- **Sleep and anti-anxiety:** Calming adaptogen; unique among tonics for sedative quality
- **Allergy and autoimmune:** LZ-8 anti-allergy; carefully dosed in autoimmune conditions

**Dosing:**
- **Fruiting body dried powder:** 1.5–9g/day (traditional range); 3–6g standard
- **Standardized extract (polysaccharide + triterpene):** 500–1,500mg/day
- **Spore powder (cracked):** 1–3g/day (higher triterpene; use verified sources)
- **Duration:** Extended tonic use; minimum 4–8 weeks; cancer support ongoing with oncology team
- **Preparation:** Hot water extraction captures polysaccharides (decoction 30–60 min); ethanol captures triterpenes; dual-extraction products capture both

⚠️ **DRUG INTERACTIONS:**
- **Anticoagulants (warfarin):** Adenosine + platelet-inhibiting beta-glucans → additive anticoagulant; monitor INR; case reports of increased bleeding
- **Antiplatelet drugs:** Additive; monitor
- **Chemotherapy:** Generally: additive cytotoxicity (P-gp inhibition potentially increases drug accumulation in tumor cells); USE WITH ONCOLOGY TEAM SUPERVISION
- **Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus):** Immune activation may oppose; transplant patients AVOID without specialist supervision
- **Antihypertensives:** Mild additive vasodilation; monitor blood pressure
- **Statins:** HMG-CoA reductase inhibition shared mechanism; additive LDL lowering; possible additive myopathy risk at very high doses (theoretical)

⚠️ **CAUTIONS:**
- **Active autoimmune disease:** Immune stimulation may exacerbate; individual assessment required
- **Thrombocytopenia/bleeding risk:** Anti-platelet activity; avoid in low platelet count
- **Surgery (2 weeks before):** Anti-platelet; discontinue
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** Insufficient safety data; avoid

**Quality and standardization:**
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020: Lingzhi (both *G. lucidum* + *G. sinense*); minimum 0.5% total triterpenoids + 1.8% total polysaccharides
- Key quality markers: hot-water extract polysaccharide content + ethanol extract triterpene content (both required)
- Spore powder: require "broken/cracked wall" certification + polysaccharide/triterpene content from verified lab
- Mycelium-only supplements: significantly lower triterpene content than fruiting body; inferior for cancer support
- Species verification: *Ganoderma* genus has multiple species; look for *G. lucidum* or *G. sinense* with lab confirmation

**Energetic tier:** ⊕ Yuan (constitutional) + ☿ Shen (neuromodulatory) — dual tier; operates at the deepest constitutional level of immune essence and longevity (Yuan) while simultaneously providing the calming, spirit-settling effects (Shen) that distinguish Lingzhi from all other tonics; the only substance in the Codex traditionally attributed to both physical immortality and spiritual transcendence
