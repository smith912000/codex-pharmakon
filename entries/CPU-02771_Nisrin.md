# Nisrīn (نسرين)

## *A fragrant flowering plant whose flower and oil the Canon compares to jasmine (weaker) and narcissus; the Arabic name is applied to different plants (a rose among them in later usage) and the Canon text does not settle which, so no species is named; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. Book II, entry *nisrīn*, Arabic text from line 15519 of `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk was not run beyond the spine check. 

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** What it is: "it is like jasmine in power and weaker than it, and like narcissus; and its oil is near in power to the oil of jasmine" (هو كالياسمين في القوة وأضعف منه وكالنرجس ودهنه قريب القوة من دهن الياسمين). Temperament: "hot and dry in the second" (حار يابس في الثانية). Properties: "all its kinds clear (*munaqqin*) and thin (*mulaṭṭif*), and its flower more so". Joints and nerves: "benefits cold of the nerve, as is said". Head: kills worms in the ears; benefits ringing and buzzing (*ṭunīn*, *dawī*) and toothache; "the wild kind is smeared on the forehead and quiets headache"; "its kinds open the blockages of the nostrils". Chest: benefits swellings of the throat and tonsils. Digestive organs: "if four *durkhamiyyāt* (drachms) of it are drunk, it quiets vomiting and quiets hiccup, especially the wild kind".

**Kinds.** The text speaks of "all its kinds" and singles out a wild one (*barrī*). It does not name them.

**Identity doubt.** The comparison with jasmine and narcissus gives scent but not species. The spine's first-pass note offers a rose; modern lexica attach *nisrīn* to more than one plant. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None is given in the text. Scented flowers in general carry volatile oils; this is a general fact, not from the text.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The text records a scented flower and its oil for ear, tooth and head complaints, throat swellings, and (in drink) vomiting and hiccup. No protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

One dose is printed: four drachms (*durkhamiyyāt*) drunk, for vomiting and hiccup. The vehicle is not stated. This is the Canon's own figure; it is not advice. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General fact only. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Avicenna: hot and dry in the second degree; clearing and thinning in action.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No cultivation framing is attested. **(c) Absent.** The *Canon* entry is organised by organ and complaint and says nothing here about innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruh*. 

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to practise. It is a scented flower used for the head, throat and stomach.

### Contraindications

- Persistent vomiting, ear discharge, throat swelling and toothache need medical care.
- The species is not identified.
