### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā:** Devadāru — literally "timber of the gods" (*deva* + *dāru*) — is among the most frequently invoked woods of the classical canon. Its heartwood and the oleoresin/oil derived from it are workhorse dravyas for *śotha* (inflammatory swelling and oedema), *āmavāta* (rheumatic/arthritic conditions), *śūla* (colic and pain), *kāsa–śvāsa* (cough and dyspnoea), *prameha* (metabolic/urinary disorders), *kṛmi* (worm/parasitic conditions), and skin disorders. Suśruta groups it among warming, drying, Kapha-and-Vāta-reducing agents; it recurs in anti-inflammatory decoctions, medicated oils, and fumigations. It is a defining member of the classical **Vātahara / śothahara** wood group and heads its own eponymous formulary lineage (*Devadārvādi* type formulations).

**Bṛhat-trayī continuity (Caraka / Vāgbhaṭa):** Devadāru appears across *Caraka*'s therapeutic lists for oedema, arthritis, and metabolic disorders; it is *lekhana* (scraping/reducing), *dīpana–pācana* (digestive-kindling), and *mūtrala* (diuretic). The wood is standard in *Devadārvādi kvātha* and in numerous compound formulations for joint and metabolic disease.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Divine Timber and the Warm Current:**
The name marks Devadāru as a sacred material — the great Himalayan cedar under which, in the mythic imagination, sages and gods dwell. In Kenetica this "divinity of the timber" is read as *incorruptible warmth*: cedar wood is aromatic, resinous, resistant to rot and infestation — it is a substance that *does not stagnate*. That anti-stagnation is the whole of its doctrinal signature.

Where Uśīra is the cool reservoir beneath fire, Devadāru is the **warm current that dissolves the blockage**. Its work is against *stasis* — the cold, heavy, congealed accumulation (Kapha) and the knotted, obstructed pain (Vāta) that settle in the joints, the channels, the swollen tissue. It heats, it dries, it moves, it scrapes clear. Symbolically it is the *cedar-fire that keeps the temple free of rot*: the principle that whatever has grown stagnant, boggy, and congested must be warmed, mobilised, and drained. In the training arc Devadāru governs the **mobilisation / unblocking phase** — the active clearing of accumulated dampness and pain so that circulation, movement, and transformation can resume.

### Active Components

The **heartwood essential oil** of *Cedrus deodara* is rich in **sesquiterpenes and sesquiterpenoids**, notably **α- and β-himachalene** and **allo-himachalol / himachalol**, along with **(+)-himachalene**-type and related cedrane/atlantone constituents; **cedeodarin, deodarin (a dihydroflavonol), and other lignans/flavonoids** (e.g. matairesinol-type lignans, cedrin, cedrinoside) have been reported from the wood. These sesquiterpene and lignan/flavonoid fractions are associated with the anti-inflammatory, analgesic, spasmolytic, and antimicrobial activities traditionally attributed to the wood and its oil.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

- **Anti-inflammatory / anti-swelling (śothahara):** the flagship use — inflammatory swelling, oedema, joint inflammation.
- **Analgesic (vedanāsthāpana):** pain of arthritic and colicky type.
- **Vāta–Kapha mobilisation:** warming-drying clearance of stagnant, cold, damp accumulation in joints and channels.
- **Respiratory (kāsa–śvāsa):** warming expectorant/decongestant use for cough and congestion.
- **Metabolic / diuretic (prameha, mūtrala):** in classical metabolic-disorder formulations.
- **Antimicrobial / dermatological:** topical wood-oil applications for skin conditions and as a wood preservative-grade antimicrobial.

Phase role: the **unblocking / anti-inflammatory** agent — active, warming, mobilising.

### Scientific Correlation

*According to PubMed:*

- **Shinde et al., 1999, *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* — anti-inflammatory and analgesic activity of *Cedrus deodara* wood oil.** The steam-distilled wood volatile oil (oral, 50 and 100 mg/kg) produced significant inhibition of carrageenan-induced rat paw oedema and of both the exudative-proliferative and chronic phases of adjuvant arthritis, and showed analgesic activity in the acetic-acid writhing and hot-plate models in mice — a direct preclinical correlate to the classical *śothahara/vedanāsthāpana* use. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0378-8741(98)00150-0)
- **Saab et al., 2017, *Natural Product Research* — phytochemistry and pharmacology of *Cedrus* essential oils (review).** Reviews the ethnobotany, sesquiterpene-dominated chemistry, and reported cytotoxic, spasmolytic, immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antimicrobial/antiviral activities across the *Cedrus* genus, including *C. deodara*, while noting that further in vivo and clinical work is required. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786419.2017.1346648)

Evidence grade: **preclinical (in vivo rodent anti-inflammatory/analgesic models) plus a peer-reviewed pharmacological review.** The rodent data are among the stronger preclinical correlates in this batch, but there is no cited human clinical trial here — efficacy and dosing in people are not established. Do not attach human-outcome or percentage claims.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa:** tikta (bitter), kaṭu (pungent)
- **Guṇa:** laghu (light), rūkṣa (dry), tīkṣṇa (penetrating/sharp), snigdha in oil form
- **Vīrya:** uṣṇa (heating)
- **Vipāka:** kaṭu (pungent)
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha-śāmaka and Vāta-śāmaka; Pitta-vardhaka (heating — caution in Pitta excess)
- **Karma:** śothahara (anti-inflammatory), vedanāsthāpana (analgesic), dīpana–pācana (digestive), lekhana (reducing/scraping), mūtrala (diuretic), kṛmighna (anthelmintic), kaphanissāraka (expectorant), kaṇḍūghna (anti-itch)
- **Srotas / dhātu:** acts on māṃsa, asthi/sandhi (flesh, joints), mūtravaha and praṇavaha (urinary, respiratory) srotas; anti-āma
- **Gaṇa / formulary:** classical Vātahara/śothahara wood group; head of *Devadārvādi* formulations

### Contraindications

- **Pitta excess / inflammatory heat states of the hot type:** the uṣṇa (heating) potency can aggravate Pitta, bleeding-heat, hyperacidity, and hot inflammatory conditions.
- **Dryness / Vāta-dryness at high dose:** rūkṣa–tīkṣṇa qualities can over-dry with prolonged use.
- **Essential oil:** external use only unless supervised; dilute in carrier; standard essential-oil cautions in pregnancy and infancy apply. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy / lactation:** insufficient verified human safety data for concentrated preparations; use only under qualified supervision.
- **No validated human clinical dose** — internal use rests on classical practice and preclinical data.
