# Kulinga flesh — Kulinga and "the domestic Kulinga", flesh of a bird of the Pratuda group, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the bird Bhishagratna prints as "Kulinga" and, separately, "the domestic Kulinga"; his Vol. 3 index marks the name "(bird)"; no English name and no binomial is printed in the passage* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Kulinga is named in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 177 lists "Kulinga", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1", flagged id_uncertain). The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The high-ground (*Jangala*) animals fall into eight sub-groups: the *Janghala*, *Vishkira*, *Pratuda*, *Guhashaya*, *Prasaha*, *Parnamriga*, *Vileshaya* and *Gramya* (lines 403-409). The Kulinga stands in the third: "Birds such as the dove, pigeon Bhringaraja, cuckoo, Koyashtica, Kulinga, the domestic Kulinga, Gokshada, Dindimanaka, Shatapatraka, Matrinindaka, Bhedashi, Shuka, Sharika, Valguli, Girisha, Alahva, Dushaka, Sugrihi, Khanjaritaka, Harita, Datyuha, etc. belong to the group known as the Pratuda" (lines 505-512).

The group text then reads: "The Pratudas live on fruit, and their flesh has a sweet and astringent taste. It generates Vayu and produces a parched condition in the organism. It is cooling in its potency and reduces the Pittam and Kapham. It suppresses the discharge of urine and reduces the quantity of stool" (lines 513-518). Of the members it singles out the Bhedashi (flesh "tends to vitiate the humours"), the Kana Kapota or wild dove, and the Kulinga: "The flesh of the Kulinga is sweet, demulcent, and spermatopoietic, and increases the bodily Kapham. The flesh of the domesticated Kulinga is highly spermatopoietic, and proves curative in cases of haemoptysis" (lines 528-532).

Other places. *Sutrasthana* ch.45 (liquids) says of the fat, marrow and the like: "The fat, marrow, etc. of animals of the Pratuda and Vishkira species (doves, pigeons, etc.) reduce the bodily Kapham" (`SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`, lines 1068-1070), a statement about the group, not the Kulinga by name. The name occurs a second time in a different sense: *Kalpasthana* ch.6, on poisoning, names "a rat of the Kulinga species", whose bite "is marked by pain, swelling and stripe-like marks about the seat of the bite" and for which a lambative of the two kinds of *parni* (printed Mudga-parni and Masha-parni) pasted with *Sindhuvara* and mixed with honey is given (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch06.txt`, line 102; the name also stands in the chapter's list of eighteen rats at lines 21-25). That is a rat, not the bird, and is not part of this entry. Bhishagratna's Vol. 3 index has "Kulinga (bird)" (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 21711). No Dioscorides, Tibetan or other text is attached to this entry; the tag is SS only.

**Identity.** The translator gives the bird no English name in ch.46, and no binomial anywhere in the passage. The only gloss is "(bird)" in the Vol. 3 index. No species is proposed here, and none should be substituted from other sources without a text. The distinction between "Kulinga" and "the domestic Kulinga" is the translator's rendering of two names in the list. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Bird flesh is mostly water and muscle protein with some fat, and supplies iron, zinc and B vitamins. No analysis specific to this entry was read, and the species is not established. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Kulinga is a small wild bird of uncertain identity, and nothing in the text justifies sourcing, hunting or eating it today. Wild birds are commonly protected by national and international law, they can carry infections, and a species cannot be chosen from this text. As a medicine, the sourced record is a dietary one: the text states qualities and two indications, and the Codex does not recommend the flesh as a therapy for haemoptysis or for anything else. It gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation or dose is given for the Kulinga in ch.46. Ch.46 describes ways of preparing meat in general, but none is attributed to this bird in the passage read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Wild bird flesh must be cooked through and handled hygienically: birds can carry *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*, and wild birds can carry avian influenza viruses. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 513-532). For the *Pratuda* group: sweet and astringent in taste; generates *Vayu*; produces a parched condition; cooling in potency; reduces *Pittam* and *Kapham*; suppresses urine and reduces stool. For the Kulinga: sweet; demulcent; spermatopoietic; increases *Kapham*. For the domesticated Kulinga: highly spermatopoietic; curative in haemoptysis. The group is said to reduce *Kapham* while the Kulinga is said to increase it; the text does not reconcile the two, and both are recorded as printed. No post-digestive taste (*vipaka*) is printed for the Kulinga. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The Kulinga is named in the food-and-drink chapter, whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food in general, not about this flesh. The text calls the Kulinga "spermatopoietic" and the domestic Kulinga "highly spermatopoietic", which are properties stated among foods and not a placement in a *vajikarana* or *rasayana* chapter; no line read puts the Kulinga in either, and the name does not occur in the *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapters of the *Chikitsasthana* (a name search of that folder found it nowhere). No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. *Sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta the Kulinga is a food with stated qualities, and the one cautionary note a cultivator could take is that the text itself says it increases *Kapham*. The bird cannot be identified from the sources retrieved, so no one can follow this text to a particular animal. A person who eats meat may read the classification as the tradition's own description of the food; a person who does not has nothing to adopt.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** the bird is not identified; do not substitute a species on this basis. The same name means a rat in *Kalpasthana* ch.6. [USER: verify]
- **Food safety:** wild and domestic birds carry *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*; cook through and keep raw and cooked foods apart.
- **The text's own cautions:** it increases *Kapham*, and the *Pratuda* group generates *Vayu* and parches the body (ch.46, lines 513-516).
- **Legal and conservation status:** most small wild birds are protected in many countries, and trade or hunting may be unlawful. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food is a moral question for many traditions, and meat is restricted in some; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
