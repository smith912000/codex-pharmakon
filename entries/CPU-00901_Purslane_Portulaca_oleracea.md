# Purslane — baqla ḥamqāʾ (Arabic, "the foolish herb"), andrákhnē (Greek), ma chi xian (Chinese, "horse-tooth amaranth"), lonika / golasag (Sanskrit/Persian)

## *Portulaca oleracea* L. | Family: Portulacaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT · CM · BM · DMM · EP · SBJ · SS (7 traditions)
**Energetic Classification:** TCM — sour, cold (*ma chi xian*); clears Heat, resolves toxicity, cools Blood, stops dysenteric diarrhoea. Galenic — cold & moist (2nd–3rd degree); cooling, moistening, astringent.
**Training Phase:** Clearing · Cooling · Restoration

### Origin & Tradition

Purslane is a succulent, sprawling pot-herb of astonishing geographic reach — found wild across Eurasia, Africa and the Americas — and one of the very few plants to earn a near-identical energetic reputation (**cooling, moistening, heat-clearing**) on multiple continents independently. It is at once a weed, a salad green and a formal medicine, and it carries a modern distinction: among the richest leafy plant sources of omega-3 fatty acids (alpha-linolenic acid).

**Chinese tradition (*ma chi xian*, "horse-tooth amaranth"):** Purslane is a standard herb of the Chinese materia medica in the "clear Heat, resolve toxicity" category. Sour and cold, entering Large Intestine, Liver and Heart, it is a classic remedy for **damp-heat dysentery and bacillary/bloody diarrhoea**, for hot sores, carbuncles, snake and insect bites (applied fresh-pounded), and for cooling the Blood in bleeding from heat. It remains in routine clinical use for acute intestinal infection.

**Greco-Roman / Dioscorides tradition (*andrákhnē*):** Dioscorides describes purslane (*andrákhnē*) as cooling and moistening, eaten and applied to cool inflammation, quench heat and thirst, settle a hot stomach, and soothe the teeth set on edge; Galen ranks it cold and moist. It was a standard Mediterranean cooling salad-medicine for hot, bilious and feverish states.

**Unani / Avicenna tradition (*baqla ḥamqāʾ*):** Unani inherits the Galenic reading — cold and moist in the second/third degree — and prescribes purslane (and especially **its seeds, *bazr al-baqla*, *tukhm-e-khurfa***) for hot dyscrasias: burning urination, hot fluxes, bilious fevers, haemorrhage from heat, and as a cooling demulcent. Avicenna's *Canon* treats it as a reliable refrigerant.

**Egyptian tradition:** Purslane is native to and long eaten in Egypt (*rigla*), used as a cooling summer pot-herb and folk cooling/soothing medicine within the Egyptian-Arab continuum — a food-medicine for heat and thirst.

**Ayurvedic / South-Asian tradition:** Purslane (*lonika* and regional names) is used as a cooling, Pitta-pacifying pot-herb and mild diuretic; the succulent leaves and seeds serve cooling, soothing and urinary indications consistent with its cold-moist nature.

**Assyrian / Mesopotamian tradition:** As a widespread edible succulent of the region, purslane belongs to the ancient Near-Eastern cooling pot-herb tradition — a food-medicine for hot and feverish conditions, aligned with its later Unani classification.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the humble weed that cools the blood:** Purslane's Arabic name, *baqla ḥamqāʾ* — "the foolish/silly herb" — is said to reflect its habit of sprawling low and rooting wherever water pools, growing "foolishly" underfoot in gardens and cracks; yet this despised weed is a potent cooler of the blood. Its doctrinal signature in Kenetica is *the overlooked coolant* — the medicine that is everywhere, trodden on, dismissed, and precisely for that reason always available to quench heat. Fleshy, water-storing, glossy and cold to the touch, it embodies **stored coolness**: a succulent reservoir of moisture that the plant carries through drought and gives to a body burning with damp-heat, dysentery or fever. Where fire and flux consume, purslane is the wet green weight that settles and cools. Fool's herb, sage's remedy.

### Active Components

- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** alpha-linolenic acid (ALA) — purslane is among the richest leafy-vegetable sources.
- **Polyphenols and flavonoids:** including catecholamines, kaempferol, quercetin, apigenin; betalain/betacyanin pigments.
- **Antioxidant micronutrients:** vitamins C, E, beta-carotene; minerals (potassium, magnesium).
- **Organic acids & alkaloids:** oxalic acid (notable), portulaca-specific alkaloids; mucilage (the succulent demulcent fraction).
- **Seeds:** additional fatty acids, polyphenols and mucilage — the classic Unani cooling fraction.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Purslane is the **cool-moist heat-clearer** — food and medicine at once. Kenetica roles: (1) a **cooling, blood-cooling herb for damp-heat states** — hot dysenteric diarrhoea, hot sores and skin inflammation (applied fresh), burning urination; (2) an **omega-3-rich, antioxidant pot-herb** with emerging metabolic and hepatoprotective interest (lipids, fatty liver, oxidative/inflammatory markers); (3) a demulcent cooling seed preparation in the Unani manner for hot fluxes. Training-phase use: Clearing and Cooling (heat/damp-heat resolution), Restoration (nutrient-dense metabolic support food).

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- Milkarizi et al. (2024, *Frontiers in Nutrition*) — a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in **70 patients with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease** — found that **700 mg/day of *Portulaca oleracea* for 8 weeks** (with a calorie-restricted diet) significantly improved body weight/BMI, blood sugar, lipid profile, liver enzymes (AST, ALT, GGT), systolic blood pressure, inflammatory and oxidative-stress markers, and reduced liver steatosis and stiffness on elastography versus placebo. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2024.1371137)
- Sabzghabaee et al. (2014, *Medical Archives*) — a triple-blinded randomised placebo-controlled trial giving **powdered *P. oleracea* seeds 500 mg twice daily for one month** to obese adolescents — reported significant reductions in LDL-cholesterol and triglycerides versus placebo, with good tolerability. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5455/medarh.2014.68.195-199)

**Evidence grade:** *Emerging-to-moderate.* Two randomised placebo-controlled human trials (one double/triple-blinded) support metabolic benefits — lipid-lowering and improvement of NAFLD parameters — though sample sizes are modest and durations short. The traditional **heat-clearing / anti-dysenteric** use is broadly and independently attested across TCM, Greco-Roman and Unani sources (historically strong), with mechanistic support from the plant's polyphenol and omega-3 content, but fewer modern controlled trials for the acute-infection indication; graded *traditionally robust, clinically promising*.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Sour, cold; enters Large Intestine, Liver, Heart. Clears Heat, resolves (Fire) toxicity, cools Blood, stops dysenteric diarrhoea, reduces swelling.
- **Ayurvedic:** Cooling *vīrya*, Pitta-pacifying; mildly diuretic and soothing.
- **Galenic / Unani:** Cold & moist (2nd–3rd degree); refrigerant, moistening, mildly astringent; seeds a classic cooling demulcent for hot fluxes.

### Cultivation Register

Purslane is an Old World cosmopolitan weed, so its Eurasian and Mediterranean tags are not chronologically suspect. But its role everywhere is that of a **clearing, cooling, heat-resolving** agent — the therapeutic opposite of a life-nourishing tonic — so a cultivation status is largely absent even where the therapeutic attestation is strong.

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The classical record is genuine: Dioscorides describes *andrákhnē* as cooling and moistening, and Galen ranks it cold-and-moist. But Dioscorides carries no subtle-body system to assign a cultivation status; what he offers a cultivator is his discipline of attention, not a status for this plant. **(c) absent, atop a real therapeutic record.**

**Chinese medicine (SBJ).** *Ma chi xian* is a standard "clear Heat, resolve toxicity" drug — a cooling, blood-cooling, anti-dysenteric agent. That is a clearing therapeutic function, not a *yang sheng* one; no *san pin* upper grade or life-nourishing status is attested. **(c) absent (clearing, not cultivating).**

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** *Baqla ḥamqāʾ* and its seeds are a reliable refrigerant and cooling demulcent for hot dyscrasias. Cooling a hot humour is therapeutic correction, not conservation of innate heat; no cultivation status attested. **(c) absent.**

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Lonika* is a cooling, Pitta-pacifying pot-herb and mild diuretic; no *rasāyana* or *vājīkaraṇa* status is attested. **(c) absent.**

**Egyptian (EP).** *Rigla* is a cooling summer pot-herb and folk soothing food-medicine; no *heka* cultivation framing attaches. **(c) absent.**

**Assyrian (AT).** A widespread edible succulent in the region's cooling pot-herb tradition; the blocking warning on Assyrian identifications applies. No cultivation status attested. **(c) absent.**

**Nahua — Badianus (BM).** ⚠️ This tag is unsupported in the entry body, which records no Mesoamerican attestation for purslane; the Badianus manuscript is in any case naturalistic and does not carry the *tonalli/teyolia/ihiyotl* model (reconstructed from later ethnohistory, López Austin). No cultivation status attested; the tag itself should be reviewed. **(c) absent.**

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator uses purslane to cool and clear — a fresh potherb or poultice against damp-heat, hot sores and burning flux, and a nutrient-dense omega-3-rich green. Its work is subtractive (draining heat and toxicity), not the building of a vital substance. Across every tradition that claims it, the honest register is (c): a heat-clearer, not a life-nourisher.

### Contraindications

- **High oxalate content** — caution or avoidance in oxalate kidney-stone formers and significant renal impairment; excessive intake may aggravate stone risk.
- Cold nature (TCM) may aggravate Spleen-Yang-deficient / cold-damp digestive pictures; avoid in cold-type diarrhoea and in pregnancy where classical texts caution against its cooling, potentially uterus-active action `[USER: verify pregnancy status with practitioner]`.
- Metabolic/hepatoprotective use should complement, not replace, standard care and monitoring for NAFLD and dyslipidaemia.
- Correctly identify the plant — succulent look-alikes (e.g. spurge, *Euphorbia*, with milky irritant sap) can be mistaken for purslane; purslane has watery, not milky, sap.
