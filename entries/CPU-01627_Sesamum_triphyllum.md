# Sesamum triphyllum — Sesamoeides mikron (Dioscorides 4-153)

## *Sesamum triphyllum, "Wild Sesame", the first of two names on the translators' SUGGESTED line for 4-153 ("Sesamum triphyllum — Wild Sesame Hyptis spicigera — Black Sesame")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides (DMM).** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The binomial is the suggestion of the translators (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000), not Dioscorides' own identification, and the Codex does not state the identity as certain. The SUGGESTED line (line 9395) prints two names, *Sesamum triphyllum* ("Wild Sesame") and *Hyptis spicigera* ("Black Sesame"), which are plants of two different genera; the row takes the first. It was added from that line (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is *4-153. SESAMOEIDES MIKRON* ("the small sesamoides"), heading at line 9394, SUGGESTED line at 9395, text at line 9396, interrupted by a page break (the page number 703 and the running head "BOOK FOUR: OTHER HERBS & ROOTS", lines 9397 to 9399) and resumed at line 9400. It stands in Book Four ("Other Herbs & Roots"), after *4-152. SESAMOEIDES* (the great sesamoides, line 9389) and before *4-154. SIKUS AGRIOS* (a wild cucumber, line 9401). The name index of the Codex's own extraction lists it (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`, row 731); the translators' general index prints "Sesamum triphyllum 703" (line 17516).

The whole chapter reads: "The small sesamoides has small stalks twenty centimetres long, with leaves similar to coronopis, yet rougher and smaller. On top of the little stalks are small heads, with somewhat purple little flowers, the middle of which is white, in which is seed similar to sesama [2-121], bitter and yellowish. The root is thin. Half an acetabulum [vinegar cruet] of a decoction of the seed (taken as a drink with honey water) purges bile and phlegm downwards. Applied with water it dissolves small swellings and oedema. It grows in rough places. It is also called coronion, or sylvestre sesamon."

The chapter gives, in order: the height of the stalks (twenty centimetres); the leaves, compared with *coronopis* (the text does not cite a chapter number; the plant *coronopus* has its own chapter, 2-158, line 5893) and said to be rougher and smaller; small heads on top of the stalks, with "somewhat purple" flowers white in the middle; seed like that of *sesama* (chapter 2-121), "bitter and yellowish"; a thin root; and the place, "rough places". Its use is one: a decoction of the seed, taken as a drink with honey water, "purges bile and phlegm downwards", and the seed applied with water "dissolves small swellings and oedema". Other names printed are *coronion* and *sylvestre sesamon*.

**The sister chapter.** *4-152. SESAMOEIDES* (the great sesamoides, lines 9389 to 9393) carries the same SUGGESTED names, with the further note "ALSO: *Sesamum indicum*, *Sesamum orientale*" and the translators' gloss "sesamoides means 'like sesame'". It is described as "a herb similar to senecio [ragwort] or to rue", with a white flower, a slender useless root and bitter seed "which purges both phlegm and bile upward", pounded and taken with fifteen grains of white hellebore. That is a different chapter on a different plant by Dioscorides' own division, and it is not read into this entry beyond this labelled note. No entry for 4-152 was found in the entries folder (see Overlap).

**Overlap.** A chapter-level search of the entries folder for 4-153 and *SESAMOEIDES* found one file, I_1162 (*Plantago coronopis*), which uses 4-153 only as a leaf comparison ("similar to coronopis, yet rougher and smaller", line 9396). Nothing treats 4-153 as its own plant. The spine's sesame entry, I_217 (*Sesamum indicum*), was not read for this entry and is a different species. The chapter's *sesama* (2-121, line 5519) is the cultivated sesame; its text begins "Sesamum is hurtful to the stomach and causes a stinking breath in the mouth", and its SUGGESTED line names *Sesamum indicum*. That is a labelled cross-reference only; the 4-153 text says no more than that the seed is *similar* to it.

### Active Components

The chapter prints no constituents. It is a general fact that *Sesamum* (sesame) belongs to the family Pedaliaceae and that *Hyptis* belongs to the mint family (Lamiaceae), so the two names on the translators' line are not near relatives, and nothing about the composition of one can be assumed for the other. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Dioscorides reports a seed drunk as a purge of bile and phlegm "downwards" and applied for small swellings. These are the text's reports, the identification is the translators' and is divided between two genera, and the Codex gives no protocol. A purge is a use that carries risk of dehydration and harm, and nothing here supports taking an unidentified wild seed.

### Preparation & Dosing

4-153 gives two preparations. Taken as a drink: "Half an acetabulum [vinegar cruet] of a decoction of the seed (taken as a drink with honey water)" (line 9400). Applied: the seed "with water" for small swellings and oedema (line 9400). The chapter gives no frequency or duration, and the volume of the *acetabulum* is not given in the text read. This is the traditional dose of the text and not a modern one. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** the chapter prints one quality, of the seed: "bitter and yellowish". It prints no warming, cooling, drying or moistening quality.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter records no amuletic use and no rite. What it gives is of the kind the cultivation spec calls a discipline of attention: the height of the stalks, the shape of the leaves compared with a named plant, the colour of the flowers, the taste and colour of the seed, the thin root, the place it grows, and the two other names. A comparison with a sister plant is also part of that discipline, since the great and the small *sesamoides* are given as two chapters. The plant is given no status beyond that of a purging seed with a use on swellings. The identification is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. The useful part is the habit the chapter teaches: say how tall, what leaf, what colour, what seed, which ground. The purge it describes is not something to take on the strength of this entry, and the abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

A purge of bile and phlegm "downwards" is a use that can cause dehydration and harm, and the chapter gives a measure (half an *acetabulum*) that cannot be converted with confidence. The plant's identity is a translators' suggestion and is divided between two genera, so no wild plant should be gathered or taken on the strength of this entry. Swellings, oedema and digestive complaints need medical assessment. None of the traditional uses above is advice. [USER: verify]
