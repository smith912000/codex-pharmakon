# Indivara (Sushruta Samhita); glossed Nilotpala by the commentator Jejjata, as Bhishagratna reports

## *Tentatively a blue water-lily or blue lotus (the commentator Jejjata's gloss Nilotpala, "blue utpala"), but Bhishagratna's footnote adds that the other commentator, Gayadasa, "does not support this". The plant's Latin name is not printed in the text read; "a blue water-lily, probably a Nymphaea species" is our rendering of the commentator's gloss, of low confidence. The row's `check` guess ("Unidentified (indivara)") is corrected to this tentative reading* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, in four places.** This is a single-text entry. The loci are in Bhishagratna's translation, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15403, and its chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/`.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS).**

1. **Sutrasthana ch.38, the Viratarva'di group** (Vol. I line 15403; `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 62). Indivara is a member of the group that also contains Virataru, Darbha, Kusha, Kasha, Agnimantha and others. The group's Metrical Text says that it "subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles" (ch.38 file lines 66-70). The statement is about the group, and not about Indivara singly.
2. **Sutrasthana ch.46, the bulb group** (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 1932; Vol. I line 21592). Among the bulbs and tubers, the text lists "Pindaluka, Madhvaluka, Hastyaluka, Kasthaluka, Shankhaluka, Raktaluka, Indivara and Utpala etc.", and says of them that they "alleviate" a bleeding complaint (the OCR prints "lifemo^tysis", which reads as haemoptysis; [USER: verify]), "are cooling in their potency, sweet in their taste and heavy of digestion", and "tend to increase the semen in large quantities and augment the quantity of milk in the breast of a human mother". That too is said of the listed bulbs together.
3. **Chikitsasthana ch.7, the treatment of stone** (Vol. II line 15015). A paste (kalka) of "Shilajatu, Madhuka (flower) and the seeds of Indivara, Trapusha and Ervaruka" is used with a decoction in Pittaja Ashmari ("calculi, etc."). Bhishagratna's footnote (line 15042) reads: "Jejjata explains 'Indivara' as 'Nilotpala.' But Gayadasa does not support this." This is the source for the tentative identification, and it also shows that the commentators disagreed. The text names the seeds as the part used in this recipe.
4. **A later chapter on Rakta-pitta** (Vol. III line 12002): "The alkaline water prepared with the ashes of Indivara and taken with honey, powdered [name damaged in the OCR]-seeds taken with honey and clarified butter, and the decoction of Jambu, Arjuna and Amra — these three compounds prove curative in cases of Rakta-pitta." The chapter number was not checked. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, water-lilies of the genus *Nymphaea* carry flavonoids and aporphine-type alkaloids, but the identification here is tentative and no analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says.** Group-level effects (Vata and urinary disorders; the bulb group's cooling, sweet, semen- and milk-increasing character); the seeds in a stone paste; the ashes in an alkaline water for Rakta-pitta.
- **What the text does not say.** Any dose, or any quantity of seeds or ashes. The identity is also a matter on which the commentators differed.

The Codex gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant or any substitute. Stones in the urinary tract and bleeding disorders need proper medical care.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives these preparations: seeds in a paste (Vol. II line 15015), and ashes made into an alkaline water taken with honey (Vol. III line 12002). Neither gives an amount. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

The identification is tentative, so no scientific correlation is made. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Bhishagratna, ch.46).** For the bulb group in which Indivara is named: cooling in potency (*virya*), sweet in taste (*rasa*), heavy of digestion. These are printed for the group together and are not assigned to Indivara singly.
- No classification is printed for Indivara in ch.38.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Indivara is named in therapeutic groups. The ch.46 bulb passage says of the group that it increases semen and milk, but it does not call Indivara a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* drug, and nothing in the lines read assigns it *ojas*. It therefore carries a group-level therapeutic and nutritive frame, and no cultivation status of its own in this tradition.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do. A water-plant of uncertain identity, used for stones and bleeding in the text and listed among nourishing bulbs. The commentators themselves disagreed on the plant, and a cultivator has no sourced course to follow.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** Disputed (Jejjata and Gayadasa differ). Do not substitute a plant for it.
- **Toxicity.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
