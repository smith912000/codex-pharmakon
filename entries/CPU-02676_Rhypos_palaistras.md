# Rhypos palaistras — RUPOS PALAISTRA, "grime from the wrestling school" (Dioscorides *De Materia Medica* 1-35)

## *Dirt or filth from the wrestling school (the palaestra): a deposit of oil, sweat, sand and skin scrapings, not a mineral in the ordinary sense; identification is by description only. The translator gives no "SUGGESTED" line, the name-index calls the class "other", and the spine row is classed mineral by a forced rule, so the class is a filing decision and not a finding; confidence in the description is high, in the class low* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk, under this Greek name.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is *De Materia Medica* 1-35, heading "RUPOS PALAISTRA" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 3824; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 36, "DMM, RUPOS PALAISTRA, 1-35 (line 3824), Grime from the wrestling school, other"). The chapter was read in full (lines 3824 to 3825).

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The whole chapter is one sentence. The translator's subtitle is "Grime from the wrestling school". The text: "The dirt or filth from the wrestling school helps the joints, applied as a warm compress." The text names no other use, no vehicle, no quantity and no length of use. The "warm compress" is the only mode of application it gives.

**The neighbouring chapters, read for context.** The grime chapters form a run of three in Book I, among the oils (the page opens with the Sicyonian oil, 1-33):

- 1-34 RUPOS, "Grime from the baths" (line 3821): "The scrapings which are taken up in public baths are able to heat, soften, and disperse fluids, and are good for splits in the perineum, and for rubbing on joints." This is the spine row CPU-02606, not yet written.
- 1-35 RUPOS PALAISTRA (this row): joints, as a warm compress.
- 1-36 RUPOS GYMNASION, "Grime from the gymnasium walls" (line 3826): the filth on the gymnasium walls or scraped off statues "warms and dissolves tubercles [growths] that ripen only with difficulty", and helps abrasions, scaliness and old ulcers. This is CPU-02643, entry II_233.

The three differ by the place the filth was taken from and by the complaints named. Bath grime (1-34) and wrestling-school grime (1-35) both name the joints. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The joints, applied as a warm compress.
- **What the text does not say.** It gives no dose, no preparation, no length of use, and no warning.
- **Why it is not a practice.** Applying filth from a communal place to the skin is unhygienic and risks infection. The Codex records the passage as a piece of the history of materia medica and recommends nothing from it.

The Codex does not translate "joints" into a modern diagnosis and gives no protocol.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Dirt and body residues from communal places are not sterile; applying them to the skin, above all to broken skin, is a recognised route to infection. Warmth applied to a joint is a general comfort measure, and the chapter does not say that the grime adds anything to it. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** The chapter prints no quality. The sister chapter 1-34 prints "heat, soften, and disperse fluids" and 1-36 prints "warms"; neither is applied to this chapter here.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is attested for this substance. What the chapter shows is the attention to provenance that runs through his book: filth is sorted by where it was taken (baths, wrestling school, gymnasium wall), and each place has its own named use.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this. It is a one-sentence topical remedy, and its place in a book of cultivation is that of a curiosity: the place where bodies were trained also gave the physician a substance. Nothing in the record supports a cultivation use.

### Contraindications

- **Infection.** Material from communal places is unclean. Do not apply it to the skin, above all not to wounds.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved; avoid. [USER: verify]
