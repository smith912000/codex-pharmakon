# Mantis egg case — 桑螵蛸 *sang piaoxiao* (Shennong bencao jing)

## *The hardened egg case of a praying mantis, our identification (medium confidence) as the case of Tenodera sinensis, with Statilia maculata also a candidate; the Mingyi layer itself says only "the child of the mantis" (螳螂子)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Shennong bencao jing (SBJ).** The egg case of the mantis is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The species is ours; the text names no species. The source is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`); the entry stands in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 桑螵蛸 *sang piaoxiao*, same file, line 1495). The name means "the *piaoxiao* on the mulberry".

**The Shennong text proper** (line 1495): "Flavour salty, balanced. It governs injury within the middle, *shan-jia* (hernia-like masses), impotence; it benefits the *jing* and produces children; for women, blocked blood and pain in the lower back; it frees the five *lin* (urinary difficulties) and makes the urine and water passages flow. One name, 'eater of warts' (蚀疣). It grows on mulberry twigs. Gather it, steam it." (味咸，平。主伤中，疝瘕，阴痿，益精生子，女子血闭腰痛，通五淋，利小便水道。一名蚀疣。生桑枝上。采，蒸之。) Note that "gather it, steam it" is part of this paragraph, so the one preparation the Shennong layer gives is steaming.

**The Wu Pu layer** (3rd century CE, line 1497): the Wu Pu lists the names "eater of warts" (蚀疣; Sun notes that the present text lacks these two characters), "*haijiao*" (害焦) and "*zhi*" (致), and records "Shennong: salty, non-toxic" (神农：咸，无毒), with the *Taiping yulan* given as the source of that quotation. This is Wu Pu's report of what Shennong said, a different layer from the paragraph above.

**The Mingyi bielu layer** (line 1499): "It is the child of the mantis (螳螂子也). Gather in the second and third months; roast over fire." (二月、三月采，火炙。) The two preparations differ: the Shennong paragraph says steam, the Mingyi layer says roast. Both are on the page.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 1501).** Sun quotes the *Shuowen*, the *Guangya* and the *Erya* with Guo Pu's gloss, which says the case is the egg of the mantis; he quotes the *Fan Zi Jiran* as saying "*piaoxiao* comes from the Sanfu; the best price is three hundred" (螵蛸，出三辅，上价三百; the unit is not given); and he remarks that the old text wrote the first character differently and calls that a scribal error from similar sound. Several characters in this passage are dropped in the text file. I read only what is legible.

**Preface.** The upper class "mainly nourishes life, to correspond to heaven; non-toxic; taken long does not harm people" (line 43).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, a mantis egg case (an ootheca) is a foamy secretion that hardens around a mass of eggs; it is made largely of protein and holds the developing eggs inside. Nothing more specific is stated in the texts, and no analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records the egg case for urinary difficulties (the "five *lin*"), impotence, injury within, and in women, blocked blood and lower-back pain, and says it benefits the *jing* and produces children. The Codex does not turn any of these into a present-day indication. Urinary retention and painful urination, lower-back pain and absent periods all need diagnosis. Wild-gathered egg cases are also a conservation and hygiene matter (see Contraindications).

### Preparation & Dosing

Only the preparations the layers give: steaming (Shennong paragraph, line 1495) and roasting over fire (Mingyi, line 1499); the harvest is the second and third months (Mingyi). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

The case is an arthropod product and, like other insect cases and cocoons, it is mainly protein. Whether it affects urinary function or fertility, as the text says, has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong).** One flavour and one nature are printed: salty (咸), balanced (平). Wu Pu adds, for Shennong, "non-toxic" (无毒). The class is upper by placement. No further quality is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The egg case sits in the upper classic (上经), the class the preface (line 43) describes as nourishing life, corresponding to heaven, non-toxic and fit to be taken long (主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人). On the grading rule used for this series, placement in the upper classic is itself the ground for (a). The Shennong paragraph, however, has no *jiu fu* ("taken for long") clause and promises no lightening of the body, no ageing-resistance and no extension of life. The nearest wording is "benefits the *jing* and produces children" (益精生子), which is a statement about generation, not about long taking. The attestation is therefore of the upper-class placement; the paragraph itself makes no life-nourishing promise, and the Codex does not read one into it.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing here for a cultivator to take up. The classic offers a steamed egg case for a short list of urinary and generative complaints, gives no dose and no course, and does not call for long use. The "upper" label is the tradition's grading of safety, not an instruction to take the substance for years.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and sourcing.** Egg cases collected from the wild may hold live nymphs or other insects and must not be eaten raw. Cases are used in the tradition only after steaming or roasting.
- **Identification.** The species is not fixed by the text; do not substitute material from another mantis or insect case.
- **Conservation.** Gathering of wild invertebrates is subject to local rules. [USER: verify]
- **Medical conditions.** Urinary difficulty, impotence, blocked menses and lower-back pain require diagnosis. The record is historical.
- **Pregnancy and children.** The text says nothing; no use is supported. [USER: verify]
