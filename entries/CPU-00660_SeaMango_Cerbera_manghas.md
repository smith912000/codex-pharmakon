> **The kernel of *Cerbera manghas* contains cerberin and related cardenolides — Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase inhibitors that stop the human heart.** A single kernel can kill an adult. There is **no therapeutic dose**, no safe preparation, and no self-experimentation protocol in this codex or any other. **This entry contains no dosing table, deliberately.** Its close relative *Cerbera odollam* is among the most notorious plant poisons on Earth. If you have ingested any part of this plant or its fruit, **this is a medical emergency — call emergency services now.** Nothing in this entry is a permission.

### Origin & Tradition

**Carl Linnaeus named this genus after Cerberus, the three-headed hound that guards the gate of Hades and lets nobody back out.** Botanical nomenclature is rarely so candid, and almost never so accurate. The name is not decoration. It is a warning label applied in Latin by a man who knew exactly what the seed did, and it has now been carried, unheeded, across three centuries and half a tropical world.

*Cerbera manghas* — sea mango, pong-pong, bintaro, othalanga (a name it shares with its sibling *C. odollam*) — is a small coastal and mangrove-margin tree of the Apocynaceae, distributed from the Seychelles and Madagascar through South and Southeast Asia to Australia and across the Pacific. It bears glossy leaves, fragrant white flowers with a red or pink throat (hence "red-eye sea mango"), and a smooth, green-to-red drupe that ripens to something a hungry stranger would take for a mango. It is not a mango. The resemblance is the whole tragedy: an edible-looking fruit on a shoreline tree, buoyant, sea-dispersed, washing up on beaches across the Indo-Pacific with a lethal kernel inside.

**Contemporary Folk Medicine (CM)** — *external and expulsive only*
Traditional use across the plant's range is, tellingly, almost entirely **non-ingestive**. The tree is used as:
- a **fish poison (piscicide)** — crushed fruit and bark thrown into pools or tidal traps to stun and float fish
- a **rodenticide and insecticide** — the seed's toxicity turned against pests
- an **emetic and purgative** in some folk accounts, in vanishingly small quantities [USER: verify]
- **external** applications — bark and latex preparations reported for skin complaints [USER: verify]
- **oil and lamp fuel** from the kernel, and timber for carving

Note what that list is: a catalogue of ways to use a poison *without swallowing it*. The tradition is not squeamish; it is **informed**. Peoples who lived with this tree for millennia arrived at the correct conclusion — that its power is destructive and its correct use is at arm's length. Where an emetic or purgative use is claimed, treat it as marginal, poorly documented, and superseded by the fact that the therapeutic window between "vomiting" and "cardiac arrest" is not a window a modern practitioner should be standing in. **This codex does not endorse it.**

Modern research follows the same external logic. Indonesian work on bintaro seed extracts (According to PubMed: Aziz A et al., *BMC Res Notes*, 2025;18(1):309 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-025-07343-8)) evaluates them as **bioinsecticides** against *Aedes aegypti*, and Chinese work (According to PubMed: Deng Y et al., *Nat Prod Commun*, 2014;9(9):1265–8) established **acaricidal** contact activity of *C. manghas* methanol extracts against the citrus red mite *Panonychus citri*, isolating (−)-17β-neriifolin — a cardiac glycoside — as the active principle. The plant's twenty-first-century application is the same as its twentieth-century-BCE one: **kill something that isn't you.**

**Sacred / Shamanic & Symbolic (SS)** — [USER: verify]
The sea mango's ritual and symbolic standing across the Pacific and Indian Ocean littoral is real but under-documented in retrievable sources: it appears in ornamental and funerary planting, in narratives of ordeal and judgement, and in the ambient cultural knowledge of "the tree you do not eat from." The most robustly attested "sacred" dimension of the *Cerbera* genus is not ceremonial but **judicial** — the ordeal poison, of which more below. Specific ritual protocols, cosmological placements, or ceremonial preparations should not be asserted without primary ethnographic sources. **Flagged.**

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

Almost every entry in this codex must ask whether a doctrine survives contact with chemistry. This one is different. **Here the doctrine and the chemistry are the same statement in two languages.**

Cerberus guards a threshold that admits traffic in one direction. Cerberin is a **cardenolide** — a steroid glycoside that binds the extracellular face of the **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase**, the ion pump that every animal cell spends roughly a fifth of its resting energy budget running. That pump *is* a gate, and it *is* directional: it pushes three sodium ions out and hauls two potassium ions in, against their gradients, consuming ATP to do it. It is the machine that maintains the difference between inside and outside — the electrochemical gradient on which the resting membrane potential, and therefore every heartbeat and every nerve impulse, depends.

Cerberin jams the gate. Intracellular sodium rises; the sodium–calcium exchanger, which normally rides the sodium gradient to expel calcium, stalls and reverses; intracellular calcium accumulates. At therapeutic concentrations of a related molecule — **digoxin**, from foxglove — this is *inotropy*: a stronger contraction, and a drug that treated heart failure for two hundred years. At toxic concentrations it is **electrical chaos**: automaticity, blocks, ventricular arrhythmia, asystole. And potassium, locked out of cells by the poisoned pump, floods the serum — the **hyperkalaemia** that is the hallmark and the prognostic marker of acute cardenolide poisoning.

So the doctrine is exact. The tree named for the gatekeeper of the dead contains a molecule that poisons the gatekeeper of the cell. The Greeks did not know this — Linnaeus was reaching for a myth, not a mechanism — but the metaphor landed on the mechanism anyway, because the metaphor was chosen from the *effect*, and the effect is what the mechanism produces. **This is one of the few places in the codex where a symbolic name is a load-bearing pharmacological description.** Cerberus lets you in. He does not let you out. Neither does the pump, once cerberin is on it.

The second doctrinal layer is the **ordeal**. *Cerbera odollam* served historically as an ordeal poison — the accused swallows, and survival is acquitted as innocence. The doctrine claims the poison discriminates moral states. It does not. It discriminates **dose, kernel ripeness, gastric contents, vomiting reflex, individual potassium status, and luck.** The ordeal is a randomiser dressed as a judge. **Deflate this completely.** Any framing of *Cerbera* as a truth-revealer, a discerner of hearts, or a spiritual test is a framing that has historically killed innocent people, and the codex records it as ethnographic fact and rejects it as doctrine.

### Active Components

The toxicological principal is the **cardenolide** class, concentrated overwhelmingly in the **kernel** at the core of the fruit:

- **Cerberin** — the signature cardiac glycoside of the genus; a cardenolide with a **mechanism of action closely similar to digoxin**, such that *Cerbera* poisoning presents essentially as acute digoxin toxicity (According to PubMed: Menezes RG et al., *J Forensic Leg Med*, 2018;58:113–116 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jflm.2018.05.007))
- **17β-Neriifolin and 7α-neriifolin** — cardenolides isolated from *C. manghas* fruit; 17β-neriifolin is the acaricidal principle and the most potent of the isolated series in a leukaemia cell assay (Takase S et al., *J Nat Med*, 2023;77(1):180–187 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11418-022-01659-6); Deng Y et al., *Nat Prod Commun*, 2014;9(9):1265–8)
- **17α- and 17β-digitoxigenin β-D-glucosyl-(1→4)-α-L-thevetoside** — further cardenolide glycosides from unripe fruit (Takase et al. 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11418-022-01659-6))
- **Acetylthevetin B** — cardenolide, same source (Takase et al. 2023)

Note the recurring structure: a **digitoxigenin-type steroid aglycone** bearing sugar chains. This is the same chemical grammar as foxglove (*Digitalis*), oleander (*Nerium*), and yellow oleander (*Cascabela thevetia*) — all Apocynaceae or Plantaginaceae converging on cardenolide defence. The sugars tune potency and pharmacokinetics; the steroid core does the killing.

**Distribution matters for the cultivator.** Cardenolides are present through the plant — leaves, twigs, bark, latex, fruit — but the **kernel** is the concentrated depot and the vehicle of nearly every fatality. The methanol-extract acaricide work found activity in fruit, twigs, *and* leaves (LC₅₀ 3.39, 4.09, 4.11 g/L respectively — Deng et al. 2014), which tells you the whole tree is loaded, merely less so. There is no safe part.

**Cerberin is notoriously difficult to detect at autopsy.** This is a well-known feature of *Cerbera* forensic toxicology: it is not found on routine toxicology screens, requires targeted LC-MS methods to identify, and can therefore convert a homicide into an unexplained cardiac death on the certificate. The plant's reputation as an instrument of both suicide and homicide rests substantially on this analytical invisibility. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**There is none.** *Cerbera manghas* is excluded from every training phase, every protocol, and every preparation in the Kenetic system. It appears in this codex for four reasons and no others:

1. **Identification and avoidance.** The fruit looks edible. It is buoyant, it washes ashore, it is planted ornamentally and as a windbreak far outside its native range, and it fruits abundantly in places where children play. Knowing this tree on sight is a survival skill. Learn the milky latex, the whorled glossy leaves, the white flower with the coloured throat, the green drupe on a coastal tree. **Then do not touch the fruit.**

2. **The genus is a public-health problem.** *C. odollam* — the sibling — is called the **"suicide tree"** and is implicated in a very large number of deaths in Kerala, southern India, where it is known as *othalanga*. It is used in suicide and, the forensic literature notes plainly, in homicide (Menezes et al. 2018, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jflm.2018.05.007)). *C. manghas* carries the same cardenolide armament. **The codex names this because concealment kills.**

3. **The trophic warning — this is the detail most people never hear.** In New Caledonia, **23 cases of human poisoning, 4 of them fatal, were caused by eating coconut crabs (*Birgus latro*)** that had themselves fed on *Cerbera manghas* — the crabs sequestered the plant's cardenolides in their tissues and passed them, undiminished, to the people who ate the crabs. The treating team characterised the toxin complex and proposed **digoxin-specific antibody fragments** for life-threatening cases (According to PubMed: Maillaud C et al., *Med Sante Trop*, 2015;25(3):285–90 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1684/mst.2015.0486)). **You do not have to eat the tree to be poisoned by the tree.** This is the single most important practical fact in the entry, and it generalises: cardenolide-bearing plants can poison at one remove through the animals that browse them.

4. **It is a real oncology lead, in a laboratory, by people with a mass spectrometer.** 17β-neriifolin from unripe *C. manghas* fruit inhibits HOXA9-dependent transcription and induces apoptosis in the human acute myeloid leukaemia line THP-1 (Takase et al. 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11418-022-01659-6)). **This is a cell-line finding.** It is a legitimate reason for a medicinal chemist to be interested in this scaffold. It is not a reason for anyone to consume any part of this plant, and any source that cites the anticancer literature as a rationale for using sea mango is committing the oldest and deadliest error in herbalism: mistaking a cytotoxin's cytotoxicity for a cure.

**Cultivator's note — and this is a real one.** *C. manghas* is widely planted as an **ornamental and coastal windbreak** across the tropics, valued for salt tolerance, fast growth, dense glossy canopy, and fragrant flowers. It is also under study as a **biodiesel feedstock** and, per the research above, as a **botanical pesticide source**. A cultivator may legitimately grow it. If you do:
- **Never plant it where children, dogs, or livestock can reach fallen fruit.** The fruit is the hazard and it falls constantly.
- **Collect and destroy fallen drupes** as routine maintenance. Do not compost them into a vegetable bed.
- **Wear gloves when pruning.** The latex is irritant and the sap carries glycosides.
- **Do not burn the wood or fruit near living space** — smoke from cardenolide-bearing plants is not something to breathe.
- **Do not plant it where the fruit will drop into water that crabs, fish, or foraged shellfish inhabit.** See the coconut crab data above. This is not hypothetical.
- Interestingly, the tree is a **host to useful microbiota** — yeasts isolated from *C. manghas* (*Rhodotorula mucilaginosa*, *Aureobasidium* sp.) showed biocontrol activity against postharvest apple moulds (According to PubMed: Sukmawati D et al., *Recent Pat Food Nutr Agric*, 2020;11(3):219–228 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/2212798411666200423101159)). The tree is worth more to a grower as a phyllosphere and as a hedge than as anything to put in a body.
- It is also, charmingly, the reason wild **Goffin's cockatoos manufacture and use tool sets** — three distinct wooden tools to extract the seed matter from *Cerbera manghas* fruit, one of the few documented cases of tool-set use outside primates (According to PubMed: O'Hara M et al., *Curr Biol*, 2021;31(20):4512–4520 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.08.009)). A bird evolved a technology to open this fruit. **The bird is better equipped for it than you are.**

### Preparation & Dosing

# ⛔ NO DOSING TABLE IS PROVIDED. THIS IS DELIBERATE.

There is no dose of *Cerbera manghas* that this codex will state, imply, bracket, or gesture at. There is no "traditional range," no "start low," no "experienced practitioners only." The lethal quantity is **approximately one kernel**, the margin between effect and death is not navigable without a coronary care unit, and any number printed here would function as an instruction.

**The only preparations recorded, and recorded as history rather than method:**

| Application | Route | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Fish poison (piscicide) | Crushed fruit/bark into water | Ethnographic record. Illegal in most jurisdictions. Do not. |
| Rodenticide / insecticide | External, extract | Documented; modern research active (Aziz et al. 2025) |
| Acaricide | Contact spray, extract | Documented in citrus mite research (Deng et al. 2014) |
| Lamp oil / biodiesel | Non-medicinal | Legitimate industrial use |
| Emetic / purgative | Oral | ⛔ **Rejected.** [USER: verify] the attestation; regardless, the codex does not transmit it. |

**If exposure has occurred:** this is an emergency. Presentation mirrors acute digoxin poisoning — nausea, vomiting, hyperkalaemia, thrombocytopenia, and ECG abnormalities — and management is supportive care, atropine, temporary pacing, and consideration of **digoxin immune Fab**, whose efficacy in *Cerbera* poisoning is **variable and supported only by isolated case reports** (Menezes et al. 2018, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jflm.2018.05.007)). **Call emergency services. Bring the plant if you safely can.**

**Three honest nulls, stated plainly:**

1. **The antidote does not reliably work.** Digoxin-specific Fab is the best available intervention and the evidence for it in *Cerbera* poisoning is case reports with variable outcomes. This is not foxglove overdose in a Western ICU with a validated protocol. **There is no dependable rescue.**
2. **The anticancer signal is a cell line, and cardenolides poison all cells.** Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase is not a tumour-specific target — it is in every one of your cells. A cardenolide killing leukaemia cells in a dish is *expected*, not remarkable, and the selectivity problem is precisely why this scaffold has repeatedly failed to become a drug. Treat the HOXA9 finding as chemistry, not medicine.
3. **The traditional emetic use has no evidential support and is not defensible.** The codex flags it and refuses it.

**No cross-species laundering.** Where *C. odollam* data is cited above, it is **named as *C. odollam***. The two species share the genus, the cardenolide class, and the name *othalanga*, and *C. odollam* is the better-studied poison. They are **not the same plant** and *C. manghas*-specific data has been kept distinct throughout. Where a claim rests on *odollam*, it says so.

### Contraindications

# ⛔ ABSOLUTE — ALL ROUTES, ALL PERSONS, ALL QUANTITIES

**Internal use of any part of *Cerbera manghas* is contraindicated in every human being without exception.** There is no population for whom this is safe, no condition it treats, and no quantity that is sub-threshold in a way you can verify without an assay you do not have.

**⚠️ The fruit resembles an edible mango and is the primary vector of accidental death.** Coastal, buoyant, ornamental, widely planted, abundantly fruiting. Teach children this tree.

**⚠️ Trophic transfer is real.** Do not eat coconut crabs, or other browsers/scavengers, from areas where *C. manghas* fruits fall — 23 poisonings and 4 deaths in New Caledonia from exactly this route (Maillaud et al. 2015, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1684/mst.2015.0486)). Extend the caution to any foraged animal in the tree's fruit-fall zone.

**⚠️ Latex and sap are irritant.** Glove and eye-protect for any pruning or handling. Do not rub eyes. Wash after contact.

**⚠️ Smoke and vapour.** Do not burn wood, leaves, or fruit near occupied space.

**⚠️ Detection failure compounds risk.** Cerberin is not a routine toxicology-screen analyte. A poisoning may be missed. If someone presents with unexplained bradyarrhythmia, hyperkalaemia, and vomiting in or returning from the plant's range, **tell the clinicians about this tree.** [USER: verify the screening-evasion claim against a primary method paper.]

**⚠️ Extreme caution — cardiac medication interaction is moot but worth stating:** anyone on digoxin, or with any cardiac conduction disorder, renal impairment, or electrolyte disturbance, is at compounded risk from even incidental exposure.

**⚠️ Pregnancy, lactation, paediatric, veterinary:** absolute exclusion, as for everyone else.

**⚠️ Legal:** possession, transport, and use of *Cerbera* seed as a piscicide or poison is regulated or criminal in many jurisdictions. Preparing it with intent is a crime. This entry is written to prevent harm and confers no permission whatsoever.

**If you are reading this entry because you are in pain and looking for a way out — please stop and talk to someone.** The forensic literature on this genus exists because a great many people reached this plant at the worst moment of their lives. In the UK, call Samaritans on 116 123, free, any hour. Elsewhere, find your local crisis line at findahelpline.com. This tree is not the answer to anything.
