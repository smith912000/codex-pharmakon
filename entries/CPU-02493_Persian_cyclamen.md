# Persian cyclamen — فقلامينوس (*fuqlāmīnūs*, as printed; glossed *bakhūr Maryam*, بخور مريم)

## *Cyclamen: the tuberous root of a Cyclamen, called in the Canon bakhur Maryam; the species "Persian cyclamen" (Cyclamen persicum) is the spine's reading and is not fixed by the text* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the Arabic texts on disk. The locus is *Canon* Book II, entry *fuqlāmīnūs* (فقلامينوس; the heading is a transliteration of Greek *kuklaminos*), Arabic text line 17146 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; it runs to line 17168. It does not name Dioscorides, but it follows the English Dioscorides *kuklaminos* (2-194, line 6223 on disk) closely: the same jaundice dose with lying down covered to sweat bile-coloured sweat, the same antidote to the sea hare, the same abortifacient claims. The resemblance is mine from reading both; the Canon does not say it is quoting.

**Identity (CM).** "It is said to be *bakhūr Maryam*, a genus of *ʿarṭanīthā*" (قيل هو بخور مريم وهو جنس من العرطنيثا; lines 17147-17148). The text names no species. "Persian cyclamen" is our label (identity doubt, moderate: genus sure, species not fixed). Compare the Dioscoridean entries already filed, I_1397 and I_1398.

**Actions (CM).** "Its power is cleansing by polishing and cutting, opening and dissolving, and it is very sweat-inducing when its root is drunk, and it intoxicates" (lines 17148-17149). Jaundice: "three *mithqāl*, not to be exceeded, with boiled-down must (*ṭilāʾ*) or with *mālī qarāṭun* (my reading: Greek *melikraton*, honey-water) mixed with water cured jaundice; the patient must lie down and be covered with many cloths to sweat heavily, the sweat the colour of bile" (lines 17149-17151). Skin: the root cleanses the skin and removes the blotch (*kalaf*); the decoction helps cracks from cold (lines 17151-17152). Swellings: the root removes pustules, the juice dissolves hardness, spleen swelling and scrofula (lines 17153-17154). Wounds: the root with vinegar and honey heals wounds (lines 17154-17155). Joints: applied as a poultice it helps twisted sinew and gout (line 17156). Head: with wine it intoxicates strongly; its water is snuffed to clear the head and the decoction poured on the head quiets cold headache (lines 17156-17158). Eye: its water with honey suits water (cataract-type) in the eye and weak sight, also snuffed (lines 17158-17159). Chest: some give the root to asthmatics (line 17160). Spleen: poultice with vinegar (line 17160).

**Excretory organs, with the abortifacient claims (CM).** "Drunk with *adrūmālī* it purges phlegm and watery chyme and promotes the menses, drunk or as a pessary" (lines 17161-17162). "Some claim its fresh root causes miscarriage; if tied on the neck or arm it prevents conception" (lines 17162-17163). "It kills the fetus strongly, and its juice is stronger" (line 17164). Smeared on the navel and flanks it loosens the bowels and drops the fetus (lines 17163-17164). A prolapsed rectum is replaced by its water with vinegar (lines 17164-17165). "Five dirhams of the root with honey purge strongly" (lines 17166-17167), and "the dose is up to four *dirham*-weights" (والشربة إلى أربع درخميات; lines 17167-17168). Poisons: "drunk with wine for deadly drugs and poisons, especially the sea hare" (line 17168).

### Active Components

Cyclamen tubers are known to contain triterpenoid saponins (cyclamin) which are irritant and toxic. This is general toxicology; the Canon names no constituent, but its own words (intoxicates, purges strongly, kills the fetus) describe a strong drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The entry gives drastic purgative, abortifacient and intoxicating uses and a single antidote claim; none is a recommended practice. Cyclamen is a toxic plant, and the abortifacient statements are recorded as history, plainly, not as guidance.

### Preparation & Dosing

As the text gives them: jaundice, three *mithqāl* with boiled-down must or *mālī qarāṭun* (honey-water, my reading), lying covered to sweat (lines 17149-17151); purge, five dirhams of the root with honey (line 17167); "the dose up to four *dirham*-weights" (line 17168). The two doses are not reconciled in the text and are not converted here. Other forms: poultice, juice, decoction, snuff, oil warmed in the hollowed root (lines 17152-17153). [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No temperament degree is printed in this entry. Printed qualities: cleansing, cutting, opening, dissolving, strongly sweat-inducing (*Canon* Book II, entry *fuqlāmīnūs*, lines 17148-17149).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* Cyclamen in the Canon is a strong evacuant, a poultice and a poison-antidote. It carries no cultivation status, and nothing in the entry concerns the vital faculty.

**Practitioner's note.** Not a cultivation substance and not a drug to experiment with. The text describes violent purging, intoxication and miscarriage, which is the warning.

### Contraindications

Toxic. Cyclamen tuber is poisonous and the text itself records purging, intoxication and killing the fetus; pregnancy is an absolute exclusion. The jaundice treatment by forced sweating and the purgative doses are historical, not advice. Do not use. The antidote claim for poisons is the Canon's, unverified. [USER: verify]
