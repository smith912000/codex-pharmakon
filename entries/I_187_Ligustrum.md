# I-187 · Ligustrum
**Ligustrum lucidum** W.T.Aiton | Oleaceae

---

## Section 1 — Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Accepted name:** *Ligustrum lucidum* W.T.Aiton (1812)  
**Common names (English):** Glossy privet, Chinese privet, broad-leaved privet, white wax tree  
**Traditional names:**
- **Nü Zhen Zi (女貞子):** "Chaste Lady Seed/Fruit" — from legend of a chaste woman who died rather than submit to forced marriage; found growing at her grave; encodes the plant's cooling, stabilizing, yin-nourishing character
- Japanese: *Nezumimochi*; Korean: *Yeong-jeong*
- Fruit (*Fructus Ligustri Lucidi*) is the pharmacopoeial drug

**Etymology note:**
- *Ligustrum* = Latin name from Pliny for privet, possibly from *ligare* (to bind) — flexible branches used in basketry
- *lucidum* = Latin = shining/glossy — leaf surface characteristic

**Species note:**
- *L. lucidum*: large evergreen tree/shrub; primary Chinese pharmacopoeial species; shiny leaves
- *L. japonicum*: Japanese privet; similar; smaller fruit
- *L. sinense*: Chinese privet; smaller; more shrub-like; NOT the pharmacopoeial species
- *L. ovalifolium* (California/oval-leaf privet): European/North American ornamental; NOT medicinal; higher toxic ligustroside
- Common garden privet (*L. vulgare*, European): toxic (galactose-binding lectin); AVOID internal use

**Botanical morphology:**
- Evergreen tree or large shrub to 10m; glossy, opposite, ovate leaves; panicles of white flowers (June–July); clusters of black-purple berries (fruit = drupe, 8–10mm); ripened late autumn-winter
- Fruit turns black/blue-black when ripe; harvested October–December; dried or processed with wine or honey

**Part used:** Ripe dried fruit (*Fructus Ligustri Lucidi*); sometimes processed with yellow wine (wine-steaming = *jiu zhi*) to enhance tonic properties and reduce cooling nature

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**The black fruit — yin tonic correspondence:**

In TCM Five-Phase theory, black color corresponds to the Kidney organ system (Water phase). Black-fruited plants (Ligustrum, Morus nigra, Lycium — also known as goji berry, and Eclipta) are collectively categorized as Liver-Kidney yin tonics — the "three black" or "black supplement" herbs. This color-organ system correspondence is a theoretical framework (Five-Phase theory) applied to botanical observation; the question is whether it has pharmacological correlate.

Modern findings: Oleuropein and oleanolic acid from Ligustrum lucidum have measurable nephroprotective effects in animal models. The kidney attribution has partial pharmacological support. The convergence of observable black-fruit color, theoretical kidney correspondence, and measurable nephroprotective mechanism is an interesting case of traditional theory partially predicting pharmacological action through a different pathway than intended.

**Premature graying — the most culturally specific traditional claim:**

The traditional use of Nü Zhen Zi for premature hair graying (early whitening/silver hair = kidney deficiency in TCM) represents a highly specific traditional claim. Modern pharmacology found: oleanolic acid modulates tyrosinase activity in melanocytes; melanin synthesis has demonstrable connection to kidney tubular cell function via shared biochemical pathways. The pharmacological connection is indirect but not absent — more nuanced than simple coincidence.

**Oleuropein across Oleaceae:**

The primary active compound class (oleuropein, oleanolic acid, ursolic acid) occurs throughout Oleaceae — the same family as olive (*Olea europaea*). Olive leaf's cardioprotective oleuropein and Ligustrum fruit's oleuropein are chemically identical; this represents chemical convergence within the plant family that bridges the East Asian and Mediterranean traditions at the molecular level. When olive leaf supplementation studies cite oleuropein as the active, they are investigating the same compound found in Nü Zhen Zi — pharmacological convergence through shared family chemistry.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Primary active compounds:**

**1. Iridoid Glycosides (Primary actives)**
- **Oleuropein** (secoiridoid glycoside): primary active compound; 0.5–4.0% dry weight of fruit
  - Same compound as in olive leaf (*Olea europaea*); antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, cardioprotective
  - Oleuropein → oleuropein aglycone (GI) → hydroxytyrosol + elenolic acid
  - Antioxidant (DPPH IC50 ~15 μM, comparable to vitamin E)
- **Nüzhenide** (oleuropein analog, ligustrum-specific): immunomodulatory
- **Ligustroside** (similar to oleuropein): antioxidant; lower concentration than oleuropein

**2. Triterpenoids**
- **Oleanolic acid** (aglycone): 0.5–1.5%; anti-inflammatory; hepatoprotective; anticancer screening
- **Ursolic acid**: isomer of oleanolic acid; same pharmacological profile; significant content
- These pentacyclic triterpenes are widespread across traditional hepatoprotective herbs (also in lemon balm, thyme, rosemary)

**3. Polysaccharides**
- Ligustrum polysaccharides (LPS): immunomodulatory; TLR4 agonism
- 15–25% dry weight

**4. Flavonoids**
- Quercetin-3-O-glucoside, kaempferol derivatives: antioxidant
- Luteolin, apigenin: anti-inflammatory
- Neolignan-type phenolics unique to Ligustrum

**5. Special Compounds**
- **Salidroside** (also in Rhodiola!): phenylpropanoid glycoside; adaptogenic; found in both Ligustrum and Rhodiola rosea — rare cross-species chemical link between a Chinese privet and an Arctic adaptogen; supports the "black/alpine yin-tonic" functional category
- Phenylethanoid glycosides: acteoside/verbascoside (also in plantain, mullein, comfrey — see I-178, I-179)

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**1. Antioxidant and Free Radical Scavenging (Oleuropein)**
- Oleuropein + hydroxytyrosol: direct ROS scavenging (DPPH, ABTS assays)
- Nrf2 pathway activation → upregulation of HO-1, NQO1, GPx, catalase
- Inhibition of LDL oxidation (cardiovascular relevance)
- Reduction of 8-OHdG (DNA oxidative damage marker)

**2. Anti-inflammatory (Oleanolic Acid, Ursolic Acid)**
- COX-1/COX-2 inhibition: prostaglandin synthesis reduction
- NF-κB pathway inhibition: reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines
- 5-LOX inhibition: leukotriene reduction
- These mechanisms parallel NSAIDs but at much lower potency; selectivity different

**3. Immunomodulatory (LPS Polysaccharides, Nüzhenide)**
- LPS → TLR4 agonism → macrophage activation, NK cell enhancement
- Nüzhenide: specific modulatory effects on T-cell proliferation
- Cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression reversal in animal models

**4. Hepatoprotective (Oleanolic Acid Primary)**
- CCl4-induced hepatotoxicity reduction in animal models
- Lipid peroxidation reduction in hepatocytes
- Hepatic GSH maintenance under toxic challenge
- Mechanism: antioxidant + mitochondrial protection (parallel to schisandrin B)

**5. Nephroprotective (Oleuropein, Oleanolic Acid)**
- Reduces cisplatin-induced nephrotoxicity in animal models
- Renal tubular cell protection from oxidative injury
- Traditional kidney-tonic attribution has partial mechanistic basis

**6. Anti-tumor Activity (Oleanolic Acid, Ursolic Acid)**
- Oleanolic acid: multiple mechanisms — apoptosis induction (p53-dependent and independent), cell cycle arrest (G1/S), anti-angiogenic
- Ursolic acid: STAT3 signaling inhibition; VEGF reduction
- These are in vitro and animal data; no clinical oncology evidence

**7. Tyrosinase Modulation (Hair Pigmentation)**
- Oleanolic acid: modulates melanocyte tyrosinase activity
- Partial mechanistic basis for traditional hair-graying indication
- No human RCT data for hair pigmentation

**8. Salidroside Contribution (Adaptogenic)**
- Shared mechanism with Rhodiola (I-entry): AMPK activation, HIF-1α stabilization
- Mild adaptogenic support to primary anti-inflammatory/antioxidant activity

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Chemotherapy support (best human evidence):**

- Zhang QH et al (1997) *Chung Kuo Chung Hsi I Chieh Ho Tsa Chih* 17(6):359–62: Ligustrum-based formula for chemotherapy-induced leukopenia in cancer patients; N=60; significant leukocyte recovery vs. control
- Multiple Chinese clinical studies (variably designed): Ligustrum extracts as adjuncts to chemotherapy for leukopenia; consistent positive findings; Western standards generally not met for RCT quality

**Hepatoprotection:**
- Chinese clinical literature: oleanolic acid injection/oral for chronic hepatitis; transaminase normalization; moderate evidence; some randomized studies
- Oleanolic acid tablets: registered as hepatoprotective agent in China (pharmaceutical use)

**Immune function:**
- Animal: robust; human: limited
- Consistent with TLR4-mediated macrophage activation mechanism

**Cardiovascular (oleuropein — from olive/Ligustrum comparison):**
- Olive leaf standardized oleuropein: modest antihypertensive effects in human RCTs (Susalit et al 2011 *Phytomedicine*: 500mg/day vs. captopril; non-inferior for mild hypertension); data from *Olea europaea* but same compound as in Ligustrum
- Direct Ligustrum fruit human cardiovascular data: sparse

**Regulatory:**
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020: official drug
- EMA/Commission E: not assessed
- Used as ingredient in numerous commercial Chinese patent medicines

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The Legend of the Chaste Lady (Nü Zhen):**

The name Nü Zhen Zi (女貞子) preserves a story from the Han dynasty: a young woman (nü zhen = "chaste lady" or "woman of integrity") was forced to remarry after her husband's death. Rather than comply, she died of grief, and this plant grew from her grave. The plant's attributes — evergreen (persisting through winter without losing leaves = constancy), black fruit (kidney-yin correspondence = replenishing what grief depletes), cooling nature — are all encoded in the legend. The pharmacological "grief depletes kidney-liver yin → Nü Zhen Zi replenishes" is a coherent narrative framework for the traditional stress-restorative use, even if the mechanism is antioxidant-anti-inflammatory rather than yin-nourishing in any literal sense.

**The Three Black Herbs:**

In classical TCM, Ligustrum, Eclipta prostrata (Han Lian Cao), and Morus nigra (Sang Shen, black mulberry) form the "three black tonics" for liver-kidney yin deficiency — standardly combined in the formula *Er Zhi Wan* (二至丸, "Reaching the Two Solstices Pill"). This formula, documented since the Ming dynasty (1368–1644), uses Nü Zhen Zi (winter harvest, winter solstice) and Han Lian Cao (summer harvest, summer solstice) — the two solstice-harvested herbs creating a yin-replenishing pair. The seasonal harvest encoding (maximum yin in winter = most yin-replenishing herb) is a pharmacological-astrological classification system.

**Oleanolic acid as pharmaceutical:**

Oleanolic acid, one of the primary active compounds in Ligustrum, was developed as a registered hepatoprotective pharmaceutical in China (1983, Shanghai Institute of Materia Medica). The traditional Nü Zhen Zi → isolation of active compound → pharmaceutical registration pathway took approximately 20 years. Oleanolic acid tablets are still prescribed in Chinese hospital hepatology for viral hepatitis with transaminase elevation — one of the more complete traditional → pharmaceutical translation pathways in the Codex.

**Invasive tree problem:**

In the southeastern United States, *L. lucidum* and related privets became highly invasive species (Category I invasive in Florida, significant in Georgia, Alabama). The same fruit that provides Nü Zhen Zi spread throughout American hardwood forests via bird dispersal, replacing native understory species. Traditional Chinese medicine's premier kidney-tonic berry is an ecological threat in the American South — the same plant, two completely different cultural relationships with the same organism.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

**Primary indications (TCM framework, evidence-supported):**
- Liver-kidney yin deficiency: premature graying, blurred vision, dizziness, lower back weakness, tinnitus
- Chemotherapy support: leukopenia, immune reconstitution
- Chronic fatigue with inflammatory component
- Hepatoprotective adjunct (chronic hepatitis, elevated transaminases)

**Dosing:**
- **Standard dose:** 6–12g dried fruit as decoction daily
- **Wine-processed (jiu zhi) form:** Traditional for enhanced absorption and reduced cold nature; preferred in clinical TCM
- **Standardized extract:** 500–1,000mg/day (standardized to oleuropein ≥5%)
- **Duration:** Extended tonic use; 4–12 weeks minimum; continuous months for yin-deficiency patterns
- **Er Zhi Wan formula:** Combined with Eclipta prostrata (1:1 ratio) as classic preparation

⚠️ **DRUG INTERACTIONS:**
- **Warfarin:** Oleuropein antioxidant activity may affect platelet function; monitor INR
- **Cyclosporine, tacrolimus:** Immunomodulatory; less interaction risk than astragalus but theoretical
- **Hepatotoxic drugs:** Hepatoprotective (additive protective effect may be beneficial in drug-induced hepatotoxicity — emerging research)
- **Antihypertensives:** Oleuropein mild antihypertensive → additive; monitor blood pressure

⚠️ **CAUTIONS:**
- **Spleen-stomach cold/weakness (TCM: loose stools, cold limbs):** Cold-natured herb; avoid or combine with warming digestive herbs (ginger, Atractylodes)
- **Pregnancy:** Traditional caution; insufficient safety data; avoid
- **Diarrhea:** Can aggravate loose stools (cold nature); discontinue if GI worsening
- European privet (*L. vulgare*): DO NOT USE for internal medicine; contains higher concentrations of toxic glycosides (syringoside); only *L. lucidum* fruit is the pharmacopoeial drug

**Standardization:**
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020: minimum 0.5% nüzhenide + G13 + specnuezhenide (sum of specified iridoid glycosides)
- Commercial: specify oleuropein content ≥5% in standardized extracts
- Wine-processing (jiu zhi): modifies thermal nature (reduces cold property); preferred for patients with weak digestion

**Energetic tier:** ◈ Jing (structural/constitutional) — operates at the Jing/yin level; constitutional replenishment of liver-kidney essence; addresses structural deficiency states (premature aging, exhaustion, graying) at the deepest tissue-essence level
