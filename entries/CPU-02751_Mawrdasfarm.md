# Mawrdasfarm — مورداسفرم (Avicenna, *Canon* Book II)

## *Mūrdāsfaram: a plant with flowers and thin stems, grey and yellow, whose identity the Canon reports as disputed (wild myrtle, or a Roman ʿafār). No identification is adopted; "wild myrtle?" in the spine name is the first of the reported opinions only (confidence low)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The entry is *mūrdāsfaram* (*Canon* Book II, Arabic text lines 15335-15341).

*What it is.* زهر وقضبان دقاق منفركة إلى الغبرة والصفرة وقوته كالباذاورد عند بعضهم (15336-15337): "flowers and thin stems, [tending] to dustiness and yellowness, and its power is like *bādhāward* according to some". The word منفركة is not clear in the e-text. There are varieties more inclined to whiteness and more inclined to yellowness (15337-15338).

*Identity, as the Canon reports it.* قال ابن ماسة: هو الآس البري (15338): "Ibn Māsa said: it is the wild myrtle". وقال الآخرون: إنه عفار رومي (15338-15339): "and the others said: it is Roman *ʿafār*". The text then begins قال ابن ماسرجويه: إنه (15339): "Ibn Māsarjawayh said: it is", and breaks off into the next heading's text, so his opinion is lost in this e-text. [USER: verify against another witness.] *ʿAfār* is a gall-bearing plant name whose modern botanical meaning is not settled here. [USER: verify]

*Temperament.* الطبع: حار يابس في الثانية (15339): "hot and dry in the second [degree]".

*Head.* نافع للصرع والرطوبات في الدماغ (15340): "useful for epilepsy and the moistures in the brain". An OCR marker (`ms0559`) sits inside the line.

*Digestive organs.* يقوي المعدة والكبد وينفع من السقطة على الأحشاء (15340-15341): "strengthens the stomach and liver and benefits a fall upon the viscera".

*Excretory organs.* يحتمل لديلان المقعدة (15341): "is inserted as a suppository for the *dīlān* of the anus"; *dīlān* is not a clear word in the e-text and is not translated. [USER: verify]

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

The Canon's entry places this plant, hot and dry in the second degree, mainly on the head and the digestive organs. For the head it says it is useful for epilepsy and for moistures in the brain. For the digestive organs it says it strengthens the stomach and liver and benefits a fall upon the viscera. Its only named form for the excretory organs is a suppository, inserted for the dilan of the anus, a word the e-text leaves unclear. Some hold its power is like that of badhaward. The entry notes varieties tending to whiteness and to yellowness. Its identity is disputed within the passage: Ibn Masa says wild myrtle, others say Roman afar, and the third opinion breaks off. The Codex records these as the Canon's claims, not established effects, and does not adopt an identification, so no organ action is attributed to a particular plant.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** Disputed even in the Canon; do not substitute myrtle or any other plant. [USER: verify]
- **Epilepsy.** A medical condition; do not replace treatment with an unidentified plant.
