# Jatropha glandulifera — dur.byid, Dhurji (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Jatropha glandulifera, the first of five identifications Clark 1995 prints for dur.byid (the others are Baliospermum montanum, Iris dichotoma, Euphorbia adenochlora and Euphorbia fischeriana)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi, read in two modern editions.** The editions are Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, both in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. They are two translations of one tradition's text and are not two independent traditions. Only Clark prints *Jatropha glandulifera* (line 1133; the Latin-name index, line 1602, prints it as "Jatropha grandulifera"). A search of Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 for "Jatropha" found nothing. A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Sushruta texts on disk for the genus name found nothing. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (line 211) the Tibetan name *dur.byid* is recorded at Clark line 1133 with the identification "Jatropha glandulifera; Baliospermum montanum (Physic Nut); Iris dichotoma; Euphorbia adenochlora; Euphorbia fischeriana" and the note "more than one Latin name given; key uses the first". The key, *Jatropha glandulifera*, is therefore the first of the five names Clark prints, and not a later one. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance".

**Identity doubt.** Four of the five names are plants of the spurge family as the Codex understands it (*Jatropha*, *Baliospermum* and two *Euphorbia*; not checked against a flora), and the fifth, *Iris dichotoma*, is an iris, which belongs to a different family. The Codex cannot say which plant the Tibetan texts meant. The entry stands for the Tibetan drug and not for the plant. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1133).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, within "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (heading at line 997). It reads:

"dur.byid: Jatropha glandulifera: Baliospermum montanum (Physic Nut): Iris dichotoma: Euphorbia adenochlora: Euphorbia fischeriana: [has ten names, for example tri.byi.ta. It has a single root, horizontally protruding petioles, a green leaf, reddish-brown four-petalled flower, and rounded, three-sided fruit. The inferior type is without fruit]."

Clark's preface (line 82) says that interpolations in square brackets come from the Blue Aquamarine and Spreading Tree commentaries. Everything after the five Latin names is inside the brackets, so it is all commentary layer: the number of names, the root, the petioles, the leaf, the flower and the fruit. The line has no root-text sentence of its own, no taste, no power, no part used and no action. The Latin names, and the parenthesis "(Physic Nut)" beside the second, are Clark's identifications and not the root text's.

*The next line.* Line 1134 is *thar.nu* ("Euphorbia pallassii: Baliospermum sinulatum: Euphorbia wallichiana: Croton tiglium"), and it ends: "Jatropha and Euphorbia purge all hot and cold disorders." Clark uses "Jatropha" and "Euphorbia" there as the names of the two neighbouring drugs, *dur.byid* and *thar.nu*, and this is the nearest sentence she prints to an action for this drug. It is not a separate sentence for *dur.byid*. The Men-Tsee-Khang running text agrees on the pair and the order (see below).

*Clark 1995, chapter 21.* In "21. The Principles of Medicines: Compounding of Medicines" (heading at line 1205), line 1225 reads: "The category of medicines inducing purgation includes (long-tipped) Chebulic myrobalan, Castor Oil seed, Cassia pods, Euphorbia sp., Physic nut (Baliospermum montanum), Euphorbia pallassii, Rheum emodi (a type of rhubarb), Lactuca dissecta (a type of larkspur), Physalis peruviana, Rheum nobile, Stellera chamaejasme and chhu.rtsa (Rheum spiciforme)." The word "Jatropha" is not in this list. The "Physic nut (Baliospermum montanum)" in it uses the same parenthesis as line 1133, and the Codex reads this as the same drug under another of its names; the text does not say so. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text.* The text prints "Dhurji", with no Latin name. In chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225), within the run headed "To reveal the efficacy of herbal medicines" (line 3428), line 3495 reads: "Dhurji and Tharnu purge all forms of hot and cold disorders." In chapter 21, "The Group and the Method of Compounding Medicine" (contents, line 229), line 3661 reads: "Arura, Dhenda, Dhong ga, Shri khenda, Dhurji, Thamu, Chum tsa, Ngonbu, Tronbu, Tsi takmo, Rechagpa and Chhu tsa are the group of medicines that are purgatives." The index lists "Dhurji 211,223" (line 7337). That the "Dhurji" of these lines is Clark's *dur.byid* rests on the order (*dur.byid*, *thar.nu*; "Dhurji and Tharnu") and on the glossary's "dur byid" transliteration (line 5206, "dur byid sgron shing"). [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary.* The "D" block (lines 5179 to 5261) prints "Dhurji" as the twelfth phonetic name (counting line 5183's two names separately), and the twelfth scientific name is "Euphorbia helioscopia" (line 5235, English "Sun spurge"). The glossary therefore sets a different binomial beside the Tibetan name than any Clark prints for it, and has no *Jatropha*. The pairing is by position and is treated in I_1274. The Codex does not choose between the two texts. [USER: verify]

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Jatropha*, *dur.byid*, *Dhurji* and Clark line 1133 found no entry on *Jatropha glandulifera* under that key. Three entries treat the same Tibetan name or passage: I_106 (Fischer's Spurge, *Euphorbia fischeriana*, tags FMT and TCM) has an FMT paragraph on *dur.byid* that lists the same five Clark binomials and reports the *thar.nu* sentence of line 1134; I_1274 (*Euphorbia helioscopia*, tag FMT) is the glossary-by-position entry on "Dhurji"; and I_1276 (*Euphorbia wallichii*, tag FMT) is the entry on the neighbouring *thar.nu*. I_1108 (*Iris ensata*) mentions *Iris dichotoma* under *dur.byid* only in passing. There is no entry on *Baliospermum montanum* or on *Iris dichotoma* or *Euphorbia adenochlora* as such. Nothing is merged; the same Tibetan drug is therefore treated under three binomials in three entries.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general fact that *Jatropha* is a genus of the spurge family (Euphorbiaceae), that members of that family commonly carry a latex, and that the seeds and latex of some *Jatropha* species are known to be toxic and strongly purgative. The sources read do not say which part of the Tibetan drug was used, and with five candidate plants no general statement about the drug's constituents is made.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts group this drug with *thar.nu*: "Jatropha and Euphorbia purge all hot and cold disorders" (Clark, line 1134), "Dhurji and Tharnu purge all forms of hot and cold disorders" (Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3495), and it is in the Men-Tsee-Khang purgative group (line 3661). A purgative of this kind is not a self-treatment, and the plant behind the name is uncertain. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** No taste, power or post-digestive taste is printed for *dur.byid* or "Dhurji" in the lines read; the commentary at Clark's line 1133 describes only the plant's appearance. The action printed, for the pair, is purging all hot and cold disorders (Clark, line 1134; Men-Tsee-Khang, line 3495). Clark's *thar.nu* commentary (line 1134) gives "hot taste" for its own Euphorbia, which is the neighbouring drug and is not carried over to this one.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica gives this drug only by its place with its neighbour: both purge hot and cold disorders, and chapter 21 and the Men-Tsee-Khang text list purgatives among the groups of medicines. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. A search of both Tibetan texts for the drug's names found it only in the lines cited above, the glossary and the indexes, and not in the rejuvenation material; no line read places it in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice. Its framing is therapeutic only, and it attaches to the Tibetan name, not securely to this binomial.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a purgative in the Tibetan texts, with no preparation and no dose, and the plant behind the Tibetan name is uncertain. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning for this drug. It is recorded as a purgative, and strong purgatives can cause serious harm; the plants among Clark's five candidates include spurges, whose latex is irritant in general. No preparation or dose is given, and no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Because the identity is uncertain, a plant could not be matched to the Tibetan name from this entry in any case. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read.
