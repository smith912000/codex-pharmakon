# Sairiyaka — Sairiyaka, "the two kinds of Sairiyaka" (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *A drug printed "the two kinds of Sairiyaka" in the Varuna'di group and as one of five thorny shrubs in the Pancha-kantaka group; the translator's chapter text gives no gloss. Identification: unresolved. The first-pass guess (Barleria prionitis, CPU-01827) is not supported by anything read: the Vol. III appendix lines read do not pair this name with a Latin binomial. A spelling "Saireyaka" occurs elsewhere in the same translation and may be the same name, but the text does not say so. Confidence: low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The plant is not identified. This entry is a historical record only. Nothing in it is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of the name alone.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this transliteration.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. The first-pass check on 30 October suggested "possibly *Barleria prionitis*, CPU-01827"; that suggestion is not adopted, because no line read supports it. A search of all the Sushruta text on disk (the chapter files under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and Bhishagratna's three volumes) for *Sairiyaka* found the two chapter 38 lists below and nothing else; a search for the spelling *Saireyaka* found four more places, described under the spelling variant.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Chapter 38 of the *Sutrasthana* is the chapter "which deals with the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam)" and says that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (Vol. I lines 15343 to 15350; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3 to 9). The drug is placed there by group, and a group's virtue is stated for the group as a whole.

1. **Varuna'di group.** (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 15385 to 15391; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 44 to 50). Bhishagratna prints: "The drugs known as Varuna, Artagala, Shigru, Madhu-Shigru, Tarkari, Mesha-Shringi, Putika, Naktamala, Morata, Agnimantha, the two kinds of Sairiyaka, Vimvi, Vasuka, Vasira, Chitraka, Shatavari, Bilva, Ajashringi, Darbha, and the two kinds of Vrihati form the group known as the Varunadi." (The OCR prints "Xaktamala" and "Mlva" for Naktamala and Bilva; the OCR forms are not corrected where a line is quoted elsewhere in the Codex.) The metrical text says that the group "is possessed of the efficacy of reducing the deranged Kapham and fat and proves efficacious in cases of cephalaegia, Gulma and internal abscesses" (lines 52 to 55). "The two kinds" is the translator's wording; the text read does not name the two kinds or say how they differ.
2. **Pancha-kantaka group.** (Vol. I lines 15860 to 15863; `text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 518 to 522). "The group consisting of the five medicinal (thorny) shrubs known as Karamradda, Trikantaka, Sairiyaka, Shatavari, and Gridhranakhi, is called the Pancha-Kantaka." Here the name stands once, as one of five. The metrical text that follows is said of this group and of the Valli-Panchamulam group before it: they "prove curative in Haemoptysis and in all the three types of anasarca or oedema (Shopha)", have "the incontestable virtue of arresting all sorts of urethral discharges" and are "a potent remedy in all cases of seminal disorders" (lines 524 to 529). That is the virtue of two groups together, not of this drug alone. The thorny description given to the five shrubs is the only plant description found, and it is given for the group.

**The spelling variant "Saireyaka".** The same translation prints *Saireyaka* in four other places. It is named among drugs pasted into an oil (*Chikitsasthana* ch.3, `text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch03.txt` line 346; Vol. II line 13269), in a plaster pasted with she-goat's milk (ch.5, line 316; Vol. II line 13950), and in a list of drugs from which oils may be prepared (ch.15, line 303; Vol. II line 18270). In *Kalpasthana* ch.8, line 57 (Vol. II line 31740) *Saireyaka* is the name of one of thirteen families of insects, which is a different thing and is mentioned only to prevent confusion. Whether the plant *Saireyaka* is the same drug as *Sairiyaka* is not stated in any line read; the spellings are close, and no more is claimed. The *Saireyaka* passages are given here as leads for a later reader, not as attestation of this row. [USER: verify]

**The appendix.** A search of Bhishagratna's Vol. III appendix of names for *Sairiyaka*, *Saireyaka* and *Sairey* found no line. The guess of *Barleria prionitis* therefore has no support in the appendix columns read.

The group lists are the only places found that place the drug under this spelling. No description of the plant, no part used, and no *rasa* or potency was found.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Unidentified.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Varuna'di group: reducing deranged Kapha and fat, and serving in headache, abdominal lumps (*Gulma*) and internal abscesses (group-level). As a member of the Pancha-kantaka group: with the Valli-Panchamulam, serving in haemoptysis, in the three kinds of swelling, in urethral discharges and in seminal disorders (group-level).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no part used, no taste or potency for this drug on its own, no account of the two kinds, and no identification.

The Codex gives no protocol and recommends no plant for any of the conditions named.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given for this drug under the spelling *Sairiyaka*. The groups are classifications of drugs by shared virtue; the text read here (chapter 38) does not say how any member is prepared. The oils and plaster that name *Saireyaka* (ch.3, 5, 15) are given in the translation as drug combinations, and their quantities, if any, are not carried over to this row, because the identity of the two spellings is open. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for this drug in the lines found. The qualities given are those of whole groups: the Varuna'di group reduces Kapha and fat (Vol. I lines 15393 to 15395), and the Pancha-kantaka group is spoken of with its neighbour as arresting discharges (lines 15864 to 15869). No such classification is imported for Sairiyaka itself.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in two chapter 38 group lists, which classify drugs by the use of the whole group (*Dravya-Sangrahaniya*). Chapter 38 is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns this drug no cultivation status; it carries a therapeutic one only, and only at the level of its group. A search of the Sushruta chapter files found no occurrence of the name *Sairiyaka* outside chapter 38, and none of the spelling *Saireyaka* in *Chikitsasthana* ch.26 to 30 (the vitality and rejuvenation chapters). The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it.

**Practitioner's note.** An unidentified name in two group lists, with two kinds the text does not tell apart. Nothing supports cultivation use, and without an identification nothing can be gathered or used. A cultivator reads it as a name in a record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified, and it is not established that *Sairiyaka* and *Saireyaka* are one drug. Do not substitute any herb for it. Do not take the Barleria suggestion as an identification.
- **The conditions named.** Headache, abdominal lumps, abscesses, haemoptysis, swellings and discharges are named for the groups. These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Group membership.** The drug is one of many in its groups, and a group's virtues are not safety statements about any one member. The Pancha-kantaka group is described as thorny shrubs.
