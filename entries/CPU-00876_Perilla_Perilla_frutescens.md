# Perilla — Shiso · Zi Su · Beefsteak Plant · Egoma · Purple Mint

## *Perilla frutescens (L.) Britton* | Family: Lamiaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SS, BM
**Energetic Classification:** Warm and dry (2nd degree) — pungent, aromatic, dispersing
**Training Phase:** Phase II — Dispersal & Surface-Release (opening the exterior, settling the descending current)

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Shennong / Chinese Materia Medica (CM):** Perilla enters the classical Chinese pharmacopoeia as two distinct drugs drawn from one plant. The leaf, **Zi Su Ye** (紫蘇葉), is catalogued among the pungent-warm exterior-releasing herbs: it "releases the exterior, disperses cold, promotes the movement of qi, and harmonises the middle." It is the archetypal remedy for early wind-cold with cough and nasal congestion, and — uniquely — a corrective for seafood-crab poisoning, taken as a decoction after eating shellfish. The seed, **Zi Su Zi** (紫蘇子), is a separate entry among the cough-and-wheeze descending herbs, used to "direct qi downward, arrest cough, calm wheezing, and moisten the intestines." The stem, **Zi Su Geng**, is a milder qi-mover favoured in pregnancy-related nausea and chest oppression. The plant's Japanese career as *shiso* (culinary purple leaf) and *egoma* (oil seed) preserved the same warming, aromatic, digestive identity.

**Sushruta / Ayurvedic stream (SS):** Perilla travelled the East-Asian trade routes into the Indian materia medica as an aromatic *dipana-pachana* (digestive-kindling) seed and leaf, grouped with the pungent Lamiaceae carminatives. Its warming, *vata*- and *kapha*-reducing profile and its use for cough, cold, and sluggish digestion mirror the Chinese reading closely — a rare instance of two independent traditions converging on the same energetic verdict.

**rGyud-bzhi / Tibetan-Himalayan stream (BM):** In the Tibetan and broader Himalayan herbal traditions Perilla seed (a pressed-oil crop across the region) is used as a warming, phlegm-dispersing and digestive agent, consistent with the "cold-disorder" corrective logic of the *Four Tantras*.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** The name *Zi Su* means "purple that restores" — *su* (蘇) is the same character used for "revival." Classical commentators tied the deep anthocyanin purple of the leaf to its power to "revive" a body sinking under cold, damp, and toxic food. Modern phytochemistry gives that symbolism a literal footing: the same anthocyanins and rosmarinic-acid polyphenols that colour the leaf are the antioxidant, mast-cell-stabilising fraction that blunts the histamine "revolt" of an allergic or food-reactive body. The doctrine of a purple that calms an over-reacting system reads, in retrospect, as an accurate folk-encoding of anti-inflammatory pharmacology.

### Active Components

- **Rosmarinic acid** — the dominant polyphenol of the leaf; antioxidant, mast-cell-stabilising, anti-allergic and α-glucosidase-inhibiting.
- **Perillaldehyde (perilla aldehyde)** — the principal monoterpene of the essential oil; responsible for the characteristic aroma, with antimicrobial, prokinetic and antidepressant-like activity. Also the main contact allergen.
- **Luteolin, apigenin** and other flavonoids — anti-inflammatory, antioxidant.
- **Anthocyanins** (shisonin and related) — the purple pigments of red-leaf cultivars.
- **Perilla ketone (egoma ketone)** and the isomer **isoegomaketone** — furan-type ketones; a pulmonary toxicant in livestock (see Contraindications).
- **α-Linolenic acid–rich seed oil**, triterpenes (tormentic acid), phytosterols, tocopherols.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Perilla is a **Phase II dispersal-and-descent** agent with two complementary vectors. As leaf (Zi Su Ye) it opens the surface — useful for the earliest chilled, congested, "coming-down-with-something" state, and for settling a stomach turned by cold or suspect food. As seed (Zi Su Zi) it turns the current downward: a moistening antitussive-and-laxative for the dry, upward-rebelling cough with constipation. Across both forms the modern rationale is anti-allergic and anti-inflammatory: Perilla is a practical adjunct where surface irritation, rhinitis, or a reactive gut dominate the picture. It is a food-grade herb, positioning it as a gentle daily-use tonic rather than a heroic intervention.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the anti-allergic reputation of Perilla leaf is supported by mechanistic and animal work. Oh et al. showed that a 30% ethanol extract of *Perilla frutescens* var. *acuta* and its component rosmarinic acid reduced IgE, histamine, and pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α) and inhibited NF-κB and caspase-1 in ovalbumin-sensitised mice, ameliorating allergic rhinitis and rhinoconjunctivitis ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1258/ebm.2010.010252)). Zhu et al. isolated a rosmarinic-acid-enriched Perilla-leaf extract that combined DPPH radical scavenging, mast-cell (β-hexosaminidase) anti-allergic activity (IC50 ≈ 53 µg/mL), and α-glucosidase inhibition, flagging dual anti-allergy and anti-diabetic potential ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/jf404318j)). A standardised, cell-culture-derived Perilla phytocomplex enriched in rosmarinic acid preserved epithelial barrier proteins and downregulated cytokine release via JNK signalling in human keratinocyte and mucosal models (Pressi et al., [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/pharmaceutics15010240)). A comprehensive review by Ahmed catalogues 271 identified constituents and the plant's antioxidant, antimicrobial, anti-allergic, antidepressant and anti-inflammatory activities, while stressing that clinical trials remain insufficient ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules24010102)).

**Evidence grade:** *Moderate–preclinical.* Consistent in vitro and animal support for the anti-allergic and anti-inflammatory actions; robust chemistry; controlled human trials still sparse. Traditional digestive and exterior-releasing uses are **traditional-grade**.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** *Zi Su Ye* — pungent, warm; enters Lung and Spleen. Releases exterior, disperses cold, moves qi, harmonises the middle, resolves seafood toxicity. *Zi Su Zi* — pungent, warm; enters Lung and Large Intestine. Directs qi downward, arrests cough and wheeze, moistens the intestines.
- **Ayurveda:** *Rasa* — pungent (katu); *Guna* — light, dry, mobile; *Virya* — heating (ushna); *Vipaka* — pungent. *Vata*- and *kapha*-pacifying, *pitta*-provoking in excess.
- **Galenic / Unani:** Warm and dry (approx. 2nd degree); attenuating, carminative, resolvent.

### Contraindications

- **Perilla ketone / isoegomaketone toxicity:** The furan-ketone chemotypes are established **pulmonary toxicants in grazing livestock** (acute bovine pulmonary oedema / "perilla ketone lung"). Culinary and medicinal human use of leaf and seed is regarded as safe, but concentrated essential-oil preparations of ketone-rich chemotypes should be avoided, and Perilla is not for internal use as an undiluted essential oil.
- **Perillaldehyde contact allergy:** Perillaldehyde is a recognised skin sensitiser; occupational and cosmetic contact dermatitis to shiso/perilla aldehyde is documented. Patch-test-positive or sensitive individuals should avoid topical exposure.
- **Warm-dry caution:** As a pungent-warming herb, use cautiously in heat/yin-deficient dry cough patterns and in those with a tendency to profuse sweating.
