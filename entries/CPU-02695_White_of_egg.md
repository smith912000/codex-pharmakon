# White of egg — LEUKON TOU OOU (Dioscorides 2-55)

## *The albumen of a hen's egg as the English translation renders it; the chapter says "egg" without naming the bird, and the identification as the hen's egg white is the translator's and ours, at medium confidence; distinct from the yolk row (CPU-01144) drawn from 2-54* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** The white of egg is attested here only in Dioscorides, *De Materia Medica*, book 2, chapter 55, LEUKON TOU OOU, "White of Egg" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 5155). The row is separate from the yolk, which is drawn from the preceding chapter 2-54 (OON, "Eggs and their Yolk") and is already on the spine as CPU-01144.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter reads: "The white of an egg used raw cools and closes the pores of the skin, and when dropped on inflamed eyes it soothes them. Rubbed on afterwards it prevents burns from breaking out into pustules. It protects the face from sunburn. It constipates those troubled with discharges, and will repress them when laid on the forehead with frankincense. It lessens inflammation of the eyes applied with wool and with rosaceum [1-53], wine and honey mixed with it. If it is sipped raw it helps bites of the snake called haemorrhois [a fable]; and warmed a little it is good for bladder distress, ulcerated kidneys, rough arteries, and the throwing up of blood, mucus and fluids in the chest." The bracketed words are the translator's, including the note that the snake is "a fable".

**Chapter 2-54 (context, not this entry).** The neighbouring chapter on whole eggs says that "a soft-cooked egg nourishes more than an uncooked one and a hard-cooked egg more than a soft", and gives uses for the yolk with saffron, with melilot and fried with sumach or galls (line 5152). Those statements concern the whole egg and the yolk. They are not assigned to the white, and the white is stated to be used raw in 2-55.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, egg white is about nine-tenths water, with the rest mostly proteins (ovalbumin and others, including lysozyme and avidin). It carries almost no fat. Raw white is a potential carrier of Salmonella on the shell surface or within the egg. No analysis was retrieved. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Cooling and closing the pores; soothing inflamed eyes; guarding burns from pustules; protecting the face from sun; stopping discharges; sipped raw for a snake bite (the translator marks this "a fable"); warmed, for bladder, kidney, artery and chest complaints.
- **What the Codex does.** It gives no protocol. The eye uses and the burn use are not endorsed: raw egg on the eye or on broken skin is an infection risk, and the snake-bite claim should not be acted on. A person with a burn, an eye problem or a bite needs proper care.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations printed are all simple: used raw, dropped on the eye, rubbed on a burn or the face, laid on the forehead with frankincense, applied with wool and with rosaceum (rose oil, cross-reference 1-53), wine and honey, sipped raw, or warmed a little (line 5155). No quantity is given for any of them. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Egg white is a protein food. Raw egg can carry Salmonella, and raw white contains avidin, which binds the vitamin biotin until the protein is denatured by cooking. A film of protein on skin dries and tightens as it sets, which is a mechanical effect; it is not shown to protect a burn from blistering. No clinical studies were retrieved for the uses Dioscorides lists. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. The chapter is a list of indications. The discipline of attention it models is small and practical: which form (raw, warmed), which carrier (wool, rose oil, wine and honey), and which place on the body for each use. It records a snake-bite use that the translator labels a fable; recording is not endorsing. No cultivation status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to practise beyond ordinary kitchen caution. The chapter is a record of what a Greek physician said of egg white, with no bearing on the vital constitution in the sources read. The sensible reading is historical, and the only cultivation use is to learn how the source separates yolk from white and cooked from raw.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

The chapter maps raw white of egg onto the skin, the eyes, the face, the bowels, the bladder and kidneys, the arteries and the chest. Used raw, it cools and closes the pores of the skin, and dropped on inflamed eyes it soothes them. Rubbed on afterwards it prevents burns from breaking out into pustules, and it protects the face from sunburn. It constipates those troubled with discharges, and represses them when laid on the forehead with frankincense. Applied with wool and rose oil, wine and honey, it lessens inflammation of the eyes. Sipped raw, it is said to help a snake bite, a claim the translator marks as a fable. Warmed a little, it is good for bladder distress, ulcerated kidneys, rough arteries, and the throwing up of blood, mucus and fluids from the chest. The text assigns the white no temperament beyond cooling and constipating, and the Codex adds no humoral or subtle-body status to it.

### Contraindications

- **Egg allergy.** Egg white is a leading food allergen, and the text says nothing on this. Avoid with a known allergy. [USER: verify]
- **Raw egg.** Salmonella risk. Higher risk in pregnancy, infancy, old age and illness.
- **Eyes and burns.** Do not put raw egg on the eye or on a burn; seek care.
- **Snake bite.** A raw-egg drink is not a treatment. Seek emergency care.
