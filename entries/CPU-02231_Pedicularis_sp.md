# Pedicularis sp. — Ma Xian Hao 马先蒿 (Shennong bencao jing); alias Ma Niao Hao 马尿蒿 (Sun Xingyan), Ma Shi Hao 马矢蒿 (plain e-text), "Horse Droppings Tall Weed" (Yang)

## *A lousewort (*Pedicularis*), the herb *ma xian hao*; the identification as *Pedicularis resupinata* L. is Yang 1998's ("Herba Pedicularis Resupinatae") and is of medium confidence at species level, since the classic describes no plant and several *Pedicularis* species are used under the name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **NOTE.** This is a middle-class drug of the classic, "nontoxic or toxic" as a class (see the Cultivation Register). The classic prints no toxicity for this herb. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation.

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; the other editions on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) and the plain e-text (`Shennong_plain_daizhige.txt`), are read as further witnesses. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Pedicularis sp.", taken from Yang's Latin; this entry replaces it. The names.csv row (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv`, row 141) records the genus only.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun's text. The entry is 马先蒿 in the middle classic (Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, 卷二 中经, entry 马先蒿, line 2271; the drug is also in the middle-class list at line 1677, among the herbs "上草，中品四十九种"). The paragraph (line 2273) reads: 味平 ("flavour balanced"); 主寒热鬼注 ("governs cold and heat, and *gui zhu*, demonic influx"), 中风湿痹 ("wind stroke and damp impediment"), 女子带下病 ("women's vaginal-discharge disease"), 无子 ("childlessness"); 一名马尿蒿 ("one name *Ma niao hao*"); 生川泽 ("grows in rivers and marshes"). The Mingyi bielu layer (line 2275) adds only 生南阳 ("grows in Nanyang"). There is no 久服 clause and no promise of lightening the body or of not ageing.

Sun's note (案, line 2277 and following) is philological. It cites the *Shuowen*, the *Guangya* and the *Erya* on the plant 蔚, and Lu Ji's gloss on the *Shijing* (line 2279 and following), which describes a plant that "begins to grow in the third month, flowers in the seventh month, the flower like a sesame flower, purple-red; in the eighth month it forms pods like small beans, pointed and long", and gives the name 马新蒿, noting that 新 and 先 are close in sound. That is Sun's equation of the drug with a plant of the *Shijing* commentaries; it is not part of the classic and is not our identification.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 3063): "Ma Xian Hao (Herba Pedicularis Resupinatae) is acrid and balanced. It mainly treats cold and heat, demonic influx, wind stroke, damp impediment, and, in females, vaginal discharge disease and infertility. Its other name is Ma Shi Hao (Horse Droppings Tall Weed). It grows in rivers and swamps." Yang is in the middle class (PDF p.82, "Herbs: Middle Class"). Yang adds "acrid" to the flavour; Sun's text has "balanced" alone, and the plain e-text (line 285) has 味苦平 ("bitter and balanced") with the alias 马矢蒿. The three editions therefore differ on the flavour: balanced (Sun), bitter and balanced (plain e-text), acrid and balanced (Yang). The indications agree across all three.

**Appendix.** A search of the other texts on disk for *Pedicularis* found no entry for this drug. The spine has other lousewort rows (CPU-00873, 00874, 01763 to 01765) from other traditions; they are different attestations and are not merged here.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Cold and heat, demonic influx, wind stroke, damp impediment, women's vaginal-discharge disease and childlessness.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no part used, no harvest time.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic gives no preparation and no dose. The preface (Sun, line 1677; Yang, line 385) frames the middle drugs generally: they "may or may not be toxic" and are to be weighed before use. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Pedicularis* species are low herbs of damp ground and mountain meadows in the northern hemisphere. The classic's *gui zhu* and the damp-impediment terms have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle classic, whose preface (Sun, line 1677) reads 中药一百二十种为臣，主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经: the middle drugs are the "ministers", "mainly nourishing the nature to correspond with the human; nontoxic or toxic, to be weighed as appropriate; for those who wish to check disease and replenish the emaciated". Yang prints the same preface at line 385 ("mainly nurture personality ... may or may not be toxic ... supplement vacuity, and replenish exhaustion"), and his own discussion (line 358 and following) glosses the middle class as drugs that "cultivate personality or modify temperament" and notes that they "may be toxic". The drug's own paragraph carries no 久服 clause and no promise of lightening the body or of not ageing, so no cultivation status is attested for it: it is framed by its class and therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade herb for cold and heat, wind and damp impediment, and women's discharge and infertility, with no long-term clause. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use.
