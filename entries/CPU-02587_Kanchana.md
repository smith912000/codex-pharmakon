# Kanchana — Kovidara (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna); Bauhinia variegata

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta Samhita (Bhishagratna) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry.

**Sushruta (SS), the Appendix index.** The Appendix 3 index in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` (page 71 for the names, page 71-72 for the identifications) pairs two different Sanskrit names with the same plant:

- **Kanchana**: the first name on the page (line 31904, printed "Ea'nchana"). The first identification printed is "Bauhinia Variegata" (line 31992).
- **Kovidara**: the name at line 31976 (printed "Eovida'ra"). Its identification, "Bauhinia Variegata" (line 32047), follows after the sequence Kodrava (Paspalum), Kokilaksha (Hygrophila) and before Koranta (Barleria) and Koshataki (Luffa).

Checking the column order: the names run Kanchana, Kankarola, Kangu, Kantakshiri, Kapikachchhu, Kapittha, Karanja, Karavella, Karavira and on; the identifications run Bauhinia variegata, Alangium hexapetalum, Panicum italicum, Solanum xanthocarpum, "See Atma-Gupta", Feronia elephantum, Pongamia glabra, Momordica, Nerium odorum, "See Gaja-pippali". Each matches in order, including the cross-references. The same holds at the second locus: Kodrava, Kokilaksha, Kovidara, Koranta, Koshataki are matched by Paspalum, Hygrophila, Bauhinia, Barleria, Luffa.

**Sushruta (SS), the chapters.** The form *Kanchana* does not itself appear in the chapter text in the lines found, apart from a translator's footnote at `chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch07.txt` line 42, which says Chakradatta reads "Kanchana" in place of "Kakubha" and is not about this plant's use. The forms *Kanchanaka* (Sutra ch. 39 and ch. 46) appear in lists of leaves and rice, and whether Kanchanaka is this plant is not stated. [USER: verify.] The name *Kovidara* appears:

- **Sutra ch. 39** (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` line 12), in the emetic drugs: "Madana fruits, Kutaja, Jimutaka, Ikshvaku, Dhamagarba, Krita-vedhana, Sarshapa, Vidanga, Pippali, Karanjaka, Prapunnada, Kovidara, Korvudara, Arishta ... are possessed of emetic properties." The text then directs that the fruits (seeds) of plants before Kovidara in that list be used, "and the roots of plants from Kovidara to its close".
- **Sutra ch. 46** (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 1588), in the leaves: "Karvudara and Kovidara etc., have an astringent, sweet and bitter taste, and prove curative in haemoptysis. They subdue the Kapham, generate the Vayu and are astringent in their action and light of digestion." And at line 1775, in the flowers: "The flowers of such trees as the Kovidara, Shana, and Shalmali are sweet in taste and digestion and prove curative in cases of haemoptysis."
- **Sutra ch. 43** (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch43.txt` line 40) mentions "drugs of the Kovidaradi group" as a decoction vehicle for a powder.
- **Sutra ch. 42** (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` line 358) lists "Kovidaraka" among the astringent group.

The translation never equates Kovidara with Kanchana in the chapter text; only the index puts both on one plant.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (ch. 46).** Leaf: astringent, sweet and bitter taste; subdues Kapha; generates Vata; astringent in action; light to digest. Flower: sweet in taste and in digestion. These statements are made of Kovidara in group lists ("etc."), not of the single plant alone.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The lines read place Kovidara among emetics, leaves and flowers, and as the head of a named drug group. None places it in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no statement about *ojas* is attached to it.

**Practitioner's note.** A group-head drug in the Sushruta lists, used as an emetic root and as a leaf and flower food-drug. Nothing in the record supports a cultivation use.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

The entry maps Kovidara onto the stomach, the chest and airways, and the doshas Kapha and Vata. The root is an emetic: the sutra lists it among emetic drugs and directs that roots be used from Kovidara onward (Sutra 39), so the stomach and vomiting reflex are the sites named. The leaf is astringent, sweet and bitter, curative in haemoptysis (coughing of blood from the chest), subdues Kapha, generates Vata and is light to digest (Sutra 46). The flower is sweet in taste and digestion and curative in haemoptysis (Sutra 46). Those leaf and flower lines use etc. within group lists, so they describe the group more than the single plant. The text gives no modern anatomy and no channel. The Appendix pairs Kanchana with the same plant, but the chapter text does not equate the two names, so no organ is mapped to Kanchana itself. The root is an emetic and should not be self-administered, and coughing blood is a medical emergency.

### Contraindications

- **Emetic use.** The text lists the root as an emetic; vomiting is a medical procedure and not for self-use.
- **Identity.** The mapping Kanchana-Kovidara-Bauhinia depends on the Appendix index; the chapter text does not equate the names. [USER: verify.]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]
- **Haemoptysis.** Coughing blood is a medical emergency.
