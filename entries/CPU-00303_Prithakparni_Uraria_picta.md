### Origin & Tradition

Pṛśniparṇī is a herb/undershrub of the Indian subcontinent's grasslands and open forest, prized for its root. Alongside Śālaparṇī (*Desmodium gangeticum*), it is one of the two *paribhāṣika* "parṇī" roots that anchor the classical Ayurvedic root-formulae — the pair are so consistently used together that they function almost as a single therapeutic unit.

**Ayurvedic (Sushruta & Caraka):** In the *Sushruta Saṃhitā*, Pṛśniparṇī is a component of the **Laghupañcamūla** ("lesser five roots"), which together with the Bṛhatpañcamūla forms the **Daśamūla** ("ten roots") — the pre-eminent anti-Vāta, anti-inflammatory, febrifuge, and post-partum decoction of Ayurveda. It is thus, with Śālaparṇī, one of the structural pillars of Daśamūla therapy. Caraka lists it among *balya* (strengthening), *śothahara* (anti-oedema/anti-inflammatory), *saṃgrāhaka/grāhī* (astringent, anti-diarrhoeal), and *jvaraghna* (febrifuge) drugs. Classical indications: *jvara* (fever, especially *sannipāta*/tridoshic and *viṣama* fevers), *atisāra* and *raktātisāra* (diarrhoea/bloody diarrhoea), *śvāsa-kāsa* (respiratory conditions), *raktapitta* (bleeding disorders), *śotha* (inflammation/oedema), and general debility.

Its Sanskrit names encode observation and doctrine: **Pṛśniparṇī / Pṛthakparṇī** ("spotted-leaf" / "distinct-leaf"); **Siṃhapucchī** ("lion's-tail," for the tapering inflorescence); **Kalaśī** ("pitcher"); and **Guhā** ("the hidden/secret one"). The name **Pṛthakparṇī** ("separate-leaved") is the vernacular root of "Prithakparni."

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the Febrifuge Root of the Laghupañcamūla:** Pṛśniparṇī's dravyaguṇa logic mirrors and complements Śālaparṇī's, which is precisely why the two are paired. It is *madhura-tikta-kaṣāya* (sweet–bitter–astringent) in rasa, *śīta* (cooling) in vīrya, *madhura* in vipāka, and *laghu-snigdha* in guṇa. The sweet/unctuous axis makes it *balya* (building); the bitter/astringent axis makes it *grāhī* (binding, drying) and *jvaraghna* (fever-clearing). This dual nature — nourishing yet astringent, building yet clearing heat and flux — is the doctrinal engine of Daśamūla itself: a formula that simultaneously *pacifies aggravated Vāta* (via unctuous sweetness) and *clears inflammation and fever* (via bitter-astringent cooling). Pṛśniparṇī's role in *raktapitta* and *raktātisāra* (bleeding, bloody flux) expresses the astringent-cooling signature: it *stanches and steadies* while it *cools*. The name **Guhā** ("the hidden one") is a fitting emblem for a root whose action is quiet and structural — it rarely acts alone or dramatically, but is a hidden pillar of the tradition's most important compound.

### Active Components

- **Isoflavanones and isoflavonoids** — including novel isoflavanones isolated from the roots (Fabaceae-characteristic), the antimicrobial fraction.
- **Triterpenes and steroids / phytosterols** — reported from the roots.
- **Flavonoids and phenolics** — antioxidant constituents.
- **Alkaloids** (minor) reported in the genus.

Per PubMed (Rahman, Gibbons & Gray 2007), two new isoflavanones together with six known compounds (isoflavanones, triterpenes, and steroids) were isolated from the roots and showed antimicrobial activity.

### Scientific Correlation

Attribution: findings from PubMed. (The published pharmacological literature on *Uraria picta* is limited.)

- **Isoflavanones & antimicrobial activity (phytochemistry, in vitro):** Rahman, Gibbons & Gray (2007, *Phytochemistry*) isolated two new isoflavanones plus six known compounds (isoflavanones, triterpenes, steroids) from *U. picta* roots; minimum inhibitory concentrations against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria and fungi ranged 12.5–200 µg/mL. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2007.04.015)

**Evidence grade:** **Preclinical / phytochemical only** for isolated actions; the substance's principal standing is **traditional** — as a classical Daśamūla/Laghupañcamūla root with longstanding anti-inflammatory, febrifuge, grāhī, and balya use, biologically plausible but not established by human clinical trials. The modern literature base is thin; specific therapeutic claims beyond the cited antimicrobial screen should be treated as traditional, not clinically proven.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** *Madhura* (sweet), *Tikta* (bitter), *Kaṣāya* (astringent)
- **Guṇa (quality):** *Laghu* (light), *Snigdha* (unctuous)
- **Vīrya (potency):** *Śīta* (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** *Madhura* (sweet)
- **Prabhāva (specific effect):** Its structural role — with Śālaparṇī — as a paired pillar of the Laghupañcamūla/Daśamūla, and its use in tridoshic (*sannipāta*) fevers, may be regarded as its distinctive standing.
- **Doṣa action:** *Vāta-śāmaka* and *Pitta-śāmaka* (primary); Kapha-neutral (its bitter/astringent secondary rasa offsets the sweetness) — hence its reputation as broadly tridoshic-balancing
- **Karma (actions):** *Balya* (strengthening), *jvaraghna* (febrifuge — including in *sannipāta*/tridoshic fever), *śothahara* (anti-inflammatory/anti-oedema), *grāhī / saṃgrāhaka* (astringent, anti-diarrhoeal), *raktapitta-hara* (checks bleeding disorders), *śvāsahara / kāsahara* (respiratory-soothing), *rasāyana*-adjacent (restorative, in Daśamūla convalescent use)

**Gaṇa membership (Sushruta):** Component of **Laghupañcamūla** → **Daśamūla**; paired with Śālaparṇī (*Desmodium gangeticum*).

### Contraindications

- Generally well-tolerated in classical doses and within compound formulae. Its astringent (*grāhī*) action means caution in constipation-predominant states or where reduced gut motility is undesirable.
- Data in pregnancy/lactation are traditional (Daśamūla is a classical post-partum formula) rather than trial-based — use under qualified guidance. `[USER: verify]`
- No robust human safety/interaction dataset; the modern evidence base is limited. `[USER: verify]`
- **Authentication:** *Uraria picta* is traded alongside *Desmodium* and *Pseudarthria* species under overlapping vernacular names ("Prishniparni"/"Shalaparni" confusion is common in the market) — confirm botanical identity before use.
