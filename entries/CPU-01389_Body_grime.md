# Body grime — Rupos / Rupos palaistra / Rupos gymnasion (Dioscorides: grime from the baths, the wrestling school and the gymnasium walls)
## *Scrapings of dirt and filth gathered at the public baths, the wrestling school and the gymnasium walls; the text names the places and does not say what the grime consists of* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The loci are three consecutive short chapters of *De Materia Medica* Book 1 in the 2000 English edition of the Dioscorides herbal (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`; name-index rows `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` lines 35-37, class "other"; the book index sends the reader to page 36 for all three, lines 15945-15953). They stand in the part of the book given to oils and unguents, after 1-33 *Elaion sikuonion*, the sicyonian oil (line 3815), and before 1-37 *Elaiomeli* (line 3832). They are 1-34 *Rupos* (line 3821), 1-35 *Rupos palaistra* (line 3824) and 1-36 *Rupos gymnasion* (line 3826). Each is a sentence or two; the three together are about eighty words. They are set out in the Varieties section below.

The Greek word the translator transliterates as *rupos* is rendered by him "grime", "dirt" and "filth" in the three chapters. The chapters do not say what the grime is made of or whose bodies produced it. The material is filed in Volume III on the spine's classification as animal-derived, which rests on the understanding that bath and gymnasium grime is what the bodies of bathers and athletes leave behind; the chapters themselves do not state that. ### Varieties

**Grime from the baths — *Rupos*** (DMM; 1-34, line 3821). "The scrapings which are taken up in public baths are able to heat, soften, and disperse fluids, and are good for splits in the perineum, and for rubbing on joints." The chapter names no tool for the scraping; the word "strigil" does not occur in the English text on disk, so the batch note "strigil scrapings" is not supported by the chapter. [USER: verify]

**Grime from the wrestling school — *Rupos palaistra*** (DMM; 1-35, line 3824). "The dirt or filth from the wrestling school helps the joints, applied as a warm compress." That is the whole chapter: one use, one preparation (a warm compress).

**Grime from the gymnasium walls — *Rupos gymnasion*** (DMM; 1-36, line 3826, the heading printed in the OCR in the middle of the previous line). "The filth on the walls of the gymnasium (or that which is scraped off from statues) warms and dissolves tubercles [growths] that ripen only with difficulty, and it is helpful for abrasions, the removal of scaliness, and old ulcers." The parenthesis, "or that which is scraped off from statues", adds a second source to this variety: statues, by implication those of the gymnasium. Square brackets are the translator's.

*Synthesis of the varieties.* The text separates the three kinds by the place of gathering and by nothing else: the public baths, the wrestling school, and the gymnasium walls or statues. It ranks none above another. Two of the three, the baths and the wrestling school, are given for the joints; the bath grime has the longest list of actions in Dioscorides' own words (it heats, softens and disperses fluids, and serves splits of the perineum), and the wrestling-school grime is given only for the joints, as a warm compress. The gymnasium-wall grime is the one credited with dissolving slow-ripening growths, scaliness and old ulcers, and the only one that "warms" in so many words; the bath grime "heats". Here three chapters in one author name the same kind of material from three places; no second text in this project's sources is tagged to this entry, so there is nothing to place beside them.

### Active Components

None can be stated from the text, which gives no composition. In general terms, the grime that collects on bath and gymnasium surfaces is a mixture of skin oils (sebum), sweat residue, shed skin cells, dust and whatever oils or other substances were applied to the body, and it can carry bacteria and fungi. This is a general statement about such material and not a reading of the chapters.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records the grime as an external material for the joints, perineal splits, growths, scaliness and old ulcers. Applying other people's bodily residue, or scrapings from shared surfaces, to broken skin or ulcers is a hygiene hazard, and the sources give no reason to revive it. No modern protocol is supplied.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation given is for the wrestling-school grime, "applied as a warm compress" (1-35), and for the bath grime, "rubbing on joints" (1-34). No quantity, no cleansing of the material and no duration is given for any variety. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM).** Dioscorides prints actions in the vocabulary of heating and softening: the bath scrapings "are able to heat, soften, and disperse fluids" (1-34); the gymnasium-wall filth "warms and dissolves" (1-36). The wrestling-school chapter prints no quality (1-35). No other quality such as dryness, moistness or astringency is printed for any of the three.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What the herbal offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention (provenance, season of gathering, sensory assay, adulteration detection), and these three chapters give none of it: they say where the material is gathered and what it is good for, and nothing about how to judge its quality. The text records an everyday by-product of the bath and the gymnasium as a drug; recording is not endorsing, and no amuletic use is recorded here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The most a cultivator takes from these chapters is the habit of the bath and the gymnasium as places where the body is cleaned and exercised. The grime itself is not a practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Infection:** used grime from shared bathing and exercise places can carry bacteria and fungi; it should never be applied to open sores, ulcers or the perineum.
- **Shared surfaces:** scraping statues or gymnasium walls may damage property or heritage objects; legal status not retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Joints and ulcers:** persistent joint pain and non-healing ulcers need medical assessment.
