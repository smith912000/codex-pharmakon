### ⚠️ TOXICITY — READ FIRST

**Arka is a poisonous plant. The milky latex contains cardiac (cardenolide) glycosides — calotropin, calactin, uscharin, calotoxin and related — plus proteolytic and irritant proteins.** The latex is a **severe ocular and mucosal irritant** (documented cases of keratitis, corneal oedema and temporary/serious vision loss from latex contact with the eye), a **skin vesicant/dermatitis agent**, and **cardiotoxic if absorbed or ingested** — producing the digitalis-type picture (nausea, vomiting, bradycardia, arrhythmia, potentially fatal cardiac arrest). Classical Ayurveda treats Arka as an **upaviṣa (semi-poisonous)** drug requiring **śodhana (formal detoxification/purification)** before any internal use, and even then at minute, expert-controlled doses. **It is not a self-administration substance.** In this codex Arka is documented for completeness and traditional/topical context; **internal use is practitioner-and-toxicology–gated.**

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta Saṃhitā (Ayurveda — surgical tradition):** Arka *names its own gaṇa*: the **Arkādi gaṇa**, a group of sharp, hot, caustic drugs indicated for **kuṣṭha (skin disease), kaṇḍū (itch), kṛmi (parasites/worms), viṣa (poison), gulma (abdominal masses), meda (fat), and kapha disorders**. Its actions in the surgical tradition are aggressively **śodhana** (purificatory): the latex is a classical **bhedana/recanā** (purgative-breaking) and **vamana** (emetic) adjunct, a **kṛmighna** (anthelmintic), and a topical caustic for warts, corns, ringworm, and to open/clean abscesses and sinuses. It is a foremost **viṣaghna** (anti-poison) in the paradoxical Indic logic of "poison against poison." The **latex, root-bark, leaf and flower** are all used; the leaf is warmed and applied over painful, swollen joints and the abdomen.

**Caraka & nighaṇṭus:** Caraka enrols Arka in the bhedanīya (breaking) and kuṣṭhaghna currents and in vamana/virecana therapy. Nighaṇṭus distinguish the purple-flowered **Arka** from the white-flowered **Alarka** (often held superior/more sāttvic), and codify indications for asthma, cough, oedema, and obstinate skin disease.

**Solar & ritual tradition:** The very name **Arka = "the Sun" / "ray"**. The plant is sacred to **Sūrya** (and associated with fire/heat), its wood used for sacred fire (samidh) and its flowers offered — notably to **Śiva** and **Hanumān**. Its hot, radiant, "solar" nature in ritual perfectly mirrors its uṣṇa-tīkṣṇa pharmacology. Across arid Africa, Arabia (the "Apple of Sodom") and India it is a famous folk remedy — and a famous poison.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — Solar Fire and the Poison-Medicine Threshold:** Arka is the codex's clearest example of the **viṣa/auṣadha dialectic** — the doctrine that the most dangerous substances, correctly purified and dosed, become the most powerful medicines. It is *solar*: hot, penetrating, drying, dissolving. Where Kadalī is lunar softness and Lodhra is cool containment, Arka is **radiant caustic force** — it burns through obstruction (gulma, meda, kapha, kṛmi, viṣa). Its milk-white latex, so nourishing in appearance yet corrosive in fact, is itself a doctrinal emblem: *the deceptive gift, the fire disguised as milk.* This is why it heads a gaṇa and why it demands śodhana — it is power that must be *tamed at the threshold*. In Kenetica its RESET placement is strictly the **topical/purificatory** sense (clearing, not building), and always under the toxicity gate above.

### Active Components

The pharmacologically defining class is the **cardenolide (cardiac) glycosides — calotropin, calactin, uscharin, calotoxin, uscharidin, voruscharin, frugoside** — concentrated in the latex; these drive both the therapeutic and the toxic (digitalis-type) cardiac activity. Also present: **the cysteine proteases procerain and procerain B** and other latex proteins (anti-inflammatory and irritant), **flavonoids, triterpenes and sterols (β-amyrin, taraxasterol, calotropenol), tannins, and resins**. The **non-dialysable latex protein fraction** carries much of the reported anti-inflammatory activity independent of the small-molecule cardenolides.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Filed under **`[RESET]`**, in the narrow, gated sense: Arka is a **caustic, purificatory, anti-inflammatory topical agent** — a "clearer" of obstruction, not a builder or stimulant. Kenetica's only sanctioned self-use is **external** (e.g., traditional joint/skin applications, always with irritation and eye-splash precautions). Any internal, cardenolide-bearing preparation is **out of scope for self-administration** and belongs to expert Ayurvedic toxicology (śodhana + micro-dosing). Its documented anti-inflammatory latex-protein signal is the scientifically legitimate thread.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, *C. procera* has a **preclinical** pharmacology base — strongest and most credible for the **anti-inflammatory latex-protein fraction** — alongside a substantial, well-documented **toxicology** literature (cardiac glycoside poisoning, ocular injury) that the traditional upaviṣa classification anticipates.

- **Anti-inflammatory latex protein (rat, multiple models):** A non-dialysable protein fraction of *C. procera* latex, given IV, strongly inhibited carrageenin-induced neutrophil migration (up to ~80% at 100 mg/kg) and paw oedema, and prevented ifosfamide-induced haemorrhagic cystitis (mesna-like) — a cell-mediated, proteinaceous anti-inflammatory action distinct from the cardenolides (Alencar et al., 2004, *Planta Medica*). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2004-835842)
- **Anti-inflammatory & antioxidant in experimental arthritis (rat):** A methanol latex extract (50–500 mg/kg) attenuated Freund's-complete-adjuvant monoarthritis — lowering PGE₂, TNF-α, nitric oxide and myeloperoxidase, and restoring glutathione, SOD, catalase and GPx — comparably (though somewhat less than) rofecoxib (Kumar & Roy, 2007, *Mediators of Inflammation*). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/2007/47523)

**Evidence grade: preclinical (anti-inflammatory) with strong documented toxicity.** The latex-protein anti-inflammatory effect is reproducible in animals; **no human efficacy trials exist, and the cardenolide toxicity is real and clinically reported.** The risk profile dominates. Attribution: PubMed.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** No cultivation status can be assigned. Dioscorides carries no subtle-body system into which a plant could be graded, so what the tradition would offer a cultivator is only its discipline of attention — provenance, the assay of an acrid milky latex, the guarding against a dangerous adulterant. Beyond that, the entry itself records no Greek attestation for this plant (the "Apple of Sodom" belongs more to Josephus and Pliny than to any secure *De Materia Medica* chapter in the retrieved sources), so even the therapeutic claim is thin here. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status — see attestation flag on the DMM tag.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** Where Unani takes up Arka (*ʿushar* / *madār*) it is as a hot, sharp, caustic drug for skin disease, obstruction and topical work — consonant with its heating temperament. The corpus's genuine cultivation idea, the conservation of *ḥarārat gharīzīya* (innate heat) and *raṭūbat aṣlīya* (radical moisture), is a frame of the whole regimen, and a violently heating, desiccating caustic is if anything a spender of that endowment, not a conserver of it; no *Canon* chapter and no *mufarriḥ* role assigns it a longevity standing. Its use is therapeutic and external. **(b) framed:** used, no cultivation status sourced.

**Ayurveda (SS).** Arka is a paradigm *śodhana* and *bhedana* drug — an *upaviṣa* (semi-poisonous) caustic that heads its own *Arkādi gaṇa*, worked against *kuṣṭha*, *kṛmi*, *viṣa*, *gulma* and *kapha*. Its whole logic is clearing, breaking and expelling, the opposite pole from the *bṛṃhaṇa*/*rasāyana* drugs that build the *dhātus* and *ojas*; no *rasāyana* or *vājīkaraṇa* assignment is recorded for it in the sources retrieved, and its classical status is instead *upaviṣa*, requiring formal *śodhana* (purification of the drug itself) before any internal use. Its solar and ritual associations — the name *Arka* = "Sun," sacredness to Sūrya, offerings to Śiva and Hanumān, wood used as *samidh* — are real, but they are the standing of the plant as sacred material in the ritual sphere, not a cultivation status assigned to a preparation taken to build a person's vitality. **(b) framed:** heavily claimed by the tradition, but as a purificatory/caustic drug, with no rejuvenative cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing here to cultivate with, and the honest cultivation answer is abstentive and cautionary. Arka is a gated caustic and cardiotoxic *upaviṣa*: its only sanctioned self-use is external, and every internal preparation is practitioner-and-toxicology-gated behind *śodhana* and micro-dosing. A cultivator's relationship to it is the poison-medicine threshold — respect, purification, minute expert dosing — not the daily building of vitality. Its sacred solar charge is ritual and material, and does not make it something taken to nourish life.

### Contraindications

- **⚠️ Cardiotoxicity:** contains digitalis-type cardiac glycosides — **do not ingest.** Deadly in overdose; contraindicated with any heart disease, arrhythmia, or cardiac-glycoside/digoxin therapy.
- **⚠️ Ocular hazard:** latex causes serious eye injury (keratitis, corneal oedema, vision loss) — **never let latex contact the eyes**; wash immediately and seek care on exposure.
- **Skin:** latex is a vesicant/irritant — causes contact dermatitis and blistering.
- **Pregnancy/lactation & paediatrics:** absolutely contraindicated internally (abortifacient/toxic potential).
- **Self-use:** internal use forbidden without qualified Ayurvedic toxicological supervision and prior śodhana. This entry does **not** constitute a dosing recommendation for internal use.
