# Tangerine Peel / Chen Pi (陈皮) / Mandarin Peel / Pericarpium Citri Reticulatae / Guang Chen Pi (广陈皮) / Chachi Peel

## *Citrus reticulata* Blanco | Family: Rutaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SBJ, SS

**Energetic Classification:** Warm, acrid-bitter, aromatic; descending-and-dispersing. Enters Spleen, Stomach, Lung.

**Training Phase:** Foundation — Digestive Regulation

### Origin & Tradition

Almost every entry in this codex names a plant. This one names a *process*. The drug is not "tangerine peel"; it is **陈皮** — *chen pi*, literally **"aged peel."** The character 陈 does not describe the fruit, the tree, the region, or the part used. It describes time spent in storage. A fresh mandarin peel is not chen pi and the tradition will not accept it as such. This is one of the very few points in world materia medica where a doctrine of *deliberate ageing* is written into the drug's name — and therefore one of the very few places where a traditional claim is directly, cheaply, chemically testable.

**Chinese Medicine (SBJ) — 陈皮 (chén pí); Guang Chen Pi 广陈皮**

Chen pi is among the most-used single drugs in the Chinese pharmacopoeia. Its classical functions are stated in four movements: **理气 (lǐ qì)** regulate qi; **燥湿 (zào shī)** dry damp; **化痰 (huà tán)** transform phlegm; **和中 (hé zhōng)** harmonise the middle. The clinical picture that calls for it is a stagnant, waterlogged middle burner — epigastric and abdominal distension, fullness that worsens after eating, belching, poor appetite, nausea, loose stool, a thick greasy tongue coat — and the phlegm-damp cough that the same terrain produces higher up: productive, white, copious sputum, a sense of oppression in the chest.

It is a *regulating* drug rather than a forcing one. Chen pi appears in Er Chen Tang (二陈汤, "Two Aged [Drugs] Decoction" — the name itself advertising that two of its ingredients must be aged), in Ping Wei San, in Liu Jun Zi Tang. In these formulas its job is usually to keep tonics from cloying: it is added to sweet, moistening, spleen-tonifying herbs precisely so that the tonic does not itself generate the damp stagnation it was meant to relieve. This is a sophisticated pharmacological idea dressed in doctrinal language — a *counter-agent against the side effect of the principal agent*.

The ageing doctrine is explicit and graded. Peel stored one year is ordinary; three years is the minimum threshold for the protected designation; and the finest grade — **广陈皮 (Guang Chen Pi)**, from the Chachi (茶枝柑) cultivar grown around Xinhui in Guangdong — is aged for years to decades, priced accordingly, and traded much as vintage wine or pu-erh tea is traded. The stated rationale is that ageing "removes the fire-dryness" (去燥性) of the fresh peel: fresh peel is held to be too acrid, too scattering, too aggressive — it disperses qi rather than regulating it. Time is said to soften the acridity while the qi-regulating and phlegm-transforming actions mature and deepen. The tradition summarises this as **"陈久者良"** — *the long-aged is superior*.

**Sun Bin Jing / Sino-Japanese Kampo lineage (SBJ)**

Transmitted into the Japanese Kampo repertoire as **陳皮 (chinpi)**, where it retains its role as a *rigi* (理気) qi-regulating drug and appears in the standard formulas inherited from Chinese sources. Kampo practice is generally more standardised and more sceptical of vintage grading than the Guangdong trade: chinpi is specified as a defined crude drug, and the elaborate age-hierarchy of Guang Chen Pi is not a governing feature of Kampo prescribing. The distinction matters — the same drug, in a closely related tradition, without the ageing doctrine attached. **Shen Nong / Materia Medica lineage (SBJ)** *(⚠️ code corrected 2026-08-10: this block was lettered **(SS)**; that code denotes a different tradition in `TRADITION_CODES.md`. The content is genuine and belongs under **SBJ**.)*

Citrus peel enters the Chinese materia medica early and is refined across the classical commentaries into the modern distinction between *chen pi* (the mature, aged peel), *qing pi* (青皮, the immature green peel — colder, more aggressive, liver-directed), and *ju hong* (橘红, the outer red flavedo alone, considered more strongly drying and phlegm-transforming because the white inner pith has been scraped away). That the tradition bothered to split one fruit's peel into three drugs by *maturity and anatomical layer* is itself evidence of careful empirical attention: these fractions do differ chemically, since the flavedo carries the oil glands and polymethoxyflavones while the albedo carries the bulk of the flavanone glycosides.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

The doctrine makes a falsifiable chemical prediction, and this is what makes chen pi worth the codex's attention.

"Fresh peel is too acrid and scattering" is a claim about **volatiles**. The acrid, dispersing, nose-filling quality of a freshly torn mandarin peel *is* its essential oil — dominated by (+)-limonene, with γ-terpinene and related monoterpenes. Monoterpenes are volatile by definition. Store the peel in a breathable container for years and they will evaporate, oxidise, and rearrange. The doctrine says the acridity fades with time; the chemistry says the limonene fraction declines with time. **These are the same sentence in two vocabularies.**

The claim is testable, and it has been tested. Qin et al. (2024), building an age-authentication model precisely because vintage fraud in the chen pi market is profitable, found that **(+)-limonene and γ-terpinene were the key volatile components discriminating peels of different storage ages** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fochx.2024.101220), PMID 38384686). The tradition's sensory grading criterion and the analytical chemist's discriminant variable turn out to be the same molecules. That is a real hit, and it should be recorded as one.

"The qi-regulating and phlegm-transforming action deepens with age" is a claim about the **non-volatile fraction**. As volatiles leave, what remains concentrates — and what remains is the flavonoid load: the polymethoxyflavones (nobiletin, tangeretin, isosinensetin, sinensetin), which are lipophilic, oil-gland-associated, and non-volatile, and the flavanone glycosides (hesperidin above all). Sun et al. (2023) analysed Chachi peel across storage years and reported that **peel stored more than five years showed higher flavonoid content, especially polymethoxyflavones**, and that flavonoids including hesperidin, nobiletin and isosinensetin showed the highest correlation with the improved anti-spleen-deficiency activity of aged peel ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/fsn3.3629), PMID 37970411). Note what that paper is: an explicit attempt to test **"the longer the storage time, the better the bioactivity"** as a hypothesis. The tradition proposed it; the assay did not refute it.

There is a third, less-anticipated layer. Xu et al. (2024) tracked the **polysaccharides** of chen pi across fifteen years of storage and found progressive structural transformation — homogalacturonan regions degrading, molecular size falling, the triple-helical structure disappearing, arabinose displacing galacturonic acid as the dominant monosaccharide as rhamnogalacturonan side chains enriched ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.carbpol.2024.122970), PMID 39643412). The peel is not merely losing volatiles. It is being slowly, enzymatically and hydrolytically **remade**. Ageing here is not storage; it is a low-temperature, multi-year, uncontrolled reaction.

And the doctrine's fine print survives scrutiny too. Traditional practice insists chen pi must be aged in a *breathing* vessel, in a warm humid southern climate, sunned periodically — not sealed, not refrigerated. Peng et al. (2025) tested exactly this and found that **low temperature and low humidity (4°C, 35–40%) hinder the ageing process**, while storage conditions and container oxygen permeability critically govern the chemical trajectory ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/fsn3.70393), PMID 40488194). A refrigerated chen pi does not age. The Xinhui merchant who insists the peel needs the Guangdong damp is, in the narrow technical sense, correct: he is describing reaction kinetics.

Where the doctrine over-reaches: none of this establishes that a fifteen-year peel outperforms a five-year peel *in a patient*. The chemistry keeps changing; "better" is an assumption laid on top of "different." The polysaccharide data in fact show the polysaccharide fraction **fully degraded by fifteen years** — if any activity resides there, the vintage-market logic inverts. "Older is better" is supported as far as the low single-digit years and then becomes, on present evidence, a price signal rather than a pharmacological one.

**Identity discipline — distinguish from neighbouring codex entries.** *C. reticulata* (chen pi, mandarin) is not *C. aurantium* (bitter orange — the source of zhi shi/zhi ke and of synephrine, an adrenergic amine absent in any meaningful quantity here) and not *C. sinensis* (sweet orange). The three overlap in hesperidin and limonene and diverge sharply elsewhere; the polymethoxyflavone-rich, aged-peel identity is specific to *reticulata*. Cross-tradition confusion in the Western literature is common and the entries must be read together.

### Active Components

- **Polymethoxyflavones (PMFs)** — nobiletin, tangeretin, sinensetin, isosinensetin. Lipophilic, non-volatile, concentrated in the flavedo oil glands. The signature chemistry of *reticulata* peel and the class that rises in relative abundance with ageing.
- **Flavanone glycosides** — hesperidin (dominant), narirutin. Concentrated in the albedo. Poorly bioavailable as the glycoside; requires gut-microbial hydrolysis to hesperetin for absorption — which makes the microbiome a gating variable, not a footnote.
- **Essential oil (1–4% fresh, declining with storage)** — (+)-limonene dominant, with γ-terpinene, myrcene, linalool. The "acrid" fraction. [USER: verify oil percentage range against a current pharmacopoeial monograph.]
- **Methyl 2-(methylamino)benzoate (methyl N-methylanthranilate)** — an aroma marker shown to decline sharply under high-heat/high-humidity storage (1.59% → 0.14%) in Peng et al. 2025.
- **Pectic polysaccharides** — homogalacturonan and rhamnogalacturonan-I domains, progressively degraded across storage years.
- **Synephrine** — trace at most. *Not* a meaningful constituent of chen pi. Present in *C. aurantium*; do not import that pharmacology here.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Chen pi is a **regulator, not a driver**. Its Kenetica placement is in the Foundation phase, for practitioners whose digestive terrain is congested rather than deficient — the post-meal heaviness, the distension, the greasy-coat, low-appetite, damp-and-sluggish presentation. It is not a stimulant, not a purgative, and not a tonic. It moves what is already there.

Its most sophisticated use is the classical one: **as an adjuvant to prevent the stagnation that a tonic causes.** Where a protocol adds sweet, dense, moistening agents and the practitioner reports the heavy, bloated, appetite-suppressed response, chen pi is the correction — added to the formula, not substituted for it.

Sourcing guidance follows the evidence rather than the market. Aged peel — three to five years — is defensible on present data. Beyond that the practitioner is buying provenance, aroma, and cultural participation, all of which are legitimate purchases but none of which are established pharmacology. Do not pay vintage prices expecting vintage effects.

### Scientific Correlation

*All citations below retrieved from PubMed. Grades reflect strength of evidence for the specific indication, not enthusiasm.*

**1. The ageing doctrine — volatile decline. GRADE: STRONG (analytical chemistry).**
Qin et al., *Food Chemistry: X* 2024. Flash-GC e-nose + FT-NIR with deep learning discriminated peels by storage year at 98.21% classification accuracy; **(+)-limonene and γ-terpinene were the key discriminating odour components** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fochx.2024.101220), PMID 38384686). The tradition's claim that ageing changes the acrid volatile character is chemically confirmed. Note the paper's motive: age fraud is common enough in the market to warrant a detection method — which is itself a comment on the vintage trade.

**2. The ageing doctrine — flavonoid enrichment and bioactivity. GRADE: MODERATE (animal + spectrum-effect).**
Sun et al., *Food Science & Nutrition* 2023. Chachi peel >5 years storage showed higher flavonoid content, especially polymethoxyflavones; aged peel (>3 yr) outperformed young peel in a spleen-deficiency mouse model (fatigue/depression behaviours, D-xylose and gastrin secretion, colonic occludin expression, gut-microbiota shifts); spectrum-effect analysis attributed activity chiefly to hesperidin, nobiletin, isosinensetin and citrusin III ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/fsn3.3629), PMID 37970411). **Honest reading:** this is a mouse model of a *TCM-defined syndrome*, designed by TCM researchers to test a TCM claim. It is real evidence and it points the right way. It is not a human trial and the circularity of the model must be stated.

**3. Ageing alters polysaccharide architecture. GRADE: STRONG (structural), UNKNOWN clinical meaning.**
Xu et al., *Carbohydrate Polymers* 2024. Across 1→15 years: triple-helix lost, HG regions degraded, molecular size and esterification down, arabinose rising to 41.38 mol% dominance, RG-I side chains enriched, viscosity down, thermal stability up; **polysaccharides fully degraded by 15 years** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.carbpol.2024.122970), PMID 39643412). Confirms ageing is genuine chemical transformation. **Deflates** the unbounded "older is better" doctrine: one measured fraction is *destroyed* by fifteen years.

**4. Storage conditions govern the chemistry. GRADE: MODERATE-STRONG.**
Peng et al., *Food Science & Nutrition* 2025, 24-month controlled study of Guang Chen Pi. Low temp/low humidity hinders ageing; prolonged high temp/high humidity accelerates ferulic acid formation 7-fold but doubles total-flavonoid decline and crashes methyl 2-(methylamino)benzoate (1.59%→0.14%); PE-sealed bags preserved flavonoids and hesperidin better than gunny bags ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/fsn3.70393), PMID 40488194). Vindicates the traditional insistence on warm-humid breathable storage as a real kinetic requirement — while showing the traditional method also has a *cost* the tradition never described.

**5. Nobiletin and gut motility — the "harmonise the middle" mechanism. GRADE: MODERATE (preclinical), and unusually interesting.**
Xiong et al., *Fitoterapia* 2014. Nobiletin exerted **bidirectional regulation** of isolated rat jejunal contractility — stimulatory in six low-contractile states, inhibitory in six high-contractile states — dependent on the enteric nervous system, interstitial cells of Cajal, Ca²⁺ and myosin-light-chain-kinase mechanisms ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fitote.2014.01.018), PMID 24468189). **This is the closest thing in the pharmacological literature to a mechanism for a TCM concept.** "Regulate qi" and "harmonise" (和) do not mean stimulate or sedate; they mean *normalise in whichever direction is deviant*. Bidirectional, state-dependent modulation of gut contractility is precisely that, in receptor terms. The correspondence is striking and should be flagged as one of the codex's better doctrine-to-chemistry mappings — while noting it is a rat organ bath, not a patient.

**6. Citrus flavonoids, inflammation and bowel disease. GRADE: MODERATE (preclinical).**
He et al., *Int J Mol Sci* 2018. In TNBS-induced IBD rats, oral citrus fruit extract (125–500 mg/kg) alleviated weight loss and diarrhoea and reduced colitis inflammatory infiltration and pro-inflammatory cytokines; **nobiletin, naringenin and hesperetin** showed anti-inflammatory effects on LPS-stimulated RAW cells via inhibition of the TNF-α/NF-κB pathway suppressing COX-2 and iNOS; nobiletin and naringenin inhibited jejunal contraction ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms19103057), PMID 30301267). **Caveat, stated plainly:** this study used *Citrus aurantium* fruit, not *C. reticulata* peel. It supports the flavonoid *class* pharmacology, not chen pi specifically. Cited here for the mechanism, and explicitly not as a chen pi result.

**7. Hesperidin in humans. GRADE: WEAK / PENDING — a genuine null in the evidence chain.**
Hesperidin's human clinical base is thinner than its ubiquity in supplement marketing implies, and it is largely built on *orange juice* rather than aged peel. The HESPER-HEALTH randomised crossover trial (Verny et al., *BMJ Open* 2021) is a **protocol paper** — a study *design* for testing whether hesperidin (~200–215 mg/d) mediates vascular benefits of orange juice in 42 at-risk volunteers ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2021-053321), PMID 34848522, NCT04731987, pre-results). It is cited here as an honest marker of where the field stands: the trial was still being run, on a *cardiovascular* endpoint, in a *juice* matrix. **There is no comparable human trial of aged chen pi for its traditional digestive indications retrieved in this search.** Hesperidin also faces a bioavailability problem — the glycoside requires microbial hydrolysis to hesperetin before absorption, making inter-individual response highly variable.

**Overall honest grade for chen pi: mechanism GOOD, ageing doctrine PARTIALLY VINDICATED and chemically real, human clinical evidence ABSENT.** The unusual feature of this entry is not that a traditional claim was confirmed — it is that the tradition made a *specific, non-obvious, chemically testable* claim about processing, wrote it into the drug's name, maintained it for centuries, and it turned out to describe a real reaction. The unusual feature is *epistemic*, not therapeutic. Chen pi is a mild digestive regulator with a fascinating doctrine, not a proven drug. Both halves of that sentence are load-bearing.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** No Unani attestation for mandarin peel is present in the entry, and the CM tag does not fit the plant: *Citrus reticulata* is an East Asian citrus, not part of the classical Perso-Arabic materia medica, and the body carries no *Ibn Sīnā* or Unani content whatsoever. The Unani corpus's strongest cultivation idea — conservation of the finite innate heat and radical moisture — is nowhere assigned to this drug, and no *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed. The tag is a code misapplication (see attestation flag). **(c) absent.**

**Chinese — Bencao (SBJ).** Here the tradition assigns a genuine cultivation status. The citrus that yields this peel — 橘柚 (*jú yòu*) — is entered in the **upper grade (上品)** of the *Shennong Bencao Jing*, the classical grade of substances "harmless to humans" taken long-term to nourish life (養命), as rendered in Yang Shou-zhong's translation *The Divine Farmer's Materia Medica* (Blue Poppy Press, 1998; 橘 being the mandarin, *C. reticulata*). This is a true *san pin* 三品 placement from the foundational Chinese classic, not a late compilation, so it grades **(a) attested** — an upper-grade drug within *yang sheng* 養生, the conservational, non-curative project. Two honest qualifications belong to the record: the classical *shang pin* status attaches to the citrus/peel as such, while the drug's defining refinement — the *aged* peel 陳皮, the graded Guang Chen Pi vintage doctrine, and the *qing pi* 青皮 / *ju hong* 橘紅 anatomical splits — is a post-classical (TCM-stratum) elaboration layered on top; and the upper-grade placement is a longevity-class *grade*, not a claim that ageing tonifies *jing* — the materia medica records no *jing* action here, and none is asserted. **(a) attested** for the *shang pin* grade; the ageing doctrine itself remains processing-craft, not a separate cultivation status.

**Ayurveda (SS).** No Ayurvedic attestation for chen pi is present in the entry. The section filed here under the SS code — "Shen Nong / Materia Medica lineage" — is not Sushrutan at all: it is Chinese (the Shennong stratum and the *chen pi / qing pi / ju hong* distinction), mis-coded (SS = Sushruta/Ayurveda per the registry; see attestation flag). No *rasa*/*vīrya* attribution, and no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* assignment, is recorded for *C. reticulata* peel in any source retrieved. **(c) absent.**

**Practitioner's note.** Chen pi carries a real cultivation credential and a real limit, and the two must be held together. The credential: the citrus sits in the *Shennong* upper grade, the longevity-class of drugs taken over time — consistent with chen pi's actual use as a mild, daily-scale digestive regulator and as the *de-cloying* adjunct that keeps sweet tonics from generating the very damp stagnation they would otherwise cause. The limit: "upper grade" is a conservational grading, not a licence for the vintage-market inference that a fifteen-year peel out-treats a five-year one — the chemistry changes with age but "better in a patient" is unproven, and one measured fraction (the polysaccharides) is destroyed by fifteen years. A cultivator uses aged peel (three to five years is defensible) as a long-tolerable middle-burner regulator, and pays for provenance beyond that as culture, not pharmacology.

### Contraindications

- **Yin deficiency / dry cough.** Chen pi is warm and drying by classification and by chemistry. In dry, hacking, unproductive cough or in yin-deficient heat, it is the wrong direction and will aggravate. This is the tradition's own caution and it is internally coherent.
- **Qi deficiency without stagnation.** A moving drug given to a depleted, non-congested patient depletes further. Chen pi belongs with tonics, not instead of them.
- **Pregnancy.** No adequate human safety data retrieved this run for chen pi at medicinal doses. Culinary quantities are ordinary food exposure. Medicinal dosing in pregnancy: [USER: verify — do not extrapolate from food use].
- **Drug interactions — the citrus/CYP3A4 question.** Furanocoumarin-mediated CYP3A4 inhibition is the well-known grapefruit (*C. paradisi*) problem. Mandarin peel is **not** grapefruit and the furanocoumarin profile differs, but polymethoxyflavones including nobiletin and tangeretin have been reported to interact with CYP enzymes. Patients on narrow-therapeutic-index drugs (ciclosporin, certain statins, some calcium-channel blockers, warfarin) taking concentrated peel extracts — as opposed to eating mandarins — should have this checked. [USER: verify specific CYP data for *C. reticulata* PMFs before advising any individual patient.]
- **Do not import *C. aurantium* pharmacology.** Bitter orange carries synephrine and has cardiovascular stimulant concerns; chen pi does not, and conflating them produces both false alarms and false reassurance in opposite directions.
- **Concentrated extracts ≠ traditional preparation.** All traditional use, and essentially all the evidence above, concerns whole aged peel in decoction or food. Standardised high-dose nobiletin or hesperidin extracts are a different exposure with a different (and thinner) safety record.
- **Market adulteration is a documented, quantified risk.** Age fraud is prevalent enough that analytical detection methods are being published specifically to counter it (Qin et al. 2024). Peel sold as high-vintage is frequently not. Source accordingly — and note that the practitioner's protection here is that the evidence does not support paying for high vintage anyway.
