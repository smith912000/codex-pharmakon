# Hardhun / dabb — ḥardhūn (حرذون) and ḍabb (ضب), the spiny-tailed lizard (Avicenna, Canon Book II)

## *The large desert lizard the Canon calls ḍabb and equates with ḥardhūn, in modern terms a spiny-tailed lizard of the genus Uromastyx (U. aegyptia for the ḍabb); the identification is ours, from the Arabic name and the Canon's remark that it is found "only in the desert of the Arabs", and is of medium confidence at genus level and lower at species level, since the Canon gives no description beyond comparing it with the waral* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Avicenna's Canon (CM).**

*1. Ḥardhūn (Canon Book II, entry *ḥardhūn*, Arabic text line 13210).* The whole entry is two sentences. Nature: "هو الضب وطبعه قريب منه طبع الورل وهو يشبه الورل بما يتعدى به" ("it is the ḍabb, and its nature is near to it, the nature of the *waral*, and it resembles the *waral* in what extends from it", line 13211; the last clause is obscure and I do not translate it further [USER: verify]). Under the eye heading: "its dung is for whiteness [of the eye] and itching, and sharpens the sight" (زبله للبياض والحكة ويحد البصر, line 13212). That is all the entry says.

*2. Ḍabb (Canon Book II, entry *ḍabb*, Arabic text line 19477).* Nature: "the ḍabb is other than the *waral* that is found in our lands, though it resembles it and is near to it in states and powers, and the ḍabb is scarce except in the desert of the Arabs" (الضب غير الورل الموجود في بلادنا وإن كان يشبهه وكان قريب الأحوال والقوى منه وكان الضب يقل إلا في بادية العرب, lines 19478 to 19479). Cosmetic: "its droppings (*baʿr*) are smeared on *kalaf* and *namash* and it benefits" (يطلى بعره على الكلف والنمش فينتفع, line 19479); these are two skin discolourings, usually rendered liver-spots or melasma and freckles, and I give the Arabic words as the Canon prints them. Eye: "its dung is useful for whiteness of the eye and descent of the water" (زبله نافع لبياض العين ونزول الماء, line 19480). The next heading is the hyena, *ḍabʿ* (line 19481).

**Related loci, read but not under these headings.** A headless Canon entry (heading blank in the e-text, Arabic text lines 12419 to 12426) describes a large lizard of the gecko and *sām abraṣ* kind, "other than the ḍabb, and the ḍabb is not found, or seldom, except in the desert", whose head, body and tail differ from the *waral* though it may come near it in nature; "the flesh is very hot"; its dung removes *kalaf* and *namash*, "and fattens, by the strength of its fat and flesh, the layers of women" (ومسمن بقوة شحمه ولحمه طبقات من النساء); it has a power of drawing out splinters and thorns; its powdered dung pulls out warts; and its dung "like the dung of the ḍabb" helps whiteness of the eye "as is said". This is not our animal (it is expressly distinguished from the ḍabb) and is recorded only as the neighbouring entry that the Canon sets against it. Because its heading is lost, I do not name it. [USER: verify] Two droppings passages bear on the animal: the dung of the *ḥardūn* and the *waral* "improves the colour" (line 12717), and the dung of the *waral*, the ḍabb and the crocodile is for whiteness of the eye (lines 12734 to 12735). The blood of the *waral* and the *ḥardhūn* "strengthens the sight" (دم الورل والحرذون يقوي البصر, line 12139).

**Identity doubts.** (1) The Canon says in one place that the ḥardhūn "is the ḍabb" (line 13211) and in another that the ḍabb differs from the *waral* of "our lands" though it is near it (line 19478); the two headings agree that the animal is the desert lizard and are merged on that basis. (2) No description of the animal's body is given in either heading, so "spiny-tailed lizard, *Uromastyx*" rests on the Arabic name and modern usage, not on the text. (3) The Canon's *waral* is a different animal that the text does not describe in these headings; whether it is a monitor lizard is not stated in the lines read. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The Canon names no constituent. In general terms, a lizard's flesh is protein and fat, and its droppings are the waste of a largely plant-eating animal; spiny-tailed lizards are mainly herbivorous. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Legal and conservation limits apply.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The animal's dung for whiteness and itching of the eye, sharpening the sight, and cataract-like "descent of the water"; the droppings smeared on skin discolourings (*kalaf*, *namash*).
- **What the text does not say.** No flesh use under these two headings, no fat use, no dose, no cultivation use.

The Codex does not translate the Canon's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend using any part of this animal.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Canon's only preparations are the dung applied to the eye (lines 13212 and 19480) and the droppings smeared on the skin (line 19479). It gives no method of making them, and no dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any preparation from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Spiny-tailed lizards (genus *Uromastyx*, Agamidae) are stout, mainly plant-eating lizards of the dry lands of North Africa and Arabia, with a heavy spiny tail. Several species are protected, and trade in them is restricted under international wildlife rules. Reptile droppings can carry bacteria such as *Salmonella*, and applying them to the eye is hazardous. The Canon's eye uses have no modern equivalent. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **CM, *ḥardhūn*.** Its nature is "near to" that of the ḍabb and the *waral* (line 13211); no degree is given.
- **CM, *ḍabb*.** "Near to [the *waral*] in states and powers" (line 19478); no degree is given. The neighbouring headless entry calls the flesh of its animal "very hot" (حار اللحم جدا, line 12423), and this is not carried over to our animal.
- No temperament is printed for the ḍabb or ḥardhūn themselves.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* Both Canon headings treat the animal only as a source of dung for the eye and skin. They assign it no place in the maintenance of health, no life-lengthening, and no connection with the vital heat or the spirit (*ruh*). The record stops at the drug.

**Practitioner's note.** A desert lizard whose dung is a minor eye and skin remedy in the Canon, and which the Canon takes some trouble to tell apart from the commoner *waral*. There is nothing in the record for a cultivator, and several limits (hygiene, protection of the animal) against touching it.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Little to map: the two headings name only the eye and the skin. The dung is applied to the eye for whiteness and itching, to sharpen the sight, and for what the Canon calls the descent of the water, which the modern reader would link to cataract. On the skin, the droppings are smeared on kalaf and namash, two discolourations usually rendered as melasma and freckles, and the Canon gives no method for making them. No flesh, fat or other organ is assigned a use under these headings, and no temperament is given for the animal itself, only that its nature is near to that of the waral. The entry records these claims without endorsing them, and the eye is the worst place for reptile droppings, which may carry pathogens.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection.** Reptile droppings may carry pathogens; the eye is the worst place to put them. Do not apply.
- **Conservation and law.** Spiny-tailed lizards are protected or trade-restricted in many countries. Do not collect or trade. [USER: verify the status for any species and place.]
- **Eye disease.** Whiteness of the eye and cataract are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The animal is identified by name only; the *waral* and ḍabb must not be confused. [USER: verify]
