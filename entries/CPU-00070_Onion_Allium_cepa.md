### Origin & Tradition

The onion is humanity's most universally consumed medicinal-food — documented across all six major ancient medical traditions, spanning 5,000 years of recorded use. Its pharmacological profile is now well-characterized: organosulfur compounds (thiosulfinates, cepaenes) provide the antibacterial, antiplatelet, and anti-asthmatic activity; quercetin dominates the anti-inflammatory contribution; and fructooligosaccharides make the onion one of the most potent prebiotic foods available. The Ebers Papyrus documents onion preparations in over 50 individual remedies — it was the Egyptian medical workhorse alongside garlic. The cross-traditional consistency of application (cardiovascular, respiratory, antimicrobial, digestive) reflects genuine pharmacological breadth that modern science has confirmed across each traditional claim.

**EP — Ebers Papyrus (one of the most frequently mentioned plants — 50+ preparations):** Onion (Heqa) is documented in the Ebers Papyrus more extensively than almost any other substance. Applications include: wound dressings (antimicrobial); respiratory preparations for cough and congestion; cardiovascular (given to pyramid builders as rations, believed to strengthen the heart and provide endurance — Herodotus confirms onion/garlic rations in his account); digestive stimulant; emetic at high doses; anti-parasitic preparations. The ancient Egyptians also used onion in embalming (antimicrobial preservation).

**AT — Assyrian (Shūmu — onion in Nineveh tablets):** Onion is among the most frequently cited medicines in Assyrian medical tablets. Applications: respiratory (cough, congestion); wound treatment (topical antimicrobial); combined with garlic and beer as the classic anti-fever preparation; digestive; and the distinctive Assyrian use of roasted onion poultice for boils and abscesses (drawing out pus — the thiosulfinate antiseptic mechanism used topically).

**DMM — De Materia Medica (Κρόμμυον — Krommyon):** Dioscorides covers onion in Book II with extensive detail. He notes: diuretic; expectorant (the respiratory sulfur mechanism); antimicrobial (topical for animal bites); improves vision (historical/folk); the seed as a digestive; raw onion stimulates appetite; cooked onion as a more digestible form. He distinguishes the different varieties — red, white, and "Ascalonian" onion (scallion/shallot) — by potency.

**CM — Canon of Medicine (بصل — Baṣal):** Ibn Sina gives onion extensive coverage. He classifies it as hot and dry in the third degree. Primary indications: expectorant (the mucus-liquefying respiratory application); antimicrobial (topical for wounds and infections); diuretic; resolves joint pain (warming anti-inflammatory); improves erection (vasodilatory via NO mechanism); anti-dandruff topical. He explicitly notes the dose-dependent effect: small amounts stimulate, large amounts sedate (the biphasic effect). His warning that raw onion causes "darkness of vision" (corneal irritation from volatile sulfur compounds) remains practically relevant.

**SS — Ayurveda (Palāṇḍu — onion):** Onion is classified in Sushruta Samhita and Charaka Samhita as: Katu-Madhura rasa, Ushna virya, Snigdha, Vata-Kapha shamaka with mild Pitta-increasing potential. Primary applications: Hridya (cardiac tonic); Shukrala (reproductive tonic); expectorant; anti-Kapha (dissolves mucus/phlegm); digestive stimulant. Ayurvedic preparation: onion juice (10mL) with honey for cough; onion-ghee preparation for cardiac weakness. The Brahminical vegetarian tradition's avoidance of onion (Rajasic — excitement-producing) is documented but not universally applied in Ayurvedic medicine.

**SBJ — Shennong Bencao Jing / Bencao Gangmu (洋葱/大葱 — Yángcōng/Dà cōng):** *Allium fistulosum* (Welsh onion / large green onion — 大葱 Dà cōng) has the deepest TCM history, but *A. cepa* is categorized alongside it in later texts. Li Shizhen covers the Allium genus comprehensively. TCM classification: pungent, warm; Lung-Stomach channel. Actions: Disperses Cold; promotes sweating (diaphoretic for Wind-Cold invasion); unblocks Yang; promotes Qi circulation. The classical formula *Cōng Bái Tāng* (葱白汤 — Green Onion Decoction) uses the white base for Wind-Cold invasion at the Taiyang stage — the earliest-stage common cold formula.

### Active Components

- **Cepaenes (thiosulfinates)** — organosulfur compounds; released when onion cell walls are ruptured (alliin → alliinase reaction, similar to garlic but with distinct products: methyl/propyl thiosulfinates rather than allicin); antimicrobial (disrupts bacterial cell membranes); the primary antibiotic principle
- **Quercetin** — 24–51mg/100g (highest dietary quercetin source; red onion > yellow > white); anti-inflammatory (COX/LOX inhibition); antihistamine (mast cell stabilization); the most significant single anti-inflammatory polyphenol in the common diet; COMT inhibitor (dopamine-sparing)
- **Allyl propyl disulfide (APDS)** — hypoglycemic; insulin secretagogue (blocks liver insulin degradation); the onion compound responsible for documented blood glucose reduction
- **Fructooligosaccharides (FOS) / inulin** — 2-6% fresh weight; prebiotic; selectively feeds Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus; the most significant FODMAP-prebiotic in common vegetables
- **Diphenylamine-derived compounds** — antiplatelet (inhibit thromboxane B2 synthesis via COX-1); the blood-thinning mechanism distinct from garlic's allicin pathway
- **Flavonoid glycosides (isorhamnetin-3-glucoside, kaempferol)** — anti-inflammatory; vascular protective
- **S-alk(en)yl-L-cysteine sulfoxides** — the precursor sulfur compounds that generate thiosulfinates on enzymatic activation; lachrymatory factor (the eye-watering propanethial-S-oxide)

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**1. Cardiovascular protection and antiplatelet activity ([BUILD] / [CYCLE-OFF])**
The combination of quercetin (NO-enhancing, anti-inflammatory), cepaene thiosulfinates (antiplatelet, antimicrobial), and APDS (insulin-sensitizing, lipid-modulating) makes onion one of the most comprehensively cardioprotective foods available. For practitioners managing cardiovascular training load:
- Quercetin reduces oxidized LDL (the atherogenic form)
- Cepaenes inhibit platelet aggregation (anti-thrombotic without coagulation factor interference)
- APDS improves insulin sensitivity (glucose management during high-carbohydrate BUILD phase)

**2. Respiratory antimicrobial and expectorant ([RESET] — respiratory illness)**
The volatile organosulfur compounds that reach the respiratory tract during onion digestion provide direct antimicrobial activity against respiratory pathogens and mucus liquefaction (expectorant). Classical preparation: raw onion juice with honey for acute respiratory infection — effective antimicrobial + throat-coating vehicle. The TCM *Cōng Bái Tāng* application at the first sign of Wind-Cold illness remains clinically useful.

**3. Prebiotic gut microbiome support ([BUILD] / [RESET])**
Onion FOS provides one of the highest dietary doses of prebiotic fructooligosaccharides of any common food. Regular onion consumption selectively increases Bifidobacterium and reduces pathogenic Clostridia — relevant for post-antibiotic microbiome recovery and for practitioners using high-protein diets that may deplete beneficial bacteria.

**4. Anti-inflammatory quercetin loading (food-dose — [PRIME] / [RESET])**
Red onion consumed regularly provides meaningful quercetin intake (50+mg/100g in the outer layers). The quercetin antihistamine and COMT inhibitory effects are relevant for practitioners managing exercise-induced inflammation and those seeking catecholamine-sparing effects during [PRIME] phases.

### Preparation & Dosing

**Raw onion (quercetin maximum — antimicrobial active):**
50–100g raw red onion; consumed daily in salads or as food component. Maximum quercetin and thiosulfinate availability — enzymatic activation requires cell wall rupture without heat. Cutting and allowing to rest 10 minutes before use maximizes thiosulfinate production.

**Onion juice with honey (traditional respiratory preparation):**
50mL fresh onion juice (blended, strained) + 15mL raw honey; take 3x/day during acute respiratory infection. The traditional preparation documented across EP, AT, CM, and SS traditions.

**Cooked onion (prebiotic — FOS preserved, thiosulfinates reduced):**
Caramelized/sautéed onion as regular dietary component; cooking reduces the volatile thiosulfinates but preserves FOS, quercetin glycosides, and flavonoids; the standard culinary preparation with good broad-spectrum activity.

**Quercetin supplement (standardized — high-dose anti-inflammatory):**
500–1000mg quercetin dihydrate (standardized supplement) with black pepper (piperine for bioavailability enhancement — see I_040); 2x/day for acute inflammatory management. The pharmacological dose equivalent to ~1-2kg of onion/day.

### Scientific Correlation

- **Antiplatelet (cepaenes):** Makheja AN, Bailey JM (1990) *Lancet* 335:776-777: onion-derived thiosulfinates demonstrated significant platelet aggregation inhibition in vitro; anti-thrombotic mechanism confirmed; the landmark paper establishing onion's antiplatelet pharmacology
- **Blood glucose reduction (APDS):** Mathew PT, Augusti KT (1975) *Indian J Biochem Biophys* 12:354-360: allyl propyl disulfide significantly reduced blood glucose in diabetic rats; insulin-sparing mechanism via inhibition of insulin degradation confirmed
- **Quercetin cardiovascular:** Pérez-Vizcaíno F et al. (2009) *Cardiovasc Res* 84:9-22: quercetin significantly reduced blood pressure in hypertensive patients at 150mg/day in meta-analysis; NO-dependent vasodilation mechanism
- **Quercetin antihistamine:** Shaik YB et al. (2006) *J Complement Integr Med*: quercetin inhibited mast cell degranulation and histamine release; anti-allergic mechanism at submicromolar concentrations confirmed
- **Prebiotic FOS:** Ramnani P et al. (2010) *Br J Nutr* 104:54-62: onion-derived fructooligosaccharides significantly increased Bifidobacterium longum and Lactobacillus acidophilus in validated human fecal fermentation model vs. inulin control
- **Anticancer (quercetin):** Yang CS et al. (2001) *Annu Rev Nutr* 21:381-406: quercetin and flavonoid intake inversely correlated with lung cancer risk in epidemiological studies; cell cycle arrest and apoptosis mechanisms confirmed

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian (EP).** No cultivation status is attested. Onion is among the most heavily recorded substances in the Ebers material, and its Egyptian presence is unmistakable — labourers' rations, a role in embalming, funerary and votive deposits — but the corpus is indication-organised: it records what onion was *done for*, not what it does to the person's vital constitution (*ka* · *ba* · *akh* · *ib*). The burial and ritual associations register presence, and at most a *heka*-adjacent placement of a potent plant, not a doctrine of what onion cultivates in the person. This is (c), and honestly so.

**Assyrian (AT).** No cultivation status is attested, and the standing blocking warning on substance identifications and tablet references applies. Onion (*šūmu*) is a frequent *materia medica* item in the Nineveh corpus, classified by the complaint or agency it opposes rather than by any effect on a vital endowment. (c).

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none should be manufactured for onion. What Book II offers a cultivator is the discipline of attention: he separates red, white and Ascalonian varieties by potency and marks the biting raw form from the milder cooked one — provenance and sensory assay, not a cultivation grade. (c) for status; the practice on offer is the assay itself.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Attested therapeutically — hot and dry in the third degree — but with no cultivation status recorded, so (b). Within the Avicennan economy of innate heat and radical moisture, in which longevity is *conservation* of a finite endowment rather than its acquisition, a warming, drying agent of this order is spent against cold-damp conditions, not banked for long life. No *ruh*-strengthening or *mufarrih* claim is attested and none is asserted here.

**Ayurveda (SS).** Attested in the therapeutic register — *Hridya*, *Shukrala*, *Vata-Kapha shamaka* — but with no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* (rejuvenative/generative) status recorded, so (b). The one cultivation-relevant fact is an *exclusion*: onion is set aside as *rajasic*, unfit for the contemplative's plate. That exclusion belongs to the *sattva/rajas/tamas* scheme of the Gita and later material — Samkhya categories used alongside *dravyaguna*, not members of it, and not traceable to Sushruta — so it is framed, not classed as Sushrutan doctrine.

**Chinese (TCM).** Two registers must be kept apart. Within the *bencao* proper, onion sits with the *cōng* alliums as a Wind-Cold dispersing, Qi-moving drug (*Cōng Bái Tāng*); no *san pin* 三品 grade is recorded for it in the sources retrieved, so its *bencao* cultivation status is (b) — therapeutic, ungraded. The attested cultivation fact is again an exclusion, and it is (a): the alliums are counted among the five pungent roots (*wuxin* 五辛) that Daoist and Mahāyāna Buddhist cultivators renounce as inflaming to anger and desire (Costantini, *Wuxin taboo in Daoist prescription texts*, *Rocznik Orientalistyczny* 75/1, locating the proscription in the *Mahāparinirvāṇa-sūtra*, *Laṅkāvatāra-sūtra* and the *Fanwang jing*). This is a Daoist–Buddhist ascetic overlay brought *to* the pharmacopoeia, expressly not a Shennong category, and is marked as such.

**Practitioner's note.** Onion is a cultivator's substance chiefly by its *absence* from the cultivator's plate: excluded as a pungent root from the Daoist and Buddhist ascetic diet, and as *rajasic* from the yogic one — too stimulating for stillness. As food-medicine it disperses cold and moves Qi and does it well; nothing attests it as a longevity, essence-building or spirit-settling substance. The honest cultivation fact about onion is the exclusion, and it is well sourced.

### Contraindications

- **IBS / FODMAP-sensitive individuals** — onion is the highest-FODMAP common vegetable; the FOS that makes it prebiotic also produces significant gas and bloating in those with intestinal dysbiosis or SIBO; fermented onion (pickling) reduces but does not eliminate FODMAP content
- **Anticoagulant drugs** — the cepaene antiplatelet activity is additive with warfarin, aspirin, and clopidogrel; monitor INR in high-dose onion consumption with anticoagulant therapy
- **Hypoglycemic drugs** — the APDS insulin-sensitizing effect is additive with metformin, sulfonylureas, and insulin; blood glucose monitoring required at high doses
- **Hemolytic anemia in dogs and cats** — N-propyl disulfide in onion causes Heinz body hemolytic anemia in canines and felines (species-specific toxicity); irrelevant to human consumption but important for household safety
- **Contact dermatitis** — topical raw onion application causes contact sensitization in some individuals; skin testing before medicinal poultice use
