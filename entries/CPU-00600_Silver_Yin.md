**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** There is no chapter on silver itself in *De Materia Medica* Book 5 (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`). Silver appears through its by-products. 5-101 SKORIA ARGUROU, "Silver Slag" (line 10336): "Scoria [slag] of silver is called helcysma or encauma. It has the same strength as plumbago [5-100]; as a result it is mixed with black plasters, and those for forming new skin, as it is extractive [draws out] and astringent." 5-100 MOLUBDAINA (line 10327) says plumbago "is made from silver and gold". 5-102 LITHARGUROS (line 10339), headed by the translator "Monoxide of Lead Monoxide of Silver" and "TOXIC", describes *spuma argenti*, "froth of silver", made "from silver and some from lead", the best being "a yellow glittering sort called chrysitis"; the Sicilian kind is called *argyritis* and "that from silver is called calabritis". It is "astringent and softening, filling up hollownesses, repressing abnormal growths of flesh, and forming new skins", and washed litharge is "good to put in eye medicines". Silver also appears in the mercury chapter 5-110 (line 10398): quicksilver "is also found in places where silver is smelted" and is kept "in glass, lead, tin or silver jars".

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No Avicenna locus was found. `_sources/avicenna.csv` has no silver row and no Avicenna text is on disk. No *Canon* chapter reference exists to give. The tag rests on the spine row alone. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) has no silver entry; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` has none and `_sources/shennong.csv` has none (the only Chinese rows nearby are 水銀, mercury, and 丹砂). Silver is named only as a metal that other drugs act on: Shui Yin (mercury) "kills the toxins of gold, silver, copper, and tin" (line 880), Shi Dan "may change iron into copper, gold, or silver" (lines 1104-1105), and Shi Liu Huang can "transform rare matters like gold, silver, copper, and iron" (line 1141). These are mentions in other entries. The "Yin" in this entry's spine name has no printed source I could find. [USER: verify]

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Rajata* is named in the Metal group, *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 2126): "Silver has an acid taste, is laxative and cooling (in its potency), presents a glossy or oily aspect, and destroys the Pittam and Vayu" in Bhishagratna's English. It is one of seven in the *Trapvadi* group of ch.38 (`SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 439-448: Trapu, Sisa, Tamra, Rajata, Krishna-Lauha, Suvarna, Lohamala), described as "a good vermifuge" that neutralises chemical poison from incompatible combinations and covers jaundice, chlorosis, urethral discharges, heart disease and thirst. Ch.1 (lines 448-451) places silver among the earthy substances. Bhishagratna's *Uttarasthana* ch.12 (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch12.txt` lines 144-146) lists "powdered (dead) metals (Gold, Silver, Iron, Copper, etc.)" in a scarifying collyrium. Silver bowls are prescribed for serving *peya* and fluids (ch.46 lines 3018-3026), and silver appears among the ingredients of an anti-poison paste in *Kalpasthana* ch.3 (line 134, "silver (Tara), mercury (Sutara)"). Appendix 3 of Vol. III (line 33109) glosses "Ta'ra" as "Silver".

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Explanatory Tantra ch.20, precious metals (Clark 1995, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 805): "silver has ten names and is known to be smelted from ore. It is of two types—'goat silver' and 'sheep silver'", with the regional silvers named (Hor, Chinese, Indian, Mon, Russian and Kham) and 'sheep silver' coming "from iron, tin, lead and copper"; "It has the same (astringent) taste and post-digestive taste as gold, and by its power it dries lymph disorders, pus and blood." Silver ore, *dngul.rdo* (line 829), "drains lymph [accumulations]". The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text says the same (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3330): "Silver dries up chhuser, pus and impure blood." Silver is also named in the rejuvenation chapter (see the register).

### Active Components

Silver is the element Ag, a soft white metal. Silver ions are antimicrobial, and silver compounds have been used on wounds and burns in modern medicine. Silver taken in repeatedly can deposit in skin and other tissue as a permanent grey-blue discolouration (argyria). The slag and litharge of the Greek text are different materials, residues of smelting, and litharge is a lead oxide.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources read speak of silver as a drying, astringent or cooling drug, as a vessel metal, and (Tibetan) as an ash in a rejuvenation compound. Swallowing silver or silver "colloids" has no sourced warrant in these texts and risks argyria. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation for silver.

### Preparation & Dosing

DMM 5-102 gives long washing and whitening procedures for litharge (not for silver). SS gives silver as a powdered "dead" metal in collyria, as water in which "a red-hot gold or silver bar has been immersed" for bathing a newborn (*Sharira* ch.10 line 231), and as water made hot by immersing heated bits of gold, silver, etc. (*Uttara* ch.48 line 123). The Tibetan texts name the "ash" (Clark, "calcinated") of silver in the rejuvenation compound but give no method for it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Antimicrobial activity of silver ions and the use of silver compounds on burns and wounds are well-established. Argyria from ingested silver is well-established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No Egyptian locus for silver was found. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*.

**Assyrian (AT).** *(c) Absent, no locus.* Nothing was read. The blocking warning is in force, and whether silver was classed by the agency it opposes cannot be said. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, for silver slag and silver-derived litharge only.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What the chapters offer is a discipline of attention to smelting by-products: the grades of litharge by place (Attic best, then Spanish, Campanian, Sicilian), the colour of the best, the long washing, and the translator's "TOXIC". No cultivation status is assigned, and there is no chapter on the metal.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent, and the attestation rests on a spine row.* No Avicenna text or list row was found. Innate heat and radical moisture cannot be tied to silver without a source and are not tied to it here. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(c) Absent.* Silver has no entry and so no *san pin* grade; it is named only as a metal that mercury, copper and sulphur act on (Yang lines 880, 1104-1105, 1141). Nothing here is a cultivation status.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* *Rajata* is named in the ch.46 Metal group and the ch.38 *Trapvadi* group, with taste and potency as above; these are food-and-drug groups. I found it in no *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. Gold has a "tonic or restorative elixir" line in the same Metal group; silver's line does not.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed.* Silver sits among the precious metal medicines of ch.20 with a drying action (Clark line 805; MTK line 3330). It is also named in the rejuvenation chapter, ch.23: Clark (line 1270) says "well-purified mineral exudate [should be added to calcinated gold, silver, copper and iron, kept in an iron container and taken] with (other) wholesome medicines", and MTK (line 3925, p.237) has the processed *Drak shhun* "combined with the ash of gold, silver, copper and iron ... This cures all disorders and promotes longevity." The ash of four metals is one ingredient of a compound whose principal is mineral exudate, so silver is not graded (a). No *bcud len* use of silver and no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice was found.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing sourced supports taking silver for cultivation. The strongest line is Tibetan, and there silver is one of four metal ashes in a supervised rejuvenation course that begins with oil therapy, a bath and internal cleansing and demands a clean, quiet place and abstinence (Clark ch.23; MTK ch.23). A cultivator can read that as the tradition's own caution about context and leave the metal itself alone. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Ingested silver can cause permanent argyria and offers no sourced benefit here. Litharge and the "silver" by-products in the Greek text are lead-bearing and toxic. Do not take any silver preparation by mouth on the strength of this entry. Calcined or "ashed" metals in the traditional compounds are not standardised and may carry other toxic metals.
