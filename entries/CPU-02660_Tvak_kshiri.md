# Tvak-kshiri — Tvak-kshiri (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation; printed "Tvak-kshiri" in the appendix and "Tvakkshiri" in the text)

## *Curcuma angustifolia Roxb., the East Indian arrowroot plant (family Zingiberaceae), from which a starch is made; the identification is ours, taken from Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3, which prints "Curcuma AngusUfoHa" against the name by position in the OCR'd columns, and is of medium confidence, since the body text of the translation gives no Latin name, names no part and describes no plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, in two places.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 8 November 2026 as an unverified residual, "Curcuma AngusUfoHa [SS]", read from the translation's Appendix; this entry replaces it. The search covered the Sushruta chapter files and the three Bhishagratna volumes under `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`; no other text on disk was found to carry the name.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS), the index.** In Bhishagratna's Vol. III, Appendix No. 3, the Sanskrit name is printed "Tvak-kshiri" at the head of the T column on one page (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 33156). The Latin names stand in a separate block below (id line 33188). The first of them, "Curcuma AngusUfoHa" (*Curcuma angustifolia*), is the one that pairs with Tvak-kshiri by position. The pairing is supported by the neighbours: the next name, Tri-kantaka, carries "See Gokshura" (line 33189), and the one after it, Triputi, carries "Lathyrus Sativus". The OCR is damaged ("AngusUfoHa") and the Latin name belongs to Bhishagratna, not to Sushruta.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS), the text.** Sutrasthana ch. 44, on the choice of purgatives, gives a compound for fever: "A compound consisting of equal parts of sugar and powdered Ajagandha, Tvakkshiri, Vidari and Trivrit and licked with honey and clarified butter, proves curative in a fever with thirst and a burning sensation of the body" (`Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 17416; chapter file `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch44.txt` line 141). The translation prints no Latin name there, names no part of the plant, and gives no quantity beyond "equal parts". Tvak-kshiri occurs in this one compound only, together with Ajagandha, Vidari and Trivrit, none of which is identified here.

### Preparation & Dosing

The one preparation given is the compound quoted above: equal parts of sugar and of the powdered drugs (Ajagandha, Tvakkshiri, Vidari, Trivrit), licked with honey and clarified butter (Vol. I line 17416). "Equal parts" is a ratio and not a dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Curcuma angustifolia* is a plant of the Indian subcontinent whose rhizome yields a starch used as a food. Starch and sugar are digestible carbohydrates and are not in themselves a treatment for fever. The text's fever with thirst and burning has no modern equivalent and is not translated into one. ### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Tvak-kshiri stands in the therapeutic frame of Sutrasthana ch. 44, a compound for fever. The passage is not in a rasayana or vajikarana chapter and assigns no status for *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*. The sattva, rajas and tamas categories are Samkhya terms used beside dravyaguna and are not part of Sushruta's treatment of this drug.

**Practitioner's note.** A starchy powder named once, in a fever compound, with no dose. A cultivator has nothing here to act on, and the identification is uncertain.
