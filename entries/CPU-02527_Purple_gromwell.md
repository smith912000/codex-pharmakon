# Purple gromwell — Zi Cao 紫草 (Shennong bencao jing); aliases Zi Dan 紫丹, Zi Fu 紫芙

## *Arnebia euchroma (Royle) I.M.Johnst., purple gromwell (zicao), a red-rooted borage-family herb whose root is a purple-red dye; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence, because Lithospermum erythrorhizon Siebold & Zucc. and Arnebia guttata Bunge are the other plants sold as zicao and the text cannot tell them apart* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The only text read for this entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Its other edition on disk, Yang 1998, also lists "Zi Cao" (`names.csv` line 126, pharmaceutical Latin "Radix Lithospermi Seu Amebiae", the same drug under a genus-level Latin name); it is the same tradition. Other texts on disk name other gromwells: Dioscorides' *lithospermon* (DMM 3-158; spine entry I_592, *Lithospermum officinale*) and, in the Dioscorides index, an *anchousa* that includes *Alkanna tinctoria* (DMM 4-23). They are different plants from different traditions, not this drug, and they are not tagged here.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 紫草 in the middle classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 紫草 *zi cao*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2095). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，寒 ("bitter, cold"); 主心腹邪气 ("governs evil qi of the heart and abdomen"), 五疸 ("the five jaundices"), 补中益气 ("mends the centre and benefits qi"), 利九窍 ("frees the nine orifices"), 通水道 ("opens the water passages"); aliases 一名紫丹 and 一名紫芙 ("also called zi dan, zi fu"); habitat 生川谷 ("grows in river valleys"). The parenthetical in the paragraph says one source (the Taiping yulan) adds the alias 地血, "earth blood", and that the Daguan edition lacks the sentence; the Codex records the alias as a later gloss only. There is no 久服 clause in the paragraph.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century) says, with its source the Yulan: 紫草节赤。二月花, "the zicao has red joints; it flowers in the second month" (line 2097). The Mingyi bielu layer adds: 生砀山及楚地。三月采根，阴干, "grows on Mount Dang and in the Chu region; the root is gathered in the third month and dried in the shade" (line 2099). These are the only descriptive features of the plant in the whole entry: red joints, a second-month flower, and a root that is the harvested part.

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 2101) is about the name and the colour. It cites the Shuowen (茈 is a plant; 藐 is the "zi" plant; a further plant "can dye..." with a character missing in the transcription), the Guangya, the Shan hai jing (many zi plants on Mount Lao), Guo Pu (it is also called zi [missing], and "in dyeing gives purple", 中染紫也) and the Erya, where Guo Pu says of the 藐 that "it can be used to dye purple" (可以染紫). The commentators therefore describe a plant used as a purple dye. This is consistent with a red-rooted gromwell, but it does not choose between the three species, and it is the commentators' and not the Shennong text's.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the red-rooted gromwells (*Arnebia* and *Lithospermum* species used as zicao) are known to contain red naphthoquinone pigments (the shikonin / alkannin family) in the root, which is the basis of their use as a dye. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, cold root for "evil qi" of the heart and abdomen and for the five jaundices, and says it mends the centre, benefits qi, frees the nine orifices and opens the water passages. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Mingyi layer names the part (the root) and the preparation as far as it goes: gathered in the third month and dried in the shade (line 2099). The Shennong paragraph names no preparation. The preface (line 4121) says medicines differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The red root pigments of the gromwells have been used as dyes. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The classic's categories (jaundices, "evil qi", the nine orifices) are not modern diagnoses, so no modern finding can be set beside them.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味苦，寒, "bitter, cold" (line 2095). No other quality is printed in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph for 紫草 reads 味苦，寒 and files the root in the middle grade (中品). The classic's preface (line 1677) says the middle medicines "nourish the nature, to correspond to man; non-toxic and toxic, weigh what is fitting; for those who wish to check disease and mend weakness, base it on the middle classic" (主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经). The paragraph has no 久服 ("taken long") clause and no promise of lightening the body or of freedom from ageing. Its "mends the centre and benefits qi" is an action in the indication list and not a status for the person. The register is therefore (b): a middle-grade drug with a therapeutic profile and no cultivation promise. The Wu Pu and Mingyi layers add description and harvest only. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 紫草 *zi cao*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2095)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the paragraph gives a cultivator a practice. The classic puts the root in the middle grade, which its preface ties to checking disease and mending weakness with a weighing of the drug's strength, and gives it no long-use clause. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

- **Identity:** several plants are sold as zicao; they are not interchangeable and the text cannot tell them apart.
- **Pregnancy and nursing:** no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Jaundice:** the text names the five jaundices among its uses, which is historical record only; jaundice needs medical assessment.
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given.
