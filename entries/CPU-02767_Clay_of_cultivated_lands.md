# Clay of cultivated lands (Dioscorides) — ṭīn al-araḍīn al-mazrūʿa (طين الأرضين المزروعة)

## *A clay from ploughed ground, white or ash-grey, washed and made into tablets; the Canon quotes Dioscorides, and the identification as a particular named clay is not made here, since the text gives no place name and the cheap guess of Cimolian earth (CPU-02409) rests on nothing in the lines read; confidence low* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. Book II, entry *ṭīn al-araḍīn al-mazrūʿa*, Arabic text from line 13656 of `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk was not run beyond the spine check. 

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The entry opens "Dioscorides said" (قال ديسقوريدوس) and so reports a Greek source. Of all the clays used in medicine: "in general they have an astringent, softening, cooling, [agglutinating] power" (قوة قابضة ملينة مبردة مغرية; the fourth word is read as *mughriya* and is [USER: verify]), and each has its own special benefit. The clay of the sown lands "is in part very white and in part ash-coloured, and the ash-coloured is better than the white and softer" (ومنها ما هو رمادي وهو الأجود من الأبيض وألين). "If rubbed on copper it gives out the colour of basil (*rayḥān*)." It "is washed as white lead (*isfīdhāj*) is washed": water is poured on repeatedly in the evening and left until it clears, the clay is warmed in the sun and the work is repeated for ten days, then it is ground in the sun and made into tablets (*aqrāṣ*). Properties: "an astringent, cooling power, softening a little, as is said" (فيما يقال). Wounds and ulcers: "it fills ulcers with blood and glues wounds together when they first occur".

**Not merged.** The clay entries that follow are separate headings in the Canon and are separate spine rows; the lines about a bath clay used instead of *ushnān* and natron, and about hanging on a woman in labour, belong to the next heading, not to this one.

### Active Components

No composition is given in the text. Clays in general are hydrated aluminium silicates; the particular clay here is not identified. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The text records a washed clay for filling ulcers and sealing fresh wounds. The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend applying earth to wounds, which carries a risk of infection.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation is the washing and settling over ten days, sun-warming, grinding and tabletting described in line 13656 onward. No dose and no method of applying it is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General fact only: washed clays are fine mineral powders that absorb moisture; nothing about this clay is established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Avicenna quoting Dioscorides: astringent, cooling, slightly softening ("as is said"). No degree is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No cultivation framing is attested. **(c) Absent.** The *Canon* entry is organised by organ and complaint and says nothing here about innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruh*. 

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to practise. The entry is a Greek recipe for refining a clay, copied into the Canon.

### Contraindications

- Soil and clay can carry spores and pathogens; do not put them on wounds.
- The clay is not securely identified.
