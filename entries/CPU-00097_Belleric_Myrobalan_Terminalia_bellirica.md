### Origin & Tradition

Belleric myrobalan is the fruit of a large deciduous tree of the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia, and one of the three "myrobalans" that form the foundation of Ayurvedic medicine. Its Sanskrit name **Bibhitaki** is traditionally glossed as "the one that removes fear (of disease)." Together with **Haritaki** (*Terminalia chebula*) and **Amalaki** (*Phyllanthus emblica*) it composes **Triphala** ("three fruits"), the single most important and widely used compound of Ayurveda — a gentle, balancing tonic-laxative for digestion, elimination and rejuvenation in which Bibhitaki is the *kapha*-reducing member.

On its own Bibhitaki is valued chiefly as an **astringent, drying, respiratory and digestive** remedy: a remedy for cough, hoarseness and excess respiratory mucus (its drying, *kapha*-reducing action on the chest), for sore throat (the fruit chewed), and — depending on ripeness and preparation — as both a mild laxative (regulating elimination within Triphala) and an astringent for loose bowels. Across the Unani (*balela*) and Tibetan traditions it carries the same astringent-balancing, respiratory and digestive profile.

The fruit is rich in **tannins and gallic/ellagic acid derivatives**, the chemical basis of its astringency and its much-studied antioxidant activity, alongside lignans and other constituents of pharmacological interest. The myrobalans together exemplify the Ayurvedic art of balancing astringent-tonic fruits into a gentle, regulating whole.
