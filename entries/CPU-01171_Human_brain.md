# Human brain — "Human brain" (Mileh, in the Men-Tsee-Khang transliteration; rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20, "Brain")
## *Human brain tissue named in the Tibetan materia medica; a historical record, not a recommended practice* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The locus is Clark's 1995 translation, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, "the powers of medicines" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). Section (8), "Medicines of Animal Origin", lists "thirteen medicines of animal origin ... horn, bone, meat, blood, bile, fat, brain, skin, nails, hair, urine, droppings and whole body" (lines 1147-1148). Human material is therefore handled by this text inside the animal group. Under the sub-heading "Brain" (line 1181) the text reads, at line 1182:

"Goat brain: unites severed ligaments and tendons. Sheep brain: cures dizziness and vertigo. Brain of game animals (e.g. deer): halts diarrhoea. Rabbit brain: cures colic. Human brain: reduces swellings and cures lymph disorders."

Human brain is the last of five brains listed, and its stated actions are two: it reduces swellings and it cures lymph disorders. `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 367 lists "Mileh", animal, with the source "Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Efficacy of Medicinal Substances)"; that is the Men-Tsee-Khang transliteration, and the glossary of the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` lines 4920 and 4937) lists *Mi leh* (*mi klad*) in a column of five *Mi* terms whose glosses read "Human urine / Human excreta / Human brain / Human flesh / Human fat". The term-to-gloss pairing rests on the column layout of the scanned glossary and is my reading. [USER: verify]

The Men-Tsee-Khang text gives the same group of brains in the same order (line 3548): "Goat brain heals torn ligaments and tendons. Sheep brain cures dizziness and reeling. The brain of herbivorous wild animals stops diarrhea. Rabbit brain cures dysentery. Mileh reduces swelling and treats disorders of the chhuser." So "reduces swellings" agrees across the two. "Lymph disorders" in Clark corresponds to "disorders of the chhuser" in the Men-Tsee-Khang text; I did not find a definition of *chhuser* in the lines read. [USER: verify] (The same Men-Tsee-Khang line renders the rabbit brain's action as "dysentery" where Clark has "colic", which shows that the two English renderings of one Tibetan source differ in places.)

Clark's preface says that "nowadays only very few animal products are used, although many are documented in the traditional texts" (line 57). He also says that he "interpolated liberally from the Blue Aquamarine commentary" (line 80); the brackets in the neighbouring lines are not explained in the lines read. The human-brain line itself carries no bracket. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Brain tissue is about three quarters water, with the remainder mostly lipid (including cholesterol and phospholipids) and protein. The nervous system also holds the proteins that, when misfolded, cause prion disease. The text gives no preparation and no analysis was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. This entry records what the Tibetan text says about human brain, and nothing else.

It is not a recommended practice. Taking human brain tissue by mouth or otherwise is dangerous, unlawful in most places and ethically unacceptable. The Codex's application layer offers no protocol, no sourcing advice and no substitute.

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General facts: no brain tissue has been shown to reduce swellings or treat disorders of the lymphatic system. Eating human brain is a documented cause of a fatal prion disease: kuru, among the Fore people of Papua New Guinea, was transmitted through funerary handling and consumption of human tissue including brain, and prion diseases have no cure. Human nervous tissue can also carry other infectious agents.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT): the chapter 20 line gives actions only (reduces swellings, cures lymph disorders). It prints no taste, potency or post-digestive taste for human brain, and none is assigned here.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Human brain is attested in the Explanatory Tantra ch.20 animal section as a therapeutic substance, with two stated actions. The frame it sits in is read from Clark's preface and root text: the three humors (wind, bile, phlegm) "are rooted in attachment, hatred and obscuration respectively" (line 51), and treatment relies on "the fourfold food, behavioral pattern, medicine and accessory therapy" (line 1299). Nothing in the lines read gives human brain a place in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or in any practice of the subtle body, and no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition. The text does not say why this material was chosen.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this. The text lists human brain as a medicine among thirteen animal categories, with no cultivation claim and no method. It is recorded here as history only.

### Contraindications

- **Not a recommended practice.** This records a historical entry in a Tibetan materia medica. Nothing here is advice to obtain, handle or take human tissue.
- **Prion disease:** human brain tissue can transmit fatal, incurable prion disease; the risk exists whatever the preparation.
- **Infection:** human tissue can carry other infectious agents.
- **Legal:** possession, trade and use of human remains and tissue are restricted or unlawful in many jurisdictions. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** the material comes from the dead, with questions of consent, dignity and the wishes of families and communities.
- **Swellings and lymph conditions:** persistent or growing swellings need ordinary medical assessment.
