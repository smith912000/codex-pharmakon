# Long Prickly-headed Poppy — Blindeyes / Long-headed Poppy / Blaweed

## *Papaver dubium* L. — Long Prickly-headed Poppy, Long-headed Poppy, Blindeyes; native names: Greek *mḗkōn rhoiás* group (the red field poppies), Latin *papaver dubium* ("the doubtful poppy," for its uncertain distinction from corn poppy), Arabic *ḫashḫāsh aḥmar* barrī (wild red poppy) | Family: Papaveraceae

**Tradition Tags:** EP, AT, DMM, CM

**Energetic Classification:** Galenic/Unani — Cold in the first degree, Moist in the first-to-second degree (*bārid raṭb*), a gentle refrigerant-relaxant — expressly **far weaker and non-narcotic** compared with the opium poppy

### Origin & Tradition

*Papaver dubium* is a red field poppy of arable land and disturbed ground across Europe, the Mediterranean, and western Asia — the "doubtful poppy," so named by botanists because it is easily confused with its near-twin the corn poppy (*P. rhoeas*, treated separately in this Codex). Longer-capsuled and paler-scarlet than corn poppy, it belongs to the same *rhoeas*-type group of **non-opium** red poppies: the folk poppies of children's cough syrups, mild sleep teas, and petal colourants — emphatically **not** the opium poppy (*P. somniferum*), which alone carries morphine. The single most important fact of this entry is a *negative* one: *P. dubium*, like corn poppy, contains **rhoeadine-type isoquinoline alkaloids only** — no morphine, codeine, or thebaine — and was never a true narcotic in any tradition. A second important caveat governs the whole historical section: the classical and pre-classical sources almost never distinguish *P. dubium* from *P. rhoeas*; the two travelled together as "the red field poppy," and most textual attestations are, at best, group-level.

**DMM (Dioscorides / Graeco-Roman tradition, *De Materia Medica*, c. 50–70 CE):** Dioscorides describes *mḗkōn rhoiás* — the "flowing" red poppy of the fields, named for its readily shed scarlet petals — and recommends a decoction or syrup of the petals and heads as a **mild** hypnotic and soother of inflammation, explicitly weaker than the opium-yielding poppy (*mḗkōn hēmeros*). *P. dubium* falls within this red-field-poppy group and is not separated from *P. rhoeas* in the ancient text; the attestation is to the group, and the doctrine — gentle, cooling, sleep-favouring, *not* narcotic — applies to both.

**CM (Avicenna / Unani tradition, *al-Qānūn fī al-Ṭibb*, c. 1025 CE):** Avicenna grades the poppies (*khashkhāsh*) by colour and strength. The wild red poppy (*khashkhāsh aḥmar / barrī*) is Cold & Moist and **markedly weaker** than the white/black opium poppy, which he classes among the strong cold narcotics capable of dangerous stupefaction. The red poppy is directed to gentle promotion of sleep, soothing of "hot" coughs and pleuritic irritation, and calming of restlessness — a *mulaṭṭif* (demulcent-softener) rather than a *mukhaddir* (true stupefacient). This graded distinction is doctrinally central and applies to the whole red-field-poppy group, *P. dubium* included.

**AT (Assyrian / Mesopotamian cuneiform medicine):** The Mesopotamian materia medica records a poppy-group of "quieting" plants used for pain, sleeplessness, and restless/crying conditions, and red *Papaver* species grew as arable weeds across the Fertile Crescent. Whether any passage refers specifically to *P. dubium* versus *P. rhoeas* versus *P. somniferum* cannot be resolved from the surviving lexical lists, and no secure Akkadian binomial for the long-headed field poppy should be asserted [USER: verify against a current Assyrian materia medica concordance before citing any specific tablet]. Defensible only at the level of a general poppy-group of calming plants.

**EP (Ebers Papyrus / Egyptian tradition, c. 1550 BCE):** The Egyptian medical papyri include poppy-group remedies (*špnn* is the debated candidate term) for calming and for crying children and within compound sedative/analgesic preparations. Secure species assignment between field poppies and the opium poppy is impossible from the orthography, and the strong "quieting a screaming child" indication historically attached most firmly to opium-bearing material. *P. dubium*'s Egyptian role is best stated as part of a cooling, calming poppy-group, not a documented monograph [USER: verify species attribution].

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** The doctrine — inherited straight from the corn-poppy entry because the two plants share it — is *coldness without narcosis*: a refrigerant that quiets without extinguishing. It maps onto chemistry with the same precision. The opium poppy's cold, stupefying "first degree of poison" is the pharmacology of **morphinan** alkaloids at µ-opioid receptors; *P. dubium* contains **no morphinans**. Its alkaloids are **rhoeadine-type** isoquinolines (rhoeadine, rhoeagenine and relatives) plus minor aporphine/protopine-group compounds — a class with, at most, mild, non-opioid, and poorly-characterised CNS activity, and no addictive narcotic action. So the Galenic intuition "same *kind* of cold, but far gentler" is molecularly correct: same family and broad sedative *gestalt*, entirely different and far weaker receptor pharmacology. The second doctrinal thread is the **redness**: the classical eye read the blood-scarlet petal as a signature of blood and heart; the modern reading is cyanidin-based **anthocyanin** pigment, a water-soluble antioxidant flavonoid, pharmacologically distinct from any mild sedation — the colourant that made these petals a food and syrup dye is not the calming principle. Where the doctrine must be **deflated for this species specifically**: the sedative evidence that does exist is for the *congener* corn poppy, not for *P. dubium* itself, which is essentially unstudied. To claim a defined sedative virtue for *P. dubium* on the strength of *P. rhoeas* data is an inference, not a fact — and the entry says so plainly.

### Active Components

- **Rhoeadine-type isoquinoline alkaloids (rhoeadine, rhoeagenine and relatives)** — The characteristic *rhoeas*-group alkaloids, concentrated in latex, capsule, and green aerial parts; isoquinoline-derived but **not** morphinan. The plausible (but weak and poorly-defined) basis for the group's gentle "quieting" reputation. Their presence in *P. dubium* is inferred from the closely related, well-characterised *P. rhoeas* alkaloid profile; species-specific quantification is sparse.
- **Minor aporphine/proaporphine and protopine-group alkaloids (e.g. roemerine-, mecambrine-, protopine-type)** — Reported across the *rhoeas* group; none confer opioid activity.
- **Cyanidin-based anthocyanins** — Water-soluble red petal pigment; antioxidant; the food/colourant constituent, **not** sedative.
- **Mucilage / demulcent polysaccharides** — Contribute to the soothing, film-forming "pectoral" action underlying the cough-soother use.
- **Meconic-acid-poor, morphinan-free profile** — The defining *negative* feature and the single most important safety point: no opium load.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. `[RESET]` **Gentle, non-narcotic evening wind-down (petal preparations).** A petal infusion as a mild, chamomile-tier aid to winding down — framed explicitly as subtle, not hypnotic-drug-tier, to prevent dose-escalation. Note the evidence for even this is congener-based.
2. `[RESET]` **Dry, tickling cough and throat irritation.** Demulcent mucilage plus (weakly) sedative fraction to soothe an unproductive, sleep-disrupting cough — the group's most historically consistent use.
3. `[CYCLE-OFF]` **Restlessness / light sleep-onset support.** A benign, non-dependence-forming ritual during irritable recovery phases; not a nightly sedative to escalate, and not a substitute for fixing sleep architecture.

**Scope note:** because *P. dubium* is data-sparse and field-identification against *P. rhoeas* is genuinely difficult, the pragmatic Kenetica stance is to treat any red-field-poppy petal preparation as a single mild category and default to the better-studied corn poppy where a choice exists.

### Scientific Correlation

The honest grade for *P. dubium* as a sedative is **VERY LOW — the species itself is essentially unstudied pharmacologically; the case rests entirely on congener (*P. rhoeas*) data, which is itself thin.** This must be stated plainly: a targeted PubMed search this run returned **no** substantive pharmacological trial of *Papaver dubium* specifically. What follows is therefore explicitly *congener* evidence, and its transfer to *P. dubium* is an inference from close botanical and chemical kinship, not a demonstrated fact.

According to PubMed, the anchor sedative study for the group is Soulimani R, Younos C, Jarmouni-Idrissi S, Bousta D, Khallouki F, Laila A (2001), "Behavioral and pharmaco-toxicological study of *Papaver rhoeas* L. in mice," *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* 74(3):265–274 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0378-8741(00)00383-4)). A lyophilised ethanolic/aqueous **petal** extract of *P. rhoeas* reduced locomotor, exploratory and postural behaviour in mice (~400 mg/kg), confirming a genuine mild sedative effect. Three findings anchor the doctrine and transfer plausibly to *P. dubium*: (a) TLC of the petals detected anthocyanins but **no alkaloids** — the sedative petal fraction is not obviously alkaloid-driven; (b) the sedation was **not antagonised by flumazenil, naloxone, sulpiride, or atropine** — critically **not opioid** (reinforcing the opium contrast) and not classical benzodiazepine-GABAergic; and (c) the extract did **not** potentiate an infra-hypnotic pentobarbital dose, separating it from strong CNS depressants (reported LD10 ≈ 2000, LD50 ≈ 4000 mg/kg i.p. in mice).

The alkaloid chemistry of the group is documented by Çoban İ, Toplan GG, Özbek B, Gürer ÇU, Sarıyar G (2017), "Variation of alkaloid contents and antimicrobial activities of *Papaver rhoeas* L. …," *Pharmaceutical Biology* 55(1):1894–1898 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/13880209.2017.1340964)), which isolated twelve alkaloids of the proaporphine (mecambrine), aporphine (roemerine), promorphinan (salutaridine), protopine, and **rhoeadine** groups (rhoeadine, rhoeagenine, glaucamine and relatives) from *P. rhoeas* aerial parts — the chemical template inferred for *P. dubium*. Notably the paper documents *no* morphinan/opioid narcotic alkaloids, consistent with the non-opium doctrine. Further congener pharmacology — Hmamou A, El Khomsi M, El-Assri EM, et al. (2023), "Chemical characterization, anti-struvite crystal, anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antidepressant activities of *Papaver rhoeas* L. root and leaf extracts," *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* 319(Pt 2):117208 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2023.117208)) — reports analgesic (acetic-acid writhing), anti-inflammatory (carrageenan paw oedema) and antidepressant-like (forced-swim) effects for *P. rhoeas* extracts, supporting a real but mild, non-opioid CNS/anti-inflammatory profile for the group.

Net honest picture: the ancient "gentle, cooling, non-narcotic quieting" doctrine is *supported* at the group level, *cleanly separated from opium* (naloxone-insensitive, morphinan-free), and *undermined only by the absence of species-specific data* for *P. dubium* itself. The responsible chemistry and receptor target remain undefined even for the well-studied corn poppy; for *P. dubium* they are essentially unexamined. The redness/Remembrance symbolism has **no** pharmacological content and is presented purely as cultural history. Any efficacy claim for *P. dubium* specifically is **not established** [USER: verify against any future species-specific studies].

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic / Unani:** Cold (first degree), Moist (first-to-second degree); *mulaṭṭif* (demulcent-softener), mildly *munawwim* (sleep-promoting) — expressly graded weaker than opium poppy. **Attested** at the red-field-poppy group level (Avicenna, Dioscorides).
- **TCM:** No classical Chinese monograph for *P. dubium*; the opium poppy capsule (*yīng sù ké*, *P. somniferum*) is the genus member in the Chinese pharmacopoeia. **Not attested** for the field poppy — do not conflate.
- **Ayurvedic:** *P. dubium* is not a classical Ayurvedic dravya; the opium poppy (*ahiphena*) is. A *rasa/guṇa/vīrya/vipāka* profile for the long-headed field poppy is **not attested** and is not invented here.

### Contraindications

- **CRITICAL — do not confuse with opium poppy (*Papaver somniferum*).** *P. dubium* contains **no morphine, codeine, or thebaine** and is not a narcotic or opioid substitute; its (congener-inferred) sedation is naloxone-insensitive. Equally, this entry does **not** license *P. somniferum* material. Do not represent field-poppy preparations as delivering opioid analgesia.
- **Species uncertainty against *P. rhoeas*.** *P. dubium* and *P. rhoeas* are genuinely hard to tell apart in the field; treat wild-gathered red-poppy petals as a single mild category and prefer the better-studied corn poppy where identification matters.
- **Alkaloid-bearing parts (latex, unripe capsules, foliage) are mildly toxic and should not be self-dosed.** These carry the rhoeadine-type load; livestock poisoning from grazing poppy foliage is documented for the group. No established safe human dose for alkaloid-rich parts [USER: verify].
- **Children:** although the traditional syrup was a paediatric cough remedy, modern practice is cautious with any poppy-group preparation in young children; defer to current paediatric guidance over historical dosing.
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** insufficient safety data for medicinal doses; restrict to culinary/colourant quantities or avoid [USER: verify].
- **Sedative stacking:** the effect is mild, but do not combine medicinal doses with alcohol, benzodiazepines, or other CNS depressants on the "herbal = inert" assumption.
- **Escalation caution:** because the effect is gentle, users may be tempted to increase dose or switch to stronger plant parts — precisely the wrong move given the toxicity gradient toward alkaloid-rich tissues.
