# Beans [EP] — "Beans" and "Dried Beans" (Bryan 1930, English rendering of the Papyrus Ebers)

## *A pulse seed that Bryan's English calls "Beans" and "Dried Beans"; the Egyptian word is not printed in the text on disk and no species is given. The identification is unresolved, at low confidence: the broad bean, Vicia faba L., is the usual candidate in English, but Bryan names no plant, so the match rests on the common English word; the "gengent-beans" that occur in other remedies are a separately named item and are not folded in* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Ebers papyrus among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry, and the text is a modern English rendering, not the hieratic: Bryan 1930, `Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`. The name index (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`) holds the row "Beans, Bryan 1930, Ch. VI Plant Remedies (line 1252)", with the note "forms listed: Beans | Dried Beans".

**Egyptian (EP).** In Bryan's alphabetical list of "recognizable Plants" (Chapter VI; intro at lines 1231-1233) the entry reads "Beans / Dried Beans" (line 1252), between "Barley-plant" and "Caraway". Bryan prints no Egyptian word, no species and no grade. In the body of the book the beans occur in the following remedies; each is given as Bryan prints it, and no quantity is printed with any of them.

*For the thigh, in the skin diseases* (Bryan, Chapter XV, line 2609-2611). "REMEDY FOR THE THIGH: Onions, Dried Beans, Red Natron, Sea-salt, Sour Milk. Make into a poultice. It heals at once." This is the "Dried Beans" form.

*For suppleness of the limbs* (Bryan's chapter on minor medicine; lines 2180-2181 and 2188). A liniment "composed of Natron, Beans, Honey, Incense, and Sweet Myrrh, beaten up with" a list of oils (line 2180); and a salve in which Ass's dung is combined "with Yeast-of-the-Opium-drink, Goat's Fat, Lettuce, Onions, Beans, and White Oil" (lines 2187-2188). A third, "ANOTHER TO SOFTEN THE HARDENINGS", lists "Fruit-of-the-Dompalm, Beans, Edelkraut, Fresh Milk, sebesten" and ends "Crush it in the Bird-of-Passage! Crush it in its Feathers! Apply as a Poultice" (lines 2195-2198). The two capitalised lines are in the source and belong with the drug in the Egyptian way of writing a prescription; we record them without interpreting them.

*For the "met"* (Bryan's passage on the "met", lines 3164-3173; Bryan does not say here what the "met" is). For the "dry parts of the met" the first remedy "was a simple poultice of Cow's Milk and Beans"; a longer one, headed "REMEDY TO MAKE THE MET SUPPLE", begins "Fruit-of-the-Dompalm, Beans, ..." (line 3173).

*To regulate the urine* (Bryan's chapter on the urinary system, Chapter XIII; line 2425). "Crocus-from-the-South, Beans, roasted. Put in Oil and anoint the Phallus therewith." This is the only remedy read in which the beans are roasted.

*A comparison, not a remedy.* At line 3919 the "growth of the uxedu" is said to be "like beans", and at line 3534 something coming from the rectum is "like the white of beans". These are similes in Bryan's translation and not uses of the drug.

*Not folded.* "gengent-beans" (Bryan, lines 1584, 1671, 1695, 1829) is a separate ingredient name in other remedies, one of them a warm drink with "crushed gengent-beans" (line 1584); whether it is the same seed is not stated, and it is left out here.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, pulse seeds such as the broad bean are rich in starch, protein and fibre; the broad bean also contains compounds (vicine and convicine) that can cause haemolysis in people with a certain inherited enzyme deficiency. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Always as one ingredient among several, in poultices and salves for the thigh and the skin, for stiff or dry parts and "hardenings", and for regulating the urine.
- **What the text does not say.** What the beans do by themselves, which beans they are, or any quantity.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend a remedy built on this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

Preparations in the sources read: beans (dried) crushed with onions, natron, sea-salt and sour milk as a poultice (line 2609); beans beaten into a liniment or salve with natron, honey, incense, myrrh and oils or with Ass's dung and fat (lines 2180-2188); beans with cow's milk as a poultice (line 3170); beans roasted and put in oil for anointing (line 2425). ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Beans are an ancient staple food of the Near East, and a cooked or ground pulse makes a soft, moist poultice base; that is all the modern record can be said to support here. No claim about the skin, the urine or the "met" is supported, and none is made. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian (EP).** *(c) Absent.* The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution. The beans are a bulk ingredient in outward remedies, and nothing in the lines read places them in the plural person (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*). The spoken lines that sit with one of the prescriptions (the "Bird-of-Passage" lines) are part of that prescription in the papyrus's own manner, but they are not a statement about beans. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** A common food seed used as a filler in poultices and liniments. There is nothing here for a cultivator to take or do, and no cultivation use is attested. If anything, the entry is a reminder that most of the Ebers pharmacy is ordinary kitchen material combined by indication.

### Contraindications

- **Favism.** If the seed is the broad bean, people with a certain inherited enzyme deficiency can become seriously ill after eating it. Identity is open, so treat as a possible risk. [USER: verify]
- **Legume allergy.** Pulses are a common allergen; do not apply or eat if allergic.
- **Skin and urine complaints.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Identity.** The seed is not named in the text; the Vicia faba candidate is ours. [USER: verify]
