# Tree peony root bark — Mu Dan 牡丹 (Shennong bencao jing); aliases Lu Jiu 鹿韭 and Shu Gu 鼠姑

## *Paeonia suffruticosa Andrews (= P. × suffruticosa), the Chinese tree peony, whose root bark is the drug moutan or mu dan pi; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence at species level, although the classic describes only a root and Yang 1998 prints "Cortex Radicis Moutan"* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; the other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), is read as a second witness and attributed to Yang. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Cortex Radicis Moutan [SBJ]", taken from Yang's English; this entry replaces it. A search of the other texts on disk found other peonies only: Dioscorides' *paeonia* (I_696, wild peony, *Paeonia mascula*, tags EP, DMM, CM) and Clark's *Paeonia anomala* for a Tibetan drug (line 1112, with the name *aoog.chos*), which is a different plant and a different tradition. Neither is this drug and neither cites this paragraph.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 牡丹 in the middle classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 牡丹 *mu dan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2261, 味 paragraph; name line 2259). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦辛，寒 ("bitter, acrid, cold"); 主寒热 ("governs cold and heat"), 中风 ("wind stroke"), [a pair of glyphs lost in this e-text, which Yang renders "tugging and slackening"], 痉 ("tetany"), 惊痫邪气 ("fright epilepsy, evil qi"); 除症坚 ("removes hard concretions"), 瘀血留舍肠胃 ("stagnant blood lodged in the stomach and intestines"); 安五脏 ("quiets the five viscera"); 疗痈创 ("treats abscesses and sores"); the aliases 一名鹿韭 ("Deer Leek") and 一名鼠姑 ("Mouse Aunt"); and 生山谷 ("grows in mountains and valleys"). There is no 久服 clause.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 2263) records a disagreement among authorities on the flavour and toxicity: "Shennong, Qi Bo: acrid; Mr Li: slightly cold; Lei Gong, Tongjun: bitter, non-toxic; Huangdi: bitter, toxic (有毒)." It describes the plant: 叶如蓬相植，根如柏黑，中有核 ("leaves like *peng* [a plant name, not identified here], set facing each other; root like cypress, black, with a core inside"). It gives the gathering as the second and eighth months, dried in the sun, and then a sentence that belongs to Wu Pu and not to the Shennong paragraph: 人食之，轻身、益寿 ("people who eat it: lighten the body, increase longevity"; citing the *Yulan*). The Mingyi bielu layer (line 2265) gives the habitat 生巴郡及汉中 ("Ba commandery and Hanzhong") and the harvest 二月、八月采根，阴干 ("the root gathered in the second and eighth months and dried in the shade").

Sun's note (案, line 2267) is cut off in this e-text after its first words, "the *Guangya* says: *bai shu* (白术)", and so says nothing more here.

**Two editions, two grades.** Sun places 牡丹 in the middle classic. Yang's translation places it under the heading "Herbs: Inferior Class" (heading at line 3157; the entry at line 3164) and the placeholder row recorded it so. The Codex follows Sun, as the principal text, and records the difference without resolving it. [USER: verify]

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 3164): "Mu Dan (Cortex Radicis Moutan) is acrid and cold. It mainly treats cold and heat, wind stroke, tugging and slackening tetany, and fright epilepsy evil qi. It eliminates concretions, hardness, and blood stasis lodged in the stomach and intestines, quiets the five viscera, and cures abscesses and sores. Its other name is Lu Jiu (Deer Leek). Another name is Shu Gu (Mouse Aunt). It grows in mountains and valleys." (The OCR of Yang's page is damaged at "tugging" and "fright".) Yang gives the flavour as "acrid and cold" and Sun as "bitter, acrid, cold". Yang's footnote 193 (line 3176) is modern commentary and not part of the classic: "Moutan is particularly able to drain fire hidden in the blood", to "harmonize, cool, and generate the blood, to break accumulated blood, and to free the flow of the channels."

**Appendix.** The drug-relations table reads 牡丹 畏菟丝子 ("*mu dan* fears *tu si zi*", line 4405); it also reads 络石 杜仲、牡丹为使 ("*mu dan* is an envoy of *luo shi*", line 4323), 黄芩 ... 畏丹砂、牡丹、藜芦 ("*huang qin* fears cinnabar, *mu dan* and *li lu*", line 4359), and, at lines 4209, 4245 and 4341, three drugs that "dislike" it (钟乳, 磁石, 蛇床子). The preface (line 4117) says to use pairs that "need" or "serve" one another and not those that "dislike" (恶) or oppose one another; the Codex draws no further conclusion.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the root bark of *Paeonia suffruticosa* is the source of paeonol, a phenolic compound that gives the drug its smell, and of monoterpene glycosides such as paeoniflorin. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, acrid, cold drug for cold and heat, wind stroke, tetany and fright epilepsy, for hard concretions and stagnant blood in the stomach and intestines, for quieting the five viscera, and for abscesses and sores. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose. The longevity sentence belongs to the Wu Pu layer, which also says Huangdi judged the drug toxic, and neither is a reason to take the plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Wu Pu and Mingyi layers give only the harvest: the root, in the second and eighth months, dried in the sun (Wu Pu) or in the shade (Mingyi) (lines 2263, 2265). The preface (line 4121) says that drugs differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The root bark of the tree peony is a long-standing Chinese drug and paeonol is its characteristic constituent; the plant is a widely grown ornamental. Whether the bark relieves any of the conditions named in the classic has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Sun 1799).** Flavour and nature 味苦辛，寒, "bitter, acrid, cold" (line 2261). Wu Pu records a disagreement: "acrid" (Shennong, Qi Bo), "slightly cold" (Mr Li), "bitter, non-toxic" (Lei Gong, Tongjun), "bitter, toxic" (Huangdi) (line 2263), which the Codex does not settle.
- **SBJ (Yang 1998).** "Acrid and cold" (line 3164).
- No organ or channel assignment is given in the classic, and none is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle classic, whose preface (line 1677) reads 中药一百二十种为臣，主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经: the middle drugs "nourish the nature, correspond to man; non-toxic or toxic, weigh what is fitting; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the lean". The Shennong paragraph has no 久服 clause and no promise of lightening the body or of not ageing. The Wu Pu layer does say 人食之，轻身、益寿 ("people who eat it: lighten the body, increase longevity", line 2263), but that is a third-century commentary, in the same sentence-run as Huangdi's judgement that the drug is toxic, and not the classic; the Codex does not raise the status on it. Yang's edition files the drug in the inferior class, which would put it under the preface's lower-class warning ("多毒，不可久服", "mostly toxic, not to be taken long", line 2935). The register is therefore (b): framed by the middle class, with the cultivation status unattested.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-class, cold drug for blood stasis, tetany and sores, with a longevity sentence that is only in a later layer and a toxicity judgement in the same layer. The two editions disagree on its class. A cultivator has no practice to take from this entry.

### Contraindications

- **Toxicity.** Wu Pu records one authority (Huangdi) calling it toxic and two calling it non-toxic; the classic itself gives no toxicity statement. Treat it as unsettled. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy and heavy bleeding.** The classic's indications include breaking stagnant blood, and current Chinese pharmacy cautions against drugs of this kind in pregnancy and in heavy menstrual bleeding. This is a general precaution and is not in the classic. [USER: verify]
- **Epilepsy, tetany, abscesses.** The uses named are historical record only; these conditions need medical care.
- **Identity.** Other peonies (including the herbaceous peonies) are different drugs; the species is our identification, of high confidence. [USER: verify]
- **Quantities.** No dose or duration is given; the Wu Pu sentence is not a basis for a regimen.
