# I-258: Amomum subulatum / tsaoko (Black Cardamom / Nepal Cardamom / Chinese Black Cardamom)

**Codex Pharmakon Universalis — Volume I: Plant-Based Substances**
**Entry I-258 | Zingiberaceae**

---

## Section 1: Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Primary Binomials:**
- *Amomum subulatum* Roxb. — Nepal/Greater Black Cardamom (primary South Asian medicinal)
- *Amomum tsaoko* Crevost & Lemarié — Chinese Black Cardamom (primary TCM species)
**Family:** Zingiberaceae

**Common Names:**
- English: Black Cardamom, Large Cardamom, Nepal Cardamom, Brown Cardamom
- Mandarin: *Cǎo guǒ* (草果) — "herb fruit" — TCM primary name for A. tsaoko
- Hindi: *Badi elaichi* (बड़ी इलायची) — "large cardamom"
- Nepali: *Alaichi*
- Arabic: *Hāl al-kabīr* (هال الكبير) — "big cardamom"
- Tibetan: *Ka-ko-la* — documented in rGyud-bzhi

**Critical Distinction from Green Cardamom (I-229):**
This is an entirely different plant from green/true cardamom (*Elettaria cardamomum*, I-229). Despite the shared "cardamom" name, black and green cardamom have:
- Different genera (*Amomum* vs. *Elettaria*)
- Completely different volatile oil profiles (no 1,8-cineole dominance in A. subulatum; camphor-like smoky notes instead)
- Different clinical applications (black cardamom: primarily digestive/respiratory; less sweet; more smoky)
- Different traditional positions in materia medica (black cardamom more used as cooking spice in Indian and Chinese cuisines; green cardamom as the high-quality medicinal and luxury spice)

**Drying process:** Black cardamom pods are traditionally smoke-dried over fire (giving them their distinctive smoky character) vs. green cardamom (sun or shade dried).

**Morphology (A. subulatum):** Large herb; 1.5–2.5 m; dark green leaves; red-brown flowers at base; large trilocular seed pods (2–3 cm), dried to dark brown/black, with several dark aromatic seeds.

**Part Used:** Seeds (whole or powdered); essential oil of seeds; whole pods in cooking.

**Growth Region:** A. subulatum: Eastern Himalayan foothills (Nepal, Sikkim, Bhutan, northeast India — the Himalayan belt); major Sikkim production (world's largest). A. tsaoko: Southwest China (Yunnan, Guangxi), Vietnam.

**TCM Spleen-Stomach tradition:**
*Cǎo guǒ* (A. tsaoko) is a major herb in TCM for "Dampness-Cold" patterns of the Spleen and Stomach — chronic gastric distention, nausea, loose stools, and systemic "cold-damp" presentations. It appears in *Shi Pi Yin* (Firm the Spleen Decoction), one of the key classical formulas for digestive Dampness.

## Section 3: Convergence Analysis

**Primary Convergence Domain:** Warming digestive / Dampness-drying / carminative — consistent in CM, SBJ, SS, FMT.

**Unique Black Cardamom Indication:**
- **Anti-malarial / anti-febrile:** TCM uses *Cǎo guǒ* specifically for malaria (截疟, "jié nüè" — malaria-cutting herbs); in SS, black cardamom is used in fever and infection formulas where green cardamom would not be used
- This anti-malarial application is validated in vitro (see below)

**Energetic Classification:**
- ◉ Qi (functional): Primary — Spleen/Stomach Qi; digestive; carminative
- ◈ Jing (structural): Secondary — anti-infectious; anti-parasitic (anti-malarial application)

**Primary Tier: ◉ Qi** — primarily functional digestive Qi regulation

## Section 4: Phytochemical Composition

### Essential Oil (1.5–3.5% seeds)

**1. 1,8-Cineole — 20–70% (A. subulatum); 5–20% (A. tsaoko)**
- As in multiple Zingiberaceae entries; bronchodilator; antimicrobial; anti-inflammatory

**2. Camphor — 10–40% (A. subulatum; distinctive)**
- Characteristic smoky-camphoraceous quality
- Stimulant; antimicrobial; differs from green cardamom's α-terpinyl acetate dominance

**3. α-Pinene, β-Pinene — 5–20%**
- Antimicrobial; anti-inflammatory

**4. Linalool, Limonene, Geraniol — minor**

### Non-Volatile Constituents

**5. Amomum-specific flavonoids and phenolics**

**6. Spathulenol — sesquiterpene alcohol**
- Anti-inflammatory; antimicrobial

**7. β-Caryophyllene — sesquiterpene; CB2 agonist**

## Section 5: Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Digestive Enzyme Stimulation and GI Motility
- 1,8-Cineole: Promotes gastric secretion; choleretic (bile flow enhancement)
- Camphor: Mild carminative; digestive enzyme stimulation
- Combined: Accelerates gastric emptying; reduces gastric stasis (bloating, flatulence)

### 2. Anti-Malarial (Unique to A. tsaoko)
- *Cǎo guǒ* methanol extract: IC₅₀ against *Plasmodium falciparum* 0.5–2 μg/mL in vitro
- Active compounds: Likely flavonoids and phenolics (mechanism distinct from artemisinin); anti-Plasmodium activity not found in green cardamom
- Validates TCM malaria-cutting (截疟) indication

### 3. Antimicrobial (1,8-Cineole + Camphor)
- Broad-spectrum via membrane disruption
- Anti-H. pylori activity (1,8-cineole); relevant to TCM Stomach disease applications

### 4. Respiratory Expectorant / Bronchial
- 1,8-Cineole: Mucociliary clearance enhancement; bronchodilation
- Camphor: Mild expectorant; traditional use in Upper Respiratory Infection

## Section 7: Phenomenological Record

### Qualitative Experience

Black cardamom's phenomenological signature is dramatically different from green cardamom — where green cardamom is fresh, floral, and sweet (camphor-free), black cardamom is smoky, resinous, and aggressively warming. Its primary culinary encounters are in North Indian *biryani*, *garam masala*, slow-cooked meats, and Vietnamese *Phở*.

**Oral (in cooking or tea):**
- Taste: Smoky-camphoraceous; earthy resinous warmth; assertive and rustic
- GI: Strong carminative; aggressive warming sensation throughout GI tract; distinctly more intense than green cardamom
- Black cardamom is rarely consumed directly; extracted into cooking media (oil, broth, curry)

**Clinical/TCM context:**
In TCM "Cold-Damp Spleen" presentations (chronic loose stools, abdominal distention, digestive weakness, nausea in cold damp weather), *Cǎo guǒ* is combined with *Bái Zhú* (Atractylodes), *Gān Jiāng* (dry ginger, I-256), and *Fù Zǐ* (Aconite) in warming formulas. The subjective experience in such patients: gradual resolution of the "cold, heavy, damp" feeling in the abdomen; improved appetite and digestive function over 2–4 weeks.

## Section 8: Practical Application

### Formulation and Dosing

**Culinary (primary medicinal pathway):**
- 1–3 pods per serving in slow-cooked preparations (biryani, braises, Phở)
- Seeds (removed from pods): 3–5 seeds per recipe; or ½–1 g ground seed powder

**TCM compound formula (practitioner-guided):**
- *Cǎo guǒ* 3–6 g per decoction; always in compound formulas; requires TCM diagnosis

**Tea:**
- 2–3 crushed seeds in 250 mL hot water; simmer 10 minutes
- 1–2 cups for digestive use

### Safety Profile

**Generally safe.** Very limited adverse event data at culinary doses.

**Drug Interactions:**
- **CYP450:** Limited data; possible CYP1A2/3A4 modulation via camphor/1,8-cineole; clinical significance at typical culinary doses uncertain
- **Anticoagulants:** Minor; monitor with heavy use
- **Antidiabetics:** Mild hypoglycemic potential; monitor

**Contraindications:**
- Pregnancy (high dose): Camphor potentially uterotonic; culinary amounts safe
- Essential oil ingestion: Camphor concentration; toxic at high doses
