# Marjoram wine — Oinos origanites (οἶνος ὀριγανίτης, "origanites wine"), Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 5-61

## *A medicated wine made by steeping origanum in grape must; the herb is Dioscorides' "Heracleotican origanum" (3-32), which the translator's SUGGESTED line gives as Origanum heracleoticum [Loudon], winter-sweet marjoram. The identification of the herb is the translator's suggestion, not the text's, and is of medium confidence; the wine is a preparation, not a species.* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10096, chapter 5-61 "OINOS ORIGANITES", with the translator's line: "SUGGESTED: Origanum heracleoticum [Loudon] — Winter-sweet Marjoram — Origanum Wine, Marjoram Wine" (lines 10097-10098). The whole chapter reads: "Origanites is made from Heracleotican origanum in the same way as thymites, and is good for the same things."

**Dioscorides (DMM).** The chapter is two lines long and gives nothing of its own. It sends the reader to the thyme wine, *oinos thumites*, chapter 5-59 (line 10087), whose text reads: "Bind one hundred ounces of pounded sifted thyme in a linen cloth, put it into nine gallons of must for three months, and then jar it. It is good for digestive difficulties, lack of appetite, dysentery, disorders of the nerves, and hypochondria [nervous gastric disorder]; for winter shiverings, and for poison from venomous creatures [bites] that chill and putrify." The next chapter, 5-60 (thymbra wine), is likewise "made in the same way and is good for whatever thymites is," so 5-61 belongs to a run of three wines that refer back to the thyme recipe.

The wine stands in the section the translation heads "OTHER WINES". Its opening note (line 9919) is Dioscorides' own: "we think it is not out of place to describe the making of many other sorts of wine, not because they are useful or suitable, but so that we may not omit anything." The author therefore presents these wines for completeness, and says that some of them "have use."

The herb itself is described at 3-32 (line 6808): leaf similar to hyssop, seed "not thick"; "It is warming"; a decoction in wine is given for those bitten by poisonous beasts. Nothing from 3-32 is a statement about the wine; it is recorded here only to identify the herb the wine is made from.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** "The same things" as the thyme wine of 5-59: digestive difficulty, lack of appetite, dysentery, disorders of the nerves, hypochondria, winter shivering, and bites of venomous creatures.
- **What the text does not say.** It gives no separate account of the effect of origanum in wine, no dose and no duration.

The Codex gives no protocol. The wine is an alcoholic preparation, and the text's own note that the "other wines" are described for completeness is part of the record.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only method is by reference: "made from Heracleotican origanum in the same way as thymites" (5-61). The thymites recipe (5-59) gives one hundred ounces of pounded, sifted herb, bound in linen, in nine gallons of must for three months, then jarred. Whether the same quantity of origanum is intended is not stated in 5-61, so the figures are recorded as the thyme recipe's and not as a recipe for this wine. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Marjoram and oregano belong to the genus *Origanum* in the mint family and are aromatic culinary herbs. Herb-infused wines are a long-standing preparation; ethanol is a solvent for some of the herb's aromatic compounds. The classical indications (hypochondria, "disorders of the nerves", poison from bites) have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** No quality (warming, cooling, astringent) is printed for the wine in 5-61. The herb is called "warming" at 3-32, which is a statement about the herb, not the wine. No tradition-specific classification beyond this is imported.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and no cultivation status is attested for this wine. What the chapter shows is a discipline of attention in the form of a recipe: the herb, the must, the three months of steeping, the jarring. The wine is described in a section that the author says is included "so that we may not omit anything."

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here supports a cultivation use. It is a two-line pointer to a thyme-wine recipe, and the recipe is the only practical content. A cultivator can take from it the habit of tracing a thin chapter back to the entry that carries its method.

### Contraindications

- **Alcohol.** A fermented wine; the usual cautions for alcohol apply (pregnancy, liver disease, medication, driving, dependency).
- **Quantities unverified.** The quantities are the thyme recipe's; see Preparation & Dosing.
- **Identification.** The herb is the translator's suggestion at medium confidence. [USER: verify.]
- **Venomous bites and "poison."** These are emergencies. This entry is no guide to treating them.
