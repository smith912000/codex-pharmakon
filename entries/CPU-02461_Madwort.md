# Madwort (alyssum) — ālūsun (آلوسن), Greek alysson

## *Madwort or alyssum, a herb "like the lupin"; the identification is the modern one, from the Greek name that Avicenna gives, and is of low confidence at species level, because the* Canon *describes it only by resemblance to the lupin and the Greek* alysson *has been applied to more than one plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

Madwort is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk, and the entry is five lines. It is a single-text entry. The spine already has Dioscorides' *alysson* (DMM 3-105) as *Marrubium alysson* (I_1128), a plant the translator glosses "Plaited-leaved Horehound, Moonwort", with "Alyssum alpestre" as Sprengel's alternative. This entry is the *Canon*'s own record under the Arabic form *ālūsun*, and it overlaps that Dioscoridean entry in the name and in a link to the rabid dog (Dioscorides says only that it "is thought to cure madness in a dog", DMM 3-105). The two are not merged here: the *Canon* describes the plant differently, and I have not shown that they are the same species.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The *Canon* (Book II, entry *ālūsun*, Arabic text line 10694) says: "it is a herb that resembles the lupin (*tirmis*), and so it was named *tirmis*; hot and dry in the first" (هي حشيشة تشبه الترمس فسمي لذلك ترمسا حارة يابسة في الأولى). Its action is that "it dries moderately and cleanses." For cosmetics: "it benefits freckles (*kalaf*) and dissolves all of that from it moderately." For poisons: "Galen said it is beneficial by property against the bite of the rabid dog (*al-kalb al-kalib*), and it has cured a group of people, and for that reason it is called in Greek *ālūsun*" (lines 10696-10698). The explanation of the name is the *Canon*'s: it is Galen's name for a remedy against the bite of a mad dog.

### Active Components

None can be stated. The plant is not identified securely, and the entry names no constituent. If it is a member of the mustard family (*Alyssum*), the family contains glucosinolates and sometimes accumulates metals; if it is the horehound of Dioscorides (*Marrubium*), it contains bitter diterpenoid lactones such as marrubiin. Both are general botanical knowledge and neither is attested for Avicenna's plant. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The *Canon* gives two uses: a mild drying, cleansing herb for freckles, and a remedy for the bite of a rabid dog, which it ascribes to Galen. The rabies use is not safe or effective and must never replace post-exposure treatment. Rabies is fatal without prompt medical care. The Dioscorides entry also records hanging the plant as an amulet; that is Dioscorides' passage and is not in the *Canon* entry.

### Scientific Correlation

There is no established benefit of any herb against rabies; prevention after a bite is by wound washing and vaccination with immune globulin. This is general medical knowledge. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry on the cosmetic use. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **CM (Avicenna), line 10695.** "Hot and dry in the first [degree]."
- **Action.** "Dries moderately and cleanses" (line 10696). No other tradition is tagged.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The entry is five lines. It has a temperament, two actions, a cosmetic use and a poison remedy. It does not use the innate heat, the radical moisture or the *ruh*, and gives no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing is asked of a cultivator. A name preserved from Greek, a bite remedy and a freckle wash are the whole record; the entry's lasting interest is that Avicenna explains the name through Galen's use.

### Contraindications

- **Rabies.** A bite from a rabid or unknown animal requires immediate medical care. Never rely on a herb.
- **Identification.** *Alyssum*, *Marrubium* and other plants have carried this name; do not take unidentified material.
- **Skin.** The cosmetic use is historical record; test any plant wash on a small area. General caution.
