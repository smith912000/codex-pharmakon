# Kataka-phala Shaka-phala — Kataka-phala and Shaka-phala (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907)

## *Kataka-phala and Shaka-phala as printed together in the Parushakadi group of the Sushruta Samhita; two fruit-names run together on a line break, not one drug. Kataka-phala is most probably the clearing-nut, Strychnos potatorum L.f. (the spine's CPU-00984), by the conventional identification of kataka; Shaka-phala is unresolved; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk (Bhishagratna's 1907 English translation).** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from a name list (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, which records "group list (ch.38); no identification given here"). This entry replaces it. The text was opened at the line given and the neighbouring lines read. Searches of the other texts on disk (Dioscorides, Shennong, rGyud-bzhi, Ebers) were not made under a Sanskrit spelling, so absence elsewhere is not claimed.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Kataka-phala Shaka-phala is printed in the Parushaka'di group, Sutrasthana chapter 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15653 (list from line 15651). The translator's sentence reads: "The group known as the Parushakadi Gana consists of Parushaka, Draksha, Kat-phala, Dadima, Rajadana, Kataka-phala Shaka-phala and Triphala." The metrical text (lines 15657 to 15660) reads: "It subdues the deranged Vayu, allays thirst, acts as a cordial, increases one's relish for food and cures the diseased or abnormal components of urine or its defects."

**One row, two names.** In the e-text the two names stand on one line with no comma between them ("Kataka-phala / Shaka-phala"). The list is otherwise of single names separated by commas, and the evidence file on disk (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/evidence.csv`, row SS-S38-021) reads them as two, "Kataka-phala, Shaka-phala". The row therefore stands for two fruit-names. The spine already holds the clearing-nut as CPU-00984, an entry that cites *Kataka* in the Sushruta water-purification passage; the Kataka half of this row is probably a further mention of that drug and the Shaka half is not. Merging is for Smith to decide. [USER: verify]

**A Sutra 46 mention of *Kataka Phalam*.** In the chapter on the qualities of food and drugs the translation lists "the Jatikosha, the Jati Phalam, the Kataka Phalam the Kakkolakam, Lavauga and the Karpura etc." as fruits that "have a bitter pungent taste, subdue thirst and Kapham, are light in digestion, and remove bad odours from the mouth and cleanse it of all impurities" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` lines 20982 to 20988). The passage itself describes the group as removing "bad odours from the mouth", so the list is of aromatics, and whether it is the same drug as the Parushakadi *Kataka-phala* is not settled by the text. ### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. If the identification of Kataka-phala as *Strychnos potatorum* is right, the seed is generally known to contain alkaloids and a mucilaginous material, and the genus *Strychnos* includes strongly poisonous species; the entry CPU-00984 should be consulted for that drug. Shaka-phala has no identification and no composition can be stated. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says.** Only what the group's metrical text says of the group as a whole (given above). For the Parushakadi group: deranged *Vayu*, thirst, appetite and defects of the urine (lines 15657 to 15660). The translation does not give an effect for Kataka-phala Shaka-phala taken alone.
- **What the text does not say.** No single-drug indication, no preparation, no dose, no duration.

The Codex does not turn a group's virtues into a protocol for one member of the group, and it gives no modern use for a substance whose identity is not settled.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given for either name in the Parushakadi passage. The fruits are named as ingredients of the group, with no dose and no part prepared. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS, Sutra 38 (Bhishagratna 1907).** The group acts on *Vayu*, thirst, appetite and the components of urine (lines 15657 to 15660). No *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for either name there.
- **SS, Sutra 46.** For the *Kataka Phalam* of the aromatics list: "bitter pungent taste", subdues thirst and *Kapham*, light of digestion (lines 20982 to 20988); see the doubt above about whether it is the same drug.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is named inside a therapeutic drug group (*gana*) of Sutrasthana chapter 38, the Parushaka'di group. The group's metrical text speaks of curing or subduing disorders. It does not call the group, or any member, a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) drug, and it does not mention *ojas*. The sources retrieved therefore attest a therapeutic framing only, and no cultivation status. The *sattva/rajas/tamas* lists of later popular literature are not from Sushruta and are not applied.

**Practitioner's note.** Two fruit-names in a group for *Vayu*, thirst and urinary defects. Nothing in the record supports a cultivation use. A cultivator does nothing with this entry, and the row needs splitting or merging before it is used.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The plant is not identified in the sources read. Do not use any plant sold under this name on the strength of this entry. [USER: verify]
