# Yellow halloysite — be.snabs (Clark 1995, "Yellow Halloysite"); Baenab (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011), rGyud-bzhi Explanatory Tantra ch.20

## *A stone "of two types: pale yellow and yellowish brown", which cures fractures and glandular growths and stimulates flesh growth; "halloysite" is the translator's gloss (in mineralogy a white to yellowish clay mineral of the kaolin group), used by Clark for several different Tibetan names, so the identification is the translator's and of medium confidence* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The Codex's list row is `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 134, "Baenab, , mineral, rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20 (Efficacy of Medicinal Substances); Four Tantras of Tibetan Medicine (English trans.)", flagged as an uncertain identification. Two texts were read.

*Clark 1995* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). Chapter 20 lists the stone medicines in part (2) (line 816). The entry (line 824) reads:

"be.snabs: Yellow Halloysite: [is of two types: pale yellow and yellowish brown.] It cures fractures and glandular growths, and stimulates flesh growth."

By the bracket convention of Clark's introduction (line 82; square brackets carry the commentaries), the tantra's own words are the actions (cures fractures and glandular growths, stimulates flesh growth); the two colour types are commentary. The convention was not checked against the Tibetan. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011* (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`). In the stone-medicine summary of the Explanatory Tantra (page 199, line 3356): "Baenab joins fractured bones, decreases the swelling of lymph nodes and regenerates damaged tissues." The index lists "Baenab 199" (line 7266). The sense agrees with Clark: fractures, swollen glands or lymph nodes, tissue growth. The spelling "be snabs" also occurs at line 6432 of that e-text, inside a list of Tibetan headings among disease categories; what it stands for there was not determined and it is not used here. [USER: verify] The match between the spelling *be.snabs* and *Baenab* rests on the agreement of the actions and the sound of the names. [USER: verify]

**Other "halloysites" in Clark.** The translator's word "Halloysite" is used for several different Tibetan names in the same section: *rdo.klad* (lit. "stone brain", "Halloysite: ... a white 'pimply' stone that looks like a brain", line 837) and *btsag* ("Red Ochre: Halloysite: ... found in the form of a red stone", with *yug* "a blue type of Halloysite", line 852). The blue type has its own spine row (CPU-02405, blue halloysite) and is not this entry. The recurrence of the word shows that "halloysite" is a general gloss for clay-like stones in this translation and not a precise identification for each. [USER: verify]

**What is not given.** In the passages read, neither text gives a taste, potency or post-digestive taste for the stone, a dose, a preparation or a formula containing it.

**Identity.** The Codex name "yellow halloysite" is Clark's gloss. In mineralogy halloysite is a clay mineral of the kaolin group, usually white, which may take yellowish tints from impurities (a general fact, not read in the texts). The commentary's "pale yellow and yellowish brown" is consistent with a clay of that kind, but the texts do not say that; the identification is the translator's, and no further basis was found. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None is stated in the texts. As a general fact, halloysite is a hydrated aluminium silicate (a clay); yellow-brown tints in clays generally come from iron compounds. No composition is asserted for the stone the tantra means.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** Both texts record the stone as a remedy for fractures and swollen glands or lymph nodes and as promoting the growth of flesh or tissue. Fractures need proper setting and care, and swollen lymph nodes can signal infection or other serious disease. Swallowing a clay or stone of uncertain identity is not advised. The Codex supplies no modern protocol.

### Contraindications

- **Stone or clay of uncertain identity:** may carry iron, arsenic, lead or other contaminants; do not swallow or apply to open wounds.
