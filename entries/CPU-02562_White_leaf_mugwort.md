# White-leaf mugwort — 白蒿 *baihao*, "Bai Hao"; the *Erya*'s 蘩 *fan* and 皤蒿 *pohao*

## *A white-hoary Artemisia of the Shennong upper classic, identified here as Artemisia sacrorum Ledeb. (A. stechmanniana Bess.); the identification is ours, of medium confidence, and the sources on disk do not settle it, because Sun Xingyan's note runs the name together with 艾 (mugwort) and Yang's translation glosses the entry as Artemisia argyi* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Bai hao* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. Other entries on disk treat mugwort-type *Artemisia* (I_329 *Artemisia argyi*, I_164 *Artemisia* (mugwort), I_437 *Artemisia vulgaris*; I_1428 *Artemisia sieversiana* is a Tibetan-only entry), and the Codex does not merge this drug with any of them.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 白蒿 in the upper classic (卷一 上经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 549, 味 paragraph; name line 547). The paragraph reads: 味甘，平。主五脏邪气，风寒湿痹，补中益气，长毛发，令黑，疗心悬、少食、常饥。轻身、耳目聪明、不老。生川泽。In English: "Flavour sweet, balanced. Governs evil qi of the five viscera and wind-cold-damp impediment; supplements the centre and boosts the qi; makes the hair and body hair grow and turns it black; heals 'heart suspension' (心悬), with little eating and constant hunger. [It] lightens the body (轻身), sharpens ears and eyes (耳目聪明) and [prevents] ageing (不老). It grows in rivers and marshes." On disk the paragraph breaks after the hunger clause, and the life-nourishing clause sits on its own line (line 551) without the 久服 ("taken long") that heads the same sentence in Yang's translation (see below). The reconstruction is Sun's; the break is a feature of the digital file.

*Wu Pu layer.* None.

**Mingyi bielu layer** (《名医》, later additions; line 553). "Grows at Zhongshan. Gathered in the second month" (生中山，二月采). No part is named.

**Sun Xingyan's note** (案, line 555). A long philological note whose subject is the name, and which gives no description of the plant. It runs: the *Shuowen* says 蘩 (*fan*) is 白蒿, and 艾 (*ai*) is 冰台 (*bingtai*). The *Guangya*: 蘩母 is [a lost glyph]. The *Erya*: 艾 is 冰台; Guo Pu says "today's 艾 is 白蒿". The *Xia xiaozheng*: "in the second month gather 蘩." The *Erya* again: 蘩 is 皤蒿 (*pohao*); Guo Pu says "白蒿", and on a second *Erya* line, 蘩, 由胡, Guo Pu says "unknown" (未详). The *Mao shi*: "gather the 蘩", with the commentary "蘩 is 皤蒿", "蘩 is 白蒿"; Lu Ji: "in general, a 艾 that is white is 皤蒿"; Wang Yi's note to the *Chuci*: "艾 is 白蒿". Sun's own conclusion: "皤 and 白 are close in sound and sense. 艾 is a drug name which the *Bencao jing* does not have, because it is 白蒿; the *Mingyi* listing 艾 separately is wrong" (艾，是药名，《本草经》无者，即白蒿是也。《名医》别出艾条，非). So Sun holds that the classic's 白蒿 is what later works call 艾, the mugwort.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 2004) prints "Bai Hao (Folium Artemisiae Argyi) is sweet and balanced. It mainly treats evil qi in the five viscera and wind cold damp impediment. It supplements the center, boosts the qi, promotes the growth of hair, is able to turn the hair black, and cures heart suspension which is [a syndrome including] reduced eating and constant hungering. Protracted taking may make the body light, sharpen the eyes and ears, and prevent senility. It grows in rivers and swamps." The long-taking clause is therefore present in Yang's text. His footnote 118 (line 2028) adds a comment on *Artemisia argyi* in modern use; it is Yang's, not the classic's, and it speaks of mugwort leaf, which is not the plant the entry is named for in the Codex.

**Identity.** The Codex's name, *Artemisia sacrorum* (*A. stechmanniana*), rests on the later standard reading of the *Erya*'s 蘩 and 皤蒿, "the white Artemisia". Sun and Yang both point to mugwort, *A. argyi* (Yang by the Latin gloss, Sun by equating 艾 with 白蒿). The text gives no description from which to choose. The Codex keeps the proposed name and records the doubt; the plant may be a different white-hoary Artemisia, and *A. argyi* is a live alternative. Sun's own statement that the *Bencao jing* has no separate 艾 entry agrees with I_329, which records that mugwort's materia-medica record is in the later *Mingyi bielu* rather than securely in the classic.

### Active Components

No analytical source was retrieved for this plant, and no constituent is asserted. As a general fact, *Artemisia* is a genus of aromatic plants of the daisy family (Asteraceae), and many species carry volatile oils and bitter sesquiterpene lactones. That is a statement about the genus and not a finding about this drug, whose species is uncertain. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic records a sweet, balanced herb with a broad list of promises: evil qi in the five viscera, impediment, supplementing the centre, growing and darkening the hair, and, in the closing clause, lightening the body, sharpening the ears and eyes and preventing ageing. The Codex gives no protocol, does not turn "heart suspension" into a diagnosis and does not claim that hair darkens. The "ageing" clause is the classic's promise for the upper class, and the Codex does not endorse it.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the upper classic (上经), which the preface describes as "to nourish life, in correspondence with heaven; non-toxic; taken in quantity or taken long, it does not harm people; whoever wishes to lighten the body, boost the qi, not age and prolong the years should look to the upper classic" (上药...主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经; line 43). The paragraph's closing clause promises "lighten the body, sharpen ears and eyes, not age" (轻身、耳目聪明、不老; *Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 白蒿 *baihao*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` lines 549-551). In Sun's file this clause lacks a 久服 prefix, which Yang's translation has ("Protracted taking may...", Yang line 2004). The Codex grades the entry (a) on the upper classic's heading and the life-nourishing clause, and states the missing prefix. 轻身 is the classic's term and is not translated into a pharmacological effect. The later layers add no cultivation claim.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator has nothing to do with this entry beyond reading it: an upper-class drug whose plant is not settled, whose text promises hair, eyes, ears and freedom from ageing, and which carries no dose. Nothing here supports taking a mugwort on the name alone.

### Contraindications

- The classic does not warn, and the preface calls the upper class non-toxic (line 43). The Codex does not infer safety for any particular plant from this, because the species is not settled.
- **Identity.** Several *Artemisia* are called mugwort or wormwood in other entries; some carry volatile oils (thujone in certain species) that are harmful in quantity. Do not take a plant on this entry's name. [USER: verify]
