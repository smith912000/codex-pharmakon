### Origin & Tradition

**Greco-Roman (Dioscorides):** The quince — the "Cydonian apple" (*kydōnion mēlon*), named for Kydonia in Crete — is described in *De Materia Medica* as astringent, good for the stomach, useful in fluxes, and made into cordials, conserves and the fragrant honey-quince preparation. The raw fruit is hard, sour and tannic; it was valued as a stomachic and a remedy for looseness of the bowel.

**Islamic / Unani (Avicenna):** *Safarjal* (quince) is prominent in the *Canon of Medicine* as a cardiac and gastric tonic and appetite-restorer. Crucially for this entry, Unani medicine distinguishes the **fruit** (astringent, drying) from the **seed** (*bizr al-safarjal*). The seed coat is rich in a demulcent mucilage — in Unani pharmacy the soaked seeds yield *lu'āb bihidāna* / *lu'āb-e-bidana*, a cooling, moistening, lubricating slime used for dry cough, hoarseness, sore/inflamed throat and mucous-membrane irritation, and as a gentle soothing laxative-lubricant for a dry, irritated gut. The seed is thus energetically almost the *opposite* of the astringent fruit-flesh: where the flesh binds, the soaked seed lubricates and softens.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the Cooling Coat:** The doctrinal signature of quince seed sits in the polarity of the plant itself. The flesh is *drying and binding*; the seed's mucilaginous coat is *moistening and relaxing* — one plant holding both astringency and demulcence. The seed's action is enveloping: it lays a protective, cooling film over inflamed surface, quieting irritation rather than driving it out. In Kenetica this is a `[RESET]`/`[SLEEP]` signature — a soothing, membrane-protective agent for the tail end of an irritated, dried-out, over-heated phase, easing the system toward rest.

### Active Components

- **Seed-coat mucilage / polysaccharides** — the demulcent principle; on soaking, the epidermis of the seed swells into a viscous hydrogel (the pharmacologically defining feature of *lu'āb bihidāna*).
- **The seed also contains cyanogenic glycosides** (as is typical of Rosaceae seeds/kernels) — relevant to safety: do **not** crush or grind the seed to expose the kernel; the traditional method soaks whole seeds to extract only the outer mucilage.
- **Fruit flesh:** organic acids (malic), tannins (astringency), pectin, and aromatic volatiles giving the classic quince fragrance; polyphenols underpinning antioxidant activity.
- Fruit-derived preparations (syrup, "quince sauce"/concentrate) carry the fruit's polyphenols and acids.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Quince splits into two tools:

1. **Seed mucilage (the demulcent) —** a `[RESET]`/`[SLEEP]` soothing agent: a cooling, film-forming slime for dry cough, throat and airway irritation, and dry/irritated gut. Prepared by cold-soaking whole seeds and using the resulting mucilage.
2. **Fruit / quince syrup / concentrate —** a gastric-tonic `[RESET]` agent used traditionally (and in modern trials) for reflux and nausea. The clinical evidence below is for **fruit preparations**, not the isolated seed mucilage — keep that distinction honest.

In applied Kenetica, quince-seed mucilage is a gentle recovery/wind-down aid for irritated mucous membranes; the fruit syrup is a stomach-settling adjunct.

### Scientific Correlation

*Based on articles retrieved from PubMed* (note: the clinical trials use **quince fruit** preparations; the demulcent **seed** mucilage is supported by traditional Unani use, not by these trials):

- **RCT, nausea/vomiting of pregnancy.** Jafari-Dehkordi et al. (2017), *J Obstet Gynaecol* — 76 women; quince syrup vs vitamin B6 over 1 week. PUQE-24 nausea scores fell more in the quince group (baseline 9.5 → 5.2 at day 7; P < .001), with a greater change than B6; authors conclude quince appears a suitable, safe treatment for NVP. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01443615.2017.1322046)
- **RCT, GERD in pregnancy.** Shakeri et al. (2018), *J Obstet Gynaecol* — 137 pregnant women; quince concentrate ("sauce") vs ranitidine over 4 weeks. Symptom scores were comparable between groups (quince significantly better at 2 weeks; no significant difference in the primary end-of-study comparison). Concludes efficacy similar to ranitidine and proposes quince as an alternative for pregnant patients. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01443615.2018.1431210)
- **RCT, paediatric GERD.** Naeimi et al. (2019), *Complementary Therapies in Medicine* — 96 children; ranitidine vs ranitidine + quince syrup, 4 weeks. Added quince syrup significantly improved total GERD symptom scores at 2, 4 and 6 weeks (all P < 0.05). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctim.2019.06.007)

**Evidence grade:** *Emerging clinical* for **quince fruit/syrup** in functional GI complaints (nausea of pregnancy, GERD) — several small RCTs, positive but modest, mostly single-region (Iran). The **seed-mucilage demulcent** use is *traditional-grade* (well-attested in Unani practice; no dedicated RCT retrieved here). Attribution: findings from PubMed.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic / Unani:** Fruit — cool & dry, astringent stomachic/cardiac tonic. Seed mucilage — cool & moist, demulcent, softening, gentle lubricant laxative.
- **Dioscoridean (Greek):** Stomachic, astringent, good in fluxes.
- **TCM:** Not classically used — omit.
- **Ayurvedic:** Not a classical Sanskrit dravya — omit.

### Contraindications

- **Cyanogenic seed kernels** — the seed's inner kernel, like other Rosaceae kernels (apple pip, bitter almond), contains cyanogenic glycosides. Prepare mucilage from **whole, un-ground** seeds only; do not crush, chew or make a flour of the kernels. Traditional cold-soaking extracts only the harmless outer mucilage.
- **Mucilage and drug absorption** — like any bulk demulcent/soluble fibre, quince-seed mucilage may slow or reduce absorption of concurrently taken oral drugs; separate dosing.
- **Pregnancy** — fruit preparations were the tested populations (used *in* pregnant women in the cited trials) and appear safe at those doses; still, defer to the treating clinician, `[USER: verify]`.
- Choking/GI-obstruction caution with any bulk mucilage in those with swallowing disorders; take with adequate fluid.
