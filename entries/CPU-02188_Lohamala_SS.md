# Lohamala — Lohamala (as printed in Bhishagratna's Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch.38)

## *A metallic substance named only as the last member of the Trapvadi group; the translation gives no Latin name and no description, and the identification is ours and of low confidence: the Sanskrit compound is loha ("iron", or "metal") plus mala ("dross, impurity, rust"), which would make it an iron or metal rust or dross, the same reading the separate iron-rust row CPU-02420 rests on [USER: verify against a Sanskrit lexicon and Dallana]* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

### Origin & Tradition

> **METAL CAUTION.** The group this name belongs to is a list of metals (tin, lead, copper, silver, black iron, gold and Lohamala). Several metals and their salts are poisonous when swallowed. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take any metal preparation on the strength of it.

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, and only in one list.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from an extraction of the translation's drug lists; the 30 October check pass kept it as a separate row, because the first pass's suggestion that it duplicates the iron-rust row (CPU-02420) was not borne out by the text (see below).

**Sushruta (SS).** Lohamala occurs once in the Sushruta text on disk, in Sutrasthana chapter 38, the chapter on "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam)" in Bhishagratna's 1907 translation (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 441; Vol. I line 15782 of `Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`). The chapter opens: "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (line 9). The group is printed at lines 439 to 441: "The Trapvardi Group:—The group known as the Trapvadi Gana consists of Trapu, Sisa, Tamra, Rajata, Krishna-Lauha, Suvarna and Lohamala." The translator gives no gloss for any of the seven names. Six of them are, in the ordinary reading of the Sanskrit, tin, lead, copper, silver, black iron and gold [USER: verify], which fits the group's name (Trapu is the first member and gives the group its name). Lohamala closes the list.

The metrical text for the group follows at lines 443 to 449: "The present group is regarded as a good vermifuge and possessed of the virtue of neutralising the effects of chemical poison originated through incompatible combinations. Its therapeutic range covers jaundice, chlorosis, Meha (morbid secretions from the urethra), Hridroga (heart disease), thirst and maladies incidental to the effects of poison." These are claims for the group as a whole. The text attributes nothing to Lohamala separately.

**Why the identification is left open.** The text does not equate Lohamala with iron rust. Krishna-Lauha ("black iron" in the ordinary reading) is a separate member of the same group, so Lohamala is not simply iron. A different name for iron rust, Mandura, is printed by the same translation in the treatment of jaundice (Uttarasthana ch.44, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch44.txt` lines 190 to 198, 253 to 268, where the translator glosses it "iron-rust"), so Sushruta's text knows a rust of iron under another name. Whether Lohamala is the same article, the dross of some other metal, or something else is not settled by anything read. The only other chapter-file match for the word is the group list itself. No other source on disk names it.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent and give no analysis. In general terms, rust of iron is a mixture of iron oxides and hydroxides, and the dross or slag of a metal is whatever remains from smelting or working it. If the name means the rust or dross of one of the group's metals, it would consist mainly of that metal's oxides, with whatever impurities the ore carried. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Metals.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Nothing specific to Lohamala. Its group is said to be a vermifuge, to neutralise the effects of poison from incompatible combinations, and to serve in jaundice, chlorosis, Meha, heart disease, thirst and poison-related complaints.
- **What the text does not say.** No preparation, no part, no dose, no form (powder, calcined ash, decoction), and no purification (*shodhana*) for this item.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend swallowing any metal or metal residue.

### Preparation & Dosing

The group entry gives no preparation. Chapter 38 closes with a general instruction that applies to every group in it: the groups "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", with "only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group", or a whole group, or a group combined with another, "as the physician, in his discretion, would determine" (lines 561 to 568). That is a rule for choosing among the group's members, not a dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Iron oxides are insoluble and are the basis of iron-based pigments and of the rust seen on iron objects. Lead, tin and copper compounds vary widely in toxicity; lead and copper salts are poisonous when swallowed in any quantity. The classic's "chemical poison originated through incompatible combinations" has no one-to-one modern counterpart and is not translated into one here. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Bhishagratna 1907).** No taste (*rasa*), potency (*virya*) or post-digestive effect (*vipaka*) is printed for Lohamala. The Trapvadi group's metrical text gives only the group's actions (lines 443 to 449). Sutra chapters 45 and 46 (the chapters that print tastes and properties for liquids and for foods and drugs) were searched for the name and for words for rust and dross, and do not mention it.
- No dosha assignment for the single item is printed, and none is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* Lohamala is a name in a gana list, and the gana is a therapeutic grouping (a vermifuge and anti-poison group with the stated indications above). Sushruta's *rasayana* (rejuvenation) and *vajikarana* (generative vitality) chapters were not found to name it. No *ojas* assignment is attested for it, and none is made here. *Shodhana* (purification), which the tradition applies to metals and other mineral drugs, is not described for this item in the chapter read.

**Practitioner's note.** A metallic name in a list of metals, with no description, no preparation and no dose, and with an open identification. There is nothing here for a cultivator to use, and swallowing metal residues on the strength of a bare list entry would be reckless. The honest cultivation answer is to leave it alone.

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC AS A CLASS.** The group contains lead, copper and other metals whose compounds are poisonous. Do not ingest any metal, rust or dross preparation on the basis of this entry.
- **Identity.** The name is unidentified; the rust or dross reading is ours. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Given the class, avoid. [USER: verify]
- **Jaundice, heart disease, urinary discharges, poisoning.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **No preparation or purification is given.** Raw or unpurified metals are exactly what the tradition's own purification rules were written to guard against. [USER: verify]
