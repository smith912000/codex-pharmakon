# Crab flesh — Karkata (crab) and Krishna-Karkata (black crab), in the Padina group of the Sushruta meat chapter
## *The flesh of the crab. Bhishagratna prints "crab" and "black crab" in a list of animals; the source list `_sources/sushruta.csv` (lines 277 and 278) gives "Karkata (crab)" and "Krishna-Karkata (black crab)". No binomial is printed in the lines read* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The English is Bhishagratna's translation of the *Sushruta Samhita*. Crab flesh is treated in the meat group of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink (Annapana-Vidhi-madhyayam)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 4-6). The chapter lists the kinds of flesh by habitat, aquatic, marshy (*Anupa*), village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed and high-ground, with "each succeeding kind" superior to the one before (lines 385-394). The crab belongs to the second of those, the marsh animals. That group, the text says, "may be divided into five groups": the *Kulacharas* (shore-dwellers), the *Plavas* (divers or swimmers), the *Koshastha* (conchiferous aquatic animals such as molluscs), the *Padinas* and the *Matsya* (fish) (lines 659-669).

The *Padina* species is introduced in one sentence (lines 764-766): "Animals such as, the tortoise, alligator, crab, black crab, porpoise, etc., belong to this species." The metrical text that follows (lines 775-786) joins the *Padinas* with the conchiferous *Shankha* order: "The flesh of animals of the Shankha and Kurma orders is sweet in taste and digestion, cooling in its potency, demulcent, and beneficial to stool and the Pittam. It destroys the deranged Vayu and produces Kapham." Then the crab itself: "Of these, the species of black crab is strength-giving and heat-making in its potency, and tends to destroy the deranged Vayu. The white species is laxative and diuretic in its effect, and tends to bring about an adhesion of fractured bones (or produces fermentation). It destroys the Vayu and Pittam." The English does not repeat the word "crab" in the last sentence, so I read "the white species" as the white crab, which is the natural sense but is not stated. The parenthesis "(or produces fermentation)" is the translator's alternative reading of the clause. [USER: verify]

So the text separates two kinds of crab, black and white, and gives each a line. The black crab is warming in potency (*heat-making*), in contrast to the group's "cooling", and is "strength-giving". That is the only place in the lines read where a crab is given a quality that differs from its group.

Other places where Sushruta names the crab, which I opened. None of them is crab flesh; they are listed so that the crab's appearances in the book are not lost:

- *Fat or lard.* In the treatment of the major nervous disorders (*Maha-Vata-Vyadhi*), in a case of *Apatanaka*, "The fat or lard of a cock, crab, Krishna-fish, porpoise or of a boar should be taken by the patient" (*Chikitsasthana* ch.5, lines 509-511). In a chapter of mixed diseases (*Misraka-Chikitsita*) an oil is cooked with *Talapatri*, *Ashvagandha*, *Arka*, *Vakuchi* seeds, *Saindhava*, *Sarala* and *Langali*, with the "lard of a Karkata (crab) and of a Godha (a kind of lizard)", and "proves beneficial in cases of Unmantha", a disease of the ear-lobes (*Chikitsasthana* ch.25, lines 108-111).
- *Eggs, in the aphrodisiac chapter.* In the chapter on increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons (*Kshina-Valiyam Vaji-Karana*, *Chikitsasthana* ch.26): "The testes of a he-goat or of a porpoise mixed with salt and powdered long-pepper, and fried in clarified butter should be taken for speedy and effective aphrodisiac purposes. The eggs of a tortoise, of an alligator, or of a crab, or the semen of a male buffalo, of a he-ass, or of a he-goat should be similarly taken for the same purpose" (lines 130-136). A footnote marked with an asterisk is attached to "crab" there, and in the OCR the footnote reads "Some explain [a Sanskrit word, lost in the OCR] as Louse-sparrows" (line 160); I read it as attached to "crab", but the OCR does not make the attachment certain, so whether the word is a crab at all in that passage is open. [USER: verify] The footnote on "semen" says that "semen would mean the testes". These are eggs, not flesh, and the passage is the one place where crab material sits in a *Vajikarana* chapter.
- *Shells, in fumigation.* In the chapter on medicated fumes, snuffs and gargles, "Nerves, skin, horns, hoofs, shells of a crab, dried fish, dry meat or worms, etc., and such other emetic drugs should be used in the Vamaniya-Dhuma", the emetic smoke (*Chikitsasthana* ch.40, lines 32-47).
- *A name that is not the animal.* *Karkata-shringi* ("crab's horn") appears among cough-allaying drugs (ch.40 line 30) and in a commentator's footnote (ch.17 line 140); it is a drug name and not crab material, and is not used here.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, crab flesh is muscle: protein, with little fat, and minerals including zinc, copper and iodine; crab shells are chitin and calcium carbonate. Crabs, like other crustaceans, are a common cause of food allergy.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The text records crab flesh as an animal food of the marsh (*Padina*) kind: sweet, cooling, demulcent, wind-subduing and *Kapham*-producing as a group, with the black crab strength-giving and warming and the white crab laxative, diuretic and thought to help the union of fractured bones. It also records crab lard in two oils and crab shells in an emetic smoke. Crab is an ordinary food in many cuisines and needs no protocol from this Codex. The Codex does not recommend the lard, the eggs or the shell-smoke uses, or any use of crab as a bone-setting or aphrodisiac remedy.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the text gives: the flesh as food; the lard in an oil (ch.25) or taken by the patient (ch.5); the eggs, "similarly taken" with the goat and porpoise preparations (ch.26); and the shells in the emetic smoke (ch.40). The aphrodisiac passage's own preparation is for the testes of the he-goat or porpoise: "mixed with salt and powdered long-pepper, and fried in clarified butter". No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Crab meat is a source of lean protein and minerals. Shellfish allergy, including to crustaceans, is common and can be severe. Raw or undercooked crab can carry bacteria such as *Vibrio* and parasites such as the lung fluke. Crab shell is mostly chitin and calcium carbonate. Nothing in that supports healing fractures. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** ch.46 lines 775-786 (the *Shankha* and *Kurma* orders, with the crab): flesh sweet in taste and in digestion; cooling in potency; demulcent; beneficial to stool and to *Pittam*; destroys deranged *Vayu*; produces *Kapham*. Black crab: strength-giving, heat-making in potency, destroys deranged *Vayu*. White species: laxative, diuretic, tends to bring about adhesion of fractured bones, destroys *Vayu* and *Pittam*. The English prints no separate *vipaka* for the crab; the group's "sweet in digestion" is the nearest. The group statement belongs to the group, and the black and white lines are specific.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Crab flesh is attested in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 as a member of the *Padina* group, with its own black-crab and white-crab lines (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 764-786). "Strength-giving" is a property stated for the black crab in a chapter on food and drink; it is not a placement in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and it is not read as a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. The one passage where crab material sits in a *Vajikarana* chapter names the eggs of a crab, not the flesh, and its footnote leaves the identification of the word open (*Chikitsasthana* ch.26, lines 133-136). The English prints no claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for the crab. The popular *sattvic*/*rajasic*/*tamasic* food lists belong to *Samkhya*-era and later material, not to Sushruta, and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes crab flesh a cultivation substance. Sushruta classes it as a marsh-animal food of two kinds, one strengthening and warming, one laxative and diuretic. That is dietary advice, not practice. The only aphrodisiac line concerns eggs and a disputed word, and gives a cultivator nothing to rely on. A cultivator who eats crab eats it as food. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Allergy:** crustacean allergy is common and can be severe or fatal; crab is an absolute contraindication in anyone with it.
- **Hygiene and infection:** raw, undercooked or stale crab carries bacterial (*Vibrio*), viral and parasitic risk; crab should be cooked fresh and not left standing.
- **Toxins:** crabs from contaminated water can carry heavy metals and, in some regions, algal toxins; the cadmium content of crab organs is a known concern.
- **Purine load:** shellfish are high in purines and may aggravate gout.
- **Not a bone-setting treatment:** the "adhesion of fractured bones" line is a historical statement; a fracture needs medical care.
- **Conservation:** some crab fisheries are regulated or over-fished; follow local rules.
