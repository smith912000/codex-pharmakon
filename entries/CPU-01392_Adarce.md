# Adarce — Adarkes (adarces), Dioscorides 5-137
## *A somewhat salty hardened fluid that comes out of marshy ground in Galatia and sets on reeds and grass, soft and hollow like a sponge; the translation glosses it "Potassium Sulphate — Arcanum Duplicatum", a gloss the chapter does not explain* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-137, *Adarkes* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10546; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 910; list row `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 863, "Adarkes", class mineral, flagged as an uncertain identification; the book index sends the reader to page 814). The whole chapter was read. The translator's lines beneath the heading read "Potassium Sulphate — Arcanum Duplicatum" and "IRRITANT, CATHARTIC" (lines 10547-10548). The chapter then reads (line 10549):

"That which is called adarces grows in Galatia and it is (as it were) a somewhat salty hardened fluid, coming out of moist and marshy places in times of drought and adhering to reeds and grass. It is like the flower [salt] of the stone of Asia in colour, but in its whole shape like alcyonium [5-136], soft and hollow in many places (and as it were) alcyonium palustre [bastard sponges of the marshes]. It suitable used for stripping off leprosy, freckles, lichenae [skin disease], and other similar things. In brief, it is sharp, drawing fluids from depths, and it is good for sciatica."

The text thus gives a provenance (Galatia; moist and marshy places; in times of drought), a habit (adhering to reeds and grass), a colour by comparison, a shape by comparison, and uses. The comparison with "the flower of the stone of Asia" points to 5-142 *Asios lithos* (line 10572), where the "flower [salt]" is "the yellowish salt fluid which gathers on the outside of the stones", "sometimes white in colour, sometimes like pumice stone, inclining to a yellow" (line 10575). The comparison with alcyonium points to 5-136 (line 10539), which describes five kinds of a sponge-like material and says (line 10545) that the first and second kinds are used in treatments for women and for freckles, lichen, leprosy, vitiligines, black patches and spots on the face and body. Both are different chapters and are not described further here.

**On the translator's gloss.** The chapter does not call adarce a sulphate, a potassium salt or a purgative. It names no cathartic use; the uses it gives are on the skin and for sciatica, and it calls the material "sharp". The gloss "Potassium Sulphate — Arcanum Duplicatum" and the label "IRRITANT, CATHARTIC" are the translator's own and are not explained in the text read; they do not obviously fit the description of a soft, hollow crust on marsh reeds. The Codex's list row also marks the identification uncertain. Which material Dioscorides meant is not settled by the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated from the chapter. In general terms, a "somewhat salty" crust left on marsh plants when moist ground dries in drought is an evaporative salt deposit, and such deposits are mixtures of water-soluble salts (chlorides, carbonates, sulphates) with some organic and mineral matter; the proportions depend on the site. The translator's gloss, potassium sulphate, is a soluble salt that is irritating in concentrated form. These are general statements about such deposits and not a reading of the chapter, and no composition is asserted for adarce.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. Dioscorides records adarce as a sharp external material for freckles, skin scaling and sciatica. The identification is uncertain, the material was a naturally formed salt crust of unknown composition, and the text calls it "sharp". No modern protocol is supplied.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given in 5-137: the chapter says the material is "suitable used" for the skin conditions listed and gives no grinding, washing, mixing or application method, and no quantity. (Chapter 5-136, alcyonium, gives a burning and washing method in clay pots with salt and says it is "washed like cadmia", line 10545; the text does not say that adarce is treated in the same way.) ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM).** The chapter prints one quality: it is "sharp, drawing fluids from depths". The translator's heading adds "IRRITANT, CATHARTIC". No warming, cooling, moistening or drying quality is printed in the chapter itself. The comparison material 5-142 describes both the stone and its flower as "astringent" and as corroding "gently"; that statement belongs to 5-142 and is not carried across to adarce.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. What the herbal offers a cultivator is a discipline of attention (provenance, season of gathering, sensory assay, adulteration detection), and this chapter shows it in part: it gives the region (Galatia), the conditions of formation (moist marshy ground, in times of drought, on reeds and grass), and a way to recognise the product by colour and shape, each by comparison with another named drug. It gives no assay and no adulterant. The chapter is therapeutic and carries no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to take from it for cultivation beyond the author's habit of recognition: where it forms, in what weather, and what it looks like next to something already known. The material itself is an unidentified, "sharp" external salt and is not a recommended practice. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Irritant:** the text calls it "sharp"; concentrated salts burn broken skin and the eyes.
- **Unidentified material:** the identification is uncertain; salt crusts from marshes may carry other minerals, bacteria or contaminants.
- **Serious conditions:** persistent skin changes (including those the text calls "leprosy") and sciatica need medical assessment.
- **Marshland collecting:** marshes and wetlands are often protected habitats; the status of any site was not retrieved. [USER: verify]
