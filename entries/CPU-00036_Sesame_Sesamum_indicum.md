### Origin & Tradition

Sesame is the oldest known oilseed crop — its cultivation in the Indus Valley dates to 3500 BCE. The phrase "Open Sesame" from *One Thousand and One Nights* refers to the sesame seed pod bursting open when ripe — a metaphor for hidden treasure that reflects the plant's reputation as a supreme nutritive and tonic food across the ancient world. Its seed oil (*gingelly oil*, *til oil*, *sesame oil*) was the primary cooking and medicinal oil of ancient Mesopotamia, Egypt, India, and China before olive oil expansion westward. The lignan content (sesamin, sesamolin), the complete amino acid profile, and the calcium/magnesium/zinc density explain why sesame occupied the "king of seeds" position in Jing-building protocols across all traditions.

**EP — Egyptian (Šemsem — found in tomb of Tutankhamun; documented in Ebers):** Sesame seeds and sesame oil (*šemsem*) appear extensively in Egyptian medical and culinary contexts. Sesame oil was used as a primary drug vehicle and emollient; seeds as nutritive tonic and as a ceremonial offering. The Ebers Papyrus documents sesame oil preparations for wound healing, skin conditions, and as a base for medicated oils.

**AT — Assyrian (Šamaššammū — 𒊺𒈠𒊺𒄠𒈬 — the "oil of the sun plant"):** Sesame (*šamaššammū* — the "plant of the sun") was the primary oilseed of Mesopotamia. Cuneiform tablets document massive sesame cultivation and oil production in the Tigris-Euphrates valley. Medical texts document sesame oil as a wound healing medium, drug vehicle, and tonic. The Babylonian *šamaššammū*-based oil preparations appear in numerous therapeutic contexts.

**DMM — De Materia Medica (Σήσαμον — Sēsamon):** Dioscorides covers sesame in Book II. He notes the seed is nutritive and fattening; the oil is emollient and digestive; topical preparations for earache (sesame oil instillation — still a valid folk remedy); and the anti-inflammatory use of sesame oil for skin conditions. He distinguishes Indian sesame (black seed) from the white-seeded cultivated type.

**CM — Canon of Medicine (سمسم — Simsim):** Ibn Sina classifies sesame as warm in the first degree and moist — the warm-moist profile making it ideal for cold-dry (Vata/phlegmatic) constitutions. He provides extensive coverage: nutritive tonic (*muqawwi*); expectorant; topical emollient; laxative (in oil form); and notably as a *musammim* (strengthening) preparation for the brain and nerves. His description of sesame as a "brain food" anticipates the omega-6/lignan neuroprotective mechanisms.

**SS — Ayurveda (तिल — Tila):** Sesame (*Tila*) is one of the most important Ayurvedic Rasayana foods. Classification:
- **Rasa:** Tikta (bitter) + Madhura (sweet) + Kashaya (astringent) — complex triple-rasa
- **Virya:** Ushna (hot)
- **Vipaka:** Madhura
- **Dosha action:** Vata shamaka; Pitta-Kapha increasing in excess
- **Primary actions:** Balya (strength-building); Keshya (hair-promoting); Twachya (skin-nourishing); Dantya (tooth-strengthening); Stanya-janana (galactagogue); Shukrajanana (semen-promoting); Brimhana (nourishing/anabolic)
- **Til oil (*taila*):** The Sanskrit word *taila* (oil) derives from *tila* — reflecting sesame's position as the prototype oil in Indian medicine. Used in *Abhyanga* (medicated oil massage), *Nasya* (nasal administration), and *Karna Purana* (ear oil)
- **Black sesame (Kāla Tila):** Specifically classified as a superior Jing/Shukra tonic — the black-seeded variety is stronger for reproductive and anti-aging applications

**SBJ/TCM (黑芝麻 — Hēi Zhīma — Black Sesame; 脂麻 — Zhī Má):** Black sesame is a major TCM Jing tonic. Classification: sweet, neutral; Liver-Kidney channel.
- **Primary actions:** Nourishes Liver and Kidney Yin-Jing; tonifies Essence; moistens the intestines; blackens hair (the traditional use of black sesame for premature greying is a Jing-tonification clinical marker)
- **Key formula:** *Rùn Cháng Wán* (Moisten Intestines Pill) uses black sesame for constipation from blood-Jing deficiency
- **Five black foods:** Black sesame is one of the five classical TCM "black foods" (with black bean, black fungus, black rice, polygonum) for Kidney-Jing nourishment — the black color energetically targeting the Kidney

### Active Components

**Lignans:**
- **Sesamin** — primary lignan; the most pharmacologically studied; anti-inflammatory (NF-κB inhibition); antioxidant; hypocholesterolemic; liver-protective; enhances γ-tocopherol (vitamin E) bioavailability and retention
- **Sesamolin** — secondary lignan; metabolized to sesamol and sesaminol; strong antioxidant activity; neuroprotective
- **Sesamol** — the antioxidant breakdown product; one of the most potent natural antioxidants studied; DNA-protective

**Fatty acids (seed oil ~50% of weight):**
- **Linoleic acid (LA, C18:2ω-6)** — 40–50%; essential PUFA; inflammatory signaling precursor (also anti-inflammatory in appropriate balance)
- **Oleic acid (C18:1ω-9)** — 35–40%; monounsaturated; cardiovascular protective
- **Palmitic acid, stearic acid** — saturated; minor components

**Protein:**
- 20–25% protein; excellent amino acid profile including methionine; the primary limiting amino acid in plant proteins

**Minerals (exceptionally dense):**
- **Calcium** — 975mg/100g (hull-on) — the richest whole-food plant calcium source; 88mg/100g (hulled); significant for bone integrity
- **Magnesium** — 351mg/100g; ATP co-factor; muscle function; sleep
- **Zinc** — 7.8mg/100g; testosterone synthesis; immune function; the Shukra tonic mechanism partly explained by zinc content
- **Iron** — 14.5mg/100g; significant non-heme iron source
- **Phosphorus** — 629mg/100g

**Vitamins:**
- **Vitamin E (γ-tocopherol primarily)** — significant; sesamin enhances its retention
- **B vitamins** — thiamine, niacin, B6

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**1. Jing-building anabolic food ([BUILD] phase — primary)**
Sesame seeds provide the complete anabolic nutritive package: protein (muscle substrate), zinc (testosterone synthesis), calcium-magnesium (bone mineral, muscle function), lignans (hormonal modulation), and omega-6 PUFA (membrane structure). The daily handful of sesame seeds is one of the most nutrient-dense foods available — 30g provides ~5g protein, 273mg calcium, 100mg magnesium, 2.3mg zinc.

Black sesame specifically is used in Jing-building protocols: the TCM principle that black foods tonify Kidney-Jing is supported by the higher lignan and antioxidant content in black-seeded varieties.

**2. Hair, skin, joint nourishment (Jing-tissue maintenance)**
The traditional Ayurvedic-TCM use of sesame oil in *Abhyanga* massage for skin and joint lubrication has pharmacological basis: sesame oil penetrates deeply due to its small molecular structure; sesamin and sesamol provide antioxidant protection to lipid membranes in skin; the lignan-tocopherol system protects against UV-induced skin damage. For practitioners dealing with joint wear, skin dryness, and hair loss from training stress (all Jing-depletion signs in TCM), sesame oil massage is a validated restorative protocol.

**3. Intestinal lubrication ([RESET] phase)**
The high oil content lubricates the intestinal mucosa, facilitating bowel transit. The classical TCM *Rùn Cháng* (moistening intestines) application for blood-Jing deficiency constipation — dry stools, no urgency, hard pellets — is specifically treated with black sesame and other oily seeds.

**4. Oil pulling (Kavala Graha — oral health)**
Sesame oil used for *oil pulling* (swishing 15–20mL sesame oil in mouth for 15–20 minutes) is documented in Ayurveda as *Kavala Graha* and *Gandusha*. Studies confirm reduction in oral bacteria, plaque, and gingivitis compared to chlorhexidine, with fewer side effects.

### Preparation & Dosing

**Whole sesame seeds (daily Jing tonic food):**
1–3 tbsp (15–45g) sesame seeds daily in food. **Hulled sesame provides more bioavailable minerals.** Toast lightly to enhance flavor and digestibility. Tahini (sesame paste) is the most convenient high-dose preparation.

**Black sesame paste (Hēi Zhīma Hú — TCM Jing tonic):**
2 tbsp black sesame paste (lightly roasted, stone-ground) in warm water or milk; add honey; drink morning or before sleep. Classical anti-ageing, hair-blackening, Kidney-Jing formula.

**Sesame oil (Abhyanga massage):**
Warm cold-pressed sesame oil; full-body self-massage (*Abhyanga*) 20–30 minutes before shower; 3–7 times/week during [RESET] and [CYCLE-OFF] phases. Reduces Vata; lubricates joints; calms nervous system.

**Oil pulling (Kavala Graha):**
1 tbsp cold-pressed sesame oil held in mouth and swished for 15–20 minutes on empty stomach; spit out (do not swallow — contains oral bacteria); rinse mouth with water.

**Sesame goma-ae / tahini in training nutrition:**
Tahini (sesame paste) is an extremely convenient whole-food protein-mineral-fat source for training meals; 30g tahini = 9g protein + 85mg calcium + significant omega-6 + lignans.

### Scientific Correlation

- **Sesamin anti-inflammatory:** Kinoshita M et al. (2013): sesamin supplementation reduced inflammatory markers in human subjects; Bhatt DL mechanism review: NF-κB pathway inhibition confirmed
- **Sesamin/γ-tocopherol synergy:** Cooney RV et al. (2001): sesamin dramatically increased γ-tocopherol retention in vivo by inhibiting CYP4F2 tocopherol catabolism — a unique pharmacological interaction
- **Cardiovascular (lignans):** Miyawaki T et al. (2009): sesame lignan supplementation significantly reduced LDL and improved endothelial function in clinical trial; antioxidant mechanism
- **Oil pulling:** Asokan S et al. (2011): sesame oil pulling significantly reduced Streptococcus mutans counts, plaque index, and gingivitis compared to chlorhexidine mouthwash — RCT; Shanbhag VK (2017) review confirms mechanism
- **Bone health (sesame calcium):** Massey LK et al. — hull-on sesame provides significant bioavailable calcium; calcium-magnesium ratio in sesame is ~2.5:1, within optimal range for co-absorption
- **Anti-aging/neuroprotective (sesamol):** Ahmad ST et al. (2012): sesamol demonstrated neuroprotective effects in rodent models; DNA damage protection via antioxidant mechanism

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian (EP).** Sesame's Egyptian standing is strong but food-and-craft, not vital-cultivation: *šemsem* as a cooking and lamp oil, an emollient, a drug vehicle, and a ceremonial offering, with seeds recovered from Tutankhamun's tomb. A ceremonial offering and a drug base are uses of the substance, not statements about its action on the *ka* or *ib*; no *heka* recitation and no constitutional framing are attested for it in the sources retrieved. Presence — even prestigious, funerary presence — is not status. **(c) absent.**

**Assyrian (AT).** No cultivation status is attested, and the blocking warning on Assyrian identifications applies. *Šamaššammū*, the "plant of the sun," was Mesopotamia's primary oilseed — cultivated at scale, pressed for the region's staple oil, used as a wound-healing medium and drug vehicle. This is economic and therapeutic centrality, not a claim that the oil husbands the vital constitution. **(c) absent.**

**Greek (DMM).** Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. He records sesame as nutritive and fattening, the oil as emollient and digestive, with topical uses for the ears and skin, and distinguishes the Indian black seed from the white cultivated type. That varietal discrimination — knowing black from white, seed from expressed oil — is exactly the discipline of attention his text trains. No vital-cultivation status is claimed. **(c) absent.**

**Unani (CM).** Ibn Sina classes sesame warm in the first degree and moist, and gives it a genuine strengthening role: *muqawwi* (fortifying) and a *musammim*-type strengthener for the brain and nerves, its warm-moist profile suited to cold-dry constitutions. This edges toward cultivation, but the *Canon* frames it as a nourishing-tonic therapeutic action for the faculties, not as an attested regimen for conserving innate heat and radical moisture; that longevity doctrine is not tied to this substance in the sources retrieved. **(b) framed — a strengthening faculty attested, cultivation status not.**

**Chinese (SBJ).** Sesame — 胡麻 *hú má*, traditionally identified with *Sesamum* — is recorded in the *Shénnóng Běncǎo Jīng* (c. 1st–2nd c. CE) in the **upper grade (上品)**, the class of substances taken long-term to nourish life; the classic states that sustained use "lightens the body" (輕身 *qīng shēn*), extends years, and forestalls greying. This is an attested cultivation status, not a therapeutic one, and it aligns with the later Liver–Kidney tonic reputation of the black seed. (Note: some scholarship debates whether the earliest *hú má* denoted sesame or flax; the tradition from Li Shizhen onward reads it as sesame.) **(a) attested — upper-grade *san pin* status, *Shénnóng Běncǎo Jīng*.**

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Tila* is classed among the *rasayana* substances in the classical Samhitas: Charaka places it as the supreme seed for *snehana* and *vata* pacification and describes it as promoting longevity and strength, while Sushruta calls *tila taila* the foremost of oils — *brimhana* (bulk-building), *vrishya* (generative), *medhya* and *tvakprasadana* — with the black seed (*kāla tila*) held the more potent. This is an attested rejuvenative standing, distinct from *tila*'s separate ritual life in funerary and ancestral offering (*tarpana*), which is a religious observance and not a cultivation status. **(a) attested — *rasayana*/*brimhana* standing, Charaka and Sushruta Samhitas.**

**Tibetan (FMT).** Sesame enters Tibetan practice through the Indian materia medica as a nourishing oil, but no specific *bcud len* (essence-extraction) attribution and no formal subtle-body-practice category are attested for it in the sources retrieved, and the Four Tantras assert no such category to fill. **(c) absent.**

**Practitioner's note.** This is one of the rare foods with a genuine, sourced cultivation standing — and in two independent traditions at once, each in its own terms: an upper-grade *qīng shēn* substance in the Chinese classic, a *rasayana*/*brimhana* seed in the Ayurvedic Samhitas. What a cultivator actually does with it is undramatic and long-horizon: a daily handful or spoon of (preferably black) sesame or tahini as a building food, and warm sesame-oil *abhyanga* in `[RESET]` and `[CYCLE-OFF]` phases. Hold this apart from sesame's ritual use (the offering) and its sheer culinary ubiquity — those are why it is everywhere, but the cultivation claim rests on the two texts above, not on its presence in a tomb or a kitchen.

### Contraindications

- **Sesame allergy** — one of the top 9 food allergens (US FDA 2023); IgE-mediated; anaphylaxis risk; prevalence ~0.1–0.2% but reactions can be severe; screen before therapeutic protocols
- **Pitta-excess constitution (excess)** — the Ushna (hot) virya of sesame can aggravate Pitta in excess amounts; moderate consumption; use with cooling foods if Pitta-dominant
- **Kapha-excess constitution** — the oily, heavy, nourishing qualities aggravate Kapha; appropriate in Vata types; reduce in those prone to congestion, weight gain
- **Hypercalciuria/kidney stones** — sesame is extremely calcium-rich; those with calcium oxalate kidney stones or hypercalciuria should moderate hull-on sesame (the hulls contain oxalates)
- **Diverticulitis** — whole seeds are best avoided during active diverticulitis flares (particle size may lodge in diverticula); use sesame oil or tahini instead
