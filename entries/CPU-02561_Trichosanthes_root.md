# Trichosanthes root — 栝蒌根 *gua lou gen* (also 地楼 *di lou*, "Earth Tower"; 果裸 *guo luo*; 天瓜 *tian gua*, "Heaven Melon")

## *Trichosanthes kirilowii* Maxim., Chinese cucumber, the climbing gourd of northern China whose large tuberous root is the drug; the identification is the modern standard one for 栝蒌 and is of high confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** A search of the other traditions' texts in `Sources/` for *Trichosanthes kirilowii* found nothing; the Codex's *patola* entry (I_372, *T. dioica*, Sushruta) is a different species. The entry rests on one tradition, in Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction. The spine row (CPU-02561) was split off from CPU-02262, the Wang gua row, because the placeholder had run two Shennong monographs together; this entry treats 栝蒌根 only, and the other, 王瓜, is I_1508. Yang Shou-zhong's 1998 translation has the same monograph under a different pharmaceutical name, *Fructus Trichosanthis Kirlowii* ("Gua Lou", fruit), although the indications are those of the root entry in Sun (see below); the Codex notes that Yang's drug name and Sun's heading differ in the part named.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ), the Shennong text proper.** The entry sits in the middle classic (中经). Sun's 味 paragraph reads: "味苦，寒。主消渴，身热烦满，大热，补虚安中，续绝伤。一名地楼。生川谷及山阴。" ("Bitter, cold. It governs wasting-thirst, bodily heat with vexation and fullness, great heat; it supplements vacuity and calms the center; it joins severed injuries. Another name: Earth Tower [地楼]. It grows in rivers and valleys and on the shady side of mountains."). The text proper names the root in its heading and gives no part otherwise. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 栝蒌根 *gualougen*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1859)

**Second witness, Yang 1998 (attributed to Yang).** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`, lines 2697-2701) reads: "Gua Lou… is bitter and cold. It mainly treats wasting thirst, generalized fever, vexatious fullness, and great heat. It supplements vacuity, quiets the center, and mends expiry and damage. Its other name is Di Lou (Earth Building). It grows in rivers and valleys as well as shady places in the mountains." This agrees with Sun's paragraph; Yang's "Earth Building" renders 地楼 where this entry reads "Earth Tower". Yang's note 158 (line 2702 onward) is later commentary: it calls the drug "an ideal medicinal for cough and a miraculous one for thirst", and notes that "in the text, it is said to be bitter, but actually it is bitter and sweet".

**Wu Pu layer (3rd century, quoted by Sun).** Line 1861: "栝蒌，一名泽巨，一名泽姑" ("*gualou*; other names: Marsh Great [泽巨], Marsh Aunt [泽姑]").

**Mingyi bielu layer (later additions, quoted by Sun).** Line 1863: "一名果裸，一名天瓜，一名泽姑。实，名黄瓜。二月、八月采根，曝干，三十日成。生宏农。" ("Other names: *guo luo*, *tian gua*, *ze gu*. The fruit is named *huang gua* [黄瓜]. The root is gathered in the second and eighth months and dried in the sun; it is complete in thirty days. It grows at Hongnong."). This layer names two parts, the root (this entry) and the fruit (*huang gua*, yellow melon), and gives the one preparation instruction in the entry: sun-drying for thirty days.

**Sun's own note (案).** Line 1865 cites the *Shuowen* ("…，…蒌，果也", with characters lost), the *Guangya* ("王白，…也", Sun adds "should be 王…"), the *Erya* ("the fruit of 果裸 is 栝蒌"), Guo Pu ("now the people of Qi call it 天瓜"), the *Mao shi* ("the fruit of 果裸 also spreads over the eaves"; the commentary: "果裸 is 栝蒌") and the *Lüshi chunqiu* ("王善生", with Gao You: "善 may be written 瓜"), and he corrects the *Lüshi chunqiu* 善 as a mistake for a character lost in the file. The note establishes the names and an early picture of a vine that climbs over eaves. The "王…" forms in the note overlap with the 王瓜 (Wang gua) name in I_1508; the characters are lost in both entries and the Codex does not resolve how the two drugs were distinguished in those passages. [USER: verify]

**Identity.** The names 栝蒌, 果裸 and 天瓜, the vine "spreading over the eaves" and the large root agree with *Trichosanthes kirilowii*. The early text gives no further description. Confidence: high.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. As a general fact, the root of *Trichosanthes kirilowii* is rich in starch and contains a ribosome-inactivating protein, trichosanthin, which has been studied in China and has abortifacient activity. This is a general statement and not drawn from the Codex's sources. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Shennong text lists a bitter, cold root for wasting-thirst and heat with vexation and fullness, with the further actions of supplementing vacuity, calming the center and joining severed injuries. These are the text's terms; the Codex does not translate "wasting-thirst" into a modern diagnosis (in particular, it does not equate it with diabetes) and gives no protocol. Constant thirst, fever and injuries need medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation given is in the Mingyi layer: the root is gathered in the second and eighth months and dried in the sun, "complete in thirty days" (line 1863). The Shennong text proper gives none. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

*Trichosanthes kirilowii* root (the drug *tian hua fen* in current Chinese pharmacy) is in modern use, which is a general fact and not a finding. Trichosanthin has been studied in China for its effect on pregnancy and has been the subject of research on viral infection (general facts). No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ.** The Shennong text prints "味苦，寒" (bitter, cold). The Wu Pu and Mingyi layers add no flavour or temperature. The text proper prints no toxicity word. Sun's appendix of drug relations (附：诸药制使, from line 4191; a later compilation, with Tang Shenwei's remark at line 4193) lists "栝蒌 枸杞为使；恶干姜；畏牛膝、干漆；反乌头" (line 4375: "[Lycium] as its envoy; averse to dried ginger; fears [Achyranthes] and dried lacquer; opposed to wu tou"). The opposition to 乌头 is also recorded from the aconite side (line 4439, see I_1520). It is not the Shennong text proper.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle classic (中经), whose preface (line 1677) says middle drugs "nourish the nature to correspond with man; non-toxic or toxic, weigh what is fit; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the thin [羸], take the middle classic". The entry has no 久服 (taken long) clause and no promise of lightening the body or lengthening life. "Supplements vacuity and calms the center" (补虚安中) is a restorative action but not a status. The class is a frame; the drug carries no cultivation status of its own. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 栝蒌根 *gualougen*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1859)

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade root for thirst and heat, in the text's own frame, to be taken for a defined complaint and set aside. Nothing in the entry supports a long-term practice, and no dose is given. The honest practice is to read the entry for its indications and leave the root alone.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. As a general fact, trichosanthin has abortifacient activity, so root preparations of this species should not be used in pregnancy. [USER: verify] Sun's later appendix lists an opposition to 乌头 (line 4375), a reason in the tradition's own terms not to combine them. Persistent thirst and fever need medical assessment, and nothing should be gathered or taken on the strength of this entry.
