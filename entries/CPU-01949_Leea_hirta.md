# Leea hirta — Kaka-janghi / Kaka-jangha (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3); the Sanskrit name is a positional pairing

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was opened on 13 October from the extraction (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`), where the Latin line is kept as "not paired to a Sanskrit name (OCR column split)". **The pairing, by counting.** On the page that carries this Latin line, the Sanskrit run (lines 31795–31845) goes "Jambira" ... "Jayanti", then "Kachu (Mana)", "Kachchhursi", "Kadali", "Kadamba", "Kaivarta-Mustaka", "**Kaka-janghi**" (line 31824), "Ka'ka-ma'chi", "Ka'ka-na'si'" (OCR), "Kakkola", "Ka'koli", "Kalambi", "Kalinga", "Kamala" and "Kampilla". The Latin run after the twelve Jambira-to-Jayanti entries goes "Arum Indicum; A. Colocasia" (Kachu), "See Atmagupti" (Kachchhursi), "Musa Sapientum. Plantain" (Kadali), "Anthocephalus Kadamba" (Kadamba), "Cyperus Rotundus" (Kaivarta-Mustaka), "**Leea hirta.**" (line 31877), "Solanum nigrum" (Ka'ka-ma'chi), "Cocculus Suberosus; Solanum Indicum", "(Fruit of) Cocculus Indicus" and so on. The sixth Sanskrit name after Jayanti, Kaka-janghi, stands against the sixth Latin entry, and the five before it and the one after it fit (Kadali with plantain, Kadamba with *Anthocephalus kadamba*, Kaivarta-Mustaka with *Cyperus rotundus*, Kakamachi with *Solanum nigrum*). The pairing is therefore sound by count and by neighbours, but it is positional. Further down the page, the order of the names Kakoli, Kalambi and Kalinga against the Latin entries is less clear, which is a reminder that a dropped OCR line can shift a pairing by one. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The name in the chapters.* A search of all the chapter folders (Sutra, Chikitsa, Uttara, Kalpa, Nidana, Sharira) for "Kaka-jangha" and its spellings found one place; the name is not in the Sutra 38 group lists (`names.csv` has no row for it) and is not among the Sutra 46 foods.

*Chikitsa 19 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch19.txt`, lines 408–416).* The chapter "deals with the medical treatment of hernia, hydrocele, scrotal tumour, etc. (Vriddhi), diseases of the genital organ (Upadamsa) and elephantiasis (Slipada)" (lines 3–8). In the passage on Slipada, under the heading "The Alkaline Remedies", the text reads: "An alkali should be prepared from the ashes of Kakadani, Kaka-jangha, Vrihati, Kantakarika, Kadamba-pushpa, Mandari, Lamba, Sukanasa in the usual way by filtering them (twenty one times) after dissolving them in cow's urine. The expressed juice of Kakodumbarika, Sukanasa and the decoction of Madana fruit should be mixed with the above alkaline preparation (and duly cooked in the manner of Kshira-paka)." The line breaks in the OCR split the name ("Kdka- / janghd"). That is the one use of the name in the chapters read. It is a plant ash in a remedy for elephantiasis, and the part used is not stated beyond "ashes".

**What this does not establish.** The chapter gives no description of the plant, so nothing in the text links Kaka-jangha to a plant with hairy leaves, and the Latin identification rests wholly on the appendix. The name, which means "crow's leg" in Sanskrit, belongs to a family of crow names in the Sushruta lists (Kakadani, Kakamachi, Kakodumbarika) and is a descriptive name; the Latin and the English meaning are not given by the text. ### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Leea* is a genus of shrubs of the tropics of the Old World, sometimes placed in its own family and sometimes with the grape family (Vitaceae) [USER: verify]. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the Sushruta drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records one use of the name: its ashes go into an alkali made in cow's urine for elephantiasis (Chikitsa 19). The Latin name is the translator's. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose, and does not translate *Slipada* into a modern diagnosis other than the translator's own gloss, "elephantiasis".

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives one preparation, with locus. Chikitsa 19, lines 410–414: the ashes are dissolved in cow's urine and filtered twenty-one times; the filtrate is then mixed with the juice of Kakodumbarika and Sukanasa and a decoction of Madana fruit and "duly cooked in the manner of Kshira-paka". No dose, measure or duration is printed for Kaka-jangha, or for the alkali. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Ash dissolved in water gives an alkaline solution, and alkaline preparations can burn skin and mucous membranes. Elephantiasis (lymphatic filariasis) is a parasitic disease of the lymph vessels that needs medical treatment. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Contraindications

- **Caustic preparation:** the one recipe is an alkali, and alkalis can burn; the Codex does not endorse it.
- **Elephantiasis and hernia:** medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Identity:** low confidence, from a positional pairing in a translator's appendix; no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry.
