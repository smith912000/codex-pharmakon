# Oakmoss — ushna (أشنة), the tree lichen

## *Tree lichen of the oakmoss type (probably* Evernia prunastri *and kin); the identification is the modern one, made from Avicenna's description ("fine thin scales wound around the oak, pine and walnut"), and is of moderate confidence, because the Arabic* ushna *was a general name for tree lichens and mosses* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

Oakmoss is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. (The spine's Parmelia entries, such as the rock lichen I_874, are another genus and a different name, and are not merged here.)

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The *Canon* (Book II, entry *ushna*, Arabic text line 10138) begins with what it is: "fine, delicate scales that wind round the oak, the pine and the walnut, and they have a good smell. Some people said that it is brought from the land of India" (قشور دقيقة لطيفة تلتف على شجرة البلوط والصنوبر والجوز ولها رائحة طيبة). For the choice he says: "the good kind is the white one, and the black is bad." He then quotes Dioscorides (line 10141): "the best of it is that on the *shurbīn*, which is the pine, and next the best is found on the walnut"; its best is that of best scent, and that which is white inclining to blue. The *Canon* then separates the kinds by host tree in its actions: the pine kind has a moderate astringency, and the oak kind "opens obstructions and braces slack flesh" (line 10147).

### Active Components

Lichens are a partnership of a fungus and an alga. Tree lichens such as oakmoss contain lichen acids (depsides and depsidones) and polysaccharides, and the fragrant extract of oakmoss is a long-established perfumery material. The *Canon* mentions the scent. It names no constituent, and none is asserted here from the text. [USER: verify the identification before attributing any named compound.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Avicenna uses it as an external and internal remedy:

- **As an applied drug.** Painted on hot swellings it calms them; it dissolves hard lumps and calms swellings of soft flesh (line 10148); it enters oils for fatigue and dissolves stiffness of the joints, whether the oil or its decoction (line 10149).
- **For the head and eye.** Steeped in wine it makes the drinker sleep (line 10150); it "clears the sight" (يجلو البصر, line 10151).
- **For the chest, stomach and liver.** Useful for palpitation; it stops vomiting, strengthens the stomach, removes its wind, "especially in an astringent wine", and helps pain from a weak liver (lines 10151-10153).
- **For the womb.** It opens womb obstructions; sitting in its water helps womb pain; it moves the menses (line 10153).

It is not offered here as a recommended modern practice. A modern reader should note that oakmoss is also a recognised contact allergen (below).

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives these forms only: a salve or paint on swellings (line 10148); an ingredient in oils against fatigue, and a decoction (line 10149); a soak in wine (line 10150); a sitting-bath in its water for the womb (line 10153); and a substitute: "its substitute is its weight of *qardamānā*" (الأبدال: بدله وزنه قردمانا, line 10154). ### Scientific Correlation

Lichens produce characteristic secondary compounds, among them usnic acid in *Usnea* and atranorin and evernic acid in *Evernia*. The extract of oakmoss is a known cause of allergic contact dermatitis, which is why it is regulated in perfumery. This is general chemistry and not a study. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **CM (Avicenna), line 10145.** "In slight coldness tending to tepidity (*fatūr*), with a moderate astringency; some claimed it is hot in the first, dry in the second." He gives both readings.
- **Actions (line 10146).** Astringent and dissolving together, and softening, "especially the pine kind, whose astringency is moderate."

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The entry is organised under swellings, joints, head, eye, chest, stomach and womb, with a substitute. It makes no statement about the vital faculty, the *ruh*, or the innate heat and radical moisture, and assigns no cultivation status. The one substance-specific physiological claim is local: strengthening the stomach and the slack flesh. That is a therapeutic claim.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing is asked of a cultivator here. In the *Canon* this is a gentle outer and inner drug for swellings and slack flesh. If anyone handles lichen today, the real practice is identification (host tree, colour, scent), since Avicenna's own quotation from Dioscorides ranks it by host tree and scent.

### Contraindications

- **Allergy.** Oakmoss extracts are well-known skin sensitisers; patch caution applies.
- **Identification.** Several lichens and mosses share the name; some lichens are toxic. Do not take unidentified lichen internally.
- **Wine soak.** Avicenna says the wine soak puts the drinker to sleep (line 10150); sedation is a hazard if driving or caring for others.
- **Womb use.** The text says it moves the menses (line 10153); avoid in pregnancy. This is a precaution drawn from the stated action. [USER: verify]
