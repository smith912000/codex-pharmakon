### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** No locus in the text was found for a krait. The attestation is one row of the project's list `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv`, line 107: "Banded Krait", *Bungarus multicinctus* Blyth, class animal, source reference "rGyud-bzhi" with no chapter, flagged id-uncertain; the spine's note is "rGyud-bzhi; id-uncertain". `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` has no snake row. A search of Clark 1995 (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`) and of the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`) for "krait" and "Bungarus" returned nothing. What the Tibetan text does print is a plain "snake", in the animal medicines of Explanatory Tantra ch.20. Meat: "Snake meat: Elapbe dione (Pallas): [has eleven names, for example kluhi.sha]. It removes growths, dispels urinary retention, is beneficial in eye disorders, [painful fevers in children, lung diseases, leprosy and infected wounds, and it heals bone warts]" (Clark lines 1165-1166). Fat: "Snake fat: expels bullets, (shrapnel, etc.) [and cures anterior transposition of the calf muscle.]" (line 1180). Skin: "Snake skin: cures leucoderma, glang.shu (a skin disease) [and is beneficial against bhutaspirits]" (line 1184). The Men-Tsee-Khang text gives the same three: snake meat "dissolves tumors, clears obstruction and is beneficial for eye problems" (line 3529), snake fat "expels foreign bodies from the body" (line 3546), snake skin "cures vitiligo and langshu" (line 3554). In Clark the snake's species is given as *Elaphe dione* (Pallas), which is a non-venomous colubrid and not a krait; the Men-Tsee-Khang text gives no species. Both texts also place the snake among the burrowing animals in the classification of meats (Clark line 672; Men-Tsee-Khang line 2918). None of these lines names a krait, and the list row cannot be tied to them. The attestation therefore rests on a list row. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Snake flesh, fat and skin are protein, lipid and keratin. The banded krait is a venomous snake whose venom is rich in neurotoxic proteins that act at the nerve-muscle junction. The sources read give no composition for any Tibetan snake medicine.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Tibetan lines read use snake meat, fat and skin for tumours, eye complaints, retained urine, foreign bodies in the flesh and skin disease, and none of them names a krait. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation. Handling or eating a venomous snake is dangerous and is not advised.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given in the sources retrieved, other than the division of the animal into meat, fat and skin (Clark lines 1148, 1165, 1180, 1184). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

*Bungarus multicinctus* is a highly venomous elapid of East and South-East Asia, and bites can cause paralysis and respiratory failure without prompt antivenom treatment. Snake meat can carry parasites and bacteria. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

The banded krait is dangerously venomous; handling the live animal, its venom or its raw tissues is hazardous, and a bite is a medical emergency. Snake products can carry bacteria and parasites. Wild snakes are protected or restricted in many places, and the legal and conservation status of *Bungarus multicinctus* has not been checked here.
