Searches of the other texts on disk (Dioscorides, Shennong, rGyud-bzhi, Ebers) were not made under a Sanskrit spelling, so absence elsewhere is not claimed.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Kapitana is printed in the Nya'grodha'di group, Sutrasthana chapter 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15687 (list from line 15685). The translator's sentence reads: "The drugs known as Nyagrodha, Audumvara, Ashvattha, Plaksha, Madhuka, Kapitana, Kakubha, Amra, Koshamra, Chorakapatra, the two sorts of Jamvu, Piyala, Madhuka (Maula), Rohini, Vanjula, Kadamva, Vadari, Tinduki, Sallaki, Rodhra, Savara-Rodhra, Bhallataka, Palasha, and Nandi-Vriksha, combinedly form the group known as the Nyagodhradi Gana." The metrical text (lines 15693 to 15702) reads: "This group proves beneficial in cases of ulcer, cures all disorders of the uterus and vagina, favours the adhesion of fractured bones and all sorts of secretions of the body in addition to its astringent properties (Sangrahi) and proves curative in a case of haemoptysis. It is an anti-fat and assuages the burning sensation of the body."

**Two further appearances.** (1) In the Kalpasthana, chapter 7, an antivenom preparation (*Ksharagada*) is made from "the woods of" a list of trees that includes "Kapitana" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch07.txt` line 10). (2) In the Uttaratantra, chapter 48, on thirst, "The six kinds of cordials (Panaka) separately prepared (in the preceding manner) with the (four kinds of) Rajadana, Kshiri or Kapitana are also beneficial in such cases" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch48.txt` line 194). The second passage treats Kapitana as one of several fruits made into a cordial, so the text points to a tree with a usable fruit and wood. It gives no species. [USER: verify]

### Preparation & Dosing

Two preparations are named in the text, neither with a dose: the wood in an antivenom alkali preparation (Kalpa ch.7, line 10), and a cordial (*Panaka*) in thirst (Uttara ch.48, line 194). The Kalpa passage's own measure, "equal" parts, is a rule for that recipe and is not a dose for Kapitana. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is named inside a therapeutic drug group (*gana*) of Sutrasthana chapter 38, the Nya'grodha'di group. The group's metrical text speaks of curing or subduing disorders. It does not call the group, or any member, a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) drug, and it does not mention *ojas*. The sources retrieved therefore attest a therapeutic framing only, and no cultivation status. The *sattva/rajas/tamas* lists of later popular literature are not from Sushruta and are not applied. Its two other appearances (an antivenom recipe and a thirst cordial) are also therapeutic.

**Practitioner's note.** A tree-name that appears in an astringent group, an antivenom wood list and a thirst cordial. Nothing in the record supports a cultivation use, and with the tree unidentified there is nothing to do with it. A cultivator does nothing with this entry.
