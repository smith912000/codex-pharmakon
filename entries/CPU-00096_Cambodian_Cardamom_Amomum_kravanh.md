### Origin & Tradition

Cambodian or round cardamom is the dried fruit of a ginger-family plant of Southeast Asia, the source of the "white" or "round" cardamom of the spice trade and of the Chinese materia medica drug **Bai Dou Kou** (白豆蔻). Distinct from green/true cardamom (*Elettaria*) and from black cardamom, it is a globular, pale, papery capsule full of pungent, camphoraceous seeds. It has been an article of the maritime spice trade between Indochina, China and the wider region for centuries, valued as both culinary aromatic and warming digestive medicine.

In Chinese Medicine Bai Dou Kou is a classic **aromatic dampness-transforming** herb: warm, acrid and fragrant, it "wakes the spleen," disperses cold-damp stagnation in the middle burner, moves stagnant qi, harmonises the stomach and stops vomiting and nausea arising from damp-cold or qi stagnation. It is the herb reached for when digestion is sluggish, the appetite poor, the tongue greasy-coated and the middle feels heavy, cold and blocked — restoring movement and warmth to a damp, stuck centre. Like other aromatic seeds it is added late to decoctions to preserve its volatile oil.

The whole therapeutic identity sits squarely in the carminative-aromatic register shared with green cardamom, the *Amomum* drugs (Sha Ren) and the warming digestive spices — a fragrant, warming mover of the damp, cold, stagnant gut.
