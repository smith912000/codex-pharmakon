# Artocarpus lakucha — Lakucha (Sushruta Samhita; "Artocarpus Lakucha" in Bhishagratna's Appendix)

## *Artocarpus lacucha Buch.-Ham. (the monkey jack or "barhal"), a medium tree of the mulberry family, grown for its acid fruit; the identification is Bhishagratna's, printed in his Appendix as "Artocarpus Lakucha" against the name "Lakucha" in a column where the neighbouring pairs (Lajjalu with Mimosa pudica, Laksha with the lac insect) are well known, and it is of high confidence at species level on that basis, although the Sushruta text itself describes no plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, under the Sanskrit name Lakucha.** This is a single-text entry. The row was added on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from the Appendix to Bhishagratna's Volume III; this entry replaces it. The attestation was confirmed on 30 October by opening the Appendix and reading every chapter where the name occurs.

**Sushruta Samhita, Appendix (SS).** In the Appendix to Volume III (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`) the names column of the "L" page reads "Lajjala", "Laksha", "Lakucha", "Lamajjaka", "Lengali", "Lasuna", "Lavanga" and so on (Lajjala at line 32126, Lakucha at line 32128), and the identifications column reads "Mimosa Pudica.", "Coccus Lacca. Lac.", "Artocarpus Lakucha.", "Andropogon Muricatus.", "Gloriosa Superba.", "Allium Sativum.", and "Abelmoschus Moschatus." (lines 32164-32173; "Artocarpus Lakucha." at line 32166). The first three names and the first three identifications line up one for one: the sensitive plant, lac, and the tree. Lasuna against *Allium sativum* (garlic) is also one of the pairs. The pairing is read from a damaged scan. [USER: verify against the printed page.]

**Sushruta Samhita, main text (SS).** The fruit and the bark are named in four chapters.

- *Sutrasthana* ch.46, on food and drink (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, line 990): in the fruit group, "Now we shall discourse (on the specific virtues) of fruits, such as the Dadima, Amalaka, Kapittha, Vadara, Kola, Karkandhu, Sauvira, Shimvitikaphala, Matulunga, Amra, Amrataka, Karamarda, Piyala, Lakucha, Bhavya, Paravata, Vetraphala, Prachinamalaka, Tintidika, Nipa, Koshamra, Amlika, Naranga, and Jamvira, etc." The general account that follows says that fruits "are acid in taste and heavy of digestion, heat-making in their potencies, (expectorant) of (Kapham), generate Pittam and subdue the Vayu", and then takes the fruits one at a time. Lakucha has no sentence of its own in the lines read; the general account applies to the list.
- *Sutrasthana* ch.42, on the specific properties of flavours (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch42.txt`, lines 310-314): "Acid Groups: The fruits known as Dadima, Amalaka, Matulanga, Amrutaka, Kapittha, Karamarda, Vadra, Kola, Prachina-Amalaka, Tintidhi, Koshamra, Bhavya, Paravata, Vetraphala, Lakucha, Amla-Vetash, Dantashatha and curd, whey, Sura, Shukta, Sauvira, Tushodaka and Dhanyamla, etc. are generally included within the acid group." Lakucha therefore stands in the acid (*amla*) group.
- *Sutrasthana* ch.20, on salutary and unsalutary regimen (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch20.txt`, line 170): "The fruit known as Lakucha should not be taken with milk, curd or meat soup, nor with honey and clarified-butter, nor immediately before or after the drinking of milk." It appears in the list of incompatible food combinations.
- *Sutrasthana* ch.16 (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch16.txt`, line 394), on diseases of the pierced ear-lobe: "A plaster composed of the drugs known as Apamarga, Sarjarasa, Patala bark and Lakucha bark pasted together, or a medicated oil prepared and boiled with the preceding substances should be applied in a case of the Utpataka type." This is the one place where the bark, not the fruit, is named.

A search of the other texts on disk (Dioscorides, Shennong and the others) found nothing for this tree.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The fruit is a food in the acid group, with the fruit group's general account (acid, heavy, heat-making, subduing Vayu, generating Pittam); it is not to be eaten with milk, curd, meat soup, honey or ghee. The bark is an ingredient of a plaster and oil for a disease of the ear-lobe.
- **What the text does not say.** It gives no dose, no description of the tree, and no tonic use.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant. The dietary incompatibility in ch.20 is recorded as a rule of that text, not as advice.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations named are a plaster of the bark pasted with Apamarga, Sarjarasa and Patala bark, or an oil boiled with them (ch.16, line 394), and the fruit eaten as food, with the incompatibilities above. No quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Artocarpus lacucha* is a tree of South and South-East Asia whose fruit is eaten, and it belongs to the same genus as the jackfruit and breadfruit. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** Acid in taste: Lakucha is named in the acid group (ch.42). Of the fruits of the ch.46 list the text says they are "acid in taste and heavy of digestion, heat-making in their potencies"; the sentence is for the fruits as a class and is quoted here for that reason. No rasa, virya or vipaka is given for Lakucha by itself. No organ or channel assignment is imported.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* Lakucha is a food fruit of the acid group, a dietary incompatibility and the bark in a topical plaster. It is not named in the tonic-remedy chapter read (Chikitsa ch.30), and no rasayana or vajikarana status is attested for it in the sources retrieved. No ojas, tejas or prana claim is made. What the text offers is dietetic: which foods may not be eaten together.

**Practitioner's note.** A sour fruit, with a rule that it be kept away from milk and milk products, honey and ghee. If a cultivator uses the text at all, the use is the incompatibility rule, which is a restraint and not a practice.

### Contraindications

- **Food combination.** Ch.20 forbids it with milk, curd, meat soup, honey and ghee and next to milk. This is the text's rule, recorded as such.
- **Acid fruit.** The text counts it among the heat-making acid fruits that generate Pittam; take it as a caution in the text's own terms.
- **Ear-lobe plaster.** A medical procedure. Do not apply the bark to the ear on this basis.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources read. [USER: verify]
