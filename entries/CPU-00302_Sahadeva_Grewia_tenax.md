### Origin & Tradition

*Grewia tenax* is a small shrub of arid and semi-arid regions spanning the Indian subcontinent's drier tracts, Arabia, the Sahel, and the Sahara's southern margins — one of the relatively few materia medica plants genuinely shared between Ayurvedic and African/Arabian folk pharmacopoeias. It bears small orange-to-brown, sweetish, mucilaginous fruits ("gangerun" / "godheli"), which are the most-used part, alongside the roots.

**Ayurvedic tradition:** The Sanskrit name *Sahadeva* / *Sahadevī* and *Gangeruki* are applied to Grewia species in classical and folk Ayurvedic usage. (Note: "Sahadevī" is also a well-known name for *Vernonia cinerea*, and vernacular overlap is common — see Contraindications on authentication.) In its Grewia application it is regarded as sweet-astringent, cooling, and demulcent — a *balya* (strengthening) and *grāhī* (astringent/binding) drug used for weakness/debility, respiratory complaints (cough, chest conditions), and disorders of the gut with excess motility. Its mucilage makes it *snigdha* (unctuous/soothing) to irritated mucosa.

**African / Arabian folk tradition (strongest documented ethnomedical use):** In Sudan, the Sahel, and parts of Arabia the fruit of *G. tenax* is a renowned **traditional remedy for anaemia** and childhood iron-deficiency, valued for its iron content and eaten as food-medicine; it is also used in liver complaints, digestive disorders, and general weakness. This anaemia/blood-building use is the plant's best-documented traditional application and dovetails with the Ayurvedic *balya* (strengthening) reading.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the Sweet-Mucilaginous Builder of the Drylands:** The dravyaguṇa logic centres on two paired qualities: *madhura* (sweet, hence *balya/bṛṃhaṇa* — building, blood-nourishing) and *kaṣāya* (astringent, hence *grāhī* — binding, drying excess secretions/motility), unified by a demulcent mucilage that makes it *snigdha* and *śīta* (soothing and cooling). The doctrine-of-signatures reading is apt for a drought-plant: a small, tenacious shrub that concentrates sweet, moisture-holding, iron-bearing fruit in an arid landscape becomes a builder of blood and strength — storing and giving back vitality where the environment is depleting. Its cross-tradition anaemia reputation is the physiological correlate: it *nourishes rakta* (blood tissue) while its astringency *checks loss*. The convergence of Ayurvedic *balya-grāhī* doctrine with independent Saharan-Sahelian anaemia use on the same fruit is itself the notable signature.

### Active Components

- **Mucilage / polysaccharides** — abundant; the demulcent, soothing (*snigdha*) fraction.
- **Iron and minerals** — the material basis of the traditional anti-anaemia use (fruit).
- **Phytosterols, sesquiterpenes, and terpenoids** — reported by GC-MS of fruit extract.
- **Fatty acid esters, vitamin E (tocopherol), carboxylic acids, pyranone and methyl-furfural** derivatives (fruit, per GC-MS).
- **Flavonoids and phenolics** — antioxidant fraction (roots/fruit).

Per PubMed (Rehman et al. 2022, GC-MS of fruit extract), major detected constituents included a long-chain alcohol (~21%) and 3-deoxy-D-mannoic lactone (~15%), with vitamin E, phytosterols, sesquiterpenes and related classes.

- **[BUILD]:** the anaemia/blood-building (*balya*, *raktavardhaka*-adjacent) tradition is the primary framing — a food-medicine for recovery from depletion, supporting blood and strength, particularly where a gentle, nourishing, mineral-bearing agent is wanted.

Its mucilage makes it best taken as a fruit-decoction, soak, or food rather than a concentrated extract.

### Scientific Correlation

Attribution: findings from PubMed.

- **Antidiarrhoeal & antispasmodic (in vivo + ex vivo, mechanism):** Rehman et al. (2022, *Molecules*) showed the methanolic fruit extract dose-dependently protected mice against castor-oil-induced diarrhoea (40% protection at 200 mg/kg, 80% at 400 mg/kg) and, in isolated rat ileum, inhibited both carbamylcholine- and high-K⁺-evoked spasms — consistent with combined **phosphodiesterase inhibition and calcium-channel-blocking** activity (papaverine-like). This provides a pharmacodynamic basis for the traditional use in diarrhoea and hyperactive gut. GC-MS characterised the phytoconstituents. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27248880)
- **Hepatoprotective & antioxidant (preclinical):** Al-Said et al. (2011, *Journal of Food Science*) found ethanolic *G. tenax* fruit extract (250 and 500 mg/kg, 3 weeks) significantly reduced CCl₄-induced elevations of liver enzymes and lipids, raised serum haemoglobin, increased hepatic non-protein sulfhydryls and reduced malondialdehyde, with supportive histopathology — attributing hepatoprotection to antioxidant constituents and validating folkloric use in anaemic and other conditions. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1750-3841.2011.02381.x)

**Evidence grade:** **Preclinical** (rodent in-vivo, isolated-tissue, and biochemical studies). The traditional anti-anaemia use is very widely documented ethnomedically and the hepatoprotective study noted raised haemoglobin, but no robust human clinical trial establishes the anaemia claim — treat the blood-building use as **traditional with preclinical support**; the antidiarrhoeal/antispasmodic mechanism is preclinically well-characterised.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** *Madhura* (sweet), *Kaṣāya* (astringent)
- **Guṇa (quality):** *Snigdha* (unctuous/mucilaginous), *Laghu* to *Guru* (light-to-somewhat-heavy)
- **Vīrya (potency):** *Śīta* (cooling)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** *Madhura* (sweet)
- **Prabhāva (specific effect):** Its notable anti-anaemia (blood-building) reputation, converging across Ayurvedic and Saharan-Sahelian traditions, may be read as its prabhāva.
- **Doṣa action:** *Vāta-śāmaka* and *Pitta-śāmaka* (primary — sweet, cooling, demulcent); mildly Kapha-increasing in excess owing to sweet/unctuous mucilage
- **Karma (actions):** *Balya* (strengthening), *raktavardhaka*-adjacent (blood-building / anti-anaemia — traditional), *grāhī* (astringent, antidiarrhoeal), *snigdha/ślaiṣmika* demulcent (soothes irritated mucosa), *kāsahara/śvāsahara*-adjacent (respiratory-soothing), *yakṛt-uttejaka*-counter (hepatoprotective, per data), *antispasmodic* (per data)

### Contraindications

- Generally regarded as safe (widely eaten as food). Being sweet, unctuous, and mucilaginous, use cautiously in marked Kapha excess, sluggish digestion, or heavy congestion.
- **Authentication is the principal caution:** the Sanskrit name *Sahadeva/Sahadevī* is shared with **other, unrelated plants** (notably *Vernonia cinerea*), and *Grewia* comprises many species used interchangeably in folk practice — confirm botanical identity (*Grewia tenax*) before therapeutic use.
- No robust human safety/interaction dataset; pregnancy/lactation data are traditional (fruit is eaten as food) rather than trial-based. `[USER: verify]`
- The antidiarrhoeal action reflects genuine gut-motility inhibition — avoid in constipation-predominant states or where reduced motility is undesirable.
