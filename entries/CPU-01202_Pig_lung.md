# Pig lung (also lamb, bear) — PNEUMON CHOIRIOS, "Lungs of Swine, Lamb or Bear" (Dioscorides, De Materia Medica 2-40)
## *The lungs of the domestic pig, or of the lamb or the bear, applied to the feet; a Dioscoridean animal remedy* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is the 2000 English edition of *De Materia Medica* in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is 2-40, headed "PNEUMON CHOIRIOS" with the translator's title "Lungs of Swine, Lamb or Bear" (line 5100; the sub-title is at line 5101). The full text of the chapter (line 5102) is: "The lungs of a swine, lamb or bear applied to chafing and blisters on the feet made by rubbing shoes prevents inflammation."

The three animals are named together in the one chapter, and the chapter gives the same use for all three; this is why the entry's title includes "also lamb, bear". The names.csv row (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` line 230) records the heading, the locus "2-40 (line 5100)", the class "animal" and the translator's gloss, with no binomial. The translator's English word for the pig is "swine". The chapter names no species of bear and no breed of lamb.

Neighbouring chapters give the lungs of other creatures. Chapter 2-39, PNEUMON THALASSIOS, is the sea lung (*pulmo marinus*): "pounded into small pieces (whilst it is fresh), and applied", for ulcerated chilblains and for gout (line 5096 and following). Chapter 2-41 is the lungs of the fox (line 5106): "Lungs of a fox (dried and taken in a drink) help the asthmatic; and the grease of the same melted and poured in the ears lessens earache." The three chapters show three modes: applied fresh to the skin (sea lung), applied (pig, lamb, bear), and dried and drunk (fox). I do not carry the "fresh" qualifier of the sea lung or the "dried" qualifier of the fox lung to the pig lung.

The chapter prescribes an external, topical use for foot chafing and blisters. It makes no claim about the lung as a treatment for the lungs or for any internal complaint.

### Active Components

Lung tissue is a spongy mix of protein, collagen and elastin, with surfactant lipids and blood. The text names no active compound. As a general fact, fresh animal tissue applied to broken skin can carry bacteria, and a moist organ will also act as a physical dressing.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides says that lungs of swine, lamb or bear, applied to chafing and blisters on the feet from shoe rubbing, "prevents inflammation". The Codex does not recommend placing raw animal tissue on a blister or broken skin, and gives no protocol. Modern foot care is simple: reduce friction, protect the area with a clean dressing, keep it clean, and seek care for signs of infection. The line is retained for the record only.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives one preparation: the lungs are "applied". It does not say whether raw, fresh, warm or cooked, whether bound on, how long, or how often, and no quantity. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Greek: the chapter prints an action only ("prevents inflammation"), with no quality such as warming, cooling or drying. None is printed for these lungs in the chapter. No other tradition is tagged.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is supplied here. What the chapter shows is a discipline of attention: three animals are named for one use, which records a substitution (swine, lamb or bear) instead of insisting on one source, and the use is specific (chafing and blisters from shoes). It records no amulet and no ritual function. The text does record such uses elsewhere (for example, chapter 2-60, swallow stones worn "to the arm or neck" for epilepsy, lines 5173-5179), and none is recorded here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. A cultivator does not apply animal lung to the feet. The text records it against chafing and blisters, and the Codex keeps the record without carrying it into practice.

### Contraindications

Raw animal organ on broken skin invites infection, including wound infection and, in the case of bear, *Trichinella* and other parasites in the meat; bear products are also legally restricted in many places (many bear species are protected). Do not apply raw animal tissue to blisters or open wounds; clean the area, protect it, and seek care for spreading redness, pus or fever. Pig products are prohibited in some religious traditions; respect them. This entry gives no medical advice.
