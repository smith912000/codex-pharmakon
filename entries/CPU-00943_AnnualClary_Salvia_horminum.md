# Annual Clary / Bluebeard Sage

## *Salvia viridis* L. (syn. *Salvia horminum* L.) | Family: Lamiaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, DMM
**Energetic Classification:** Warm / Dry (attributed; aromatic Lamiaceae)
**Training Phase:** Foundation — Signature Study

---

### Origin & Tradition

Annual clary is a modest Mediterranean annual sage, grown far more today for ornament than for medicine: its true flowers are small, but the topmost leaf-like **bracts** flush vivid violet, pink or white, giving the plant its garden names — bluebeard sage, painted sage, annual clary. Botanically it is *Salvia viridis*, with *Salvia horminum* as the classical-sounding synonym under which most of its herbal history is filed. That synonym matters, because the whole tradition around this plant is entangled with the name it shares roots with.

**Dioscorides / Greek herbal stream (DMM).** The classical name *hormínon* / *horminum* appears in the Greek and Roman herbal record attached to a cultivated sage of this type. Its recorded uses are the generic ones of the aromatic sages — a warming, drying herb for the digestion and for "drawing" — and, most distinctively, a **seed used to cleanse the eye**: the mucilaginous seed was moistened and placed under the eyelid so that, swelling to a gel, it gathered up grit and dust and could be drawn out. This is the shared "clary/clear-eye" signature also carried by true clary sage (*Salvia sclarea*), and the two plants' reputations bleed into one another throughout the herbal literature.

**Avicenna / Unani (CM).** The same Mediterranean-into-Arabic transmission carries *horminum*-type sage into the Unani materia medica as a warm, aromatic digestive and a seed-mucilage eye remedy. **[USER: verify]** — because *S. viridis* and *S. sclarea* were routinely conflated under the *horminum* label, both the DMM and CM attestations should be read as **applied to the classical "horminum" concept, not securely to *S. viridis* as the modern binomial defines it.** The scholarship of the *use* is sound; the species pinning is loose, and Kenetica flags it rather than pretending to a precision the sources do not have.

**Doctrinal & symbolic layer.** The eye-cleansing seed is a near-perfect **mechanical signature**: a plant that literally *gathers and draws out* what does not belong. Read symbolically it became a herb of clarity and discernment — but the honest reading is that its eye action is *physical*, the swelling of mucilage, not a pharmacological cure. In Kenetica it teaches the difference between a remedy that *does something mechanical* and one that *treats a disease*.

### Active Components

Species-specific chemistry for *S. viridis* has only recently been characterised, and it is the ordinary phenolic wealth of an aromatic sage:

- **Phenolic acids and phenylethanoids.** UPLC-DAD/ESI-MS of *S. viridis* shoots identified 19 phenolics, with **rosmarinic acid** the predominant phenolic acid and **verbascoside (acteoside)** the predominant phenylethanoid, alongside apigenin/luteolin glucuronides (Grzegorczyk-Karolak & Kiss, 2018). A Jordanian study — the first phytochemical evaluation of *S. viridis* from that region — likewise found high total flavonoid content and high rosmarinic acid, with luteolin-7-*O*-glucoside abundant (Al-Jaber et al., 2020).
- **Anthocyanins (bracts).** The coloured bracts carry the anthocyanin pigments responsible for the ornamental display; these track antioxidant capacity as in other pigmented Lamiaceae.
- **Essential oil (genus-level, congener).** In a comparative study of six Turkish *Salvia* species, the dominant volatiles were **1,8-cineole, caryophyllene oxide, spathulenol and borneol** in varying ratios, with species-dependent antimicrobial activity (Demirpolat, 2023). This is **genus-level** data spanning several sages; it characterises the aromatic profile of the group rather than proving a specific *S. viridis* oil composition, and is labelled as congener/genus information.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Annual clary is a **Foundation-phase signature and antioxidant study**, not a proven therapeutic agent.

1. **The eye-cleansing seed (mechanical, not curative).** Kenetica records the historic seed-mucilage use but reframes it honestly: the action is the **physical swelling of mucilage** trapping foreign material, generic to mucilaginous seeds. It is **not** an antimicrobial or anti-inflammatory eye treatment, and applying unsterile plant material to the eye risks corneal abrasion and infection. The gesture is preserved as history, not endorsed as practice.
2. **Aromatic digestive / antioxidant profile.** As a rosmarinic-acid- and verbascoside-rich sage, *S. viridis* shares the antioxidant character of the group; this is real at the phytochemical/in-vitro level (Grzegorczyk-Karolak & Kiss, 2018; Al-Jaber et al., 2020) but has **no clinical evidence base of its own**.
3. **A study in name-conflation.** Much of what is claimed for "clary" belongs to *S. sclarea* (clary sage, a distinct plant with its own essential-oil profile) or to common sage *S. officinalis*. Kenetica uses this entry to teach the practitioner to **separate the species behind a shared common name** before trusting a claim.

Deflation stated plainly: **species-specific clinical evidence for *S. viridis* is essentially absent.** Its credible data are phytochemical (antioxidant constituents) and its most reliable pharmacology is genus-level. Anyone presenting annual clary as a treatment for a named disease is borrowing another sage's reputation.

### Scientific Correlation

Graded honestly, nulls included:

- **Eye-cleansing / ophthalmic cure:** **NULL as a cure.** The action is mechanical mucilage-swelling, not a treated pathology; no study supports an antimicrobial or therapeutic eye effect.
- **Antioxidant activity (species-specific):** **Preclinical, moderate–good.** High total phenolic/flavonoid content and antioxidant capacity, driven by rosmarinic acid and verbascoside (Grzegorczyk-Karolak & Kiss, 2018; Al-Jaber et al., 2020).
- **Antimicrobial (essential oil):** **Preclinical, genus-level.** Species-dependent antimicrobial activity across *Salvia* oils rich in 1,8-cineole/caryophyllene oxide (Demirpolat, 2023) — congener data, not a species-specific claim.
- **Anxiolytic / CNS effects:** **Genus-level only.** Acute anxiolytic activity has been reported for *Salvia* spp. in a clinical-evidence review (Sarris et al., 2013); this is **not** demonstrated for *S. viridis* specifically.
- **Species-specific clinical data for *S. viridis*:** **NULL.**

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Warm and dry, aromatic — the standard humoral reading for a cultivated sage of the *horminum* type, entering the digestion and "clearing." The classification is inherited from the classical *horminum* concept and from the plant's aromatic character; treat as coherent-but-inferred, and note the species-conflation caveat above.

### Contraindications

- **Do not apply seeds or plant material to the eye.** The historic eye-cleansing use risks corneal abrasion and microbial keratitis; genuine eye complaints need sterile irrigation and clinical care.
- **Essential-oil sensitivity / contact reactions** possible, as with aromatic Lamiaceae.
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** avoid medicinal quantities on the precautionary principle common to aromatic sages (some *Salvia* essential oils contain thujone; note this is a **congener** caution and thujone content in *S. viridis* is not established here).
- **Species confusion:** do not substitute for, or assume the properties of, *S. sclarea* (clary sage) or *S. officinalis* (common sage) — distinct plants with distinct oils.
- **No safety data** for concentrated extracts; culinary/ornamental exposure is the only well-tolerated use.
