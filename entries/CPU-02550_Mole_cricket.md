# Mole cricket — 蝼蛄 *Lougu* (Shennong bencao jing), also 蟪蛄 (as printed) and 天蝼 *tianlou*
## *The mole cricket, Gryllotalpa orientalis Burmeister (Gryllotalpidae), the burrowing insect; our identification, of high confidence, supported by the many early glosses in Sun Xingyan's note that equate the name with the mole cricket* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The identification is ours; the early commentaries agree that 蝼蛄 is the same insect as 天蝼 and similar names, though the text gives no description of the animal's body.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the lower (下) classic, reads 味咸，寒 ("salty, cold"). It governs 产难 ("difficult childbirth"), "draws out thorns in the flesh" (出肉中刺; Yulan: 刺在肉中), "bursts abscesses and swellings" (溃痈肿), "brings down choking" (下哽噎; Yulan 咽), "resolves poison" (解毒) and "removes malign sores" (除恶创). It gives two aliases, 一名蟪蛄 (the Yulan variant is printed the same in the transcription) and 一名天蝼. There is no habitat, no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 蝼蛄 *lougu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3961)

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 adds the origin 生东城 and the harvest: 夏至取，曝干, "taken at the summer solstice, sun-dried".

**Sun's note (案).** Sun collects the names. Shuowen has three entries (the transcription loses the first character) that equate the names with 蝼蛄; the Guangya gives 炙鼠, 津姑, 蝼蜮, 蟓蛉 and 蛞蝼; the Xia xiaozheng has "in the third month, [the insect] sings; 天蝼"; the Erya and Guo Pu give 天蝼 = 蝼蛄; the Huainanzi, with Gao You, has "in the first month of summer the 蝼 sings; the 蝼 is the mole cricket" (an exact reading of the line is uncertain in the transcription); the Fangyan lists regional names (杜格, 杜狗, 蟓蛉 and others); and Lu Ji's Shi shu says that the Bencao also calls the mole cricket 石鼠, "but no text now" has it (今无文). The names fit one burrowing insect.

**Identity.** High; the text's own commentaries say the same. The species name *orientalis* is the modern one for the common Asian mole cricket (another species, *G. unispina*, is also used). [USER: verify] No overlap with an existing entry: none for *Gryllotalpa* or "mole cricket" was found.

### Active Components

The text prints no constituent. In general terms an insect body is protein, fat and chitin; a soil insect may carry soil bacteria and parasites. Nothing more specific is stated without a source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records a salty, cold insect-drug for difficult childbirth, for splinters in the flesh, for abscesses, for choking and for malign sores. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends none. Obstructed labour, choking and abscesses are medical emergencies.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives only the harvest (at the summer solstice, sun-dried; Mingyi layer). It gives no preparation, route, quantity or course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

In general knowledge the mole cricket is a burrowing, soil-dwelling insect that is an agricultural pest. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 蝼蛄 in the lower grade (下品); its preface says lower-grade drugs treat disease, are mostly toxic, and are not for long use. The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise; it lists childbirth, splinters, abscesses, choking and sores. The Mingyi bielu, not the Shennong text, gives the origin and the solstice harvest. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 蝼蛄 *lougu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3961)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A lower-grade insect-drug for a short list of acute complaints; the lower grade is by the classic's own preface not for long use. SHORT entry: the sources give little.

### Contraindications

- **Obstructed labour, choking, abscess.** Emergencies; need medical care.
- **Hygiene.** A soil insect carries bacteria and parasites; do not eat or apply it to wounds.
- **Ethics.** The Codex does not recommend harvesting animals for these uses.
