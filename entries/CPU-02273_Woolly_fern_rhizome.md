# Woolly fern rhizome — Gou Ji 狗脊 (Shennong bencao jing); "dog's spine", with the aliases Gou Qing 狗青 and Chi Jie 赤节 (Wu Pu)

## *Cibotium barometz (L.) J.Sm., the golden-haired woolly tree fern, whose hairy rhizome is the modern Chinese drug gou ji; the modern identification is of high confidence for the later drug, but is doubtful for the classic itself, because the Wu Pu layer describes a thorny plant with round red leaves, which does not fit a tree fern (see Origin)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; the other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), is read as a second witness and attributed to Yang. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder under a garbled pharmaceutical-Latin name, "Rhizoma Cibotii Barometsis [SBJ]", taken from Yang's English; this entry replaces it. A search of the other texts on disk for *Cibotium* and for the Chinese and pinyin names found nothing for this drug (the only hit is the index word *cibotium* in Dioscorides, which was not read as this drug).

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 狗脊 in the middle classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 狗脊 *gou ji*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2057, 味 paragraph; name line 2055). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，平 ("bitter, balanced"); 主腰背 ("governs the lower back and back"), 强关机 ("strengthens the joint-pivots"), 缓急 ("slackens what is tense"), 周痹寒湿 ("all-round impediment, cold and damp"), 膝痛 ("knee pain"); and 颇利老人 ("benefits old people considerably"). There is no 久服 clause. In Sun's e-text the paragraph then breaks off at "一" ("one name...") and runs straight into the Wu Pu layer, so any alias or habitat belonging to the Shennong paragraph is lost in this file.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, same line 2057) gives the aliases 狗青 and 赤节, and the flavours "Shennong, Tongjun, Huangdi, Qibo, Lei Gong, Bian Que: sweet, non-toxic; Mr Li: slightly warm". It describes the plant: 如萆[薢]，茎节如竹，有刺，叶圆赤，根黄白，亦如竹根，毛有刺 ("like *bei xie* [one glyph lost]; stem joints like bamboo, thorned; leaves round and red; root yellow-white, also like a bamboo root, hairy and thorned"). It then cites a "*Qibo jing*": 茎 ("the stem...") and breaks off. The Mingyi bielu layer (same line) adds the aliases 强膂, 扶盖 and 扶筋, the habitat 生常山 ("grows at Changshan"), and the harvest 二月、八月采根，曝干 ("root gathered in the second and eighth months and dried in the sun").

Sun's note (案, line 2059) reads: "The *Guangya* says: [one glyph lost] is *gou ji*. The *Yupian* says: [one glyph lost] is the root of *gou ji*. The Mingyi separately lists a [lost-glyph] entry, which is wrong (非)." The lost glyph is in both places the same word, and it is not legible in this e-text; the usual reading in the literature is 菝葜 (the rhizome of *Smilax*), but the file does not show it. [USER: verify]

**Identity doubt.** Our identification, *Cibotium barometz*, is the modern Chinese drug of this name. The Wu Pu description does not fit it: a thorned plant with round red leaves, compared with *bei xie* (a *Dioscorea* climber, see I_1516) and with a bamboo root, reads like a thorny vine, and the *Guangya* and *Yupian* quoted by Sun appear to equate the drug with a *Smilax*-type plant (compare I_645, China root, *Smilax china*, which does not cite the Shennong text). The alias 赤节 is also given for *bei xie* by the Mingyi layer of I_1516, which is a further sign that the early names ran together. The Codex therefore records the classic's text and does not claim the early drug was the tree fern. The entry stands for the Chinese drug *gou ji*. [USER: verify]

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 2777): "Gou Ji (Rhizoma Cibotii Barometsis) is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats rigidity of the upper and lower back, slack and tense joints, generalized impediment, and cold damp knee pain. It benefits old people very much. Its other name is Bai Zhi (Hundred Branches). It grows in rivers and valleys." This agrees with Sun's paragraph, and gives an alias, 百枝, and a habitat that Sun's text has lost or does not have. Yang's footnote 162 (line 2793) is modern commentary: "This medicinal supplements the liver and kidneys. Once these viscera are made strong, the sinews and bones will also become strong." That is not in the classic.

**Appendix.** The drug-relations table (line 4387) reads 狗脊 萆[薢]为使；恶败酱 ("*gou ji*: *bei xie* is its envoy; it dislikes *bai jiang*"). *Bei xie* is I_1516 and *bai jiang* is I_1513. The preface (line 4117) says to use pairs that "need" or "serve" one another and not those that "dislike" (恶) or oppose one another; the Codex draws no further conclusion.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the rhizome of *Cibotium barometz* is covered in dense golden-brown hairs, and the dried plant is rich in starch and fibre; the hair has been used as a dressing on wounds in Chinese practice. [USER: verify] Which plant the classic meant is not settled (see Identity doubt), so no statement about its constituents is made.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, balanced drug for the back and joints, for slackening what is tense, for all-round cold-damp impediment and knee pain, and as of considerable benefit to old people. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses or into organ language. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose. The "benefits old people" clause is not a longevity claim and carries no 久服 promise.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest: the root, in the second and eighth months, dried in the sun (line 2057). The preface (line 4121) says that drugs differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Cibotium barometz* is a tree fern of south-east Asia and southern China. Its rhizome is a traditional Chinese drug, and the species is regulated in international trade as a wild plant at risk. [USER: verify] Whether the rhizome relieves any of the conditions named in the classic has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Sun 1799).** Flavour and nature 味苦，平, "bitter, balanced" (line 2057). Wu Pu records a disagreement: Shennong and five other authorities "sweet, non-toxic"; Mr Li "slightly warm" (line 2057), which the Codex does not settle.
- **SBJ (Yang 1998).** "Bitter and balanced" (line 2777).
- No organ or channel assignment is given in the classic, and none is imported here.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle classic, whose preface (line 1677) reads 中药一百二十种为臣，主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经: the middle drugs "nourish the nature, correspond to man; non-toxic or toxic, weigh what is fitting; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the lean". The paragraph has no 久服 clause, and no promise of lightening the body or of not ageing. Its closest word to such a claim, 颇利老人 ("benefits old people considerably"), is a benefit to the aged and not a status for a person who takes it long. The Wu Pu layer calls it non-toxic in six authorities' flavours; this is not a grade. The register is therefore (b): framed by the middle class, with the cultivation status unattested.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-class drug for the back, joints and knees, with a clause that it helps old people and nothing that promises longer life. No part, preparation, dose or course is given, and the plant behind the early name is itself uncertain. A cultivator has no practice to take from this entry.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The early plant is uncertain and the modern drug is the tree-fern rhizome; do not treat any hairy or thorny rhizome as this drug. [USER: verify]
- **Conservation.** The tree fern is a wild plant at risk; do not gather it from the wild. [USER: verify]
- **Joint and back pain.** The uses named are historical record only; persistent pain needs medical care.
- **Pregnancy, nursing, children.** No safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Quantities.** No dose or duration is given.
