# Camelina — Gold of Pleasure / False Flax / Wild Flax / German Sesame

## *Camelina sativa* (L.) Crantz | Family: Brassicaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, DMM, CM
**Energetic Classification:** Temperate — moist and warming; a nourishing, emollient fixed oil (Galenic: warm and moist in the first degree)
**Training Phase:** Foundation (nutritive base-building)

---

### Origin & Tradition

Camelina is one of the oldest cultivated oilseeds of the temperate Old World, an archaeobotanical companion of early European and Near Eastern agriculture whose charred seeds appear in Bronze and Iron Age deposits across the continent. It belongs to the mustard family (Brassicaceae) and, unlike the great medicinal aromatics, its tradition is primarily that of a **food and lamp oil** — a staple fat rather than a drug — which is exactly why its documentary record sits with the practical, agricultural and dietetic strands of the ancient corpora.

**Assyrian (AT):** Mesopotamian agriculture cultivated a range of oil-bearing seeds for food, lamp fuel and ointment bases; camelina was among the hardy temperate oilseeds available to the northern reaches of the cuneiform world, valued for a fixed oil that stored and burned well.

**Dioscorides (DMM):** *De Materia Medica* discusses the cultivated oilseeds and flax-relatives of the Greek agricultural world, within which "gold of pleasure" (the plant later Latinised in the *Camelina* lineage) functioned as an emollient, nourishing seed oil — the dietetic and softening register rather than the heroic-remedy register.

**Avicenna / Unani (CM):** The *Qanun*'s treatment of seed oils places nourishing, temperate fixed oils among the foods-as-medicine that build substance, soften dryness and support a depleted constitution. Camelina fits the class of mild, moistening, nutritive oils used to restore rather than to purge or heat.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Camelina's folk name, *Gold of Pleasure*, ties its golden seed and golden oil to the humoral idea of a **temperate, life-nourishing fat** — the kind of substance that builds and sustains rather than provokes. The modern pharmacology honours the symbolism precisely: camelina oil is exceptionally rich in α-linolenic acid (ALA), the plant omega-3 fatty acid that is structural, membrane-building and anti-inflammatory in tendency. Where the great aromatics *move and open*, camelina *feeds and softens* — a Foundation-phase substance in both the traditional and Kenetica sense.

### Active Components

- **α-Linolenic acid (ALA, 18:3 n-3)** — the defining constituent; camelina oil is one of the richest plant sources, typically ~35–45% of its fatty acids.
- **Linoleic acid (18:2 n-6)** and **oleic acid (18:1 n-9)** — the remaining major fatty acids, giving a favourable polyunsaturated profile.
- **Gondoic/eicosenoic acid (20:1)** — a characteristic long-chain monounsaturated marker of camelina.
- **Tocopherols (vitamin E) and other natural antioxidants** — notably high, conferring unusual oxidative stability for a polyunsaturated oil.
- **Phytosterols and carotenoids** in minor amounts.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Camelina is a **Foundation-phase nutritive oil** in Kenetica — used not as an active intervention but as a dietary base that supplies structural omega-3 fatty acids, supports membrane and nervous-system substrate, and provides a stable, antioxidant-protected fat for daily nourishment. Its role is to *build the ground* on which more active protocols operate: correcting the omega-6/omega-3 imbalance of a modern diet, softening internal dryness, and supplying tocopherols. The oil's natural oxidative stability makes it more practical for regular culinary use than flaxseed oil, which it resembles in fatty-acid profile but exceeds in shelf-life.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed: Karvonen et al. (2002), *Metabolism*, conducted a double-blind randomized trial in 68 hypercholesterolemic adults comparing camelina, rapeseed and olive oils (≈30 g/day for 6 weeks). Camelina oil raised the proportion of serum α-linolenic acid roughly 2.5-fold versus rapeseed and 4-fold versus olive oil, increased its metabolites (EPA and docosapentaenoic acid), and lowered LDL cholesterol by 12.2% — a cholesterol-lowering effect comparable to rapeseed and olive oils. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1053/meta.2002.35183

De Giuseppe et al. (2023), *The Journal of Nutrition, Health & Aging*, ran a randomized placebo-controlled pilot trial in 66 older adults (≥65 y) consuming camelina-oil-enriched crackers for 12 weeks and found a significant rise in serum α-linolenic acid versus placebo; changes in inflammatory markers and lipid profile did not reach statistical significance in this pilot. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s12603-023-1925-x

**Evidence grade:** **Moderate** for the core claim — human randomized trials consistently confirm that camelina oil raises serum ALA and its omega-3 metabolites, with a modest LDL-lowering signal in a hypercholesterolemic population. Broader clinical outcome data (cardiovascular events, inflammation) remain limited and preliminary.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Not a classical Chinese materia medica item; by analogy to nourishing seed oils it would be classed as sweet, neutral-to-warm, tonifying to Yin/Blood and moistening to Dryness.
- **Ayurveda:** Not a classical dravya; as a temperate nutritive oil it would read as *madhura* (sweet) *rasa*, *snigdha* (unctuous) *guna*, mildly heating *virya*, sweet *vipaka* — Vata-pacifying, mildly Kapha-increasing.
- **Galenic/Unani:** warm and moist (first degree); nourishing, emollient, temperate fixed oil in the class of foods-as-medicine.

### Cultivation Register

**Assyrian (AT).** Mesopotamian agriculture pressed a range of oil-bearing seeds for food, lamp fuel and ointment bases, and a hardy temperate oilseed of camelina's kind would have sat among them at the northern edge of the cuneiform world. But this is a generic agricultural placement, not a species-specific identification, and the tablets classify what they treat by causal agency — the hand of a god, a ghost, an oath — rather than by any status a fat holds in a person's vital constitution. Under the standing blocking warning for Assyrian identifications, and with the attestation here resting on oilseed-agriculture in general rather than *Camelina sativa* in particular, no cultivation status can be claimed. **(c) absent.**

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* treats the cultivated oilseeds of the Greek agricultural world as emollient, nourishing, dietetic fats rather than heroic remedies, and camelina belongs to that softening register. Two cautions hold the line here. First, the identification of a specific Dioscoridean seed with *Camelina sativa* is a modern botanical hypothesis, not a fixed ancient name, so any attribution is provisional. Second, Dioscorides carries no subtle-body doctrine into which a nutritive oil could be ranked; inventing one would be a fabrication. What the tradition genuinely offers a cultivator is its discipline of attention — knowing a sound, sweet, unrancid oil from a spoiled one, judging the pressing and the keeping of it. That is a real practice of assay, not a vital-body cultivation status. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** The *Qanun* places nourishing, temperate fixed oils among the foods-as-medicine that build substance, soften dryness and restore a depleted constitution, and camelina reads as one of these mild, moistening, nutritive oils. This touches the strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus — the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture, the finite vital endowment whose depletion is ageing — since a moistening restorative fat works with, rather than against, that endowment. But the corpus assigns the frame to the class of temperate nutritive oils, not a specific cultivation status to this seed, and no *mufarrih* or cardiac-*ruh* role is claimed for camelina in the retrieved sources; per the corpus discipline no *Canon* chapter is cited. Its standing is dietetic and restorative. **(b) framed:** attested within the nutritive-oil class, no substance-specific cultivation status sourced.

**Practitioner's note.** Camelina is a foundation fat, and what a cultivator does with it is plain: eat it. It is not a substance that moves, opens or purges, and it is not a heroic remedy to be dosed in courses — it is a temperate, moistening, structural oil taken raw and daily as dietary ground, softening internal dryness and supplying omega-3 substrate on which more active work can rest. The one traditional discipline that transfers is the assay: keep it cool and dark, use it unheated, and judge it by smell and taste, because an ALA-rich oil gone rancid feeds nothing. No source makes it a longevity drug; its whole value is as nourishment.

### Contraindications

- Generally **well tolerated as a food**; camelina oil has a long dietary history and no significant toxicity in the cited human trials.
- **Brassicaceae/mustard-family allergy** — those with known cruciferous or mustard sensitivities should exercise caution.
- **High ALA intake and anticoagulation** — omega-3 fatty acids can have mild antiplatelet effects; those on anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy should use large therapeutic doses under supervision. [USER: verify individual context]
- Not intended as a replacement for prescribed lipid-lowering therapy.
- Camelina used as animal feed/oilseed can contain glucosinolates in the meal (not the refined oil); this is a feed consideration, not a culinary-oil one.
