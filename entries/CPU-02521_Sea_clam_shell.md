# Sea clam shell — 海蛤 *haige*, also 魁蛤

## *The shell of a marine bivalve; Cyclina sinensis is the modern identification, which is ours and of medium confidence. Other marine bivalves (Meretrix, Mactra) are disputed, and the text read gives no species* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on one text in one edition, Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). A search of the other texts on disk for *Cyclina* and *Meretrix* found nothing. The name 海蛤 means "sea clam" (蛤 *ge*, clam), and the text's own word for the material is a clam shell only by the Codex's reading; the Chinese paragraph does not say "shell".

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The name line is 海蛤 (line 1505). The classic's own paragraph is at line 1507: 味苦，平。主咳逆上气，喘息烦满，胸痛寒热。一名魁蛤。 In English: "Flavour bitter, level. Governs cough with counterflow qi rising (咳逆上气), wheezing breath with vexation and fullness (喘息烦满), chest pain, cold and heat (胸痛寒热). Also called 魁蛤." The paragraph has no 久服 clause, no habitat clause and no warning. The classic places the entry in the upper class (上经; heading 卷一 上经 at line 1513), the class the preface (line 43) describes as nourishing life, correspondent to heaven and non-toxic.

**Wu Pu (《吴普》, third century) layer.** Line 1509: "海蛤. Shennong: sweet; Qi Bo: sweet; Bian Que: salty. The 'big joint head' has markings, the markings like grinding teeth (大节头有文，文如磨齿). Gathered at no fixed time (采无时)." Wu Pu thus gives a flavour for Shennong (sweet) that differs from the classic's paragraph as printed in this edition (bitter), and the Codex records the difference without resolving it. The same line continues, in the transcription, with the Mingyi layer: "grows in the South Sea" (生南海). The rendering of 大节头 is tentative: I read "the large end or hinge". [USER: verify]

**Sun Xingyan's note (案).** Line 1511. The *Shuowen* says 蛤 is "of the 蜃 kind"; the *Shuowen* also says that "海蛤 is what a hundred-year-old swallow transforms into; 魁蛤, also called 复累, is what an old 服翼 (a bat) transforms into" (海蛤者，百岁燕所化；魁蛤，一名复累，老服翼所化). The Codex records this as the *Shuowen*'s, quoted by Sun, a folk belief of the period about origin, and not as a statement of the classic. The *Erya* has 魁陆, and Guo Pu says that "the *Bencao* says: the 魁 is shaped like the 海蛤, round and thick, with grain running lengthwise and crosswise, which is today's 蚶" (魁，状如海蛤，圆而浓朴，有理纵横，即今之蚶也). The *Zhou li* and the *Zhou shu* "Wanghui" are cited for 蛤 and "East Yue 海蛤", and Kong Huang glosses 蛤 there as 文蛤. Sun ends with his own judgement: "The Mingyi separately issues a 海蛤 entry saying it is also called 魁陆 and 活东. That is wrong" (按：《名医》别出海蛤条，云一名魁陆，一名活东，非). Sun thus keeps the alias 魁蛤 of the classic's paragraph and rejects the aliases 魁陆 and 活东 that the Mingyi attaches to a separate 海蛤 entry. The next entry in the classic is 文蛤 (line 1515): "governs evil sores, and eating-away (of) the five haemorrhoids"; its Mingyi layer says "grows in the East Sea, with patterns on the surface". It is a separate monograph and is not merged here.

### Active Components

None is stated in the text. As a general fact, the shell of a marine bivalve is made mainly of calcium carbonate with a small organic matrix. That statement is about shells in general. The Codex retrieved nothing specific to this species or to the material as the classic meant it, and makes no further claim. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The classic records a bitter, level material for cough, wheezing, chest pain and cold and heat, and gives no preparation. The Codex gives no protocol and does not turn "counterflow qi" into a modern diagnosis. Cough, wheezing and chest pain need medical assessment. The material is a marine shell, and any modern collection raises conservation and hygiene issues that the text does not address.

### Preparation & Dosing

The classic gives no preparation and no part. Wu Pu says it is gathered at no fixed time. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

The identification is ours. Sun's note itself shows that the old texts used 海蛤, 魁蛤 (later glossed as 蚶, a different bivalve), 文蛤 and 蛤 with overlapping meaning, so the species of the classic cannot be fixed from the lines read. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Divine Farmer's Classic).** 味苦，平, "bitter, level" (line 1507). Wu Pu gives sweet (Shennong), sweet (Qi Bo), and salty (Bian Que) (line 1509). No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry is placed in the upper class (上经), the class the preface (line 43) describes as nourishing life, corresponding to heaven, non-toxic and fit to be taken long (主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人). On the grading rule used for this series, placement in the upper classic is itself the ground for (a). The 味 paragraph itself, however, has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing or lightening promise, only a list of chest and breath complaints (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 海蛤 *haige*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1507). The attestation is thus of the upper-class placement and not of a promise made by the paragraph; the Codex does not read a cultivation claim into the clam shell itself. The Wu Pu, Mingyi and 案 layers add none and never count toward the grade.

**Practitioner's note.** The classic offers a cultivator nothing to do with this shell beyond reading its place. It is a bitter, level remedy for cough and chest complaints, in the class the preface calls life-nourishing, with no promise attached to it and no dose.

### Contraindications

The text gives no warning. No safety information for pregnancy, children or other medicines was read. The material is a marine shell and may carry contaminants; the origin of any modern material should be known. The species is not fixed. No conservation information was read. [USER: verify]
