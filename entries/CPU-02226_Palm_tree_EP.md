**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The list. Bryan's Chapter VI, headed "PLANT REMEDIES" (line 1232), is an alphabetical list of the plant materials used in the remedies. At line 1316, after the Onion entries, the list reads: "Palm-tree Palm-juice Palm-fibres Fruit-of-the-Male-Palm Fruit-of-the-Dompalm", and the next line is "Papyrus-plant". The names.csv row for this entry lists the forms Palm-tree, Palm-juice, Palm-fibres and Fruit-of-the-Male-Palm; "Fruit-of-the-Dompalm" has its own row and was merged into the doum-palm entry (CPU-00423). The date palm has its own list line (1275, "Dates", with "Dates-of-the-Male-Palm" among its forms) and its own entry (CPU-00418). This entry is for the palm-tree, palm-juice and palm-fibres lines only.

The prescriptions I found. Two prescriptions in the e-text use a palm product from this list, and I read both.

1. **Palm-fibres, in a burns dressing.** The passage headed "AGAINST BURNS" (line 2247 onward) gives a day-by-day series of dressings: "TO USE ON THE FIRST DAY Black amat-juice", then "ANOTHER ON THE FIRST DAY Elderberries uah-corn Cat's dung", "TO USE ON THE SECOND DAY Goat's dung", "TO USE ON THE THIRD DAY Thorns-of-Acanthus, dried". At line 2265 it continues: "TO USE ON THE FOURTH DAY Wax Roasted Cow's Fat Palm-fibres. Make into one in uah-corn, and apply as a plaster." The e-text is damaged at the page break (a "70" page number and the running head "THE PAPYRUS EBERS" fall in the middle of the series), so the ordering of the days is as the OCR gives it. 2. **Palm-juice, in a neck-growth remedy.** At line 3786 a case is introduced: "WHEN THOU MEETEST A GROWTH IN THE NECK OF A PERSON ... and thou findest its point high-uplifted like a woman's breast, and the matter moves therein, then say thou: 'He has a growth in his neck in which the matter moves. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Palm-fibres appear as one ingredient of a fourth-day dressing in a burns series; palm-juice appears as one ingredient of a remedy for a fluid-filled growth of the neck.
- **What the text does not say.** It does not say which palm, what the Egyptian name is, the quantities, or any effect of the palm ingredient on its own.

The Codex gives no protocol for a palm product and does not translate Ebers's conditions into modern diagnoses.

### Preparation & Dosing

Burns dressing: the fibres are combined with wax, roasted cow's fat and uah-corn into one mass and applied as a plaster (line 2265, quoted above). Neck remedy: the ingredients are listed together (line 3788); the e-text gives no method line for them in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. General, well-established facts only. Palms are a large family of tropical and subtropical plants; several yield a sweet sap and a coarse fibre from leaf bases or trunks. ### Contraindications
