# Black francolin — durrāj (دراج), the flesh

## *The black francolin,* Francolinus francolinus*, a game bird of south-west and south Asia; the identification is the modern one, from the Arabic* durrāj*, and is of moderate confidence, because the word has been used for more than one game bird and neither text describes the bird*  | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM, TBN

---

### Origin & Tradition

The francolin is named by exactly two of the texts on disk: Avicenna's *Canon* (CM) and Ibn Qayyim's *al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī* (TBN). Both treat only the flesh as food and medicine. Neither describes the bird, so the identification rests on the Arabic word *durrāj*. [USER: verify]

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The *Canon* (Book II, entry *durrāj*, Arabic text line 12208) is four lines. It opens: "it is known." The next sentence is damaged in the printed text and is not translated whole: "its flesh is better than … of the partridge (*qabaj*), and the turtle-doves (*fawākhit*), and more temperate and finer and drier than the flesh of …" (لحمه أفضل من دم القبج. والفواخت وأعدل وألطف وأيبس من لحم, lines 12209-12210). The printed word *dam* "blood" is apparently an error for *laḥm* "flesh", and the text after "than the flesh of" breaks off. Two uses follow: for the head, "the flesh of the francolins increases the brain and the understanding"; and for the excretory organs, "the flesh of the francolin greatly increases the semen" (لحم الدراج يزيد في المني جدا, lines 12210-12212).

**Prophetic medicine (TBN).** Ibn Qayyim (*al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī*, in the section on the flesh of birds, entry *laḥm al-durrāj*, Arabic text line 6656) sets the bird among the lawful birds, after the chicken, for which he cites a hadith in the two *Ṣaḥīḥ*s, that the Prophet ate chicken (line 6647). For the francolin itself he cites no hadith and gives no verdict on one. He says: "the flesh of the francolin: hot and dry in the second, light, fine, quickly digested, generating moderate blood; and eating much of it sharpens the sight" (لحم الدراج: حار يابس في الثانية، خفيف لطيف، سريع الانهضام، مولد للدم المعتدل، والإكثار منه يحد البصر). The next entry is the partridge (*ḥajal*): "it generates good blood, quickly digested" (line 6657-6658).

### Active Components

Game-bird meat is lean, with protein, iron-rich muscle pigment and little fat. This is general food science; neither text names a constituent.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

A food. The historical record is that Avicenna ascribes to it increase of the brain and understanding and of the semen, and that Ibn Qayyim calls it a light, quickly digested food that makes moderate blood, with the warning that too much sharpens the sight (*yuḥiddu al-baṣar*; the sense of "sharpens" is as printed, and whether it is meant as praise or harm is not stated in the line). [USER: verify] It is not a recommended therapy; it is a meat. The wild bird is subject to hunting and conservation rules.

### Preparation & Dosing

Neither text gives a preparation or a dose. Ibn Qayyim's entry is on eating the flesh (line 6656), which is "light and quickly digested." No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Wild game is a lean protein source. There is no established effect of francolin meat on brain function, semen or sight. This is general knowledge. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TBN (Ibn Qayyim), line 6656.** "Hot and dry in the second [degree]."
- **CM (Avicenna), lines 12210.** "More temperate and finer and drier" than the flesh named in the damaged clause; no degree is given.
- The two agree that the flesh is fine and dry. They differ in that Ibn Qayyim gives a degree and Avicenna a comparison.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(b) Framed.* The entry claims that the flesh increases the brain and understanding and the semen "greatly" (lines 12210-12212). These are claims about the faculties in Avicenna's own terms. They are not cultivation grades: the *Canon* does not call the bird a tonic of the innate heat or radical moisture and gives no cultivation status.

**Prophetic medicine (TBN).** *(b) Framed.* The flesh is a lawful bird eaten as food, in a section that opens with the Prophet's eating chicken (line 6647-6648; the hadith is in the two *Ṣaḥīḥ*s, as Ibn Qayyim says). Ibn Qayyim's entry on the francolin is a dietary profile. He does not connect it to a *sunna* of the bird, and he does not assign it a role among the things on which, as he says, the keeping of health depends (food and drink, clothing, dwelling, air, sleep and waking, movement and rest, intercourse, evacuation and retention; line 3795-3797). It belongs, by its nature, under food and drink in that list, and no further status is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator eats the francolin as the texts present it: a light, quickly digested game meat in moderation. Both texts promise more than they can show (brain, understanding, semen), and the honest use is as food, taken in measure, since Ibn Qayyim's own warning is that much of it harms the sight.

### Contraindications

- **Game safety.** Wild birds carry parasites and bacteria; cook thoroughly.
- **Conservation and law.** Wild francolin is a hunted species with seasons and bag limits in many countries; follow local law.
- **Claims.** The brain and semen claims are historical record only and are not treatments.
