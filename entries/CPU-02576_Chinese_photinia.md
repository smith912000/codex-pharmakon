# Chinese photinia — 石南 *Shi Nan* (Shennong bencao jing), also written 石楠; alias 鬼目 *Gui Mu*
## *An evergreen Rosaceae shrub or small tree, Photinia serratifolia (Desf.) Kalkman (formerly P. serrulata), the identification of 石南 from the Tang period onward; medium confidence, because the classic's text gives no description of the plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`), which prints the name as 石南. I did not locate a matching paragraph in the readable pages of Yang's 1998 translation (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), so there is no second witness. The identification is ours, with the dating of the equation taken from the batch note and not checked against a Tang source here. [USER: verify] (The spine has no other *Photinia*. The batch note records that equations of 石南 with *Rhododendron* have been proposed and are not supported; no text on disk bears on them.)

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the lower (下) classic, reads 味辛，苦 ("acrid, bitter"). It 主养肾气、内伤、阴衰 ("mainly nourishes the kidney qi, and treats internal injury and decline of the yin"), and 利筋骨皮毛 ("benefits sinews, bones, skin and hair"). It then gives a separate line for the fruit: 实：杀蛊毒，破积聚，逐风痹 ("the fruit: kills gu poison, breaks accumulations and gatherings, drives out wind impediment"). The alias is 一名鬼目 ("ghost eye"), which stands after the fruit clause, so the text does not say whether it names the plant or the fruit alone. The habitat is 生山谷 ("grows in mountains and valleys"). There is no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 石南 *shi nan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3711.) The classical terms 肾气 and 阴衰 are given here as the text prints them and are not restated in modern clinical language.

**Wu Pu.** No Wu Pu paragraph is printed for this entry.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰：生华阴。二月、四月采实，阴干: "grows at Huayin; the fruit is gathered in the second and fourth months and dried in the shade." (Line 3713.) This is the only guidance on gathering. There is no 案 by Sun for this entry.

**Identity.** The fruit clause shows the drug is a plant with fruit, and 鬼目 is its alias. Nothing else describes it. The *Photinia* equation is the standard modern one. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. *Photinia serratifolia* is an evergreen Rosaceae shrub or tree with leathery leaves, white flower clusters and small red fruit, widely planted as an ornamental. The constituents of this species were not retrieved for this entry, and none is asserted. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records an acrid, bitter plant "nourishing the kidney qi" and benefiting sinews and bones, and a fruit for gu poison, accumulations and wind impediment. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends none. The tonic-sounding opening line sits in the lower grade, which the Classic itself describes as the grade of treating disease and not for long use.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives only the harvest: the fruit gathered in the second and fourth months and dried in the shade (Mingyi layer). It gives no preparation, route, quantity or course. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

*Photinia* is a genus of the rose family, and *P. serratifolia* is a common garden and hedge plant. Whether its leaves or fruit relieve any of the conditions the Classic names has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong text proper).** 味辛，苦: acrid; bitter. No toxicity statement is printed.
- Class: lower (下). No organ affinity is printed beyond the phrase 养肾气 in the indications. No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 石南 in the lower grade (下品); its preface (Sun ed. line 2935) says lower drugs are "for treating disease to correspond with earth", "mostly toxic, not to be taken long" (多毒，不可久服). The paragraph opens with "nourishes the kidney qi", but it has no 久服 clause and no promise of lightness or long life; the line is an indication, not a cultivation status. The fruit clause, the aliases and the harvest come from the same paragraph and the Mingyi layer. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 石南 *shi nan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3711.)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A lower-grade plant whose first indication sounds like a tonic but whose grade is a warning against long use. SHORT entry: the sources give little.

### Contraindications

- **Lower-grade drug.** The Classic says lower drugs are mostly toxic and not for long use; no toxicity is printed for this one, and none is established here.
- **Identification.** Several evergreen shrubs have been sold under the name; do not eat ornamental garden plants as medicine.
- **Gu poison, accumulations, impediment and weakness.** Need medical assessment.
- **Pregnancy.** No statement is made in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
