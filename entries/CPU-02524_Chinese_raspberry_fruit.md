# Chinese raspberry fruit — 蓬 *peng* (one character is dropped in the file; the entry is the "peng-lei" bramble), also called 复盆 *fupen*

## *The fruit of a bramble, our identification (medium confidence) as Rubus chingii (the "fupenzi" of later pharmacy); the Shennong text gives only the name, and Rubus idaeus has also been proposed, so the species is a point of doubt* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Chinese / Shennong bencao jing (SBJ).** The bramble is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. The name printed as the entry head is the single character 蓬; the file drops the second character of the usual compound, so the head reads as 蓬 and the full name is not legible in the file. The identification with *Rubus chingii* is ours, from the alternative name *fupen* (复盆) and the standard modern identification, and it is of medium confidence. The source is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`); the entry stands in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 蓬 *peng*, same file, line 1575).

**The Shennong text proper** (line 1575): "Flavour sour, balanced. It governs settling the five viscera, benefits the *jing* and *qi*, lengthens the yin and makes it firm, strengthens the will and doubles strength, and gives children. Taken for long, it lightens the body and one does not age. One name, *fupen* (复盆). It grows in level marshes." (味酸，平。主安五脏，益精气，长阴令坚，强志倍力，有子。久服，轻身、不老一名复盆。生平泽。) In the file the clause "one does not age" runs straight into the alias with no punctuation between them, so the reading of the alias as a separate sentence is mine. "Level marshes" (平泽) is the habitat the text gives; the later pharmacy plant is a hill bramble, and the text does not say whether the two agree. [USER: verify]

**The Wu Pu layer** (line 1577): *fupen* has the names *juepen* (决盆) and, from "Zhen's Bencao", for *fupenzi*, names that include "*lujing*" (陆荆) (the file drops one character in the other). **The Mingyi layer** (line 1579): further names, "*yinyao*" (阴药) among them; it grows at Jingshan and Yuanju.

**Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 1581).** Sun quotes the *Shuowen*, the *Guangya* ("*fupen*, *luying*, is the *mei*"), and the *Erya* with Guo Pu: "it is *fupen*; the fruit resembles the *mei* but is smaller, and may also be eaten" (实似莓而小，亦可食). He quotes Lu Ji on the *Mao shi*, who describes a plant that trails in vines, with leaves like mugwort, whitish, and red edible seeds (其子赤，可食); the file drops characters in this passage, and the fit to this entry is Sun's, not stated in the Shennong text. He quotes the *Liexian zhuan*: "Changrong ate the root of *peng*" (昌容食蓬 根). He quotes Li Dangzhi: it is "the *mei* that people eat" (即是人所食莓); and Tao Hongjing: *peng* is the name of the root, and *fupen* is the name of the fruit. So the layers disagree or split on the name's referent, and Tao's split is a later reading.

**Preface.** The upper class "mainly nourishes life, to correspond to heaven; non-toxic; taken much or long does not harm people; for one who wishes to lighten the body and benefit qi, not to age and to extend the years, take from the upper classic" (line 43).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, raspberry-type fruits (*Rubus*) are rich in sugars, organic acids, polyphenols (including ellagic acid derivatives) and vitamin C. No analytical source for *R. chingii* was retrieved, so none is asserted. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic assigns the fruit to settling the five viscera, to the *jing* and *qi*, to firmness of the yin, to will, strength and having children. These are generative and constitutional claims in the classic's own terms. They are not translated into a modern indication, and the Codex proposes no protocol. A ripe raspberry-type fruit is an ordinary food; any use beyond eating it needs a source this entry does not have.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong paragraph gives no preparation and no dose; Sun's note says the fruit "may also be eaten" (Guo Pu), which is a remark about food use. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Rubus fruits are eaten fresh and as preserves. Whether the fruit affects strength, fertility or ageing has not been tested in sources retrieved for this entry. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong).** One flavour and one nature are printed: sour (酸), balanced (平). Upper class by placement. No further quality is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The entry stands in the upper classic and its paragraph carries a long-taking clause: "Taken for long, it lightens the body and one does not age" (久服，轻身、不老; line 1575). In the classic's own terms the fruit is credited with "benefiting the *jing* and *qi*", "strengthening the will" and "doubling strength", and the preface (line 43) says the upper class is for those who wish to lighten the body and not to age. These are the classic's words and are not translated into another tradition's vocabulary. Sun's quotation of the *Liexian zhuan* ("Changrong ate the root of *peng*") is a later work's report and is not part of the Shennong text proper; I did not read the *Liexian zhuan* itself, so this is Sun's report only. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** The classic's grading is clear, but its guidance is not: no dose, no preparation, no season and no length of course. The fruit is ordinarily a food, so the honest practice is to eat the fruit when it is in season and to treat the classic as a statement of how the tradition ranked it. Tao Hongjing's distinction between root and fruit is a warning that the name has been used for more than the fruit.

### Contraindications

- **Identification.** The text names a bramble but not a species. A wild bramble should be identified by an expert before any use. The spine has separate entries for *Rubus idaeus* (I_837), *Rubus phoenicolasius* (I_247) and others; nothing is transferred. [USER: verify]
- **Root and fruit.** Tao Hongjing splits the name between root and fruit. The Shennong text says "fruit" nowhere; do not assume which part is meant.
- **Pregnancy, children, medicines.** The text says nothing; no use is supported. [USER: verify]
