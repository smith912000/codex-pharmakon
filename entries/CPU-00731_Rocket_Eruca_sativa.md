# Rocket / Arugula — Euzomon (Greek, Dioscoridean), Jirjir (Arabic, Avicennan), Assyrian garden cress-rocket, Sanskrit pungent green

## *Eruca sativa* Mill. (syn. *Eruca vesicaria* subsp. *sativa*) | Family: Brassicaceae (Cruciferae)

**Tradition Tags:** EP (Ebers) · AT (Assyrian) · DMM (Dioscorides) · CM (Avicenna) · SS (Sushruta)

**Energetic Classification:** Hot and Dry (Galenic, second/third degree) — a pungent, heating, stimulating green

**Training Phase:** Tempering (a warming, stimulating, "kindling" herb; the classical aphrodisiac-reputed green)

---

### Origin & Tradition

Rocket is a fast-growing pungent Mediterranean-to-Central-Asian crucifer, eaten as a peppery salad green since antiquity and carrying an unusually consistent cross-tradition reputation as a *heating, stimulating, libido-raising* plant. That reputation — from Greek and Roman through Arabic sources — is one of the most stable folk-pharmacological threads in the classical record. Historical claims below are kept to genuine general attestation.

**Ebers Papyrus / Egyptian (EP):** Pungent crucifer and cress-type greens appear among Egyptian garden and medicinal plants used as warming, appetite- and vitality-stimulating foods. As with all Ebers material this is treated at the level of real general attestation, not invented recipe detail.

**Assyrian (AT):** Mesopotamian cuneiform herbals list pungent garden greens of the cress/rocket type among cultivated stimulating and "hot" plants. The specific compounding is not preserved in verifiable detail; the attested fact is the plant's membership in the warming-garden-green category.

**Dioscorides (DMM):** *De Materia Medica* describes rocket (*euzomon*) as a heating pot-herb that stimulates sexual desire and acts as a mild diuretic and digestive; Dioscorides also notes that eaten in quantity it heats the body and that the seed was used similarly. This is the founding European attestation of rocket's aphrodisiac reputation.

**Avicenna (CM):** The *Canon* classes rocket (*jirjir*) as hot and dry; the tradition used it and its seed as a warming aphrodisiac, digestive stimulant and diuretic, while cautioning — as Greek authors did — that excess heats the blood, may cause headache, and is unsuited to hot temperaments.

**Sushruta / Ayurvedic (SS):** Pungent mustard-family greens of the rocket/cress type are placed among *ushna* (heating), *deepana* (digestive-kindling) and *vrishya*-leaning (reproductively tonic) foods, used to stimulate digestion and vitality, with the same heating caution in *pitta*-dominant constitutions.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Rocket's doctrine — a *kindling* green that raises inner heat and sexual fire — sits on a genuine chemical foundation. Its pungency is the taste of glucosinolate breakdown: bite the leaf, and the enzyme myrosinase converts glucosinolates (chiefly glucoerucin and glucosativin) into isothiocyanates (notably **erucin**) that produce the peppery burn and much of the plant's bioactivity. The "heat" the ancients tasted and the "heat" they ascribed to its action are, in this case, the same molecular event. The aphrodisiac reputation is the traditional interpretation of a real stimulating, circulation- and appetite-affecting pungent principle. In Kenetica rocket is read as a *Tempering kindler* — a food-grade way to study how a pungent, sulphur-bearing plant raises heat, stimulates, and mobilises, and how that same heat must be balanced against goitrogenic and thyroid considerations.

### Active Components

- **Glucosinolates** — chiefly **glucoerucin** and **glucosativin** (and dimeric derivatives), plus glucoraphanin-type compounds; the sulphur-rich precursors defining the Brassicaceae.
- **Isothiocyanates** — especially **erucin** (the hydrolysis product of glucoerucin) and sulforaphane-family relatives; erucin is a documented slow-release **hydrogen-sulfide (H₂S) donor** and the main bioactive downstream product, with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and cytoprotective activity.
- **Flavonoids and polyphenols** — quercetin, kaempferol and isorhamnetin glycosides; antioxidant capacity.
- **Carotenoids, chlorophylls and vitamin C**, plus folate and vitamin K.
- **Erucic acid and seed lipids** in the oil-bearing seed.
- **Thiocyanate/goitrogenic potential** — a family-typical property with thyroid relevance at high intake or low iodine.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Rocket is a **Tempering-phase kindling agent**. Its function is to introduce controlled heat and stimulation: a peppery, glucosinolate-rich green used to raise digestive fire, stimulate appetite and circulation, and — in the traditional reading — support libido and vitality. Because its bioactivity comes from reactive sulphur chemistry (isothiocyanates, H₂S donation), it also serves in the system as the food-grade teaching example of *how pungency does work in the body* — heat that is chemical, not merely metaphorical. It is safe and useful at food levels; the caution that gates it upward is thyroid function (goitrogenic glucosinolate load).

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- **Grami et al. (2024), *Nutrients*** — a narrative review of *Eruca sativa* in male reproductive health, cataloguing its antioxidant constituents (polyphenols, glucosinolates, flavonoids, carotenoids) and summarising evidence that ES extracts may exert preventive/therapeutic effects on male reproductive function, particularly against oxidative and environmental insults. This is the closest modern correlate of the classical aphrodisiac reputation, at review strength. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/nu16020253
- **Raouf et al. (2024), *Cureus*** — an animal study reporting that *Eruca sativa* leaf juice significantly improved sperm count and morphology and raised sex-hormone levels in rats exposed to cigarette smoke, indicating a protective effect on spermatogenesis under toxic stress. Preclinical (rat) evidence, not human. DOI: https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.67662
- **Grami et al. (2019), *Journal of Medicinal Food*** — showed that *Eruca sativa* aqueous leaf extract protected against bisphenol-A-induced testicular damage in Wistar rats, improving sperm parameters, hormone levels and antioxidant enzyme activity, attributed to phenolic antioxidant compounds. Preclinical. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1089/jmf.2019.0170
- **Testai et al. (2023), *Biochemical Pharmacology*** — demonstrated that erucin, the isothiocyanate H₂S-donor from *Eruca sativa*, is cardioprotective against ischemia/reperfusion injury via persulfidation of mitochondrial Kv7.4 channels, characterising a concrete molecular mechanism for the plant's key bioactive. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bcp.2023.115728

**Evidence grade: Preclinical (with a mechanistically well-characterised active).** Erucin's chemistry and several protective effects are documented in animal and cell models, and the reproductive-support findings are consistent and biologically plausible — but the human aphrodisiac/fertility claim remains **traditional and not confirmed by controlled human trials**. Present rocket as a nutritious pungent green with promising preclinical reproductive-protective and cytoprotective data, not as a proven aphrodisiac or fertility treatment.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM (by analogy):** warm/hot thermal nature, pungent flavour; disperses cold, moves qi, stimulates digestion and circulation.
- **Ayurveda (Sushruta lineage):** *rasa* — *katu* (pungent), *tikta* (bitter); *guna* — *laghu* (light), *ruksha* (dry), *tikshna* (sharp/penetrating); *virya* — *ushna* (heating); *vipaka* — *katu* (pungent). *Deepana* (kindles digestion), *vrishya*-leaning (reproductive-tonic reputation), aggravates *pitta* in excess.
- **Galenic / Unani (Avicennan lineage):** Hot and Dry, second-to-third degree; aphrodisiac, digestive stimulant, diuretic; tempered classically with cooling greens (e.g. lettuce, purslane) to blunt its heat and prevent headache.

### Contraindications

- **Goitrogenic / thyroid caution:** as a glucosinolate-rich crucifer, high or sustained intake can interfere with iodine uptake and thyroid hormone synthesis, especially in iodine deficiency or existing hypothyroidism. Those with thyroid disease should keep intake moderate and ensure adequate iodine; this is the entry's key caution.
- **Hot temperaments / *pitta* excess:** classical sources warn that excess heats the blood and may cause headache; not suited in quantity to already "hot" or inflamed states.
- **Pregnancy/lactation:** ordinary culinary salad amounts are food-safe; concentrated seed/juice preparations are uncharacterised for reproductive safety and should be avoided without professional guidance.
- **Erucic acid (seed oil):** rocket-seed oil contains erucic acid, intake of which is regulated in food; avoid high consumption of seed oil.
- **Anticoagulant interaction:** high vitamin-K content can antagonise warfarin — keep intake consistent if anticoagulated.
