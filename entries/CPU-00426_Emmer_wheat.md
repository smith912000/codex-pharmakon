The following prescriptions that name wheat were found by search and read in this session. They are quoted as Bryan prints them.

- **Line 1893.** A drink for the abdomen: "Crush and grind in Sweet Beer to which has been added the strength of the required herbs (also add Wheat and Barley Beer). Keep moist. Do not let it dry or a scum form thereover. Put thereto: Honey Goose-fat ... Make into one and let the man or woman drink it." Bryan introduces it (about line 1878) as one of the "rather involved" internal remedies.
- **Line 2531.** "Clean Grain-of-Wheat Female Reed. Plaster the abdomen therewith." The remedy stands in a run of remedies that begins with women's complaints at line 2519. Its own heading was not in the lines read, so the complaint is not known. [USER: verify]
- **Line 2644.** In Bryan's account of skin disease, "Scabs in every Limb": "a poultice of Clay-from-the-Wall, Wheaten Flour, and Fat-of-the-deher-animal, strained into Yeast-of-Sweet-Beer".
- **Line 3582.** An examination of "a Hardening in his Abdomen", ending with "the remedy from the Secret Book that is only for the Physician with the exception of thine own daughter: Green undried Wheat. Cool in Water without evaporating it. Take it off the Fire in order to mix Refuse-of-Dates with it. Strain and let him take it for four days so that he may be immediately cured." The wording of the Water and Fire steps is Bryan's and is not clear in the English.
- **Line 3637.** An abdomen examination for the *bexetu*-illness: "Fight thou against it with soothing-remedies after he has had a poultice of Wheat-water. If it moves hereafter under thy fingers then give him an enema for four mornings."
- **Line 3701.** An abdomen examination ("it breaks often ... a fault in his excrement"): "Make him for it: Wheaten-bread, Wormwood-in-particularly-large-quantities", with Garlic, Beer and ox flesh, to be taken "in order to open his eyes, to open his nose, and to make easy the passing of his excrement".

The papyrus names several other grains in Bryan's English, such as "uah-grain" (lines 1883, 2600, 3639), "huru-grain" and "durra-corn" (line 2263). The text on disk does not equate any of them with wheat or emmer, and none is treated here.

### Active Components

Wheat grain is generally known as a starchy cereal that also contains protein (gluten proteins among them), fibre, and some B vitamins. The composition of emmer specifically was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. In the passages read, wheat appears as a base or vehicle in drinks, plasters and poultices, as "Green undried Wheat" in one abdominal remedy and as bread in another. Bryan's English records what was done for a complaint. It does not credit the grain with a nutritive or constitutional action, and the Codex does not add one. The Codex gives no protocol for it.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations given in the passages read are: wheat added to a beer-based drink with honey and goose-fat (line 1893), clean grain as an abdominal plaster (2531), wheaten flour in a poultice with clay and fat, strained into yeast of sweet beer (2644), green undried wheat cooled in water, mixed with refuse of dates, strained and taken for four days (3582), wheat-water as a poultice (3637) and wheaten bread with wormwood (3701). Only the duration "four days" (3582) and "four mornings" (3637, for the enema that follows) is stated. No quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian — Ebers Papyrus (EP).** *(c) Absent.* The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the vital constitution of the person. Wheat appears in the prescriptions above as bread, flour, grain and wheat-water. The Egyptian model of the person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) and the idea of *heka*, in which the spell and the drug form one intervention, are not attached to wheat by any line read, and no recitation was seen in the lines read. The botanical identity as emmer is a modern hypothesis resting on a list row. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this grain. It is a food and a vehicle in the papyrus, and the honest answer is to do nothing with it on the strength of this entry.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Wheat contains gluten and is a common cause of food allergy and of coeliac disease, and anyone with either condition should avoid it. This is a general fact and is not found in the sources read. The abdominal remedies quoted are historical, and a swollen, painful or blocked abdomen needs ordinary medical care. Do not attempt "green undried wheat" or an enema from this entry.
