# Karanja — Karañja, Naktamāla, Ghṛtakarañja, Pongam Oiltree, Indian Beech

## *Pongamia pinnata* (L.) Pierre | Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)

**Tradition Tags:** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā), CS (Caraka Saṃhitā), Bhāvaprakāśa Nighaṇṭu; Siddha (Pungam)

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda (Suśruta & Caraka):** Karañja is the classical Ayurvedic skin-and-wound tree. Suśruta places it in the **Aragvadhādi gaṇa** — the cluster of bitter, Kapha- and kṛmi-destroying drugs used against kuṣṭha (skin diseases), kaṇḍū (itch), kṛmi (worms/parasites) and vraṇa (wounds/ulcers) — and repeatedly draws on it for external medicine. The seed oil (**Karañja taila**) is the officinal preparation: a heavy, bitter, penetrating oil applied to obstinate skin disease, scabies, eczema, ringworm, non-healing ulcers and as an anti-parasitic wash. The synonym *Naktamāla* ("night-garland") and *Ghṛtakarañja* mark it as the oil-bearing beech; Caraka groups it among skin and wound drugs. Leaves are used as a poultice and the bark/root in decoctions for the same indications. The signature is **external, cleansing, drying** action on damp, itching, worm-ridden lesions.

**Siddha:** As *Pungam*, the seed oil is likewise a first-line topical for scabies, itch and skin eruptions, and the tree is a valued village medicine and shade tree.

**Folk & agroforestry:** Across South and Southeast Asia the oil (traditionally lamp oil) doubles as a folk anti-scabetic and veterinary dressing; the tree is now cultivated globally as a nitrogen-fixing biodiesel feedstock, which is why the modern literature often reaches it through the fuel-oil route.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Cleansing Oil:** Where Citraka is the internal *fire*, Karañja is the external *scour*. Ayurvedic skin pathology reads chronic eruptions as Kapha-and-kleda (moisture/ooze) mixed with kṛmi and vitiated blood surfacing at the boundary of the body. Karañja taila is the boundary-medicine: bitter enough to kill the parasite, drying enough to close the ooze, penetrating enough to reach the seat of the lesion. Symbolically it belongs to the class of oils that *purify by contact rather than nourishment* — the anti-Kapha counterpart to the nourishing oils. In Kenetica it is a `[RESET]` agent precisely because its work is subtractive: it clears surface disorder and restores clean margins, the somatic analogue of clearing accumulated noise before rebuilding.

### Active Components

Marker furanoflavonoids: **karanjin** and **pongamol**, alongside pongapin, kanjone, pongaglabrone and a spectrum of flavones, flavans and chalcones; the seed yields a fixed oil rich in these bitter principles plus fatty acids and sterols. Karanjin is the recognised topical/anti-dermatophyte and anti-inflammatory marker.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Karañja sits in `[RESET]` as an **external cleansing and skin-restoration** agent: its classical use against itch, eczema, fungal skin disease, non-healing wounds and parasites maps onto recovery/repair of the body's surface barrier after damage, irritation or infection. It is chiefly a topical (Karañja taila) in the Kenetica frame — a clearing agent rather than a systemic tonic — used to close and clean rather than to build or stimulate.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, the evidence for Karañja is chiefly **phytochemical and preclinical**, well summarised in a peer-reviewed review.

- Al Muqarrabun et al. (2013), *Journal of Ethnopharmacology*, reviewed the traditional uses, phytochemistry and pharmacology of *Pongamia pinnata*, documenting its long-standing use for skin diseases, piles and wounds, and cataloguing isolated flavonoids (flavones, flavans, chalcones), terpenes and steroids with reported antioxidant, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory and anti-diabetic activity, alongside toxicity studies showing low mammalian toxicity of several extracts. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2013.08.041)

**Evidence grade:** preclinical / traditional. The antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory signals are congruent with the classical kuṣṭhaghna and vraṇaropana (wound-healing) uses, but human clinical trial evidence for the officinal oil is thin; treat therapeutic claims as traditional.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Kaṭu (pungent), Tikta (bitter), Kaṣāya (astringent)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Tīkṣṇa (sharp/penetrating)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** Kuṣṭhaghna — specific virtue against obstinate skin disease
- **Doṣa action:** Kapha–Vāta śāmaka (pacifies Kapha and Vāta)
- **Karma (actions):** Kuṣṭhaghna (anti-dermatosis), kaṇḍūghna (anti-pruritic), kṛmighna (anthelmintic), vraṇaśodhana-ropana (wound cleansing and healing), krimidanta (dental/gum use of twigs), Aragvadhādi gaṇa member

### Contraindications

Internal use of seed/oil requires supervision — the seed is bitter and can be an irritant/emetic in quantity. Patch-test the oil before wide topical use; discontinue on irritation. Pregnancy and lactation: internal use best avoided without qualified guidance. Not to be confused with edible oils.
