**Avicenna, Canon (CM).** The heading is حب القلقل (*Canon* Book II, entry *ḥabb al-qalqal*, Arabic text line 13303; the entry runs to line 13309). The description reads: الأبيض أكبر من القرطم ليس بخالص الاستدارة ينكسر عن لب دهني طيب الطعم (13304-13305): "the white [kind] is larger than safflower (*qurṭum*), not purely round; it breaks open on an oily kernel of pleasant taste." The passage names only the white kind and gives no plant, no place and no source author.

For the joints and nerves it says يقوي الأبدان المسترخية, "it strengthens slack bodies" (13305). Under properties: مقليه أخف, "roasted, it is lighter" (13306). Under cosmetic use: مسمن, "fattening" (13306). For the head it is said to relieve headache (*muṣadda'*) especially when taken as a nibble with old wine (13306-13307). For the digestive organs: "taking much of it causes surfeit and nausea" (الإكثار منه يتخم ويهيض); it digests better eaten with *ṭabarzad* sugar, sugar and honey; the roasted is better; and "the small one is very stinging to the stomach" (والصغير شديد اللذع للمعدة, 13307-13309). One clause (وليس خلطه برديء, 13308) is unclear in this text and is not translated beyond "mixing it with a bad [one]". [USER: verify]

The text file carries a manuscript-variant marker (`ms0491`) inside the head paragraph (line 13306); it is not part of the sense.
