# Cicada — ṭāṭīqus (طاطيقس)

## *The singing insect, Cicada orni or a related cicada; the identification is the modern one and rests on the Canon's description of a shrill insect of olive trees* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** Book II, entry *ṭāṭīqus*, Arabic text lines 13694 to 13699. The name may be the Greek *tettix* in Arabic dress [USER: verify]; the Dioscorides chapter was searched: the English Dioscorides on disk has no occurrence of "cicada" or "tettix", so no Dioscorides parallel is cited. [USER: verify.]

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** What it is: "Iṣṭifan claimed that this animal is found in olive trees and is close to the locust, and cries most of the time, and its cry is a shrill sound (*ṣarīr*); the people of Syria call it *al-dhīz*, the people of Ṭabaristān call it *ankūriyāshan* [the edition adds an unclear phrase], and the people of Khurasan call it *jathrad*" (زعم اصطفن إن هذا الحيوان يكون في شجر الزيتون وهو قريب من الجراد يصيح أكثر الزمان وصياحه صرير). The entry says nothing more about Iṣṭifan. [USER: verify who is meant.] Excretory organs: "when this animal is roasted on the griddle (*al-ṭābiq*), it benefits pains of the bladder" (إذا شوي هذا الحيوان على الطابق نفع من أوجاع المثانة). That is the whole use. No temperament, no dose, no preparation beyond roasting.

### Active Components

None is given in the text. Cicadas are insects, and their bodies are protein and chitin-rich, as in other insects. No pharmacological constituent is established.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The text records a roasted insect for bladder pain. Bladder pain needs medical care; there is no protocol. Collection of wild insects for medicine is not advised.

### Preparation & Dosing

Roasted on a griddle (line 13698 to 13699). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** A roasted-insect remedy for the bladder; nothing on the vital faculties. **(c) Absent: no cultivation framing is attested.**

**Practitioner's note.** There is nothing to practise here. The entry is mainly a record of the animal's names in Greek, Syria, Ṭabaristān and Khurasan, which is the part a reader can use.

### Contraindications

- Hygiene: eating wild insects poses a risk of infection and allergy (shellfish-type allergy cross-reactivity is known for insects).
- Bladder pain, blood in the urine or fever need medical care.
