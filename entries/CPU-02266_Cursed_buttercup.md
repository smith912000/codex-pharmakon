# Cursed buttercup — Shi Long Rui 石龙芮 (Shennong bencao jing); aliases Lu Guo Neng 鲁果能 and Di Shen 地椹, "earth mulberry"

## *Ranunculus sceleratus L., cursed buttercup (celery-leaved buttercup), a marsh and ditch buttercup; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of medium confidence at species level, since the classic describes no plant and the Yang 1998 translation names the drug as the seed ("Semen Ranunculi Sclerati")* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** The principal text read is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Its other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), also has the drug, as "Shi Long Rui (Semen Ranunculi Sclerati)" (line 1705), and is read here as a second witness, attributed to Yang. The row began as a genus-level placeholder, "Ranunculus sp.", with no species fixed; this entry is the species-level entry. Other buttercups are treated from other traditions in I_1174 (*Ranunculus repens*, Dioscorides 4-58), I_632 (*Ranunculus acris*), I_724 (*Ranunculus ficaria*) and I_1342 (*Ranunculus tanguticus*); none cites the Shennong text.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 石龙芮 in the middle classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 石龙芮 *shi long rui*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2065). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，平 ("bitter, balanced"); 主风寒湿痹 ("governs wind, cold, damp impediment"), 心腹邪气 ("evil qi in the heart and abdomen"), 利关节 ("frees the joints"), 止烦满 ("stops vexation and fullness"); 久服，轻身、明目、不老 ("taken long: lightens the body, brightens the eyes, does not age"); aliases 一名鲁果能 ("also called Lu's able fruit"; the *Yulan* reads 食果, "eating fruit") and 一名地椹 ("earth mulberry"); habitat 生川泽石边 ("grows beside stones in rivers and marshes").

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, line 2067) gives aliases 姜苔 and 天豆 and the flavours as judged by five authorities: "Shennong: bitter, balanced; Qi Bo: sour; Bian Que and Li shi: very cold; Lei Gong: salty, non-toxic" (神农：苦，平；岐伯：酸；扁鹊、李氏：大寒；雷公：咸，无毒). It also gives a date: 五月五日采, "gathered on the fifth day of the fifth month" (the *Yulan*).

The Mingyi bielu layer (line 2069) adds aliases 石能, 彭根 and 天豆, the habitat 生太山 ("grows on Mount Tai") and the harvest: 五月五日采子，二月、八月采皮，阴干 ("the seed is gathered on the fifth day of the fifth month; the 'skin' is gathered in the second and eighth months and dried in the shade"). The text does not say what the "skin" is.

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 2071) is one line, the *Fanzi jiran*: shi long rui comes from the Sanfu region, and the yellow-coloured is good. It is the commentator's, not the Shennong text's.

Yang 1998 (line 1705) renders the paragraph in English, placing it directly after Shi Hu under a heading "Herbs: Superior Class" (line 1695): "Shi Long Rui (Semen Ranunculi Sclerati) is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats wind cold damp impediment and evil qi in the heart and abdomen. It disinhibits the joints and stops vexatious fullness. Protracted taking may make the body light, brighten the eyes, and prevent senility." The Codex follows Sun's placement in the middle classic and records the difference between the two editions without resolving it. [USER: verify]

**Appendix.** An appendix of drug relations, later than the main text, says of it: 石龙芮 大戟为使；畏蛇蜕、吴茱萸 ("shi long rui: da ji is its envoy; it fears snake slough and wu zhu yu", line 4381), and says of ting li that it "dislikes shi long rui" (line 4427). The preface (line 4117) says that drugs standing in such relations should not be combined; the Codex draws no further conclusion.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, buttercups (*Ranunculus*) contain ranunculin, which breaks down when the fresh plant is crushed or chewed to protoanemonin, an acrid irritant; the amount in dried material is lower. *R. sceleratus* is among the buttercups noted for this irritancy. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, balanced drug for wind-cold-damp impediment, for evil qi in the heart and abdomen, for freeing the joints and for fullness, and, when taken long, for lightening the body, brightening the eyes and not ageing. These are the classic's categories and the Codex does not translate them into modern diagnoses. It gives no protocol and no dose. The plant is a skin and mucosal irritant (see Contraindications), and the long-use clause is not a reason to take it.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparations in the sources read are the harvests: the seed on the fifth day of the fifth month, and the "skin" in the second and eighth months, dried in the shade (Mingyi layer, line 2069). The preface (line 4121) says that drugs differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Ranunculus sceleratus* grows in wet places across the northern hemisphere, and buttercups generally are skin irritants: fresh sap can blister skin and mucous membranes, and the fresh plant is avoided by livestock. The classic's claims (lightening the body, not ageing) have no modern pharmacological equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味苦，平, "bitter, balanced" (line 2065). The Wu Pu layer records the disagreement of five authorities: "bitter, balanced" (Shennong), "sour" (Qi Bo), "very cold" (Bian Que and Li shi), "salty, non-toxic" (Lei Gong) (line 2067), which the Codex does not settle.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested, with a difference between editions.* The paragraph for 石龙芮 has a 久服 clause that includes the terms the classic uses for life-nourishing status: 久服，轻身、明目、不老, "taken long: lightens the body, brightens the eyes, does not age". The upper classic's preface (line 43) names "lighten the body ... not age" as the aim of the upper medicines. In Sun's edition, however, the drug stands in the middle classic (卷二 中经; contents, line 1677), whose preface says the middle medicines "nourish the nature, correspond to man; non-toxic or toxic, weigh what is fitting; for those who wish to check disease and supplement the lean" (主养性以应人。无毒、有毒，斟酌其宜。欲遏病补羸者，本中经). Yang's edition places it among the superior herbs (line 1695). The register is (a) because of the clause's wording, with the heading difference recorded and unresolved. This records the classic's wording and not a claim that the plant does these things. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 石龙芮 *shi long rui*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2065)

**Practitioner's note.** The classic gives this marsh buttercup a long-use promise of lightness and not ageing, and then the two editions disagree on its grade. That is a record of wording, not a practice. Buttercups are irritants, no dose or course is given, and nothing here supports taking the plant.

### Contraindications

- **Irritant:** the fresh plant and its sap can blister skin and burn the mouth and gut; do not chew, eat or apply the fresh plant. The long-use clause is the classic's wording only.
- **Identity:** the drug may be the seed or another part (the text says "seed" and "skin"); other buttercups are not interchangeable.
- **Appendix cautions:** the appendix says it "fears" snake slough and wu zhu yu, and that ting li "dislikes" it (lines 4381 and 4427). These are historical pairings and not a modern interaction list.
- **Pregnancy, nursing, children:** no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given; "taken long" is not a basis for a regimen.
