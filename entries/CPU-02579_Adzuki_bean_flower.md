# Adzuki bean flower — 腐婢 *Fu Bi* (Shennong bencao jing), glossed 小豆花 *xiao dou hua*
## *The flower of the adzuki bean, Vigna angularis (Willd.) Ohwi & Ohashi, in the standard modern identification; medium confidence, because the Wu Pu and Mingyi layers both equate 腐婢 with 小豆花 (the "small bean flower"), but a later equation with another plant is recorded in the batch note and the species of 小豆 is itself the modern reading* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). I did not locate a matching paragraph in the readable pages of Yang's 1998 translation (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), so there is no second witness. The identification is ours. (The adzuki bean seed itself, 赤小豆, is a separate middle-grade entry in the same classic, Sun ed. line 2853, and is not treated here.)

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the lower (下) classic, reads 味辛，平 ("acrid, balanced"). It governs [a character is missing in the transcription] 疟 (a kind of ague), 寒热、邪气 ("cold and heat, perverse qi"), 泄利 ("diarrhoea and dysentery"), 阴不起 ("the yin does not rise", a sexual weakness), 病酒 ("wine sickness") and 头痛 ("headache"). The habitat is 生汉中 ("grows in Hanzhong"). There is no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 腐婢 *fu bi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 4063.) It is the only grain-group drug in the lower classic (Sun's own summary: 上米、谷，下品一种, "grains, lower grade, one kind", line 4071).

**Wu Pu (3rd century).** 《吴普》曰：小豆花，一名腐婢（旧作付月，误）: "small bean flower; also called 腐婢 (the old text wrote 付月, a mistake)". It then gives a different nature: 神农：甘，毒 ("Shennong: sweet, toxic"), harvest 七月采，阴干四十日 ("gathered in the seventh month, dried in the shade for forty days"), and uses 治头痛，止渴 ("treats headache, stops thirst", with the source mark Yulan). (Sun ed. lines 4065-4067.) The Wu Pu paragraph is the one place in this entry that calls a Shennong version of the drug toxic (毒); it conflicts with the 味 paragraph's "balanced" and Sun does not comment.

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰：生汉中。即小豆花也。七月采，阴干: "grows in Hanzhong; it is the flower of the small bean; gathered in the seventh month, dried in the shade." (Line 4069.) There is no 案 by Sun for this entry.

**Identity.** Both the Wu Pu and Mingyi layers say 腐婢 is the small-bean flower. 小豆 is read in the standard modern view as the adzuki bean, *Vigna angularis*. The batch note records that a later equation with *Premna microphylla* (豆腐柴) exists and is not adopted here; no text on disk bears on it. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms the adzuki bean is a legume whose seeds are eaten, and legume flowers generally carry flavonoids and pigments. The composition of the flower was not retrieved for this entry, and none is asserted. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records an acrid flower for agues, fevers with chill, diarrhoea, sexual weakness, the effects of wine and headache, and the Wu Pu layer adds thirst. The Codex gives no protocol and recommends none. The seed of the same plant is a common food; the flower is not, and no use of it is endorsed here.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only handling is in the layers: gathered in the seventh month and dried in the shade (Mingyi layer), for forty days (Wu Pu layer). No preparation, route, quantity or course is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

The adzuki bean is a legume (Fabaceae) widely cultivated in East Asia for its seeds. No property of its flower as a medicine is established in the general knowledge used here. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SBJ (Shennong text proper).** 味辛，平: acrid; balanced. No toxicity statement is printed in the paragraph.
- **SBJ (Wu Pu layer).** 神农：甘，毒: sweet; toxic. This disagrees with the paragraph above.
- Class: lower (下). No organ affinity is printed. No other tradition's classification is applied.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 腐婢 in the lower grade (下品); its preface (Sun ed. line 2935) says lower drugs are "for treating disease to correspond with earth", "mostly toxic, not to be taken long" (多毒，不可久服). The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise; it lists agues, fluxes and headache. The Wu Pu layer's "toxic" agrees with the grade, but the 味 paragraph itself does not say so. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 腐婢 *fu bi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 4063.)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A lower-grade flower, taken if at all for a stated complaint and a short course; the plant's seed, a food, is a different matter and a different entry of the classic.

### Contraindications

- **Lower-grade drug.** The Classic says lower drugs are mostly toxic and not for long use, and the Wu Pu layer calls this drug toxic; no toxicity is established here.
- **Agues, fever with chill, persistent diarrhoea.** Need medical assessment.
- **Wine sickness.** The text's use for the effects of drink is historical record only and is no reason to drink.
- **Identification.** Other plants have been called 腐婢 in later writing; do not substitute them.
