# Aromatic wine — OINOS AROMATITES (Dioscorides 5-64)

## *A spiced wine made from grape must with dates and aromatic roots and spices; the grape link is the chapter's, since the wine is made from fermented must and passum; the botanical row is the grape (CPU-00120), and the identification of the preparation is medium confidence because it is a compound recipe with two versions* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Attested only in Dioscorides, *De Materia Medica*, book 5, chapter 64, OINOS AROMATITES, "Aromatic Wine" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 10117). The chapter that follows, 5-65 OINOS EK SMURNES, PEPEREOS, IRIDOS, is also headed "Aromatic Wine" but is a different recipe (myrrh, white pepper, iris, dill) and is on the spine as its own row (CPU-02600); it is not folded in here.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM), first recipe.** The chapter reads: "Vinum aromatites is made as follows. Pound two pints each of dates, aspalathus [1-19], calamus [1-17, 1-114] and Celtic nard [1-7] into small pieces, mix them with passum [raisin wine], and when the lumps are a good size put them into twelve pints of fermented must [grape pulp], then cork the jar and leave it alone for forty days. Then strain it and put it in jars."

**Second recipe.** "It is also made another way. Take eight teaspoonfuls of calamus [1-17, 1-114], seven teaspoonfuls of phu [1-10], two teaspoonfuls of costus [1-15], six teaspoonfuls of Syrian nard [1-6, 1-7, 1-8, 1-10], eight teaspoonfuls of cassia, four teaspoonfuls of saffron, five teaspoonfuls of amomum [1-14], and four teaspoonfuls of asarabacca [1-9], pound them together until fine, bind them in a linen cloth, and put them into nine gallons of must. After the wine has been boiled put it in new jars."

**Uses printed.** "It is good for disorders of the chest, side, and lungs, as well as for painful urination, chills, and the restrained menstrual flow. It is good taken as a drink for those who travel in extreme cold, and those who produce thick fluids. It causes a good colour, and is both sleep inducing and eases pain. It is suitable to use for suffering of the bladder and kidneys." The bracketed cross-references and glosses are the translator's.

**Context in book 5.** Chapter 5-11, "The Effects of Wines", says generally that unmixed simple wine "is warming, easily digested and good for the stomach" and that drunkenness, "especially that which is continual, is pernicious" (line 9826). That is a statement about wine in general and is not assigned to this spiced wine alone.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent of the finished wine. In general terms, it would carry ethanol from fermentation, sugars from the must and raisin wine, and the volatile oils and resins of the added aromatics (calamus, nard, cassia, saffron and others). Some of these are known to have strong individual effects; the text does not give a composition and no analysis was retrieved. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a recommended practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Chest, side and lung disorders; painful urination; chills; stopped menstrual flow; cold-weather travel; thick fluids; colour; sleep; pain; bladder and kidney complaints.
- **What the Codex does.** It gives no protocol. The wine is a compound of an alcoholic drink and a dozen aromatic drugs, several of which (asarabacca in particular) have their own caution elsewhere in the Codex. The restrained-menstrual-flow use is a historical report and not a method of bringing on a period.

### Preparation & Dosing

Two recipes are printed and neither is a dose. First: two pints each of dates, aspalathus, calamus and Celtic nard, pounded, mixed with passum, put into twelve pints of fermented must in a corked jar for forty days, then strained. Second: the eight spice quantities listed above, pounded, bound in linen, put into nine gallons of must; after boiling the wine goes into new jars (line 10117). The unit names are the English translator's renderings. No amount to be drunk, no timing and no duration is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Alcohol is a central nervous system depressant and a liver toxin in excess, and it interacts with many medicines. Fermentation, not the added materials, produces the alcohol. Several spices named (cassia, saffron, calamus) have been studied individually, but no study of this wine was retrieved, and a claim about any of them would need its own source. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM.** No quality is printed for this wine in 5-64. The general line at 5-11 that unmixed wine is "warming" is about wine as a class and is not assigned here. The chapter's uses (chills, cold travel) are indications, not a printed quality.
- No other tradition is attested for this material in the row.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(c) Absent.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system, and none is manufactured here. What a cultivator can take from the chapter is the discipline of attention it models: a long fixed interval (forty days), a stated state of the must (fermented), a choice of two recipes, and a list of particular aromatics pinned to chapters elsewhere in the work by cross-reference. That is method and provenance. It does not assign the wine a place in any cultivation scheme, and none is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to practise. This is a spiced medicinal wine from an indication-organised chapter, and the sources read tie it to complaints, not to the person's vital constitution. The responsible reading is historical.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

The uses printed for this spiced wine name the chest, side and lungs first, then painful urination and complaints of the bladder and kidneys. It is also listed for chills, for restrained menstrual flow (recorded as a historical report, not a method), for thick fluids, and for pain and sleep. The text says it is good for those who travel in extreme cold and for those who produce thick fluids, and that it causes a good colour. The chapter gives no humoral, doshic or qi term and no quality such as warming for this wine. The general line that unmixed wine is warming belongs to chapter 5-11 and is not assigned here. Its active part is fermentation alcohol plus the volatile aromatics, which the text does not analyse. The entry's contraindications cover alcohol in pregnancy, liver disease and with interacting medicines, and it warns that the restrained-flow use is not a method.

### Contraindications

- **Alcohol.** Avoid in pregnancy, breastfeeding, liver disease, alcohol-use disorder, and with sedating or interacting medicines. The text itself calls continual drunkenness pernicious (5-11).
- **Asarabacca and other spices.** Some of the named aromatics are not safe to take freely; the text gives no caution. [USER: verify]
- **Restrained menstrual flow.** Absent periods are a medical matter. Do not use a wine to bring one on; pregnancy must be ruled out.
- **Children.** Not for children.
