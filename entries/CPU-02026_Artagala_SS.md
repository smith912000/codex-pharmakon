# Artagala — Artagala (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation, 1907)

## *Artagala, a plant drug in the Varunadi group of Sutrasthana ch. 38, also used as leaf or fruit in ghee preparations. Identification: unresolved. The translator's one gloss, "Artagala (Arjua)" in Uttara ch. 53, is not a binomial and its spelling is damaged in the OCR; the botanical appendix of Vol. III has no line for it; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk. This is a single-text entry.** Spellings in quotations are normalised silently from the OCR where the damage is plain (for example Nandi for the printed Xandi); the line numbers cited give the text as printed. "Artagala" was searched in the three volumes of Bhishagratna (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, `Vol2`, `Vol3`) and in the chapter files.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Sutrasthana ch. 38, the Varunadi group (`Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15386; chapter file `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 45): "The drugs known as Varuna, Artagala, Shigru, Madhu-Shigru, Tarkari, Meshashringi, Putika, Naktamala, Morata, Agnimantha, the two kinds of Sairiyaka, Vimvi, Vasuka, Vasira, Chitraka, Shatavari, Bilva, Ajashringi, Darbha, and the two kinds of Vrihati form the group known as the Varunadi." The metrical text reads: "The group is possessed of the efficacy of reducing the deranged Kapham and fat and proves efficacious in cases of cephalaegia, Gulma and internal abscesses."

Sutra ch. 39 lists it among the drugs that soothe deranged Vata: "Bhadradaru, Kustha, Haridra, Varuna, Meshshringi, Vala, Ativala, Artagala, Kachhura, Sallaki ..." (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` line 88; Vol. I line 16007). A footnote in Chikitsa ch. 17 notes that "Chakradatta reads 'Arka', Vamsha and Artagala instead of the drugs of a heat-making potency", which Dallana explains as the drugs of the Bhadradarvadi and Pippalyadi groups (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt` lines 51 to 54). That ties the drug to the heat-making groups in the Chakradatta reading only.

In Uttara ch. 9, on ophthalmia due to deranged Vayu, "Ghrita duly cooked with (the decoction of) Pattura, Agnika (Ajamoda) and Artagala and with milk" is prescribed (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch09.txt` line 107; Vol. III line 2224). In Uttara ch. 17, on diseases of the pupil and lens, "the leaves or fruit (as the case may be) of Patola, Karkotaka, Karavella, Vartaku, Tarkari, Karira (tender bamboo plants), Shigru or Artagala, cooked with clarified butter, proves beneficial (invigorating) to the sight" (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch17.txt` line 447). In Uttara ch. 53, on hoarseness of voice, in the Vataja type the patient takes "clarified butter mixed with the expressed juice of Kasamarda, Vartaki and Markava with Artagala (Arjua)" (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch53.txt` line 78; Vol. III line 14160). The parenthesis is the translator's; "Arjua" may be a damaged "Arjuna", which is a separate name and is not equated here. [USER: verify] The parts used are leaf or fruit (Uttara ch. 17).

**Identification.** The group-list row gives none. The Appendix 3 list of Vol. III has Sanskrit names beginning "Arishta", "Arjaka", "Arjuna", "Arka" and has no line for Artagala. No binomial is printed for it in the passages read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Varunadi group: reducing Kapha and fat, headache, Gulma and internal abscess (Sutra ch. 38). As a ghee ingredient for sight (Uttara ch. 17), for Vata ophthalmia (Uttara ch. 9) and for Vata hoarseness (Uttara ch. 53). As a Vata-soothing drug (Sutra ch. 39).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose in measured units.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this drug.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations given are ghee cooked with the decoction of Artagala and other drugs and with milk (Uttara ch. 9); ghee cooked with its leaf or fruit (Uttara ch. 17); and ghee taken with its expressed juice (Uttara ch. 53). Chapter 38 closes with a rule for all the groups: "Only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group, or a similar number of drugs chosen from the different groups, or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety, or in combination with another, should be used according to the indications of any particular case, as the physician, in his discretion, would determine" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, near the end; Vol. I, ch. XXXVIII). It also says that the groups "will be more elaborately dealt with later on in the chapter on Therapeutics". This is a rule of method for the groups, not a dose for this drug. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is attested in the Sushruta Samhita, in the therapeutic groups of Sutrasthana ch. 38 and the other passages cited above, and its role there is therapeutic: groups are said to subdue Kapha, Vata or Pitta, to act as vermifuge or aseptic agents, and so on. Chikitsa ch. 26 (the vajikarana chapter, "increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons") and chs. 27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) were searched under the spellings printed here (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` to `ch30.txt`, and ch. 31) and the name was not found in them. The drug therefore carries no rasayana or vajikarana status in the sources retrieved, and nothing is said of ojas for it. The register is (b): framed by the therapeutic gana, with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** An unidentified group drug used in medicated ghees for Vata complaints of the eye and the voice. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use.

### Contraindications
