# Arjuna — Arjuna, Kakubha, Pārtha, Nadīsarja, Dhavala, White Marudah, Arjun tree

## *Terminalia arjuna* (Roxb. ex DC.) Wight & Arn. | Family: Combretaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SBJ, SS
**Tags (previous line, retained for reference):** SS (Suśruta Saṃhitā), CS (Caraka Saṃhitā), AH (Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya), Nighaṇṭus (Bhāvaprakāśa, Dhanvantari)

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta & Caraka (SS, CS):** Arjuna is present in the classical saṃhitās within the astringent, cooling, wound-healing, and haemostatic pharmacology — Suśruta the surgeon employing its bark to arrest bleeding, cleanse and close wounds, and firm ulcerated tissue. But Arjuna's defining identity as **the cardiac drug of Āyurveda** was crystallised and elevated above all by **Vāgbhaṭa in the Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya**, who names Arjuna specifically for *hṛdroga* (heart disease) — the earliest and most emphatic classical designation of a single herb as a heart tonic. From Vāgbhaṭa onward, *hṛdya* — "that which is beneficial and pleasing to the heart" — becomes Arjuna's fixed epithet.

**Nighaṇṭus (Bhāvaprakāśa, Dhanvantari):** The later materia medica confirm and extend the cardiac reputation, prescribing the bark powder taken with milk or ghee (the famous *Arjuna-kṣīra-pāka*, arjuna-milk decoction) for palpitation, chest pain, and cardiac weakness, and continuing its use as astringent and haemostatic. Synonyms — *Kakubha*, *Nadīsarja* (river-sāla, for its riverbank habitat), *Dhavala* / *White Marudah* (for its pale bark) — locate the tree botanically and poetically.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — The Bark That Girds the Heart:** Arjuna's doctrine is architectural. Its thick, pale, silica- and calcium-rich bark peels away in sheets and renews itself — a tree that is perpetually re-plating its own trunk. The tradition read this signature directly: Arjuna is the drug that *re-plates the heart's wall*, that girds and firms the muscular vessel of the circulation the way bark girds the trunk. The name carries the same charge — Arjuna, the Mahābhārata's disciplined, unbreakable warrior-archer (*Pārtha*), the hero whose strength is composure under maximal load. This is the exact doctrinal content of Kenetica's **cardiac-conditioning** phase: not stimulation of the heart but the *strengthening of the wall that holds* — the training of a circulatory system that stays composed under load, that firms rather than races. The astringent Kaṣāya rasa is the taste of contraction and tone; the cooling virya keeps the strengthened heart from overheating into Pitta agitation. Arjuna is the warrior's bark: it makes the heart strong by making it steady.

### Active Components

According to PubMed, the bark of *Terminalia arjuna* contains **triterpenoid saponins** (arjunic acid, arjunolic acid, arjungenin, the arjunglycosides — the fraction most associated with the inotropic/cardiotonic action), **flavonoids** (arjunone, arjunolone, luteolin), **oligomeric proanthocyanidins (OPCs)**, **hydrolysable tannins, gallic acid and ellagic acid**, phytosterols, and minerals including calcium, magnesium, zinc and copper (Miller/Alternative Medicine Review, 1999, PMID 10608917). The saponin–flavonoid–OPC complex underlies the cardiotonic, antioxidant, and mild lipid-modulating profile; the tannins the astringent-haemostatic action.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Arjuna is Kenetica's **cardinal cardiac and vascular conditioner** — the herb that names its own training phase. It is first-line wherever the goal is to strengthen and steady the heart and vasculature under load: cardiac weakness, palpitation, chest tightness of cardiac origin, and the general conditioning of circulatory resilience. Its astringency carries it secondarily into the tissue-firming/consolidation track (haemostasis, wound repair, mucosal tone) and its mild Medohara action into metabolic-cardiac overlap. Classically potentiated by milk/ghee anupāna, which buffers its dryness and delivers it to *rasa* and *rakta* dhātus and thence to the heart.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed, *Terminalia arjuna* is among the better-studied Ayurvedic cardiac herbs, though the human trial base is small and methodologically limited — evidence grade **suggestive but not established**.

- Dwivedi & Chopra (1999/2014 era) and the classical review: Miller (1999), *Alternative Medicine Review*, summarises the clinical use of *T. arjuna* bark as a cardiac tonic with reported benefit in coronary artery disease, heart failure, and possibly hypercholesterolaemia, and catalogues the saponin/flavonoid/OPC chemistry. PMID 10608917.
- **Chronic stable angina — systematic review/meta-analysis:** Kaur et al. (2014), *Cardiology Research and Practice*, systematically reviewed the trial literature and found the available studies to be of **poor methodological quality**, with **no statistically significant benefit** demonstrable on pooled outcomes — concluding the evidence is currently **insufficient** to endorse or reject *T. arjuna* in chronic stable angina, and calling for well-controlled multicentre trials. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/281483)
- **Long-term safety (pharmacovigilance):** Dwivedi, Chopra & Bhandari (2020), *Ayu*, followed chronic coronary-artery-disease patients given *T. arjuna* bark-extract powder (500 mg thrice daily) as an adjunct to conventional therapy for up to ~4.75 years and reported it **safe** — no significant derangement of haemogram, liver, or kidney function versus control, with only minor GI effects (gastritis, constipation). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/ayu.AYU_114_18)

The honest synthesis: Arjuna has a strong traditional cardiac reputation and a plausible (saponin-inotropic, antioxidant OPC) mechanism, a reassuring long-term safety signal as an *adjunct*, but the controlled-efficacy evidence in humans is weak and inconclusive. It should be described as a **traditional cardiac tonic with supportive but not confirmatory clinical data**, used adjunctively and never as a replacement for evidence-based cardiac care. `[USER: verify — do not overstate cardiac efficacy]`

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa:** Kaṣāya (predominant), Tikta
- **Vīrya:** Śīta (cooling)
- **Vipāka:** Kaṭu
- **Guṇa:** Laghu, Rūkṣa
- **Doṣa action:** Reduces Kapha and Pitta; may aggravate Vāta if used dry/unbalanced
- **Karma:** **Hṛdya** (cardiac tonic — the signature action), Raktastambhaka (haemostatic), Vraṇaropaṇa (wound-healing), Medohara (lipid/fat-reducing), Grāhī/Saṅgrāhī (astringent-binding), Kṣatakṣīṇahara (restorative in wasting/chest injury), Bhagnasandhānakara (aids fracture union)
- **Srotas:** Rasavaha & Raktavaha (circulatory — primary), Māṃsavaha, Medovaha
- **Prabhāva:** The specific cardiotonic effect on the heart muscle — the *hṛdya* prabhāva that sets Arjuna apart from other Combretaceae astringents (compare Dhava, same family)

### Contraindications

- Drying/astringent — caution in Vāta constitutions, dryness, constipation; classically buffered with milk/ghee.
- **Cardiac patients must not self-treat.** May interact additively with cardiac glycosides, antihypertensives, antiplatelet/anticoagulant, and lipid-lowering drugs — use only with prescriber knowledge and monitoring. `[USER: verify]`
- Pregnancy and lactation safety not established — avoid without supervision. `[USER: verify]`
- Not a substitute for emergency care in acute coronary events.
