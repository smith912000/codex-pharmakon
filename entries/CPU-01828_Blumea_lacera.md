# Blumea lacera — Bhu-kadamba (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3)

## *Blumea lacera, the corrected spelling of the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints as "Blumnia  lacera." (line 31471) against the Sanskrit name Bhu-kadamba (OCR "BhuKadamba", line 31416). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The chapters retrieved print the Sanskrit name in a longer spelling (Bhumi-Kadamba) and the translator's chapter notes give no Latin for it, so the tie between the chapter drug and the appendix Latin is a reading of the Codex and is flagged below.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` (row 437) gives the name as "BhuKadamba", the locus "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31416; id line 31471)" and the Latin "Blumnia  lacera.". The extraction merged the printed form "Blumnia lacera" into the spine binomial *Blumea lacera*; the double n is an OCR or printer's slip for the genus *Blumea* and the Codex reads it so.

**The pairing, checked by counting.** The Sanskrit run from "Bhringa-ra'ja" (line 31414) to "Briddha daraka" (line 31433) has fifteen entries: Bhringa-raja, Bhuchampaka, **Bhu-kadamba**, Bhumi-kushmanda, Bhumyamalaki, Bhu-nimba, Bhurjapatra, Bhuta-keshi, Bhu-trina, Bilva, Bimbika, Brahmadandi, Brahma-yashtika, Brahmi and Briddha-daraka. The Latin run from "Wedelia Calendulacea ; Eclipta Erecta" (line 31467) to "Argyreita speciosa" (line 31497) also has fifteen, counting the Herpestis line that the OCR splits across lines 31493 to 31495 as one: Wedelia/Eclipta, "Kemferia Rotunda", **"Blumnia lacera"**, "Batatas paniculata ; Ipomoea Diijilata", "Phyallanthus Niruri", "Swertia Cherata", "Betula Bhojapattra", "Corydalis Govoniana", "Andropogon Schoenanthus", "Aegle Marmellos", "Coccinia Indica", "Mimosa pudica", "Clerodendron Siphonanthus", "Siphonanthus Indica ; Herpestis Mouniera" and "Argyreita speciosa". Bhu-kadamba is the third entry in each run. The neighbours fit: Bhringa-raja with Wedelia/Eclipta, Bhuchampaka with *Kaempferia rotunda*, Bhumyamalaki with *Phyllanthus niruri*, Bhu-nimba with *Swertia*, Bhurjapatra with *Betula* and Bilva with *Aegle marmelos*. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* The appendix spelling Bhu-kadamba does not occur in the chapters. A search of all chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) finds a name Bhumi-Kadamba and a name Mahi-Kadamba, and the plain tree name Kadamba. The Codex reads Bhu- and Bhumi- as spellings of one name (Bhumi being the full word that Bhu- shortens) and notes that this is its reading, not the translator's statement. [USER: verify]

*Bhumi-Kadamba in a wound oil (Chikitsa ch.2).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch02.txt` lines 524 to 534, in the chapter on "recent or traumatic wounds or sores" (Sadyo-vrana), list drugs in which an oil is cooked, among them "Saindhava salt, Tila, Bhumi-Kadamba, Suvaha, Shukakhya, Langalakva, Naipali ..." and end: "or with as many of them as would be available, should be used for the purification (of a malignant sore or ulcer)". A medicated ghrita cooked with the same drugs as Kalka is allowed "if found applicable".

*Kadamba-pushpi (Bhumi-Kadamba) in a ghrita for jaundice (Uttara ch.44).* `SS_Uttara_ch44.txt` lines 151 to 155 read: "Clarified butter duly cooked with the Kalka and decoction of the two kinds of Vrihati, Rajani, Shukakhya (Shuka-shimbi), Kakadani, Kakamachi, Addri-vimbi and Kadamba-pushpi (Bhumi-Kadamba) should be taken as a remedy for Pandu-roga." The chapter heading reads "Jaundice, etc. (Pandu-roga Pratishedha)". The bracket "(Bhumi-Kadamba)" is the translator's, and it is the only gloss in the chapters that ties the flower name Kadamba-pushpi to this name.

*Mahi-Kadamba (Chikitsa ch.17; Uttara ch.41).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt` lines 122 to 125 list "Mahi-Kadamba" among drugs for a plaster in the Kaphaja type of Visarpa. `SS_Uttara_ch41.txt` lines 290 to 296 list it among drugs for a ghrita "efficacious in cases of Phthisis, Ascites and Vataja-Prameha". Mahi is a different word from Bhumi, and the text does not say the two names are one drug; the Codex lists the passages only as nearby evidence. The tree Kadamba, which the same chapters use in other lists (for example in the Nyagrodhadi group, Sutra ch.38, and in Chikitsa ch.25), has its own appendix line ("Kadamba" at line 31821 against "Anthocephalus Kadamba." at line 31873) and is not this plant.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Bhu-kadamba, Bhumi-Kadamba, Mahi-Kadamba and Kadamba-pushpi found nothing.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Blumea* and for Bhu-kadamba found no entry on this plant. **I_1368** (*Batatas paniculata*), **I_1383** (*Mimosa pudica*) and **I_1389** (*Amaranthus spinosus*) each count the same appendix block (lines 31414 to 31497) for their own names. **I_391** (*Kadamba*, *Neolamarckia cadamba*) covers the tree. Nothing is merged. A separate spine row, CPU-01832 *Corydalis govoniana* (Bhuta-keshi, line 31421), sits two places further along the same block. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Blumea* is a genus of the daisy family (Asteraceae). Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. If the Bhumi-Kadamba of the chapters is this plant (a reading, not a text statement), Sushruta uses it as one ingredient among many in a wound-cleansing oil and in a ghrita for Pandu-roga. In each list the text sets no action of its own against it. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters give two preparations that contain the name: an oil cooked with the listed drugs for a malignant sore (Chikitsa ch.2), and a ghrita cooked with the Kalka and decoction of the listed drugs for Pandu-roga (Uttara ch.44). No quantity of Bhumi-Kadamba is given in either passage. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
