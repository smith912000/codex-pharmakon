# Clematis montana — dbyi.mong, E mong (rGyud-bzhi); "Mountain clematis"

## *Clematis montana, the first of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for dbyi.mong, and the one the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary prints* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** **Named only by the Tibetan texts in this row.** No other text read for this entry names the plant under this name. Both loci are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one: Clark's own preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance", and the row's binomial is the first of two on the line. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed names (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

*Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1120).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, within the section headed "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (line 997). The entry reads: "dbyi.mong: Clematis montana: Buch-Ham: Clematis alpina (white type): [has fourteen names, for example a.za.mo, rlung.ni.brla.reng.dus, rtsa.bya, etc., and is of black and white types. It is a climbing (shrub) with a coarse, dark-green leaf and a dull, white bell-shaped flower. It has hot and slightly sweet taste and removes tumors. The white, yellow-tinted Clematis is asserted to be khra.wo and is most inferior in quality. The black type, also known as za.byed.nag.po.khar, has a dark slightly coarse leaf and a dark flower]. Ranunculus and Clematis both have the same use as Anemone." The square brackets are as printed. So the text names a drug with fourteen names and two types, a black and a white. It describes the climbing shrub, gives its taste (hot, slightly sweet) and one action (it removes tumors), ranks a white yellow-tinted kind as "most inferior in quality", and refers its use to that of Anemone.

The Anemone it is referred to is the entry just above (line 1118, *srub.ka*): "Anemone has hot and bitter tastes], halts necrosis, increases digestive heat, drains lymph [accumulations and removes cold tumors]". The next entry, Ranunculus (*lche.tsha*, line 1119), "removes cold tumors, diphtheria and quinsy, etc., and dries oedema fluid". Clark's other entries use "Clematis" as a comparison plant for recognition: the climber *snyi.wa* is "similar to Clematis" (line 1061), as is *ba.le.ka* (line 976) and the trunk of the vine *rgun.hbrum* (line 905). Clark's name index (line 1775) also prints *a.za.mo*, one of the fourteen names.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 (glossary and Explanatory Tantra).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`. The Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients prints a one-row entry under the letter E: the phonetic name "E mong" (line 5265), the Tibetan "dbyi mong" (line 5268), the scientific name "Clematis Montana" (line 5270) and the English "Mountain clematis" (line 5272). The pairing is therefore not positional. The binomial printed here is the same as the first of Clark's two, and the second (*Clematis alpina*) does not appear in the glossary. Two statements of use follow. Line 3493: "Che tsha and E mong have the same efficacy as the Subka", where the same sentence gives Subka's action as "heals necrosis, generates heat and drains chhuser". Lines 3621 to 3627: E mong is named in a list ending "are the group of medicines that cure cold disorders associated with baekan". The text's index (line 7362) gives "E mong 211, 221". The terms *chhuser* and *baekan* are the translation's own and were not resolved further.

**Overlap.** *Clematis alpina*, Clark's second binomial, also stands on the translators' SUGGESTED line of Dioscorides chapter 4-7b *KLEMATIS ETERA* (`Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, line 8070). That is a translators' suggestion for a Greek chapter, not a statement that the two texts mean the same plant, and no Dioscorides tag is added here. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Plants of the genus *Clematis* belong to the buttercup family (Ranunculaceae), many members of which are generally known to be irritant when fresh. The constituents of the plant Clark and the glossary name were not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a hot, slightly sweet climbing shrub said to remove tumors, with the same use as Anemone, and a drug that cures cold disorders associated with *baekan*. These are the texts' own claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol for tumors, and the botanical identity is uncertain.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources read give no preparation and no quantity for *dbyi.mong*. Clark (line 1120) gives the plant's description, taste and use. The Men-Tsee-Khang text (lines 3493 and 3621 to 3627) gives only efficacy and group. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The identity of the plant is itself uncertain, so a modern study of one *Clematis* species would not bear on the Tibetan drug.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Clark line 1120 prints "hot and slightly sweet taste", and for the white yellow-tinted kind "most inferior in quality". No power (cool or warm) is printed for the drug. The Men-Tsee-Khang text places E mong in the group that cures cold disorders associated with *baekan* (lines 3621 to 3627) and gives it the efficacy of Subka (line 3493). No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Quintessence Tantra's materia medica classes the plant by its taste and by what it does: a hot, slightly sweet climbing shrub that removes tumors, with the use of Anemone. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. Nothing read places *dbyi.mong* in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or any practice. Its framing is therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a hot-tasting medicine for tumors and cold *baekan* disorders in the Tibetan texts and nothing more can be said from them. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. Fresh plants of the buttercup family can irritate skin and mouth, and tumors need medical care. The botanical identity is uncertain (two binomials in Clark), so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No safety information for pregnancy was read.
