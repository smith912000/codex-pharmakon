# Pagoda tree fruit — Huai Shi 槐实 (Shennong bencao jing)

## *Styphnolobium japonicum (L.) Schott (synonym Sophora japonica L.), the Chinese pagoda tree, here its fruit (the pod with seeds); the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence, since the Sun Xingyan note's own tree glosses (large leaves, leaves that fold by day) fit a Sophora-type tree* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk.** The only text read for this entry is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. No other text on disk was found naming this tree under Styphnolobium or *Sophora japonica*. The *Sophora* entries on the spine (I_1358 *Sophora flavescens*, I_1359 *S. hookeriana*, I_1360 *S. moorcroftiana*) are other species and other traditions.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 槐实, "pagoda-tree fruit", in the upper classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 槐实 *huai shi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1163). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味苦，寒 ("bitter, cold"); 主五内邪气热 ("governs heat of evil qi within the five interiors"), 止涎唾 ("stops drooling and spittle"), 补绝伤 ("mends severed wounds"), 五痔 ("the five haemorrhoids"), 火创 ("fire sores"), 妇人乳瘕 ("breast lumps of women") and 子藏急痛 ("acute pain of the womb"); habitat 生平泽 ("grows on level marshy ground"). There is no 久服 clause, no alias and no preparation in the paragraph.

The Mingyi bielu layer adds only the origin: 生河南, "grows in Henan" (line 1165).

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 1167) cites the Shuowen (槐，木也), the Erya, and Guo Pu on it. The Erya line, with one character missing in the transcription, says the huai has large leaves and is dark; Guo Pu says the huai tree with large, dark leaves has a special name. A second Erya line is about the "palace-guarding huai" (守宫槐), whose leaves "fold by day and spread out by night" (昼聂宵炕), and Guo Pu glosses it the same way. These are descriptions of the tree and not of the fruit, and they are the commentators' and not the Shennong text's.

A separate appendix at the end of the volume lists drug pairings (诸药制使). A line there reads 槐子 景天为使, "huai seed: jingtian is its envoy" (line 4495). It is presumably the same drug under a variant name, but the appendix is later than the Shennong paragraph and gives no further statement. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the Chinese pagoda tree is a legume (Fabaceae); its flower buds are the best-known commercial source of the flavonoid rutin, and the fruit contains flavonoids and related compounds. [USER: verify for the fruit]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, cold fruit for heat in the interior, drooling, wounds, haemorrhoids, fire sores, breast lumps and womb pain, in its own categories ("the five interiors", "severed wounds"). The Codex does not translate these into modern diagnoses and gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The paragraph gives the part (the fruit, 实) and no preparation. The preface (line 4121) says medicines differ in suitability for pills, powders, decoction, wine or paste, with no rule for this one. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. The tree is widely planted; rutin is a known flavonoid obtained from its flower buds. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The classic's categories ("evil qi", "severed wounds") are not modern diagnoses, so no modern finding can be set beside them.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味苦，寒, "bitter, cold" (line 1163). No other quality is printed for this fruit in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The paragraph for 槐实 reads 味苦，寒 and is filed in the upper classic (上经), whose preface (line 43) says upper medicines "nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic, and may be taken much and long" (主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人). On the grading rule used for this series, placement in the upper classic is itself the ground for (a). The paragraph itself, however, has no 久服 ("taken long") clause and promises no lightening of the body, no freedom from ageing and no extension of years. It is an indication list for heat, wounds and gynaecological complaints. The attestation is of the upper-classic placement; the drug's own clause is absent, and the Codex does not claim a promise that this paragraph does not make. The Mingyi bielu supplies only the place of origin. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 槐实 *huai shi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1163)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the paragraph gives a cultivator a practice. The fruit is an upper-classic drug with a therapeutic indication list and no long-use promise, so the classic's own wording gives no basis for taking it as a tonic. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy:** the paragraph names womb pain among its uses and says nothing about pregnancy; no safety information was read. [USER: verify]
- **Identity:** other *Sophora* species (for example *S. flavescens*, I_1358) are different drugs; do not substitute.
- **Wounds and haemorrhoids:** the uses named are historical record only and need medical assessment.
- **Quantities:** no dose or duration is given.
