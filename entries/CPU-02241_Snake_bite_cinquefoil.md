# Snake-bite cinquefoil — 蛇含 *shehan* (head printed 蛇合), "She Han"; also 蛇衔 *shexian*

## *A herb of the Shennong lower classic, identified in modern works as Potentilla kleiniana Wight & Arn. (a cinquefoil, Rosaceae); the identification is ours, of medium confidence, and the old sources give no description of the plant, while Sun Xingyan's own note points the name to a quite different plant* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

*She han* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The spine row it replaces was the placeholder "Potentilla sp.", added on 13 October from Yang's English translation at genus level.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files the entry in the lower classic (卷三 下经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3229, 味 paragraph; name line 3225). The head is printed 蛇合, with a note below it (line 3227): "the original note says 合 is the character 含" (原注云，合是含字). The paragraph reads: 味苦，微寒。主惊痫寒热邪气，除热，金创，疽痔鼠[瘘]，恶创，头疡。一名蛇衔。生山谷。In English: "Flavour bitter, slightly cold. Governs fright-epilepsy (惊痫), cold-and-heat evil qi; it removes heat; [and] incised wounds (金创), flat abscess (疽), haemorrhoids, mouse-fistula (鼠[瘘]; one glyph is lost in the file, and the reading follows Yang's "mouse fistulas"), malign sores (恶创) and head sores (头疡). Another name is She Xian ('snake holds'). It grows in mountains and valleys." There is no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement.

*Wu Pu layer.* None.

**Mingyi bielu layer** (《名医》, later additions; line 3231). "Grows in Yizhou. Gathered in the eighth month and dried in the shade" (生益州。八月采，阴干). No part is named.

**Sun Xingyan's note** (按, line 3233). Sun does not describe the plant. He relays an identification: the *Bencao tujing* (the Song illustrated materia medica) says "some say it is *quepiao* (雀瓢), which is another name of 萝摩 (*luomo*)", and he follows the argument through Lu Ji: 芄兰 (*wanlan*) is called *luomo*, and in Youzhou it is called *quepiao*; therefore, Sun says, this is the *Erya*'s [lost glyph], *wanlan*. He adds that the *Tang bencao* lists 萝摩 as a separate entry, "which is wrong" (非), and sends the reader on to the entry 女青. So Sun's note equates 蛇含 with a plant of the 萝摩 type. The Codex does not follow this and keeps the later standard identification, with the doubt stated.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 3133) prints the entry under the romanisation "She Quan" with the Latin gloss *Herba Potentillae Kleinianae*: "bitter and slightly cold. It mainly treats fright epilepsy, cold and heat, and evil qi. It eliminates heat and [is good for] incised wounds, flat abscesses, hemorrhoids, mouse fistulas, malign sores, and head sores. Its other name is She Xin (Snakebite). It grows in mountains and valleys." The text agrees with Sun's paragraph. Yang places the entry under his "Herbs: Middle Class" heading (line 3093), where Sun's edition has it in the lower classic. The two editions therefore disagree on the grade, and Sun's is followed here because the entry is read in his text. Yang's romanisations "She Quan" and "She Xin" differ from Sun's *she han* and *she xian*; the Codex takes the name from Sun's text and records Yang's forms here.

### Active Components

No analytical source was retrieved for this plant, and no constituent is asserted. As a general fact, *Potentilla* is a genus of the rose family (Rosaceae), many of whose species are rich in tannins. That is a statement about the genus and not a finding about this drug, whose identity rests on a later reading. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, slightly cold herb for fright-epilepsy and fevers, for incised wounds, abscesses, haemorrhoids, fistulas and sores of the head. The Codex gives no protocol and does not turn these terms into modern diagnoses. Open wounds, abscesses and seizures need medical care.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation-related statement is the Mingyi layer's: gathered in the eighth month and dried in the shade (line 3231). No part, quantity, frequency, duration, or form (decoction, powder, poultice) is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic (下经), which the preface describes as "to treat disease, in correspondence with earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long" (下药...主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服; line 2935). Its 味 paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 蛇含 *shehan*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3229). Its status is therapeutic only. Yang's edition lists it under the middle class (Yang line 3133 under the heading at line 3093), which would frame it as a drug that "nourishes the nature" and is chosen to fit; Sun's placement is followed here, and neither edition gives the entry a cultivation promise. The later layers add none.

**Practitioner's note.** There is no cultivation practice here. This is a lower-class record of a herb for wounds, abscesses and fits, with no description of the plant, no dose, and a grade that two editions give differently.

### Contraindications

- The classic does not warn for this herb, but the preface calls lower-class drugs mostly toxic and not for long taking (line 2935). The grade as printed in Sun's edition is a caution in itself.
- **Identity.** The name has been read as a plant of the 萝摩 type by Sun's authorities, and as a cinquefoil by the later standard works. Do not gather or use a plant on this entry.
- **Conditions named.** Seizures, abscesses, haemorrhoids and non-healing wounds need medical care.
