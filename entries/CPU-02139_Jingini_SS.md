### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The locus is Sutrasthana chapter 38, "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (Dravya-Sangrahaniya-madhyaryam), in Bhishagratna's translation: `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15433, and `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 92.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS).** The chapter opens by saying that "these drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)". Jingini is printed in the Rodhrardi (the text also spells it Rodhradi) group: Rodhra, Savararodhra, Palasha, Kutannata, Ashoka, Phanji, Katphala, Elabaluka, Sallaki, Jingini, Kadamva, Sala and Kadali (Vol. I lines 15430-15433). The Metrical Texts read: "The group is antidotal to the deranged Kapham and fat, is astringent in its properties, removes vaginal and uterine disorders, neutralises the effects of poison (anti toxic) and acts as a styptic and purifying agent in a case of ulcer and arrests all secretions and excretions of the body" (ch.38 file lines 95-100). That statement is made of the group as a whole, and the text does not say which member does what. The chapter closes with a rule of use (ch.38 file line 558): the physician should prepare plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, ghritas or potions "according to the exigencies of each individual case", and "only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group ... or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety" are to be used as the physician decides. That is the only guidance on how a member of a group is taken.

A search of Bhishagratna Volumes I to III and of the chapter files found the name only here. The remark that it is probably a tree rests only on the company it keeps in the list. [USER: verify]
