# Hermann's Tortoise Shell / Gui Jia — Tortoise Plastron (龜甲 Guī Jiǎ) (project Shennong list) / Kurma (tortoise) (Bhishagratna)
## *The shell, plastron and other parts of a tortoise or turtle; the spine's binomial Testudo hermanni (Hermann's tortoise) is the spine's own identification and no text on disk prints it, and the earlier Shennong row of the same name carried Chinemys reevesii* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** CM, SBJ, SS

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The project's list `_sources/shennong.csv` has one row for it, line 233, "Tortoise Plastron (龜甲 Guī Jiǎ)", with no binomial and no class. Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) lists "Animals: Superior Class", "Middle Class" and "Inferior Class" in its contents (lines 770-776), but the text layer is readable only to PDF p.104 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/README.md`), so the monograph could not be read and nothing of what the Chinese text says of it was seen. The earlier spine row for the same drug (CPU-00272, now merged into this one) carried the note "middle"; that is the spine's note and no printed page read confirms it. [USER: verify]

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Kurma* (tortoise) is in the animal-food chapter, *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`). Under the *Padina* species, "Animals such as, the tortoise, alligator, crab, black crab, porpoise, etc., belong to this species" (lines 764-765). The metrical text that follows says "The flesh of animals of the Shankha and Kurma orders is sweet in taste and digestion, cooling in its potency, demulcent, and beneficial to stool and the Pittam. It destroys the deranged Vayu and produces Kapham" (lines 775-780). Among the best of each kind, "the flesh of the ... Varmi and Kurma (tortoise) should be regarded as the best of those of all other animals respectively belonging to their own species" (lines 2160-2164). Other parts are used elsewhere. In *Chikitsasthana* ch.18, on glandular swellings and *Apachi*, "The ashes (Masi) of the skins of a peacock, cow, lizard (Godha) and snake and of tortoise shells should be dusted (over an Apachi after lubricating it) with the (expressed) oil of the Ingudi" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt` lines 319-322). In *Uttarasthana* ch.17 a collyrium powder is "soaked again in the bile of a tortoise or of a Rohita fish and dried and reduced to powder", called the *Churna-anjana*, for pacifying Pitta (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch17.txt` lines 97-100). In *Uttarasthana* ch.45, on haemorrhage (*Rakta-Pitta*), "soup of the meat of pigeons, S'ankha (conch) and tortoise" is given as diet (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch45.txt` lines 131-133). In the aphrodisiac chapter, *Chikitsasthana* ch.26, "The eggs of a tortoise, of an alligator, or of a crab, or the semen of a male buffalo, of a he-ass, or of a he-goat should be similarly taken for the same purpose" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` lines 133-135).

### Active Components

A tortoise shell or turtle plastron is bone covered by horny plates. It is mainly calcium phosphate and collagen, with keratin in the outer plates. Bile contains bile acids and bilirubin. Tortoise eggs are an egg of the usual kind, mainly protein, fat and water. The sources read here give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. In the Sanskrit text the animal is food (flesh, listed among the best of its kind), a source of burnt shell ash for a glandular swelling, bile for a collyrium, and eggs in one aphrodisiac recipe. The Chinese and Unani attestations are a list row and a spine row, with nothing read of what they say. Tortoises and turtles are widely protected animals and several are endangered, shell products are a conservation concern, and nothing here supports using any part. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation.

### Preparation & Dosing

SS: the ashes of tortoise shell are dusted over an *Apachi* after the swelling has been lubricated with expressed *Ingudi* oil, together with medicated oils and *Vairechanika* fumes and a stated diet (Chikitsa ch.18 lines 319-324); the bile is used to soak and dry a powdered collyrium (Uttara ch.17); the flesh is taken as soup (Uttara ch.45); the eggs are taken as an aphrodisiac "similarly" to the preceding recipe (Chikitsa ch.26). No quantity is given in any of these lines. SBJ: not read. CM: nothing. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Burnt shell is largely mineral ash (calcium phosphate and carbonate) and has no established effect on a glandular swelling; the traditional claims were not tested in any study retrieved. Wild-caught tortoise material can carry bacteria such as Salmonella, as reptiles in general do. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The tortoise is claimed by the animal-food chapter (*Sutrasthana* ch.46) and by wound, eye and bleeding chapters for the shell ash, bile and flesh. Its eggs are named in the aphrodisiac chapter, *Chikitsasthana* ch.26, which Bhishagratna heads as the chapter on "increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons (Kshina-Valiyam Vaji-Karana)" (lines 3-5); that places the eggs in a *vajikarana* chapter but says nothing of the shell, which is what this entry is named for, so no status is assigned to the shell. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is printed for any tortoise part in the lines read, and *sattva*/*rajas*/*tamas* are *Samkhya* categories, not Sushruta's.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here for a cultivator to take. The Sanskrit text records the animal as food and as an ingredient of wound, eye and aphrodisiac recipes; none of it is a long-term practice, and the shell, the part the entry is named for, has only an ash-for-swelling use. The Chinese and Unani attestations could not be read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Tortoises and turtles are protected in many places, and trade in wild shell and in live animals is restricted by wildlife law; the status of Hermann's tortoise has not been checked here. [USER: verify] Wild reptile material can carry Salmonella. Burnt shell dusted on a swelling is not sterile. Eggs, bile and shell should not be taken as medicine on the basis of these sources. Hermann's tortoise is a European species, whereas the Chinese drug is a plastron from East Asian freshwater turtles in the older spine row; the merge into one entry does not show that the Sanskrit *Kurma* is the same animal.
