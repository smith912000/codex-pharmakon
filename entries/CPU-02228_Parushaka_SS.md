# Parushaka [SS] — Parushaka, Parushakam (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna tr.)

## *A fruit-bearing tree whose fruit is eaten raw or ripe and whose juice is made into a drink; the identification as *Grewia asiatica* L. (phalsa) is ours, from the standard Ayurvedic identification of the name, and is of medium confidence: Bhishagratna's English does not give a botanical name for it* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Parushaka [SS]"; this entry replaces it. The text is Bhishagratna's English, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` (and Vols. 2 and 3 for the clinical uses).

**Ayurveda (SS).** The gana. Sutrasthana ch. 38 (Vol. I line 15651): "The Parushaka'di Group:— The group known as the Parushakadi Gana consists of Parushaka, Drāksha, Kat-phala, Dadima, Rajadana, Kataka-phala, Shaka-phala and Triphala." Its stated effect (line 15656): "It subdues the deranged Vayu, allays thirst, acts as a cordial, increases one's relish for food and cures the diseased or abnormal components of urine or its defects."

The fruit. Sutrasthana ch. 46 (Vol. I line 20823): "Raw or unripe Parushaka fruit is extremly acid in taste with a shade of the sweet, leaves an astringent after-taste, is light of digestion, generates the Pittam, and subdues the Kapham; while in its ripe state it is sweet in taste, subdues the Vayu and Pittam, is sweet in digestion and cooling in its potency and proves curative in cases of haemoptysis." In the ranking of foods in ch. 46 (line 21852) "Pugaphalam and Parushakam are the best of all astringent fruit", and among fruits (line 21835) "Kharjura, Parushaka, Rajadana and Matulunga should be considered as the most commendable and efficacious." In the drinks passage (line 22211): "Water potion flavoured with the (expressed juice of) Kola or Parushaka is agreeable and long retained in the stomach in an undigested state." A translator's footnote (line 22165) gives a Rasala as made of "sugar, Saindhava salt, tamarind, Sarjikshara, Parushaka and the expressed juice of Jambuline fruits".

Clinical uses I found. The branches of a Parushaka tree are named, with Pilu, Nimba and Vadari, for fanning a patient after surgery (Vol. II line 10737). A decoction of Draksha and of Parushaka is used as a gargle in the Pittaja type of Rohini (Vol. II line 20787). It is one of the drugs in a compound for Kaphaja fever (Vol. III line 8413), is named among cooling foods "grapes, dates and aquatic fruits" (Vol. III line 11111), and its expressed juice is a vehicle in a Kapittha preparation (Vol. III line 12442). It appears in compounds at Vol. II lines 27420 and 27443. I did not read these in full chapter context. [USER: verify.]

### Active Components

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The group: deranged Vayu, thirst, relish for food, abnormal urine. The fruit: ripe, cooling and curative in haemoptysis; unripe, acid and Pitta-raising.
- **What the text does not say.** It gives no dose and no preparation of the fruit beyond the juice-in-water drink.

The Codex does not translate the text's terms into modern diagnoses and gives no protocol. The ordinary fact that a sour fruit may be eaten or drunk as juice is not a recommendation to gather an unidentified fruit.

### Preparation & Dosing

Juice of Parushaka in water as a drink (line 22211); a decoction as a gargle (Vol. II line 20787). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Grewia asiatica* is a small tree of South Asia grown for its dark, sour-sweet fruit, which is eaten fresh and made into drinks. The identification is ours and the text's tastes and potencies are the tradition's own and are not mapped onto measurable properties. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** Unripe fruit: very acid with a trace of sweet, astringent after-taste, light of digestion, raises Pitta, subdues Kapha. Ripe fruit: sweet in taste, sweet in digestion, cooling in potency, subdues Vayu and Pitta (Vol. I line 20823). The gana as a whole subdues Vayu (line 15656). Sushruta's rasa, virya and vipaka are given in these words in Bhishagratna's English only.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug sits in the Parushakadi gana of Sutrasthana ch. 38 and in the food and drink chapter, with a therapeutic and dietetic account. No rasayana or vajikarana assignment for it was found in the passages read, and *ojas* is not mentioned for it, so the status is unattested. "Most commendable" and "best of all astringent fruit" (line 21852) rank it as a food; the ranking is not a cultivation grade.

**Practitioner's note.** A sour-sweet fruit and cooling drink in the Sushruta scheme, used for thirst and for Pitta and Vata complaints. Nothing here makes it a cultivation substance.

### Contraindications

- **Unripe fruit.** The text itself says the raw fruit raises Pitta.
- **Identity.** The species is our identification, of medium confidence; do not rely on it. [USER: verify]
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Haemoptysis, fever, urinary disorders.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
