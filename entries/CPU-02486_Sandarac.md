# Sandarac — سندروس (*sandarūs*, as printed)

## *Sandarac: a brittle pale resin from a tree; the modern sandarac is the resin of Tetraclinis articulata (identification modern, uncertain: see doubt below)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Sandarac (as a resin) is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. The locus is *Canon* Book II, entry *sandarūs* (سندروس), Arabic text line 15721 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; the entry runs to line 15735 (the heading is spelt الماهبة at line 15722 in the printed text).

**Name collision.** *Dioscorides* 5-122 (*sandarache*) is a red mineral found with arsenic and is already filed as II_83 (Volume II). The Canon's drug is a gum, so the two are not the same material, whatever the shared Greek-derived name.

**Dioscorides quoted (CM).** Avicenna opens: "Dioscorides said: it is the gum of a tree found in the land of the Arabs and the land of India, in which is a slight likeness to myrrh, of repellent taste; people fumigate with it, and clothes are fumigated with it together with myrrh and *mayʿa*, and these gums are cooked on fire and become *sandarūs*" (قال ديسقوريدوس: هو صمغ شجرة تكون في بلاد العرب وبلاد الهند ...; lines 15722-15725). The English Dioscorides on disk has only *sandarache* as a mineral (5-122) and I did not look further for a tree-gum chapter, so the attribution is Avicenna's and is not checked here. [USER: verify]

**What the text says (CM).** Temperament: "hot and dry in the second degree" (line 15725). It is astringent and "holds the blood" (يحبس الدم); "wrestlers use it to be light and strong and not to pant" (ويستعمله المصارعون ليخفوا ويقوا ولا يبهروا; lines 15725-15726). Cosmetic: it has "a very emaciating power if three-quarters of a dirham is drunk daily with *sikanjabīn* (oxymel)" (lines 15726-15727). It dries fistulas when used as a fumigation (line 15727). Head: its smoke prevents catarrhs and it is of great benefit in quieting toothache, "nothing equals it", and it corrects the gums (lines 15728-15729). Chest: it helps palpitation "like *kahrubāʾ* (amber)", prevents bleeding, and prevents moist asthma by drying, which is why wrestlers use it (lines 15729-15731). Eye: it clears marks in the eye if dissolved in wine and used as kohl (lines 15731-15732). Excretory: good for chronic diarrhoea; its smoke helps haemorrhoids (lines 15734-15735). One line on the *maṭḥūlūn* (line 15734) is garbled in the OpenITI text and is not used.

**Identification.** The English name is ours and the modern one. The resin of *Tetraclinis articulata* (a north-west African tree) is the sandarac of trade; Avicenna, quoting Dioscorides, places the tree in Arabia and India. That does not match, so the identification is an identity doubt (low to moderate).

### Active Components

Sandarac resin is rich in diterpene resin acids (among them sandaracopimaric acid, named for it). That is general chemistry; the Canon names no constituent.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The entry records fumigation, a tooth remedy, an astringent for blood and bowel, and the wrestlers' use. The emaciating dose is a historical statement, not advice. No modern protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only quantity is the Canon's: three-quarters of a dirham, drunk daily with oxymel, as the emaciating dose (lines 15726-15727); the *dirham* is not converted. Other forms: smoke (fumigation) and, for the eye, dissolved in wine as kohl. No other dose is given. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General fact: sandarac is a resin used in varnishes and incense.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** Hot and dry in the second degree; astringent (*Canon* Book II, entry *sandarūs*, line 15725). No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(b) Framed.* The text records an athletic use, "wrestlers use it to be light and strong and not to pant" (line 15726), and an emaciating action. That is a regimen of the body for performance, not a cultivation status, and nothing about the vital faculty, innate heat or *ruh* is said.

**Practitioner's note.** Sandarac is a resin for smoke and tooth remedy in the text, with a wrestlers' use. Nothing is supported for long-term cultivation, and the weight-reducing dose is a warning, not a practice.

### Contraindications

No contraindication is given in the text, though the entry itself calls the daily drink strongly emaciating. Do not ingest resin of uncertain species. Fumigation smoke irritates airways. Resin allergy is general knowledge. [USER: verify]
