# Pigeon flesh — Sushruta Samhita, Kapota (pigeon, dove), Pratuda group
## *Flesh of the pigeon or dove as food and medicine; Bhishagratna's translation prints "Kapota (pigeon)" in Sutra ch.20, "the dove, pigeon" in the Pratuda list of Sutra ch.46, and "Kana Kapota (wild dove)"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurvedic / Sushruta (SS).** The primary locus is Sutrasthana ch.46, "the Chapter which treats of food and drink" (Annapana-vidhi), in Bhishagratna's translation (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, line 506). The chapter divides the flesh of animals of the dry-land (Jangala) class into eight sub-groups, naming "the Janghala, the Viscira, the Pratuda, the Guhashaya, the Prasaha, the Parnamriga, the Vileshaya, and the Gramya" (lines 403-409). The pigeon is in the Pratuda group: "Birds such as the dove, pigeon Bhringaraja, cuckoo, Koyashtica, Kulinga, the domestic Kulinga, Gokshada, Dindimanaka, Shatapatraka, Matrinindaka, Bhedashi, Shuka, Sharika, Valguli, Girisha, Alahva, Dushaka, Sugrihi, Khanjaritaka, Harita, Datyuha, etc. belong to the group known as the Pratuda" (lines 506-511). The printed line has no comma between "pigeon" and "Bhringaraja"; they look like two separate names run together, but the line is ambiguous in the file read. [USER: verify] The translation does not explain the word Pratuda.

The group's properties follow in verse ("Metrical Texts", lines 513-520): "The Pratudas live on fruit, and their flesh has a sweet and astringent taste. It generates Vayu and produces a parched condition in the organism. It is cooling in its potency and reduces the Pittam and Kapham. It suppresses the discharge of urine and reduces the quantity of stool." The text then singles out members of the group: "the flesh of the Bhedashi tends to vitiate the humours and to derange the three excrements of the body. The flesh of the Kana Kapota (wild dove) is heavy and has a palatable, saline and astringent taste. It proves beneficial in haemoptysis and is sweet of digestion. The flesh of the Kulinga is sweet, demulcent, and spermatopoietic, and increases the bodily Kapham. The flesh of the domesticated Kulinga is highly spermatopoietic, and proves curative in cases of haemoptysis" (lines 519-531). The "Kapota" of the other chapters is glossed "pigeon" by the translator; the lines read do not say whether Bhishagratna takes Kana Kapota and Kapota to be one bird.

Further loci, all in Bhishagratna's translation: Sutra ch.20, the chapter on salutary and non-salutary regimen, lists "the Kapota (pigeon)" among the "beasts and birds" whose flesh is a safe food (`SS_Sutra_ch20.txt`, line 54), and later warns that "Flesh of pigeon fried with mustard oil should not be eaten" among the unwholesome incompatible combinations (line 178). Sutra ch.45, on liquid substances, says "The fat, marrow, etc. of animals of the Pratuda and Vishkira species (doves, pigeons, etc.) reduce the bodily Kapham" (`SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`, line 1069). Uttara ch.45, on bleeding disease (Rakta-Pitta), gives "Soup of the meat of pigeons, S'anikha (conch) and tortoise" with "a profuse quantity of clarified butter" as a diet for the patient (`SS_Uttara_ch45.txt`, line 132). Uttara ch.50, on hiccup, lists the soup of the meat of "Kapota and Pdrdvata (different kinds of pigeon)" among meats to be taken lukewarm with acid fruit juice, rock salt and a fat (`SS_Uttara_ch50.txt`, line 186). Chikitsa ch.13, the chapter on diabetes (Madhu-Meha), says that a person using Shilajatu and Makshika "should refrain from taking pigeon-flesh and Kulattha pulse (during his life-time)" (`SS_Chikitsa_ch13.txt`, line 105). The text read gives no reason for this prohibition.

### Active Components

Poultry-type bird flesh is mostly water and protein, with some fat, B vitamins and iron; domestic squab (young pigeon) is a food in several cuisines. No composition specific to this entry was read, and no active principle is claimed by the sources. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced content is a set of food and diet statements in Bhishagratna's translation: the Pratuda group's flesh is cooling in potency, sweet and astringent, aggravates Vayu, and reduces Pittam and Kapham; the Kana Kapota's flesh is beneficial in haemoptysis; pigeon soup is a diet in bleeding disease and in hiccup; pigeon fried in mustard oil is an unwholesome combination; and pigeon flesh is to be avoided by one using Shilajatu and Makshika. The Codex gives no protocol. Eating wild-caught birds raises legal and food-safety issues (see Contraindications), and the conditions named (bleeding disease, hiccup, diabetes) need clinical care. ### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations named in the text read are: soup of the meat (Uttara ch.45, line 132; Uttara ch.50, line 186, taken lukewarm "with the expressed juice of acid fruits and with Saindhava and a Sneha"), flesh fried with mustard oil (named as incompatible, Sutra ch.20, line 178), and the fat and marrow (Sutra ch.45, line 1069). No quantity or duration is given for any of them. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Bird flesh is a source of protein, and undercooked poultry and game birds can carry *Salmonella*, *Campylobacter* and other pathogens. Wild pigeons and doves can carry *Chlamydia psittaci*. There is no established dietary treatment of haemoptysis, bleeding disease or diabetes by eating pigeon; those conditions need clinical care. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurvedic / Sushruta (SS).** What the text prints, in Bhishagratna's rendering, for the Pratuda group (Sutra ch.46, lines 513-520): taste (rasa) sweet and astringent; potency (virya) "cooling"; it "generates Vayu" and "produces a parched condition", "reduces the Pittam and Kapham", and checks urine and stool. For the Kana Kapota (wild dove, lines 521-528): "heavy", a "palatable, saline and astringent taste", and "sweet of digestion", which is the vipaka position in the translation's wording; the translation does not use the term *vipaka* in these lines. Sutra ch.45 (line 1069) adds that the fat and marrow of Pratuda and Vishkira animals reduce Kapham. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Pigeon flesh is claimed by the tradition as a food and diet in the lines read, but no cultivation status is attested for it. No line read places it in a rejuvenation (*rasayana*) or generative-vitality (*vajikarana*) division; nothing in the passages read names it as building *ojas*. The frame it sits inside is the chapter on food and drink, which opens with Sushruta's statement that food is the source of "the growth, strength, and healthful glow of organic beings" and of "the albuminous (Ojas) principle in their organisms" (Sutra ch.46, opening lines); that is said of food in general and is not attached to the pigeon. Within the Pratuda paragraph the text calls the flesh of the Kulinga, and of the domesticated Kulinga, "spermatopoietic" (lines 528-531), but prints no such term for the group as a whole or for the Kana Kapota, so no generative-vitality status can be read onto the pigeon. The one restriction attached to the pigeon, to avoid its flesh while using Shilajatu and Makshika (Chikitsa ch.13, line 105), is an abstention tied to two named mineral preparations; the lines read give no reason for it and it is not a cultivation status. The *sattva*/*rajas*/*tamas* food lists are Samkhya and Gita-era categories and are not found in Sushruta; none is applied here.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this. The tradition gives pigeon flesh a place in diets for specific conditions and records an avoidance, not a vital-substrate role, and the game bird itself is a legal and hygiene question. The honest use of the entry is as a record of what Sushruta says about this meat, and the abstentive answer is a legitimate one: the text's own cautions (pigeon fried in mustard oil; pigeon with Shilajatu and Makshika) are the practical content.

### Contraindications

- **Food safety:** poultry and game birds must be fully cooked; wild pigeons and doves can carry pathogens.
- **Conditions named:** bleeding disease, hiccup and diabetes need clinical care; do not rely on this entry.
- **Traditional cautions recorded in the text:** pigeon fried in mustard oil is named as an unwholesome combination (Sutra ch.20, line 178), and pigeon flesh is to be avoided with Shilajatu and Makshika (Chikitsa ch.13, line 105).
- **Legal and conservation status:** wild pigeons and doves are protected in many places; farmed squab is the usual lawful source. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and diet:** killing a bird for a medicine with no supported benefit is hard to justify; vegetarian and other dietary rules exclude it.
