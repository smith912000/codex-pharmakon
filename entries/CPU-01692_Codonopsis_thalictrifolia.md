### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT), and only by the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary.** No other text read for this entry names the plant. The Tibetan texts are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The extraction note reads: "MTK glossary scientific-name column; Tibetan name not paired (OCR column split)" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, line 310). This entry rests on one glossary line. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, glossary (lines 5435 to 5454).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients, the "L" block (the letter "M" that opens the next block is at line 5443). The glossary prints its columns in separate runs. The phonetic names are "Lalaphue Langthangtse / Lishi / Looh dhue dorje Lugchhung Lugmur Lugnyal Lugru mukpo Lugru serpo" (lines 5435 to 5437). The Tibetan transliterations are "la la phud lang thang rtse / li shi / klu bdud rdo ije lug chung lug mur lug ngal lug ru smug po lug ru ser po" (lines 5439 to 5441; "rdo ije" is the OCR's reading as printed in the file). The scientific names are "Cnidium monnieri" (line 5445), "Hyoscyamus niger L. Syzygium aromaticum Codonopsis thalictrifolia" (line 5446), "Aster strachei" (line 5447) and "Phlomis younghusbandii Corydalis meifolia Pedicularis siphonantha Pedicularis longiflora" (line 5448). Counting in order, "Looh dhue dorje" is the fourth phonetic name and "Codonopsis thalictrifolia" is the fourth scientific name. Nine phonetic names pair with nine scientific names. The English column (lines 5450 to 5454) holds seven names ("Celery", "Henbane", "Clove", "Lesser starwort", "Mountain turkeycom", "Lousewort", "Yellow lousewort"), none of which stands plainly against this drug, so the English column does not help to fix the pairing.

*Checking the count.* Three neighbours can be checked against Clark 1995 (`Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). Clark's *la.la.phud* line (896) gives *Cnidium monnieri* as its second binomial, and "Lalaphue" is first in both runs. Clark's *lug.chhung* line (1105) gives "Aster diplostaphioides ... Aster strachei ...", and "Lugchhung" stands fifth against *Aster strachei*. Clark's *lug.ngal* line (1106) gives "Corydalis, meifolia", and "Lugnyal" stands seventh against *Corydalis meifolia*. The positional count therefore holds at the first, fifth and seventh places, and by that count the fourth place, which lies between the first and the fifth, is *Codonopsis thalictrifolia*. The count rests on order and not on rows, the pairing is by column position only, and it was not printed in a single row. [USER: verify]

*What is missing.* A search of both Tibetan texts for "Looh dhue dorje", "klu bdud", "Codonopsis thalictrifolia" and "Dhue dorje" returned only the glossary lines above. The Men-Tsee-Khang running text of chapters 19, 20 and 21 (the taste lists, the efficacy passage and the compounding groups) does not print the name, and its index does not list it. Clark's materia medica run does not print the name either. No taste, power, action, part used or preparation is therefore read for this drug. The only fact the sources give is that the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary lists a drug with this Tibetan name and sets this binomial against it by position. A different *Codonopsis* is a separate Tibetan drug: Clark's *snyi.wa* (line 1061) is *Codonopsis convolvulacea*, and the Men-Tsee-Khang glossary also lists *Codonopsis convolvulacea* (line 5570). That drug is the subject of I_1054 and is not this one.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Codonopsis thalictrifolia*, "Looh dhue dorje" and "klu bdud" found no entry on this plant. I_1022 (*Aster strachei*) quotes the same glossary block and is not an entry on this plant. I_1054 (*Codonopsis convolvulacea*) is on Clark's *snyi.wa*. I_073 (*Codonopsis pilosula*) and the Chinese entries that mention *Codonopsis* in passing are on other binomials and other traditions. Nothing is merged.
