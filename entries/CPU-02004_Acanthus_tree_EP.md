# Acanthus-tree — Resin-of-Acanthus, Fruit-of-Acanthus, Thorns-of-Acanthus (Ebers papyrus, in Bryan's 1930 English)

## *A tree or thorny plant that Bryan's 1930 English renders "Acanthus-tree", with its resin, dough, fruit, nuts and thorns used in remedies; Bryan gives no Egyptian word and no binomial, so the botanical identity is not given by the source and is unresolved: the audit declined to equate it with Vachellia nilotica (CPU-00022), and so do we* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** EP

### Origin & Tradition

> **CAUTION.** This is a historical record of an ancient remedy list, including one abortifacient recipe. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should apply or swallow these preparations.

**Single-text entry.** The attestation is the *Papyrus Ebers* in C. P. Bryan's 1930 English version, as OCR'd on disk (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`). The name row is `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 42. Bryan's identifications are his English renderings only; the extract's README says "no Egyptian word and no binomial is given in these lists".

**Ebers (EP).** Chapter VI, "Plant Remedies", opens with Bryan's remark that many substances are "completely hidden from us under the guise of meaningless words" and that "of the recognizable Plants, and of their preparations, there is a not inconsiderable showing as the following list will reveal" (line 1233). The list begins (lines 1234-1236): "Acanthus-tree / Dough-of-Acanthus / Fruit-of-Acanthus Nuts-of-Acanthus Resin-of-Acanthus Thorns-of-Acanthus", after which "Aloes" follows. The Codex's acacia row (CPU-00022) also carries an Ebers attestation; whether Bryan's "Acanthus" is the same plant is not stated by Bryan, and the audit left the question open. [USER: verify.]

The forms recur in the remedies Bryan prints (the headings below are as printed; the OCR is damaged in places):

- **Stercoral masses and indigestion** (lines 1659 and 1817): "Resin-of-Acanthus" stands in lists with figs, sebesten, grapes and caraway under "ANOTHER TO DRIVE OUT THE STERCORAL MASSES IN THE BODY" and "ANOTHER READY REMEDY TO DRIVE INDIGESTION OUT OF THE BODY".
- **Tapeworm** (line 1986): under "TO DRIVE OUT THE DISEASES CAUSED BY THE TAPEWORM", a poultice of Resin-of-Acanthus with peppermint blossoms and wild lettuce.
- **Diseased toes** (line 2152): Resin-of-Acanthus and Dough-of-Acanthus among a long list with fennel, wax, incense, cyperus, wormwood, myrrh, poppy and others, "Make into one and Poultice for four days."
- **Burns** (line 2257): on the third day of the burn remedies, "Thorns-of-Acanthus, dried".
- **Abortion** (line 2516): Bryan writes that "only one remedy is given: Dates, Onions, and the Fruit-of-the-Acanthus, were crushed in a vessel with Honey, sprinkled on a cloth, and applied to the Vulva."
- **Skin** (line 2702): "REMEDY TO FREE THE FLESH": ass's milk, Resin-of-Acanthus, indigo, duat-plant, turpentine-nuts and honey; "Cook, strain, and take for four days."
- **Wounds** (line 3059): a "ball which dries the wound" of Resin-of-Acanthus, Resin-of-the-Zizyphus-Lotus, Berry-of-the-Willow and caraway, "Crush and apply."
- **Splinter and resin list** (line 3183): Resin-of-Acanthus among splinters of several trees, resins, oil and garlic, under a section Bryan headed the nervous system. [USER: verify the recipe's heading.]
- **Back remedy** (line 3756): Resin-of-Acanthus with peppermint, "Spring Plant" and mason's clay, crushed, cooked in yeast of sweet beer and smeared.
- **A growth of the atut** (line 3828): a pill of dried blood, caraway oil, onions, Resin-of-Acanthus, Fruit-and-Nut-of-Acanthus, "Seezunge-grains", copper and coal.
- **Domestic hint** (lines 4247-4250): "TO PREVENT THE PIGEON HAWK STEALING. Let a Rod of Acanthus be set up. The Person says: 'O Horus, the Hawk steals in City and Garden ...'" The text is spoken over the acanthus-rod, and a sugar-cake is put on it.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The papyrus uses the resin in internal and external remedies for the bowels, parasites, skin, wounds and the back, uses the fruit in a recipe for abortion, and uses a rod of the tree in a spoken formula against a bird. The identity of the plant is unknown, so the Codex gives no protocol and recommends none.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations above are as Bryan prints them: poulticing for four days (toes, line 2152), cooking and straining then taking for four days (skin, line 2702), a pill (atut, line 3828), a ball (wound, line 3059). The OCR'd lists do not print amounts for Acanthus in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify.]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian (EP).** *(b) Framed.* The frame is *heka*, the operative power in which a spoken formula and a material act belong to one intervention. In the single text where a ritual is attached, the acanthus-rod is set up and the spell is spoken over it to keep a hawk off the crops. That is a domestic charm, not a statement about the person's *ka*, *ba* or *akh*, and it is a use of the rod, not of the resin. The medical recipes are indication-organised: they record what was done for a complaint, and they carry no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do. The plant is unidentified and the recipes are for complaints, one of them an abortion. The charm is a record of how a text joins word and object.

### Contraindications

- **Abortifacient recipe.** The fruit is used to cause abortion in the papyrus. Nothing here is advice; seek proper obstetric care.
- **Identity.** The plant is unidentified, so nothing is known of its safety.
- **Internal use.** Several recipes are taken by mouth; the Codex advises against any use.
- **Wounds, burns, parasites.** These need proper care.
