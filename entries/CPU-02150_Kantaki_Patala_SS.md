### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk (Bhishagratna's 1907 English translation).** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from a name list (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`, which records "group list (ch.38); no identification given here"). This entry replaces it. The text was opened at the line given and the neighbouring lines read. Searches of the other texts on disk (Dioscorides, Shennong, rGyud-bzhi, Ebers) were not made under a Sanskrit spelling, so absence elsewhere is not claimed.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Kantaki Patala is printed in the A'ragvadha'di group, Sutrasthana chapter 38, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15369 (list from line 15367). The translator's sentence reads: "The drugs known as Aragvadha, Madana, Gopaghonta, Kutaja, Patha, Kantaki^ Patala, Murva, Indrayava, Saptaparna, Ximva, Kuruntaka, Dasi-kuruntaka, the two kinds of Karanja, Patola, Kirattikua, Guduchi, Chitraka, Shringshta, and Sushavi form the group known as the Aragvadhadi." The metrical text (lines 15377 to 15381) reads: "The group under discussion destroys the deranged Kaphani and the effects of poison and proves beneficial in cases of Meha (morbid discharges from the urethra), Kushtha, fever, vomiting and itching of the body and acts as a purifying (aseptic) agent in the case of an ulcer."

**A problem with the name.** The "^" after "Kantaki" is a mark of the e-text and is most likely a damaged comma. The Sushruta evidence file on disk (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/evidence.csv`, row SS-S38-002) reads the list as "Kantaki, Patala", two names. If so, "Kantaki Patala" is not one drug, and this row is a placeholder for two. The entry is kept under the name the spine gives, with this doubt stated. Which plants *Kantaki* and *Patala* are is not told by the text. [USER: verify against the Sanskrit before any further use of this row.]

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda, Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is named inside a therapeutic drug group (*gana*) of Sutrasthana chapter 38, the A'ragvadha'di group. The group's metrical text speaks of curing or subduing disorders. It does not call the group, or any member, a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) drug, and it does not mention *ojas*. The sources retrieved therefore attest a therapeutic framing only, and no cultivation status. The *sattva/rajas/tamas* lists of later popular literature are not from Sushruta and are not applied.
