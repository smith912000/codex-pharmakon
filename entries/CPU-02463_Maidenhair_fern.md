# Maidenhair fern — barshiyāwshān (برشياوشان)

## *Maidenhair fern,* Adiantum capillus-veneris *L.; the identification is the modern one, from Avicenna's description (a fine herb of water edges and wells, like fresh coriander, with red-to-black stalks and no stem, flower or bloom) and the matching description in Dioscorides, and is of fairly high confidence* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

Maidenhair fern as *barshiyāwshān* is named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The spine has Dioscorides' *adianton* (DMM 4-136), whose translator's suggestions include *Adiantum capillus-veneris* and "Adiantum foliis coriandri"; the dedupe note on the spine row says that CPU-01878 ("Adiantum foliis") is unverified and may merge. I read that chapter for comparison: it has "little leaves similar to coriander", black, thin, glistening stalks, and "no [other] stalk, flower, or seed", which is close to Avicenna's wording. The entries are not merged here, because the *Canon* is a separate text and the merge is the editor's decision. The other spine entries are different ferns (for example *Adiantum pedatum* I_1225 from Tibetan sources).

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** The *Canon* (Book II, entry *barshiyāwshān*, Arabic text line 11216) says: "a fine herb whose growing-place is the basins of water, the shores and rivers and the inside of wells; it resembles fresh coriander but its stalks are red inclining to black, with no stem, no flower and no bloom; its strength goes quickly" (lines 11217-11219). For temperament he quotes Galen, "it is moderate", and adds his own view that it "perhaps leans to a very slight heat and dryness." He cites Ibn Māsawayh at the end, on the bowel (line 11229).

### Active Components

*Adiantum capillus-veneris* contains triterpenoids (such as adiantone and related fernenes) and flavonoids, and fern fronds carry tannins. This is general botanical chemistry; the *Canon* names no constituent.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The *Canon* gives a long list, by organ:

- **Skin and hair.** Its ash with vinegar and oil for alopecia (*dāʾ al-thaʿlab*) and *dāʾ al-ḥayya*; "with myrtle oil and wine it lengthens the hair and prevents its falling" (lines 11222-11223).
- **Swellings and wounds.** Useful for abscesses (*dubaylāt*), scatters scrofula (*khanāzīr*), helps fistulae and malignant and moist ulcers (lines 11223-11225).
- **Head, eye.** The water of its ash for scurf (*ḥazāz*); for the eye complaint *gharab* (lines 11225-11226).
- **Chest.** "It cleans the lung greatly and benefits cough" (line 11226).
- **Spleen, liver.** With wine for flux of superfluities into the bowel and stomach; benefits pain of the spleen and jaundice (lines 11227-11228).
- **Urinary and womb.** It moves urine, breaks stone, moves the menses, expels the afterbirth, cleans the woman after childbirth, and stops bleeding (lines 11228-11229).
- **Poisons.** With wine, for the bites of snakes, rabid dogs and other vermin (lines 11230-11231).
- **Fowl.** "Mixed with the feed of cocks and quail, it strengthened them for scuffling" (line 11221).

It is not offered as a recommended practice.

### Preparation & Dosing

The forms in the text are: the ash with vinegar and oil (lines 11222-11223); the ash with myrtle oil and wine (line 11223); the water of the ash (line 11225); the herb with wine (lines 11227, 11230); the herb mixed into feed (line 11221). ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **CM (Avicenna), line 11219-11220.** "Galen said: it is moderate; and I say it perhaps leans to a very slight heat and dryness." Actions: "dissolving, subtilising, opening, with some binding, and it prevents flux" (محلل ملطف مفتح وفيه قبض ويمنع السيلان).
- On the bowel the authorities disagree: "to most it binds the belly, and to Ibn Māsawayh it loosens the belly" (line 11229).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The entry is a long organ-by-organ list of external and internal uses with a temperament from Galen. It has nothing on the innate heat, the radical moisture or the *ruh*, and gives no cultivation status.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing is asked of a cultivator. The entry is a good example of the *Canon* reporting two authorities who disagree on the bowel (Galen on temperament, the many against Ibn Māsawayh on binding or loosening) and leaving the disagreement standing.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy and the womb.** The text says it moves the menses, expels the afterbirth and stops bleeding after childbirth; avoid in pregnancy as a precaution. [USER: verify]
- **Rabies and snakebite.** The bite uses are historical record only. A bite from a rabid animal or a snake needs urgent medical care.
- **Identification.** Several small ferns carry the name "maidenhair"; do not take unidentified fronds.
- **Fern constituents.** Some ferns contain compounds that are harmful in quantity; for this species no safety data were retrieved. [USER: verify]
