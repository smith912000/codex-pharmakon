# Hippopotamus fat — "Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus", "Fat-from-the-Hoof-of-a-Hippopotamus" (Bryan's English renderings of the Ebers Papyrus); "Hippopotamus Oil" printed separately
## *The fat of the hippopotamus, a river animal on Bryan's animal list; Bryan prints no Egyptian word, no binomial and no preparation of the fat, and he prints "Hippopotamus Oil" and fat "from the hoof" as separate items* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter VII "Organic Remedies (animals)". "Hippopotamus" stands second on Bryan's list of the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1391, after "Lion" and before "Crocodile"; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 93, class animal, "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). Bryan's list of animal parts (lines 1405-1413) includes "Fat" and "Tallow" but does not tie either to any animal. The spine row ("Hippopotamus fat, Hippopotamus amphibius", with "baldness pomade; history.com; crocodile-oil.com") was not used as authority; the websites were not opened. The passages below were read in Bryan's text.

**Where the fat is prescribed.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "hippopotamus". The fat occurs in these places.

- **The baldness pomade, lines 4023-4029**, in Chapter XXII "The Hair" (heading line 3994): "ANOTHER FOR THE GROWTH OF THE HAIR ON A HEAD WHICH IS BECOMING BALD. Fat-of-the-Lion, Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus, Fat-of-the-Crocodile, Fat-of-the-Cat, Fat-of-the-Serpent, Fat-of-the-Egyptian-Goat. Make into one and rub the head of the Bald One therewith." The hippopotamus fat is the second of six fats in one compound. The word "ANOTHER" shows that it follows an earlier hair remedy.
- **A hair remedy with writing-fluid, line 4030**: "a concoction of Writing-fluid, Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus, and Gazelle's dung", which Bryan calls "frankly and positively revolting"; the sentence continues into a second remedy of vulva, phallus and black lizard, and the text on disk does not say whether the first remedy is for baldness or for the graying of hair. [USER: verify]
- **Fat from the hoof, lines 4074-4076**: to remove superfluous hair, "a mixture compounded of Tortoise-shell crushed and heated in Fat-from-the-Hoof-of-a-Hippopotamus". Bryan's comment that this is "probably on the theory that superfluous hairs are seldom, if ever, found on the shell of the Tortoise nor on the hoof of the Hippopotamus" is his own.
- **A wound poultice, lines 2371-2374**, in the chapter section headed "Minor Surgery" (heading line 2209), introduced by Bryan as a way "to dry a wound": "ANOTHER TO HEAL Refuse-of-durra. Mix in Fat-of-the-Hippopotamus, or of the Hog, and apply as a poultice."

**Related items, printed separately, not counted as the fat.** "Hippopotamus Oil" is named in a headache salve, where "Mint-of-the-Mountains" is dissolved in it along with copper and lead-vitriol (line 2112); in the liniment for suppleness of the limbs with natron, beans, honey, incense and myrrh, "beaten up with Hippopotamus Oil, Crocodile Oil, Oil-of-the-Shadfish [...]" (lines 2180-2181); on the third day of a four-day course for eczema of the head (line 2718); and in an ointment of "Hippopotamus Oil and Honey" for the "woodlike *uha*-disease" (line 3293). The "Hide-of-the-Hippopotamus" with poppy-seeds is an unguent for the head (line 2110), and the hoof itself is named at line 4075. Bryan does not say whether the oil is the fat rendered or something else. A Codex project file (`Sources/EP_Ebers/EP_Overview.md` line 156) prints a "fat of a hippopotamus" in a heart remedy in a quotation presented as a translation, without edition or page; it could not be checked against Bryan and was not used. [USER: verify]

**What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say how the fat was obtained or rendered, how much was used, or why the hippopotamus was chosen for the hair. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. The Ghalioungui 1987 glossary row for "Hippopotamus" in `_sources/ebers.csv` line 124 was not opened.

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it. In general terms, mammalian fat is mainly triglycerides, and rendered fat is a solid or semi-solid ointment base. Nothing in the sources retrieved says that hippopotamus fat contains any component that acts on hair. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show hippopotamus fat in a six-fat hair pomade, in a hair remedy with writing-fluid, in a depilatory with tortoise-shell, and as a wound poultice. No quantity is legible in any of them. The animal is protected over much of its range, and its fat is not an ordinary ointment base. No modern protocol is supplied and none is implied.

### Preparation & Dosing

Only what Bryan reports. The six fats are "made into one" and rubbed on the head (lines 4028-4029); fat is mixed with *durra* refuse into a poultice (line 2374); fat from the hoof is heated with crushed tortoise-shell (line 4074); fat is combined with writing-fluid and gazelle's dung (line 4030). No quantity is printed in Bryan's summary for any of these. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution, and a hair pomade is a cosmetic or restorative for an external complaint. Bryan's remark in the hair chapter that the first remedy for baldness was made "for a Queen" (lines 3995-3997) concerns a different remedy of dog's toes, date refuse and an ass's hoof. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*). The one spoken text in the hair chapter, "TO CHARM AWAY ALOPECIA O Shining One, Thou who hoverest above! [...] To be spoken over Iron Red-lead Onions Alabaster Honey" (lines 4069-4072), is attached to a different mixture and not to the fat. Bryan prints no recitation with the six-fat pomade, though he summarises, and that is no proof the papyrus gives none. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The record is a fat used in hair preparations, with no quantity and no cultivation role. A cultivator has nothing to do with it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** animal fat on broken skin or a wound carries infection risk; the poultice for wounds is not recommended.
- **Toxic co-ingredients:** the oil passages mix hippopotamus oil with copper and lead-vitriol, which are poisonous.
- **Conservation and law:** the hippopotamus is a large, threatened animal in many range states, and trade in its products is controlled in many countries; its present legal and conservation status was not retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics:** hippopotamus products come from killing a large wild animal and should not be used.
