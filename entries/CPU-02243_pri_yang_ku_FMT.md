# pri.yang.ku [FMT] — pri.yang.ku (rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch. 20 materia medica, as rendered by Clark 1995); alias rtsi.hu among six names

## *A herb of the Tibetan materia medica with blue leaves and flowers and thin petioles; Clark prints two modern identifications for it, "Callicarpa macrophylla Vahl or Dracocephalum tangutium Maxim" (the second spelling as printed; *D. tanguticum* is the usual spelling), and does not choose between them. Identification: unresolved between the two; basis, Clark's own list; confidence low. The Sanskrit-looking name and the first identification coincide with Priyangu (CPU-00364) of the Sushruta, but the text does not say they are the same drug* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan text among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "pri.yang.ku [FMT]"; this entry replaces it. The text is Clark 1995, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`. The names.csv row (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv`, row 130) gives the same two identifications.

**Tibetan (FMT).** The entry. In the Explanatory Tantra's materia medica, section "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (Clark line 997), the entry at line 1005 reads: "pri.yang.ku: Callicarpa macrophylla Vahl or Dracocephalum tangutium Maxim: [has six names, rtsi.hu, etc. It has bitter-sweet taste, is hemostatic, heals wounds, dries lymph accumulations, cures all types of liver fevers and has inconceivable qualities. The petioles are thin, with blue leaves and flowers]. It cures fevers of the stomach and the liver." The square brackets are in Clark's printed text; what they mark is not stated in the lines read here. [USER: verify.] Clark's endnote 12 (line 1543 to 1544) says that this section, headed in his notes "Exposition of the Powers of Plants", "does not appear as a separate section in the original text; nor is there agreement among scholars and commentators through the ages about which substances should be included in this section". The entry therefore rests on Clark's compilation, and the Tibetan tradition itself did not fix the list. The entries beside it (re.skon, kyi.lche, sum.chu.tig, zangs.tig) are bitter herbs for fevers, which is the passage's theme.

The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text on disk (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`) has, at line 5901, a glossary row "tig ta pri yang ku khrag rkang", which is a different name (a *tig ta* herb, "bitter", with a blood-related second term). It is not read here as this entry's drug. [USER: verify.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Callicarpa macrophylla* is a shrub of the Himalaya and East Asia, and *Dracocephalum* includes herbs of high-altitude meadows. Which is meant is not established. The Tibetan fever categories (liver fever, stomach fever) are the tradition's own and are not translated into modern diagnoses. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT.** Taste: bitter-sweet (Clark line 1005, in brackets). Power: not printed for this drug. Action: hemostatic, wound-healing, drying lymph accumulations, and curing liver and stomach fevers.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan (FMT).** *(b) Framed.* The herb sits in the fever section of the Explanatory Tantra's materia medica, inside the tradition's frame in which the three *nyes pa* derive from the three poisons and medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root. No *bcud len* (essence extraction) or other cultivation use is attested for this herb in the lines read, so its status is therapeutic only. No "substances for subtle-body practice" category exists in this tradition's formal scheme.

**Practitioner's note.** A bitter herb for liver and stomach fevers and for bleeding and wounds, unresolved between two plants. Nothing here supports any cultivation use, and the plant should not be gathered on this entry's authority.
