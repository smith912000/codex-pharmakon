# Quail — سماني / السمانى (*samānī*, as printed in both texts)

## *Quail: the flesh of the quail, a small migratory game bird (the common quail, Coturnix coturnix, is the modern identification; moderate confidence)* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM, TBN

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Two-text entry.** Named by Avicenna's *Canon* and by Ibn Qayyim's *al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī* among the texts on disk, and by no others here.

**Avicenna (CM).** Locus: *Canon* Book II, entry *samānī* (سماني), Arabic text line 16174 in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`; the entry is three lines (16175-16177). Description: "known" (معروف). Joints: "eating its flesh is feared to bring *tamaddud* (stretching, tension) and *tashannuj* (spasm), not only because it eats hellebore (*kharbaq*) but because this power is in its substance; and if it is thought that its feeding on hellebore is the cause, that is by the *mushākala* (kinship) of temperament" (أكل لحمه يخاف منه التمدد والتشنج لا لأنه يأكل الخربق فقط بل لأن في جوهره هذه القوة ...; lines 16175-16177). That is the whole entry. No temperament degree and no benefit is given.

**Ibn Qayyim (TBN).** Locus: *al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī*, entry *laḥm al-samānā* (لحم السمانى), Arabic text line 6691 in `Sources/TBN_Prophetic/text/IbnQayyim_TibbNabawi_OpenITI_Shamela0023649.txt`, running to line 6694, within the discussion of the flesh of birds. "The flesh of the quail: hot and dry; it benefits the joints and harms the hot liver, and its harm is averted by vinegar and coriander (*kusfara*)" (لحم السمانى: حار يابس، ينفع المفاصل، ويضر بالكبد الحار، ودفع مضرته بالخل والكسفرة; lines 6691-6692). He adds the general advice that "of the flesh of birds one should avoid those that live in thickets and rotten places" (lines 6692-6693), that bird meat digests faster than meat of livestock, and that the quickest to digest is the least nourishing: the necks and wings (lines 6693-6694). No hadith is cited in this entry and none is graded; the entry is a dietetic note.

**Where they differ.** Avicenna fears the quail for the nerves and joints (tension, spasm); Ibn Qayyim says it benefits the joints. The texts do not address each other. Avicenna's worry is tied to the bird's reputed feeding on hellebore.

**Identification.** Both texts say only *samānī*; the species is the modern reading. The Arabic name is the quail, a migratory bird, and the Codex adds no more.

### Active Components

Quail flesh is a lean game meat (protein, with some fat in the skin). The texts give no composition. A general fact relevant to the Canon's warning: a rare illness of muscle breakdown (coturnism) has been reported after eating migratory quail; its cause is not settled.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only: a game bird eaten as food, with a hot-and-dry temperament in Ibn Qayyim and a warning of spasm in Avicenna. Not a recommended practice and no protocol is offered.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation is a corrective: vinegar and coriander to avert harm to the hot liver (TBN line 6692). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No temperament is printed for quail in the *Canon* entry. **Prophetic medicine (TBN).** Hot and dry (*al-Ṭibb al-Nabawī*, line 6691).

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent.* The *Canon* entry is a caution about the flesh. It assigns no cultivation status and says nothing about the vital faculty.

**Prophetic medicine (TBN).** *(c) Absent.* Ibn Qayyim's entry is a dietetic note on one bird's flesh, without a hadith, without a grading and without any *sunna* reference. It falls under food and drink, one of the six necessary things, as ordinary regimen; it is not a Prophetic remedy.

**Practitioner's note.** Quail is food, with a caution from each text. For a cultivator the usable point is Ibn Qayyim's: if eaten, correct it with vinegar and coriander, and avoid birds from foul or marshy ground. Nothing here supports it as a long-term practice.

### Contraindications

Both texts caution against it (Avicenna: spasm; Ibn Qayyim: the hot liver). Coturnism is a documented risk with migratory quail meat. Wild quail are protected or regulated in many places; farmed quail are the ordinary source. Raw or undercooked poultry carries bacterial risk.
