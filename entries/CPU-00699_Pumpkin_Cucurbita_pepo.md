### Origin & Tradition

A crucial naming caution frames this entry. *Cucurbita pepo* — the true pumpkin, marrow and field-gourd — is a **New World domesticate**, cultivated in Mesoamerica for thousands of years and unknown to the Old World until after the Columbian exchange (16th century). Every pre-Columbian Old-World reference to "gourd" (Greek *kolokýnthē*, Arabic *qarʿ*, Hebrew *qishu'im*, Latin *cucurbita*) denotes **bottle gourd (*Lagenaria siceraria*), snake-gourd, or colocynth (*Citrullus colocynthis*)** — NOT *Cucurbita*. The traditions below therefore split into two honest lineages: (1) an ancient Old-World "gourd" medicine that *Cucurbita pepo* inherited by substitution once it arrived, and (2) a genuine indigenous American *Cucurbita* tradition. Kenetica files both under the pumpkin because the cooling, moistening, seed-anthelmintic and urinary uses transferred wholesale onto the new plant — but the entry does not pretend Dioscorides held a pumpkin.

**Mesoamerican / Indigenous American (genuine *Cucurbita* lineage):** *Cucurbita pepo* is one of the "Three Sisters" of Mesoamerican and later North American agriculture (maize, beans, squash), among the earliest domesticated plants in the Americas. Flesh, flowers and seeds were staple food; the seeds (*pepitas*) were eaten and used as a vermifuge across indigenous nations. This is the plant's true cradle — a food-medicine of the milpa long before it reached Cairo, Baghdad or Guangzhou.

**Egyptian / Ebers tradition (by substitution):** The Ebers Papyrus and later Egyptian practice used cucurbit gourds (bottle gourd, colocynth) as cooling pulp poultices, purgatives and febrifuges. When *Cucurbita pepo* entered Ottoman-era Egypt it slotted directly into the "cooling gourd" role — flesh applied to inflamed heads and fevers, seeds given for worms.

**Assyrian tradition (by substitution):** Cuneiform medical tablets list gourd (*qarʿu*) preparations as cooling and demulcent agents for fevers and urinary heat — the same energetic slot the pumpkin later filled in the region's folk medicine.

**Unani / Avicenna tradition:** Unani medicine (inheriting Galen and Dioscorides) classifies *qarʿ* as cold and moist, a *muraṭṭib* (moistener) prescribed for bilious fevers, insomnia from heat, and as a gentle diuretic; seeds (*bazr al-qarʿ*) are the classic **anthelmintic**, expelling tapeworm. Post-Columbian Unani texts and living hakim practice apply these indications to pumpkin seed without hesitation.

**Chinese tradition (*nan gua*, "southern gourd"):** *Cucurbita* entered China in the Ming and was named *nan gua*. In Chinese dietary medicine the flesh is sweet and warm, tonifying Spleen-Qi and Middle Burner, moistening the Lung; **the seed (*nan gua zi*) is a recognised anthelmintic**, classically paired with areca (*bing lang*) to expel tapeworm and roundworm — one of the few Chinese materia medica items of clearly foreign origin to earn a standard anthelmintic indication.

**Ayurvedic tradition:** Ayurveda's *kūshmāṇḍa* group (ash gourd and allied cucurbits) is sweet, cooling, *rasāyana* and calming to Pitta; pumpkin (a later addition) is used as nourishing, cooling flesh and the seed as a mild vermifuge and genito-urinary tonic — congruent with the pan-traditional pattern.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — the vessel that holds water:** Across cultures the gourd is the archetypal **container** — hollow, water-bearing, a vessel carved into bottles, bowls and instruments. It is the plant that becomes a holding-shape. In Kenetica the pumpkin is read as the medicine of *containment and cooling storage*: it draws heat out of an overheated system and holds moisture in reserve, the swollen fruit as a reservoir. Its seeds — dense, oily, flat, arrayed in the wet dark of the cavity — carry the opposite signature: they *expel* what should not remain (worms) and *drain* stagnant fullness (the boggy, over-full prostate). Fruit = holds; seed = clears. The two halves of one plant teach the reciprocal gestures of retention and release.

### Active Components

- **Seed:** Δ7-sterols (spinasterol, Δ7-avenasterol), tocopherols, cucurbitin (the amino acid credited with anthelmintic action), zinc, phytosterols, unsaturated fatty acids (linoleic, oleic), and lignans.
- **Seed oil:** rich in phytosterols and free fatty acids implicated in benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) symptom relief.
- **Flesh:** carotenoids (beta-carotene, lutein), pectin, potassium, water-soluble fibre; low energy density.
- **Flower:** carotenoids and flavonoids.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Pumpkin is a **dual-gesture ally**: cooling nourishment from the flesh, draining clearance from the seed. In practice its two headline roles are (1) the seed as **anthelmintic** — the pan-traditional expulsion of intestinal worms — and (2) the seed/seed-oil for **lower-urinary-tract fullness in ageing men (BPH symptoms)**, where the phytosterols appear to ease flow and reduce the sense of obstructed heaviness. As food-medicine the flesh grounds and cools: a gentle Spleen-Qi and fluid-restoring staple for depleted, overheated or convalescent states. Training-phase use: Grounding (nourishing flesh), Clearing (seed anthelmintic / urinary drainage), Restoration (convalescent food).

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- A single-blind randomised clinical trial (Zerafatjou et al., 2021, *BMC Urology*) compared **pumpkin seed oil 360 mg twice daily against tamsulosin** in 73 men ≥50 with BPH. Both groups showed significant improvement in International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) and quality of life; pumpkin seed oil was **effective but less so than tamsulosin**, and notably produced **no drug side effects** (versus dizziness, headache and retrograde ejaculation in the tamsulosin arm). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12894-021-00910-8)

**Evidence grade:** *Moderate-to-preliminary* for BPH symptom relief (a small single-blind RCT, positive but inferior to standard drug; broader phytosterol/BPH literature is supportive but heterogeneous). The **anthelmintic** use is deeply and consistently attested across independent traditions (Unani, Chinese, indigenous American) — historically robust — but modern controlled human trials retrieved here are limited; graded *traditionally strong, clinically under-tested*.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** Flesh — sweet, neutral-to-warm; tonifies Spleen-Qi, moistens Lung. Seed (*nan gua zi*) — sweet, neutral; anthelmintic, enters Stomach/Large Intestine.
- **Ayurvedic:** Sweet (*madhura*) *rasa*, cooling *vīrya*, Pitta-pacifying; seed mildly *kṛmighna* (anthelmintic).
- **Galenic / Unani:** Flesh cold & moist (2nd degree), *muraṭṭib* and gently diuretic; seed (*bazr al-qarʿ*) temperate-cool, softening, anthelmintic.

### Contraindications

- Seeds are calorie-dense and oily — large anthelmintic doses may cause GI upset. Seed allergy is possible.
- Cool, moist flesh may aggravate Spleen-Yang-deficient / cold-damp digestive pictures if eaten in excess (TCM caution).
- Pumpkin seed oil is a food supplement, not a substitute for medical evaluation of prostate symptoms — new or worsening urinary obstruction, blood in urine, or retention require clinical assessment, since BPH symptoms overlap with prostate cancer.
- Do not self-treat suspected worm infestation without diagnosis; confirm species and appropriate therapy.
