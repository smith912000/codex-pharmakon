### Origin & Tradition

Solomon's seal is a graceful woodland lily-relative with arching stems hung with pendant white bells and a knotted, jointed white rhizome. The name has two traditional explanations: the round, seal-like scars left on the rhizome where each year's stem detaches (said to resemble the seal of Solomon), and the plant's repute for "sealing" and knitting wounds and broken bones. Dioscorides records *polygonaton* and the European tradition that followed valued the rhizome above all as a **vulnerary and consolidant** — a "knitter" of wounds, bruises and broken bones, used as a poultice for injuries and the discolouration of bruises (a famous folk use, with the macerated root applied to black eyes and contusions) and internally as a soothing, moistening, restorative.

It is in **Chinese Medicine** that the genus reaches its fullest therapeutic development, through the closely related species: **Yu Zhu** (玉竹, *Polygonatum odoratum*) and **Huang Jing** (黃精, *Polygonatum sibiricum/cyrtonema*). Yu Zhu is a gentle **yin- and fluid-nourishing** herb that "moistens dryness" — used for dry cough, dry throat and thirst, and the dryness of febrile recovery, prized for nourishing yin without being cloying or stagnating. Huang Jing is a **qi- and yin-tonic** and longevity herb, building the Spleen, Lung and Kidney. The European Solomon's seal shares this moistening, nourishing, tissue-restoring character, the through-line being a cool, sweet, mucilaginous rhizome that soothes dryness and supports the repair of sinew and tissue.

A point of caution: while the rhizome is the medicine, the **berries are toxic** (the plant is in the same broad group as the toxic lily-of-the-valley and false Solomon's seals), and the raw rhizome of some species needs proper preparation. The aromatic, moistening rhizome is the valued part; the attractive berries are not for eating.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. **Yin/fluid-nourishing moistener (CM Yu Zhu register).** The defining tonic use of the genus — moistening dryness, easing dry cough, dry throat and thirst, supporting febrile recovery without cloying.
2. **Vulnerary / consolidant (European).** Rhizome poultice to knit wounds, bruises and broken bones, and disperse contusion discolouration — the "sealing" tradition.
3. **Qi-yin tonic / longevity (CM Huang Jing register).** Building Spleen, Lung and Kidney; a gentle restorative.
4. **Demulcent for dry irritation.** The mucilage soothing dry cough and dry, irritated tissues.
5. **Anti-inflammatory / joint-soothing (folk).** Traditional use for joint and connective-tissue complaints. [USER: verify before any clinical claim.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM / Galenic humoral degree:** cool-to-temperate and moist — a moistening, soothing, consolidating rhizome; the vulnerary "knitter" of European tradition.
- **Chinese Medicine mapping:** **Yu Zhu** — sweet, slightly cold; Lung, Stomach; nourishes yin, moistens dryness, generates fluids. **Huang Jing** — sweet, neutral; Spleen, Lung, Kidney; tonifies qi and yin, a longevity tonic.
- **Kenetica Qi/Shen tier:** ◉ **Qi** — on the cooling-moistening, yin-nourishing, tissue-consolidating axis (moisten dryness, knit tissue); a gentle building/restorative rhizome.

### Contraindications

- **Berries — toxic.** Do not eat; keep from children; distinguish from edible look-alikes and from toxic false Solomon's seals/lily-of-the-valley.
- **Damp/phlegm-cold patterns (CM).** Moistening yin tonics are cautioned where there is interior damp-cold or phlegm congestion or weak, damp digestion.
