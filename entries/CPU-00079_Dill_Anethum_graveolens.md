# Dill — شبت (Šabat) / Ἄνηθον (Anēthon) / शतपुष्पा (Śatapuṣpā) / 莳萝 (Shíluó)
## *Anethum graveolens L. — Dill*
**Tradition Tags:** EP · AT · DMM · CM · SS · SBJ
**Energetic Classification (TCM):** ◉ Qi
**Training Phase:** `[PRIME]` `[RESET]` `[SLEEP]`

---

### Origin & Tradition

Dill is documented as a medicinal plant in Egypt since at least 1550 BCE (Ebers Papyrus) and its seeds have been found in Bronze Age sites from Anatolia to India. The name derives from the Old Norse *dilla* meaning "to lull" — reflecting its traditional use as a calming herb for infants (gripe water). It shares the *Śatapuṣpā* name with fennel in Ayurvedic texts, reflecting the similar properties (though dill is *A. graveolens* and fennel is *F. vulgare* — distinct species). The carvone-rich dill profile is uniquely sedative among the Apiaceae digestive herbs — making it the carminative most aligned with the [SLEEP] phase.

**EP — Egyptian (Imw / Antyw — documented in Ebers Papyrus):** Dill appears in the Ebers Papyrus in digestive and calming preparations. Egyptian medicine used dill seed infusions for colic, flatulence, and in sedative compound preparations.

**AT — Assyrian (Šibittu / Šabtu — documented in Nineveh tablets):** Dill (*šabat*) appears in Assyrian medical texts in carminative and digestive formulas. Its presence in Mesopotamian gardens is confirmed by palace records from Nineveh.

**DMM — De Materia Medica (Ἄνηθον — Anēthon):** Dioscorides provides detailed dill coverage in Book III. He notes it as warming, carminative, and notably "stops hiccups" (the smooth muscle antispasmodic action). He recommends dill for digestive gas, promotes sleep in the form of fomentations (warm poultice applications), increases milk production. He distinguishes cultivated from wild dill.

**CM — Canon of Medicine (شبت — Šabat):** Ibn Sina classifies dill as warm in the second degree and dry in the first — similar to fennel. Indications: carminative; galactagogue; diuretic; promotes sleep (the sedative property noted explicitly); reduces intestinal pain. He recommends dill water (*mā al-šabat*) as a calming digestive preparation.

**SS — Ayurveda (शतपुष्पा — Śatapuṣpā — sometimes applied to dill, sometimes to fennel; Shatahva is more specific to dill):** Dill (*Anethum sowa* — the Indian variety) is documented in Ayurveda. Classification: Katu-Tikta rasa, Ushna virya, Vata-Kapha shamaka. Primary actions: Deepana-Pachana; Shula-prashamana (colic relief); Stanya-janana (galactagogue); Nidrajana (sleep-promoting — the distinctive dill-specific action among Apiaceae).

**SBJ/TCM (莳萝 — Shíluó):** Dill entered TCM via Silk Road. Classification: pungent, warm; Spleen-Kidney channel. Warms Kidney and Spleen; carminative; regulates Qi; used for cold-type digestive pain and hernia-type Liver Qi stagnation (similar to small fennel but with additional sedative character).

### Active Components

- **D-Carvone** — 35–65% of essential oil; carminative; antispasmodic; sedative (GABA-A modulation at higher concentrations); antimicrobial; same compound dominates caraway oil but dill carvone has a slightly different fragrance profile
- **Limonene** — 15–35%; anxiolytic; antimicrobial; the citrus-terpene contribution
- **α-Phellandrene, β-phellandrene** — 5–20%; distinctive phellandrene-dill aroma; antimicrobial
- **Apiol** — minor; diuretic; uterotonic at high doses
- **Flavonoids** — vicenin, quercetin, isorhamnetin; anti-inflammatory; antioxidant
- **Coumarins** — bergapten (minor); the photosensitizing concern shared with other Apiaceae

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**1. Sleep-promoting digestive ([SLEEP] phase — the distinguishing application)**
Dill's classical *nidrajana* (sleep-promoting) effect makes it unique among the carminative Apiaceae herbs. The GABA-A modulation via carvone and limonene creates a sedative-carminative combination: it simultaneously settles post-evening-meal gas and promotes sleep onset. Traditional "dill water" or gripe water (dill + sodium bicarbonate) is the oldest documented pediatric sedative for colic — the same mechanism applies in adult practitioners managing sleep with digestive discomfort.

**2. Galactagogue (nursing practitioners)**
Parallel to fennel and caraway, dill has consistent cross-traditional galactagogue documentation. Used together, the three (fennel + dill + caraway tea) form the classical northern European "mother's milk" blend.

**3. Digestive carminative ([PRIME] and [RESET])**
The carminative mechanism is shared with fennel and caraway. Dill's specific advantage over the others is its sedative dimension — when digestive carminative action is needed at evening meals (pre-sleep), dill is preferred over the more stimulating fennel.

### Preparation & Dosing

**Dill seed tea:**
1 tsp crushed dill seeds in 250mL hot water; steep 10 minutes; drink after evening meal for carminative-sedative combined effect. The standard preparation.

**Dill water (traditional infant gripe water):**
1g dill seed simmered in 250mL water 5 minutes; strain and cool; small amounts for infant colic. The classical preparation.

**Dill seed + warm milk (sleep preparation):**
1/2 tsp crushed dill seed steeped in warm whole milk 10 minutes; strain; drink before bed. The warming-sedative combination.

### Scientific Correlation

- **Carminative:** Savino F et al. (2005): dill seed extract-based preparations effective for infant colic; Alexandrovich I et al. (2003): similar findings with related Apiaceae preparations
- **Antidiabetic:** Yazdanparast R et al. (2008): dill extract significantly improved insulin sensitivity and reduced glucose in diabetic rats; chlorogenic acid mechanism proposed
- **Antimicrobial:** Delaquis PJ et al. (2002): dill essential oil (carvone + limonene) effective against *Listeria monocytogenes* and *E. coli* O157:H7

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy (concentrated preparations)** — apiol content; uterotonic at high doses; culinary use safe, medicinal-dose tea and essential oil to avoid
- **Apiaceae allergy** — cross-reactivity with fennel, caraway, coriander, carrot
- **Photosensitivity (bergapten)** — minor furanocoumarin content; avoid topical dill preparations in sun-exposed skin
