# Five-colour halloysite (Wu Se Shi Zhi) — Qing Shi, Chi Shi, Huang Shi, Bai Shi, Hei Shi (Halloysitum Viridis, Rubrum, Aureum, Album, Negrum, etc.) (Yang 1998); list row 青石赤石黃石白石黑石脂等
## *Five coloured stones of the Divine Farmer's Classic (green-blue, red, yellow, white and black), printed by Yang under one monograph as "Halloysitum" in five colours; Yang's footnote 45 says their identities are controversial: most think the first four are kaolinite clays, and Hei Shi Zhi (black) is a kind of graphite* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The five stones are printed in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) on PDF page 26, as a single monograph in the run of "Jades and Stones: Superior Class". The page itself prints no class heading in the OCR; the heading is printed at its top on the page before (line 985, page 25) and at the top of the page after (line 1060, page 27), and the entries between them are in that run. The text (lines 1022-1031, with OCR damage marked): "Qing Shi, Chi Shi, Huang Shi, Bai Shi, Hei Shi (Halloysitum Viridis, Rubmm [sic, Rubrum], Aureum, Album, Negrum, etc.)45 are sweet and bil)nnced [balanced]. They mainly treat jaundice, diarrhea and dysentery, intestinal afflux with pus and blood, genital erosion, precipitation of blood, red and white [vaginal discharge], evil qi, welling abscesses and swellings, flat abscesses, hemorrhoids, malign sores, head sores, and itching scabs. Protracted taking may replenish the marrow, boost the qi, and make one fat and strong, free from hunger, and the body light while prolonging life. The five colors of Halloysitum [each] respectively supplement the five viscera in accordance with their colors." The words "balanced" (garbled in the OCR as "bil)nnced") and "vaginal discharge" are restored from the damaged print; [USER: verify] against the page image.

Yang's footnote 45 (lines 1042-1045): "The identities of these medicinals are controversial. Many people believe that all except for Hei Shi Zhi are variously colored Kaolinite clays, while Hei Shi Zhi is a kind of Graphite." Footnote 46 (lines 1046-1050) gives the correspondence of the five viscera with the five colours: "the heart to red, the lungs to white, the liver to green-blue, the spleen to yellow, and the kidneys to black", with the example that Halloysitum Album supplements the lungs and Halloysitum Rubrum the heart.

### Active Components

Halloysite is a clay mineral, a hydrated aluminium silicate of formula Al2Si2O5(OH)4 with water between its layers (the spine's formula), of the kaolin group, and its crystals commonly form small hollow tubes. Kaolinite, the clay mentioned in footnote 45, has the same basic formula in a layered, non-hydrated form. Clays are coloured by small amounts of other minerals, and iron oxides commonly give red, yellow and brown tones. Graphite, the footnote's gloss for the black stone, is a form of carbon and not a clay. The sources read give no composition for the stones Yang translates.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records the five stones for jaundice, dysentery, bleeding, genital and skin complaints, and says that long taking may "replenish the marrow", boost the qi and make one "fat and strong", free from hunger, light-bodied and long-lived, and that the five colours supplement the five viscera by colour. The Codex does not endorse these claims and offers no protocol. Swallowed clay is not recommended: it can bind other medicines and may carry impurities.

### Scientific Correlation

Kaolin-group clays are used industrially in ceramics, paper and fillers, and kaolin has a long modern use in anti-diarrhoeal preparations as an adsorbent, a general pharmacological fact about kaolin and not about the stones of the Classic. Clays can bind other drugs taken at the same time. No medical benefit of swallowing these stones for the conditions the Classic names is established. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- SBJ: "sweet and balanced" (Yang line 1023, restored from damaged OCR). Class: Superior (heading lines 985 and 1060). "Balanced" is Yang's rendering for neither cold nor hot, neither warm nor cool (his footnote 32, lines 849-855). The text correlates the five colours with the five viscera (line 1030; footnote 46, lines 1046-1050). No other quality is printed.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: an upper-grade drug (上品), the five stones taken as one monograph.* The monograph stands between two printings of the heading "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (Yang lines 985 and 1060); the page itself prints no heading, and in this the entry is placed as Mica (Yun Mu) is. Yang's preface says the superior class medicinals "mainly nourish life and correspond to heaven. They are nontoxic and taking them in large amounts and for a long time will not harm people. If one intends to make one's body light, boost the qi, prevent aging, and prolong life, one should base [one's efforts] on the superior class" (lines 352-358). The monograph prints its own lines: "Protracted taking may replenish the marrow, boost the qi, and make one fat and strong, free from hunger, and the body light while prolonging life" (lines 1027-1029). The text says "marrow" and no *jing* claim is made here. The grade is a statement of the Classic as translated by Yang in 1998 and is not carried forward from later materia medica works. The grade attaches to the entry as the Classic prints it, five stones in one; it does not settle what the stones were.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: the Classic held it safe for long use. Whether it was a clay, a graphite or something else is open, and the Classic's claims have no established support, so nothing here supports taking it. For a cultivator the entry is a record of how the Han-period elixir tradition ranked five coloured earths, matched to the five viscera, among the "nourish life" drugs. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Clays sold as remedies vary widely in composition and may carry heavy metals or other contaminants. Swallowed clay can reduce the absorption of other medicines. If the black stone was graphite (footnote 45), that is a carbon and not a clay, and the same caution about unstandardised mineral products applies. The Classic's "prolonging life" is not a claim of fact. [USER: verify] the legal and conservation status of collecting these materials; the sources retrieved do not address it.
