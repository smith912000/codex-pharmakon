# Dandelion — Pissenlit / Piss-a-bed / Pu Gong Ying / Löwenzahn / Taraxacum

## *Taraxacum officinale* (L.) Weber | Family: Asteraceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, DMM, SBJ, SS
**Energetic Classification:** Cool & dry, bitter; cleansing-deobstruent to the liver and water pathways (Galenic reading)
**Training Phase:** Drainage / Cleansing (the liver-and-water gate)

---

### Origin & Tradition

**The name is the doctrine.** Before any tradition is cited, the plant announces its own indication. The French **pissenlit** — literally "piss-in-bed" — and the English country name **"piss-a-bed"** both encode a single clinical claim: eat this and you will pass water. The common English "dandelion" is *dent-de-lion*, "lion's tooth," for the jagged leaf. Across European languages the diuretic doctrine is fossilised directly in the plant's everyday name, which is as close to an unbroken folk-clinical consensus as ethnobotany offers.

**Dioscorides (*De Materia Medica*).** The Greek tradition describes bitter *Taraxacum*-type chicory-like herbs (the *aphakē* / wild-endive complex) as cooling, bitter, good for the stomach and "for the belly," opening obstructions and moving water. Dandelion sits inside this bitter, cooling, deobstruent chicory-endive family that Dioscorides treats as liver- and gut-cleansing. *(The classical Greek names cover a cluster of related Asteraceae/Cichorieae; the bitter-deobstruent doctrine is the genuine continuity. **Avicenna (*Canon of Medicine*).** In the Arabic-Persian tradition the bitter endive-dandelion group (*ṭaraxacon* / *hindibāʾ*) is classed as cool and dry, a cleanser and opener of obstructions of the **liver and spleen**, used for jaundice, hot liver conditions and to promote urine. This is the fully-formed hepatic-cholagogue-diuretic doctrine: a cool bitter that *opens the liver and moves the water*.

**Shennong / TCM (*Pu Gong Ying*, 蒲公英).** Chinese medicine knows dandelion as **Pu Gong Ying**, classed as **bitter, sweet and cold**, entering the Liver and Stomach channels. Its canonical actions are to **clear heat and resolve toxicity (*qing re jie du*)**, reduce fire-toxin swellings and abscesses — famously **mastitis / breast abscess** — and to promote urination and drain damp-heat, including damp-heat jaundice. The "clears heat, drains dampness, resolves toxic swelling" reading is strikingly convergent with the Greco-Arabic "cool bitter that opens the liver and moves water."

**Sushruta / Ayurvedic stratum.** *Taraxacum* species (Himalayan *T. officinale*) enter later Ayurvedic and Unani-influenced Indian practice as a bitter (*tikta*) hepatic and diuretic, *pitta*- and *kapha*-reducing, used for liver heat and as a *deepana*/*pachana* bitter tonic. *(Classical Sushruta-era attribution of the exact species is uncertain; the tikta hepatic-diuretic use is genuine in the Indian materia medica. **Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** Two doctrines run through every tradition — **"moves water" (diuretic)** and **"opens the liver / cools heat / moves bile" (bitter cholagogue-hepatic)** — and both map onto distinct, real chemistry in distinct parts of the plant. The **diuretic** doctrine (pissenlit, "promotes urine," *li niao*) maps onto the **leaf**, which is exceptionally rich in **potassium**. A high-potassium botanical eaten as a food-herb behaves osmotically as a mild aquaretic — the "piss-a-bed" name is a folk observation of a genuine electrolyte-driven diuresis, and it does so without the potassium-wasting of pharmaceutical loop diuretics (dandelion *supplies* the potassium a diuretic would deplete). The **bitter hepatic-cholagogue** doctrine ("opens the liver," "for jaundice," *qing gan*) maps onto the **root and its bitter principles** — the sesquiterpene lactones (taraxacin, and the triterpene **taraxasterol**) that produce the intense bitterness. Bitterness on the tongue is not decorative: it is the sensory signature of the compounds that reflexively stimulate salivary, gastric and biliary secretion (the classic *amara*/cholagogue reflex). The plant's symbolism — the sun-yellow flower that turns to a "clock" of seeds dispersed on the breath, a herb of the waste-ground and the threshold — folds neatly into a drug of *clearing, draining and letting-go*: it moves what is stagnant (bile, water, heat) out of the body.

### Active Components

- **Leaf:** very high **potassium**; flavonoids (luteolin, apigenin glycosides); the diuretic-relevant fraction.
- **Root:** **sesquiterpene lactones** (taraxacin and eudesmanolide/germacranolide bitters) and the triterpene **taraxasterol / ψ-taraxasterol** — the bitter cholagogue principles.
- **Inulin** (fructan storage carbohydrate, abundant in autumn root) — prebiotic, mild laxative.
- **Phenolic acids:** chlorogenic and chicoric acids.
- **Sterols, choline, carotenoids, vitamins (A, C, K).**

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Dandelion is the **Drainage-phase** workhorse: leaf as a food-diuretic and potassium-rich bitter salad/tea for fluid retention and puffiness, root as a bitter cholagogue-hepatic decoction to stimulate bile flow and support liver "clearing" — sluggish digestion of fats, mild damp-heat pictures, post-rich-diet reset. It is used where the signal is *stagnation and fluid* — bile that isn't moving, water that isn't clearing — rather than deficiency or cold. The whole plant is a food-grade bitter, positioned early and gently in a drainage sequence.

### Scientific Correlation

*According to PubMed:*

**The diuretic doctrine — actually tested in humans.** A pilot clinical study gave a fresh-leaf hydroethanolic extract of *Taraxacum officinale* (8 mL, three doses) to 17 volunteers and measured urine output against each subject's own baseline. It found a statistically significant increase in **urination frequency in the 5 hours after the first dose (p < 0.05)** and a significant increase in the **excretion ratio (urine volume : fluid intake) after the second dose (p < 0.001)** — the first human data to substantiate the diuretic use (Clare 2009, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1089/acm.2008.0152)). This is a direct, if small, vindication of the *pissenlit* / "piss-a-bed" name: the folk name predicted the endpoint the trial measured.

**The hepatic / anti-inflammatory doctrine.** A mechanistic study of *T. officinale* methanolic extract in LPS-stimulated human endothelial cells found it suppressed pro-inflammatory cytokine and adhesion-molecule expression by inhibiting the **NF-κB pathway**, and the authors explicitly note the plant's traditional choleretic, diuretic and anti-inflammatory reputation (Jeon 2017, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-017-2022-7)). This supports the "clears heat / resolves toxicity" (Pu Gong Ying) and "opens the liver" doctrines at a cellular level, but it is in-vitro, not a clinical demonstration of cholagogue effect in people.

**Evidence grade.** *Low.* The diuresis finding is a genuine but small (n = 17), uncontrolled-design pilot — promising and doctrine-consistent, not definitive. The hepatic/anti-inflammatory rationale is mechanistically plausible and matches convergent Greek, Arabic and Chinese doctrine, but human cholagogue/liver-outcome trials are lacking. Honest reading: dandelion's **diuretic** name is the best-supported of its claims and has at least one confirmatory human study; its **cholagogue/hepatic** claim is traditionally near-universal and mechanistically supported but clinically unproven. Do not state a quantified bile-flow or liver-function benefit in humans — that data does not yet exist.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM (attested — Pu Gong Ying):** Bitter, sweet, cold; Liver and Stomach channels. Actions: clear heat, resolve toxicity, reduce swelling/abscess (esp. mastitis), promote urination, drain damp-heat.
- **Ayurvedic / Indian materia medica (attested at genus level; species [USER: verify]):** *Rasa* bitter (*tikta*); *virya* cooling (*shita*); *pitta*- and *kapha*-reducing; hepatic-diuretic bitter tonic (*deepana/pachana*).
- **Galenic / Unani (Avicenna, attested):** Cool and dry; deobstruent opener of liver and spleen; for jaundice and hot liver; promotes urine.
- **Greek humoral (Dioscorides-tradition, attested):** Cooling, bitter, good for stomach and belly; opens obstructions.

### Contraindications

- **Asteraceae (Compositae) allergy** — dandelion can cross-react in people sensitised to ragweed, chrysanthemum, marigold, etc.; contact dermatitis is reported from the latex. Avoid in known Compositae allergy.
- **Bile-duct obstruction, cholangitis, gallstones with obstruction, or acute gallbladder disease** — a cholagogue that stimulates bile flow is contraindicated where the bile duct is blocked; use only with medical clearance.
- **Concurrent pharmaceutical diuretics** — potential additive fluid/electrolyte effect; monitor.
- **Potassium-affecting drugs** — the leaf is potassium-rich; theoretical interaction with potassium-sparing diuretics/ACE inhibitors.
- The milky latex may irritate skin; gastric hyperacidity may be aggravated by strong bitters in sensitive individuals.
