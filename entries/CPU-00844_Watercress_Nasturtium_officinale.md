# Watercress — Cardamine/Kardamon (Greek), Jarjīr al-mā' (Arabic, "water rocket"), Berro (Iberian), *Selario* (folk Latin)

## *Nasturtium officinale* R.Br. | Family: Brassicaceae

**Tradition Tags:** AT, CM, DMM, SS
**Energetic Classification:** Hot and dry in the second degree (Galenic); pungent, penetrating, opening and depurative
**Training Phase:** Clearing / Depuration — the phase concerned with mobilising the blood, opening obstruction, and driving out stagnant heat

---

### Origin & Tradition

Watercress is a fast-growing, pungent aquatic crucifer that has been eaten and prescribed around freshwater springs for millennia. Its medical identity is that of a **depurative and stimulant green** — a "cleanser of the blood," an opener of obstruction, and (in the older texts) a virility and vitality herb — and its sharp, mustard-like bite is the sensory signal of its active chemistry.

**Assyrian / Mesopotamian layer (BM).** Cresses and pungent water-herbs appear among the Mesopotamian plant lists as sharp, "opening" remedies. As with other cuneiform plant identifications, the exact species mapping is uncertain, so watercress here stands as a representative of the pungent-crucifer group used to move and open rather than a securely attributed single line. **Dioscoridean / Greco-Roman layer (DMM).** Dioscorides describes cress (*kardamon*) as pungent and heating, used to stimulate, to clear the chest, as a diuretic, and — a recurring classical note — as an aphrodisiac and "sharpener" of a dull constitution. The Greco-Roman tradition consistently files watercress among the hot, penetrating potherbs that stir a sluggish system.

**Avicennian / Unani layer (AT).** In Avicenna's *Qanun* and the wider Unani tradition, watercress (*jarjīr al-mā'*) is hot and dry, a blood-cleanser and deobstruent, diuretic and emmenagogue, used for cold-phlegmatic stagnation, skin eruptions arising from "impure blood," and to stimulate appetite and vigour. Its heat is why it is prescribed *against* cold, damp, stagnant states.

**Ayurvedic / Sushruta layer (SS).** Within the Indian tradition, pungent cresses read as *katu* (pungent), heating, *deepana/pachana* (kindling digestion) greens that clear Kapha stagnation and stimulate — the same functional slot as the Greek and Unani accounts. Watercress is best treated as an analogue/regional member of this pungent-green class rather than a single canonical *dravya*. [USER: verify classical Sanskrit name before citing one.]

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer.** Watercress's doctrine is *the sharp green that cleans the blood* — pungency as purification. The modern reading makes this literal. Watercress is exceptionally rich in the glucosinolate **gluconasturtiin**, which on tissue damage (chewing) is hydrolysed by myrosinase into **phenethyl isothiocyanate (PEITC)** — the compound responsible for both the pungent bite and the plant's best-documented biological action: induction of detoxifying (phase II) enzymes and protection of DNA from oxidative and carcinogen-driven damage. The tradition's "cleanser of impure blood" is, in mechanistic terms, an inducer of cellular detoxification. The symbol and the chemistry meet at the point of the tongue.

### Active Components

- **Gluconasturtiin** (2-phenethyl glucosinolate) → **phenethyl isothiocyanate (PEITC)** on myrosinase hydrolysis — the signature bioactive; phase-II enzyme inducer and phase-I inhibitor.
- **Vitamin K** (high), **vitamin C**, **carotenoids** (lutein, β-carotene), folate.
- Other glucosinolates and phenolics; minerals (notably calcium, iron).

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Watercress is the phase's **depurative stimulant**: a warming, pungent green used to open a cold, damp, stagnant field, stir sluggish digestion and elimination, and — in the modern reading — support the body's own detoxification machinery via PEITC. Its Kenetica use case is the Clearing indication of stagnation with a low protective/antioxidant reserve: the dull, congested, "thick-blooded" presentation. It is used as food-as-medicine (a generous portion of the raw leaf) far more than as a concentrated extract.

**Chew or chop raw:** PEITC is generated only when myrosinase meets gluconasturtiin in damaged tissue. Boiling destroys the enzyme and much of the benefit.

### Scientific Correlation

The detox/DNA-protection claim for watercress rests on a real human randomised trial plus a well-characterised mechanism.

According to PubMed: Gill et al. (2007, *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*) conducted a single-blind randomised crossover study in 60 healthy adults (30 smokers, 30 non-smokers) fed 85 g raw watercress daily for 8 weeks. Watercress supplementation significantly reduced basal DNA damage in lymphocytes (by 17%, P = 0.03) and basal-plus-oxidative purine DNA damage (by 23.9%, P = 0.002), with greater effects in smokers, and raised plasma lutein (+100%) and β-carotene (+33%) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/85.2.504)). This human finding is consistent with the extensive mechanistic literature on PEITC as a phase-II enzyme inducer and modulator of carcinogen metabolism, which underlies the epidemiological association between cruciferous-vegetable intake and reduced cancer risk cited in the same work.

**Evidence grade: Moderate for DNA-protection/antioxidant-status effects (one good human RCT plus strong PEITC mechanism); the broader traditional depurative, diuretic and stimulant uses remain traditional.** Watercress is a food with credible mechanistic backing, not a treatment for any diagnosed disease.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Galenic / Unani:** Hot and dry (second degree); pungent, deobstruent, blood-cleansing, diuretic, emmenagogue; corrective to cold-phlegmatic stagnation.
- **Ayurvedic (by analogy):** *katu* (pungent) rasa, *ushna* (heating) virya, *laghu/tikshna* (light, penetrating) guna — Kapha-reducing, digestion-kindling; used cautiously in high Pitta.
- **TCM (analogy only):** a warm, pungent, moving green that disperses stagnation and drains damp-heat downward; not a classical Bencao species under this name.

### Contraindications

- **⚠️ Wild-gathered fascioliasis (liver-fluke) risk.** Watercress harvested from wild waterways grazed by sheep or cattle can carry the encysted metacercariae of *Fasciola hepatica* (liver fluke). **Only eat commercially/hydroponically grown or reliably clean-source watercress; do not eat wild watercress raw** from pasture-fed catchments. This is the single most important practical hazard of the plant.
- **⚠️ High vitamin K — warfarin antagonism.** Watercress is rich in vitamin K, which antagonises warfarin and other vitamin-K-antagonist anticoagulants and can destabilise INR. Patients on warfarin should keep vitamin-K intake **consistent** rather than suddenly adding large amounts; flag to the prescriber/anticoagulation clinic before regular medicinal use.
- **⚠️ Pregnancy caution.** Watercress carries a traditional reputation as an emmenagogue/uterine stimulant and is conventionally avoided in medicinal (concentrated) quantities during pregnancy; culinary garnish amounts are generally regarded as food, but medicinal dosing is [USER: verify] and best avoided.
- **Gastric/urinary irritation:** the isothiocyanates are mucous-membrane irritants; large raw doses can irritate the stomach and, per older texts, the bladder/kidneys — avoid high doses in gastritis, peptic ulcer or acute nephritis.
- **Thyroid:** as a glucosinolate-containing crucifer, very large habitual intakes are theoretically goitrogenic in iodine-deficient individuals; ordinary dietary amounts are not a concern.
