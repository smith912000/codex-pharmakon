# Lead stone — Molubdoeides Lithos (MOLUBDOEIDES LITHOS), Dioscorides 5-98

## *A stone that Dioscorides says has the same properties as lead slag; the translator glosses it "Lead Stone" and marks it TOXIC. The identification is unresolved: the chapter is one sentence, names no mineral and gives no description, so the material cannot be tied to an ore, a slag or a stone from the text alone* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** The translator marks this chapter TOXIC. This entry is a historical record only. It is not a recommendation, and nobody should take or apply lead stone.

**Named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder. The first-pass check treated it as a duplicate of the lead ore of 5-100 (CPU-02430); an audit disagreed, because 5-98 and 5-100 are separate chapters with separate headings, and this entry keeps it apart. A search of `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` for "lead stone" and "molubdoeides" found this chapter, the index entries and nothing in another tradition on disk.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-98, *Molubdoeides Lithos* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` line 10317; name-index row in `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv` at row 871, translator gloss "Lead Stone TOXIC", class guess mineral; list row in `_sources/dioscorides.csv` at line 824, "Molubdoeides Lithos", mineral; the book index lists "Lead Stone 792", and "MOLUBDOEIDES LITHOS" in an index at text line 12320). The whole chapter was read. It reads (line 10320):

"Lead stone has the same properties as scoria [slag] and is washed in the same way."

In the text file this sentence is followed on the same line by the heading of the next chapter ("5-99. STIMMI", antimony), which is a layout fault of the e-text, not part of the chapter.

**What this sends the reader to.** "Scoria" is the lead slag of the chapter before, 5-97 (CPU-02424). That chapter says the best slag "looks like cerussa [white lead ore], thick and hard to break with no trace of lead in it, yellowish in colour and glistening like glass", that it is "good for the same things as burnt lead but is more astringent", and that it is washed in a mortar with repeated changes of water, settled and made into tablets (line 10316). By Dioscorides' own cross-reference, the lead stone has those properties and is washed that way. The chapter gives nothing more.

**What the chapter does not give.** No place, no colour, no test of quality, no season, no adulterant, no use of its own and no dose. The only thing the chapter itself says about the material is that it is a *lithos* (stone) of lead-like kind, as the heading and the translator's gloss have it.

**Not the same as the chapters around it.** The lead slag (5-97) is a smelting residue. The lead ore *molubdaina* (5-100, CPU-02430) has a long description of its own, with a place (near Sebastia and Corycum), a colour and a method of making. Dioscorides lists all three separately. The relation between them is not stated by the text; the lead-stone chapter links itself only to the slag.

**Identity.** The material cannot be identified from what the chapter says. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated from the chapter. The name and the translator's gloss say it is a lead-like stone, and the chapter ties its properties to lead slag, but no mineral is named. A stone called "lead stone" might contain lead compounds; this is a general inference from the name and is not asserted.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice. Toxic.** The chapter records that the lead stone has the properties of lead slag and is washed the same way. It gives no use of its own. Lead compounds are poisonous, and the Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this material.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives no preparation of its own: the lead stone "is washed in the same way" as lead slag (line 10320). The slag's washing is given in 5-97: wash in a mortar with water poured on, pour off the yellowish water, repeat until the material is used up, settle, pour out the water and make into tablets (line 10316). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any quantity from this entry.** [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Greek (DMM).** The chapter prints no quality of its own; it says the lead stone has "the same properties" as the slag, and the slag chapter prints one, "more astringent" than burnt lead (line 10316). That quality reaches the stone only through the cross-reference.

### Contraindications

- **TOXIC.** Lead compounds are poisonous. Do not ingest, inhale or apply this material.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children:** no statement in the sources retrieved. Lead is especially harmful in pregnancy and childhood. [USER: verify]
- **Identity:** the chapter does not say what the stone is; no material should be assumed to match it.
