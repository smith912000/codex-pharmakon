### Origin & Tradition

Kurchi is a small deciduous tree of the Indian subcontinent and tropical Africa whose bark and seeds are among the most esteemed anti-diarrhoeal and anti-dysenteric remedies of the Indian traditions — a reputation so fixed that it is enshrined in the botanical epithet *antidysenterica*. In **Ayurveda** the bark and seed (**Kutaja** and its seed **Indrayava**) are the classic remedy for *atisara* and *pravahika* — diarrhoea and dysentery, including the bloody, mucoid dysentery of amoebic and bacillary infection — and for chronic bowel complaints, intestinal parasites and bleeding disorders. The Unani tradition (*kurchi*) and colonial-era Western pharmacy (under the name *conessi* or *tellicherry bark*) adopted it for the same purpose, and it was investigated as an amoebicide.

The bark's therapeutic identity is unusually focused and well-grounded: a **bitter, astringent, cooling** drug specifically for the inflamed, dysenteric, over-loose bowel — combining astringency (to bind), bitterness (anti-inflammatory/digestive), and a genuine **antiprotozoal/antibacterial** action against the organisms of dysentery. Its activity is attributed to a family of **steroidal alkaloids** (conessine chief among them), making kurchi a notable example of a traditional remedy with a specific, mechanistically plausible anti-infective action matching its name.
