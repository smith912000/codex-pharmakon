# Jack-in-the-Pulpit / Tian Nan Xing (天南星)

## *Arisaema erubescens* (Wall.) Schott | Family: Araceae

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ, FMT

**Energetic Classification:** Warm, dry, acrid, dispersing; drying to Damp, transforming to Phlegm — **toxic (有毒) in the raw state by the tradition's own classification**

**Training Phase:** Advanced / Practitioner-Supervised only — never self-administered, never raw

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Arisaema erubescens* throws a single hooded spathe out of the leaf litter — a green-and-purple striped cowl bent over an upright spadix. English-speakers call the genus Jack-in-the-Pulpit: the preacher under his canopy. The Chinese name is stranger and older — **Tian Nan Xing** (天南星), "Heavenly Southern Star," a name whose reference is not settled and which sits oddly on a low, damp-woodland aroid. The drug is the corm-like tuber, dug in autumn. It looks like an unassuming flattened bulb. It is one of the most aggressive irritants in the entire Chinese materia medica.

This entry exists because of what the tradition does to that tuber before it will allow it near a patient. Pasque Flower (CPU-00595) teaches processing-as-chemistry in one step — dry it. Tian Nan Xing teaches the same lesson at ten times the scale, ten times the elaboration, and ten times the consequence for getting it wrong. **The processing *is* the drug.**

**Chinese Medicine / Formula Tradition (FMT)** — *Arisaematis Rhizoma* (天南星) is a major and long-attested drug, classed as acrid, bitter, warm, and **toxic**, entering the Lung, Liver and Spleen channels. Its classical function statement is fourfold and tightly worded: **dry damp** (燥湿), **transform phlegm** (化痰), **dispel wind** (祛风), **stop spasm** (止痉). The indications that follow from it are the wind-phlegm disorders — cough with copious thick sputum; and, more distinctively, the **wind-stroke patterns**: hemiplegia and loss of speech following stroke (中风), epileptic seizure (癫痫), facial paralysis and deviation of eye and mouth (口眼歪斜), tetany and opisthotonos. It is a drug for the body that has seized, gone rigid, or gone slack on one side. Externally, applied as a paste, it is used for swellings and sores.

Modern Chinese Pharmacopoeia recognises *Arisaematis Rhizoma* as the dried tuber of *A. erubescens*, *A. heterophyllum*, or *A. amurense* — three species under one drug name. According to PubMed, a 2021 review in *Zhongguo Zhong Yao Za Zhi* confirms this three-species pharmacopoeial definition and catalogues the constituents across all three: alkaloids, flavonoids, phenylpropanoids, lignans and benzene-ring derivatives, steroids and terpenes, glycosides and esters, with reported cytotoxic, antitumour, antibacterial and nematicidal activities — while noting that most published bioactivity work is on **crude extract** rather than characterised monomers ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.19540/j.cnki.cjcmm.20210513.601)). That three-species pooling matters: much "Tian Nan Xing" data is not *erubescens*-specific, and is not treated as such here.

**The processing tradition (FMT) — the actual doctrine.** The raw tuber (生南星, *Sheng Nan Xing*) is essentially not used internally. The tradition instead specifies distinct processed forms, each a different drug with a different function statement:

- **制南星 / Zhi Nan Xing** — the tuber soaked repeatedly in changed water over days, then processed with **fresh ginger juice and alum** (明矾), then dried. This is the standard internal-use form. The soaking-and-changing of water is not incidental; it is repeated to exhaustion.
- **胆南星 / Dan Nan Xing** — the processed powder combined and **fermented with animal bile** (ox or pig), aged, and dried. This transforms the drug's energetics entirely: the warm, acrid, damp-drying Zhi Nan Xing becomes a **cool** drug that clears heat, resolves phlegm-heat, extinguishes wind and calms fright — used for phlegm-heat cough, childhood convulsions with fever, and seizure. A warm drug is turned into a cool drug by fermentation. The tradition asserts this transformation flatly and builds separate prescriptions around each form.

**Shamanic / Ethnobotanical layer (SBJ) — attribution caution.** The wind-stroke and seizure indications sit adjacent to older Chinese conceptions of possession, fright, and wind-as-invasive-agent, and Araceae aroids appear in ritual contexts across Asia. But a *specific* attested shamanic or entheogenic use of *A. erubescens* is **not established** and should not be asserted. The SBJ tag here reflects the drug's position in the wind/fright/spirit-disorder conceptual field, not a documented ritual practice. **[USER: verify]** before this tag survives to print — it may be better dropped.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer**

The obvious symbolic reading is the hooded preacher and the star-name: a plant of the covered thing, the hidden speech, prescribed for the patient whose speech has been taken by stroke. That is signature-thinking and it explains nothing. Deflate it.

The doctrine that *does* survive is the one the tradition wrote in procedure rather than in poetry. **The tradition insists that this drug cannot be used as it is found, and specifies exactly, laboriously, and non-negotiably what must be done to it.** Days of soaking with changed water. Ginger. Alum. Bile. Fermentation. Ageing. No other class of Chinese drug carries this weight of processing instruction, and the tradition marks the drug 有毒 — *toxic* — in its own classification system, which is a warning written in the tradition's own hand, not one imposed by modern toxicology.

Map that onto real chemistry and it holds up. The acrid toxicity of Araceae tubers is carried by **calcium oxalate raphides** — microscopic needle crystals, packed in specialised ejector cells, that on chewing or crushing are fired into oral and pharyngeal mucosa, where they lacerate tissue and open a channel for co-delivered **irritant proteins** to inflame it. This is a mechanical-plus-chemical injury system. Now read the processing: **prolonged soaking with repeated water changes leaches and disrupts the raphide-bearing tissue and washes out water-soluble irritants**; the physical damage to the ejector apparatus removes the delivery mechanism; alum and long processing further degrade the irritant protein fraction; fermentation with bile subjects what remains to weeks of enzymatic and microbial attack.

The tradition did not know about raphides or oxalate crystals. It knew that this tuber burned, that soaking-and-changing-water made it stop burning, and that the exact sequence mattered enough to write down and enforce for centuries. That is empirical detoxification chemistry, encoded as ritual procedure. **It is chemically necessary.** A practitioner who treats the processing as superstitious ballast and shortcuts it has not liberated the drug — they have re-armed it.

### Active Components

Data specific to *A. erubescens* is genuinely sparse, and much of what circulates is pooled across the three pharmacopoeial species or drawn from crude extracts. Stated honestly:

- **⚠️ CALCIUM OXALATE RAPHIDES** — needle-form crystals in ejector cells; the primary acrid-toxic principle of the raw tuber. Mechanical mucosal injury. **FLAGGED.**
- **⚠️ Irritant proteins / co-delivered inflammatory fraction** — the chemical half of the injury system; the raphides open the wound, these inflame it. Characterisation in *A. erubescens* specifically is incomplete. **FLAGGED.**
- **Alkaloids, flavonoids, phenylpropanoids, lignans, benzene-ring derivatives, steroids, terpenes, glycosides, esters** — the constituent classes catalogued for pharmacopoeial *Arisaematis Rhizoma* across *A. erubescens*, *A. heterophyllum* and *A. amurense* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.19540/j.cnki.cjcmm.20210513.601)). Pooled, not species-resolved.
- **Bile acids** — **not from the plant.** Present in Dan Nan Xing because bile was added. These are quantifiable and differ measurably between cattle-bile and pig-bile preparations (see Scientific Correlation). The processing does not merely subtract toxins; it *adds a second pharmacology*.

The compound responsible for the classical anti-spasmodic and anti-seizure claims is **not identified**. This is the central gap in the entry and it should not be papered over.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Tian Nan Xing sits in this system as the **type-specimen of processing-dependent materia** — the drug that most completely refutes the idea that traditional preparation is decorative. It is included in this Codex for study, not for practice.

Its classical use case — the wind-phlegm seizure and stroke patterns, the body gone rigid or slack — is a real, coherent, centuries-attested indication field within its own system. Whether it *works* is a separate question and the answer is: **not demonstrated**. There is no retrieved controlled evidence that *A. erubescens*, in any processed form, treats stroke sequelae, epilepsy, or facial paralysis in humans.

What it teaches the practitioner is more valuable than what it treats:

1. **Toxicity can be an artefact of state, not of substance.** The tuber is not inherently unusable. It is unusable *raw*.
2. **Processing can invert energetics.** Warm-acrid Zhi Nan Xing becomes cool Dan Nan Xing through fermentation. That is not a rebranding — modern data (below) shows the bile form has measurably different chemistry and a measurable antipyretic action. The tradition's claim that fermentation cools the drug is the closest thing to a *validated* energetic transformation in this Volume.
3. **A tradition's own toxicity flag is evidence.** 有毒 is not modern squeamishness retrofitted onto an innocent plant. The people who used this drug said it was poisonous.

**Cultivator's note.** *A. erubescens* is a woodland-floor aroid of moist, humus-rich, well-drained shade — it wants leaf litter, dappled light, and no summer drought. It is dioecious and, notably, **sex-switching**: an individual's expressed sex tracks its stored resources, small tubers flowering male and large well-fed tubers flowering female. Pollination is by fungus gnats trapped in the spathe; according to PubMed, work on sympatric *Arisaema* species documents partial pollinator-sharing with fungus gnats and incomplete pre-pollination isolation between species ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.10696)) — which is a practical warning that garden *Arisaema* hybridise and that seed-grown material may not be the species on the label. Tubers are lifted in autumn after the foliage dies back. **Handle with gloves at every stage.** The sap is acrid; a cut tuber against bare skin, and especially against eye or mouth, is an injury. Do not taste to identify. Do not let children or livestock near lifted tubers or the bright red berry spikes.

**Identity warning.** *Arisaematis Rhizoma* and *Pinelliae Tuber* (Ban Xia, *Pinellia ternata*) are reciprocally substituted in commerce, and the dried tubers are morphologically hard to tell apart. According to PubMed, DNA barcoding work using *matK* and *rbcL* was developed precisely because conventional identification of these two drugs and their adulterants is unreliable ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.4238/gmr.15017064)). Separately, a shotgun-metabarcoding study of the patent medicine Wuhu San **failed to detect any of the three Tian Nan Xing species in any commercial sample**, which the authors attributed to the material having undergone extensive processing ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2021.607200)). Read that twice: the processing is thorough enough to destroy the plant's own DNA. That is a striking incidental confirmation of how severe these procedures are — and a serious problem for anyone hoping to verify what is actually in a commercial preparation.

### Scientific Correlation

Graded honestly. As with Pasque Flower, the processing doctrine is the strongest thing here and the clinical claims are the weakest.

**Raphide mechanism of raw-tuber toxicity — WELL ESTABLISHED as Araceae class chemistry (Grade A for the class; Grade B species-specific).** The calcium-oxalate-raphide-plus-irritant-protein injury mechanism is standard, well-described toxicology for the Araceae, and the tradition's 有毒 classification and its prohibition on raw internal use are correct. Species-resolved quantitative raphide data for *A. erubescens* specifically was not retrieved this run — a genuine gap.

**Bile fermentation changes the drug measurably — SUPPORTED (Grade B).** This is the strongest direct validation in the entry, and it is a good one. According to PubMed, a 2024 study in the *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* compared **cattle bile Arisaema (CBA)** and **pig bile Arisaema (PBA)** — both fermented from *Arisaema erubescens* (Wall.) Schott with animal bile, both recorded in the Chinese Pharmacopoeia. The authors established a UPLC-TQ MS method and found measurable **differences in bile acid content between the two preparations**. In a dry-yeast-induced fever rat model, **both** exerted a significant antipyretic effect, but **CBA showed quicker onset and longer duration than PBA**. Mechanistically, the antipyretic action was associated with inhibition of inflammation via the **TLR4/NF-κB pathway**, with metabolomics showing disturbance of lipid and amino acid metabolism ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2024.118951)).

Sit with what that supports. The tradition claims that (a) bile fermentation turns a warm drug into a cool, heat-clearing one, and (b) the *choice of bile* matters. A rat febrile model finds that bile Arisaema is genuinely antipyretic — "cooling," in the plainest possible operational sense — and that cattle bile and pig bile give **different kinetics**. Two traditional claims, both non-obvious, both surviving contact with an animal model. This is rare and it deserves to be stated plainly. It is also, equally plainly, **a rat fever model, not a human clinical trial**, and the effect may owe as much to the bile as to the plant.

**Stroke sequelae / hemiplegia / loss of speech — NO EVIDENCE (Grade F).** No retrieved controlled study. The classical indication is unvalidated in humans.

**Epilepsy / seizure / "stop spasm" (止痉) — NO EVIDENCE (Grade F).** No retrieved anticonvulsant study of *A. erubescens* in this run. No responsible compound identified. This is the drug's most distinctive classical claim and it is entirely unsupported by evidence retrieved here.

**Facial paralysis — NO EVIDENCE (Grade F).**

**"Transform phlegm" / expectorant action — NOT ASSESSED.** No retrieved evidence either way. The claim is untested rather than refuted.

**Cytotoxic / antitumour / antibacterial / nematicidal activity — PRELIMINARY, and mostly crude extract (Grade D).** Catalogued in the 2021 constituent review, which itself flags that most bioactivity reports concern crude extracts rather than characterised monomers, and calls for monomer-level work ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.19540/j.cnki.cjcmm.20210513.601)). Species-pooled across three *Arisaema*. Not a basis for any claim.

**Net:** the processing doctrine is substantially vindicated — the raw drug is genuinely dangerous, the detoxification is genuinely necessary, and the bile-fermentation energetic inversion has real animal-model support with the traditional bile-choice distinction detectable in the data. The *therapeutic* claims — stroke, seizure, paralysis — are entirely unvalidated. This is a drug whose preparation is better evidenced than its purpose.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Raw / Zhi Nan Xing:** Acrid, bitter, **warm**. **Toxic (有毒).** Channels: Lung, Liver, Spleen. Dries damp, transforms phlegm, dispels wind, stops spasm. Strongly dispersing and drying — the tradition treats it as a drug that moves stuck, cold, thick things and quiets violent movement.

**Dan Nan Xing (bile-fermented):** Bitter, **cool**. Toxicity substantially reduced by processing. Clears heat, resolves phlegm-heat, extinguishes wind, calms fright. Preferred where the wind-phlegm pattern presents with heat — febrile convulsion, phlegm-heat cough.

The two forms are the same plant and, energetically, opposite drugs. This is the sharpest illustration in Volume I that in this tradition **energetic classification attaches to the prepared substance, not the species**. A cultivator who grows *A. erubescens* has grown neither of these drugs. They have grown a raw material that is currently a poison.

### Contraindications

⚠️ **RAW TUBER — SEVERELY TOXIC. ORAL AND PHARYNGEAL INJURY RISK.**

- **Never chew, taste, swallow, or juice the raw tuber.** Calcium oxalate raphides fire into oral and pharyngeal mucosa on crushing, delivering irritant proteins into the lacerations. The result is immediate intense burning, profuse salivation, and **severe swelling of the lips, tongue, mouth and throat**. Pharyngeal and laryngeal oedema is the acute danger and can obstruct the airway. Nausea, vomiting, and gastrointestinal injury follow ingestion.
- **Airway swelling is a medical emergency.** Anyone who has bitten raw *Arisaema* — most often a child, or an adult attempting field identification by taste — requires urgent medical assessment, not home management.
- **Never taste-test to identify.** There is no safe amount.
- **Gloves at all stages of handling, lifting, cutting, and processing.** Sap contact causes dermatitis and blistering. **Keep away from eyes** — ocular exposure to Araceae raphides causes serious injury.
- **The bright red berry spike is attractive and dangerous.** Keep children and livestock away from fruiting plants and lifted tubers.
- **Do not shortcut the processing.** Partial soaking, skipped water changes, or omitted ginger/alum steps leave an active irritant. The procedure's length is the point.
- **Pregnancy — contraindicated.** A drug classed as toxic in its own tradition, with no safety data.
- **Lactation — contraindicated.** No data.
- **Children** — Dan Nan Xing has classical paediatric use for febrile convulsion, but **only** as a processed drug within a formula, under a trained practitioner. Nothing here authorises paediatric use.
- **Yin deficiency with dryness, or dry cough without phlegm** — classically contraindicated; this is a drying drug.
- **Identity risk is real.** Commercial *Arisaematis Rhizoma* is reciprocally substituted with *Pinelliae Tuber* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.4238/gmr.15017064)), and processing is severe enough that species DNA may be undetectable in finished patent medicines ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2021.607200)). You frequently cannot verify what you have.
- **Practitioner-tier drug.** This entry is documentation. It is not a protocol.
