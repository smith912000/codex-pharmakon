# Mocharasa — Mocharasa (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna 1907; printed "Mocharasa")

## *A vegetable juice or exudate used in the Priyangvadi group and in compound preparations for urinary disease and for ulcers; the translation gives no Latin name or description. The identification is ours, at medium-to-low confidence: the name is read as moca, a Sanskrit name of the silk-cotton tree, plus rasa, "juice, exudate", that is, the gum of Bombax ceiba (Shalmali) [USER: verify against a Sanskrit lexicon and Dallana]; the text on disk never says so, and in one passage it prints Shalmali and Mocharasa side by side as two items* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Sushruta Samhita* among the texts on disk, in three places.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder from an extraction of the translation's group lists; the 30 October check pass kept it as a distinct substance with a likely identification as the gum of *Bombax ceiba*. The spine's Shalmali entry (`Volume_I_Plant_Based/entries/I_860_Shalmali_Bombax_ceiba.md`, CPU-00394) already says that the gum of that tree is *mocharasa*; that statement there rests on secondary reading and not on the Sushruta text, so the two rows are kept apart until the identification is settled. A merge would be Smith's decision. [USER: verify]

**Sushruta (SS), Sutrasthana ch.38, the Priyangvadi group.** The chapter classifies drugs "usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 9). The group is printed at lines 320 to 324 (Vol. I lines 15661 to 15666): "The group of medicinal drugs known as the Priyangvadi Gana consists of Priyangu, Samanga, Dhataki, Naga-pushpa, Chandana, Kuchandana, Mocharasa, Rasanjana, Kumbhika, Srotohnjana, Padma-keshara, Jojanvalli, and Dirghamula." No metrical text is printed under this group alone. The next group, the Amvashtha'di, follows at lines 326 to 334, and a single metrical text after it (lines 337 to 342) speaks of "the two medicinal recipes or groups": they "prove beneficial in a case of deranged Pitta, favour the healing of ulcers, bring about the adhesion of fractured bones and prove curative in cases of dysentery where the stools are found to consist of lumps of thick and matured mucous (Pakvatisara)." That the sentence covers the Priyangvadi group as well as the Amvashtha'di is a reading by position. [USER: verify against the printed page]

**Sushruta (SS), Chikitsitasthana ch.11, urinary disease.** In the treatment of *Prameha* (the urinary disorders), fermented and decoction preparations are to be made "with Priyangu, Ananta, Yuthika, Padma (Bhargi), Trayantika, Lohitika, Amvashtha, bark of pomegranate, Shala-parni, Padma (lotus), Tunga, Kesara, Dhataki, Vakula, Shalmali, Shriveshtaka and Mocharasa", and are to be given to the patient (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch11.txt` lines 187 to 193; Vol. II line 16909). The list names Shalmali and Mocharasa as two items. That does not prove they are two plants (the tree and its gum would give two items), but it shows that the translator treated them as two names.

**Sushruta (SS), Chikitsitasthana ch.17, an oil for ulcers.** In the passage on the sinus due to a foreign body (*Shalyaja Nadi*), "oil cooked with the decoction of the tender fruits of the Kumbhika, Kharjura, Kapittha, Vilva and the Vanaspatis (Vata, etc.), and with the Kalkas of Musta, Sarala, Priyangu, Sugandhika, Mocharasa, Ahi-pushpa (Nagesvara), Rodhra and Dhataki flowers leads to a speedy healing up (granulation) of ulcers (Vrana) and traumatic sinuses" (`SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt` lines 205 to 217; Vol. II line 18738). A translator's note on the oil says that Dallana's reading differs for one ingredient ("Tali-lala") and that Chakradatta takes another plant in its place; it does not concern Mocharasa.

In all three places Mocharasa is one ingredient among many, and in the formulas it is named with Rodhra and Dhataki flowers, which are usually read as astringent drugs [USER: verify]. Any astringent action in those places is the company it keeps and is not stated of Mocharasa itself.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the gums and exudates of trees are mainly polysaccharides (gum) with tannins and other phenolic compounds in many species; if this is the exudate of *Bombax ceiba*, it would be expected to be such a gum, but that is not established for this item. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Only through its groups and formulas: in the Priyangvadi and Amvashtha'di pair, Pitta, ulcer healing, fractures and dysentery with mucus; in the Prameha preparations, urinary disease; in the Shalyaja Nadi oil, healing of ulcers and traumatic sinuses.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no amount in any formula, no statement of which part of which tree.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend using this item.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources give it as an ingredient of a fermented or decoction preparation (Chikitsa ch.11) and as a "Kalka" (paste) in a medicated oil cooked with a fruit decoction (Chikitsa ch.17). No quantity of Mocharasa is printed. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Tree gums are widely used as thickeners and demulcents, and tannin-rich barks and gums act as astringents on tissue. Whether this item is such a gum is not established by anything read. The classic's "Prameha", "Pitta", "Pakvatisara" and "Vrana" have no one-to-one modern equivalents and are not translated into any. ### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(c) Absent.* Mocharasa appears in a therapeutic group and in two compound formulas, one for urinary disease and one for ulcers and sinuses. It was not found in a *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) chapter, and no *ojas* effect is assigned to it. Whatever the Ayurvedic tradition at large says of the gum of the Shalmali tree, that is not in the text read here.

**Practitioner's note.** A named ingredient with a probable but unverified identity, found in formulas for urinary disease and ulcers. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use, and the entry's use is to keep the name and its three contexts on record.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The reading as the gum of *Bombax ceiba* is ours; do not substitute a product for it on the strength of this entry.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, the weak.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Urinary disease, ulcers, sinuses, dysentery.** These are medical conditions; use proper care. Do not treat a wound with an unidentified gum or oil.
- **Uncertain text.** The translation's OCR spells the name "Mocharasa" in all three places; the Priyangvadi group has no metrical text of its own in the translation.
