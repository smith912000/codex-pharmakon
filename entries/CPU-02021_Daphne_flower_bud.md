# Daphne flower bud — Yuan Hua 芫花 (Shennong bencao jing); alias Qu Shui 去水, "water remover"

## *Daphne genkwa Siebold & Zucc., lilac daphne, a small deciduous shrub whose flower buds are the Chinese drug yuan hua; the identification is ours, from the Chinese name and the standard modern identification, and is of high confidence for the modern drug; the classic's Wu Pu layer describes a shrub with purple, red or white flowers that appear before the leaves, which fits* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

> **TOXICITY CAUTION.** Yuan hua is a poisonous drug and its source, like other daphnes, is a skin and gut irritant. The classic's own Wu Pu layer calls it toxic ("有毒"), uses it "to poison fish", and says that taken long it makes people purge. This entry is a historical record of what a classic text said and is not a recommendation to use the plant in any form.

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** The principal text read is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`. Its other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), also has the drug, as "Yuan Hua (Flos Daphnis Genkwae)" (line 3260), and is read here as a second witness, attributed to Yang. European daphnes are treated from other traditions in I_1063 (*Daphne gnidium*) and I_1064 (*Daphne mezereum*), which do not cite the Shennong text.

**A name collision on the spine.** The spine row began as an unverified placeholder, "Aos Wikstroemiae Japonicae [SBJ]" ("Aos" is a garble of *Flos*, "flower"). That Latin name is Yang's for a different Shennong drug, 荛花 *rao hua* (Yang: "Yao Hua", line 3293; Sun: entry 荛花, line 3361, whose paragraph is 味苦，平，寒 and governs cold damage and warm malaria and "precipitates the twelve waters"). Sun and Yang both list *rao hua* and *yuan hua* as separate drugs. This entry is the 芫花 monograph as assigned; 荛花 is not written here and, as far as the files read show, has no entry of its own. **Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 芫花 in the lower classic (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 芫花 *yuan hua*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3585). The Shennong paragraph reads: 味辛，温 ("acrid, warm"); 主咳逆上气 ("governs counterflow cough and rising qi"), 喉鸣、喘 ("rattling in the throat, wheezing"), 咽肿、短气 ("swollen throat, short breath"), 蛊毒、鬼疟 ("gu poison, ghost malaria"), 疝瘕、痈肿 ("mounting and conglomeration, abscesses and swellings"), 杀虫鱼 ("kills worms and fish"); alias 一名去水 ("water remover"); habitat 生川谷 ("grows in river valleys", line 3587). As printed, the paragraph does not say "toxic" and has no 久服 clause. Sun himself moved the drug into the herb section from the tree section of the older arrangement: the closing note of the herbs reads "lower grade, forty-nine kinds; formerly forty-eight; having examined, yuan hua belongs here from the tree section" (line 3595), and the tree section's note reads "now yuan hua is moved into the herbs" (line 3779).

Directly after the alias and habitat, the text continues inside a bracket that is not closed in the printed text, headed 旧《吴普》曰 ("the old Wu Pu says", lines 3587 and 3589). It gives the aliases 败花, 儿草根 and 黄大戟, and the flavours of the authorities: "Shennong and Huangdi: toxic; Bian Que and Qi Bo: bitter; Li shi: very cold" (神农、黄帝：有毒；扁鹊、岐伯：苦；李氏：大寒). It then describes the plant: 二月生，叶青，加浓则黑。华有紫、赤、白者。三月实落尽，叶乃生 ("it is born in the second month; the leaves are green, turning black as they thicken [as printed]; the flowers are purple, red or white; in the third month the [fruit or flowers] have all fallen, and then the leaves grow"). Flowers before leaves fit *Daphne genkwa*, which flowers before its leaves open; the Codex records the fit and does not claim more. The same layer gives harvest and root: 三月、五月采花 ("flowers gathered in the third and fifth months"); the root, 一名赤芫根 ("red yuan root"), judged by "Shennong and Lei Gong: bitter, toxic", grows at Handan and is gathered in the eighth and ninth months and dried in the shade; and 久服，令人泄 ("taken long, it makes people purge") and 可用毒鱼 ("can be used to poison fish").

The Mingyi bielu layer (《名医》曰, line 3591) adds the aliases 毒鱼 ("fish poison") and 杜芫, says its root is called 蜀桑 and "can be used to poison fish", gives the habitat 生淮源 ("grows at the source of the Huai") and the harvest 三月三日采药，阴干 ("the 'medicine' is gathered on the third day of the third month and dried in the shade").

Sun Xingyan's note (案, line 3593) is philological and is the commentator's, not the Shennong text's. It cites the *Shuowen* (芫 is "fish poison"), the *Erya* with Guo Pu's gloss on a southern tree whose fruit has a thick skin and red juice and "stores egg-fruits", and the *Fanzi jiran* (yuan hua comes from the Sanfu region). It also quotes the *Shiji* life of Cang Gong: a woman of Linzi sick with a pinworm conglomeration was given "a pinch" (一撮) of yuan hua to drink, and "expelled pinworms by the sheng, and the illness ended" (出蛲可数升，病已). Sun then cites Yan Shigu's gloss on the *Jijiu pian*, which says Guo Pu's description is mistaken and belongs to another tree, not the fish poison. The pinch is a quantity in a reported case and not a dose to follow.

Yang 1998 (line 3260) renders the paragraph as "Yuan Hua (Flos Daphnis Genkwae) is bitter and warm. It is toxic, treating mainly cough and counterflow qi ascent, rales in the throat, panting, swollen throat, shortness of breath, gu toxins, ghosts, malaria, mounting conglomeration, welling abscesses, and swellings. It kills worms and fish. Its other name is Qu Shui (Water Eliminator)." Yang therefore prints "bitter" where Sun prints "acrid" (辛) and "toxic" where Sun's paragraph does not; the Codex records the difference and does not settle it. His footnote 200 (line 3270) is commentary: "This medicinal is woody rather than herbaceous. It is able to drastically precipitate water", used for various rheums.

**Appendix.** An appendix of drug relations, later than the main text, says of it: 芫花 决明为使；反甘草 ("yuan hua: jue ming is its envoy; it is opposed to gan cao", line 4429). Gan cao is in turn said to be "opposed to" gan sui, da ji, yuan hua and hai zao (line 4295). Yuan hua is also named as the envoy of ba dou (line 4527) and as a thing that shi dan, huang lian, fang feng, mi la and others dislike or fear (lines 4207, 4321, 4325, 4579, 4587, 4609). The preface (line 4117) says that drugs standing in "dislike" or "opposed" relations should not be combined.

### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, the flower buds of *Daphne genkwa* contain flavonoids and daphnane-type diterpene esters (for example yuanhuacine); the diterpene esters are irritant and are generally regarded as the toxic principles of the drug and of related daphnes. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice, and the drug is poisonous. The classic records an acrid, warm flower for cough, throat, swelling and abscess complaints and for killing worms and fish, and its Wu Pu layer notes that it is toxic, can poison fish, and makes people purge when taken long. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose and recommends no use. The "gu poison" and "ghost malaria" of the paragraph are historical categories and are not translated into modern diagnoses.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations in the sources read are harvests: the flower in the third and fifth months (Wu Pu layer) and the "medicine" on the third day of the third month, dried in the shade (Mingyi layer, line 3591); the root is gathered in the eighth and ninth months. The one quantity is the "pinch" in the *Shiji* case quoted in Sun's note (line 3593), a reported event and not a dose. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Daphnes (family Thymelaeaceae) are irritant, poisonous shrubs; contact with the sap or buds can inflame skin and mucous membranes, and swallowing them causes severe vomiting and diarrhoea. *Daphne genkwa* flower buds are a recognised toxic drug in modern Chinese pharmacy, used only with processing and under professional direction. The classic's claim "kills fish" matches the plant's use as a fish poison, as the text itself records. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**SBJ:** flavour and nature 味辛，温, "acrid, warm" (line 3585). The old Wu Pu layer records "toxic" (Shennong and Huangdi), "bitter" (Bian Que and Qi Bo) and "very cold" (Li shi) (line 3587), and "bitter, toxic" for the root (Shennong and Lei Gong); Yang prints "bitter and warm". The Codex does not settle these disagreements.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The paragraph for 芫花 stands in the lower classic, whose preface says the lower medicines "treat disease, correspond to the earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to remove cold and heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and cure illness" (主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经; line 2935). The paragraph has no 久服 clause; the only long-use statement is the Wu Pu layer's 久服，令人泄, "taken long, it makes people purge", which is the opposite of a life-nourishing promise. The register is (b). (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 芫花 *yuan hua*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3585)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. This is a toxic lower-class drug that the classic's own preface says is not for long use, whose Wu Pu layer says long use purges, and whose other use was to poison fish. The honest use of the entry is as a record, and as a warning.

### Contraindications

- **Poisonous:** do not eat, drink or apply any form of the flower buds, root or sap. Poisoning (vomiting, severe diarrhoea, skin and mouth burns) needs medical care.
- **Skin and eyes:** handling the fresh plant or buds can irritate skin and eyes.
- **Pregnancy:** the drug is a strong purgative and irritant; do not use in pregnancy. [USER: verify]
- **Appendix pairings:** the appendix lists many "opposed" and "dislike" relations with yuan hua, notably with gan cao (lines 4295 and 4429). These are historical pairings and not a modern interaction list.
- **Quantities:** no dose is given and none should be inferred from the "pinch" case.
