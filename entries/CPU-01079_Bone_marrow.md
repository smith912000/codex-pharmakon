# Bone marrow — Mueloi (as printed in Dioscorides 2-95) / Marrow, Majja (Sushruta, Bhishagratna's English) / Bone-marrow, Marrow-of-an-Ox (Bryan's Ebers)
## *The soft fatty tissue inside the bones of animals (the translator of Dioscorides glosses "Bone Marrow"; Dioscorides names the deer, calf, bull, goat and sheep); Bryan gives "Bone-marrow" and "Marrow-of-an-Ox"* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP, DMM, SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Note: `Volume_III_Animal_Derived/entries/III_10_BoneMarrow.md` is an earlier unlinked draft, superseded by this entry.*

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The English text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 *The Papyrus Ebers*, a translation with summaries (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`); the Egyptian words are not printed. The spine's list row is `_sources/ebers.csv` line 101 ("Bone-marrow", id uncertain: yes), citing "Ghalioungui 1987 trans., Glossary I (Medicaments)", which is not on disk and was not read. Two lines of Bryan's text name marrow. (1) In Chapter VII, Organic Remedies, he lists the parts of animals the Egyptians "were forced to yield": "Hair Feathers Horns Hoof Hide Eyes Ears Teeth Brain Blood Bile Bones Marrow Liver Spleen Guts ..." (lines 1409-1415). (2) In his summary of the eye remedies (line 2734) he writes that salves are given in which the collyrium is combined with various things: "in one the Collyrium is simply combined with Honey; in another with Honey and Sap-from-Fresh-Onions; in another with the Marrow-of-an-Ox; in another with Goose-grease and Water ...". That is Bryan's paraphrase of the papyrus, and the Egyptian text, the column and the quantities were not read. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 2-95, *Mueloi*, "Bone Marrow" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt` lines 5349-5351; page 220 in the translation's index). The whole chapter: "Deer marrow is the best, then that from a calf, after, that from a bull, then a goat and a sheep. They are gathered at the time when spring is drawing on, and towards the autumn, for at other times of the year it is found in the bones — looking bloody and similar to flesh that is easily broken. It is hard to recognise except by whoever takes it out of the bones and preserves it. All marrow is softening, purifying, and healing, and fills up the hollow sores of ulcers. The bone marrow of a deer (rubbed on) also drives away venomous creatures. It is prepared like fat [above] being taken out of the choicest and freshest bones. Water is poured on it, and afterwards it is strained through a linen cloth and similarly washed until the water becomes clean. Afterwards it is melted in a double jar, the filth that swims on top is taken off with a feather, and it is strained out into a mortar. After it has congealed it is stored in a new earthenware jar, the filth that lies at the bottom having been carefully scraped away. If you want to store it unprepared follow the directions given in fats of poultry and of geese." Two neighbouring chapters were read. 2-94 *Steaton Dunamis*, "Medicinal uses of Fats" (line 5342), says "All fat is warming, softening and purifying", and 2-91 *Stear pos aromatisteon* (line 5318) gives a method of scenting "Calves' fat as well as fat from bulls and deer and the bone marrow of deer": the fat is skinned, washed, boiled in fragrant wine, cooled overnight, melted again and skimmed, and perfumed with spices.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The English is Bhishagratna's 1907 translation. Marrow appears in four places, and the first two concern the body's own marrow, not marrow as a substance. (1) *Sutrasthana* ch.14 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch14.txt` lines 93-96): "The chyle produces blood. From blood is formed flesh. From flesh originates fat which gives rise to bones. From bones originate marrow, which, in its turn, germinates semen." Ch.46 (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 930) lists "the seven fundamental principles of the organism (such as the lymph chyle, blood, flesh, fat, bone, marrow, and semen)", marrow being the sixth. Ch.15 (`SS_Sutra_ch15.txt` lines 84-92) says the marrow "contributes towards the formation and increase of semen, and fills in the internal cavities of the bones, and forms the chief source of strength, amorous feelings and hilarity"; the loss or waste of marrow gives "a lesser quantity of semen, aching pain in the bones and breaking pain in the bone-joints which have become marrowless" (lines 195-200), and an excess of marrow "a heaviness of the eyes and to the members of the body" (lines 322-324). (2) Ch.42, the specific properties of tastes (`SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` lines 158-163): the sweet taste has "the virtue of increasing the quantity of lymph-chyle, blood, flesh, fat, bone marrow, albumen (ojas), semen, and milk in a parturient woman". That is a property of the sweet taste, not of animal marrow. (3) Ch.45, the chapter on liquid substances (`SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 1051-1072), among the fats: "The oil, myosin (Vasa), fat, marrow, and Ghritam obtained from animals, which live in villages (Gramya), or frequent the marshy swamps (Anupa), or are aquatic (Audoka) in their habits, are heavy, heat-making in their potency, and sweet in taste. They subdue the bodily Vayu, while those obtained from Jangala (such as deer, etc.) or carnivorous animals, or from those possessed of unbifurcated hoofs, are light, cool in their potency, astringent in taste, and prove curative in cases of haemoptysis. The fat, marrow, etc. of animals of the Pratuda and Vishkira species (doves, pigeons, etc.) reduce the bodily Kapham. Of clarified butter, oil, myosin (Vasa), fat and marrow of animals each is heavier in digestion, and possesses a greater power of subduing the bodily Vayu than the one immediately preceding it in the order of enumeration." (4) *Chikitsasthana* ch.31, on the medicinal uses of the Snehas, or oleaginous substances (`SS_Chikitsa_ch31.txt`): a footnote says "The Sneha is of four kinds, viz., clarified butter, oil, lard and marrow, of which clarified butter and oil are generally used" (line 43), and "The use of marrow or of medicated Ghritas mixed with appropriate drugs is beneficial to men of strong digestive capacity (Dipta-vahni), or to those afflicted with a deranged Vayu, or to those whose bowels are not easily moved, or who are capable of undergoing a large amount of physical hardship" (lines 269-273). In ch.40 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch40.txt` lines 646-652), in the choice of snuff, "the snuff should consist of Majja (marrow) in a case marked by (the concerted actions of the deranged) Vayu and Pitta". Marrow also enters unguents for elongating the ear lobes (ch.16 of the *Sutrasthana*, lines 305-309: "milk, fat, and marrow of any such animals and birds as the Godha, the Pratudas, the Vishkiras, the Anupas, or the Audakas").

### Active Components

Bone marrow is the soft tissue in the cavities of bones. Red marrow makes blood cells; yellow marrow is mostly fat. Eaten as food it supplies mostly fat (triglycerides and cholesterol), with some protein and small amounts of minerals and vitamins; these are general facts. The sources name no active component and give no quantities; no study is cited.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The texts record marrow as a softening, healing salve for ulcers (Dioscorides), as an eye-salve ingredient (Bryan's summary of Ebers), and, in Sushruta, as one of four Snehas, a snuff base, and an unguent ingredient. The Codex has no protocol for any of this and offers none. The earlier unlinked draft of this entry (III_10) proposed marrow as a restorative for deep depletion; no source read for this entry says that, and it is not carried over. Sushruta's physiology places marrow in the body's sequence of tissues, but nothing read says that eating animal marrow builds the body's marrow. Animal tissue can carry infection, and the animal is killed for the use.

### Preparation & Dosing

Greek (2-95): deer marrow best, then calf, bull, goat, sheep; gathered towards spring and autumn from the choicest and freshest bones; washed with water, strained through linen, melted in a double jar, skimmed with a feather, strained into a mortar, congealed and stored in a new earthenware jar; or perfumed as in 2-91. Used rubbed on. Ayurvedic: marrow as one of the four Snehas (ch.31, with ghee, oil and lard); as a snuff in combined *Vayu* and *Pitta* cases (ch.40); in ear-lobe unguents (ch.16). Egyptian: marrow of an ox combined with collyrium in an eye salve (Bryan's summary only). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Marrow is high in fat; the fat of ruminants is mostly saturated. Red marrow is the site of blood-cell formation in the living animal, and cooked marrow is a food. Raw or undercooked animal tissue can carry bacteria and parasites. Animal products from cattle and sheep are subject to controls for prion disease in some countries, and the Codex gives no advice beyond noting that. Nothing read ties eating marrow to restoration of the eater's own marrow, blood or strength. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** Bryan prints no quality for marrow; the corpus is organised by complaint.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** "All marrow is softening, purifying, and healing" (2-95); fat generally "warming, softening and purifying" (2-94, the neighbouring chapter).

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Per Sutra ch.45: marrow of animals of village, marsh and aquatic habit is heavy, heat-making in potency and sweet, and subdues *Vayu*; marrow of Jangala, carnivorous and non-cloven-hoofed animals is light, cool in potency and astringent, and curative in haemoptysis; marrow of the Pratuda and Vishkira birds reduces *Kapham*; and marrow is heavier and more *Vayu*-subduing than the fat, myosin (*Vasa*), oil and clarified butter before it in the order of enumeration.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Marrow is named in a list of animal parts and, in Bryan's summary, as an eye-salve ingredient for a complaint. Nothing says what it does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution, and the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. Bryan reports no recitation for the eye salves summarised; since he summarises, that is not proof that none existed. No deity is attributed. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied here. What he offers is a discipline of attention in preparation: which animal's marrow is best and in which seasons it is gathered, how to wash and strain it, how to store it, and the warning that it is "hard to recognise except by whoever takes it out of the bones". The chapter is therapeutic (softening, purifying, healing).

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Marrow has two roles in the text, and neither is a placement in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter in anything read here. As a bodily tissue it is the sixth of seven *dhatus*, formed from bone and the source of semen, and "the chief source of strength, amorous feelings and hilarity" (ch.15). As a substance it is one of four Snehas, with ghee, oil and lard (ch.31), of which ghee and oil are "generally used". The doctrine of the *dhatu* sequence and the passage on the sweet taste (ch.42) could be read together to suggest that animal marrow nourishes the body's marrow and then its semen and *ojas*; the text does not say so for animal marrow, and the inference is withheld. The sattvic, rajasic and tamasic labels are *Samkhya* and are not used. [USER: verify] against the *rasayana* and *vajikarana* chapters of the *Chikitsasthana* for any marrow prescription.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do. Marrow is a sourced ingredient in three traditions, but each uses it as a salve, a fat or a snuff base for a defined complaint, not as a vital tonic. The seductive reading, that marrow feeds the deepest tissue, is a chain of inferences the Codex does not make: Sushruta states the tissue sequence and states the properties of the animal fat, and does not join them. A cultivator can take from the entry how carefully Dioscorides prepared and stored the stuff, and Sushruta's observation that marrow suits the strong of digestion, and leave the rest.

### Contraindications

- **Infection.** Animal marrow and bone broths can carry bacteria and parasites; cook thoroughly. Never apply raw marrow to wounds or ulcers.
- **Animal-disease controls.** Bone and spinal tissue from cattle and sheep are regulated in some countries because of prion disease; follow local rules. [USER: verify]
- **Fat load.** Marrow is mostly fat; people on fat- or cholesterol-restricted diets, with gallbladder, pancreatic or liver disease should not use it as a remedy.
- **Strong digestion only.** Sushruta recommends marrow as a Sneha for men of "strong digestive capacity", for deranged *Vayu*, for the constipated and for those who can bear hardship; this is traditional doctrine and is not tested here.
- **Dose unknown.** No dose is given for any use in the sources read.
- **Ethics and law.** Using the animal means killing it, and for deer and wild species, hunting and trade may be regulated or banned. [USER: verify]
