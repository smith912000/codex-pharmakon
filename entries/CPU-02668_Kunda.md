**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** The chapter files were searched for Kunda as a word of its own. Hits, with the compounds (Mukundaka, Vata-kundalika, Kukundara) excluded:

- *Uttara ch.51 (asthma), `SS_Uttara_ch51.txt` lines 195 to 197:* "A potion prepared with the flowers of S'irisha, Kadali [OCR "Kada/t"] and of Kunda and with Magadhika and dissolved in the washings of rice would completely cure all forms of asthma." The part named is the flower.
- *Uttara ch.60 (the offerings to spirits), `SS_Uttara_ch60.txt` lines 242 to 244:* the Pitri Grahas "should be propitiated with the offerings made on Kus'a grass together with Madhavi and Kunda flowers on the banks of a river". This is a ritual use, recorded as the text records it.
- *Uttara ch.7 (eye disease), `SS_Uttara_ch07.txt` line 158, and Chikitsa ch.30, `SS_Chikitsa_ch30.txt` line 159:* the flower is used only as a colour for comparison. The first describes a patch "as white as a conch-shell, a Kunda flower or the moon"; the second an Oshadhi plant of the Ajat species "white-coloured like the moon, a conch shell, or a Kunda flower". Neither passage uses the plant as a drug.

Kunda is not among the flowers whose properties Sutra ch.46 lists by name in the passage read (lines 1800 to 1822: Kumuda, Kuvalaya, Utpala, Sindhuvara, Mallika, Malati, Vakula, Patala, Nagam, Champakam), so no property is printed for it there. The vajikarana and rasayana chapters (Chikitsa ch.26 to 30) were not found to name it as a drug.
