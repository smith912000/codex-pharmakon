# Hibiscus vitifolius — Bharadvaji; Vana-karpasa (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Hibiscus vitifolius, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR "Hibiscus Vitifolius.", line 31460) against the Sanskrit name Bharadvaji (line 31407), and, in the OCR spelling "Hibiscus Vitiolius." (line 33250), against a second name, Vana-karpasa (line 33224). Both pairings of a Sanskrit name with its Latin line are positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; neither is printed on one line. The chapters do not print Bharadvaji; they print the near-by name Vana-Karpasi once* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The name the row was keyed on, Bharadvaji, is not in any chapter file. A second appendix name for the same Latin, Vana-karpasa, is matched by a single chapter occurrence of Vana-Karpasi, in a diet for diarrhoea. The Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 431 gives the name as "Bharadvaji", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31407; id line 31460)" and the Latin as "Hibiscus  Vitifolius.", with the note that the pairing was proposed by an agent from the two OCR columns and checked by script. **First pairing, Bharadvaji, checked by counting.** On the page headed "67" (line 31367) the Sanskrit names run from Bahu-mulak (line 31370) to Briddha-daraka (line 31433). The OCR brackets three pairs (Bakula with Vakula, Bala-bhadra with Bala, Barbara with Barbula), so the forty printed lines are thirty-seven entries. The Latin lines (31436 to 31497) are also thirty-seven, the thirty-sixth ("Siphonanthus Indica ; Herpestis Mouniera") being split over two OCR lines. Bharadvaji is the nineteenth Sanskrit entry (Bahu-mulak, Bahu-vara, Bahu-vija, Baka, Batkuchi, Bakula/Vakula, Bala-bhadra/Bala, the damaged "Bskla", Balga, Bandhu-jiva, Bandhuka, Bandhyakotika, Barbara/Barbula, Bhadra-kali, Bhallataka, Bhindi, Bhandira, Bhantaki, **Bharadvaji**), and "Hibiscus Vitifolius." is the nineteenth Latin entry. The neighbours agree: Bhallataka stands against "Semecarpus Anacardium" (line 31455), Bhargi against "Clerodendron Siphonanthus" (31462), Bhavya against "Dilenia Indica" (31465) and Bhringa-raja against "Wedelia Calendulacea ; Eclipta Erecta" (31467). The same page is counted in the entries on Mimosa pudica (I_1383), Mimusops elengi (I_1371) and Amaranthus spinosus (I_1389), which reach the same alignment for other names. [USER: verify]

**Second pairing, Vana-karpasa, checked by counting.** On the page of V-names the Sanskrit lines run from Vacha (line 33214) to Vata-patri (33235) and the Latin lines from "Acorus Calamus" (33240) to "Colcus Ambjnicus" (33262). Vamsa-lochana stands against "Bamboo Mama" (line 33249), Vana-yamani against "Seseli Sambac" (33251) and Vanjula against "Calamus Rotong" (33252), and at the end of the run Vata stands against "Ficus Bengalensis" (33261). **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Bharadvaji with the spellings Bharadvaji, Bharadwaji and Bharadvaja, for OCR-garbled letters, and by fuzzy matching of every word against the name. No plant of that name was found. The only near hit is the sage's name in the libation formula of Sutra ch.2 (`SS_Sutra_ch02.txt` lines 99 to 101, a footnote): "Svaha to Brahma, Svaha to Prajapati ... Svaha to Dhanvantari, Svaha to Bharadvaja, and Svaha to Atreya". That is a person receiving an offering at a pupil's initiation, not this plant.

*Vana-Karpasi (Uttara ch.40).* `SS_Uttara_ch40.txt` lines 578 to 583, in the chapter on diarrhoea (Atisara): "Diet: The diet of the patient suffering from Atisara should be prepared with the admixture of Kapittha, S'almali, Phanji (a kind of Patha), Vana-Karpasi (D. R. Vata and Karpasi), Dadima, Yuthika, Kachchhura, S'elu, S'ana, Chuchchu, Dadhi (curd), S'ala-parni, Pris'niparni, Vrihati, Kantakarika, Bala, S'vadamstra, Vilva, Patha, Nagara and Dhanyaka and may also consist of the pastes of sesamum and Mudga pulse or of Mudga soup." (spellings normalised from the OCR, which prints the name across a line break as "Vana-" and "KarpAsi"). The translator's bracket "(D. R. Vata and Karpasi)" records a variant reading in which the one name is read as two. The chapter prints Vana-Karpasi, the appendix Vana-karpasa; they differ in the final vowel only. Whether the appendix and the chapter mean the same plant is not stated by the text, and the Codex does not claim it. [USER: verify]

*The name Karpasi, not tied to this entry.* Karpasi stands in the Vata-samshamana group of Sutra ch.39 (`SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` lines 85 to 94: "... Kanchanaka, Bhargi, Karpasi, Vrishchiaali, Pattura, Vadara, Yava, Kola, Kulattha, etc."), of which the text says the drugs have "the general virtue of soothing (restoring to its normal state) the deranged" Vayu (the OCR garbles the last words). Kdrpasa-phala is the fruit added to a purifying ghrita in Chikitsa ch.1 (lines 535 and 567) and Kdrpasi fruit juice is given with honey in Uttara ch.21 (line 268). The chapters give no Latin for any of these and do not say that Karpasi is Vana-Karpasi.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Bharadvaji, Vana-Karpasi and Karpasi found nothing.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Hibiscus vitifolius*, Bharadvaji and Vana-karpasa found no entry on this plant. **I_1400** (*Hibiscus mutabilis*, Sthala-padma), **I_739** (*Hibiscus rosa-sinensis*, tagged SS) and **I_454** (*Hibiscus sabdariffa*, tagged SS) cover other species; none prints Vana-Karpasi. **I_1389** (*Amaranthus spinosus*, Bhandira) and **I_1383** (*Mimosa pudica*, Brahmadandi) count the same B page; **I_1370** (*Calamus rotang*, Vanjula) counts the V page. **I_1371** (*Mimusops elengi*) quotes the Chikitsa 11 passage that names Yuthika, one of the neighbours of Vana-Karpasi in the diet above. ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Hibiscus* is a genus of the mallow family (Malvaceae). Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the name the chapters print, the plant (if it is this one) is one of some twenty ingredients named for the diet of a patient with diarrhoea; the text gives it no action singly. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The one preparation the chapters give for Vana-Karpasi is a diet "prepared with the admixture of" the listed articles, with pastes of sesamum and Mudga pulse or Mudga soup (Uttara ch.40, lines 578 to 583). No quantity is given for Vana-Karpasi. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** none is given for Vana-Karpasi or Bharadvaji. The chapters read print no *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* under either name. The one quality printed for the neighbouring spelling Karpasi is the Vata-soothing virtue of its group (Sutra 39), which the text does not tie to this plant.

### Cultivation Register

The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. **Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with a plant under the name Bharadvaji, and the one diet that names Vana-Karpasi is an illness diet. Nothing should be gathered or eaten on the strength of a Latin name that the chapters do not give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this plant under either name. The identity is open, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Diarrhoea that is bloody, prolonged or in a child needs medical care. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
