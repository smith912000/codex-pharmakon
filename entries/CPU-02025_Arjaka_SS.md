# Arjaka — Arjaka (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna translation, 1907)

## *Arjaka, a pungent herb of the Surasadi group and of the spices-and-herbs list. Identification: Ocimum album (a basil of the mint family) by a positional pairing in the botanical appendix of Bhishagratna's Vol. III ("Axjaka" in the OCR); confidence medium-low, because the pairing is positional and the appendix gives no statement about the Sutra 38 drug* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk. This is a single-text entry.** Spellings in quotations are normalised silently from the OCR where the damage is plain (for example Nandi for the printed Xandi); the line numbers cited give the text as printed. "Arjaka" was searched in all three volumes of Bhishagratna (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, `Vol2`, `Vol3`) and the chapter files.

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Sutrasthana ch. 38, the Surasadi group (`Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15461; chapter file `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 120): "The drugs known as Surasa, white Surasa, Fanijjhaka, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka, Sumukha, Kalamala, Kashamarda, Kshavaka, Kharpushpa, Vidanga, Katphala, Surasi, Nirgundi, Kulahala, Indurakarnika, Phanji, Prachivala, Kakamachi and Vishamushtika form the group known as the Surasadi Gana." The metrical text reads: "The group acts as a vermifuge and is an aseptic agent. It subdues the deranged Kapham and proves beneficial in catarrh, non-relish for food, asthma and cough."

In Sutra ch. 46, the chapter on foods and drinks, Arjaka is one of "the spices and herbs" (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 1420; Vol. I): "Pippali, Maricha, Shringavera, Ardraka, Hingu, Jiraka, Kustumvuru, Jamviraka, Sumukha, Surasa, Arjaka, Bhustrina, Sugandhaka ..." The same chapter, at line 1498, describes it with two others: "The herbs known as the Surasa, Arjaka and Bhustrina destroy the Kapham, are light of digestion, and tend to produce a state of parchedness in the organism. They are demulcent, generate the Pittam, and are heat-making in their potency and pungent in taste and digestion." (The word "demulcent" is the translator's and sits oddly beside "parchedness"; it is quoted as printed. [USER: verify]) The preceding sentence in the same passage says Surasa "generates the Pittam, relieves the colic and pain at the sides".

Sutra ch. 39 lists Arjaka among the errhines (drugs for the nose), and states which part: "the leaves of those commencing with Talisha and ending with the Arjaka therein" (`sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt` lines 52 and 64; Vol. I lines 15971 ff.). The part used in that list is therefore the leaf.

It is named in further preparations: in Chikitsa ch. 5, the "juice of Mulaka, Eranda, Sphurja, Arjaka, Arka, Saptala and Shamkhini" for washing (Parisheka) the body of an Apatanaka patient (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch05.txt` line 494); in Uttara ch. 11, "burnt ashes of Arjaka, Ashphotaka, Kapittha, Vilva, Nirgundi and Jati flowers" mixed with other ashes (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch11.txt` line 84); in Uttara ch. 39, in a passage on fever from aggravated Kapha or Vayu, "plasters of the leaves of Surasa, Arjaka and Shigru pasted together with water" (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch39.txt` line 1510); and in Uttara ch. 42, in a passage on Kaphaja colic (Sula), "the soup (of any Sula-subduing article) should be taken with an equal part of Arjaka" (`uttara/SS_Uttara_ch42.txt` line 619).

**Identification.** The group-list row gives none. In Bhishagratna's Vol. III Appendix 3, the name "Axjaka" (OCR; line 31269) falls in position against "Ocimum Album" (Latin column, line 31328), and the neighbouring pairs hold (Arjuna with Terminalia Arjuna, Aragvadha with Cassia fistula, Aparajita with Clitoria ternatia). ### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent for this drug. In general terms, plants of the basil genus (Ocimum, mint family) are aromatic herbs that carry volatile oils. Whether this applies to the drug as Sushruta used it was not checked. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Surasadi group: killing worms, cleansing ulcers, subduing Kapha, and benefiting catarrh, loss of appetite, asthma and cough (Sutra ch. 38). As a herb: Kapha-destroying, light, heat-making, pungent (Sutra ch. 46). As an errhine (Sutra ch. 39). As a leaf plaster in fever (Uttara ch. 39) and in colic (Uttara ch. 42).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose in measured units.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations given are a juice for washing (Chikitsa ch. 5), a leaf paste in water as a plaster (Uttara ch. 39), a nasal drug using the leaf (Sutra ch. 39), burnt ashes (Uttara ch. 11), and a portion mixed "with an equal part of Arjaka" (Uttara ch. 42). The "equal part" is a proportion and not a dose. Chapter 38 closes with a rule for all the groups: "Only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group, or a similar number of drugs chosen from the different groups, or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety, or in combination with another, should be used according to the indications of any particular case, as the physician, in his discretion, would determine" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, near the end; Vol. I, ch. XXXVIII). It also says that the groups "will be more elaborately dealt with later on in the chapter on Therapeutics". This is a rule of method for the groups, not a dose for this drug. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS.** Sutra ch. 46 gives, for Surasa, Arjaka and Bhustrina together: destroying Kapha, light of digestion, tending to produce parchedness, demulcent (as printed), generating Pitta, "heat-making in their potency and pungent in taste and digestion". These are the only per-drug qualities in the passages read. The Surasadi group in Sutra ch. 38 is said to subdue Kapha.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is attested in the Sushruta Samhita, in the therapeutic groups of Sutrasthana ch. 38 and the other passages cited above, and its role there is therapeutic: groups are said to subdue Kapha, Vata or Pitta, to act as vermifuge or aseptic agents, and so on. Chikitsa ch. 26 (the vajikarana chapter, "increasing the strength and virile power of weak persons") and chs. 27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) were searched under the spellings printed here (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch26.txt` to `ch30.txt`, and ch. 31) and the name was not found in them. The drug therefore carries no rasayana or vajikarana status in the sources retrieved, and nothing is said of ojas for it. The register is (b): framed by the therapeutic gana, with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A pungent, heat-making kitchen herb with errhine and plaster uses for Kapha complaints. Nothing in the record supports any cultivation use; it is a therapeutic and culinary herb in the text.

### Contraindications

- **Pitta.** The text says the herb generates Pitta, and is heat-making. Take that as a warning in its own terms.
- **Identity.** Positional OCR pairing only; do not substitute another basil.
- **Fever, colic, asthma, cough.** Medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
