# Madana — मदन (Madana), Madanaphala, Emetic Nut, Mainphal, Marukkarai

## *Randia dumetorum* Lam. (≡ *Catunaregam spinosa* (Thunb.) Tirveng.) | Family: Rubiaceae

**Tradition Tags:** Suśruta Saṃhitā (SS — chief vamana-dravya) · Caraka Saṃhitā (CS — head of the Vamana-gaṇa) · Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya · Siddha (Marukkarai)
**Energetic Classification (Ayurvedic):** Madhura–Tikta–Kaṣāya rasa · Uṣṇa vīrya · Kaṭu vipāka — *kapha-vāta-śāmaka*, urdhva-going (emetic)

**Training Phase:** Purification (Śodhana) — the sovereign emetic · Detoxification (Vamana-karma) · Pañcakarma preparation

### Origin & Tradition

**Suśruta & Caraka — the sovereign emetic.** No plant in the Ayurvedic pharmacopoeia is more firmly the *first* of its kind than Madana. Caraka opens his enumeration of emetic drugs by declaring Madanaphala **śreṣṭha** — the best, the chief — among all *vamana-dravyas*, and it stands at the head of the *Vamana-gaṇa*. Suśruta concurs, treating Madanaphala as the reference emetic against which others are measured, precisely because it produces a thorough, controllable *vamana* (therapeutic emesis) with comparatively low toxicity — it evacuates without lingering, and, in the classical phrase, "does no harm even when it does much." The fruit (madanaphala) is the officinal part: harvested, deseeded, its pulp processed by the *madana-kalpa* — soaking in decoctions, sun-drying, coating (e.g., in Kṣāra or honey-liquorice pastes) — to yield the pillules used to induce the vomiting that opens every major Pañcakarma course. Vamana with Madana is the classical treatment for aggravated Kapha: bronchial asthma, chronic cough, the "seven kapha diseases," skin disease, poisoning, and the mental heaviness of *tamas*.

**Method and mastery.** The Ayurvedic texts lavish unusual procedural detail on Madana — the pre-emetic oleation (*snehana*) and sudation (*svedana*), the milk-and-yaṣṭi draught to fill the stomach, the counting of *vega* (bouts) to grade a proper (*samyak*) versus deficient or excessive emesis. This is not a casual purge; it is a staged clinical procedure, and Madana is its instrument. That the *lakṣaṇas* (signs) of correct vamana are catalogued so carefully tells us the tradition treated this fruit as a scalpel, not a hammer.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer — Madana, the Intoxicator-Emptier:** The name Madana means "the intoxicating one," "he who maddens/delights" — an epithet of Kāma, god of desire, and of that which fills and overwhelms. It is a deliberate paradox that the herb of *madana* (filling/overwhelming) is the great *emptier*. The plant that shares its name with the god of desire is the plant that voids the body of accumulated Kapha — the doṣa of attachment, heaviness, clinging, and *tamas*. Symbolically Madana enacts the whole Ayurvedic logic of *śodhana*: you cannot fill a vessel already full; before nourishment (*bṛṃhaṇa*) must come emptying (*śodhana*). In the Kenetica frame Madana is the archetypal **reset agent** — the pharmakon of the clean slate, the ritual purge that precedes any true rebuilding. Its intoxicant name is the reminder that emptying is itself a kind of ecstasy: the lightness that follows a completed purification.

### Active Components

- **Triterpenoid saponins** — the *randialic* / *dumetoronin*-type oleanane saponins are the principal emetogenic agents; saponin content drives the gastric irritation → reflex emesis.
- **Iridoid glycosides** — randinoside and related iridoids.
- **Ursolic acid, β-sitosterol, mannitol, D-mannitol**, and fixed oils in the seed.
- **Tannins** and flavonoids in the fruit rind.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Madana is the **canonical emetic / reset pharmakon** of the Purification phase. It is deployed to evacuate *upward* (urdhva) an excess Kapha burden — physical (mucus, congestion, metabolic sludge) or, in the doctrinal reading, psycho-somatic heaviness. Use is bounded, procedural, and never casual: oleation and sudation first, full stomach, graded bouts, defined endpoint, rest and *saṃsarjana-krama* (graduated re-feeding) after. Indicated for kapha-dominant congestion, before major protocol resets, and as the reference instrument when a controlled, thorough emesis is genuinely wanted. Filed strictly as an operator-administered agent — this is Pañcakarma technology, not a household remedy.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Rasa (taste):** Madhura (sweet), Tikta (bitter), Kaṣāya (astringent)
- **Guṇa (qualities):** Laghu (light), Rūkṣa (dry), Tīkṣṇa (sharp) — some texts note Sara (mobile)
- **Vīrya (potency):** Uṣṇa (heating)
- **Vipāka (post-digestive):** Kaṭu (pungent)
- **Prabhāva (specific action):** *Vāmaka-prabhāva* — an idiosyncratic, whole-plant emetic potency not fully explained by taste/vīrya; the defining prabhāva that makes Madana the chief emetic despite a partly sweet rasa.
- **Doṣa action:** ↓↓ Kapha (evacuated upward), ↓ Vāta; broadly tridoṣa-balancing through śodhana. *Kapha-vāta-śāmaka.*
- **Karma (actions):** Vamana (emesis — chief), Śodhana (purification), Lekhana (scraping), Kaphaghna, Viṣaghna (anti-toxic), Krimighna, Vraṇaśodhana (wound-cleansing), Śvāsa-kāsahara.
- **Suśruta / Caraka gaṇa membership:** **Head of the Vamana-gaṇa** (emetic group) in Caraka; the reference *vamana-dravya* of the Suśruta corpus. This is Madana's signature classificatory position.

### Contraindications

Pregnancy and lactation — contraindicated. Not for Vāta-predominant depletion, the emaciated, the very young or very old, cardiac disease, hypertension, active ulcer, or any state where vomiting is hazardous (recent surgery, hernia, retinal fragility, severe debility). Emesis is a strong intervention: risk of aspiration, electrolyte disturbance, Mallory-Weiss-type injury with excessive/improper *vega*. Must never be self-administered; requires trained supervision, pre-oleation/sudation and graded re-feeding. High Pitta/inflammatory states: use cautiously (heating).
