# Cocos nucifera — Narikela (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Cocos nucifera, the corrected spelling of the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints as "Cocos  Nucefera.    Cocoa-nut." (line 32438) against the Sanskrit name Narikela (OCR "Nsrikela", line 32378). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters themselves print Narikela and, in Uttara ch.12, the translator's gloss "(cocoanut)", so for this plant the chapters and the appendix agree* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita. The Latin name comes from the translator's appendix, but the English "cocoanut" is the translator's own word in the chapters, so this is the best supported of the appendix pairings in this batch.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` (row 593) gives the name as "Nsrikela", the locus "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 32378; id line 32438)" and the Latin "Cocos  Nucefera.    Cocoa-nut.". The extraction merged the printed form "Cocos nucefera" into the spine binomial *Cocos nucifera*.

**The pairing, checked by counting.** On the page of N-names (page number "74" at line 32338) the Sanskrit run ends "... Narikela, Nichula, Nidigdhika, Nikumbha, Nila with Nili and Nilini (bracketed), Nilotpala, Nimba, Nimbuka, Nirbishi, Nirgundi, Nisi, Nishpava, Nivara, Nyagrodha". Narikela is fourteenth from the end. The Latin run ends "Cocos Nucefera. Cocoa-nut.", "Calamus rotang", "See Kantakari", "See Danti", "Indigofera Tinctoria", "Nymphea Stellata", "Melia Azadirachta Indica", "Citrus Medica", "Kyllingia Monocephala", "Vitex Negundo", "Curcuma Longa", "Vigna Catjang", "Wild variety of Oryzo sativa", "Ficus Bengalensis" (lines 32438 to 32456); the Cocos line is also fourteenth from the end. The neighbours fit: Nichula with *Calamus rotang*, Nidigdhika with "See Kantakari", Nikumbha with "See Danti", Nila with indigo, Nimba with *Melia*, Nimbuka with *Citrus medica*, Nirgundi with *Vitex negundo*, Nisi with turmeric and Nyagrodha with *Ficus bengalensis*. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* A search of all chapter files for Narikela and its OCR spellings (Narikela, Ndrikela, N^rikela) and for "cocoanut" finds the passages below. Of the plant the text says what it says of a food, a water, an oil and a burnt ash; it names no rasayana or vajikarana use.

*The fruit group (Sutra ch.46, food and drink).* `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1191 to 1206: "The fruits such as, the Tala, Narikela, Panasa and Maucha, etc. are sweet in taste and of digestion, and subdue the Vayu and Pittam. They are cooling in their potencies, and act as constructive tonics." Of the fruit itself: "The Narikela fruit is heavy of digestion, demulcent, anti-choleric, sweet, cooling in its potency, tonic, tissue-building, pleasant, laxative and diuretic. It subdues the Pittam." Lines 2015 to 2019 add: "The top-piths of such trees, as the Tala, Narikela, Kharjura etc., are sweet in taste and digestion. They prove curative in cases of haemoptysis, and are spermatopoietic. They subdue the Vayu and generate the Kapham in the body." (The words "spermatopoietic, tonic, rejuvenating and restorative" that stand a few lines above in the same chapter, lines 2009 to 2012, belong to the Vrahakanda bulb and not to Narikela.)

*The water (Sutra ch.45, liquids).* `SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 390 to 396: "The water found inside the shell of a cocoanut is heavy, demulcent, cool, pleasant and appetising etc. It is diuretic, (Vasti-shodhaka) spermatopoietic, and subdues Pittam and thirst." A translator's footnote reads "Light according to Jejjada" for "heavy". The next sentence recommends water "boiled and subsequently cooled down" in dysentery, burning of the skin, haemoptysis, diseases from abuse of wine, effects of poison, thirst, vomiting, catarrh, vertigo and loss of consciousness. The translation does not make clear whether that sentence continues the cocoanut-water sentence or begins a general statement about boiled water (the passage just before it, lines 368 to 388, is about boiled water in general), and the Codex does not assign it to the cocoanut.

*The oil (Sutra ch.45; Chikitsa ch.31).* `SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` lines 981 to 989: "The Oils obtained from the Kirata-tiktaka, Atimuktaka, Vibhitaka, Narikela, Kola, Akshoda, Jivanti, Piyala, Karvudara, Surjavalli, Trapusa, Ervaruka, Karkaru, and Kushmanda seeds, etc. are sweet in taste, potency and digestion, and tend to pacify the deranged Vayu and Pittam. Cooling in their potency, they increase the slimy secretions of the organs, impair digestion, and help the copious evacuation of stool and urine." In the chapter on the Snehas (`SS_Chikitsa_ch31.txt` lines 63 to 67) "the expressed oils of the fruits of Tala, Narikela, Panasa, Mocha, Piyala, Vilva, Madhuka, Shleshmataka and of Amrataka are used in diseases due to the deranged Vayu acting in concert with the deranged Pitta".

*The group heading (Sutra ch.42).* `SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` lines 288 to 300 list Narikela with Tala, Kharjura, Draksha and others, among the drugs "generally included within the Madhura group", the sweet taste.

*Wine, incompatibility and descriptions.* `SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` line 2842: "Cocoanut and date palm wines should be used after having eaten the flesh of the cave-dwelling (guha-shaya) species." `SS_Sutra_ch20.txt` lines 195 to 197, in the chapter on salutary and non-salutary regimen, forbids a combination: "The flesh of a heron prepared with hog's lard should not be taken with the pulp of the cocoanut fruit." In the chapter on the secretions of ulcers (`SS_Sutra_ch22.txt` lines 152 and 159) the colour "of the internal juice or water of a cocoanut" is one of the colours of the discharge, which is a description and says nothing of use.

*Medicine (Chikitsa ch.18; Uttara ch.12).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch18.txt` lines 329 to 332, in the chapter on glandular swellings, tumour (Arvuda) and goitre: "In a case of Vataja Arvuda, a poultice composed of Karkaruka, Ervaruka, cocoanut, Piyala and castor seeds, boiled with milk, water and clarified butter, and mixed with oil, should be applied lukewarm (to the tumour)." `SS_Uttara_ch12.txt` lines 217 to 225 (the chapter whose heading is Raktabhishyanda-Pratishedha, with the passage on the eye disease Sukra): "Young bamboo-sprouts, Arushkara, Tala (palm) and Narikela (cocoanut) should be burnt to ashes and alkaline water should be duly prepared therewith. The burnt ashes of elephant's bones should be soaked several (seven) times with the above alkaline preparation ..." and, applied as an Anjana, it "tends to remove the discolouring nature (whiteness) of a case of Sukra". A footnote (line 250), citing Shivadasa, says the spathe of the palm and the shell of the cocoanut fruit should be taken in preparing the compound.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Narikela and "cocoanut" found nothing.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Cocos nucifera*, Narikela and "cocoanut" found no entry on this plant. **I_1370** (*Calamus rotang*, Nichula) and **I_1385** (*Vanda roxburghii*, Nakuli) count the same appendix block (lines 32370 to 32456), and **I_1399** (*Ficus cingalensis*, Nandi) stands one place before Narikela in it. ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, the coconut palm is a palm of the family Arecaceae; the fruit holds a clear watery liquid and a white kernel that is rich in fat, from which an oil is expressed. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. In Sushruta the coconut is a sweet, cooling fruit, a cooling water, a sweet oil, a wine, an ingredient in a poultice, and a burnt ash in an eye preparation. Eating the fruit or drinking the water as food needs no protocol. The Codex gives no protocol for the poultice, and the alkaline-ash eye preparation is not for use: alkaline ashes in the eye are a hazard.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapters give these preparations: the water of the shell (Sutra 45); expressed oil of the fruit (Sutra 45; Chikitsa 31); wine (Sutra 46); a poultice of Karkaruka, Ervaruka, cocoanut, Piyala and castor seeds boiled in milk, water and ghee, mixed with oil and applied lukewarm (Chikitsa 18); and an ash-alkali with elephant-bone ash soaked seven times, applied as an Anjana (Uttara 12). No quantity is given in these passages. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] It is a general, well-established fact that coconut water is mostly water with some sugars and mineral salts, and that coconut kernel and oil are fatty foods. These facts do not test any Sushruta statement about the humours.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** the fruit is called sweet in taste, "cooling in its potency", heavy of digestion, subduing Pittam (Sutra 46, lines 1199 to 1206); the group of fruits is said to subdue Vayu and Pittam. The water is "heavy" (or light, per Jejjada), "cool" and subdues Pittam and thirst (Sutra 45). The oils are "sweet in taste, potency and digestion" and "cooling in their potency", pacifying Vayu and Pittam (Sutra 45). The top-piths subdue Vayu and "generate the Kapham". Narikela is in the Madhura group (Sutra 42).

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Narikela is named in the dravya chapters on fruits, water, oils and the sweet group (Sutra 42, 45 and 46), and the fruit group is said to "act as constructive tonics" and the fruit itself to be "tonic, tissue-building"; the water and the top-piths are called "spermatopoietic". These are properties of foods in the chapters on food and drink. The rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) and the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) do not name Narikela, so the text assigns it no rasayana or vajikarana status, and it does not speak of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** The coconut is an ordinary sweet, cooling food and drink in Sushruta, and a cultivator does with it what he does with a food: take it as the text describes, with attention to digestion (it is "heavy"), and not combined with heron flesh in hog's lard (Sutra 20). Nothing in the text makes it a rasayana, and none should be claimed.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text prints one prohibition: the pulp of the cocoanut fruit is not to be taken with the flesh of a heron prepared with hog's lard (Sutra 20). It calls the fruit "heavy of digestion" and the top-piths Kapham-generating. The alkaline-ash eye preparation is not for self-use. No conservation information was read.
