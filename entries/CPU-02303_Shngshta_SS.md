### Origin & Tradition

> **IDENTITY CAUTION.** The name is damaged in the text on disk and no plant is identified. Nothing in this entry is a recommendation, and nobody should take or gather a plant on the strength of this name.

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk, under this form.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder taken from the group list in chapter 38; this entry replaces it. A search of all the Sushruta text on disk (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/`, the chapter files and the three Bhishagratna volumes) for "Sh^ngshta" and for near spellings found the one place below. The names.csv row for it reads "group list (ch.38); no identification given here".

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *Sutrasthana* ch.38, the chapter on the general classification of drugs by their therapeutic properties (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 31; `Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` line 15372, one line after the row's stated 15371). The group list reads: "The drugs known as Aragvadha, Madana, Gopaghonta, Kutaja, Patha, Kantaki, Patala, Murva, Indrayava, Saptaparna, Ximva (as printed), Kuruntaka, Dasi-kuruntaka, the two kinds of Karanja, Patola, Kiratttikua (as printed), Guduchi, Chitraka, Sh^ngshta, and Sushavi form the group known as the Aragvadhadi." The name stands between Chitraka and Sushavi. The caret and the missing letters are OCR damage; what the printed Sanskrit was is not shown by the file.

The metrical text gives the virtue of the group as a whole (file lines 37 to 42): it "destroys the deranged Kaphani and the effects of poison and proves beneficial in cases of Meha (morbid discharges from the urethra), Kushtha, fever, vomiting and itching of the body and acts as a purifying (aseptic) agent in the case of an ulcer." This is the group's virtue and not a statement about this drug alone.

**On the Shringataka guess.** A similar-looking name, *Shringataka*, does occur in the same book, but in the food chapters, as a bulb: "The bulbs known as the Shringataka and the Kasheruka are heavy of digestion, are long retained in the stomach in an undigested state, and are cooling in their potency" (`SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1973 to 1976), and in lists of bulbs and of *asava* wines (ch.21 line 336, ch.42 line 292, ch.46 lines 1922 and 2854). Nothing in the lines read says the damaged ch.38 name is that word. The two are not equated here. [USER: verify against a Sanskrit edition of the Aragvadhadi list.]

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter closes with a general rule for all the groups: the physician should prepare "plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, Ghritas ... or potions", using "only two, three or four drugs of the same medicinal group ... or a group of medicinal drugs in its entirety" as the case requires, at his discretion (file lines 558 to 569). It is the rule for the groups in general and is not a preparation for this drug. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The name is attested in chapter 38's group list only. That chapter classifies drugs by therapeutic action; it is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, so the text assigns the drug no cultivation status, only the group's therapeutic one. The concepts of *ojas*, *rasayana* and *vajikarana* are not claimed for it.
