# Mayih (Dioscorides fuel plant) — māyiḥ (مايح)

## *A plant with one stem, round leaves and shield-like double-layered fruit growing in rough mountain places, quoted from Dioscorides; the Greek name behind the Arabic is not given and the plant is not identified; confidence low* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. Book II, entry *māyiḥ*, Arabic text from line 15470 of `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`. A search of the other texts on disk was not run beyond the spine check. 

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** What it is (*al-māhiya*), "Dioscorides said": "it is a plant used as fuel for fire; it has a single stem, round leaves, and at the bases of the leaves a fruit like a shield (*turs*) of two layers, which becomes broad; it grows in mountainous places and rough ground". Uses: "when its decoction is drunk it quiets hiccup (*fawāq*) if there is no fever, and so does holding it in the hand or looking at it"; "if it is ground and mixed with honey and smeared on freckles (*kalaf*) and vitiligo-type patches (*baraq*) it cleans them"; "it is thought that if it is pounded, put in food and eaten it benefits dog-bite". Finally: "it is said that if it is hung in a house it keeps health for the people and the cattle in it, and if it is tied to a ... and hung on the necks of cattle it wards off illness and calamity." The word after "tied to" is read here as *ḥawḍih* but is uncertain. [USER: verify]

**Recording is not endorsing.** The hanging and holding uses are amuletic. Avicenna passes them on with "it is said" (ويقال) and "it is thought" (ويظن). The Codex records them as reported.

**Identity doubt.** No Greek name is printed, so no chapter of the Dioscorides on disk can be named from this entry. [USER: verify]

The plant is unidentified, so no modern link can be drawn. A dog bite needs medical care, and no dose is given.
