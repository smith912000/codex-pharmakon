### Origin & Tradition

**CM (Classical Chinese Medicine — Shennong Bencao Jing through present):** Lóng Yǎn Ròu (龍眼肉, "dragon-eye flesh") is the dried aril — the translucent, sweet flesh surrounding the seed — of *Dimocarpus longan*, a subtropical evergreen of the Sapindaceae (soapberry family), the same family as the closely related lychee (*Litchi chinensis*). The name derives from the appearance of the seed seen through the translucent flesh: a dark pupil within a pale orb, resembling a dragon's eye. Longan is recorded in the *Shennong Bencao Jing* — the foundational Han-dynasty materia medica — as an upper-class (*shàng pǐn*, 上品) substance, the highest category, reserved for tonics suitable for sustained use without toxicity. The classical reading attributes to it the actions of "quieting the will, augmenting the Heart, and, taken long, lightening the body and prolonging the years," language reserved for the deepest nutritive-spiritual tonics of the corpus.

The defining role of Lóng Yǎn Ròu in classical CM is as a **Blood-and-Spirit tonic** (補血安神, *bǔ xuè ān shén*): it is one of the small set of herbs held to nourish both the Heart (心, *Xīn*) and the Spleen (脾, *Pí*) simultaneously. In the classical formula construction of later dynasties, this dual action is exemplified by **Guī Pí Tāng** (歸脾湯, "Restore the Spleen Decoction"), a Song-through-Ming era formula in which Lóng Yǎn Ròu is a core ingredient. The formula treats the pattern of Heart-and-Spleen dual deficiency (*xīn pí liǎng xū*, 心脾兩虛): palpitations, anxiety, insomnia, poor memory, fatigue, and reduced appetite, understood in the classical framework as the Spleen failing to generate Blood and the Heart consequently failing to "house the Shen" (the spirit/consciousness that resides in the Heart-Blood and, when the Blood is deficient, becomes restless — producing insomnia, anxiety, and disturbed dreaming).

The energetic logic is internally consistent: the Spleen governs the transformation of food into Blood; the Heart stores the Blood and houses the Shen; when Blood is abundant the Shen is settled and sleep is sound. Lóng Yǎn Ròu, being sweet, warm, and nourishing, is held to tonify the Spleen's Blood-generating function and thereby settle the Heart-Shen — a single substance addressing the root (Spleen Blood production) and the branch (Heart Shen agitation) of the same pattern.

**SBJ (Bencao Gangmu, Li Shizhen, 1596 CE):** Li Shizhen catalogues longan in the *Bencao Gangmu*, distinguishing it from its relative the lychee and noting its characteristic sweetness and Heart-nourishing reputation. He records its use for tonifying the Blood, settling the spirit, treating forgetfulness and palpitations, and as a restorative for the depleted and the convalescent. The classical comparison of longan and lychee runs through the literature: lychee is regarded as more warming and more apt to generate internal Heat with overconsumption, while longan flesh is the gentler, more purely tonic of the two, favoured for the deficiency patterns where a sustained Blood-Shen tonic is required.

**SS (Sanskrit / Ayurvedic-adjacent and South Asian usage):** Longan is cultivated and consumed across South and Southeast Asia as a sweet, nourishing, "cooling-then-strengthening" fruit, valued as a restorative food for convalescence and for those described as weak or "thin-blooded." Its classification in folk and regional materia medica aligns with a *Madhura* (sweet) *rasa* and a strengthening (*Balya*) and tissue-nourishing character. ### Active Components

- **Sugars (glucose, sucrose, fructose) and the sweet tonic matrix** — Dried longan aril is predominantly carbohydrate; the high readily-available sugar content is the immediate basis for its energizing, "Qi-and-Blood replenishing" food-medicine reputation and its use as a rapid restorative in convalescence. The sweet matrix is the carrier for the secondary metabolites and the reason longan is classed as a *yào shí tóng yuán* (medicine-food) substance.

- **Polysaccharides (longan pulp polysaccharide fractions)** — Water-soluble polysaccharides are a principal bioactive fraction of the aril. Reported activities in the literature include antioxidant and immunomodulatory effects (macrophage activation, modulation of immune cell function). These provide a plausible mechanistic substrate for the classical Jing-tonifying / strengthening classification. - **Phenolic compounds (gallic acid, ellagic acid, corilagin and related polyphenols — concentrated especially in the seed and pericarp)** — Longan seed and pericarp are notably rich in polyphenols with documented antioxidant activity; the aril contains a lower but still meaningful phenolic load. These contribute antioxidant capacity relevant to the RESET / recovery applications.

- **Minerals and vitamin C** — Longan contributes potassium, modest iron, and vitamin C (higher in fresh than dried fruit; vitamin C is substantially reduced on drying). The iron-plus-vitamin-C combination is consonant with the classical "Blood-nourishing" attribution (vitamin C enhances non-heme iron absorption), though the iron content of the aril itself is modest and the Blood-tonic action in CM is functional rather than strictly hematinic.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

1. `[SLEEP]` **Blood-deficiency insomnia and Shen-settling wind-down** — The signature Kenetica application mirrors the classical one: where poor sleep, palpitations, and anxious overthinking arise from a "Blood-deficient, Shen-unsettled" pattern (the tired-but-wired, light-sleeping, dream-disturbed presentation common after prolonged training stress or under-recovery), Lóng Yǎn Ròu provides a gentle, nourishing, sweet sedative-tonic. It is used as the calming-tonic base — not a forceful sedative but a substance that "houses the spirit" by replenishing the nutritive substrate. Preparation: 9–15 g dried aril simmered into a warm evening tonic, alone or with *Suān Zǎo Rén* (sour jujube seed) and *Dà Zǎo* (red jujube, I_086) for the classical Blood-Shen sleep stack.

2. `[BUILD]` **Convalescent and post-depletion Blood-and-Qi replenishment** — As a sweet, warm, calorically dense tonic food, longan suits the rebuilding phase after illness, heavy competition, or a depletion block: it provides rapidly available energy plus the polysaccharide and phenolic fraction, in a palatable form that supports appetite and intake when appetite is poor. Classical context: longan in **Guī Pí Tāng** as the Heart-Spleen dual tonic. Dose: 10–15 g dried aril daily, in congee, soup, or tea.

3. `[RESET]` **Antioxidant and gentle restorative support** — The phenolic and polysaccharide fractions provide antioxidant coverage during recovery phases; the gentle, non-aggressive character makes longan suitable as a daily restorative that will not overstimulate or generate the kind of internal Heat associated with stronger warming tonics. Pairs well with cooling antioxidant herbs to balance its warm nature.

4. `[BUILD]` **Memory, focus, and "settled-mind" cognitive support during high-load blocks** — The classical indications include forgetfulness and poor concentration arising from Heart-Spleen deficiency. In the Kenetica framing, longan is used to support the calm, settled cognitive state needed to sustain disciplined training and recovery routines, on the same Blood-Shen-nourishing logic — a tonic for the over-extended, frazzled, under-slept mind rather than a stimulant.

### Preparation & Dosing

**Dried aril (the standard medicinal and culinary form):** 9–15 g per day (classical decoction range), up to 30 g in food-tonic use. Taken directly as a sweet dried fruit, simmered into congee or tonic soup during the last 15–20 minutes of cooking, or steeped in hot water as a tea. Frequently combined with red dates (*Dà Zǎo*), goji (*gǒu qǐ zǐ*), and ginger for a daily Blood-and-Qi tonic beverage.

**Guī Pí Tāng (Restore the Spleen Decoction) context:** Lóng Yǎn Ròu is one core ingredient among several (the formula classically also contains ginseng/codonopsis, astragalus, atractylodes, poria, longan, sour jujube seed, dang gui, polygala, saffron-thread/aucklandia, licorice, ginger and dates). [USER: verify the exact ingredient list and per-herb gram amounts against a CM formulary before reproducing them — proportions vary by source.]

**Fresh fruit:** Eaten as a fruit; higher vitamin C, lower concentration of the tonic constituents per gram than the dried aril. Less used as medicine than the dried form.

**Honey-tonic / paste:** Dried longan cooked down with honey into a paste is a traditional convalescent restorative; convenient but high in sugar.

**Species note:** *Dimocarpus longan* (longan) is distinct from its Sapindaceae relative *Litchi chinensis* (lychee). The medicinal substance is specifically the dried aril (Lóng Yǎn Ròu); the seed and pericarp have separate, more astringent/polyphenol-rich profiles and are sometimes used distinctly.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Primary Tier:** ◈ Jing — Lóng Yǎn Ròu's role as a deep nutritive Blood-and-Qi tonic, suitable for sustained use and classed in the highest (*shàng pǐn*) category of the Shennong Bencao Jing, places it primarily in the Jing tier: it replenishes the body's nutritive substance (Blood, the material basis from which the Shen is housed and the tissues rebuilt).

### Contraindications

- **Damp-Heat and Phlegm-Heat patterns (TCM):** The sweet, warm, moist quality of longan readily aggravates internal Damp-Heat and Phlegm-Heat. Contraindicated where there is Heat with thick yellow phlegm, oral ulceration, a greasy yellow tongue coating, or constipation from Heat. "Tonifies the deficient, burdens the congested."

- **Blood sugar / insulin resistance:** Dried longan is high in readily-available sugars; high-frequency or high-dose consumption is unsuitable for individuals managing blood glucose, and inappropriate as a concentrated between-meal snack for those with diabetes or insulin resistance.

- **Dental caries:** High sugar content; rinse after consumption, take with meals, avoid in those with active dental disease.

- **Excess / fullness patterns:** As a tonifying substance, longan is for deficiency presentations. In conditions of fullness, stagnation, or active acute infection ("Heat") it is inappropriate and may worsen the picture.

- **Pregnancy:** Longan is traditionally regarded as warming and is approached with caution in pregnancy, particularly where there are Heat signs. [USER: verify the specific traditional pregnancy caution from a CM source before stating a definite prohibition.]
