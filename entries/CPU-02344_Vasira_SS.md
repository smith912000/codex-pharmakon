### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Vasira [SS]", taken from an extraction of Bhishagratna's translation; this entry replaces it. The locus given was "SS Sutra 38, Varuna'di group (Vol. I line 15388) | Sutra 38, Viratarva'di group (Vol. I line 15402)".

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Vasira is printed in the Sutrasthana, chapter 38 (locus: Varuna'di group, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 48; Viratarva'di group, line 61; the same text is in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, line 15388). Chapter 38 is "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3-6), and says that "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (lines 8-10). The name is given by the translator without a gloss, a botanical name or a description.

In the Varuna'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 44-51 (the list), lines 52-55 (the metrical text)), Vasira is one of the drugs listed. The group's metrical text reads: "The group is possessed of the efficacy of reducing the deranged Kapham and fat and proves efficacious in cases of cephalaegia, Gulma and internal abscesses." That is a property of the group as a whole, not of Vasira alone.

In the Viratarva'di group (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 57-64 (the list), lines 65-72 (the metrical text)), Vasira is one of the drugs listed. The group's metrical text reads: "The group subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and ... proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles." (the sentence runs across a page break after "Vata and") That is a property of the group as a whole, not of Vasira alone.

**Other places the name appears.** Sutrasthana ch.39 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch39.txt`, lines 85-92), in the Vata-Samshamana-Varga (the drugs that soothe deranged Vayu): "... Punarnava, Vasuka, Vasira. Kanchanaka, Bhargi ...". The same list adds that the Vidarigandhadi group and the first two groups of Panchamula share "the general virtue of soothing ... the deranged Vata". In Chikitsasthana ch.7 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch07.txt` lines 24-28), under the heading for Vataja Asmari, the ghee "cooked with a decoction of Pdshdnabheda, Vasuka, Vas'ira, As'mantaka, S'atdvari, S'vadamstrd, Vrihati, ..." (list continues) and the Kalka of the Ushakadi group. In Sutrasthana ch.46 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, line 1254) the text speaks of "fruits such as the Vasiram, and the Shitapakyam", which it says are hard to digest and long retained in the stomach; whether this Vasiram is the same drug is not stated. The chapter closes (lines 554-566) by saying that the groups "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", and that plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, Ghritas or potions are to be prepared "according to the exigencies of each individual case". That is a general rule of the chapter and is not a recipe for this drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The text uses it in two ganas (the Varuna'di, said to reduce Kapham and fat and help headache, Gulma and internal abscesses; the Viratarva'di, said to subdue Vata disorders and help stone, gravel and urinary troubles), in the Vata-soothing list of ch.39, and in a ghee for Vataja Asmari (ch.7).
- What the text does not say: what Vasira is, which part is used, or any dose.

The Codex does not translate the text's terms (Vayu, Pitta, Kapham, Gulma, Shosha, Asmari and the rest) into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend a plant it cannot identify.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Vasira stands in the dravya-group classification of Sutrasthana ch.38, a classification by therapeutic action. Both groups are therapeutic (Kapham and fat; Vata and urinary disorders), not rasayana or vajikarana groups. A search of the chapter files on disk for the spellings printed here found no mention of Vasira in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters, and no text read calls it an elixir or a drug of vitality. The register is therefore (b): framed by its therapeutic group, with no cultivation status attested. The Codex does not assign *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* effects to it, because no source read does.

**Practitioner's note.** Vasira is a name in a list. The tradition's text gives the drug a place among therapeutic groups and little more, and nothing in the record supports a cultivation use. A cultivator can learn from it only how Sushruta organises drugs into groups by action, and that some names in that classification are no longer certainly identified. Nobody should try to find a plant to match the name.
