## *Rock crystal (quartz, silicon dioxide) in the Divine Farmer's Classic; Yang 1998 prints "Quartz Crystal" for Bai Shi Ying, and the list `_sources/shennong.csv` prints 白石英* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Bai Shi Ying is printed in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) on PDF page 25, under "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (heading at line 985, the top of that page, and again at line 1060). The text (lines 1004-1010): "Bai Shi Ying (Quartz Crystal) is sweet and slightly warm. It mainly treats wasting thirst, impotence, yin [essence] insufficiency, cough and counterflow, and enduring cold in the chest around the diaphragm. It boosts the qi and eliminates wind damp impediment. Protracted taking may make the body light and lengthen life. It is produced in mountains and valleys." The bracketed "[essence]" is the translator's. In the OCR the line "yin [essence]" and the next, "insufficiency, cough and", are broken across lines 1005-1006; the sense is as quoted.

The entry stands directly after Zi Shi (Fluorite) and before the five-colour "Shi" clays. Yang prints no footnote on this entry, and no footnote doubting the identification. `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` has the row (line 14) with the identification "Quartz Crystal"; the list `_sources/shennong.csv` carries 白石英 (line 14). The spine note for this row is "high". The name "Bai Shi Ying" is printed once as the entry's own name; the only other printed "Bai Shi" in this section is a different entry (the white halloysite, line 1022) and Yang's "other name" of Yang Qi Shi (line 1146), so the shared syllables are a possible source of confusion. ### Active Components

Quartz is silicon dioxide, SiO2, one of the commonest minerals of the Earth's crust. Clear, colourless, well-formed crystals are called rock crystal. It is hard (7 on the Mohs scale), insoluble in water and acids other than hydrofluoric acid, and chemically inert. It is piezoelectric, which is the reason it is used in oscillators and watches. The sources read give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records the stone for wasting thirst, impotence, "yin [essence] insufficiency", cough, chest cold and wind damp impediment, and says that long taking may lighten the body and lengthen life. These are in part the aims of the Daoist elixir tradition, and the Codex does not endorse them. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation, and powdered quartz is not recommended for swallowing: it does not dissolve, and its fine dust is hazardous to breathe.

### Scientific Correlation

Respirable crystalline silica dust (quartz dust) is a well-established cause of silicosis and is classified as a human carcinogen when inhaled in occupational settings; those facts concern inhaling the dust and not swallowing a crystal. Quartz is used industrially in glass, ceramics, electronics and abrasives. No medical benefit of swallowing quartz for the conditions the Classic names is established. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- SBJ: "sweet and slightly warm" (Yang line 1004). Class: Superior (heading lines 985 and 1060). No other quality is printed. Yang's footnote 32 (lines 849-855) explains that his rendering omits the word "flavor" and that "balanced" means neither cold nor hot; this entry is not "balanced" but "slightly warm".

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: an upper-grade drug (上品).* Bai Shi Ying stands under "Jades and Stones: Superior Class" (Yang lines 985, 1060). Yang's preface says the superior class medicinals "mainly nourish life and correspond to heaven. They are nontoxic and taking them in large amounts and for a long time will not harm people. If one intends to make one's body light, boost the qi, prevent aging, and prolong life, one should base [one's efforts] on the superior class" (lines 352-358). The monograph itself prints: "It boosts the qi" and "Protracted taking may make the body light and lengthen life" (lines 1007-1009). It also treats "yin [essence] insufficiency" (line 1005); the "[essence]" is the translator's bracket and no *jing* claim is made here beyond that printed gloss. The grade is a statement of the Classic as translated by Yang in 1998 and is not carried forward from later materia medica works. The page that carries the entry prints the Superior heading at its top. Yang's foreword (lines 312-328) warns that elixirs of this kind made some who took them ill.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade drug in the Chinese scheme: the Classic held it safe for long use. Quartz is an inert silicate-group mineral whose dust harms the lungs, and the Classic's claims have no established support, so nothing here supports taking it. For a cultivator the entry is a record of how the Han-period elixir tradition ranked clear crystal among the "nourish life" drugs. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Quartz dust is a lung hazard, and powdered stone sold as a remedy may be a different mineral or may be contaminated. Mineral medicines are unstandardised and their sale is regulated in many places.
