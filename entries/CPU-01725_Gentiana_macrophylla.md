# Gentiana macrophylla — Qin Jiao 秦艽, Radix Gentianae Macrophyllae (Shennong Bencao Jing)

## *Gentiana macrophylla, as the pharmaceutical Latin "Radix Gentianae Macrophyllae" that Yang 1998 prints for the entry Qin Jiao* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong text (SBJ).** No other text read for this entry names this species. The entry is in Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`). The Latin name is the translator's pharmaceutical Latin and was matched to the binomial by stem in the extraction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv`, row 107: "pharmaceutical Latin 'Radix Gentianae Macrophyllae' matched by stem to on-disk binomial 'Gentiana macrophylla'"). A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Sushruta texts on disk (including the three Bhishagratna volumes) for the genus name *Gentiana* found Dioscorides 3-3 GENTIANE (line 6541, glosses *Gentiana lutea* and Yellow Gentian) and, in the Shennong text itself, Long Dan (Radix Gentianae Scabrae, line 1936), a different gentian in the Superior Class herbs; the Tibetan texts (Clark 1995 and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011) print no *Gentiana macrophylla*, and the Tibetan gentians are not attributed to this row. The Chinese characters are those of the Codex's own list of Shennong names (`_sources/shennong.csv`, line 144: "Gentiana macrophylla Root (秦艽 Qín Jiāo)"), not of Yang's English text. The row was added from the printed name (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) and has not been verified against POWO. [USER: verify]

**Chinese — Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ).** *Qin Jiao, PDF page 66.* The page marker `[[p66]]` is at line 2492 and the entry is at lines 2494 to 2497. The governing heading is "Herbs: Middle Class", printed at line 2460 on the page marked `[[p65]]` (line 2459) and again at line 2526 on `[[p67]]` (line 2525). The entry has no heading directly above it on its own page; it stands under the middle-class heading by position. The text reads:

"Qin Jiao (Radix Gentianae Macrophyllae)149 is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats cold and heat, evil qi, and cold, damp, wind impediment with pain in the limb joints. It precipitates water and disinhibits urination. It grows in mountains and valleys."

The superscript 149 is Yang's footnote number. The footnote (lines 2503 and following) is the translator's commentary and not the classic's text; it begins "Gentiana Macrophylla disinhibits dampness, dissipates wind, removes heat from the stomach and intestines, and boosts the qi of the liver and gallbladder", and it is not used here as the Shennong text's claim. The entry prints no "protracted taking" sentence, no other name, and no toxicity statement. The preface (Book One, lines 385 to 390) says the middle-class medicinals "mainly nurture personality and correspond to humanity", that they "may or may not be toxic", and that one bases the effort "to control disease, supplement vacuity, and replenish exhaustion" on the middle class.

**Overlap.** I_316 (*Gentiana straminea*) is titled "Qín Jiāo (秦艽) source, Tibetan Ji-ma, Radix Gentianae Macrophyllae" and carries the same Chinese drug name under another species; it overlaps this entry at the level of the drug name Qin Jiao and the pharmaceutical Latin, and it is not merged. I_315 (*Gentiana crassicaulis*) also carries Qin Jiao. I_113 (Gentian, *Gentiana*) is a general entry. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The entry itself prints no constituent. It is a general fact that *Gentiana* is a genus of the gentian family (Gentianaceae) and that bitter secoiridoid glycosides, of which gentiopicroside is an example, are characteristic of the family; the entry's own word is "bitter". Which species or which root a given supply contains is a matter the sources read do not settle.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Shennong entry records a bitter, balanced drug that "mainly treats cold and heat, evil qi, and cold, damp, wind impediment with pain in the limb joints" and "precipitates water and disinhibits urination". These are the text's claims in its own terms and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Cultivation Register

**Chinese — Shennong Bencao Jing (SBJ).** *(a) Attested: the middle class (中品), by position.* The class is assigned by the printed heading "Herbs: Middle Class" (line 2460, `[[p65]]`), repeated at line 2526 (`[[p67]]`). The entry on `[[p66]]` has no heading directly above it, so the grade rests on its position between the two. This is a printed class and not a promise of cultivation use. In the preface (lines 385 to 390) the middle class mainly "nurture[s] personality", may or may not be toxic, and is the class on which one bases the effort to control disease, supplement vacuity and replenish exhaustion. It is not the class the preface ties to making the body light and prolonging life, which is the superior class (lines 352 to 355).

The entry's own words fit that frame and go no further: it treats cold, heat, evil qi and wind-damp impediment, and it disinhibits urination. It says nothing of protracted taking, of making the body light or of prolonging life. It asserts no *jing*, *shen* or *neidan* action, and none is added here.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-class entry in the Chinese scheme: for controlling disease, weighed before use, and not the class of long-term life-nourishing drugs. The entry promises no cultivation outcome and gives no dose. Nothing here supports taking this root as a cultivation practice. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The entry prints no warning and no toxicity statement; the preface says the middle class "may or may not be toxic" and that one should "weigh and ponder before putting them to their appropriate use" (line 387 onward). No dose, preparation or safety information is given for this root, and the entry's identity is the translator's pharmaceutical Latin. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
