# Black Cardamom — Greater Cardamom, Bara Elaichi, Hill Cardamom, Nepal Cardamom

## *Amomum subulatum* Roxb. | Family: Zingiberaceae

**Tradition Tags:** DMM, CM, SBJ, SS, FMT

**Energetic Classification:** Warming, Dry — aromatic, carminative, qi/vata-moving

**Training Phase:** Foundation — a digestive-fire and breath tonic that settles the base before harder work

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Dioscorides (DMM):** Greek and Greco-Roman materia medica knew *amomum* as a warming, aromatic Eastern spice-drug — a fragrant seed capsule imported along the same routes as true cardamom, valued as a digestive, a warming stomachic, and an ingredient in compound theriacs. Classical authors did not distinguish the smoky Himalayan *Amomum subulatum* from related aromatic capsules with botanical rigour; the *amomum* of *De Materia Medica* is best read as the aromatic-Zingiberaceae/cardamom complex, of which black cardamom is a genuine member.

**Avicenna / Unani (CM):** In the *Canon* and the wider Unani tradition, cardamom (*heel*, in greater and lesser forms) is a warm-dry aromatic classed as *muqawwi-e-meda* (stomach-strengthening), carminative, cardiotonic (*muqawwi-e-qalb*), and *kasir-e-riyah* (wind-breaking). Greater cardamom specifically was used for cold stomach, nausea, and heaviness after eating.

**Shennong / Chinese (TCM):** The Zingiberaceae aromatic capsules (the *sha ren* / *cao guo* group of "aromatic-damp-transforming" drugs) are foundational to the Chinese warming-digestive tradition descending from the *Shennong Bencao Jing* lineage. *Amomum* species in this family are used to warm the middle, transform damp, move stagnant qi, and stop nausea and epigastric fullness — the classic pattern for a smoky, penetrating aromatic seed.

**Sushruta (SS):** Ayurveda knows greater cardamom as *bhadra* / *sthula-ela* (the "large cardamom," distinct from *sukshma-ela*, the small green cardamom). It appears in the dipana-pachana (kindling-digestive) group, for *kapha*-*vata* disorders, cough, asthma, poor appetite, and as a mouth-freshener and cardiac aromatic.

**rGyud-bzhi / Tibetan (FMT):** Tibetan medicine (the Four Tantras tradition) uses greater cardamom — *ka ko la* — as one of the "six good" aromatic fruit-drugs, specifically directed at the *spleen/stomach* and the digestive fire, warming a cold digestion and treating disorders of the "cold" type. Its inclusion among the canonical fragrant fruits places it squarely in the Himalayan pharmacopoeia where the plant actually grows.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** Black cardamom's signature across every tradition is *warming the centre and moving what is stuck* — kindling digestive fire, breaking trapped wind, lifting damp heaviness. Its smoky character (from traditional fire-drying over open flame) reinforces the doctrinal image of an inner warmth carried into a cold, sluggish middle. Modern pharmacology gives this a coherent basis: the essential-oil fraction (dominated by 1,8-cineole) is a real carminative and antimicrobial, and the phenolic-acid fraction of the fruit is a genuine antioxidant and anti-inflammatory agent. The "warming spice that settles the gut" doctrine sits on top of measurable smooth-muscle, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory activity.

### Active Components

The defining constituents are in the **essential oil**, dominated by **1,8-cineole (eucalyptol)**, with terpinene, sabinene, limonene, and other monoterpenes — the source of the pungent, camphoraceous, smoky aroma and much of the carminative/antimicrobial action. The fruit and especially the **husk (pericarp)** are rich in **phenolic acids** and flavonoids (including protocatechuic-acid-type phenolics), which carry the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity. Chalcones and diarylheptanoid-type compounds characteristic of the genus have also been reported. Modern metabolomic profiling confirms the seed as sugar- and amino-acid-rich and the husk as a phenolic-acid reservoir — meaning the traditionally discarded husk is itself a bioactive material.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Black cardamom is a Foundation-phase agent: it is used to *warm and steady the base* — digestion, appetite, and breath — before the system is loaded with harder Tempering or Purification work. The Kenetica framing is "kindle the fire, then move." It supports a cold, damp, sluggish digestive pattern; it is an aromatic that opens the chest and eases the breath; and its antioxidant/anti-inflammatory phenolic load makes it a gentle background protective agent rather than an acute intervention. It is deployed as a daily culinary-medicinal, not a heroic dose — the substance of steady inner warmth.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed:

- Drishya et al. (2021), *Journal of the American College of Nutrition* — a methanolic extract of *Amomum subulatum* fruit reduced acute and chronic inflammation in mouse paw-edema models, lowering pro-inflammatory cytokines, COX-2 and iNOS, and acting via the heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) pathway. DOI: [https://doi.org/10.1080/07315724.2020.1806139](https://doi.org/10.1080/07315724.2020.1806139)
- Drishya et al. (2023), *Food Research International* — *A. subulatum* fruit extract protected against radiation-induced esophagitis in mice by boosting antioxidant status and downregulating inflammation- and fibrosis-associated genes. DOI: [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2023.113582](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2023.113582)
- Farrag et al. (2025), *Food Chemistry* — NMR-based metabolomics of three cardamom species showed *A. subulatum* husk to be rich in phenolic acids and terpenes with antioxidant and antimicrobial activity, supporting valorization of the husk waste-stream. DOI: [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodchem.2025.143687](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodchem.2025.143687)

**Evidence grade:** *Preclinical / traditional.* The antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial activities are supported by consistent in-vitro and rodent data and by phytochemical characterisation of the oil and phenolics. Human clinical trials specific to *Amomum subulatum* are limited; the cardiovascular and digestive claims rest on traditional use plus mechanistic and animal evidence, not on robust RCTs.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **Ayurvedic:** *Rasa* — pungent (katu), slightly sweet; *Guna* — light (laghu), dry (ruksha); *Virya* — heating (ushna); *Vipaka* — pungent (katu); action *kapha-vata* reducing, dipana-pachana (kindling-digestive), hridya (cardiac-aromatic), shwasahara (anti-dyspnoeic).
- **TCM (by family analogue):** Acrid, warm; enters Spleen/Stomach; warms the middle, transforms damp, moves qi stagnation, stops nausea — the aromatic-damp-transforming category.
- **Unani/Galenic:** Warm and dry; carminative, stomachic, cardiotonic, wind-breaking.
- **Tibetan (rGyud-bzhi):** Warming fruit-drug for the digestive fire and cold-type stomach/spleen disorders; one of the canonical fragrant fruits.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The *amomum* of *De Materia Medica* is a warming aromatic import valued as a stomachic and theriac ingredient, and Dioscorides carries no subtle-body doctrine that could assign this capsule a cultivation status. Two honesty problems compound here: he offers no vital-constitution frame at all, and the identification of the smoky Himalayan *Amomum subulatum* with his *amomum* is a modern reading of an aromatic-Zingiberaceae complex, not a secure ancient equation. What the tradition genuinely gives a cultivator is his discipline of attention — the provenance of the fragrant capsule, the season and route of import, the sensory assay of a true aromatic against an adulterated one. That is a real practice of judgement, not a doctrine of vitality. **(c) absent** as to cultivation status.

**Unani — Avicenna (CM).** Greater cardamom (*heel*) is a warm-dry aromatic worked as *muqawwi-e-meda* (stomach-strengthening), *kasir-e-riyah* (wind-breaking), and *muqawwi-e-qalb* (heart-strengthening) for a cold, heavy stomach and nausea. The strongest cultivation idea in the Unani corpus — the conservation of innate heat and radical moisture, that finite vital endowment whose depletion is ageing — is a frame of the whole regimen, not a status the tradition assigns to this warming stomachic. Its cardiotonic standing sits near the gladdening cardiac-drug idea, but no *mufarrih* role in the *Kitāb al-Adwiya al-Qalbiyya* is attested for it in the sources retrieved, and none is asserted here. **(b) framed:** attested therapeutically, no cultivation status sourced.

**Chinese — Shennong stratum (SBJ).** In the Chinese warming-digestive lineage, black cardamom is read through the *sha ren* / *cao guo* group of aromatic damp-transforming drugs — acrid and warm, entering Spleen and Stomach, warming the middle (溫中), transforming damp (化濕), moving stagnant qi and stopping nausea. Two things keep this framed rather than attested: the attribution to *Amomum subulatum* specifically is by family analogue, not a named classical entry, and these aromatic damp-transformers are curative, middle-warming drugs — the treating-illness (治病) work — with no *san pin* 三品 grade recorded for this species and no standing within *yang sheng* 養生, the conservational nourishing-of-life project. It carries a therapeutic, not a cultivation, status. **(b) framed.**

**Ayurveda (SS).** Greater cardamom is *bhadra* / *sthula-ela*, placed in the *dipana-pachana* (kindling–digestive) group with *ushna virya*, worked as *hridya* (cardiac-aromatic) and *shwasahara* for *kapha-vata* patterns, cough and poor appetite. This is a therapeutic and dietetic standing; no *rasayana* (rejuvenation) or *vajikarana* (generative vitality) assignment is recorded for it in the sources retrieved, and no *ojas*-building action is claimed here. Its place is in *dravyaguna* as a digestive-aromatic, not among the formal Sushrutan rejuvenative divisions. **(b) framed.**

**Tibetan (FMT).** Black cardamom is *ka ko la*, counted among the "six good" aromatic fruit-drugs (*bzang po drug*) and directed at the spleen/stomach and the digestive fire to warm a cold, sluggish digestion. That membership is a therapeutic-aromatic classification within a pharmacopoeia native to the plant's own Himalayan range; it is not a cultivation status. Tibetan medicine holds no formal category of "substances for subtle-body practice," and medicine is in any case the third of the four treatment branches — it supports practice without reaching the root, which is a matter of the three poisons. No *bcud len* (essence-extraction) role is attested for *ka ko la* in the sources retrieved. **(b) framed.**

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator uses black cardamom exactly as every tradition places it: a Foundation-phase, food-dose aromatic to warm and steady a cold, damp, sluggish digestive base — kindle the fire, then move. It is a daily culinary-medicinal, not a heroic dose and not a longevity substance: no tradition on this tag line assigns it a rejuvenative or vitality-building status, only a warming-digestive and mildly cardiotonic one. The honest use is the settling of the centre before harder work, and the smoky husk — a genuine phenolic reservoir — is worth keeping rather than discarding.

### Contraindications

Black cardamom is a **culinary spice with a strong safety record at food doses**. Concentrated extracts and essential oil warrant more caution. The essential oil is dominated by **1,8-cineole**, which in concentrated form can cause mucosal irritation and, in significant oral quantities, CNS effects — do not ingest neat essential oil, and keep cineole-rich oils away from the faces of infants and young children (risk of laryngospasm). Possible allergic sensitivity in individuals reactive to Zingiberaceae spices. Its warming, qi-moving nature makes large medicinal doses theoretically inadvisable in clear "heat"/pitta-excess or acute inflammatory-hot patterns per traditional logic. Insufficient safety data for high-dose therapeutic use in **pregnancy and lactation** — keep to culinary amounts. Theoretical additive effect with antioxidant/anti-inflammatory or antidiabetic regimens; monitor if combining concentrated extracts with medication. Not a substitute for treatment of cardiac or respiratory disease.
