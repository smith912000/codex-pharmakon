# Gold — Suvarna / Hema (Bhishagratna) / Gold, gser.rdo gold ore (Clark 1995)
## *Gold, the metal (Au); the spine's "Huang Jin" has no printed source in the Chinese text on disk* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP, AT, DMM, CM, SBJ, SS, FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** No Egyptian locus was found. Bryan's Chapter V mineral list (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` lines 1137-1260) has no gold. The word "gold" occurs once in the Bryan file in a medical context, line 2844, in Bryan's remark that the scribe "made an attempt at a golden-eye ointment" by crushing collyrium in a vulture's egg; that is a colour joke about an eye salve and not a gold ingredient. `_sources/ebers.csv` and `extract/names.csv` have no gold row. The tag rests on the spine row alone. [USER: verify]

**Assyrian (AT).** No Assyrian locus was found. `_sources/assyrian.csv` has no gold row, and the Assyrian text on disk (Thompson 1923, `Sources/AT_Assyrian/text/Thompson_1923_Assyrian_Medical_Texts.txt`) is OCR of cuneiform plates that cannot be searched for a substance. The project's own overview file in `Sources/AT_Assyrian/` carries a gold line with no source and was not used. The blocking warning is in force. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** Dioscorides has no chapter on gold in Book 5 (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`). Gold appears in passing, and once with an effect. In the quicksilver chapter 5-110 (line 10405) a parenthesis says: "(Gold dust, that is, the smallest scraping, is a miraculous help for hydrargyrum poisoning)." In 5-91 (line 10275) verdigris may be made from "the plates between which leaves of gold are held when pounded out"; in 5-92 (line 10284) goldsmiths make a rust "with which they solder gold"; 5-100 (line 10332) says plumbago "is made from silver and gold"; and 5-104 CHRUSOKOLLA (line 10363), "gold solder" in Greek, is glossed by the translator "a name for Borax or Malachite", and "TOXIC".

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No Avicenna locus was found. `_sources/avicenna.csv` has no gold row and no Avicenna text is on disk. The project's own files in `Sources/CM_Avicenna/` print a gold passage as a quotation with no source; it was not used. No *Canon* chapter reference exists to give. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`) has no gold entry, and none is in `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/extract/names.csv` or `_sources/shennong.csv`. Gold is named as something other drugs act on: Shui Yin (mercury) "kills the toxins of gold, silver, copper, and tin" (line 880), Kong Qing "is able to transform copper, iron, lead, and tin into gold" (line 912), Ceng Qing "is able to transform into gold and copper" (line 918), Shi Dan "may change iron into copper, gold, or silver" (lines 1104-1105), and Shi Liu Huang can "transform rare matters like gold, silver, copper, and iron" (line 1141). These are mentions in other entries. The "Huang Jin" in the spine name has no printed source I could find. [USER: verify]

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Suvarna* is in the Metal group of *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 2121-2125): "Gold has a sweet and agreeable taste, acts as a tonic or restorative elixir, imparts rotundity to the body, and subdues the action of all the three deranged humours of the body. It is cooling and antitoxic in its potency and invigorates the eyesight." It is one of the seven *Trapvadi* drugs of ch.38 (`SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 439-448) and is classed with the earthy substances in ch.1 (`SS_Sutra_ch01.txt` lines 448-451). Gold recurs through the *rasayana* chapter of the *Chikitsasthana* (see the register). Elsewhere: gold dust is licked with honey, ghee and *Brahmi* juice at the natal rites (`sharira/SS_Sharira_ch10.txt` lines 220-226, "half a Rati weight of gold dust"), four linctuses with powdered gold are said to improve "the growth, memory, strength and intellect of a child" (lines 936-942), and the iron-preparation method of *Chikitsasthana* ch.10 is said to be "the mode of medically preparing all kinds of Loha (zinc, copper, lead and gold)" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch10.txt` lines 207-208). *Chikitsasthana* ch.13 (`SS_Chikitsa_ch13.txt` line 25) lists gold among the six metals present in *Silajatu*.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Explanatory Tantra ch.20, precious metals, is the first of the eight sections (Clark 1995, `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 804): gold "has thirty-two names and is of two types—red and yellow"; red gold from the hDzambu River, "eaten by the Me.zan which deposited it in its droppings in China and Hor"; another red gold, *dzhye.khyim*, "resembles rust red copper" and, with a solution of aconite applied, "takes on a rainbow hue and thus is a most excellent test for poison"; yellow gold is superior (unwrought) or common. The *Eight Branches* is quoted: "Just as water does not adhere to the petals of a lotus, so ingestion of gold will prevent poison from adhering to the limbs." The action: "Gold promotes longevity, strengthens the aged, acts as an antidote to gem poisoning [and dispels spirits and nagas]." Gold ore, *gser.rdo* (line 828), "drain[s] lymph [accumulations]". The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3330) says: "Gold promotes longevity, is rejuvenating and acts as an antidote against precious metal poisoning."

### Active Components

Gold is the element Au, a dense, soft, chemically inert metal that does not dissolve in water, acid or body fluids as the metal. Gold leaf and gold dust are the metal itself. Soluble gold salts (used in some modern anti-rheumatic drugs) are different substances and can be toxic.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources are clear that gold held a place in two traditions' rejuvenation practice, but both use it as one ingredient of a prepared compound under the discipline of a course (see the register). Elemental gold is inert, and the longevity claims have no sourced clinical support here. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation. Ritual uses (natal rites, mantra, homa) are recorded as ritual.

### Preparation & Dosing

SS gives gold powdered ("pulverised", "well-powdered gold") or as gold dust, in electuaries with honey and ghee, or in milk (*Chikitsasthana* ch.28, below). The only quantity in the lines read is "half a Rati weight of gold dust" for a newborn (*Sharira* ch.10 line 225); the lines give no adult quantity. Red-hot gold or silver is also used to heat the water for a newborn's bath (*Sharira* ch.10 lines 230-231). Tibetan texts name "calcinated" or "ash of" gold in the rejuvenation compound (Clark line 1270; MTK line 3925) without a method. DMM gives only the parenthesis above. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Gold metal is biologically inert and is not absorbed from the gut. Soluble gold compounds were used in rheumatoid arthritis and can cause kidney and blood toxicity. No clinical studies were retrieved for the traditional uses. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- SS: "sweet and agreeable" taste; "cooling and antitoxic in its potency"; subdues all three humours; invigorates the eyesight; "tonic or restorative elixir" (ch.46 lines 2121-2125).
- FMT: in the test for authenticity, "artificial gold has astringent taste and cool digestive power" (Clark line 804); silver is said to have "the same (astringent) taste and post-digestive taste as gold" (line 805). No taste is printed for gold itself.
- DMM, SBJ, EP, AT, CM: none printed for gold.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No Egyptian locus for gold was found. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*.

**Assyrian (AT).** *(c) Absent, no locus.* Nothing was read. The blocking warning is in force. [USER: verify]

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, thinly.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is supplied. There is no chapter on gold; the single therapeutic statement is the parenthetical in 5-110 on gold dust against quicksilver poisoning, which is an antidote use, not a cultivation status. What Book 5 offers a cultivator is the discipline of attention: whether metals and by-products are pure, how adulteration is detected, which place is best.

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(c) Absent, and the attestation rests on a spine row.* No Avicenna text or list row was found. Innate heat and radical moisture cannot be tied to gold without a source and are not tied to it here. [USER: verify]

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(c) Absent.* Gold has no entry and so no *san pin* grade. It is named only as the end of transmutation (Kong Qing, Ceng Qing, Shi Dan) or as a metal whose toxin mercury counters (lines 880, 912, 918, 1104-1105, 1141). The preface (lines 348-400) ties the superior class to elixir-seeking Daoists, but no gold elixir is assigned a grade in the pages read.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(a) Attested: named in the rasayana chapter.* *Chikitsasthana* ch.28 is headed by Bhishagratna as the chapter on "the elixirs and remedial agents which tend to improve the memory and invigorate the mental faculties as well as to increase the duration of human life (Medhayushkamiyam Rasayanam)" (`chikitsa/SS_Chikitsa_ch28.txt` lines 5-10). In it gold is in many recipes: powdered *Vilva* root with gold, honey and ghee, licked each morning and used a year (line 224); gold with Padma reed, *Priyangu* and fried paddy, with honey and cow's milk (line 241); milk cooked with *Nilotpala* petals, with gold and sesamum (line 251); "Cow's milk with gold, wax and *Makshika* (honey)", after a homa a hundred thousand times, "the best *Rasayana*" (lines 254-256); *Vacha*, gold and *Vilva* with ghee, said to improve "health, memory, intellectual powers and physical growth" and to increase "the duration of one's life" (lines 258-260); *Amalaka* and gold with honey imparting "vitality to a dying man" (line 274); *Shatavari* ghee with honey and powdered gold (line 277). Many of these are joined to mantra and homa, which Bhishagratna calls "a combination of medicine and Mantra" (lines 224-232). Ch.46 gives gold a "tonic or restorative elixir" line. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim for gold is printed in the lines read, and the *sattva*/*rajas*/*tamas* categories are *Samkhya*, not Sushruta.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(a) Attested: longevity and rejuvenation.* Ch.20 says gold "promotes longevity, strengthens the aged" (Clark line 804) and, in the Men-Tsee-Khang text, "is rejuvenating" (line 3330). The rejuvenation chapter (ch.23, "Normal health" in Clark) names calcinated gold with silver, copper and iron as additions to purified mineral exudate, "kept in an iron container and taken" (Clark line 1270; MTK line 3925, p.237: "This cures all disorders and promotes longevity"). The course is tied to preliminaries (oil therapy, a bath, internal cleansing), a quiet place and abstinence, and "Moen nae and Gyasen should completely be avoided" in the course (MTK line 3925). This is the Explanatory Tantra's rejuvenation branch, not *bcud len*; no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Gold is the one metal on which two traditions agree in their own terms: Sushruta puts it in a *rasayana* chapter and Tibetan medicine says it promotes longevity and rejuvenates. Both set it inside a supervised course, with mantra and homa in the Sanskrit text and with oil therapy, cleansing, a quiet place and abstinence in the Tibetan text. A cultivator can honestly take from the sources the discipline, not the metal: nothing sourced supports swallowing gold for cultivation. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

Gold leaf and gold dust are inert, but "bhasma", "calcinated" and "ash" preparations of gold in the traditional compounds are not standardised and can contain other toxic metals (the Tibetan compound combines it with copper and iron ash and the Sanskrit method is not given in the lines read). Soluble gold salts are toxic. Purchase and use of metal ash preparations are regulated in many places. Mantra, homa and natal-rite uses are ritual, not therapy.
