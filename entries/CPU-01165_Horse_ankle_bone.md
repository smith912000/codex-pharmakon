# Horse ankle bone — "Horse's ankle bone" (rGyud-bzhi, Explanatory Tantra ch.20; Tibetan name not printed in Clark's line)
## *The ankle bone of the horse, a bone of the "Nails" group of the animal medicines in the Tibetan materia medica* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** The locus is Clark's 1995 translation of the *Quintessence Tantra* material, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, "the powers of medicines" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`). The chapter's eighth section is headed "(8) Medicines of Animal Origin" and opens: "The thirteen medicines of animal origin consist of horn, bone, meat, blood, bile, fat, brain, skin, nails, hair, urine, droppings and whole body" (lines 1147-1148). Under the sub-heading "Nails" (line 1186) the text gives the crocodile claw, a donkey hoof, and then, at line 1189:

"Horse's hoof: removes tumors [and is beneficial against nagas]. Horse's ankle bone: cures anterior transposition of the calf muscle [and subdues nagas]."

So the ankle bone is placed in the animal group of the materia medica, in the "nails" division, with a stated action and a bracketed second action. Clark's chapter notes define *nagas* as "Serpent spirits" (Notes, Explanatory Tantra chapter 20, note 5, line 1541). Clark's phrase "anterior transposition of the calf muscle" is his English rendering; its sense is not explained in the lines read. [USER: verify]

The same sentence in the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 translation (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3556) reads: "Donkey hooves cure obstruction of urine, whereas horse hooves cure tumors and the fetlock cures cramps of the calf muscle." The "fetlock" there appears to be the item that Clark renders "ankle bone", and "cramps of the calf muscle" appears to be what Clark renders "anterior transposition of the calf muscle". That correspondence is my reading of the two sentences, not a statement of either book. [USER: verify] The Men-Tsee-Khang line does not carry the *naga* clause.

`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` and `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` were not found to carry a separate row for this item that gives a Tibetan name. The Tibetan name is therefore not given here. [USER: verify]

Clark's preface adds a general caution about the whole animal section: "Nowadays only very few animal products are used, although many are documented in the traditional texts" (line 57). The square brackets in the chapter 20 lines are Clark's. He says he "interpolated liberally from the Blue Aquamarine commentary" (line 80) and does not, in the lines read, say which bracketed words belong to the root text. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Bone is a composite of the mineral hydroxyapatite (a calcium phosphate) and a collagen protein matrix, with marrow in the cavities. The ankle bone of a horse is the same material. The texts do not describe how it was prepared, and no composition figure is given or needed. No analysis of the Tibetan material was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The text gives a therapeutic action (the calf-muscle condition) and a spirit-related action (*nagas*). The Codex's application layer adds nothing to either, and the entry is not a protocol.

It is not a recommended practice. A bone from a slaughtered or dead horse is raw animal material with hygiene risks; there is no dose or method in the sources; and the conditions it is said to treat are better met by modern assessment. Clark himself notes the decline of animal products in current Tibetan practice (line 57).

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation is given for the ankle bone in either translation: the line says only what the bone "cures" and "subdues". No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify] Whether the bone was burnt, ground or worn is not stated in the lines read.

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General facts: bone is a calcium phosphate and collagen composite; ingesting or applying powdered bone has no established effect on muscle cramps or on any condition of the calf. Calf cramps have well-known ordinary causes (exertion, dehydration, circulatory and nerve conditions), and persistent or recurrent cramps merit medical assessment. The *naga* clause belongs to the Tibetan spirit-aetiology of illness and is not something modern science tests.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT): the chapter 20 line gives actions only (removes, cures, subdues). It prints no taste, potency or post-digestive taste for the horse's ankle bone, and none is assigned here. The framework in which a Tibetan substance would carry them (six tastes, eight potencies, three post-digestive tastes) is described in Clark's preface for the materia medica as a whole (line 57), but its values for this bone are not in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The horse's ankle bone is attested in the Explanatory Tantra ch.20 section on animal medicines, inside a materia medica whose use is therapeutic: the text names a condition it "cures" and a class of beings (*nagas*) it "subdues". The frame it sits in is read from Clark's preface and root text: the three humors (wind, bile, phlegm) "are rooted in attachment, hatred and obscuration respectively" (line 51), and treatment relies on "the fourfold food, behavioral pattern, medicine and accessory therapy" (line 1299), so medicine is one of four branches of treatment. The lines read do not say more than that about where medicine sits in practice. Nothing in the lines read gives this bone a place in *bcud len* (essence extraction) or in any practice of the subtle body, and no formal category of "substances for subtle-body practice" exists in this tradition. The *naga* clause is a statement about spirit-caused illness, not about cultivation.

**Practitioner's note.** A cultivator does nothing with this. The text offers a therapeutic line in a section of the materia medica that Clark says is little used today, and the frame read here makes medicine one of four branches of treatment, not a practice. The honest use is to read the line as a record.

### Contraindications

- **Not a recommended practice.** This records a Tibetan materia medica entry. Nothing here is advice to use horse bone.
- **Hygiene and infection:** raw or poorly processed animal bone can carry bacteria, spores and parasites.
- **Unknown preparation and dose:** neither is given in the sources, so no safe use can be described.
- **Calf-muscle symptoms:** persistent cramping, swelling or pain in the calf needs medical assessment; it can have serious causes.
- **Spirit claims:** the *naga* clause is recorded as the text states it and is not a basis for treatment.
