# Hasti-Pippali (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's rendering of the Sanskrit name)

## *A pungent drug named in the Pippalyadi group (Sutrasthana ch. 38), in an antivenom alkali (Kalpasthana ch. 7) and in a drink for the mother after childbirth (Sharirasthana ch. 10); the first-pass guess Scindapsus officinalis (Roxb.) **Sushruta (SS).**

- *Pippalyadi group* (Sutra 38, line 15493): the group reads "Pippali, Pippali mulam, Chavya, Chitraka, Shringavera, Maricha, Hasti-Pippali, Harenuka, Ela, Ajamoda, Indrayava, Patha, Jiraka, Sarshapa, Maha-Nimva-Phala, Hingu, Bhargi, Madhurasa, Ativisha, Vacha, Vidanga and Katurohini". The metrical text reads: "The present group acts as a good appetiser and is an absorbant of intestinal mucous and unassimilated lymph chyle. The range of its therapeutical application includes catarrh, deranged Kapham and Vatam, non-relish for food, abdominal glands, colic and gastralgia." The Sutra 42 pungent group (line 324 onward of `sutra/SS_Sutra_ch42.txt`) takes the Pippalyadi group as a whole into the pungent group.
- *Kalpasthana ch. 7* (`kalpa/SS_Kalpa_ch07.txt` line 22), the chapter "which treats of the sounds of a (medicated) drum, etc., possessed of anti-venomous virtues": an alkaline solution is prepared from the ashes of many woods dissolved in cow's urine and boiled, and the powders of a long list of drugs, among them Pippali-mula, Maricha and Hasti-Pippali, are added. The chapter's metrical text says that drums, banners and the gateways of houses are smeared with this alkaline preparation.
- *Sharirasthana ch. 10* (`sharira/SS_Sharira_ch10.txt` line 269): if the lochia is abnormal after parturition, the mother is given "a luke-warm solution of treacle mixed with powders of Pippali, Pippali roots, Hasti-pippali, Chitraka and Shringavera", continued for two or three days or longer till the vitiated blood disappears.

The text prints no description of the plant, no part used and no habitat for Hasti-Pippali.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.** The text uses Hasti-Pippali as a member of a group said to aid appetite and to act on catarrh, Kapha and Vata, as one ingredient of an antivenom alkali, and as one powder in a treacle drink for the mother after childbirth. The Codex does not translate these terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and recommends no use. The postpartum use in particular is a medical matter and not a Codex practice.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Sharira ch. 10 passage names the preparation (powders of the five drugs in a lukewarm treacle solution, taken for two or three days or longer) without quantities. The Kalpa ch. 7 passage names the alkali preparation. Neither gives a dose for Hasti-Pippali. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]
