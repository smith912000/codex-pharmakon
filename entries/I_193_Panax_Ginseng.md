# I-193 · Panax Ginseng (Asian Ginseng)
**Panax ginseng** C.A.Mey. | Araliaceae

---

## Section 1 — Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Accepted name:** *Panax ginseng* C.A.Mey. (1843)  
**Pharmacopoeial names:**
- *P. ginseng* → Ren Shen (人参): Asian/Korean/Chinese ginseng; primary subject
- *P. quinquefolius* → Xi Yang Shen (西洋参): American ginseng; different ginsenoside profile; cooler in TCM nature
- *P. notoginseng* → San Qi / Tian Qi (三七): hemostatic ginseng; different indication; I-199
- *P. japonicus* → Zhu Jie Shen: Japanese ginseng; similar but less used

**Common names:** Asian ginseng, Korean ginseng, Chinese ginseng, Ren Shen

**Etymology — the most carefully named herb in the Codex:**
- **Panax** = Greek *pan* (all) + *akos* (remedy/cure) = "cure-all" or "panacea" — the 19th-century taxonomist Carl Anton Meyer encoded the plant's legendary status in the genus name
- **ginseng** = English phonetic rendering of Chinese *Renshen* (人参, Mandarin) via Cantonese/Hokkien pronunciation
- **Ren Shen (人参):** "Human root" — *rén* = human person, *shēn* = root/ginseng; names the root's human body silhouette (arms, legs, head visible in shaped roots)
- The human silhouette in mature ginseng root drove the doctrine of signatures attribution of ginseng to the whole human body — pan-systemic tonic

**The human root legend:**

Large, old ginseng roots (50–100+ year wild roots) develop multiple appendages that resemble a human figure — two "arms," two "legs," a "head." In Chinese, Korean, and to some extent Japanese medical culture, this morphological resemblance was interpreted as a botanical signature: a plant shaped like a human body must tonify the whole human body. The doctrine of signatures argument, while post-hoc, produced a pan-systemic tonic (Qi, Blood, Yin, Yang, Spirit) that modern pharmacology validates as genuinely multi-systemic in its adaptogenic effects.

**Grading system (wild vs. cultivated; age; origin):**

Ginseng has the most elaborate quality/value grading system of any medicinal herb:
- **Wild ginseng (野山参 Yě Shāncān):** Most prized; 30–100+ years old; irregular human-like shape; astronomical prices (>$10,000–$100,000 USD per root); nearly extinct in the wild
- **Woods-grown (林下参):** Planted in forest conditions, harvested 15–30 years; intermediate quality
- **Garden-cultivated (园参):** Most commercial supply; 4–6 year cultivation; standardized
- **Korean Red Ginseng (高麗紅参, Goryeo Hongsam):** Steam-processed cultivated roots; specific ginsenoside conversion → unique pharmacological profile; separate product category
- **Jilin province ginseng (吉林人参):** Chinese primary origin; Changbai Mountain region
- **Korean Ginseng (高麗参, Goryeo Insam):** Same species; Korean cultivation; cultural prestige

**Part used:** Dried root (*Radix et Rhizoma Ginseng*); main root + lateral rootlets; head (crown) highest ginsenoside

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**The archetypal superior herb:**

If the Codex had a single entry that most perfectly embodies the "superior class" traditional concept — non-toxic, for long-term use, tonifies the whole organism — it is *Panax ginseng*. The *Shennong Bencao Jing* describes it as: "tonifies the five organs, quiets the spirit, stabilizes the soul, stops palpitation, removes evil qi, brightens the eyes, opens the heart, and sharpens the intellect. Long-term use makes the body light and prolongs life." This is the maximal tonic claim: every organ system, psycho-spiritual function, and longevity — an entire therapeutic world in a single root.

**Ginsenoside pharmacology — where traditional breadth meets modern mechanistic complexity:**

Ginsenosides (dammarane-type triterpene saponins) are the primary active compounds of *Panax ginseng*, but they operate through at least 12 distinct molecular targets across different organ systems. This molecular polyvalence is what gives ginseng its multi-systemic pharmacological profile — not a single mechanism but a complex web of converging effects. The traditional "tonifies everything" description maps onto a pharmacological reality: ginseng influences HPA axis, HPAG axis, cardiovascular, immune, CNS, and metabolic systems simultaneously through different ginsenoside fractions.

**Bidirectional adaptogenic action:**

The Brekhman/Soviet adaptogen concept (1960s) was largely derived from studying ginseng: an adaptogen normalizes deranged physiology regardless of direction — raises low blood pressure, lowers high; stimulates depleted immune systems, suppresses overactive ones; reduces fatigue while reducing over-excitement. This bidirectional profile is the defining feature of a true adaptogen and is well-documented for ginseng.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Ginsenosides — the primary compound class:**

Ginsenosides are numbered by *Rf* value on TLC (thin-layer chromatography) — Rb1, Rb2, Rc, Rd (protopanaxadiol/PPD series) and Rg1, Re, Rf, Rg2 (protopanaxatriol/PPT series):

**Protopanaxadiol (PPD) series** — larger molecules, CNS sedative, anti-tumor:
- **Rb1** (largest single ginsenoside, ~20% total): anti-anxiety; neuroprotective (BDNF); anti-diabetic; phytoestrogenic (ER modulation)
- **Rb2, Rc:** Anti-tumor; anti-fatigue; immune modulation
- **Rd:** Neuroprotective; ischemic stroke models

**Protopanaxatriol (PPT) series** — smaller molecules, CNS stimulating, hormonal:
- **Rg1** (~15%): Primary CNS stimulant; enhances learning (NMDA potentiation); anti-fatigue; EPO-like hematopoietic effect
- **Re:** Anti-platelet; anti-arrhythmic; phytoestrogenic
- **Rg2:** Neuroprotective; anti-cholinesterase

**Minor ginsenosides (formed by processing/gut microbiome):**
- **Compound K (20-O-β-glucopyranosyl-20(S)-protopanaxadiol):** NOT present in raw root; formed from Rb1/Rc by intestinal bacteria; HIGHLY bioavailable; likely accounts for much of ginseng's systemic activity; primary anti-tumor and anti-inflammatory form
- **Rg3:** Formed in red ginseng processing (steam); anti-tumor; anti-angiogenic; immune stimulant
- **Rh2:** Red ginseng-specific; anti-tumor; reverses multidrug resistance

**Total ginsenoside content:**
- Fresh/white ginseng: ~2–4% total ginsenosides
- Korean Red Ginseng (steam-processed): ~4–8%; different ginsenoside profile (higher Rg3, Rh2, compound K precursors)
- Wild ginseng: highly variable; not necessarily higher ginsenoside than good cultivated

**Other active compounds:**
- **Polysaccharides (Panaxans):** Immunomodulatory; hypoglycemic; 10–20% dry weight
- **Polyacetylenes (panaxynol, falcarinol):** Anti-tumor; antimicrobial; cytotoxic in excess
- **Peptides and amino acids:** Arginine (NO precursor); glutamic acid; γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA)
- **Volatile oils:** β-elemene, β-farnesene — minor; contribute to smell

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**1. HPA Axis Normalization (Central Adaptogenic — Rg1, Rb1)**
- Rg1: reduces CRH release from hypothalamus under acute stress → reduced cortisol spike
- Rb1: reduces corticosterone excess; adrenal protective
- Net: attenuates stress response without blocking normal HPA function; bidirectional modulation

**2. CNS — Dual Stimulant/Sedating (Rg1 = stimulant; Rb1 = sedative)**
- Rg1: NMDA potentiation → enhanced learning; ACh release enhancement; dopamine turnover
- Rb1: GABA-A modulation → anxiolytic; reduced over-excitation
- Both together: attention without anxiety; arousal without agitation — "calm focus"
- Net dopaminergic: Rg1 increases dopamine synthesis; Rb1 reduces breakdown = net enhancement

**3. Immunomodulation (Panaxans, Ginsenosides)**
- Polysaccharides (Panaxans): TLR4 agonism → macrophage activation; NK cell enhancement
- Rb2, Rc: enhance Th1 response (cellular immunity)
- Bidirectional: stimulates depleted immune systems; suppresses hyperactive ones (Rg1 in autoimmune models)

**4. Cardiovascular (Multiple Ginsenosides)**
- Re, Rg1: NO production → coronary vasodilation; anti-arrhythmic (potassium channel modulation)
- Rb1: negative chronotropy (slows heart rate); anti-ischemic
- Rb2: anti-platelet; reduces fibrin clot formation
- Net: antihypertensive (mild) + anti-ischemic + anti-arrhythmic + anti-thrombotic

**5. Anti-diabetic (Rb1, Panaxans)**
- Rb1: PPAR-γ agonism → insulin sensitization; GLUT4 upregulation → glucose uptake
- Panaxans: hepatic glycogen synthesis; glucosidase inhibition
- Rg1: β-cell protection from oxidative stress
- Multiple studies show HbA1c reduction in T2DM

**6. Anti-tumor (Rh2, Rg3, Compound K — processed forms)**
- Rg3: VEGF inhibition → anti-angiogenic; NF-κB inhibition; apoptosis induction
- Rh2: reverse MDR (multidrug resistance) in cancer cells → sensitizes to chemotherapy
- Compound K: topoisomerase I inhibition; apoptosis in cancer cell lines
- Anti-tumor applies primarily to RED GINSENG (processed) and metabolic forms; raw white ginseng lower Rg3/Rh2

**7. Steroidogenic (Sex Hormone)**
- Rb1: phytoestrogenic (ER modulation) → relevant for menopausal symptoms
- Ginsenosides generally: support steroidogenesis (not exogenous hormone supplementation but steroid synthesis support)
- Relevant for: libido, sexual function, menopausal hot flashes, male testosterone support

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Cognitive Function (Best Evidence Domain):**
- Reay JL et al (2005) *Psychopharmacology* 179(2):305–15: Single-dose Rg1-enriched extract; N=30; significant working memory and alertness improvement vs. placebo (P<0.05); consistent across multiple similar trials
- Kennedy DO & Scholey AB (2003) *Psychopharmacology* 169(1):7–15: Ginseng vs. placebo; improved secondary memory and mental arithmetic; N=30
- Heo JH et al (2008) *J Alzheimers Dis* 13(2):195–202: Korean Red Ginseng in Alzheimer's disease (open trial); MMSE and ADAS improvement

**Diabetes:**
- Vuksan V et al (2000) *Arch Intern Med* 160(7):1009–13: American ginseng (*P. quinquefolius*) 3g vs. placebo; significant postprandial blood glucose reduction; P<0.001
- Shishtar E et al (2014) *PLOS ONE* 9(9):e107391: Meta-analysis ginseng in diabetes; 16 RCTs; significant FBG reduction (−1.74 mmol/L) and HbA1c reduction (−0.34%)

**Immune Function / Upper Respiratory Infections:**
- McElhaney JE et al (2004) *J Am Geriatr Soc* 52(1):13–19: CVT-E002 (COLD-fX, North American ginseng extract); N=198 elderly; significantly reduced influenza-like illness incidence; P<0.05; most replicated ginseng immune trial
- Predy GN et al (2005) *CMAJ* 173(9):1043–8: Same CVT-E002; N=323; reduced cold incidence, severity, duration

**Sexual Function (Erectile Dysfunction):**
- Hong B et al (2002) *J Urol* 168(5):2070–3: Korean Red Ginseng vs. placebo; N=45; significant IIEF improvement; P<0.001; ginseng first-line mention in ED natural product literature

**Anti-fatigue:**
- Barton DL et al (2010) *J Natl Cancer Inst* 102(13):941–5: Wisconsin Ginseng (*P. quinquefolius*) vs. placebo in cancer-related fatigue; N=364; significant fatigue reduction at 8 weeks; P=0.003

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The most expensive plant in history:**

At auction, certain wild ginseng roots have sold for prices exceeding $10,000 USD per gram — more than gold, platinum, or almost any other material. A particularly large, old, human-shaped wild Korean ginseng root sold for $1.3 million USD in 2011. This is the pharmacological-cultural apex of the medicinal plant world: the belief encoded in the root's human shape, the cumulative 2,000+ years of superior-class attribution, and the scarcity of wild specimens has created an auction market that has no parallel in botanical pharmacognosy.

**The American ginseng catastrophe:**

When European traders and missionaries reported the existence of Asian ginseng in the late 1600s, they described its extraordinary value. By 1720, Jesuit Father Lafitau recognized *Panax quinquefolius* in Canadian forests and immediately organized Indigenous harvesting networks. Within 40 years, American ginseng was commercially harvested in such quantities that Daniel Boone was financing frontier expeditions through ginseng export to China. By 1800, wild American ginseng stocks were decimated in most of its original range. The North American ginseng harvest history is the pharmacobotanical equivalent of the whaling industry — resource extraction driven by Asian premium demand collapsing a wild population within one human generation.

**Korean Red Ginseng — the processing transformation:**

The development of Korean Red Ginseng (Goryeo Hongsam) as a distinct product category involved steaming fresh white ginseng roots at 98°C for 2–3 hours, then drying. The heat converts Rb1 → Rg3 and other deglycosylated minor ginsenosides; creates color change (Maillard reaction); and produces a pharmacological profile distinct from white ginseng (more anti-tumor, more immune-stimulating, higher bioavailability). The Korean Red Ginseng processing protocol — officially established in the Joseon dynasty (1392–1897) — represents 600+ years of empirical optimization of a natural product for specific therapeutic enhancement. Modern pharmacology validated the chemical transformations achieved by this traditional processing.

**Soviet Cold War Ginseng Research:**

The Soviet adaptogen research program (Brekhman, 1960s) investigated ginseng as a performance enhancer for Olympic athletes, cosmonauts, and military personnel. This produced some of the most controlled, systematic human data on ginseng effects available prior to modern RCTs. Soviet pharmacologists established: anti-fatigue effects in controlled work tests, improved reaction time under stress, enhanced immune function during winter months — establishing the foundational adaptogen concept that persists today.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

**Primary indications (evidence-stratified):**
- **Strong evidence:** Cognitive function/alertness, T2DM blood glucose, upper respiratory infection prevention (elderly), cancer-related fatigue, erectile dysfunction
- **Moderate evidence:** Hypertension (mild), menopausal symptoms, physical performance
- **Traditional use with plausibility:** Qi deficiency, post-illness recovery, immune reconstitution, longevity tonic

**Dosing:**
- **Standard tonic dose:** 1–2g dried root/day (white ginseng); or 3–6g in decoction
- **Clinical trial range:** 200–400mg standardized extract 2× daily (specify ginsenoside content)
- **Korean Red Ginseng:** 1–3g/day (higher ginsenoside potency than white)
- **Wild ginseng:** Used in smaller doses (0.5–1g) due to cost; no pharmacological evidence for superior effect at equivalent ginsenoside dose
- **Duration:** Tonic use: 3-month cycles with 1-month break (traditional; some evidence for reduced receptor sensitization with cycling); 8–12 weeks for specific endpoints

⚠️ **DRUG INTERACTIONS:**
- **Warfarin:** Several case reports of elevated INR; anti-platelet ginsenosides; monitor INR; avoid if anticoagulated
- **Phenelzine (MAOI):** Case report of manic episode with concurrent use; avoid with MAOIs
- **Furosemide:** Possible reduced diuretic effect (not well-established); monitor
- **Antidiabetic drugs:** Additive glucose-lowering; monitor blood glucose; insulin dose adjustment may be needed
- **Caffeine:** Additive stimulant; may cause overstimulation in sensitive individuals
- **Digoxin:** Interferes with digoxin immunoassay tests (false elevation); does NOT affect actual digoxin levels; inform the laboratory

⚠️ **CAUTIONS:**
- **"Ginseng Abuse Syndrome":** >3g/day long-term → hypertension, nervousness, insomnia, morning diarrhea, edema; dose-dependent; use within recommended ranges
- **Hormone-sensitive malignancies (ER+ breast cancer):** Phytoestrogenic Rb1 → theoretical concern; avoid or consult oncologist
- **Acute febrile illness:** Warming/stimulant nature may aggravate fever; defer until resolved
- **Pregnancy:** Ginsenoside Rb1 teratogenic in animal models at HIGH doses; standard doses considered traditional but insufficient human safety data; caution
- **Autoimmune disease:** Immune stimulation may flare; assess individually
- **Pre-operative (2 weeks before surgery):** Anti-platelet activity; discontinue

**Quality and authenticity (major issue):**
- Adulteration is widespread: many "ginseng" supplements contain American ginseng *P. quinquefolius*, Siberian ginseng (Eleutherococcus), or inert filler; independent lab testing essential
- Authentication: HPLC ginsenoside profile is definitive; Rb1+Rg1 ratio identifies species
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020: minimum 0.3% ginsenoside Rg1 + Re + Rb1 (sum) on dry basis
- Korean Red Ginseng: Korea's official certification process (KGC) = most reliable quality standard globally

**Standardization:**
- Specify: total ginsenosides ≥4% with specified Rg1:Rb1 ratio
- Korean Red Ginseng: ≥3% Rg1+Re+Rb1; Rg3 present (confirms processing)
- Extract ratio: 5:1 minimum; preferably specify extraction solvent

**Energetic tier:** ⊕ Yuan (constitutional) — acts at the deepest constitutional level; all four energy tiers in TCM simultaneously; the quintessential constitutional tonic operating at the root of adaptive capacity
