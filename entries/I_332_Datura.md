# I-332: Datura stramonium (Jimsonweed / Thorn Apple)

## 1. Nomenclature & Botanical Identity

**Primary name:** Jimsonweed (North America); Thorn apple (Europe)  
**Botanical name:** *Datura stramonium* L.  
**Family:** Solanaceae  
**Synonyms:** Devil's snare, devil's weed, mad apple, stinkweed, locoweed, moon flower (misapplied), Jamestown weed (corrupted → Jimsonweed)  
**Traditional names:** Dhatura (Sanskrit धतूर) — principal name across South Asian traditions; Bao man zi / Yang jin hua (Chinese 洋金花); Jusquiame des fous (French); Stechapfel (German); Dāturā (Arabic داتورة); Toloache (Nahuatl — refers primarily to *D. inoxia* but used interchangeably); Chaamico (Mexica)

**Botanical description:** Robust annual herb 30–150 cm; hairless to sparsely pubescent (distinguishing from *D. inoxia* which is densely hairy); leaves large, ovate, irregularly sinuate-dentate, dark green above, paler below, fetid when crushed; flowers solitary, erect, white (rarely pale violet), funnel-shaped, 6–10 cm, fragrant at night (moth-pollinated); fruit distinctive spiny capsule 3–6 cm, splitting into 4 valves at maturity; seeds dark brown-black, reniform, ~3.5 mm.

**Taxonomy note:** *Datura* genus (~9 species); *D. stramonium* (Old World origin, widely naturalized), *D. inoxia* (Mexican/SW USA — the primary Nahuatl toloache), *D. metel* (Asia — the Chinese yang jin hua), *D. wrightii* (SW USA sacred to multiple indigenous nations), *Brugmansia* species (angel's trumpets — closely related tree form; South American; all alkaloids present).

**Part used:** Seeds (highest alkaloid), leaves, flowers (dried for smoking historically); root historically

## 3. Convergence Analysis

**Pattern:** *Datura* occupies the most dangerous position in the Solanaceae tropane alkaloid triad (belladonna → henbane → datura) — alkaloid content is highest and pharmacological predictability lowest. The Ayurvedic tradition provides the most detailed traditional pharmacology: dhatura features in the *Vishavaidyajyotsnika* (toxicology sections) and multiple Ayurvedic nighantus as a poisonous plant used for specific surgical, ophthalmic, and fumigation purposes, always with extreme caution and complex preparation.

The South American tradition, particularly involving *Brugmansia* (closely related; scopolamine dominant), represents the most elaborate cultural elaboration of datura pharmacology: Amazonian and Andean shamanic traditions use *Brugmansia* (called toé, borrachero, angel's trumpets) for initiation, divination, and oracle — often administered by specialists with decades of experience in managing the narrow window between therapeutic vision and lethal dose.

The Chinese pharmacopoeia (*D. metel* — yang jin hua) lists the dried flowers as an official drug for bronchial asthma (via anticholinergic bronchodilation), whooping cough, and as a preoperative anesthetic adjunct — one of the few traditional systems that systematically exploited datura's bronchodilator mechanism.

**Alkaloid profile distinction:** *D. stramonium* alkaloids are predominantly L-hyoscyamine (as Atropa), with scopolamine secondary; *D. metel* and *Brugmansia* have higher scopolamine proportions, explaining their greater use in "vision" contexts.

## 4. Phytochemical Composition

### Tropane Alkaloids

| Alkaloid | Seed content | Leaf content | Notes |
|----------|-------------|--------------|-------|
| L-Hyoscyamine | 0.2–0.45% | 0.2–0.45% | PRIMARY — highest of all Solanaceae |
| Scopolamine | 0.06–0.18% | 0.04–0.12% | Secondary; higher in *D. metel* |
| Atropine | Trace | Trace | Racemization artifact |
| Norhyoscyamine | Trace | Trace | Minor alkaloid |
| Aposcopolamine | Trace | — | |
| **Total alkaloids** | **0.2–0.6%** | **0.2–0.5%** | **HIGHEST in Solanaceae** |

**Critical toxicological fact:** Alkaloid content varies 3–10 fold between individual plants, seasons, and plant parts — making any dose estimation inherently unreliable. Seeds from a "low" batch vs a "high" batch may differ by 10× in potency. This variability is the primary reason all crude datura preparations are clinically unacceptable.

### Other constituents
- Withanolides: minor (genus overlap with Withania)
- Daturolactone: chlorogenic acid derivative
- Tannins, flavonoids: secondary
- Fatty acid seed oil 24–27%
- Phenolic acids: caffeic, ferulic

## 5. Mechanisms of Action

**Same mechanistic class as I-330 (Belladonna) and I-331 (Henbane):** competitive, reversible muscarinic receptor blockade (M1–M5). Key differences from belladonna/henbane:

**Higher alkaloid loading → earlier toxicity threshold:**
- Datura's total alkaloid content is 2–4× that of belladonna leaf
- Therapeutic window effectively ZERO for crude plant — no safe herbal dose can be reliably established
- The Chinese pharmacopoeia dosing for yang jin hua flowers (0.2–0.4 g) reflects extremely low-end use with pharmaceutical quality-controlled material

**Chinese medicine anti-asthmatic mechanism:**
- M3 blockade → bronchial smooth muscle relaxation → bronchodilation
- Exactly the mechanism of anticholinergic bronchodilators (ipratropium/Atrovent®, tiotropium/Spiriva®) — pharmaceutical anticholinergic bronchodilators are the pharmacological legacy of datura/stramonium smoking (19th-century British physicians observed asthmatic relief from burning datura leaves)

**Historical link to modern respiratory pharmacology:**
Potter's Asthma Remedy (Potter's Herbals, UK) — stramonium-based cigarettes prescribed for asthma through mid-20th century. The efficacy was real (M3 bronchodilation); the method was abandoned due to narrow therapeutic window and carcinogenicity of smoke.

## 6. Clinical Evidence

### Pharmaceutical Legacy Only

**Anticholinergic Bronchodilators — Level A (pharmaceutical derivatives)**
- Ipratropium bromide (Atrovent®): synthetic quaternary ammonium anticholinergic bronchodilator; mechanism directly derived from datura/stramonium alkaloid research; Cochrane Stoodley 2012: significant benefit in COPD, acute asthma
- Tiotropium (Spiriva®): long-acting M3-selective; GOLD guidelines recommend for COPD
- Connection: 19th-century stramonium smoking → isolation of hyoscyamine/atropine → development of synthetic anticholinergic bronchodilators (ipratropium 1974)

**Crude *D. stramonium* Preparations — Level D**
No modern RCTs; historical use only; NOT recommended for any indication.

**Scopolamine/Hyoscyamine (from genus) — Level A**
As documented in I-330 (Belladonna) and I-331 (Henbane) — pharmaceutical purified alkaloids only.

## 7. Phenomenological Record

### Jimsonweed Name Origin
"Jamestown weed" — 1676, Virginia Colony: British soldiers sent to suppress Bacon's Rebellion ingested *D. stramonium* in a salad (accidentally or deceptively). Robert Beverley's *The History and Present State of Virginia* (1705) documents 11 days of anticholinergic delirium: "Some of them eat plentifully of it, the Effect of which was a very pleasant Comedy: for they turn'd natural Fools upon it for several days; one would blow up a Feather in the Air; another would dart Straws at it with much Fury." Complete recovery with no memory of the episode — characteristic scopolamine-component amnesia.

### Ayurvedic Surgical Anesthesia and Poison Lore
The *Sushruta Samhita* references dhatura in the context of surgical preparation and the neutralization of poisons. More elaborately, the *Charaka Samhita* references "madanaphala" preparations incorporating toxic plants including dhatura for surgical stupefaction. *Yoginis* (female tantric practitioners) were said to use dhatura preparations — the phenomenology of datura intoxication (loss of boundary between self and environment, erotic visions, contact with spirits) made it a tool for certain tantric ritual contexts.

### Carlos Castaneda and Datura in Popular Culture
Carlos Castaneda's *The Teachings of Don Juan* (1968) features extensive accounts of datura (yerba del diablo — "devil's herb") as a power plant used under shamanic guidance. While Castaneda's anthropological credentials are disputed, his books popularized datura in the counterculture and contributed to dangerous recreational experimentation. The phenomenological accounts — sensation of flying, animal transformation, complete dissolution of ordinary identity — are consistent with high-dose anticholinergic delirium.

### Delirium vs. Psychedelic: A Critical Distinction
Datura's psychoactive effects are categorically different from classical psychedelics (psilocybin, LSD, mescaline):
- **Psychedelics:** Enhance or distort perception of REAL environment; user maintains awareness that effects are drug-induced; 5-HT2A agonism
- **Datura:** Produces COMPLETE HALLUCINATIONS indistinguishable from reality (full-blown delirium); user does not know they are drugged; sees people, insects, objects that do not exist; often interacts with hallucinations; M1/M4 blockade
- **Clinical outcome:** Datura intoxication is a medical emergency, not a recreational experience; hospital rates for recreational datura use are orders of magnitude higher than classical psychedelics

### 19th-Century Stramonium Cigarettes
Grimault's Indian Cigarettes (Paris, 1850s–early 1900s) — commercially sold stramonium cigarettes for asthma, available in pharmacies across Europe and America. Physicians prescribed them; patients smoked them for bronchodilation. Effect was genuine; replaced by pharmaceutical bronchodilators when ipratropium isolated. Documents transition from crude botanical to pharmaceutical alkaloid.

## 8. Practical Application

### ⚠ EXTREME TOXICITY WARNING — NO SAFE HERBAL USE ⚠

**DO NOT USE CRUDE DATURA PREPARATIONS**

*D. stramonium* is perhaps the most dangerous of all commonly encountered toxic plants due to:
1. **Highest total alkaloid content** of any Solanaceae (~0.2–0.6% seeds)
2. **Most extreme alkaloid variability** (3–10× between specimens)
3. **No reliable dose → response relationship** possible with crude material
4. **Complete delirium** at low overdose — user cannot self-regulate
5. **Multiple routes of accidental exposure** — transdermal from handling, inhalation from burning, GI from seeds

**Minimum lethal dose estimates (unreliable due to variability):**
- Children: 4–5 g seeds (potentially fatal)
- Adults: 15–25 g seeds (potentially fatal)
- Accidental poisonings from insufficient cooking of contaminated grain are documented

**Emergency treatment:**
- Physostigmine IV (cholinesterase inhibitor) — specific antidote for life-threatening anticholinergic toxicity (bradyarrhythmia, severe agitation, hyperthermia); dose: 1–2 mg IV slow push; specialist supervision only
- Benzodiazepines for agitation
- Active cooling for hyperthermia
- Cardiac monitoring (ECG — QRS widening at high doses)
- Urinary catheter (urinary retention)
- DO NOT use neostigmine (does not cross BBB)

### Pharmaceutical Legacy Applications
Modern therapeutic use of *Datura* alkaloid pharmacology is exclusively through:
- **Ipratropium bromide** (Atrovent®) — COPD/asthma
- **Tiotropium** (Spiriva®) — COPD
- **Aclidinium** (Tudorza®) — COPD
- **Umeclidinium** (Incruse Ellipta®) — COPD
- **Atropine, scopolamine, hyoscyamine** (as documented in I-330, I-331)

### Regulatory Status
- Not approved for any therapeutic use in EU, US, UK, Australia, or Canada as herbal medicine
- Listed as poisonous plant in most pharmacopoeias
- Traditional use in some Ayurvedic and Chinese medicine contexts under strict practitioner supervision using pharmaceutical-quality dried flower preparations

### Energetic Classification
**Primary:** ☿ Shen (neuromodulatory) — datura's central effects (delirium, amnesia, hallucination) represent the extreme end of Shen disruption; used historically only for controlled consciousness-alteration under specialist supervision  
**Secondary:** ◉ Qi (functional) — bronchodilator, antispasmodic, analgesic properties
