# Bhalluka [SS] — Bhalluka (Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch. 38, Viratarva'di group; Chikitsa ch. 7)

## *A plant name in the Viratarva'di group of drugs, which Bhishagratna's translation prints without a botanical equivalent; the identification is unresolved, at low confidence. The name is not the same word as Bhallataka (the marking nut, Semecarpus anacardium), which the same translation prints separately and its index glosses as "Bhallektaka"; the first-pass merge of this row into the Bhallataka entry (CPU-00110) assumed an identity the text does not state, so the row is kept apart. Bhalluka is also the name of two animals in the translation's index, which is a further reason for care* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry, and the text is Bhishagratna's English translation, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt` (Sutra, Vol. I) and `Bhishagratna_Vol2_djvu.txt` (Vol. II). The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it.

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Sutra ch. 38, the Viratarva'di group* (Vol. I, line 15402-15403). Chapter 38 is the classification of drugs "according to their therapeutical properties" (*Dravya-Sangrahaniya*), in thirty-seven groups. The group is given as: "The drugs known as Virataru, the two kinds of Sahachara, Darbha, Vrikshadani, Gundra, Nala, Kusha, Kasha, Ashmabhedaka, Agnimantha, Morata, Vasuka, Vasira, **Bhalluka**, Kuruntaka, Indivara, Kapotavanka, and Shvadanstra enter into the composition of the group known as the Viratarvadi." The metrical text that follows (lines 15408-15412) reads, with a page break in the middle: "The group subdues all disorders incidental to the deranged state of Vata and [the text at the page break is cut] proves curative in Ashmari, Sharkara, Mutrakrichhra, Mutraghata and urinary troubles." The word after "Vata" is lost at the page break in this e-text. No property is given for Bhalluka alone; it holds only the place of one member in a group, and the group's effects are stated for the group.

**Ayurveda (SS).** *Chikitsa ch. 7, the treatment of urinary calculus (Asmari)* (Vol. II, "Chapter VII" at line 14968). Two recipes name Bhalluka among many drugs. In the treatment of *Vataja Asmari* (line 14983 and following) clarified butter "cooked with a decoction of Pashanabheda, Vasuka, Vasira, Ashmantaka, Shatavari, Shvadamstra, Vrihati, Kantakarika, Kapotavamka, Artagala, Kakubha, Usira, Kubjaka, Vrikshadani, **Bhalluka**, Varuna, Shaka-phala, barley, Kulattha, Kola and Kataka fruits and with the Kalka of the drugs constituting the group of Ushakadi, speedily brings about the disintegration of Asmari (urinary calculi, etc.)" (lines 14984-14992, with a note on variant readings of the neighbouring names). In the treatment of *Pittaja Asmari* a decoction of "Kusha, Kasha, Shara, Gundra, Itkata, Morata, Ashmabhid, Shatavari, Vidari, Varahi, Shali-mula, Trikantaka, **Bhalluka**, Patala, Patha, Pattura, Kuruntika, Punarnava, Shirisha", with a paste of shilajatu, the flower of Madhuka and seeds of Indivara, Trapusha and Ervaruka, "would speedily bring about the disintegration of Pittaja Asmari" (lines 15008-15016).

*Other words spelled Bhalluka.* In the same translation Bhalluka is also a name of an animal. Sutra ch. 46 lists "Shankha, Shankhanakha ..., Shukti, Shambuka and Bhalluka, etc." among the conchiferous (*Koshastha*) animals (Vol. I, line 20421), and the animal index (Vol. III, Appendix No. 2, lines 30404-30406) gives "Bhalluka: a particular class of shells" and "Bhalluka: a bear". These are not the plant and are not folded in. The plant index (Appendix No. 3) has no entry for "Bhalluka" in the text on disk, so the translation gives no botanical equivalent for the plant.

*Not Bhallataka.* The marking nut, Bhallataka, occurs elsewhere in the translation (for example Vol. I, lines 15690, 15733 and 18887), and the plant index lists the marking nut as "Bhallektaka" with the equivalent "Semecarpus Anacardium" (lines 31399 and 31455). Bhalluka is printed as a distinct name in the Viratarvadi group, and the text does not say whether it is a short form of Bhallataka. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** It is one member of the Viratarva'di group, whose action is stated for the group (derangement of Vata, and *Ashmari*, *Sharkara*, *Mutrakrichhra*, *Mutraghata* and urinary troubles), and one ingredient in two urinary-calculus preparations.
- **What the text does not say.** What the plant is, which part is used, what it does alone.

The Codex does not translate the Sanskrit disease names into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend any plant on the strength of this entry. Urinary stones are a medical condition that needs proper care.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations in the sources read are the two decoctions above (clarified butter cooked with a decoction and a paste in the *Vataja* recipe; a decoction with a paste and further drugs in the *Pittaja* recipe). The translation prints no weight for Bhalluka alone in the lines read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved for this plant. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The plant stands in the Viratarva'di group of chapter 38, a group classified by therapeutic action on the humours and the urinary tract, and in two *Asmari* recipes in Chikitsa chapter 7. Neither place is a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter; no passage found names Bhalluka in either of those formal disciplines, and no relation to *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* is stated. The status in this tradition is therefore therapeutic only, and the cultivation status is unattested. The popular *sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas* lists come from Samkhya and later material and not from Sushruta, and none is applied here.

**Practitioner's note.** An unidentified plant that holds a seat in a group and in two stone recipes. There is nothing here for a cultivator to take or do. The entry is held open so that a reader who can identify the Sanskrit plant can attach it; until then it is a name with a use-list and no species.

### Contraindications

- **Identity unknown.** Do not use any plant on the strength of this entry, and do not assume it is the marking nut, which is a strong irritant of skin; the text does not equate them.
- **Urinary stones.** A medical condition; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
- **Animals of the same name.** Bhalluka is also a bear and a shell group in the same translation; none of that applies here.
