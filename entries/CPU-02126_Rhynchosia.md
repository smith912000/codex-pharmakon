# Rhynchosia — 鹿藿 (Lu Huo); "deer bean" (鹿豆, Guo Pu)

## **Rhynchosia volubilis* Lour. (Fabaceae), the twining herb; the identification is of medium confidence, based on Guo Pu's gloss ("now called deer bean, leaf like soybean, root yellow and fragrant, spreading vine") and the modern standard reading* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Lu huo* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The placeholder row it replaces was named "Herba Rhynchosiae Volubilis."

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 鹿藿 in the lower classic (卷三 下经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3515, 味 paragraph; name line 3513). The Shennong text proper: "bitter, balanced" (味苦，平). It "mainly treats" "gu poison; in women, pain of the lumbar region and abdomen, and unhappiness [不乐]; intestinal abscess, scrofula (瘰[疬]; the *Yulan* has 历 for the lost glyph), and sore-qi (疡气)" (主蛊毒，女子腰腹痛，不乐，肠痈、瘰疬、疡气). "It grows in mountains and valleys" (生山谷). There is no alias, no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement.

*Wu Pu layer.* None.

*Mingyi bielu layer* (line 3517). "Grows at Wenshan." Nothing more.

*Sun's note* (案, line 3519). *Shuowen*: [lost glyph] is *lu huo*, "read as 剽." *Guangya*: [lost glyph] is *lu huo*. *Erya*: [lost glyph] is *lu huo*; "its fruit (实) is [lost glyph]." Guo Pu: "today's *lu dou* (deer bean); the leaf is like the soybean, the root yellow and fragrant, it grows spreading as a vine." That is the only description, and it describes a twining legume.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 3652) prints "Lu Huo (Herba Rhynchosiae Volubilis) is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats gu toxins, lumbar and abdominal pain and discomfort in females, intestinal abscesses, scrofulas, and sores. It grows in mountains and valleys." No footnote on this entry.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

This is a historical record of one text, not a recommended practice.

- **What the text says it is for.** "Gu poison," women's lumbar and abdominal pain with low mood, intestinal abscess, scrofula and sores.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no 久服 promise, no toxicity statement.

Intestinal abscess and swellings are medical conditions. No Kenetica protocol is derived from this entry.
