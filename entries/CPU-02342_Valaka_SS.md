### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta Samhita among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Valaka [SS]", taken from an extraction of Bhishagratna's translation; this entry replaces it. The locus given was "SS Sutra 38, Elardi group (Vol. I line 15509)".

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** Valaka is printed in the Sutrasthana, chapter 38 (locus: Elardi group, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 169; the same text is in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol1_djvu.txt`, line 15509). Chapter 38 is "the general classification of drugs according to their therapeutical properties" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 3-6), and says that "These drugs are usually made into thirty-seven different groups (Gana)" (lines 8-10). The name is given by the translator without a gloss, a botanical name or a description.

In the Elardi group (printed "Eladi-Gana" in the list) (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt`, lines 164-170 (the list), lines 171-186 (the metrical text)), Valaka is one of the drugs listed. The group's metrical text reads: "subduing the action of Vayu and Kapham and in neutralising the effects of poison. It is a cosmetic and arrests the eruption of pimples and other vegetations on the skin such as rash, urticaria etc. and checks the itching sensation incidental thereto." (the sentence runs across a page break; its opening words are cut off in the e-text) That is a property of the group as a whole, not of Valaka alone.

**Other places the name appears.** In Uttarasthana ch.40 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/uttara/SS_Uttara_ch40.txt`, lines 221-222) the recipe list for Amatisara (Ama Atisara) includes a third recipe printed "Abhayd, Dhanyaka, Musta, Vdlaka and Vilva", with the chapter's own heading "Twenty different Recipes for Amati-Sara" (lines 216-217). Vdlaka is printed again at lines 336 and 346 of the same chapter, in later compounds ("Chandana, Vdlaka, Musta, ..."; "Indra-yava, Ambhoda (Musta), Vdlaka and Ativisha"), which I did not read to the end. [USER: verify] The spelling Vdlaka differs from Valaka only by a vowel mark, and it is probably the same drug, but the e-text does not say so. [USER: verify] In Sutrasthana ch.46 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`, line 740) a bird called "Valaka" is listed among the Plava (water-bird) family of flesh foods. That is a different thing sharing the name and is not this entry's drug.

The chapter closes (lines 554-566) by saying that the groups "should be therapeutically used according to the nature of the deranged humours involved in each individual case", and that plasters, decoctions, medicated oils, Ghritas or potions are to be prepared "according to the exigencies of each individual case". That is a general rule of the chapter and is not a recipe for this drug.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** The text uses it in the Elardi group (a group said to subdue Vayu and Kapham, to neutralise poison, and to act as a cosmetic against skin eruptions and itching) and in one recipe for Ama Atisara (Uttara ch.40).

The Codex does not translate the text's terms (Vayu, Pitta, Kapham, Gulma, Shosha, Asmari and the rest) into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend a plant it cannot identify.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Valaka stands in the dravya-group classification of Sutrasthana ch.38, a classification by therapeutic action. The Elardi group is a therapeutic group (Vayu, Kapham, poison, skin), not a rasayana or vajikarana group. A search of the chapter files on disk for the spellings printed here found no mention of Valaka in the rasayana or vajikarana chapters, and no text read calls it an elixir or a drug of vitality. The register is therefore (b): framed by its therapeutic group, with no cultivation status attested. The Codex does not assign *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* effects to it, because no source read does.

**Practitioner's note.** Valaka is a name in a list. The tradition's text gives the drug a place among therapeutic groups and little more, and nothing in the record supports a cultivation use. A cultivator can learn from it only how Sushruta organises drugs into groups by action, and that some names in that classification are no longer certainly identified. Nobody should try to find a plant to match the name.
