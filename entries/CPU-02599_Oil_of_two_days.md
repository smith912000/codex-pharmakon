# Oil-of-Two-Days — in Bryan's Ebers list of "Mineral Remedies" (Papyrus Ebers)

## *An oil listed by Bryan among the mineral remedies and not identified; the Egyptian name and the substance are not stated in the text on disk, so the identity is open and the confidence is low, and the name is Bryan's English rendering of a hieratic term that we cannot read* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Ebers papyrus (Bryan 1930) among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry and a thin one. The row began on 8 November 2026 as an unverified placeholder; this entry replaces it. The class "mineral" comes from where Bryan lists it, not from any statement about what it is.

**Ebers (EP).** In Bryan's English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`), the chapter on the pharmacopoeia of ancient Egypt (Chapter IV) is followed by an alphabetical list headed "MINERAL REMEDIES" (Chapter V). In the "O" entries, line 1193 reads: "Oil Castor Oil Olive Oil White Oil Oil-of-Two-Days", followed on line 1194 by "Oil-of-the-Earth (Petroleum) Oil-from-the-Top-of-the-des-jar Pieces-of-dried Oil". The names.csv row for this term (`Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, row "Oil-of-Two-Days") carries the note "Bryan's English identification". The list is a list of names, with no description of any item.

A second place in the same book names it. In Bryan's chapter on the limbs, the paragraph on suppleness (line 2180 to 2181) says the limbs could be made supple with a liniment, and gives a run of oils: "Hippopotamus Oil, Crocodile Oil, Oil-of-the-Shadfish, Olil-of-the-adu-animal, and Oil-of-Two-Days." It continues: "But the remedy par excellence for suppleness was Ass's dung." So the term appears there among animal fats and oils, which sits oddly with the "Mineral Remedies" heading. The class assigned by the list is therefore doubtful. [USER: verify the class.]

The name's meaning is not explained in either place. A reading such as an oil aged two days, or one that takes two days to prepare, is a guess and is not made here. The Ebers corpus is organised by indication, and the substance identity of such names is a modern hypothesis.

### Functional–Anatomical Correlate

Little to map: the only body part the text gives is the limbs. The oil is one of several named in a liniment that the text says could make the limbs supple, placing its claimed action on the limb joints, muscles and soft tissue that a liniment rubbed onto the skin reaches. The chapter gives no other site, and no organ, channel or energetic classification. The heading Mineral Remedies under which the term is listed places it in the mineral kind, but the same term appears among animal fats in the limb passage, so the class is unsettled. Traditional Egyptian frames of the person, such as the ka, ba, akh and ib, are not attested for this oil in the text, and the entry assigns it no vital or constitutional site. The limbs, in modern terms the musculoskeletal system of arms and legs, are therefore the whole of its mapped claim.
