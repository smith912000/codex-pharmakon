# Goose fat — "Goose-fat", "Goose-grease", "Goose-oil" (Bryan's English renderings of the Ebers Papyrus; the animal "Goose" is on Bryan's animal list)
## *Rendered fat of the goose, printed by Bryan under three English names; no Egyptian word, no species and no preparation of the fat itself is given in the sources retrieved* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian Tradition (EP).** The locus is Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers*, Chapter VII "Organic Remedies (animals)". "Goose" stands on Bryan's list of the animals "whose identity is not in doubt" (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt` line 1398: "Goose Duck Stag Gazelle Ram Goat"; name-index row `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv` line 108, class animal, "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list"). Bryan's list of the parts of animals that the papyrus used (lines 1405-1413) includes "Fat" and "Tallow" but does not tie any part to any animal. The spine row ("Goose fat, Anser sp.", citing Wikipedia) was not used; the passages below were read in Bryan's text.

**The fat in Bryan's running text.** The Bryan text on disk was searched for "goose". Bryan prints the fat under three spellings, "Goose-fat", "Goose-grease" (once "Goosegrease") and "Goose-oil" or "Goose Oil", and does not say whether they are the same preparation. They are listed here as printed. The passages are these.

- **Goose-oil, line 1377** (Bryan's chapter on organic remedies): "a mixture of Rotten Flesh, Herbs-of-the-Field, and Garlic, cooked in Goose-oil and swallowed for four days against Debility".
- **Goosegrease, line 1686**, in the alimentary chapter: "a draught composed of Goosegrease and Lead-vitriol, flavoured though it be with Wine", which Bryan cites among remedies he doubts.
- **Goose-fat, line 1895**, in the section on indigestion (heading line 1766): a drink ending with "Honey Goose-fat [...] Make into one and let the man or woman drink it". Some words between "Goose-fat" and "Make" are lost in the text on disk.
- **Goose-oil, line 2644**, in "Diseases of the Skin" (heading line 2607), for scabs in every limb: "Cyperus-from-the-Marshes, Cyperus-from-the-Fields, Cyperus-knots, and Red Corn, were crushed in Fresh Oil, Goose-oil, and Semen".
- **Goose-grease in the eye chapter** (heading line 2721): salves in which collyrium is combined "with Goose-grease and Water" (line 2734); "ANOTHER TO DRIVE OUT FAT-IN-THE-EYES: Red-lead [...] Goose-grease [...] Smear the eyes therewith" (lines 2760-2767); a poultice of "Goose-grease and Honey" for cataract (line 2823); and for a flow of matter, collyrium "soaked in Water for four days; then for another four days [...] soaked in Goose-grease", then washed in the milk of a woman who had borne a son, dried for nine days, crushed with myrrh, made into a ball and rubbed on the eye (line 2856).
- **Goose-grease in the ear, nose and mouth chapter** (heading line 3027): for an ear that discharges foul matter, "Incense-in-Goose-Grease ground up with Cream-from-the-Milk-of-a-Cow" (line 3032); a chewing remedy of "Goose-grease Milk. Chew." (lines 3134-3135); and another of goose-grease, honey, incense, caraway seeds, fresh lead-earth and water, to be chewed "Nine Times" (line 3136).
- **Goose Oil, line 3185**, in "The Nervous System" (heading line 3148), in the section on the *met*: one item in a long ingredient list headed "REMEDY TO MAKE THE MET SUPPLE", between "White Oil" and "Hog's dung".
- **Goose-grease, line 3434**, in the heart section (heading line 3303): a drink of "Fat Flesh, Figs, Berries, Incense, Caraway, Nasturtium, Goose-grease, Writing-fluid, and Sweet Beer" to "put the Heart into proper working order and make it take up nourishment".
- **Goose-fat, line 3858**, in the diagnostic chapter (heading line 3438), for a "mattery-tumour in the neck of a grown-up man": "Wax Cow's fat Xet-plant Writing-fluid teun-plant Caraway Copper-shavings Verdigris Fresh lead-earth Sea-salt Goose-fat Incense-berries Collyrium. Cook and poultice the neck therewith."

**Related items not used.** "Blood-of-a-Goose" (line 2357, for the *uases* abscess), "Egg-of-the-Goose" (line 2080) and "Guts-of-the-Goose" (line 2084) are other goose parts and are not this item. A Codex project file (`Sources/EP_Ebers/EP_Original_Hieroglyphic.md` lines 475, 489, 855) prints "oil of goose" and "goose fat" in passages labelled as Ebbell translations; they could not be checked against Bryan and were not used. **What is not said.** The sources retrieved do not say which goose, how the fat was rendered, or whether the three English names correspond to three Egyptian words. The Ebers material is indication-organised, so the identity of any named substance is a modern hypothesis. ### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the papyrus names it. In general terms, rendered poultry fat is mainly triglycerides, rich in monounsaturated and saturated fatty acids, with small amounts of other lipids. Rendered fat is a bland vehicle for ointments and for dissolving other ingredients. This is a general statement, not a reading of Bryan.

---

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources retrieved show goose fat as a vehicle in ointments, eye salves, chews, drinks and poultices for many complaints (debility, scabs, eye and ear disorders, heart and neck conditions), always with other drugs and without a quantity. The fat is a food fat; the Codex does not recommend any of these mixtures, several of which contain lead, copper, verdigris or animal matter. No modern protocol is supplied.

---

### Preparation & Dosing

Only what Bryan reports. Goose fat is cooked with rotten flesh, herbs and garlic and swallowed for four days (line 1377); drunk with honey (line 1895); crushed with oil, semen and cyperus for scabs (line 2644); mixed with red-lead and smeared on the eyes (lines 2760-2767); used to soak collyrium for four days (line 2856); ground with incense and cow's cream for the ear (line 3032); chewed with milk, or with honey, incense, caraway, lead-earth and water (lines 3134-3136); and cooked in a neck poultice (line 3858). No quantity is printed in Bryan's summary for any of these. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's vital constitution; the debility remedy at line 1377 is the nearest thing to a constitutional complaint and is a treatment of it, not a practice for the healthy. Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*). Two spoken texts stand close to goose-fat passages and are not transferred to it. At lines 2824-2832 the cataract incantation, "Come, Verdigris! Come, Verdigris! [...]", follows Bryan's mention of the goose-grease and honey poultice but belongs to a separate remedy addressed to verdigris. At line 3858 the physician is told to say "He has a mattery-tumour. I will fight the disease." before the goose-fat poultice; that is the case's diagnostic address, not a recitation over the drug. The person as plural (*ka*, *ba*, *akh*, *ib*) is not engaged by this entry. No deity is attributed to the fat.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The record shows a common fat used as a base in many unrelated mixtures, with no quantity and no cultivation role. A cultivator has nothing to do with it. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** rendered animal fat on broken skin, in the eye or ear, or in a poultice can carry bacteria and, when rancid, cause irritation; the eye and ear passages are not recommended.
- **Toxic co-ingredients:** several of the cited mixtures contain red-lead, lead-vitriol, lead-earth, verdigris or copper shavings, which are poisonous.
- **Allergy:** poultry products can cause allergic reactions in some people.
- **Conservation and ethics:** geese are common domestic and wild birds; no conservation issue arises from the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
