# Anthericum tuberosum — Dravanti, Dravantika (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3)

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The chapters name the drug only by Sanskrit name (Dravanti), and in one footnote the translator reports that authorities differ on what it is (see below). A search of the Dioscorides, Shennong (Yang 1998), Ebers, Assyrian and Tibetan text folders for *Anthericum* found only Dioscorides' translators' SUGGESTED line for 3-122 *PHALAGGION* ("Anthericum liliago — Unbranched Lily Spiderwort", line 7632), a different species treated in the Codex's I_1004. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. Quotations keep the translator's words; the OCR's garbled diacritics are normalised. [USER: verify]

**Row key.** The row has two source rows in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv`: line 464, "DravantikaC" and line 465, "Dravanti", both with appendix line 31634 and Latin line 31642, "Anthericum Tuberosum." The OCR prints the two Sanskrit lines as a bracketed pair ("Dravantika 1", "Dravanti /"), so one Latin line serves both. The extraction's note says the pairing was proposed by an agent from the two OCR columns and checked by a script.

**The pairing, checked by counting.** On the page, the Sanskrit run is **"Dravantika" and "Dravanti"** (31634, 31635, bracketed), "Drona-pushpi" (31636), "Dugdhika" (31637), "Dursilabha" (31638) and "Durva" (31639); the Latin run is **"Anthericum Tuberosum."** (31642), "Leucas Linifolia." (31644), "Oxystelma esculentum." (31645), "Alhagi Maurorun." (31646) and "Cynodon dactylon." (31647). Five Sanskrit entries and five Latin entries line up in order, and the neighbours fit (*Leucas* with Drona-pushpi, *Alhagi* with Dursalabha, *Cynodon dactylon* with Durva). [USER: verify]

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *The name in the chapters.* "Dravanti" occurs in ten chapters of the chapter files (Sutra 11, 39, 42, 44, 45; Chikitsa 2, 18, 31; Uttara 42, 52). It is always in a list of purgative or cleansing drugs, usually beside Danti and Trivrit.

*The purgative list (Sutra 39, lines 21 to 28).* In the chapter on drugs of cleansing and soothing effect: "Purgative Drugs: The trees, plants, herbs and creepers, etc. known as Trivrita, Shyama, Danti, Dravanti, Saptala, Shankhini, Vishanika, Gavakshi, Chhagalantri, Snuk, Suvarnakshiri, Chitraka, Kinihi, Kusha, Kasha, Tilvaka, Kampillaka ... are possessed of purgative properties." The text then says "the roots of plants, which precede Tilvaka in the above list, should be used for purgative purposes" (lines 28 to 36). Dravanti is before Tilvaka, so its root is the part.

*The bitter group (Sutra 42, lines 333 to 347).* Dravanti is in the list that "collectively constitute the bitter group", between Shankhini and Trivrit.

*The alkali chapter (Sutra 11, lines 183 to 189).* An alkali "prepared with the addition of the powders of the drugs known as Danti, Dravanti, Chitraka, Langulaki, Putika-Pravala, Talpatri, Vidha, Suvarchika, Kanaka-Kshiri, Hingu, Vacha, and Visha, or with as many of them as are available, each weighing four tolas, is called the strong Kshara (extremely irritating alkali)." The Codex's II_55 (carbonate of soda) quotes the same line.

*The choice of purgatives (Sutra 44, lines 330 to 383).* "The rules and processes regarding the preparation of Trivrit compounds hold good in cases of similar preparations made of the rest of purgative drugs (such as, Danti, Dravanti, etc.)." The roots of Danti and Dravanti are pulled up, dried in the sun, mixed with honey and pasted Pippali, placed in a Kusha-grass box plastered with clay and cooked by *Putapaka* in a cowdung fire, and then used "in diseases due to the action of the deranged Kapham and Pittam". Pastes and decoctions of Danti and Dravanti are boiled with clarified butter, or with *Chakra Tailam* (sesamum oil pressed in an oil mill): the ghee "would prove curative in cases of Erysipelas, Kaksha, burning sensation of the body and Alaji," the oil in "Meha, Gulma, retention of flatus, (kapham) and obstruction of the bowels"; and one of the four oily substances cooked with them serves in "retention of urine, semen and Vayu or fecal matter." A compound of "Danti, Dravanti, Maricha, Kanakahvaya, Yavasaka, Vishaa-veshaja, Mridvika, and Chitraka powdered together and successively soaked in cow's urine for seven days" is given as a purgative through ghee, followed by "a diet of powdered barley, stirred in honey", for "indigestion, pain at the sides, jaundice, enlargement of the spleen" and the combined Kapha and Pitta diseases.

*The oils (Sutra 45, lines 1012 to 1020; Chikitsa 31, lines 33 to 36).* "The Oils obtained from the seeds of Tumvi, Koshamra, Danti, Dravanti, Shyama, Saptala, Nilika, Kampillaka, and Shankhini, are bitter, pungent and astringent in their tastes," serve "to cleanse the system ... through their purgative properties," purify malignant ulcers and are curative in diseases of deranged Vayu and Kapham, Kushtha and parasitic complaints. In the chapter on the Snehas, the expressed oils "prepared from (the seeds of) Lodhra, Eranda, Kos'anira, Danti, Dravanti, Saptala, S'amkhini, Palas'a, Vishanika, Gavakshi, Kampillaka, Sampaka and of Nilini act as purgatives."

*Recipes (Chikitsa 2, 18; Uttara 42, 52).* In Chikitsa 2 (traumatic wounds, lines 524 to 531), "Oil cooked with Dravanti (Satamuli, according to certain authorities, Mushika-parni according to others), Chiravilva, Danti, Chitraka, Prithvika, Nimba-leaves ..." is used "for the purification (of a malignant sore or ulcer)". In Chikitsa 18 (glandular swellings, line 188 to 193), clarified butter cooked with Jimutaka and Koshavati fruit "and with (the roots of) Danti, Dravanti and Trivrit" is for advanced cases of *Apachi*. In Uttara 42 (Gulma, lines 391 to 396), a compound of "Guggulu, Trivit, Danti, Dravanti, Saindhava and Vacha" is given "through milk, wine or the expressed juice of grapes, according to the strength (of the disease and of the patient)". In Uttara 52 (cough, lines 152 to 154), "a pulverised compound consisting of Danti, Dravanti and Tilvaka" is one of the remedies listed.

*The identity in the translator's hand.* The Chikitsa 2 parenthesis (line 525) is the only place in the chapters where Bhishagratna reports an identity for the name, and it reports two, *Satamuli* and *Mushika-parni*, "according to certain authorities". Neither is the appendix's Latin. The Codex therefore reads Dravanti as a purgative root of undetermined identity and the appendix's *Anthericum tuberosum* as a positional proposal.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Dravanti*, *Anthericum tuberosum* and the Sanskrit variants found no entry on this plant. II_55 (carbonate of soda) quotes the Sutra 11 line above; I_1004 (*Anthericum liliago*, Dioscorides 3-122) is a different plant and tradition; I_394 (Nagadanti, *Croton oblongifolius*) is an entry on a name of similar sound that the text lists separately from Danti. Nothing is merged.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Anthericum* is a genus of herbaceous, lily-like plants, and which plant the chapters mean by a purgative root is not shown by the lines read. Nothing is stated here about its composition. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records Dravanti as a root and seed used to purge, as a bitter, and as an ingredient of purgative oils, ghees, alkalis and compounds given by a physician. Several of those uses are strong ones (an "extremely irritating" alkali; seven-day soaks in cow's urine). They are the text's claims about a Sanskrit name whose identity the translator himself leaves open, and the Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The text gives these preparations, each with its locus. Sutra 44, lines 335 to 343: the dried root, with honey and Pippali paste, cooked by *Putapaka* in a Kusha-grass box. Sutra 44, lines 354 to 366: pastes and decoctions boiled with ghee or sesamum oil. Sutra 44, lines 368 to 383: the powder in a compound, soaked in cow's urine for seven days, through ghee. Sutra 45 and Chikitsa 31: oils from the seeds. Sutra 11, lines 183 to 189: powder added to the strong alkali, "each weighing four tolas". Uttara 42, lines 391 to 395: with Guggulu, Trivit, Danti, Saindhava and Vacha through milk, wine or grape juice. The only measure printed for Dravanti is "each weighing four tolas" in the alkali recipe, which is not a dose to be taken. No dose to be taken is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] The Sushruta terms (Kapham, Pittam, *Gulma*, *Apachi*, *Meha*) are not modern diagnoses, so no modern finding can be set beside them. It is a general, well-established fact that purgative roots and seeds can cause severe diarrhoea and dehydration. The identity is open (the appendix's *Anthericum*, or *Satamuli*, or *Mushika-parni*), so a study of any one plant would not bear directly on the Sushruta drug.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** the chapters read print for Dravanti: a place in the purgative list (Sutra 39), in the bitter group (Sutra 42), and, as an oil, "bitter, pungent and astringent" in taste and purgative (Sutra 45, lines 1012 to 1017). No *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for it alone in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Sushruta text names Dravanti only as a purgative and bitter drug, and as an ingredient of cleansing recipes. A search of the chapter files for "Dravanti" found it in none of the vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) or the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30), and the Sutra 44 chapter on purgatives is the text's own frame for it, so the Codex assigns it no rejuvenative or generative status. The chapters say nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for this drug. The Codex makes no claim that it is a *shodhana* (purification) drug in the Codex's cultivation sense, because the lines read give it only a cleansing use in the body and the alkali, not a status. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** A cleansing drug in the Sushruta scheme, to be given by a physician in a defined recipe and not taken for its own sake. The text itself rates its alkali "extremely irritating". Nothing here supports gathering or taking a root on the strength of a Latin name that the chapters do not give.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta passages read print no warning for the drug itself, but every use is a purgative or irritant one: the text's own phrase for the alkali is "extremely irritating". Purgatives are unsuitable in pregnancy, in dehydration, in children and in the weak without medical supervision. The identity is open, and some purgative roots are toxic, so no root should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. No conservation information was read. [USER: verify]
