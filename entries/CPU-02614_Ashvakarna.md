# Ashvakarna — As'va-karna, As'vakarna (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna)

## *A tree drug of the Sushruta Samhita whose bark is used in plasters, medicated oils and ghee, and which is named in the Salasaradi group; the identification is not settled. Bhishagratna's Appendix 3 gives no Latin name against it but a cross-reference printed in OCR as "See S'iila", and the treatise itself calls it a tree (it is named with trees and barks). Species left open* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

This is a single-text entry. The row was entered on 8 November 2026 from the residual key "As'va-karna [SS]" with the locus "SS Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31295; id line 31343)". The appendix is the only list; the use of the drug is in the chapters, which this entry has read at the lines below.

**Sushruta (SS), Appendix 3.** In Bhishagratna's Volume III, Appendix No. 3, "Names of drugs (with Latin synonyms)" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt` line 31119), the Sanskrit name stands at line 31295 ("As'va-karna"). The Latin column, starting at line 31330, runs in the same order as the Sanskrit names: Asoka = "Saraca Indica" (31336), Asthi-sambakraka = "Vitis quadrangularis" (31338), Asva-gandha = "Withania Somnifera; Physalis Flexuosa", and then, in the place of As'va-karna, the line "See S'iila" (line 31343), followed by "Nerium Odorum" (Asva-maraka) and "Ficus Religiosa" (Asvattha). The appendix thus gives no botanical equivalent for As'va-karna, only a cross-reference whose target is an OCR form ("S'iila", probably a form of *Sala*); the target entry was not followed. [USER: verify] The row's earlier note, that the ID block "does not align cleanly", is correct in this sense: the alignment holds, but the cell is a see-reference.

**Sushruta (SS), the chapters.** The drug is used by its bark, or in decoctions and pastes:
- *Salasaradi group* (Bhishagratna Vol. I, line 15421): the group "consists of Salasara, Ajakarna, Khadira, Kadara, Kalaskandha, Kramuka, Bhurjja, Meshashringi, Tinisha, Chandana, Kuchandana, Shinshapa, Shirisha, Asana, Dhava, Arjuna, Tala, Shaka, Xaktamala, Putika Ashvakarna, Aguru and Kaliyaka". The verse says the group "destroys the germ of Kushtha, absorbs the deranged fat and Kapham and proves beneficial in morbid discharges from the urethra (Meha), chlorosis or jaundice (Pandu)". Whether "Putika Ashvakarna" is one name or two is not clear from the English; the group is attested, the reading is not. [USER: verify]
- *Powder* (Vol. I, line 15105): "Kanguka, Triphala, Lodhra, Kasisam, Shravana and the barks of Dhava and Ashvakarna powdered together" is "possessed of a similar healing property" to the preceding compound.
- *Fruit list* (Vol. I, line 20785): "Ashvakarna" appears in a list of trees whose fruits are "cooling, and astringent in their effect", subduing "the Pittam and Kapham", among Jamva, Rajadana, Todana, Tinduka and others.
- *Wound oil* (Vol. II, line 12741): "a medicated oil, prepared with the bark of the Asvakarna, Dhava, S'almali, Mesha-S'ringi, S'allaki, Arjuna, Vidari, and Kshiri trees and Vala roots should be applied to the wound" for its healing.
- *Ghee* (Vol. II, line 15755): "clarified butter prepared with (a decoction and Kalka of) Dhava, As'vakarna, Kakubha, Palasa, Pichumardha, Parpataka, Madhuka, Rodhra and Sainanga" (OCR), for the Pittaja type of the case under discussion.
- *Plaster* (Vol. II, line 19662): "a plaster composed of the barks of Sala, As'va-karna, Aja-karna and Dhava pasted with Sura and mixed with oil, should be applied hot to the affected part" in the Kaphaja type.
- *Poisoned water* (Vol. II, line 29849): the "cold ashes of Dhava, Asvakarna, Asana, Paribhadra, Patala, Siddhaka, Mokshaka, Rajadruma and Somavalka burnt together" are cast into a poisoned pool or tank, which is said to remove oedema (Shopha).
- *Colic* (Vol. III, line 11062): "Asva-karna and Sauvarchala" are in a compound with Vidanga, Shigru, Kiwipilla and others taken with wine "in an attack of the Vayu-origined type of Sula".

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a bark in wound-healing oils and ghees, in hot plasters (Kaphaja type), in a decoction group for Kushtha, urinary discharges and Pandu, in the purification of poisoned water, and in a colic compound with wine.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no weight of bark, and no length of treatment in the lines read; the English renders the forms (kalka, decoction, paste) without amounts.

The Codex gives no protocol, and because the plant is not identified, gives no way to find it.

### Preparation & Dosing

Forms attested: bark powder, bark decoction and paste (kalka), medicated oil, medicated ghee, hot plaster, and burnt ash. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Many tree barks are astringent because of tannins, which is consistent with the "cooling and astringent" description at Vol. I line 20785, but the identification is open, so no claim is made about this bark. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (Bhishagratna).** For the fruit: "cooling, and astringent", subduing Pittam and Kapham (Vol. I, line 20785). For the Salasaradi group as a whole: action on Kushtha, fat and Kapham, Meha and Pandu (line 15421). No rasa, virya and vipaka are printed for the bark itself in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug sits in a therapeutic group (Salasaradi) and in wound, plaster and colic prescriptions. No *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter naming it was read, and no promise of long life or generative vitality is attached to it in the lines read. The register is therefore (b): framed by its group and uses, with no cultivation status attested.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to do. A tree bark of many uses and uncertain identity, found in wound and skin prescriptions. It is not a recommended practice, and with the species unresolved nobody can select the right bark. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The species is not established, so substitution is a real risk. Do not use any bark on the strength of this entry. [USER: verify]
- **Wounds, poisoning, colic, jaundice, urinary disease.** These are medical conditions; use proper care. Nothing here treats poisoned water or poisoning.
- **Pregnancy, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
