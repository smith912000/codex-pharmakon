# Dalbergia oujeinensis — Tinisa (Tinis'a), Sushruta Samhita Appendix 3

## *Ougeinia oojeinensis (Roxb.) Hochr., printed in the Appendix as "Dalbergia Oujeinensis", a tree of the pea family, as the translator's Latin gloss to the Sanskrit name Tinisa; the Latin is Bhishagratna's and is printed on the line beside the name, and the identification of the Sanskrit name is of medium to high confidence. It is a different species from Dalbergia sissoo, which the same appendix glosses under another name* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named in the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) only. This is a single-text entry.** In Appendix No. 3, "Names of drugs with Latin synonyms" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`), the Sanskrit name stands at line 33133 as "Tinisa'" and the Latin at line 33135 as "... ... Dalbergia Oujeinensis." In this stretch of the e-text the OCR prints each name with its gloss on the next line, in the same pattern as the neighbouring pairs "Tinduka ... Dyospyros Glutinosa"  and "Tintida ... Tamarindus Indica", so the pairing needs no column counting. The extraction file `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` nevertheless lists the Latin line as unpaired ("OCR column split"). An earlier first pass treated this row as a duplicate of the entry for *Dalbergia sissoo* (CPU-00336); the audit disagreed, because *Ougeinia oojeinensis* is a distinct species, and the same appendix glosses a different Sanskrit name as "Dalbergia Sissoo".

**The name in the main text.** The Sanskrit name (Tinisha, Tinis'a, Tinisa in the OCR) occurs repeatedly in the Samhita, and the lines below were opened and read. In the Sutrasthana the drug-group list of the Salasaradi Gana reads "Salasara, Ajakarna, Khadira, Kadara, Kalaskandha, Kramuka, Bhurjja, Meshashringi, Tinisha, Chandana, Kuchandana, Shinshapa, Shirisha, Asana, Dhava, Arjuna, Tala, Shaka, Naktamala, Putika, Ashvakarna, Aguru and Kaliyaka" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` line 78). The metrical text that follows says the group "destroys the germ of Kushtha, absorbs the deranged fat and Kapham and proves beneficial in morbid discharges from the urethra (Meha), chlorosis or jaundice (Pandu)" (same file, lines 81 to 87). The classification of the pungent group (`SS_Sutra_ch42.txt` line 331) states that the Salasaradi Gana, with other drugs, "collectively form the pungent group".

In the Chikitsasthana the bark is used in an astringent role: an Asthapana enema of a decoction of "Ajakarna, Dhava, Tinis'a and Palas'a" with honey is recommended in a bowel disease called Parisrava (`SS_Chikitsa_ch34.txt` line 443), and Tinis'a appears in a list of drugs for medicated gruels and similar preparations (`SS_Chikitsa_ch11.txt` line 196; the condition treated was not read). In the chapter on diarrhoea, a potion of the barks of Piyala, S'ahnali, Plaksha, S'allaki and of Tinis'a pasted with milk and mixed with honey is given to stop the discharge of blood (`SS_Uttara_ch40.txt` line 609). In the chapter on antivenom preparations the wood is among the drugs of a Ksharagada (`SS_Kalpa_ch07.txt` line 8) and the flowers of Tinisa are in another compound (line 129). A commentator's note (`SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt` line 8) records that Dallana reads "Tini" for "Nemi" and that both mean Tinis'a. The text does not say which tree is meant in these lines; the identification rests on the appendix.

**No other text.** A search of the other source texts on disk for *Ougeinia*, *oojeinensis* and the Sanskrit name found nothing.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** As a member of the Salasaradi group: against Kushtha, excess fat and Kapha, urethral discharges and jaundice. As a single bark: pasted with milk and honey to stop a bloody diarrhoea (Uttara 40), and in an enema decoction for Parisrava (Chikitsa 34).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no duration, and no statement that the tree in these lines is the species glossed in the Appendix.

The Codex gives no protocol and does not recommend this tree.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Samhita gives these preparations: a decoction of the wood or bark with Ajakarna, Dhava and Palasa used as an Asthapana enema with honey (`SS_Chikitsa_ch34.txt` line 443); a paste of the bark with milk and honey as a potion (`SS_Uttara_ch40.txt` line 609); the wood and the flowers within antivenom compounds (`SS_Kalpa_ch07.txt` lines 8 and 129). **No dose for Tinisa alone is given in the lines read.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The drug is a member of the Salasaradi group of the Sutrasthana and a bark and wood of astringent and antivenom compounds. No chapter of the Chikitsa on rasayana or vajikarana (chapters 26 to 31 of the Chikitsasthana on disk) contains this name in a search of `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/`, so no cultivation status is attested for it. The register is therefore (b): framed by the dravya-guna group and the therapeutic recipes, with no *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status attested for it.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing to cultivate here. A tree whose bark and wood serve in astringent, urinary and antivenom recipes, with no cultivation status in the sources read. A cultivator has no sourced use for it.
