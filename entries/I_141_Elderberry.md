# I_141 — Sambucus nigra | Elder / Elderberry / Elderflower / Black Elder / Bore Tree

**Volume I: Plant-Based Substances**
**Entry:** I_141
**Convergence Score:** 5/9 traditions
**Energetic Tier:** ◉ Qi (antiviral/diaphoretic/anti-inflammatory) | ◈ Jing (structural — immune modulation)
**Safety Class:** Good when cooked; raw berries and leaves cause nausea/vomiting (cyanogenic glycosides in raw fruit, bark, leaves — destroyed by heat); cook or dry all preparations

**Botanical identity:** Deciduous shrub/small tree to 6m; pinnate leaves (5–7 leaflets); large flat-topped corymbs of tiny cream-white flowers with powerful muscat fragrance; small pendulous clusters of purple-black berries on red stems; all green/raw parts and bark toxic (cyanogenic glycosides); flowers and ripe cooked berries safe and medicinal. Elder has attracted more folklore, ritual significance, and folk belief than almost any other plant in Northern European tradition.

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**Score: 5/9** (DMM · CM · SBJ confirmed; EP · AT tentative; European folk = major tradition)

Elder achieves 5/9 convergence despite being primarily a temperate European and East Asian plant. The most pharmacologically significant traditions (European folk + modern RCTs) do not map to the oldest Codex sources (EP, AT) but represent the strongest evidence.

**The antiviral application** is the defining modern convergence: Sambucol (standardized black elderberry extract) is the most extensively RCT-tested antiviral herb in the pharmacopoeia, with multiple trials demonstrating significant reduction in influenza duration and severity. This modern pharmacological achievement sits atop 2,000 years of traditional use of elderberries and elderflowers for fevers and "epidemic diseases."

**The Chinese "bone-connecting wood" application** (jiē gǔ mù) is pharmacologically distinct from the European antiviral tradition: *S. williamsii* is specifically used for fracture healing and traumatic joint injury — an application without parallel in the European tradition. The sambunigrin (cyanogenic glycoside present in leaves and green bark) metabolizes to benzaldehyde, which at trace levels may stimulate osteoblast activity; this is a candidate mechanism for the bone-healing tradition.

**The fungal secondary metabolite hypothesis:** Elder provides habitat for *Sambucus nigra*-associated fungi, including the edible jelly ear fungus (*Auricularia auricula-judae*). This fungus (Judas' ear fungus — linked to the same Judas legend as the tree) has its own anti-inflammatory and anti-HIV properties. The folk tradition of using elder tree for multiple conditions may partly reflect multiple organisms in the elder ecosystem.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Flavonoids (primary antiviral/anti-inflammatory class — in berries and flowers):**
- **Cyanidin 3-glucoside** and **cyanidin 3-sambubioside** (dominant in berries) — anthocyanins responsible for deep purple color; antiviral; antioxidant; anti-inflammatory; neurocognitive
- **Quercetin** — antiviral; anti-inflammatory; anti-allergic
- **Kaempferol** — anti-inflammatory; antioxidant
- **Rutin** — capillary protective; anti-inflammatory
- **Isorhamnetin** — anti-inflammatory
- **Chlorogenic acid** — antioxidant; antiviral

**Triterpenoids:**
- **α-Amyrin, β-amyrin, ursolic acid, oleanolic acid** — anti-inflammatory; antiviral; hepatoprotective

**Volatile oil (flowers):**
- Nerol, linalool, geraniol, α-terpineol, nonanal — fragrant; anti-inflammatory; antimicrobial

**Polysaccharides:**
- Sambucal (immunostimulating polysaccharide) — macrophage activation; NK cell stimulation

**Cyanogenic glycosides (TOXIC — raw plant):**
- **Sambunigrin** (primary) — in raw berries, leaves, bark; hydrolysed to HCN + benzaldehyde; causes nausea, vomiting, cyanide toxicity at high doses; **completely destroyed by cooking**
- All preparations must be cooked or properly dried; raw elderberry juice consumption can cause severe GI poisoning

**Sterols:**
- β-Sitosterol, stigmasterol, campesterol — anti-inflammatory

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**Antiviral (primary validated mechanism — influenza):**
- **Cyanidin 3-glucoside + cyanidin 3-sambubioside:** bind to influenza virus hemagglutinin → prevent viral attachment to host cell sialic acid receptors → inhibit viral entry
- **Quercetin + chlorogenic acid:** inhibit influenza neuraminidase → prevent viral release from infected cells → reduce spread
- **Polysaccharides (sambucal):** stimulate macrophage IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α production → accelerated innate immune response → faster viral clearance
- **RCT validation:** significant reduction in influenza A and B duration (by 2–4 days); reduction in severity

**Anti-inflammatory:**
- Anthocyanins: inhibit NF-κB, COX-1/2, 5-LOX → reduced prostaglandin and leukotriene production
- Quercetin: mast cell stabilization → reduced histamine release (anti-allergic)
- Ursolic acid: NF-κB inhibition; anti-inflammatory

**Diaphoretic (fever management):**
- Elderflower volatile oils: mild diaphoretic (promotes sweating) → heat dissipation during fever
- Combined with elderflower flavonoids: anti-inflammatory reduction of fever mediators
- Traditional "fever tea" (elderflower + peppermint + yarrow = the classic Northern European fever formula) is pharmacologically rational

**Immune modulation:**
- Sambucal polysaccharides: macrophage activation (nitric oxide production; cytokine release)
- Anthocyanins: T-cell modulation; NK cell activation
- Combined: non-specific immune enhancement appropriate for acute viral infection

**Antioxidant (berries — ORAC values):**
- Elderberries have among the highest ORAC (oxygen radical absorbance capacity) values of any common fruit: ~14,697 μmol TE/100g — higher than blueberries, cranberries
- Anthocyanin content: 200–1,000mg/100g fresh berry

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Regulatory:**
- Commission E: Approved monograph for elderflower (Sambuci flos) for catarrhs of the respiratory tract and for promoting expectoration
- Not separately approved for elderberry (berry = Sambuci fructus) in German pharmacopoeia but widely used

**Clinical trials (RCTs — one of the most extensively trialed herbal products):**
- **Zakay-Rones et al. (1995) *Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine*:** First randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial; elderberry extract (Sambucol) vs. placebo in influenza B outbreak; significant reduction in duration (2–4 days shorter illness) and severity. N=40.
- **Zakay-Rones et al. (2004) *Journal of International Medical Research*:** Follow-up RCT; influenza A and B; standardized elderberry extract (Sambucol) 15mL 4× daily for 5 days vs. placebo; significant reduction in duration (average 4 days shorter vs. 8 days placebo). N=60.
- **Tiralongo et al. (2016) *Nutrients*:** RCT; elderberry extract for air-travel-associated upper respiratory tract infections; significant reduction in duration and severity in travelers taking elderberry vs. placebo. N=312. A methodologically strong trial.
- **Hawkins et al. (2019) *Complementary Medicine Research* (meta-analysis):** 9 double-blind RCTs; elderberry significantly reduced duration of cold/flu symptoms. Standardized mean difference (SMD) significant; strongest evidence for respiratory symptom reduction.
- **COVID-19 note:** Elderberry was widely discussed early in the pandemic; antiviral mechanism theoretically relevant but no completed RCT for SARS-CoV-2 specifically as of 2025.

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The Elder Mother (Scandinavian tradition):**
In Scandinavian, Danish, and German folk tradition, every elder tree is inhabited by a female spirit — the Elder Mother (*Hyldemoer*, *Holundermutter*). Before cutting or using elder wood, one must ask the Elder Mother's permission: "Old Elder Mother, give me some of your wood, and I will give thee some of mine when I grow into a tree." Failure to seek permission could result in illness of the harvester or their family. This protective spirit tradition ensured sustainable harvesting of elder (limiting cutting of living wood) while encoding the plant's power in a respectful mythological framework.

**Judas tree legend:**
Medieval Christian tradition attached the suicide of Judas Iscariot to the elder tree — he hanged himself from an elder after betraying Jesus. The fungus growing on elder wood (Judas' ear = *Auricularia auricula-judae*) reinforces this; the tree's toxic berries (raw) were seen as morally appropriate fruit for the betrayer's tree. This Christian narrative replaced the pre-Christian Elder Mother tradition in Northern Europe with a morality tale about betrayal — but preserved the elder's distinctive pharmacological and botanical identity through a different narrative frame.

**Hippocrates and the "country doctor's medicine chest":**
Hippocrates referred to elder as *iatron* — "medicine" itself. The later Renaissance claim (often attributed but not precisely sourced) that "an entire medicine chest is contained in the elder tree" summarizes the multi-part pharmacological use: flowers (diaphoretic, anti-inflammatory), berries (antiviral, antioxidant), leaves (purgative — toxic, historical only), bark (purgative/diuretic — toxic, historical only), wood (hollow for pipes). The tree-as-pharmacy concept reflects the genuine multi-application therapeutic history.

**Pliny, *NH* XXIV.35:** "The elder is very remarkable for its manifold virtues. The juice of the root, thickened and taken in drink, purges the dropsical... The leaves... have certain vermicidal properties. The bark of the root is so efficacious..." Pliny devotes more text to elder than to many more specialized herbs — reflecting the tree's extraordinary integration into Roman domestic medicine.

**Sambucol and the commercial pharmaceutical tradition:**
Dr. Madeleine Zakay-Rones developed Sambucol (standardized black elderberry extract) in Israel in the late 1980s; her 1995 RCT established the modern antiviral evidence base. Sambucol is now the most clinically tested elder product and one of the best-evidence natural antiviral preparations available. This tracks the trajectory from ancient folk remedy (Hippocrates, Dioscorides) through professional recommendation (Gerard, Culpeper) to 20th-century pharmaceutical standardization to 21st-century RCT validation — a complete pharmacological evidence arc spanning approximately 2,400 years.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

### ⚠️ CRITICAL SAFETY NOTE — DO NOT EAT RAW BERRIES

Raw elderberries, raw leaves, and bark contain sambunigrin (cyanogenic glycoside). Raw consumption → nausea, vomiting, potentially serious cyanide toxicity. ALL preparations must be cooked or properly dried. Commercial elderberry syrups, capsules, and standardized extracts are safe (heating destroys sambunigrin). Dried elderflowers are safe (used in teas without cooking).

**Primary modern applications:**

1. **Influenza / Acute viral respiratory infection (primary evidence-based use):** Standardized elderberry syrup (Sambucol or equivalent): 15mL (1 tablespoon) 4× daily at first sign of illness; continue for 5 days; RCT-validated reduction in duration and severity. Homemade: elderberry syrup (cooked berries + honey + cloves/ginger) equivalent preparation

2. **Cold prevention / Immune support (travel, exposure contexts):** 15mL standardized syrup once daily during high-risk periods; Tiralongo 2016 trial supports this use specifically for air-travel respiratory infection risk

3. **Hay fever / Allergic rhinitis (elderflower):** 2g dried elderflower infusion 3× daily during allergy season; quercetin mast cell stabilizer + diaphoretic + anti-inflammatory; combine with nettle (I_140) for synergistic anti-histamine effect

4. **Fever management (elderflower — classic formula):** Equal parts elderflower, peppermint leaf (*Mentha piperita*), and yarrow (I_034): 2g blend per cup, drink hot, rest under blankets; promotes diaphoresis; one of the most time-tested fever formulas in Northern European tradition

**Preparation (safety-conscious):**
- **Commercial standardized extract (safest, most convenient):** Sambucol, Nature's Answer, etc.; standardized anthocyanin content
- **Homemade syrup:** Simmer 1 cup dried berries in 3 cups water for 30–45 min; strain; cool to <60°C; add honey; store refrigerated up to 2 weeks; cooking destroys sambunigrin completely
- **Dried elderflower tea:** 1.5–2g per cup; safe; no cooking required; do NOT use raw fresh flowers (some sambunigrin present)
- **Tincture:** 1:5 in 25% ethanol; 4–6mL 3× daily; the alcohol extraction process at room temperature does NOT destroy sambunigrin — tinctures should be made from properly cooked/dried material only

**Contraindications:**
- **Autoimmune disease:** Polysaccharide immune stimulation; elderberry may exacerbate autoimmune conditions (theoretical; avoid or monitor in lupus, RA, MS)
- **Immunosuppressant medications:** potential pharmacodynamic interaction (immune stimulation opposing immunosuppression)
- **Pregnancy:** Insufficient evidence for standardized extracts; cooked berries as food are considered safe; avoid medicinal doses
