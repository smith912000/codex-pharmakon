# Goat's Milk (Sushruta Samhita)

## *Milk of the she-goat (the domestic goat, Capra hircus) as named in Sushruta Sutra ch.45; the text says "she-goat", and the identification is of high confidence. This entry is kept apart from the bovine "Milk" entry (CPU-00535), whose attestation is Assyrian, Ebers and Avicenna and carries no Sushruta locus* | *(Volume III — Animal)*

**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named by the Sushruta Samhita only, among the texts on disk.** This is a single-text entry. The English text is Bhishagratna's translation, Sutra ch.45, `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt`. The Sushruta Samhita (Bhishagratna's English translation, as on disk) opens its milk group in Sutra ch.45 with the sentence "The milk of a cow, she-goat, she-camel, ewe, she-buffalo, mare, she-elephant, or of a woman, is what generally comes to the use of man" (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch45.txt` line 415). The group paragraph that follows is about milk as a whole, not about one animal: "heavy, sweet, slimy, cold, glossy, emollient, laxative and mild" (line 422), "rejuvenating and aphrodisiac" (line 453), "increases the duration of life, and acts as a vitaliser" (line 457) and, through kindred properties, "augments the quality of bodily albumen (Ojah)" (line 461).

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** The she-goat is the second animal in the list at line 415. Her milk is the first after the cow's to be described (lines 476-488): it "is possessed of properties similar to those of a cow, and is specially beneficial to persons suffering from phthisis. It is light, astringent, appetising (Dipana), and is efficacious in dyspnoea, cough and haemoptysis (Amlapitta — A.T.)". The text then gives its reason: "The milk of a she-goat proves curative in all diseases owing to the smallness of her limbs and her agile habits, as well as for the fact of her drinking comparatively a less quantity of water and living upon bitter and pungent herbs." The translator's footnote (line 510) adds a remark about tuberculosis bacilli and goat's blood attributed to a German physician; it is the translator's nineteenth-century comment, not the classic's. The curd of goat's milk is "light, and subdues the deranged Pittam and Kapham... curative in Vata and wasting diseases, and is a good appetiser" and is helpful in piles, dyspnoea and cough (lines 578-581). The clarified butter is "appetising (Dipanam), eye-invigorating and strength-increasing", a wholesome diet in cough, dyspnoea and consumption, and light (lines 806-810). Her urine is treated at line 1732 and belongs to another entry.

### Active Components

The sources name no constituent. In general terms goat's milk contains water, protein, fat, lactose and minerals, and its fat globules are smaller than those of cow's milk. These are general facts, not read in the source.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Phthisis (the translator's word), dyspnoea, cough and haemoptysis; the milk is "light, astringent, appetising" and, on the text's own reasoning, curative "in all diseases".
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no amount, no duration. The tuberculosis footnote is the translator's and is not a recommendation.

The Codex does not translate the Sanskrit disease terms into modern diagnoses, and a person with a cough or wasting illness should seek proper care. It gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources retrieved give no dose. The milk is described fresh, as curd (line 578) and as clarified butter (line 806). The chapter's general rules on the time of milching and on boiling apply (lines 524-543). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Goat's milk is a common dairy food. Raw milk from any animal can carry brucellosis and other infections. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry; the translator's remark on tuberculosis bacilli is a nineteenth-century opinion and the Codex does not adopt it. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS (ch.45).** Qualities printed: light, astringent, appetising (*dipana*) (lines 476-480). The milk is said to have properties like the cow's (heavy, sweet, cold, lines 422-423) but the goat's own description differs in being light and astringent. No *virya* or *vipaka* is printed for it separately.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed.* Goat's milk is described in the dietetic chapter, with a stated reason for its general usefulness (the animal's small limbs, agile habits, little water and bitter, pungent herbs). It is not named in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and no *ojas* claim is made for it. The group paragraph (lines 419-461) says milk as a whole is rejuvenating, increases the duration of life and augments *ojas* (rendered "bodily albumen (Ojah)" by the translator). Those words are printed for the group and are not repeated under this animal, so this entry does not carry them across: the status is framed, not attested for this milk alone. 

**Practitioner's note.** The text values the goat's milk for wasting and respiratory conditions and gives its reasons in terms of how the animal lives, a sort of discipline of provenance. A cultivator reading it learns where this milk sits in the tradition's diet and nothing about a practice.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene.** Raw goat's milk is a recognised source of brucellosis and other infections.
- **Allergy.** Cow's-milk allergy may cross-react; this is a general statement, not from the source.
- **Wasting illness and cough.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
