# Stalactite root — 殷孽 *Yin Nie* (Shennong bencao jing), "ginger stone" 姜石; Yang 1998 prints "Yin Nie (Stalactitum)"
## *The base or root of a stalactite, calcium carbonate (CaCO3) in the modern view; the classic's own Mingyi layer says 钟乳根也 "it is the root of the stalactite", and Sun Xingyan thinks the entry should be merged with Kong Gong Nie; medium confidence* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`) and, as a second witness attributed to him, Yang's 1998 English translation (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`). The identification is ours.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the middle (中) classic, reads 味辛，温 ("acrid, warm"). It governs 烂伤瘀血 ("rotting wounds and static blood"), 泄利寒热 ("diarrhoea and dysentery, cold and heat"), 鼠寒[gap] (the transcription leaves a gap after the second character; Yang's "mouse fistulas" suggests 鼠瘘, which is our inference [USER: verify]) and 症瘕结气 ("concretions, conglomerations and bound qi"). The alias is 一名姜石 ("ginger stone"), and the habitat 生山谷 ("grows in mountains and valleys"). There is no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement. Sun adds in brackets an editorial remark: 按：此当与孔公孽为一条, "this should be one entry with Kong Gong Nie". (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 殷孽 *yin nie*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1779.)

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰：钟乳根也。生越国，又梁山及南海，采无时: "It is the root of the stalactite (钟乳根). It grows in the land of Yue, and at Mount Liang and in the South Sea; gathered at any time." (Line 1781.) There is no Wu Pu paragraph and no 案 by Sun for this entry.

**The neighbour.** The entry just before it in Sun's middle classic is 孔公孽 *kong gong nie* (味辛，温; for food damage with failure to digest, bound evil qi, malign sores, and "promotes the nine orifices and brings down milk"). Its Mingyi layer says 殷孽根也, "root of Yin Nie", so the two entries define each other as base and root, and Sun reads them as one drug (lines 1769-1773). The spine carries Kong Gong Nie as CPU-02445, still without an entry file, and the stalactite proper (石钟乳) as the existing entry II_126 Stalactite tip.

**Yang 1998 (second witness).** Under "Jades and Stones: Middle Class", PDF page 28 (line 1111), Yang prints: "Yin Nie (Stalactitum) is acrid and warm. It mainly treats frostbite, blood stasis, diarrhea and dysentery, cold and heat, mouse fistulas, and concretion and conglomeration bound qi. Its other name is Jiang Shi (Ginger Stone). It is produced in mountains and valleys." Yang's "frostbite" stands where Sun's reading is 烂伤 ("rotting wounds"); the two witnesses differ, and the Chinese of Yang's source is not on disk. Yang's footnote 53 (lines 1121-1125), on the stalactite entry that precedes this one, repeats Li Shizhen's picture of the stalactite as a breast: "The nipple is Shi Zhong [Ru], the body is Kong Gong Nie, and the root is Yin Nie." That is a Ming explanation and not the Classic's.

**Identity.** The name 殷孽 is taken by the Mingyi layer, and by Li Shizhen as Yang reports him, as the root of the stalactite. The mineral is a calcite formation. The classic gives no description. The 南海 habitat in the Mingyi layer is a locality and not a feature of the stone. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Stalactites and their bases are cave formations of calcium carbonate, CaCO3, mostly calcite, deposited from dripping water; trace magnesium, iron and other elements may colour or flaw them. The alias "ginger stone" (姜石) is recorded, but the sources read do not describe the stone, so nothing is built on it. The sources read give no composition.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Classic records a warm, acrid stone for rotting wounds, bloody or stagnant conditions, diarrhoea and fever with chill, fistulas and lumps. The Codex does not endorse these claims and offers no protocol. Cave formations take thousands of years to form and cannot regrow on a human timescale, which is an argument against any use of them.

### Preparation & Dosing

No preparation, route, quantity or course is printed. The only handling is the Mingyi layer's "gathered at any time" (采无时). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Calcium carbonate is an antacid and a dietary calcium source in general; it is the main ingredient of chalk, limestone and shell. A ground stalactite root would be calcium carbonate with trace contaminants, and no property specific to it is established. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 殷孽 in the middle grade (中品); its preface (Sun ed. line 1677) says middle drugs "mainly nourish the nature to correspond with humanity", that some are toxic and some not, and that one should weigh them before use, taking them to check disease and make up for emaciation. The monograph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise; it lists wounds, flux, fistulas and lumps. The entry's own link to the stalactite entries places it in a family of stones but does not give it a status. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, middle classic, entry 殷孽 *yin nie*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1779.)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A middle-grade stone-drug for wounds and fluxes, with no promise of long use. The Classic's editor himself thought it belonged with Kong Gong Nie.

### Contraindications

- **Wounds, dysentery, fistulas and lumps.** Need medical assessment.
- **Quality.** Powdered stone sold as a remedy may be a different mineral or carry contaminants; mineral medicines are unstandardised.
- **Conservation and law.** Cave formations are protected in many countries and collecting them is restricted or prohibited. [USER: verify the legal status in any given place]
- **Calcium.** A large dose of calcium carbonate can cause constipation and, over time, excess calcium.
