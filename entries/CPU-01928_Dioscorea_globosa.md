# Dioscorea globosa — Pindalu (Pinda-alu) and Varahikanda, Sushruta Samhita Appendix 3

## *Dioscorea globosa Roxb., a twining yam, as the translator's Latin gloss to two Sanskrit names in Appendix 3, read in the OCR as "Pindailu" (Pindalu) and "Va'ra'hikanda" (Varahikanda); the Latin is Bhishagratna's, the pairings are ours, and the identification is of low to medium confidence because the species is often treated today as a form of Dioscorea alata (accepted status not checked)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named in the *Sushruta Samhita* (SS) only. This is a single-text entry, and the Appendix prints the Latin twice.** Appendix No. 3 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`) has "Dioscorea globosa." at line 32576 and again as "Dioscorea Globosa." at line 33254. The extraction file `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` lists both as unpaired Latin lines ("OCR column split"). Both pairings below are ours, by counting entries in the two columns from unambiguous anchors, and must be checked against the page scan. [USER: verify]

**First occurrence, Pindalu.** On the P page the Sanskrit name "Pindailu" stands at line 32515. Counting from the other end of the column, where "Nelumbium speciosum" falls on Pundarika, "Boerhavia diffusa" on Punarnava, "Areca Catechu" on Puga, "Aglaia Roxburghiana" on Priyangu and "Flacourtia cataphracta" on Prachinamalaka, the run continues "Ficus Infectoria" on Plaksha, "Buchanania Latifolia" on Piyala, "Pterocarpus Marsupium" on Pita-sala, "Piper longum" on Pippali, "Randia Dumeterum" on Pinditaka, "Phoenix Dactylifera" on Pinda-kharjura and so "Dioscorea globosa" (line 32576) on Pindalu. The main text knows the name: in the chapter on tubers (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 1931 and 1976) the "Pindaluka" stands in the list "Pindaluka, Madhvaluka, Hastyaluka, Kasthaluka, Shankhaluka, Raktaluka, Indivara and Utpala etc.", which are said to "alleviate haemoptysis, are cooling in their potency, sweet in their taste and heavy of digestion", and later "The Pindalukam generates the Kapham, is heavy of digestion, and tends to enrage or agitate the bodily Vayu."

**Second occurrence, Varahikanda.** On the V page the Sanskrit name "Va'ra'hikanda" stands at line 33228, followed by "Va'rta'ku" (line 33229). Counting from Vajri (Euphorbia Antiquorum, line 33244), Vakula (Mimusops Elengi), Vamsa (Bamboo) and then, below the Dioscorea line, Varta ku to "Bringal." and Varuna to "Crataeva Nurvala", the Latin "Dioscorea Globosa." (line 33254) falls on Varahikanda, and "Bringal." on Varta ku. The main text on disk has no line for Varahikanda (searched in the chapter files). The Latin line just before it, "Randia Dumetorum.", falls on the preceding name Varahi. [USER: verify]

**What this means.** The glossary gives *Dioscorea globosa* as the translator's identification of two names, one of them a name the main text uses for a tuber (Pindaluka, with the properties read above) and one of them found only in the appendix. Whether Bhishagratna meant the same plant by both is not stated. The two Sanskrit names are therefore carried in this single entry as the glossary's two names for one Latin species.

**No other text.** A search of the other source texts on disk for *Dioscorea globosa* found nothing.

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Dioscorea* is a genus of climbing yams whose starchy tubers are staple foods in warm regions and which are generally cooked before eating. Current botanical treatments commonly regard *D. globosa* as a form of *D. alata*; this entry may therefore overlap with the entry for *Dioscorea alata* (CPU-01927). ### Cultivation Register

**Sushruta Samhita (SS).** *(b) Framed.* The Pindaluka is described in the Sutrasthana as a food bulb in the dravya-guna list of tubers and the Varahikanda is a glossary name only. No chapter of the Chikitsa on rasayana or vajikarana (chapters 26 to 31 of the Chikitsasthana on disk) contains this name in a search of `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/chikitsa/`, so no cultivation status is attested for it. The register is therefore (b): framed by the food-bulb chapter, with no cultivation status attested.
