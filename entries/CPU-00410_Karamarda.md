**Ayurvedic — Sushruta Samhita (SS).** Karamarda is found in five passages, all read in this session. In each the name is a list item. None of them describes the plant's appearance.

- **Sutra ch.38, lines 518 to 522** (chapter on the classification of drugs by their therapeutic properties, per the chapter opening at line 3). "The Pancha-kantakam Group: Similarly, the group consisting of the five medicinal (thorny) shrubs known as Karamradda, Trikantaka, Sairiyaka, Shatavari, and Gridhranakhi, is called the Pancha-Kantaka." The next lines (524 to 529) say of this group and the Valli-Panchamulam group before it: "The two preceding groups prove curative in Haemoptysis and in all the three types of anasarca or oedema (Shopha)", and add that the group arrests "all sorts of urethral discharges" and is "a potent remedy in all cases of seminal disorders". At lines 541 to 553 the chapter sums up the five root-groups: those "standing third and fourth in order of enumeration (the Valli and Kantaka Panchamulas) subdue the deranged Kapham". This is membership of a group named for its thorny shrubs, and the claims are made for the group.
- **Sutra ch.42, lines 302 to 304** (chapter on the specific properties of flavours). "Acid Groups: The fruits known as Dadima, Araalaka [sic, OCR of Amalaka], Matulanga, Amrutaka, Kapittha, Karamarda, Vadra, Kola, Prachina-Amalaka, Tintidhi, Koshamra, Bhavya, Paravata, Vetraphala, Lakucha, Amla-Vetash, Dantashatha and curd, whey, Sura, Shukta, Sauvira, Tushodaka and Dhanyamla, etc. are generally included within the acid group." So here Karamarda is a fruit, placed with the acid flavour.
- **Sutra ch.46, lines 986 to 991 and 1082 to 1084** (chapter on food and drink). Karamarda stands in the "Fruit Group (Phala-varga)" list. Of the fruits as a class the text says (lines 995 to 997) "they are acid in taste and heavy of digestion, heat-making in their potencies, (expectorant) of (Kapham), generate Pittam and subdue the Vayu." Then, fruit by fruit, at lines 1082 to 1084: "The Karamardakam is relishing and acid in its taste, allays thirst, and generates the Kapham." This is the only sentence found that is about Karamarda alone. The chapter's opening speaks of food as the source of the *Ojas* principle in the body, and that statement is general. It is not tied to this fruit.
- **Uttara ch.47, lines 270 to 286** (chapter on alcoholism and kindred maladies, heading at line 270, "Treatment of Pana-vibhrama" in the translator's printing). One of the drinks listed for the distempers from drinking excess is made of "the tender sprouts of Kshiri-trees, Visa (Mrindla), Jiraka, ... Padmaka, ... Bhavy?i, Karamarda, Kapittha, Kola, Vrikshdmlt, ... Jiraka, Dddima, Yashti-madhu and Utpala, all pounded together" (OCR spellings as printed; the ellipses are mine; Karamarda is at line 284) and mixed with a cold infusion of spices. Whether "tender sprouts" governs the whole list is not clear from the English, and this is read as printed.

*Uses of the Pancha-kantaka group found elsewhere.* Chikitsa ch.17 (the Kantaka-Pancha-mula among the groups whose drugs are used for plasters, affusions, medicated ghee and oils in erysipelas, `SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt`, lines 44 to 48), Chikitsa ch.37 (line 200 and the translator's footnote at lines 213 to 214, which reads "Kantaka" as the Kantaka-Pancha-mula) and Uttara ch.48 (lines 143 to 145: water boiled with Vilva, Adhaki, "the drugs of the Kantaka-Pancha-mula" and Darbha "allays a case of Kaphaja thirst") use the group by name. None of these passages names Karamarda, so they reach it only through the ch.38 list. The Uttara ch.48 footnote (lines 163 to 165) also records a commentator's variant reading in which "Kantaka" is Gokshura, so even the group's identity in that line is not settled by the text. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. In Sushruta the name occurs as an acid fruit with a stated effect on thirst and Kapha (Sutra ch.46), as an item in a drink for drinking-excess (Uttara ch.47), and as a member of the thorny-shrub group (Sutra ch.38). These are the text's own claims. The Codex gives no protocol for this plant, and the identification with a named species is unconfirmed (see above).

### Preparation & Dosing

Uttara ch.47 (lines 270 to 286) gives the plant among drugs "pounded together" and mixed with a cold infusion of spices, strained through linen and perfumed. Sutra ch.46 treats the fruit as food. The Pancha-kantaka group is used as a group in decoctions, plasters and oils in the passages above, and the text of those passages does not say which members are meant. No quantity is given for Karamarda in any line read. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** Sutra ch.46 (lines 1082 to 1084) prints for the Karamardakam the taste "acid" and that it is "relishing", "allays thirst" and "generates the Kapham". The same chapter prints, for fruits as a class, "heat-making in their potencies" (potency, *virya*), and this is a general statement that the text does not repeat for this fruit by name. Ch.42 places the fruit in the acid group. No *vipaka* is printed for it in the lines read.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Karamarda is attested in Sushruta as an acid fruit (Sutra ch.42 and ch.46), as an ingredient in a drink for the effects of drinking excess (Uttara ch.47), and, under the spelling "Karamradda", as one of five thorny shrubs that make up the Pancha-kantaka group (Sutra ch.38). The Sushruta frame for it is therapeutic and dietetic. Membership of a ch.38 group is a grouping by action on the humours and is not a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* status. The name was not found in any Chikitsa chapter, and nothing read ties it to *ojas*, *rasayana* or *vajikarana*. The identification with *Carissa carandas* rests on a list row. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the sources read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a sour fruit and a group-member in a classical Sushruta list, and the abstentive answer is the right one. Use in any preparation is a matter for a qualified practitioner, and the texts give neither a regimen nor a quantity.

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. The texts name Karamarda as a fruit that "generates the Kapham", which is a Sushruta caution about the humours and not a medical one. The botanical identity is uncertain, so no fruit or shrub should be gathered or eaten on the strength of this entry. None of the traditional uses above is advice. Seek a qualified practitioner for any urinary, seminal, bleeding or swelling complaint, and seek ordinary medical care for these. Do not use in pregnancy.
