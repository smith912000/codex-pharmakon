# Deutzia — 溲疏 *Soushu* (Shennong bencao jing), with 巨骨 (Mingyi layer)
## *Deutzia scabra Thunb., a deciduous shrub of the hydrangea family, the identification we give to 溲疏; of high confidence by modern convention, but the one description in Sun Xingyan's note (a red winter fruit like wolfberry) does not clearly fit, so the identity of the classic's plant is uncertain* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`). The identification is ours and is the modern one. This is a short monograph and the Codex writes only what it says.

**The Shennong text proper.** The 味 paragraph, in the lower (下) classic, reads 味辛，寒 ("acrid, cold"). It governs 身皮肤中热 ("heat within the body and the skin"), "removes perverse qi" (除邪气) and "stops involuntary urination" (止遗溺), and adds **可作浴汤**, "it can be made into a bath-decoction". The habitat is 生山谷及田野、故邱虚地 ("mountain valleys, fields, and old mounds and ruined ground"). There is no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 溲疏 *soushu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3731)

**Mingyi bielu layer (later).** 《名医》曰 adds the alias 巨骨, the origin 生熊耳山 ("Mount Xiong'er") and the gathering month: 四月采, the fourth month. No part is named.

**Sun's note (案).** The only note quotes Li Dangzhi (3rd century): the plant is also called 杨栌, 牡荆 and 空疏; "the bark is white, hollow in the middle, with joints from time to time; the fruit is like wolfberry (枸杞); ripe in winter, red, tasting sweet and bitter". Sun adds nothing of his own.

**Identity.** The Shennong paragraph gives no description. Li Dangzhi's description of a red, wolfberry-like winter fruit does not obviously match a *Deutzia*, whose fruit in general botany is a dry capsule, and the alias 牡荆 is also the name of a different shrub; the Codex therefore says that Li's plant may not be the modern *Deutzia scabra*. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The classic records an acrid, cold drug "for heat within the skin" and for involuntary urination, which can be made into a bath-decoction. The Codex gives no protocol; the text's "heat in the skin" is not a modern diagnosis, and involuntary urination calls for medical assessment.

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* The classic files 溲疏 in the lower grade (下品); its preface says lower-grade drugs treat disease, are mostly toxic, and are not for long use. The paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 溲疏 *soushu*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3731)

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. A lower-grade drug for skin heat and involuntary urination, to be used (by the text's own words) as a bath-decoction. SHORT entry: the sources give little.
