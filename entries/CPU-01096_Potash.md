# Potash (yavakshara) — Yavakshara / Yava-kshara, printed "Javakshara" in one OCR passage (Bhishagratna) / ya.ba.ksha.ra, Yabak khyara (Clark; Men-Tsee-Khang)
## *Alkali from the ash of burnt barley or plant matter, which Bhishagratna glosses "carbonate of potash" (and once "nitrate of potash"); Clark glosses the Tibetan "Potash: Aragonite"* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS, FMT

---

### Origin & Tradition

Potash enters the Codex as an alkali that two texts name and give uses for. The translators do not agree among themselves about what it chemically was, and the entry keeps their glosses as printed.

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *Yavakshara* is the first alkali named in the "group of Alkalis" in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt` lines 2086-2105; the OCR prints it "Javakshara"). Bhishagratna's English reads: "The different varieties of alkali (Kshara) such as the Javakshara (Carbonate of potash), the Svarjikakshara, the Pakima and the Tankana cure abdominal glands, piles, mesenteric diarrhoea, and gravel and stone in the bladder. All these varieties of alkali should be regarded as possessed of digestant or stomachic properties. Their abuse is known to have brought about cases of haemoptysis. Of these, the varieties known as the Javakshara and Svarjikakshara are inflammatory as fire, and they tend to reduce Kapham, remove Vibondha (suppression of stool etc.), piles, and abdominal glands and prove curative in cases of enlarged spleen. They are anti-spermatopoietic." The other alkalis in the group are described separately: *Usharakshara* "is heat-making in its potency, and subdues the deranged Vayu ... and is detrimental to the strength of the body"; *Pakima* reduces obesity and is a bladder-cleanser; *Tankanakshara* (borax) dries the organism, "generates the Vayu, subdues the Kapham", and deranges the *Pittam*. Elsewhere in the *Sutrasthana* the translator lists it with the salts: ch.42 (line 320), the "Saline Group: ... Saindhaba, Sauvarchala, Vida, Fakya, Romaka, Samudraka, Paktrima, Yavakshara (nitrate of potash), Ushara and Suvarchika", where the gloss is *nitrate*, not carbonate; in *Sharirasthana* ch.10 (line 372) the same word is glossed "carbonate of potash". The translator's chemical gloss is therefore not constant, and which substance the Sanskrit word meant is not settled by the English text. [USER: verify]

The compounds in which Bhishagratna's *Yavakshara* is an ingredient, as printed:
- *Purgatives* (ch.44): a compound of equal parts of powdered *Trivrit*, *Shyama*, *Yavakshara*, *Shunti* and *Pippali*, taken with honey, "acts as one of the most effective purgatives in diseases due to the action of the deranged Kapham" (line 160); three parts *Trivrit* with one each of *Triphala*, *Yavakshara*, *Pippali* and *Vidanga* as an electuary "proves curative in cases of enlarged spleen, in Gulmas due to the action of the deranged Kapham and Vayu, in Halimaka (chlorosis), as well as in cases of abdominal dropsy" and is "one of the most harmless purgative compounds (of our pharmacopoeia)" (lines 205-215); *Triphala* pulp with *Vidanga*, *Pippali* and *Yavakshara* licked with honey and ghee, or made into a confection with treacle, "does not entail any strict regimen of diet and conduct" (lines 578-596).
- *With buttermilk* (ch.45): in the passage on *Takram* (buttermilk), in a case of deranged *Kapham* it "should be mixed with Yavakshara and the powders of the drugs known as Vyosha" (line 722).
- *Wound and lint* (ch.36, line 99): *Yavakshara* is one of the ingredients of the plaster on the lint for purifying an ulcer ("Shodhana Varti"), with Trikatu, the five salts and others.
- *Chikitsasthana:* *Hingvadi-Vati* with salts and *Svarjikakshara* (ch.5, line 680); plasters of *Yavakshara* and cow's urine for a fistula (ch.8, line 158); *Kshara (Yava-kshara)*, Trikatu, cow's urine and oil as an enema for *Kaphaja Udara* (ch.14, line 82); "an abundant quantity of Yava-kshara" with honey and ghee on a wound (ch.18, line 182); a commentator's note that a swelling be "first plastered with Yava-kshara, oyster-shells and Saurashtri" before leeches (ch.20, line 41, footnote).
- *Sharirasthana* ch.10 (line 372): a "potion of carbonate of potash (Yavakshara) dissolved in tepid water or in clarified butter" for a newly delivered woman with the symptoms of *Makkalla* (obstructed lochia with nodules and piercing pain).
- *Uttaratantra* ch.42 (line 208): *Yava-kshara* is one of the six drugs of the *kalka* in a ghee called *Dadhika-Ghrita*.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** Clark 1995, Explanatory Tantra ch.20, "(3) The Exposition of the Power of Earth Medicines" (`Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt` line 861): "ya.ba.ksha.ra: Potash: Aragonite: [The hDod.hjor commentary asserts that when calcinated blue barley is left overnight a saline formation, (potash) appears on the surface. An alternative translation is that ya.ba means barley and ksha.ra means transform. The Zla.ba text states that it is the ash of burnt barley, and, with regard to its power, the Zla.zer text states, 'The burnt barley salt, ya.ba.ka.ra, cures tumors, heart disease and first stage oedema. These days it forms like white ash at the base of boulders and makes a crunching sound when squeezed in the hands. Thus there is little chance of confusing it with other salts.'] It increases heat and purges tumors." Clark's "Aragonite" is a calcium carbonate mineral, a different material from potash; the double gloss is Clark's. The Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 text lists "Yabak khyara" in the salty-taste group with the salts (`MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt` line 3211) and says "Yabak khyara generates heat and eliminates tumors" (line 3373). `_sources/rgyud-bzhi.csv` line 163 carries it as "Yabak khyara".

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent beyond the translators' glosses. In general terms, potash is the traditional name for potassium salts obtained by leaching wood or plant ash: potassium carbonate (K₂CO₃) is the main alkaline component, which gives a strongly alkaline, caustic solution. Potassium nitrate (saltpetre, KNO₃) is a different compound, an oxidising salt. If the Sanskrit and Tibetan names mean burnt-barley ash, as the Tibetan commentary says, the material would be a mixed plant ash with potassium carbonate and other salts. Aragonite is calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) and is not potash. Whether the sources mean one or several of these cannot be told from the English texts.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sources record an alkali used as a purgative component, a wound and ulcer application, and a remedy for tumours, piles, and a swollen spleen. They also warn that alkalis' abuse brings on haemoptysis and that this one is anti-spermatopoietic. Caustic alkali is corrosive; potassium salts taken internally can be dangerous. The Codex gives no protocol.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparations the texts give are those listed under Origin: the Sushruta compounds of *Yavakshara* with *Trivrit*, *Triphala*, *Pippali*, *Vidanga* and other drugs, taken with honey, ghee or treacle; the potion in tepid water or ghee (Sharira ch.10, line 372); and the plasters and enemas with cow's urine and oil. For the Tibetan material, the commentary the translator cites says that the burnt barley salt forms "like white ash at the base of boulders" (Clark line 861), which describes how it is found and not how it is prepared. In the passages read the proportions are given as parts of the whole ("equal parts"; "three parts ... one ... one"), and no weight of *Yavakshara* alone. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. ### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Potassium carbonate is a strong base; in concentrated form it is a caustic that burns skin and mucous membranes, and swallowed in quantity it can cause oesophageal and gastric injury and raise serum potassium to dangerous levels, particularly in kidney disease. Potassium salts have an osmotic laxative effect when taken in quantity. Potassium nitrate has historically been used as a diuretic and in toothpaste for sensitive teeth. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** Sutra ch.46 (lines 2086-2105) prints for *Javakshara* and *Svarjikakshara* that they are "inflammatory as fire" and tend to reduce *Kapham*; they belong to a group all of whose members are "digestant or stomachic" and whose abuse produces haemoptysis. The text gives no *rasa* or *vipaka* for them in the passage read, and the "saline group" of ch.42 (line 320) gives none. The heat-making quality is that of the printed English; the Sanskrit term was not checked.

**Tibetan (FMT):** Clark line 861: "It increases heat and purges tumors." Men-Tsee-Khang: salty-taste group (line 3211); "generates heat and eliminates tumors" (line 3373). The commentary quoted by Clark adds "cures tumors, heart disease and first stage oedema".

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, with an abstentive caution.* *Yavakshara* is named in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 inside the alkali group, whose English lists it as "inflammatory as fire" and "anti-spermatopoietic" (lines 2086-2105), and as a purgative and wound ingredient in ch.36, 44 and 45 and in the *Chikitsasthana* (see Origin). It is not placed in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter in any passage read, and the one statement that touches generative vitality is negative ("anti-spermatopoietic"). Nothing is said of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana*; the sattvic and rajasic food lists are *Samkhya*-era material, not Sushruta, and are not used. The shodhana (purification) of alkalis, if any, is not described in the passages read.

**Tibetan / rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed.* Potash is named in the Explanatory Tantra's materia medica of earth medicines (Clark 1995, line 861) and in the salty-taste group (Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, line 3211), as a heat-increasing substance that purges tumours. No cultivation status is attributed: the Tibetan frame is that pathology is at root a spiritual condition and that medicine is one of four treatment branches, supporting practice but not reaching the root, and nothing read ties potash to that. No *bcud len* use was found. No formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists in this tradition, and none is asserted.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here makes potash a substance for cultivation. Both texts treat it as a corrective for a defined complaint (tumour, piles, spleen, bowel), Sushruta's English says its abuse brings on haemoptysis, and a caustic alkali has no place in any long-term practice. A cultivator takes nothing from this entry. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Caustic and toxic:** concentrated potash solutions burn skin, eyes and mucous membranes, and ingestion can injure the gullet and stomach; the sources themselves warn of haemoptysis from abuse (SS ch.46).
- **Kidney disease, heart disease, potassium-sparing drugs:** potassium salts can raise blood potassium to dangerous levels and disturb cardiac rhythm.
- **Anti-spermatopoietic (SS ch.46):** the English text says so; people for whom that matters should note it.
- **Pregnancy and childbirth:** the Sushruta potion for a woman after delivery is a historical record and not a recommendation; no evidence of safety was read.
- **Identity:** the sources do not settle whether the substance is potassium carbonate, nitrate or burnt-barley ash; nothing sold under the name can be assumed to match.
