# Chian earth — Chia (Dioscorides 5-174), also "terra chia" and "Chian earth" in other chapters

## *A white, nearly ash-coloured, crusty earth from Chios, said to have the same properties as Samian earth, smoothing the face and body, and used in a bath in place of saltpetre as a scour. The translator's heading gloss is "Chian Earth — ? Bicarbonate of Soda", with a query, and a general note that "earth" can also mean metallic oxides. Identification: an unidentified white earth from Chios; the translator's guess is queried and no text on disk settles it; confidence low* | *(Volume II — Mineral)*

**Tradition Tags:** DMM

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Chian earth is named only by Dioscorides among the texts on disk. The locus is *De Materia Medica* 5-174, *Chia* (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, heading line 10735, translator's gloss line 10736, text line 10738, book page 827 of this translation; name-index row `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/extract/names.csv`; list row `_sources/dioscorides.csv` line 899, "Chia", mineral). The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Chian earth", and this entry replaces it.

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** The translator's gloss reads "SUGGESTED: Chian Earth — ? Bicarbonate of Soda" and adds "Earth also means metallic oxides i.e. Magnesia, Alumina, Zirconia." The chapter reads: "Chian earth which is white and almost an ashy colour like the Samian [above] should be used. It is crusty and white and differs in the shape of its particles. It has the same properties as Samian earth. It makes the face and the whole body without wrinkles and clear, and used in a bath instead of saltpetre [potassium nitrate] it scours." The chapter gives a rule of choice (white and nearly ash-coloured, like the Samian), a description (crusty, white, with particles that differ in shape), a comparison ("the same properties as Samian earth"), and two uses: it makes the face and whole body "without wrinkles and clear", and in a bath it scours in place of saltpetre. The bracketed "[potassium nitrate]" and "[above]" are the translator's. It gives no internal use, no dose and no method of preparing it.

**What "the same properties as Samian earth" refers to.** The Samian chapter, 5-172 (lines 10722 to 10731), reads: "Samian earth is preferred which is very white and light, and on touching the tongue holds it fast like glue. Additionally it is full of liquid, soft and brittle, such as that which some call collyrium [eye salve, suppository]. There are two kinds; besides that previously mentioned there is one called aster, crusty and thick like whetstone. It has the same properties and is burnt and broken like eretrias [5-171]. It stops the spitting up of blood, and is given with flowers of wild pomegranate for women's menstrual flows. Smeared on with water it soothes inflammation from stones [urinary, kidney], and inflammation of the breasts. Taken in a drink of water it represses sweating; it helps those bitten by venomous creatures, and those who have taken a deadly medicine [antidote]." Whether Dioscorides means all of these uses to carry over to Chian earth, or only those of the Samian earth that are named in the same breath, is not said. The properties are those of 5-172 and are not asserted for Chian earth in this entry beyond the words of 5-174 itself. [USER: verify] The general remark of 5-170, *Ge* ("Earth"), is that "All earth that falls within the scope of medicinal use is supremely useful for cooling, and for closing the pores" (line 10711); it is about earths in general and is not an attribution to this one.

**Other mentions in the text.** Two plant chapters name Chian earth as an ingredient. 3-48, *Melilotos* (melilot), says that, used alone in water, melilot cures new melicerides "as well as scaly eruptions on the scalp, rubbed on with Chian [from Scios in the Aegean sea] earth and wine or galls [oak galls]" (line 6946). 4-184, *Ampelos Leuke* (white vine), says the root "cleans the skin and smooths it [wrinkles], and with ervum [2-129, 2-131], terra chia [earth from Chios], and fenugreek it takes off sunburn, varicose veins, freckles, and black scars" (line 9684). Both are uses of the plant, in which Chian earth is an additive, and neither is a statement about the earth. They agree with the cosmetic use in 5-174. The translator's gazetteer (line 3465) gives Chios (Scios) as "a Greek island in the Aegean Sea near the central west coast of Asia Minor". A search of the other texts on disk found nothing further for this earth.

### Active Components

The chapter names no constituent. The translator's guess, "? Bicarbonate of Soda", carries a query, and the same note says that "earth" can also mean metallic oxides. The text says only that the earth is white, crusty and nearly ash-coloured, and that it works as a bath scour in place of saltpetre. No composition is asserted for it. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Making the face and whole body clear and without wrinkles; scouring in a bath instead of saltpetre; and the properties of Samian earth.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no internal use of its own, no method of preparation.

The Codex gives no protocol. A white earth of unknown composition is not recommended on the skin, in the bath or by mouth, and the "wrinkle-free" claim is a cosmetic claim of the ancient world and not a recommended practice.

### Preparation & Dosing

The chapter gives no preparation. The only handling note is that it is "used in a bath instead of saltpetre". For the Samian earth that it is compared with, 5-172 says the Samian "is burnt and broken like eretrias [5-171]"; that instruction belongs to the Samian chapter and is not stated for Chian earth. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. White earths of the Aegean region include clays, chalk and carbonates, and some are alkaline; alkaline salts such as sodium carbonate are scouring agents in general. Whether Chian earth was any of these is not settled by the text. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

No quality is asserted for Chian earth beyond the words of 5-174.
- No other tradition's classification applies, because no other tradition is tagged.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek / Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system and none is given here. What the chapter gives is a discipline of attention: how to recognise the earth (white, nearly ash-coloured, crusty, with particles of a different shape from the Samian) and what it may be compared with. No cultivation status is attested for this earth. Its cosmetic use is a use of the body's surface and carries no spiritual claim in the text.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing for a cultivator to take. The chapter is useful only as a note on how a Greek physician told one white earth from another by sight and by comparison, and the earth itself has no place in practice.

### Contraindications

- **Do not eat it, bathe in it or apply it to the skin.** Its composition is unknown, and the chapter's cosmetic claim cannot be made safe on that basis.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. Avoid. [USER: verify]
