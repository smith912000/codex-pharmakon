**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *De Materia Medica* 1-136, "ITEA" (`Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`, text line 4528; gloss at line 4529: "Primum salicis genus, Alterum salicis genus [Fuchs], Salix purpurea, Salix vitellina, Salix repens [Linnaeus] — Creeping Willow [Mabberley] [other usage] Itea ilicifolia, Itea virginica, Itea riparia"). The whole chapter: "Itea is a tree known to all whose fruit, leaves, bark and juice are astringent. The leaves pounded into small pieces and taken in a drink with a little pepper and wine help those troubled with iliaca passio [painful intestinal obstruction]. Taken by themselves with water they cause inconception [birth control]. The fruit (taken in a drink) is good for those who spit blood, and the bark does the same. Burnt and steeped in vinegar it takes away calluses and corns, rubbed on them. The juice from the leaves and bark warmed with rosaceum [1-53] in a cup of malum punicum [pomegranate] helps sores in the ears, and a decoction of them is an excellent warm pack for gout. It also cleans away scurf [eczema]. A juice is taken from it at the time of its flowering, the bark being cut, for it is found coalesced within. It has the ability to clean away things that darken the pupils." The chapter gives no other names. The translator's introduction (line 363) says of the willow "itea, probably salix species", quoting the "excellent fomentation for ye gout", and does not name a species. ### Active Components

Willow bark is generally known to contain salicin and related phenolic glycosides, which the body converts to salicylic acid. This is a general statement about the genus, and the content of *S. caprea* in particular was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides' uses (a pepper-and-wine drink of leaves for intestinal obstruction, fruit and bark for spitting blood, burnt bark for corns, juice for the ears and eyes, a decoction as a pack for gout) are recorded as his, and the Codex does not recommend them. The remark that the leaves "cause inconception" is recorded as his and is not a method. The Codex gives no protocol for willow.

### Preparation & Dosing

Dioscorides gives leaves "pounded into small pieces and taken in a drink with a little pepper and wine", fruit and bark "taken in a drink", bark burnt and steeped in vinegar, and a juice warmed with rosaceum [1-53] (1-136, line 4528). No quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

Salicin from willow bark is the compound that led to salicylic acid and aspirin, and this is well established as a general matter of chemical history. No clinical studies of *S. caprea* were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

No safety data were read. Willow bark preparations are generally avoided by people who react to aspirin or salicylates, and are not suitable for children with fever. The leaf use that Dioscorides says causes "inconception" is not a safe method, and none of the traditional uses should be taken as advice. Seek ordinary medical care for gout, bleeding, intestinal obstruction or ear and eye complaints.
