# Chinese gooseberry — 羊桃 *yangtao*, "Yang Tao" ("goat peach"); also 苌楚 *changchu*, 鬼桃 *guitao*

## *A trailing, peach-leaved plant of the Shennong lower classic, taken in modern works to be Actinidia chinensis Planch. (the kiwifruit vine, formerly "Chinese gooseberry"); the identification is ours, of medium confidence, and is contested: Yang's English translation glosses the same entry as Averrhoa carambola (star fruit) and itself calls the identification provisional* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Yang tao* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The spine had no row for it before this one (CPU-02574). The spine's existing row for *Averrhoa carambola* (CPU-00614) is a different record (the job notes for this entry say it rests on a Guangxi name, 五敛子, which was not checked here).

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 羊桃 in the lower classic (卷三 下经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3461, 味 paragraph; name line 3459). The paragraph reads: 味苦，寒。主[ ]热，身暴赤色，风水积聚，恶疡，除小儿热。一名鬼桃，一名羊肠。生川谷。In English: "Flavour bitter, cold. Governs [one glyph lost] heat; the body suddenly turning red (身暴赤色); wind-water (风水) accumulations and gatherings; malign sores (恶疡); it removes heat in children. Another name is Gui Tao ('ghost peach'), another Yang Chang ('goat intestine'). It grows in rivers and valleys." There is no 久服 clause and no toxicity statement.

*Wu Pu layer.* None.

**Mingyi bielu layer** (《名医》, later additions; line 3463). Aliases 苌楚 (*changchu*), 御弋 (*yuyi*), 铫弋 (*yaoyi*); "grows in mountain forests and in fields; gathered in the second month and dried in the shade" (生山林及田野，二月采，阴干). No part is named.

**Sun Xingyan's note** (案, line 3465). The only descriptions of the plant in the sources read. The *Shuowen*: 苌楚 and 铫弋 are 羊桃. The *Guangya*: 鬼桃 and 铫弋 are 羊桃. The *Shanhai jing* ("Central Mountains"): "on Feng Mountain there is much 羊桃, in form like the peach but square; the stem can be made into [a frame for stretching] hides; the sense of 皮张 is uncertain" (状如桃而方，茎可以为皮张). The *Erya*: 长楚, 姚[ ]; Guo Pu: "today's 羊桃, also called 鬼桃; the leaf is like the peach; the flower white; the fruit like a small grain of wheat, also like a peach" (叶似桃；花白；子如小麦，亦似桃). The *Mao shi*: "in the low wet ground there is the 苌楚", with the commentary that it is 铫弋. Lu Ji: "today's 羊桃; the leaf long and narrow, the flower purple-red; the branches and stems weak, over a *chi* (a foot) long, trailing and climbing over the grasses. People today use it for drawing and irrigating water; it is heavy and sinks readily, not as good as willow. Near the root, cut the bark with a knife, put it in hot ashes, and it comes off, and can be used to sheath a brush-pen shaft" (叶长而狭，花紫赤色，其枝、茎弱，过一尺，引蔓于草上...). Guo Pu and Lu Ji disagree on the flower, white against purple-red, and Sun does not comment.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 3642) prints "Yang Tao (Fructus Averrhoae Carambolae) is bitter and cold. It mainly treats fulminant fever, sudden generalized reddening, wind water accumulations and gatherings, and malign sores. It eliminates fever in children. Its other name is Gui Tao (Ghost Peach). Another name is Yang Chang (Goat Intestine). It grows in rivers and valleys." His footnote 226 (line 3664) says: "Because this medicinal is never prescribed in modern times, its definite identity is not certain. Therefore, its Latin pharmacological identification should only be regarded as provisional. Other possibilities have been suggested." The text agrees with Sun's paragraph (where Sun's file has a lost glyph before "heat", Yang prints "fulminant fever").

**Identity.** The Codex's name, *Actinidia chinensis*, rests on the later standard reading. It fits the old descriptions in part: a trailing, weak-stemmed climber with peach-like leaves (Guo Pu, Lu Ji) agrees better with a vine than with the small tree that bears the star fruit. The sources on disk give no way to choose between the two, and the flower colour is disputed within the commentaries. The Codex keeps the proposed name and records the conflict with Yang.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, and not a recommended practice. The classic records a bitter, cold plant for fevers, sudden redness of the body, wind-water accumulations, malign sores, and fever in children. The Codex gives no protocol and does not turn these terms into modern diagnoses. The plant's identity is contested and the part used is not named. Fever in a child, spreading redness of the skin and malign sores need prompt medical assessment.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparation-related statement is the Mingyi layer's: gathered in the second month and dried in the shade (line 3463). No part, form, quantity, frequency or duration is given. Lu Ji's remarks on the bark and stems (line 3465) are about craft use and not medicine. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the lower classic (下经), which the preface describes as "to treat disease, in correspondence with earth; mostly toxic, not to be taken long; for those who wish to remove cold-and-heat evil qi, break accumulations and gatherings, and cure illness" (下药...主治病以应地。多毒，不可久服。欲除寒热邪气，破积聚，愈疾者，本下经; line 2935). Its 味 paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, lower classic, entry 羊桃 *yangtao*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 3461). Its status is therapeutic only. The Mingyi and 案 layers add none.

**Practitioner's note.** There is no cultivation practice here. This is a lower-class record of a bitter, cold plant against fevers and malign sores, of contested identity, with no dose, and the tradition's own grading says it is not for long taking.

### Contraindications

- The classic prints no toxicity statement for this entry, but the preface calls lower-class drugs mostly toxic and not for long taking (line 2935). Treat the grade as the caution.
- **Identity.** The name is contested between a kiwifruit vine and the star-fruit tree. As a general fact, star fruit contains a neurotoxin that is dangerous to people with kidney disease; this fact was not read in the sources on disk and bears on Yang's identification only. - **Conditions named.** Fever in children, spreading redness, water swelling and malign sores need medical care.
