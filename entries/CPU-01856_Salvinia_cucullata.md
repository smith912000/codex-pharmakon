# Salvinia cucullata — Akhu-parni; Mushika-parni (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Salvinia cucullata, the Latin that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints (OCR ",  Salvinia  cucullata.", line 31216) against the Sanskrit name Akhu-parni (OCR "Akbu-parni", line 31161), and prints a second time (OCR "Salvina  Cucullata.", line 32333) against the name Mushika-parni. Both pairings of a Sanskrit name with its Latin line are positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; neither is printed on one line. The chapters do not print Akhu-parni; they print Mushika-parni three times and give it three different identities, none of them this Latin* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The name the row was keyed on, Akhu-parni, is not in any chapter file. A second appendix name for the same Latin, Mushika-parni, is in three chapters, where the text calls it "a kind of Danti" (Uttara 54) and gives it as one reading of Dravanti (Chikitsa 2). The Latin pairings are positional and agent-proposed, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** `Sources/SS_Sushruta/extract/names.csv` row 361 gives the name as "Akbu-parni", the locus as "Vol. III Appendix 3 (line 31161; id line 31216)" and the Latin as ",  Salvinia  cucullata.", with the note that the pairing was positional in a block of 31. The OCR garbles the letters of this page's names (for example "Aksba" for Aksha, line 31163), so "Akbu-parni" is read as Akhu-parni. The Latin is printed in two spellings, "Salvinia cucullata" (31216) and "Salvina Cucullata" (32333), which the Codex reads as one species.

**The first pairing, Akhu-parni, checked by counting.** The first page of the appendix prints thirty-one Sanskrit names (lines 31126 to 31181, with Amalaka and Amalaki bracketed as one) and thirty-one Latin entries (lines 31184 to 31235). Akhu-parni is the twentieth Sanskrit name (after Ajamoda, the seventeenth, Aja-s'ringi and Akasa-valli) and "Salvinia cucullata." is the twentieth Latin entry, after "Pimpinella Involucrata.", "Gymnema Sylvestre." and "Cassayta filiformis.". The neighbours agree: Aksha stands against "Terminalia belerica." (line 31217), Akshota against "Juglans regia." (31218), Alarka against "Calotropis gigantea." (31219) and Alabu against "Lagenaria Vulgaris." (31220). The same page is counted in the entries on *Pimpinella involucrata* (I_1414) and *Rumex vesicarius* (I_1418), and in I_1407, I_1381 and I_1390. [USER: verify]

**The second pairing, Mushika-parni, checked by counting.** The page of the M names that begins Magadhi (page number "73" at line 32201) holds thirty-seven Sanskrit entries (lines 32204 to 32273) and thirty-seven Latin entries (lines 32276 to 32333), once the bracketed pairs are taken as one each. Mushika-parni (OCR "Mnsliika-parni", line 32273) is the last of the Sanskrit entries and "Salvina Cucullata." the last of the Latin; Mushali with Mushalika stands against "Curculigo Orchioides." **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Akhu-parni, Akhuparni and Akbu, for OCR variants, and by fuzzy matching of every word against Akhu-parni and Mushika-parni. Akhu-parni is not found. Mushika-parni is found three times.

*Chikitsa ch.2 (traumatic wounds, Sadyo-vrana).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch02.txt` lines 524 to 534, in the treatment of a malignant ulcer (Dushta-vrana): "Oil cooked with Dravanti (Satamuli, according to certain authorities, Mushika-parni according to others), Chiravilva, Danti, Chitraka, Prithvika, Nimba-leaves, Kasisa, Tuttha, Trivrit, Tejovati, Nili (indigo), the two kinds of Haridra, Saindhava salt, Tila, Bhumi-Kadamba, Suvaha, Shukakhya, Langalakhya, Naipali, Jalini, Madayanti, Mrigadani, Sudha, Murva, Arka, Kitari, Haritala and Karanja, or with as many of them as would be available, should be used for the purification (of a malignant sore or ulcer)." The parenthesis is the translator's. It makes Mushika-parni one of two readings of the name Dravanti, so the name is given there as a possible identity of another drug and not as an ingredient of its own.

*Kalpa ch.1 (protecting food and drink from poison).* `SS_Kalpa_ch01.txt` lines 307 to 316, for a poisoned plaster applied to the head ("falling off of the hair", "violent headache", "bleeding through the mouth and the nostrils" and "the appearance of glands on the head"): a plaster of black earth treated with the bile of a Rishya deer, clarified butter and the expressed juice of Shyama, Palindi and Tanduliyaka, and then "The expressed juice of Malati (flower) or of Mushika-parni, fluid-secretions of fresh cow-dung and house-soot as external applications are also beneficial in such cases." No gloss is given.

*Uttara ch.54 (worms, Krimi-roga).* `SS_Uttara_ch54.txt` lines 160 to 166: "The juice extracted from Pattura or the drugs of the Surasadi group should (similarly) be taken, or the powders of dried horse-dung or of Vidanga should be licked with honey. Pupulika-cakes prepared with (pasted Yava and) the pasted leaves of Mushika-parni (a kind of Danti) should be taken by the patient followed by draughts of Dhanyamla (fermented paddy-gruels)." The parenthesis is the translator's.

*Not tied to this entry.* `SS_Kalpa_ch01.txt` lines 436 to 439 name "the drugs known as Mushika and Ajaruha" as wrist prophylactics against poisoned food, which the text does not tie to Mushika-parni. The rat Mushika of Kalpa ch.6 is an animal.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Akhu-parni and Mushika-parni found nothing.

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Salvinia*, Akhu-parni and Mushika-parni found no entry on this plant. **I_1372** (*Anthericum tuberosum*, Dravanti) quotes the Chikitsa 2 passage above (its line 28 and its identity paragraph) and the same "Mushika-parni according to others" parenthesis. The identities the text gives, a kind of Danti (Uttara 54) and a reading of Dravanti (Chikitsa 2), are not the water fern the appendix's Latin names, and the Codex does not reconcile them. ### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Salvinia* is a genus of small floating ferns (Salviniaceae). That is a statement about the genus and not a finding of the Sushruta text, and the plants the chapters call "a kind of Danti" and a reading of Dravanti are not ferns. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the name the chapters print, the plant (if it is any of these) is a juice applied to the head for the effects of a poisoned plaster (Kalpa 1), a leaf in cakes for worms (Uttara 54, as "a kind of Danti"), and a possible reading of Dravanti in an oil to purify a malignant ulcer (Chikitsa 2). The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the chapters give are the expressed juice applied externally (Kalpa 1), the pasted leaves made into cakes with pasted barley and followed by fermented paddy-gruel (Uttara 54), and, for Dravanti, an oil cooked with some thirty drugs (Chikitsa 2). No quantity is given for Mushika-parni in any of them. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]
