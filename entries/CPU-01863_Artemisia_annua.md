# Artemisia annua — tshar.bong, Tsharbong (rGyud-bzhi)

## *Artemisia annua, printed "Artemesia annua", the first of two binomials Clark 1995 prints for tshar.bong (after it comes "Artemesia sieversiana Willd"); the Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 glossary sets the same name, by column position, against a different Artemisia* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** FMT

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Tibetan texts (FMT); this entry rests on one text, the rGyud-bzhi (Four Tantras), read in two modern translations: Clark 1995 for the binomials and the commentary, and Men-Tsee-Khang 2011 for the root-text sentence. The Tibetan name is a drug name and not a botanical one, and the two translations do not agree on the plant.** Both loci are in `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/text/`. The row was added from the printed names (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. **Row key.** In `Sources/FMT_Tibetan/extract/names.csv` (row 146) the row reads "tshar.bong" with the locus "Clark 1995 (line 1040)", the identification "Artemesia annua; Artemesia sieversiana Willd" and the note "more than one Latin name given; key uses the first". The key is the first of the two Latin names, so it is *Artemisia annua*. Clark's preface (line 83) says that "more than one Latin name is frequently given for one substance".

*Clark 1995, materia medica run (line 1040).* `Clark_1995_Quintessence_Tantras_Tibetan_Medicine.txt`, chapter 20 ("The Principles of Medicines: Powers of Medicines", line 792), in section "(7) Exposition of the Powers of Herbs" (heading at line 997). The entry reads: "tshar.bong: Artemesia annua: Artemesia sieversiana Willd: [is said to be a type of mkhan.pa and is of white, black and pale brown types. The brown type is a thick bush with many branches and has hot, bitter taste and strong odor. The white type also has strong odor, and all three types] cure throat fever and lung disorders." Clark's preface (line 82) says that "Interpolations in square brackets [ ] are taken from the Blue Aquamarine commentary and Spreading Tree commentary". So the three types, the bush habit, the hot bitter taste and the strong odor, and the link to mkhan.pa, are the commentary layer. The sentence outside the brackets, "cure throat fever and lung disorders", is the root-layer claim. The English headword Clark gives for mkhan.pa is "Mugwort"; tshar.bong has no English name in his entry.

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, running text (line 3438).* `MenTseeKhang_2011_Root_and_Explanatory_Tantra.txt`, chapter 20, "The Efficacy of Medicinal Substances" (contents, line 225), within the run on herbal medicines: "Tsharbong cures fever of the larynx and lung disorders." The index (line 7535) gives "Tsharbong 208". The sentence agrees with Clark's outside-the-brackets clause ("throat fever and lung disorders"); the text does not say they are the same drug, and the Codex reads them together from the sound of the name and the agreement. [USER: verify]

*Men-Tsee-Khang 2011, Glossary on Medicinal Ingredients (T block).* The glossary prints its columns in separate runs. The phonetic names include "Tsendhen marpo" (line 5908), and on one line "Tsendhen karpo Tshar bong" (5909) and "Tshe dhum" (5910); the transliteration line 5913 reads "tsan dan gkar po tshar bong". The scientific names (lines 5928 to 5998) run to twenty-four, and the lines that matter here are "Pterocarpus santalinus" (5984), then, on one line, "Santalum album Artemesia dracunculus" (5988), then "Ephedra saxatilis" (5989); the English names are "Red Sandalwood" (5986), "White Sandalwood" (5991) and "Little dragon mugword Ephedra" (5992). So by position the glossary sets Tshar bong against *Artemisia dracunculus* ("Little dragon mugwort", printed "mugword"), the Tsendhen names against the two sandalwoods and Tshe dhum against *Ephedra saxatilis*. The entry on *Ephedra saxatilis* (I_1076) counts the same run and makes the same reading. The glossary does not print the pairing on one row. [USER: verify]

**Identity doubt.** Clark's *annua* and *sieversiana* and the glossary's *dracunculus* are three species for one Tibetan name. The spine key follows Clark's first name. The Codex does not choose, and the row stands for the Tibetan drug and not for one species. A second Tibetan drug, mkhan.pa, shares the genus and the claim that tshar.bong is "a type" of it; that drug is treated in I_1428 (*Artemisia sieversiana*).

**Overlap.** A search of the entries folder for *Artemisia annua*, *Artemesia annua*, tshar.bong, Tsharbong and Qing Hao found no entry on this Tibetan drug. **I_1428** (*Artemisia sieversiana*, mkhan.pa) is the entry on the drug that the commentary says tshar.bong is a type of. **I_164** (Mugwort, tags SBJ, FMT, DMM, CM, EP), **I_437** (*Artemisia vulgaris*, tags include FMT), **I_329** (Chinese Mugwort, *Artemisia argyi*) and **I_249** (Wormwood, *Artemisia absinthium*) mention *Artemisia annua* or Qing Hao in passing (I_249 lines 17 and 65, I_437 line 92) and none is an entry on this drug. **I_781** (Tarragon, *Artemisia dracunculus*) is the entry on the glossary's binomial and carries no FMT tag. **I_1076** (*Ephedra saxatilis*) counts the same glossary run. Nothing is merged. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. It is a general, well-established fact that *Artemisia annua* is the plant from which the antimalarial artemisinin is obtained and that *Artemisia* species carry aromatic volatile oils, which fits the "strong odor" of Clark's commentary. No source read for this entry says that of the Tibetan drug, and its identity between *annua*, *sieversiana* and *dracunculus* is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. The Tibetan texts record a drug that cures "throat fever and lung disorders" (Clark line 1040, outside the brackets) or "fever of the larynx and lung disorders" (Men-Tsee-Khang line 3438). These are the texts' own claims and not Codex protocols. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose, and a note on artemisinin is not an indication of the Tibetan drug.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **FMT:** Clark line 1040 prints, inside the square brackets (commentary layer), that the brown type "has hot, bitter taste and strong odor" and that the white type "also has strong odor". It prints no power or post-digestive taste. The Men-Tsee-Khang line prints none.

### Cultivation Register

**Tibetan — rGyud-bzhi (FMT).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* The Explanatory Tantra classes tshar.bong by what it does: it cures throat fever and lung disorders (root layer), and the commentary adds that it is "a type of mkhan.pa". Clark's entry for mkhan.pa (line 1078) has a bracketed sentence that its white subspecies "is recommended in essence-extraction compounds" (*bcud len*), but the text does not carry that sentence to tshar.bong, and the Codex does not. The cultivation spec records that in this tradition medicine is the third of four treatment branches, supporting practice without reaching its root, and that no formal category of substances for subtle-body practice exists. The rejuvenation chapters (Clark chapter 23, lines 1264 to 1276; Men-Tsee-Khang chapter 23, contents line 235) were read for the name and do not name tshar.bong. Its framing is therapeutic only.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the lines read gives a cultivator a practice with this plant. It is a fever-and-lung drug in the Tibetan texts, its species is not settled, and a link to the neighbouring mugwort drug's essence-extraction role is not made by the text. The abstentive answer is the honest one.

### Contraindications

The sources read print no warning. The identity is uncertain between three species of *Artemisia*, and members of the genus are aromatic plants that can cause allergic reactions in people sensitive to the daisy family; no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Fever of the throat and lungs needs medical care. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
