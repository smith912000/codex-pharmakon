**Avicenna, *Canon* of Medicine (CM).** (Canon Book II, entry *Armāk*, Arabic text line 10628–10634). *What it is:* "a Yemeni aromatic wood resembling cinnamon in colour" (خشبة يمانية عطرية تشبه القرفة في اللون). *Cosmetic:* it sweetens the breath (تطيب النكهة). *Swellings:* a plaster benefits hot swellings. *Wounds:* it is of benefit in spreading sores and checks them; it is carried dry for drying, without stinging, and prevents putrefaction of the members. *Head:* it strengthens the brain, tightens the gums and suits diseases of the mouth. *Eye:* eating it benefits ophthalmia. *Excretion:* it binds the bowels altogether (يعقل الطبيعة كلها). The entry gives no temperament and no degree. The line just above the heading ("it is a killing poison") belongs to the preceding entry and is not read here. The text names no authority for this entry.
