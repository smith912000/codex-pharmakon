# Filarhark — فيلرهرخ (*fīlarharkh*, also spelt فيلرهرج in the text)

## *An unidentified tree, said in the Canon to be the tree of hudud (حضض), bearing a fruit like pepper; the identification is the Canon's own report ('it is said'), not ours, and no binomial is assigned (confidence low)* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** CM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** Named only by Avicenna's *Canon* among the texts on disk. Source file: `Sources/CM_Avicenna/text/IbnSina_Qanun_OpenITI_Shamela0010706.txt`.

**Locus (CM).** *Canon* Book II, entry *fīlarharkh* (فيلرهرخ), heading at Arabic text line 16977, entry to line 16985; the next heading is *farāsiyūn* (line 16986). The row began as a cheap first pass reading 'hadad / lycium tree, pepper-like fruit'; the Arabic confirms the pointer but only as hearsay.

**Identity (CM).** The entry opens: 'It is said: it is the tree of *ḥuḍuḍ*, and it has a fruit like pepper' (قيل: إنه شجرة الحضض وله ثمرة كالفلفل; lines 16978-16979). It adds that *ḥuḍuḍ* (an extract) 'is made from it and is made from barberry (*zirishk*), and the Arab kind is another type', and that the power of *fīlarharj* 'is near the power of the *ḥuḍuḍ* that is made from it, and slightly weaker' (lines 16979-16980). The spelling in the second mention differs (فيلرهرج). **Identity doubt:** the Canon does not name a species; the 'hudud tree' is commonly taken to be a *Lycium*, and this Codex already has *Lycium europaeum* (I_1125) and barberry (I_444, I_235) as separate entries, but whether *fīlarharkh* is the same plant is not established. [USER: verify]

**What the text says (CM).** Cosmetic: it strengthens the hair, applied as a paste alone and with oil (line 16982). Digestive: its branches are boiled in vinegar and drunk for the spleen, with great benefit, and likewise for jaundice (lines 16982-16983). Excretory: a decoction of its leaves and branches promotes the menses; and if a quantity of its fruit is drunk, described as *waznun maṭrūs* (وزن مطروس, the wording is obscure), it purges much phlegmatic humour (lines 16983-16985).

### Active Components

The text names no constituent. In general terms, extracts sold as *hudud* or *rusot* are bitter, alkaloid-bearing plant extracts; whether that applies here is not known. No analytical source was retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. The entry records a hair wash, a spleen and jaundice decoction, a menses-promoting decoction and a purgative fruit. It is not a recommended practice: the plant is not securely identified.

### Preparation & Dosing

The only preparations are those in the text: a paste alone or with oil for the hair; branches boiled in vinegar and drunk; a decoction of leaves and branches. No dose is given in the sources retrieved, and the fruit quantity (*waznun maṭrūs*) is not clear enough to quote. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify] General fact: bitter plant extracts are traditionally used as astringents. The pointer to Lycium or barberry is unproved.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** No degree of heat or dryness is printed for this entry. Its power is said to be 'near' that of *ḥuḍuḍ*, 'slightly weaker' (lines 16979-16980); no temperament is given.

### Cultivation Register

**Unani / Avicenna (CM).** *(b) Framed.* The entry is a drug-by-drug list of qualities and uses inside the *Canon*'s materia medica. It assigns no cultivation status and says nothing of innate heat, radical moisture or the *ruh*. Nothing is said of the faculties.

**Practitioner's note.** A hair wash and a spleen and jaundice decoction in the text, with an emmenagogue and purgative action. Nothing supports a cultivation use, and the identity of the tree is unsettled.

### Contraindications

A decoction that promotes the menses and purges phlegm is to be avoided in pregnancy as a general precaution. The text states no contraindication. The plant is unidentified; do not substitute any species. [USER: verify]
