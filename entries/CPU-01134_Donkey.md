# Donkey — "Ass" (Bryan's English, 1930); Ass's blood, dung, ears, fat, head, hoof, jawbone, liver, milk, tallow, teeth, testicles, semen
## *The ass or donkey as a source of remedies in the Ebers Papyrus. Bryan's list prints the English "Ass" and no Egyptian word and no binomial; the identification as the domestic donkey is Bryan's English* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** EP

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** The text on disk is C. P. Bryan's 1930 English *The Papyrus Ebers* (`Sources/EP_Ebers/text/PapyrusEbers_Unse.txt`), an OCR file in which Bryan translates and also summarises and comments. In his chapter VII, "Organic Remedies", Bryan says that a full list of the animals could not be made, "since (as with many of the plant and mineral substances) the identity of a considerable number of the animals mentioned is still beyond us", and then lists those "whose identity is not in doubt". The list runs from Lion to Tapeworm and includes, in the line printed at line 1404 (the row in `Sources/EP_Ebers/extract/names.csv`, "Bryan 1930, Ch. VII Organic Remedies (animals) (line 1404)"), "Ass Hog Dog Cat Frog Tadpole ...". The names.csv note says the row is "Bryan's English identification; no Egyptian word or binomial given in this list". The source list `_sources/ebers.csv` (line 109, "Donkey") cites Ghalioungui's 1987 translation, Glossary I; that book is not on disk and was not read.

Bryan then lists what each animal gave. Of the ass he writes (line 1427): "Amid such an assemblage it is gratifying to find the Ass, that never-failing friend of mankind throughout the ages, taking pride of place. For the relief of suffering humanity he sacrificed his blood, dung, ears, fat, head, hoof, jawbone, liver, milk, tallow, teeth, testicles, and semen." That is Bryan's own summary of the papyrus, in his own tone, not a line of the papyrus. He puts the cow "a good second". In a later note (line 3230) Bryan says that the Phallus is "the determinative sign placed after AA, the symbol by which the much-mentioned Ass is designated in the Papyrus", which is a remark about the writing and gives the only Egyptian sign for the animal in the text read.

The passages I opened, with Bryan's English and his own wording of the complaint where he gives it:

- *Semen and testicles.* In the opening of the organic chapter Bryan lists "Ass's Semen along with Rush-from-the-Green-Willow-tree, Fresh Bread, Herbs-of-the-Field, Figs, Grapes, and Wine" as curing "the Great Debility" (line 1361), and, for the *nesit* disease, a remedy which begins "The-Two-Testicles-of-a-Black-Ass" (line 1365). The rest of that remedy is cut by a page break in the OCR.
- *Fat.* Poppy-berries "beaten up in Ass's Fat" are one unguent for a stubborn headache (line 2112). For "refreshing the *met*" Bryan gives a poultice of "Fat-of-the-Ox, Fat-of-the-Ass, Fat-of-the-Ram, Fruit-of-the-Dompalm, Xehua-grain, Poppy-plant, and Sea-salt" (line 3151); *met* is a word Bryan says Egyptologists cannot fix, rendering it as nerves or vessels (line 3149).
- *Dung.* "The remedy par excellence for suppleness was Ass's dung": mixed with honey and sea-salt and smeared on the limbs, or in a longer compound with honey, figs, date-juice, sea-salt, yeast of wine, bullock's fat, natron and grains, cooked and applied as a poultice (line 2181); "In another salve Ass's dung reappears" (line 2182).
- *Milk.* "Fresh Dates and Ass's Milk" as a douche for pustules in the vagina (lines 2557-2558); ass's milk with dough, put on an ulcer "until it falls off" (line 2654); ass's milk with acanthus resin, indigo, turpentine-nuts and honey under the heading "Remedy to free the flesh" (line 2702); ass's milk with dough, wine and bitter beer, "drunk" for four days (line 3276). Bryan also lists "Ass's Milk" among the forms of milk used as a vehicle (line 1131).
- *Eyes and ears.* "Ass's Tooth. Mix in Water and apply around the Patient's eyes so that he recovers quickly" (line 2742, for bleary eyes); "Tallow-from-the-Jawbone-of-an-Ass. Mix in Cool Water and let the Patient put on his Temples" (line 3020, "to drive out hotness in the eyes"); "Ass's Blood" in a list with the blood of the cow, pig, dog and stag, with collyrium, to prevent the hair growing into the eye after it has been pulled out (lines 2879-2888); "Ass's Ear, Red-lead, Caraway and Olive Oil" as an injection for the ear (line 3032).
- *Hoof and head.* The first remedy of the hair chapter is "Toes-of-a-Dog, Refuse-of-Dates, Hoof-of-an-Ass" (line 3996), which Bryan's heading says was prepared for "Ses", mother of a king named Teta (the OCR of the heading is imperfect); the hoof, roasted, is also in a compound with the vulva of a bitch and worms, cooked in oil and gum and rubbed into the scalp (lines 4057-4058). "Hoof-of-an-Ass" is also in a salve for the "woodlike *uha*-disease" (line 3300), and "Ass's Head, Corn, and Oil" is another salve for the same (line 3293).
- *Liver.* "Ass's Liver warmed in Oil with Opium", made into little balls to rub on grey eyebrows (line 4058). See III_79.

The papyrus prescribes in many ways for one complaint, and Bryan's English renders compounds in his own style. I record only what these lines say. Whether Bryan's "Ass" always translates the same Egyptian word cannot be told from his English.

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms donkey milk is a milk low in fat; dung is a mixture of undigested plant fibre, bacteria and water; fat is mostly triglyceride. Nothing more specific can be said without a source, and the Egyptian compounds mix many ingredients.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The papyrus uses the ass in many parts and for many complaints, and Bryan gives it first place among animals for the range of its parts. The Codex gives no regimen built on any of them. The dung, blood, tooth, hoof and organ uses are an infection risk, and the eye and ear uses are applications to delicate organs. Donkey's milk is a food in some places and needs no protocol from this Codex.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations are those listed above and are given as Bryan renders them: milk mixed with dates, dough or other ingredients; tooth mixed in water; jawbone tallow mixed in cool water; fat beaten with poppy-berries; dung mixed with honey and sea-salt; hoof roasted and cooked in oil and gum; liver warmed in oil and made into balls. Where Bryan prints a duration it is on a single remedy (for example "drunk" for four days, line 3276). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. No part of the donkey listed here is an established treatment for the complaints named. Dung and unpasteurised milk carry bacteria and parasites. Dung put on a wound carries the risk of tetanus. Applying animal products to the eye or ear carries a serious infection risk. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Egyptian (EP):** None. The Ebers material is organised by complaint, and Bryan's English prints no qualities, tastes or temperaments for the ass or its parts.

### Cultivation Register

**Egyptian / Ebers (EP).** *(c) Absent.* No cultivation framing is attested. The Ebers material is indication-organised: it records what was done for a complaint, not what a substance does to the person's *ka*, *ba*, *akh* or *ib*. In the passages read the ass is an ingredient in compounds for defined complaints (the headache, the eyes, the ears, the hair, the *met*, the *uha*-disease). Egyptian practice treats spell and drug as one intervention (*heka*); Bryan prints no recitation against the ass remedies above, but since Bryan summarises, that is no proof the papyrus gives none. Bryan's remark that the ass "took pride of place" is his own praise, not a status in the papyrus. No deity is attributed to the ass in the passages read.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The papyrus uses the ass freely for complaints, and the Codex finds no cultivation status in it. For a cultivator the honest use of the entry is as a record. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Contraindications

- **Infection and hygiene:** dung, blood, and unsterilised animal products should not be put on wounds, eyes or ears; tetanus and other serious infections are a risk.
- **Raw milk:** unpasteurised milk of any animal can carry bacteria such as brucella and salmonella.
- **Eyes and ears:** do not put animal products in the eye or ear; seek medical care for eye pain or discharge.
- **Hormonal tissue:** testes and semen are glandular and hormonal tissue; do not take them as supplements.
- **Ethics and law:** donkeys are working animals in many places and the slaughter of donkeys for parts is restricted or illegal in some regions. The Codex does not recommend harming any animal for these uses.
