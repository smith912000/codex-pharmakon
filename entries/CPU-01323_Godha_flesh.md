# Godha flesh — Godha, flesh of the "hole-dweller" (*Vileshaya*) group, Sushruta Samhita
## *The flesh of the animal Bhishagratna prints as "Godha", glossed by him in a note as "a kind of wild lizard" (Uttaratantra ch.17) and in the Vol. 3 glossary as "An iguana"; no binomial is printed* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The Godha is described in the "Meat Group" of *Sutrasthana* ch.46, the chapter on food and drink (*Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya*), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`; `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 219 lists "Godha (monitor lizard)", source "Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1"). The "monitor lizard" is that list's gloss; the chapter does not print it (see below). The chapter says the flesh of animals that are aquatic, marsh-dwelling, village-dwelling, carnivorous, unbifurcated-hoofed or high-ground "is generally used as food", each succeeding kind superior to the one before it (lines 385-394). The Godha sits in the *Vileshaya* group: "Animals such as, the Shvavit, Shalyaka, Godha, Shasha, Vrishadansha, Lopaka, Lomasha-Karna, Kadali, Mriga-Priyaka, Ajagara, Sarpa, Mushika, Nakula and Maha-Vabhru belong to the group of Vileshaya (hole-dwellers)" (lines 574-578). The group text says the flesh increases "the consistency of stool and urine", is "heat-making in potency, sweet in digestion", "subdue[s] the Vayu and generate[s] the Pittam and Kapham", and is "demulcent and beneficial in cough, dyspnoea and cachexia" (lines 580-587). Of the Godha by name: "The flesh of the Godha is sweet of digestion and has a pungent, astringent taste. It is tonic and constructive and pacifies the Vayu and Pittam" (lines 590-596).

The name recurs outside ch.46, always in Bhishagratna's English. *Kalpasthana* ch.1, in the regimen that guards a king against poisoned food, has him use "the meat and soup of the flesh of a peacock, mungoose, Godhi (a species of lizard), or Prishata deer" (lines 447-449), and gives the soup: "The flesh of a Godha, mungoose, or deer should be cooked and spiced with pasted Palindi (Trivrit), Yashtimadhu and sugar" (lines 451-455). *Kalpasthana* ch.5 includes the bile of the Godha among the biles (boar, Godha, peacock, Sallaka, cat, Prishata deer, mungoose) with which a powder compound is pasted with honey and kept in a horn, the antivenom called *Rishabhagada* (lines 408-421). *Sutrasthana* ch.16 (piercing and bandaging the ear-lobes) elongates an adhered ear-lobe with an unguent of "the milk, fat, and marrow of any such animals and birds as the Godha, the Pratudas, the Vishkiras, the Anupas, or the Audakas as would be available", boiled with clarified butter and white-mustard oil and a decoction (lines 304-316). *Uttaratantra* ch.17, among eye preparations, has "the liver of a Godha (a kind of wild lizard)" opened, stuffed with Pippali, clay-coated and roasted, used once as an *anjana* with honey (lines 163-168); the sentence does not name the condition. Cautions and omens also occur. *Sutrasthana* ch.20 names "the flesh of the Godha, and the Varaha (wild boar)" among articles that "being eaten simultaneously with milk" can "act as deadly poisons through incompatible combinations" (lines 86-92), and says the flesh of Kapinjala, peacock, Lava, Tittira and Godha, "boiled with castor oil and on a fire of the twigs of castor plants, should not be eaten" (lines 179-182). Ch.21, under the aggravation of *Pittam*, says "the flesh of a Godha or a goat or mutton may lead to its aggravation, if taken injudiciously" (lines 288-301). *Sutrasthana* ch.29 counts "the sight and the sound of a Godha or a Krikalasha" as inauspicious omens for a physician on the road (lines 232-234). *Sharirasthana* ch.3 says a pregnant woman's longing for Godha flesh "indicates the presence of a sleepy, drowsy person in her womb who would be tenaciously fond of good things in life" (lines 229-231). *Krishna-Godha* in *Kalpasthana* ch.8 (line 59) is a name in a list of insect families, a different thing.

**Identity.** Ch.46 gives no gloss. The note in *Uttaratantra* ch.17 says "a kind of wild lizard", *Kalpasthana* ch.1 says "a species of lizard", and the Vol. 3 glossary pairs *Godha* with "An iguana" (`Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`, glossary near lines 30540-30590; the OCR separates names from glosses, so this pairing is by column order). The spine list's "monitor lizard" matches none of these. The species is not settled here. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

Animal flesh is mostly water and muscle protein, with fat that varies by species, and supplies iron, zinc and B vitamins. No analysis of the animal Sushruta calls Godha was read, and its species is not settled. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The sourced material is a dietary and materia medica description from the *Sushruta Samhita*: a lizard-like animal's flesh with stated qualities, a few external and eye preparations, and several cautions. The Codex gives no protocol. The identity of the animal is uncertain, lizards are reptiles commonly carrying *Salmonella*, and large lizards of the monitor and iguana kinds are protected by wildlife law in many countries. Nothing here is needed by a reader who eats no meat, and the text's own warnings (not with milk; not boiled with castor oil) are the practical content.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the texts give are those above: the flesh cooked and spiced with Palindi, Yashtimadhu and sugar as a soup for the king (*Kalpasthana* ch.1, lines 451-455); the bile pasted with honey into the *Rishabhagada* (ch.5, lines 408-421); the fat, milk and marrow in an ear-lobe unguent (*Sutrasthana* ch.16, lines 304-316); the liver stuffed with Pippali and roasted, as an *anjana* with honey, "for only once" (*Uttaratantra* ch.17, lines 163-168; a footnote reports Dallana's view that the roasted Pippali is used with honey); and a lukewarm soup (ch.50, line 183). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General facts only. Reptile meat and handling carry a recognised risk of *Salmonella* infection, and wild-caught animals can carry parasites; thorough cooking and hygienic handling are standard precautions. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Sushruta (SS).** Printed in *Sutrasthana* ch.46 (lines 580-596). For the *Vileshaya* group generally: heat-making in potency; sweet in digestion; demulcent; subdues *Vayu*, generates *Pittam* and *Kapham*; increases the consistency of stool and urine. For the Godha specifically: sweet of digestion; pungent and astringent in taste; tonic and constructive; pacifies *Vayu* and *Pittam*. The text as printed is in tension with ch.21, which lists Godha flesh among foods that may aggravate *Pittam* if taken injudiciously (lines 288-301), and with the group statement that the group generates *Pittam*. No explanation is given in the lines read; it is recorded as printed. No Chinese classification applies: the entry is not tagged SBJ.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Godha flesh is named in a food-and-drink chapter whose opening lines tie food as a class to bodily strength, complexion and *ojas* (ch.46, lines 9-12). That is a claim about food, not about Godha flesh. The ch.46 text calls it "tonic and constructive", a property listed among foods, not a placement in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter; no line read puts the Godha in either. The *Kalpasthana* use is protective (a king's guard against poison), not a cultivation status. No *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* claim is made for it. Sorting foods into *sattvic*, *rajasic* and *tamasic* belongs to *Samkhya* and later material, not to Sushruta, and is not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice. In Sushruta the Godha is a food and drug with stated qualities, and the passages a cultivator can use are the cautions: not with milk, not prepared with castor oil, and watch *Pittam*. A reader who eats meat may read the classification as the tradition's own description; one who does not has nothing to adopt, and with the animal's identity uncertain there is no reason to seek it out.

### Contraindications

- **Hygiene and infection:** reptile flesh and viscera can carry *Salmonella* and parasites; cook through; wash hands and surfaces.
- **The text's own cautions:** not eaten with milk (*Sutrasthana* ch.20, lines 86-92); not boiled with castor oil on a castor-twig fire (lines 179-182); possible aggravation of *Pittam* (ch.21).
- **Identity:** the animal is glossed as a lizard, wild lizard or iguana in different places and is not fixed; do not substitute any species on this basis. [USER: verify]
- **Legal and conservation status:** large lizards are protected in many countries and traded under international wildlife rules; status for the species intended was not checked. [USER: verify]
- **Ethics and religion:** killing an animal for food or medicine is a moral question for many traditions; this entry takes no position beyond recording the text.
