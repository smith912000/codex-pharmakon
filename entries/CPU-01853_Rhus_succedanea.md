# Rhus succedanea — Karkata-s'ringi; S'ringi (Sushruta Samhita, Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 and the chapters)

## *Rhus succedanea, the first of two Latin forms that Bhishagratna's Appendix No. 3 ("Names of Drugs, with Latin synonyms", Vol. III) prints on one line (OCR "Rhus  succedanea  ;  Pistaca  Integerruna.", line 32014) against the Sanskrit name Karkata-s'ringi (OCR "Earkata-S'ringi", line 31930). The pairing of the Sanskrit name with its Latin line is positional, proposed by an agent from two OCR columns and re-checked here by counting; it is not printed on one line. The chapters print Karkata-s'ringi (and, in two chapters, S'ringi) and give no Latin name. The second form on the line, Pistacia integerrima, is the subject of an existing entry, I_422* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Sushruta text (SS); this entry rests on one text, Bhishagratna's English translation of the Sushruta Samhita, and on a Latin name that comes from the translator's appendix and not from the chapters. The Latin pairing is positional and agent-proposed, and the appendix line prints two species, so the botanical identity is open.** The chapters are in `Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/` and the appendix is in `Bhishagratna_Vol3_djvu.txt`. The row was added from the printed Latin (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP, confidence "medium") and has not been verified against POWO. The Codex does not state the botanical identity as certain. The Latin line holds two binomials separated by a semicolon; the batch key is the **first**, *Rhus succedanea*, and the second (the OCR "Pistaca Integerruna", read as *Pistacia integerrima*) belongs to the existing entry I_422. **Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *What the chapters say.* All chapter files (`sutra`, `chikitsa`, `uttara`, `kalpa`, `nidana`, `sharira`) were searched for Karkata-s'ringi in its OCR spellings ("Karkata-Shringi", "Karkata-s' ringi", "Karkata- / S'ringi" across a line break) and for S'ringi alone, and by fuzzy matching of every word. The name is not found in the Latin form the appendix gives, and no chapter says what plant it is. The hits are these.

*Sutra ch.38 (the groups of drugs).* `SS_Sutra_ch38.txt` lines 250 to 262: the Kakolyadi group is "Kakoli, Kshira-Kakoli, Jivaka, Rishabhaka, Mudgaparni, Mashaparni, Meda, Mahameda, Chhinna-ruha, Karkata-Shringi, Tugakshiri, Padmaka, Prapaundarika, Riddhi, Vriddhi, Mridvika, Jivanti and Madhuka". The metrical text that follows describes the **group**: it "subdues the action of the deranged Pittam, blood and Vayu. It increases the quantity of milk in the breast (galactagogue) and favours the accumulation of phlegm (Kapham) in the body. It is a restorative and an elixir and is endued with the therapeutic virtue of augmenting the virile potency of a man." The text states these of the group and not of Karkata-s'ringi singly.

*Chikitsa ch.17 (erysipelas, sinus and diseases of the mammary glands).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch17.txt` lines 116 to 122, in the treatment of Kaphaja Visarpa: "a medicinal plaster (Pradeha) composed of Aja-gandha, Ashva-gandha, Sarala, Kala, Ekaishika and Aja-s'ringi pasted with the urine of a cow". A footnote to Aja-s'ringi (line 140) says: "Gayadasa explains it as Karkata-S'ringi." So in this one place the commentator Gayadasa, as the translator reports him, reads Aja-s'ringi as Karkata-s'ringi; the text itself prints Aja-s'ringi, and the appendix pairs Aja-s'ringi with "Gymnema Sylvestre" (line 31214).

*Chikitsa ch.40 (inhalation of medicated fumes, snuffs and gargles).* `SS_Chikitsa_ch40.txt` lines 29 to 33: "Vrihati, Kantakarika, Trikatu, Kasa-marda, Hingu, Ingudi bark, Manah-shila, Guduchi, and Karkata-s'ringi and such other drugs which allay cough should be used in the Kasaghna-Dhuma", the smoke that allays cough.

*Uttara ch.21 (diseases of the ear).* `SS_Uttara_ch21.txt` lines 270 to 276, for Karna-srava: "The oil duly cooked with the tender sprouts of Shaivala, Maha-vriksha, Jambu and of Amra, as well as with Karkata-S'ringi honey and Manduki is highly efficacious in these cases." The OCR prints no comma between Karkata-S'ringi and honey; whether honey is a separate ingredient is the Codex's reading.

*Uttara ch.51 (asthma).* `SS_Uttara_ch51.txt` lines 161 and 175 to 184, the five lambatives: "any of the following five groups of drugs mentioned in a hemistich each, viz., Timsha-seeds, Karkata-S'ringi and Suvarchika (Jatuka-creeper), or Durlabha, Pippali, Kattika and Haritaki ... should be licked with honey and clarified butter by a patient suffering from cough and asthma." Lines 206 to 214: "Draksha, Haritaki, Krishna, Karkata-s'ringi and Durlabha in combination with honey and clarified butter, should be licked by a patient whereby he would get rid even of a violent attack of asthma."

*The name S'ringi alone.* `SS_Uttara_ch51.txt` lines 103 to 109, the "S'ringyadi Ghrita": a Prastha of clarified butter "cooked with S'ringi, Madhurika, Bhargi, Shunthi, Rasanjana, sugar, Ambuda, Haridra and Yashti-madhu all taken in equal parts and as Kalka and with four times of water, would cure cases of cough, asthma and hiccough". `SS_Uttara_ch52.txt` lines 116 to 126 (cough): "A compound consisting of Sringi, Vacha, Katphala, Ka-trina, Musta, Phanijjhaka, Abhaya, Bhargi, Deva-daru, Vishva and Hingu taken in hot water", and "a lambative composed of the equal quantity of Triphala, Vyosha, Vidanga, S'ringi, Rasna, Vacha, Padmaka and Deva-daru ... mixed with a copious quantity of honey, sugar and clarified butter". `SS_Uttara_ch60.txt` line 294 names S'ringi in a list of articles that exorcise evil spirits. The chapters do not say that this S'ringi is Karkata-s'ringi; the appendix does. `SS_Kalpa_ch02.txt` line 107 (S'ringi-visha poisoning) and `SS_Kalpa_ch08.txt` line 21 (Sringi among insects) name a poison and an insect and are not this plant.

*Rasayana and vajikarana chapters.* A search of Chikitsa ch.26 (the vajikarana chapter) and ch.27 to 30 (the rasayana chapters) for Karkata-s'ringi and S'ringi found nothing.

### Active Components

The sources read print no constituent. As a general fact, *Rhus* and *Pistacia* are genera of the cashew family (Anacardiaceae), many of whose members carry resins or irritant sap; that is a statement about the family in general and not a finding of the Sushruta text. Nothing is stated here about the composition of the drug. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only, not a recommended practice. Under the name the chapters print, the plant (if it is this one) is one drug among many in a group set against deranged *Pittam*, blood and *Vayu* (Sutra 38), in a plaster for erysipelas (Chikitsa 17, by Gayadasa's reading), in a smoke that allays cough (Chikitsa 40), in an oil for discharge from the ear (Uttara 21) and in lambatives for asthma (Uttara 51). The text gives it no action singly. The Codex gives no protocol and no dose.

### Preparation & Dosing

The preparations the chapters give are a plaster pasted with cow's urine (Chikitsa 17), a smoke (Chikitsa 40), an oil cooked with tender sprouts (Uttara 21) and lambatives "licked with honey and clarified butter" (Uttara 51). No quantity is given for Karkata-s'ringi in any of them. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **SS:** the chapters print no *rasa*, *virya* or *vipaka* for Karkata-s'ringi. The one set of qualities in the text is the property of its group (Sutra 38, Kakolyadi): it "subdues the action of the deranged Pittam, blood and Vayu", increases milk, "favours the accumulation of phlegm (Kapham)", and is "a restorative and an elixir" with the "virtue of augmenting the virile potency of a man". These are the group's, and not the plant's singly.

### Cultivation Register

**Ayurvedic — Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Sushruta names Karkata-s'ringi in the Kakolyadi group of the classification chapter (Sutra 38), and the metrical text on that group calls it "a restorative and an elixir" with the virtue of "augmenting the virile potency of a man". That is a group description in a chapter on the classification of drugs, and the text does not apply it to Karkata-s'ringi alone. The plant is also named in a plaster, a cough-smoke, an ear oil and lambatives (Chikitsa 17, 40; Uttara 21, 51), all therapeutic. The vajikarana chapter (Chikitsa 26) and the rasayana chapters (Chikitsa 27 to 30) do not name it, and the text says nothing of *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* for it. The grade is (b) and not (a) for that reason. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing. The Sushruta text as retrieved gives a cultivator no practice with Karkata-s'ringi: it is a member of a group whose description includes words that sound like rejuvenation, and an ingredient in remedies for cough and ear complaints. The group description is not a ground for taking it, and the Latin name is not the text's.

### Contraindications

The Sushruta text retrieved prints no warning for this plant. The identity is open between two species and a commentator's reading, so no plant should be gathered or used on the strength of this entry. Several members of the cashew family carry irritant sap that can injure the skin, which is a general fact about the family and not about this plant. Discharge from the ear, persistent cough and asthma need medical care. No conservation information was read. No safety information for pregnancy was read. [USER: verify]
