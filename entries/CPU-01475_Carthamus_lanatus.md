# Carthamus lanatus — Krokodeilion, "crocodilium" (Dioscorides 3-12)

## *Carthamus lanatus* (printed by the Dioscorides translators as the first suggested binomial for 3-12, glossed "Blush-flowered Centaury"; the same line also gives *Centaurea crocodylium*) | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** DMM

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Single-text entry.** This plant is named only by Dioscorides (DMM). It was added 2026-10-09 from the extraction list (ATTESTATION_QUEUE §PLANTGAP) at the locus 3-12, line 6635.

**Identity note.** The binomial is the translators' (Osbaldeston and Wood 2000) suggestion and not Dioscorides' identification. The SUGGESTED line (6636) reads: "Carthamus lanatus, Centaurea crocodylium — Blush-flowered Centaury". The chapter defines the plant only by its likeness to another plant (the black chamaeleon), so the identification rests almost entirely on the translators' list. [USER: verify]

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** The text is `Sources/DMM_DioscoRides/text/Dioscorides_De_Materia_Medica_2000_English.txt`. The chapter is in Book Three under the heading "Roots of Akanthoda or Prickly Plants", between 3-11 CHAMAILEON MELAS and 3-13 DIPSAKON.

*3-12 KROKODEILION* (heading at line 6635; text at line 6637, running across a page break). The whole chapter reads: "Crocodilium is similar to black chamaeleon [above] but it grows in woody places. It has a long root — light, somewhat broad, with a sharp smell, like nasturtium [2-185]. The root warmed in water and taken as a drink is able to drive out quantities blood through the nostrils. It is given to the splenetic evidently helping them. The seed of it (round and double like a buckler [shield]) is diuretic."

The chapter gives: a likeness (to the black chamaeleon, 3-11), a habitat (woody places), a root (long, light, somewhat broad, sharp-smelling like *nasturtium*), and three uses: the root warmed in water and drunk "is able to drive out quantities blood through the nostrils" (the words are as printed, "quantities blood"), the root is given to "the splenetic", and the seed, "round and double like a buckler", is diuretic. It gives no other name and no dose.

*3-11 CHAMAILEON MELAS* (heading at line 6630), the "black chamaeleon" to which the chapter refers, was read for context. It has leaves "similar to scolymus hispanicus", "a stalk the thickness of a finger, twenty centimetres high", "prickly flowers", and a root that is "thick, black, compact and sometimes eaten-into", and its uses are for parasitic skin diseases, teeth and ulcers. Its description is the only description 3-12 builds on. It is a separate chapter and is not attributed to this row.

This chapter is cited in the entry I_901 (Small-flowered Catchfly, *Silene gallica*) as 3-12 KROKODEILION, "blush-flowered centaury", as one of the chapters "nearest" to that spine botanical. I_901 states that no text read gives *Silene gallica* as an identification, and it is not this row.

### Active Components

*Carthamus* (distaff thistles, Asteraceae) includes the safflower, *C. tinctorius*, and the woolly distaff thistle, *C. lanatus*, which is a weedy thistle. The composition of the woolly distaff thistle, or of the plant Dioscorides meant, was not read. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only. Dioscorides records the warmed root as a drink for nosebleeding (driving out quantities of blood by the nostrils), a remedy for "the splenetic", and the seed as a diuretic. The Codex gives no protocol for it. The plant is not securely identified.

### Preparation & Dosing

One preparation is printed (3-12): the root is "warmed in water and taken as a drink". The seed's use as a diuretic has no stated preparation. No quantity is given. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **DMM:** no warming, cooling or drying quality is printed for 3-12. The root has "a sharp smell, like nasturtium", and the word "warmed" describes the preparation and is not a quality.

### Cultivation Register

**Greek — Dioscorides (DMM).** *(b) Framed, not a cultivation status.* Dioscorides has no subtle-body system. The chapter offers a discipline of attention: how to tell this plant from the black chamaeleon (it grows in woody places), and a sensory assay of the root by shape and smell. It records two uses of the root and one of the seed, and assigns no status as a long-term substance. The identification with *Carthamus lanatus* is the translators'. [USER: verify]

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing in the chapter gives a cultivator a practice. It is a short chapter on a plant defined by comparison, with a bleeding remedy and a diuretic seed. The abstentive answer is the correct one.

### Contraindications

No contraindication is printed in the chapter. As a general caution, bleeding from the nose that is heavy or recurrent calls for medical assessment and not a home drink, and a diuretic is not advice for pregnancy. The identification is uncertain, so no plant should be gathered or used on the basis of this chapter.
