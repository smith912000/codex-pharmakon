# I_140 — Urtica dioica | Stinging Nettle / Common Nettle / Greater Nettle / Brennessel

**Volume I: Plant-Based Substances**
**Entry:** I_140
**Convergence Score:** 7/9 traditions
**Energetic Tier:** ◈ Jing (structural — nutritive/mineralizing) | ◉ Qi (anti-inflammatory/diuretic/anti-allergic)
**Safety Class:** Excellent when cooked or dried; fresh plant causes contact urticaria (the trichome-injected formic acid + histamine effect); one of the most nutritious and safest medicinal herbs

| SS | Vrishchalyā / Bichhu-booti (Hindi) — *Urtica* species in Ayurvedic texts; joint pain, diuretic, reproductive |

| Latin | Urtica (from *urere* = to burn); *dioica* = two houses (dioecious = separate male and female plants) |

**Botanical identity:** Dioecious perennial to 1.5m; square stems; opposite cordate leaves with serrate margins; both stems and leaves covered with silica-tipped hollow trichomes (stinging hairs) containing formic acid, histamine, serotonin, and acetylcholine; tiny greenish flowers in catkin-like clusters. Grows in nitrogen-rich disturbed habitats (near human settlement, latrines, animal enclosures, compost) worldwide. One of the most globally distributed medicinally significant plants; present on every inhabited continent.

## Section 3 — Convergence Analysis

**Score: 7/9** (DMM · CM · SS · SBJ confirmed; AT · EP · European folk tradition)

Nettle achieves near-maximum convergence (7/9) through a combination of extraordinary global distribution and pharmacological breadth: wherever humans lived and created nitrogen-rich disturbed soils, nettle grew. Wherever nettle grew, humans ate it (one of the most nutritious wild greens), were stung by it, and discovered its medicinal properties.

**The global distribution creates the convergence:** Unlike most high-convergence plants in this Codex (garlic, saffron, opium — high-value trade items), nettle's convergence required no trade. Each tradition discovered nettle independently at their own doorstep.

**The urtication tradition (deliberate sting therapy)** appears in DMM and SS independently: the deliberate application of fresh nettle stings to arthritic or paralyzed joints. This counter-irritant therapy (inducing superficial inflammation to distract from deeper inflammation) is documented across European, South Asian, and East Asian traditions. It remains a folk practice today in some regions. Interestingly, controlled trials suggest genuine anti-inflammatory effect of urtication (possibly via histamine-mediated suppression of joint inflammation via H₃ receptors).

**BPH application (European — RCT level evidence):** The use of nettle root for benign prostatic hyperplasia is a modern pharmacological discovery, not part of ancient tradition — but represents the most rigorously validated modern application of nettle, demonstrating that systematic pharmacological research of traditional plants continues to yield clinically significant discoveries.

## Section 4 — Phytochemical Composition

**Leaf / aerial parts:**

**Flavonoids:**
- **Quercetin** — anti-inflammatory; anti-allergic (mast cell stabilization); inhibits histamine release
- Kaempferol, isorhamnetin, rutin — anti-inflammatory; antioxidant

**Phenolic acids:**
- Caffeic acid, chlorogenic acid, neochlorogenic acid — antioxidant; anti-inflammatory

**Sterols:**
- β-Sitosterol, stigmasterol, campesterol — anti-inflammatory; anti-BPH (root)

**Lectins:**
- Urtica dioica agglutinin (UDA) — binds chitin; antiviral (HIV-1, influenza) in vitro; immune modulating

**Amines (in stinging trichomes — NOT in processed herb):**
- Histamine — causes urticarial sting reaction
- Serotonin — contributes to sting pain
- Acetylcholine — cholinergic pain contribution
- Formic acid — burning pain
- *Note: These are destroyed by cooking, drying, or pressing; processed nettle is non-stinging*

**Chlorophyll** (3–4% in dry leaves — one of the richest natural sources)

**Nutritional profile (dried leaf — extraordinary):**
- Protein: 20–29% dry weight (one of the highest protein contents of any wild leafy vegetable)
- Iron: 5–8mg/100g — significant (Vitamin C co-presence enhances absorption)
- Calcium: 450–500mg/100g
- Magnesium, potassium, zinc, silica
- Vitamins: A, C, K, B complex (B₂, B₅ particularly high)

**Root-specific compounds (BPH application):**
- Polysaccharides: immunostimulating; BPH modulatory
- Lectins (UDA): anti-proliferative in prostate cell models
- β-Sitosterol: 5α-reductase inhibition (DHT reduction)
- Steryl glycosides: sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG) modulation

## Section 5 — Mechanisms of Action

**Anti-inflammatory (primary — multiple convergent mechanisms):**
- **Quercetin:** COX-1/2 inhibition + 5-LOX inhibition → prostaglandin/leukotriene reduction; mast cell stabilization → histamine release reduction
- **UDA (lectin):** modulates immune cell signaling; inhibits inflammatory cytokines
- **Sterols:** membrane-stabilizing anti-inflammatory
- **Caffeic acid derivatives:** NF-κB inhibition → reduced IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6
- **Combined effect:** multi-target anti-inflammatory across arachidonic acid cascade, cytokine pathway, and mast cell pathway

**Anti-allergic (hay fever/allergic rhinitis):**
- Quercetin: potent mast cell stabilizer → reduced histamine and tryptase release
- Caffeic acid derivatives: prostaglandin D₂ inhibition → reduced allergic cascade initiation
- UDA: immunomodulatory regulation of Th2 → Th1 shift → reduced allergic response
- RCT: Mittman (1990) *Planta Medica*: freeze-dried nettle leaf vs. placebo for allergic rhinitis; significant improvement (moderate effect size)

**BPH / Prostate (root compounds):**
- β-Sitosterol: inhibits 5α-reductase → reduced dihydrotestosterone (DHT) → slows prostate growth
- SHBG binding (steryl glycosides): competes for SHBG → reduces free testosterone available for prostate DHT conversion
- Polysaccharides: immune regulation of prostate inflammation
- Clinical: Randomized trials show significant improvement in urinary flow rate, postvoid residual volume, International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS)

**Hemostatic:**
- Vitamin K: clotting factor synthesis cofactor
- Tannins: local astringent styptic
- Explains traditional application for nosebleed and hemoptysis (DMM, CM)

**Nutritive (Jing-tier):**
- Nettle is more nutritive food than most manufactured supplements: protein + iron + calcium + vitamins A/C/K in a bioavailable whole-food matrix
- Traditional "spring tonic" use (young spring nettles) is a genuine nutritional therapy for winter-depletion of iron, vitamins, and minerals

**Counter-irritant urtication (deliberate sting):**
- Fresh nettle application to arthritic joints: histamine + serotonin + acetylcholine → acute superficial inflammation → possible H₃-receptor-mediated suppression of underlying joint inflammation
- Counter-irritation principle: same as capsaicin, mustard plaster, tiger balm — activate TRPV1/TRPA1 → substance P depletion → analgesia
- Not recommended as standard practice (unpredictable, painful) but documented historically across traditions

## Section 6 — Clinical Evidence

**Regulatory:**
- Commission E (Germany): APPROVED for two indications — (1) Treatment of rheumatic complaints (leaf, aerial parts); (2) Irrigation therapy for urinary tract (leaf/aerial parts); ALSO separate monograph for root in BPH
- EMA: Traditional herbal medicinal product for urinary tract maintenance and rheumatic complaints

**Clinical trials:**
- **Allergic rhinitis:** Mittman P (1990) *Planta Medica*: freeze-dried nettle leaf RCT; significant symptom improvement vs. placebo; the first decent RCT on nettle for allergy
- **Arthritis (OA):** Chrubasik et al. (1997) *Phytomedicine*: stewed nettle leaves (50g) vs. diclofenac for OA; nettle equivalent to the NSAID for pain reduction. Key finding: dietary nettle was comparable to pharmaceutical NSAIDs. Allowed diclofenac dose reduction by 75%.
- **BPH:** Safarinejad MR (2005) *Journal of Herbal Pharmacotherapy*: RCT, nettle root extract 120mg vs. placebo for 6 months; significant improvement in IPSS, urinary flow, postvoid residual. N=620. One of the largest herbal RCTs in prostate medicine.
- **Iron deficiency:** Multiple observational studies; nettle's high iron + Vitamin C combination produces better iron repletion than many supplements in food-security contexts

## Section 7 — Phenomenological Record

**The Roman urtication tradition:**
Roman soldiers reportedly introduced nettle to Britain (Pliny, *NH* XXII.9) specifically to warm their legs during cold campaigns — by flogging their legs with fresh nettles. The Roman name for this area of Britain was later reported by Camden (1586) as having a legend that Romans planted nettle to have it available for urtication therapy. The soldiers used a medicine they knew worked (counter-irritant vasodilation for cold extremities) by carrying their pharmacy with them as seed.

**Dioscorides on seeds for sexual vigor (Book IV.93):**
"The seed taken in wine provokes lust." Nettle seeds contain sterols and fatty acids; modern herbalists (notably Michael Moore, Jim McDonald) have revived this application based on their adaptogenic and testosterone-supportive properties in contemporary practice. The ancient application survived in European folk medicine and has been "rediscovered" through modern herbalist clinical observation.

**The Sleeping Beauty parallel:**
The Hans Christian Andersen fairy tale "The Wild Swans" (and related Celtic tradition) features nettles as the transformative herb: the heroine must weave shirts from nettle fibers (nettle has genuine textile fiber — used as a bast fiber like flax) to break the enchantment on her brothers. The narrative encodes two truths about nettle: the pain (stinging) required for healing (the hero's task is painful), and the textile use (nettle cloth was made throughout Northern Europe through the 18th century, before cotton displaced it).

**Nettle textiles:**
*Urtica dioica* produces a bast fiber similar to flax; nettle cloth was commercially produced in Germany, Austria, and Scotland through the 18th century; German WWI army uniforms contained nettle fiber when cotton shortages occurred (1914–1918); modern artisan nettle cloth production has been revived in Austria and the Himalayas. The plant that stings also clothes — a pharmacological/agricultural duality.

**The protein revolution:**
Modern food science interest in alternative proteins has rediscovered nettle: 20–29% protein, complete amino acid profile, sustainable (self-propagating weed), no irrigation required, no pesticide required. Indigenous cultures globally have eaten nettle greens as a staple food in spring. The contemporary protein-food movement has not yet commercialized nettle at scale, but the nutritional profile makes it one of the most viable wild-harvest protein sources in temperate regions.

## Section 8 — Practical Application

**Primary modern applications:**

1. **Seasonal allergic rhinitis (hay fever):** Freeze-dried nettle leaf capsules 300–600mg 3× daily starting 2–4 weeks before allergy season; OR fresh nettle tincture (1:5 in 25%): 5mL 3× daily; Commission E and EMA support; RCT evidence for freeze-dried preparation specifically (freeze-drying preserves quercetin and lectins better than heat-dried)

2. **Osteoarthritis / Rheumatic joint pain:** 50g fresh or frozen nettle leaves cooked (as food or medicinal serving) daily; OR infusion 4g dried leaf 3× daily; RCT shows equivalence to diclofenac; the food-as-medicine format (cooked nettles as vegetable) is both safest and most effective

3. **BPH (root — separate from leaf applications):** Nettle root extract 120–360mg daily (standardized dry extract); OR 4–6g dried root as decoction; Commission E approved; use with saw palmetto (*Serenoa repens*) for synergistic BPH benefit; NOT for urinary tract infection or acute urinary retention (seek medical attention)

4. **Iron deficiency anemia (nutritive):** Fresh nettle soup/tea as regular dietary addition; 2–3 cups nettle infusion daily; superior to many supplement formulations when food sources are preferred; combine with Vitamin C-rich foods for iron absorption enhancement

5. **Urinary tract support:** 4g dried leaf infusion 4× daily; diuretic + anti-inflammatory combination for mild cystitis, urethritis; combine with bearberry (I_098) for antimicrobial action

**Preparation notes:**
- Fresh cooking: blanch in boiling water 30–60 seconds → completely neutralizes stinging compounds; then use as any leafy green; nettle soup, nettle pasta, nettle quiche are traditional European recipes
- Dried herb: all stinging compounds destroyed by drying; safe
- Fresh plant: WEAR GLOVES; stinging contact with skin causes urticarial reaction lasting 30–60 minutes; neutralized by dock leaf (*Rumex*) rubbing (tannin-acid reaction) or commercial antihistamine

**Contraindications:**
- **Fresh plant handling without protection:** skin reaction (short-lived urticaria)
- **Edematous states from cardiac or renal insufficiency:** irrigation therapy contraindicated (Commission E warning); increase in urine output can stress compromised kidneys or heart
- **Pregnancy (seeds):** avoid nettle seed at medicinal doses (androgenic potential)
- **Anticoagulant medications:** Vitamin K content; monitor INR with regular high-consumption
