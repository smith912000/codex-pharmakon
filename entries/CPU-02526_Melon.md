# Melon — 瓜蒂 *Guadi* (melon stalk) and 瓜子 *Guazi* (melon seed) (Shennong bencao jing)
## *Two Shennong monographs on the melon: the stalk (瓜蒂) and the seed (瓜子). Our identification of the stalk is Cucumis melo L., the sweet melon, of medium confidence (Citrullus lanatus is also proposed); for the seed the text itself points elsewhere, to the winter melon (冬瓜), Benincasa hispida* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*
**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the Shennong bencao jing among the texts on disk.** (The winter melon *Benincasa hispida* has its own entry, I_526, tagged SBJ among others; see the identity note below.) The entry rests on Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`), which has two separate monographs on the one fruit, the stalk and the seed. Each is read in turn.

### Varieties

**Stalk — 瓜蒂 *guadi*.** The 味 paragraph reads 味苦，寒 ("bitter, cold"). It governs 大水身面四肢浮肿 ("great water: swelling of body, face and limbs"), "brings down water" (下水), "kills *gu* poison" (杀蛊毒), cough with counterflow and rising qi (咳逆上气), and "eating various fruits, with the illness in the chest and abdomen: all are vomited and purged" (及食诸果，病在胸腹中，皆吐下之). It gives the habitat 生平泽, "grows in level marshes". There is no 久服 clause. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 瓜蒂 *guadi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1643) The Mingyi layer adds 生蒿高 (the characters as printed; probably a misprint for the mountain name 嵩高, which the Codex does not assume [USER: verify]) and the harvest 七月七日采，阴干, "gathered on the seventh day of the seventh month, dried in the shade". Sun's note cites Shuowen (the melon, a pictograph; 蒂 is 瓜当, the part that "meets" the melon), the Guangya (水芝, "water fungus/herb", is a name for the melon), and Tao Hongjing: "this is the stalk of the sweet melon" (甜瓜蒂).

**Seed — 瓜子 *guazi*.** The 味 paragraph reads 味甘，平 ("sweet, balanced"). It "makes a person 阅泽" (the characters as printed; the sense is of a lustrous, pleasing complexion [USER: verify]), gives "good colour of the face" (好颜色) and "adds qi, no hunger" (益气不饥). Its long-use clause reads 久服，轻身、耐老 ("taken long: lighten the body, withstand ageing"). Alias: 一名水芝 (the Yulan has 土芝). Habitat: 生平泽. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 瓜子 *guazi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1653) Wu Pu adds 一名瓣 and "gathered on the seventh day of the seventh month; can be made into face-cream (面脂)". The Mingyi layer adds 一名白瓜子, 生蒿高, and the identification **冬瓜仁也**, "it is the kernel of the winter melon", gathered in the eighth month. Sun's note cites Shuowen (瓣, "the fruit inside a melon"), the Guangya on the winter melon and its seeds, and Tao Hongjing, who says that 白 ("white") should be 甘 ("sweet"); Sun answers that, since the Mingyi lists "白瓜子", the original name had no 白 at all.

**Synthesis of the varieties.** The two monographs are two parts of "the melon" with different characters: the stalk is bitter, cold, and acts by making the body vomit and purge; the seed is sweet, balanced and has a long-use promise. They are the same fruit only if the plant is the same; and that is where the text raises a doubt. Tao Hongjing puts the stalk with the sweet melon (*Cucumis melo*), so the stalk fits our identification; the Mingyi layer and Sun's note put the seed with the winter melon (*Benincasa hispida*), which is a different plant of the same family. The Codex therefore keeps the two monographs together under the name "Melon" but states the plain finding: the stalk is consistent with *Cucumis melo*; the seed, on the text's own word, is the winter-melon kernel, and I_526 (Ash gourd, *Benincasa hispida*) is the entry for that plant. ### Active Components

The texts print no constituent. In general terms the melon stalk (pedicel) of the Cucurbitaceae is known to contain bitter cucurbitacins, which are toxic and strongly emetic; cucurbit seeds are mostly oil and protein. ### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice, and for the stalk a warning. The classic says the stalk is bitter and cold and is used to cause vomiting and purging in swelling, cough and food-related illness of the chest and abdomen. The Codex gives no protocol for it: induced vomiting is dangerous. The seed's promises (complexion, "adds qi, no hunger", long-use lightening of the body) are the classic's claims, not recommended practices.

### Preparation & Dosing

Stalk: gathered on the seventh day of the seventh month and dried in the shade (Mingyi layer); the Shennong paragraph says the illness is to be "vomited and purged" (皆吐下之). Seed: gathered on the seventh day of the seventh month (Wu Pu) or in the eighth month (Mingyi), and Wu Pu says it can be made into face-cream. No decoction, powder, quantity or course is given for either. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

In general knowledge, melon stalk is a strong emetic in Chinese pharmacy, and the effect is attributed to its cucurbitacins, which are also toxic; it has an old place as an emetic. The seeds are an ordinary food. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *Stalk: (a) Attested. Seed: (a) Attested.* The classic files both monographs in the upper grade (上品); its preface says upper-grade drugs nourish life, correspond to heaven, are non-toxic, and may be taken for a long time. On the grading rule used for this series, placement in the upper classic is itself the ground for (a). The **seed** paragraph also has its own 久服 clause, 久服，轻身、耐老 ("taken long: lighten the body, withstand ageing"), and "adds qi, no hunger" (益气不饥), so it is (a) on both grounds. The **stalk** paragraph has no 久服 clause and no life-nourishing promise: it is a drug that makes the patient vomit and purge, and its (a) rests on the upper-grade placement alone. The two grounds sit in one fruit and should not be merged. The Mingyi bielu, not the Shennong text, gives the harvest dates and the identification of the seed with the winter melon. (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 瓜蒂 *guadi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1643); (*Shennong bencao jing*, Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, upper classic, entry 瓜子 *guazi*, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1653)

**Practitioner's note.** The seed carries the classic's long-use promise, and the text itself says it is the winter-melon kernel; the stalk is an emetic and has no place in any long practice. Nothing in the entry tells a cultivator what to do; eating melon or winter-melon seeds as food is ordinary food and is not a practice drawn from the text. Never use the stalk.

### Contraindications

- **Stalk.** An emetic whose action rests on bitter, toxic compounds in general knowledge; do not use it. Induced vomiting is dangerous, and swelling and cough need medical assessment.
- **Seed.** Ordinary food seed; allergy to cucurbit seeds is possible.
- **Identification.** The seed and the stalk may be from two different plants (sweet melon and winter melon); do not treat them as one drug.
- **Pregnancy.** No information read. [USER: verify]
