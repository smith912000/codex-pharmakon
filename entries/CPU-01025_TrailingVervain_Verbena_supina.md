# Trailing Vervain / Prostrate Vervain

## *Verbena supina* L. | Family: Verbenaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, DMM
**Energetic Classification:** Cool–Dry, astringent/nervine — *genus-level attribution to* Verbena officinalis*; not independently graded for* V. supina
**Training Phase:** Foundation — Signature Study

---

### Origin & Tradition

*Verbena supina* L. — trailing or prostrate vervain — is a low, mat-forming annual/short-lived herb of the vervain family (Verbenaceae), native to the Mediterranean basin, North Africa, the Middle East and into western/southern Asia, and naturalised elsewhere in warm dry ground. As the epithet *supina* ("lying flat") indicates, it sprawls along the soil surface with deeply cut, pinnately lobed leaves and small pale-lilac to bluish flowers in slender spikes — a habit quite unlike the tall, erect European vervain. It is a plant of waste ground, trackways, seasonally-dry hollows and sandy or stony soil.

The honest spine of this entry is the sparsest of the three. **The famous medicinal and magical "vervain" of the classical and European tradition is *Verbena officinalis* (common vervain) — not *V. supina*.** Common vervain is the sacred *verbena* of Roman altar-herb lore, the "hierobotane"/"peristereon" of the Greek stream, the wound-herb, nerve-tonic and febrifuge of the Western herbal record, and it is already in the codex. *V. supina* is a genuine wild vervain that shares the genus and the name — and, critically, **has essentially no species-specific biomedical literature of its own.** Its standing in a materia medica is almost entirely a **genus/name inheritance** from *V. officinalis*.

**DMM (Dioscorides / Greek herbal stream).** Dioscorides describes *peristereon* / *hierobotane* ("sacred herb," "dove-plant") — the vervain of the Greek record — used for wounds, fevers and as an amulet-herb. The plant so described is overwhelmingly identified with *V. officinalis* (and its erect relatives), **not** the prostrate Mediterranean *V. supina*. **[USER: verify]** — the DMM tag here should be read as almost certainly *V. officinalis*; *V. supina* carries it only by shared genus and name. Flagged.

**CM (Avicenna, *Canon of Medicine*, Unani).** The vervain of the *Canon* (the *ra'i al-hamam* / vervain entry) again corresponds to the common medicinal vervain of the Mediterranean and Near East, read as cooling, drying, astringent and wound-consolidating. There is no basis for reading *V. supina* specifically into Avicenna. **[USER: verify]** — genus/name inheritance, not species attestation.

The training value of trailing vervain is therefore as the codex's clearest case of **near-total reputation borrowing**: a plant whose entire apparent medicinal identity is carried by a celebrated genus-mate, with the species' own evidence base close to empty. The cultivator studies it to internalise the discipline of *not* importing a congener's dossier as if it were the species' own.

### Active Components

No species-specific phytochemical profile for *V. supina* was retrieved from the biomedical literature (see Scientific Correlation — the PubMed record for this species is effectively empty). What can honestly be said is **genus-level**, and must be read as *Verbena*-typical rather than confirmed for *V. supina*:

- **Iridoid glycosides** — verbenalin (cornin) and hastatoside are the characteristic vervain iridoids, confirmed abundant in *V. officinalis* aerial parts.
- **Phenylpropanoid glycosides** — verbascoside (acteoside) and isoverbascoside, the dominant polyphenols of *V. officinalis* biomass.
- **Phenolic acids** — protocatechuic, chlorogenic, vanillic, caffeic, ferulic and rosmarinic acids (quantified in *V. officinalis* cultures).
- **Triterpenoids and sterols** — ursolic acid, oleanolic acid and their epi-derivatives, β-sitosterol (isolated from *V. officinalis*).
- **Flavonoids** — reported at genus level.

**Congener note — do NOT assume for this species:** every constituent above is documented in *V. officinalis* (or genus-level *Verbena* culture work), **not** measured in *V. supina*. Any folk claim that *V. supina* possesses a particular iridoid or verbascoside content is, on current evidence, an assumption inherited from the common vervain. Treat *V. supina*'s chemistry as **presumed-typical but unverified.**

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

In Kenetica, trailing vervain is filed as a **Signature Study of inheritance**, not a working remedy. Because no species-specific efficacy data exists, it carries **no independent Kenetica indication.** Where the tradition speaks of vervain as a mild astringent nervine, wound-wash and febrifuge, those are *V. officinalis* uses that *V. supina* has borrowed; the codex records them only as **congener context**, not as validated properties of the trailing species.

The cultivator's use-case is: (1) an easy, drought-hardy ground-covering annual for dry Mediterranean-type beds and pollinators; and (2) a teaching specimen for the single most important honesty lesson in the Foundation phase — distinguishing a species' own evidence from the reputation of its genus. If a locally-abundant *V. supina* is to be used at all, it is at traditional/food-herb level only, and explicitly not on the strength of *V. officinalis* data.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

The Cool–Dry, astringent and mildly nervine reading in the header is the standard humoral/Western-herbal characterisation of **common vervain (*V. officinalis*)** — cooling, drying, astringent, wound-consolidating and calming to the nerves. It is applied to *V. supina* only by genus membership and shared name; **no distinct classical energetic grading of *V. supina* survives.** Recorded as genus-level, inherited.

### Contraindications

- **Pregnancy — AVOID (precautionary, congener-derived):** common vervain (*V. officinalis*) carries a traditional **uterine-stimulant / emmenagogue** reputation and is customarily avoided in pregnancy. Whether *V. supina* shares this activity is **unverified**, but the prudent course under the shared name is to **apply the same precaution: avoid in pregnancy.** Stated explicitly as a congener-derived caution, not a measured *V. supina* effect.
- **Lactation:** insufficient data for either species at medicinal doses; avoid concentrated preparations while breastfeeding.
- **Sparse safety data:** because *V. supina* has essentially no toxicological literature, its safety profile is **unknown.** Absence of evidence of harm is not evidence of safety; keep any use at traditional/food-herb level and do not present it as a treatment.
- **Allergy / correct identification:** Verbenaceae sensitivity is possible; and because prostrate Mediterranean vervains can be confused with other low herbs, correct botanical identification is essential before any use.
- **No validated therapeutic use:** with no species-specific efficacy or safety evidence, *V. supina* should not be used or recommended as a remedy for any condition.
