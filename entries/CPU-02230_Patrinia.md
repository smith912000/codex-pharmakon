# Patrinia — 败酱 (Bai Jiang); also Lu Chang (鹿肠), Lu Shou, Ma Cao, Ze Bai

## **Patrinia scabiosifolia* Link (Caprifoliaceae/Valerianaceae), the root and herb, the usual modern identification of 败酱 *bai jiang*, of medium confidence at species level; the text gives only a name, habitat and a smell, and Yang 1998 prints a different species, *Patrinia heterophylla** | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

**Tradition Tags:** SBJ

### Origin & Tradition

*Bai jiang* is named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk. It is a single-text entry. The placeholder row it replaces was named "Patrinia sp."; the text does not fix the species.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** Sun Xingyan's edition files 败酱 in the middle classic (卷二 中经; `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 2107, 味 paragraph; name line 2105). The Shennong text proper: "bitter, balanced" (味苦，平). It "mainly treats" "sudden heat, fire sores, red qi; scab and itch; jaundice (疸) and haemorrhoids; 'horse-saddle' heat qi" (主暴热火创、赤气，疥瘙，疸痔，马鞍热气). "One name: Lu Chang" 鹿肠, "deer intestine." "It grows in rivers and valleys" (生川谷). No 久服 clause and no toxicity statement.

*Wu Pu layer.* None for this entry.

*Mingyi bielu layer* (line 2109). Aliases 鹿首, 马草, 泽败. Habitat: Jiangxia. The root is gathered in the eighth month and dried in the sun.

*Sun's note* (案, line 2111). *Fanzi jiran*: bai jiang "comes from the Sanfu." Tao Hongjing: "the qi is like rotten soy-paste (败酱), hence the name." So the name is a smell name; Tao's gloss is the only description of the plant in the entry.

*Drug-relations table* (line 4387): 狗脊 "with 萆[薢] as envoy; dislikes (恶) 败酱."

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt` line 2813) prints "Bai Jiang (Herba Patriniae Heterophyllae Cum Radice) is bitter and balanced. It mainly treats fulminant heat, burns, red qi [i.e., blood diseases], scabs, flat abscesses, hemorrhoids, horse saddle [bruises], and heat qi. Its other name is Lu Chang (Deer Intestine)." Three points differ from Sun's text in rendering: 火创 as "burns," 疸 as "flat abscesses," and 马鞍 as "horse saddle [bruises]." Yang's species (*P. heterophylla*) is not the species named in the entry heading; modern Chinese references commonly give two sources for *bai jiang*, a yellow-flowered *Patrinia* (*P. scabiosifolia*) and a white-flowered one (*P. villosa*), and the Shennong text cannot choose between them. ### Active Components

*Patrinia* roots are known for a strong, unpleasant smell when dried, which agrees with Tao Hongjing's name gloss. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry, and no constituent is asserted. [USER: verify]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

This is a historical record of one text, not a recommended practice.

- **What the text says it is for.** Heat-sores, scab and itch, jaundice or abscess (the rendering differs), haemorrhoids, and a "horse-saddle" heat.
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation, no 久服 promise, and no toxicity statement.

The indications are wound, skin and bowel complaints that need medical care today. No Kenetica protocol is derived from this entry.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Mingyi layer gives the harvest only: the root, in the eighth month, dried in the sun (line 2109). No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(b) Framed.* The entry stands in the middle classic (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 1677 defines it: "ministers, mainly nourishing the nature to correspond with the human; toxic or nontoxic, to be judged as fitting," for those who wish "to check disease and replenish the weak"). The entry has no 久服 clause and promises nothing for the person's constitution; every indication is a sore, skin or bowel complaint. No cultivation status beyond the middle grade is attested.

**Practitioner's note.** A middle-grade drug in the classic, listed for sores and skin complaints, with no long-term promise. For a cultivator there is nothing to do with it except read the record.
