# Selaginella — Juan Bai 卷柏 (Shennong bencao jing); aliases Bao Zu 豹足, Qiu Gu 求股, Jiao Shi 交时, Wan Sui 万岁, Shen Zhi 神枝

## *A Selaginella (spike-moss genus), the whole herb being the drug juan bai; Yang 1998 prints "Herba Selaginellae Involvensis" (Selaginella involvens (Sw.) Spring) as the translator's gloss, and the classic itself describes no plant. Identification: genus Selaginella, high confidence; species uncertain, because the classic describes no plant and the Latin is the modern translator's gloss, so the entry is filed under the genus. Basis: the Chinese name and Yang's Latin* | *(Volume I — Plant-Based)*

### Origin & Tradition

**Named only by the *Shennong bencao jing* among the texts on disk, under this Chinese name.** This is a single-text entry. The principal text is Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction, `Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt`; the other edition on disk, Yang 1998 (`Sources/SBJ_Shennong/text/Shennong_Yang_1998.txt`), is read as a second witness and attributed to Yang. The row began on 13 October as an unverified placeholder, "Selaginella sp.", taken from Yang's English; this entry replaces it. A search of the other texts on disk for *Selaginella* and for the Chinese and pinyin names found nothing for this drug.

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** The entry is 卷柏 *juan bai* in the **upper classic** (Sun Xingyan ed. 1799, entry 卷柏, `Shennong_SunXingyan_1799_daizhige.txt` line 651, with the footer 卷一 上经 "Volume 1, upper classic" printed at lines 649 and 659). Sun's paragraph (line 653) reads: 味辛，温。生山谷 ("acrid, warm; grows in mountain valleys"); 主五脏邪气 ("governs evil qi in the five viscera"), 女子阴中寒热痛 ("in women, cold and heat and pain within the vagina"), 症瘕、血闭、绝子 ("concretions and conglomerations, blood block, and cut-off offspring", i.e. infertility). Then comes the 久服 clause: 久服，轻身、和 ("taken long, lighten the body, harmonise ..."), and the e-text breaks off at 和 and runs straight into the Wu Pu layer. The end of the clause is lost in this file.

The Wu Pu layer (《吴普》曰, third century, same line 653) gives the flavour in two voices: "Shennong: acrid; Tongjun and Leigong: sweet", and cites the *Yulan* for the aliases 豹足 *bao zu*, 求股 *qiu gu*, 万岁 *wan sui* ("ten thousand years"), 神枝 *shen zhi* and 时, and for the habitat 生山谷. The Mingyi bielu layer (line 655) adds the aliases 豹足, 求股 and 交时 *jiao shi*, the habitat 生常山 ("grows on Mount Chang"), and a harvest: 五月、七月采，阴干 ("gathered in the fifth and seventh months, dried in the shade"). Sun's note (案, line 657) says only that the *Fan Zi Jiran* says juan bai comes from the Sanfu region. No description of the plant is given in any of these layers.

**Yang 1998 as second witness.** Yang prints (line 2285): "Juan Bai (Herba Selaginellae Involvensis) is acrid and balanced. It mainly treats evil qi in the five viscera and, in females, genital cold and heat and pain, concretions and conglomerations, blood block, and infertility. Protracted taking may make the body light and the facial complexion harmonious. Its other name is Wan Sui (Ten Thousand Years). It grows in mountains and valleys." Two differences from Sun need stating. First, Yang files the drug in his **Middle Class** (page 60 of the PDF, headed "Herbs: Middle Class") while Sun files it in the upper classic; the register below follows Sun. Second, Yang prints the nature as "balanced" (平) where Sun's e-text prints "warm" (温); the Wu Pu layer in Sun carries the further variation of "acrid" against "sweet". Yang supplies the end of the 久服 clause that Sun's e-text has lost: "the body light and the facial complexion harmonious". Yang's footnote 137 (line 2309) is modern commentary and not part of the classic: "Ten Thousand Years is a term used to express a wish of a long life or an epithet meaning eternal or evergreen. Juan Bai has a tough life. It can survive even if it has been uprooted and dried for many days. This is why it has acquired this name." The reading of the name as a sign of toughness is Yang's, not the classic's.

**Identity.** The classic describes no plant. The Latin comes from Yang's English gloss, "Herba Selaginellae Involvensis" (*Selaginella involvens*). The standard modern Chinese identifications of *juan bai* are other species of the same genus (*Selaginella tamariscina* and *S. pulvinata* are the ones usually named); that is general knowledge not retrieved from a source on disk. ### Active Components

The sources read name no constituent. In general terms, *Selaginella* species are small vascular plants (lycophytes, not mosses), and the genus is known in general for flavonoids, among them biflavonoids such as amentoflavone. No analytical source was retrieved for this entry, and the statement is about the genus, not about the species Yang names. [USER: verify any named constituent before adding it.]

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

**Historical record only. Not a practice.**

- **What the text says it is for.** Evil qi in the five viscera; in women, genital cold and heat and pain, concretions and conglomerations, blood block and infertility. Taken long, it lightens the body (and, in Yang's text, harmonises the complexion).
- **What the text does not say.** No dose, no preparation beyond drying the herb in the shade, no duration, and no description of the plant.

The Codex does not translate the classic's terms into modern diagnoses, gives no protocol, and does not recommend this plant. The indications include blood block and cut-off offspring; these are medical conditions.

### Preparation & Dosing

The Shennong text gives no preparation and no dose. The Mingyi layer gives only the harvest: gathered in the fifth and seventh months and dried in the shade (line 655). The classic's general rule for upper drugs is that they are non-toxic and may be taken long (line 43); it is a rule for the class, not a dose for this plant. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. **Do not use any dose from this entry.** [USER: verify before any numerical use.]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. *Selaginella* is a genus of spike-mosses in the lycophytes; some species are able to dry out almost completely and revive when wetted (the "resurrection" habit), which is the plausible reason for the tough-life remark in Yang's footnote. The classic's indications (evil qi, concretions, blood block) have no modern equivalent and are not translated into one. No clinical studies were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]

### Cultivation Register

**Divine Farmer's Classic (SBJ).** *(a) Attested.* The paragraph stands in the upper classic (上经) in Sun Xingyan's 1799 reconstruction (footers at lines 649 and 659). The preface (line 43) reads 上药一百二十种，为君，主养命以应天，无毒。多服、久服不伤人。欲轻身益气，不老延年者，本上经: the upper drugs are "the lords, mainly nourishing life to correspond with heaven; non-toxic; taking much or taking long does not harm people; for those who wish to lighten the body and boost the qi, not age and extend the years". The drug's own paragraph carries a 久服 clause that promises lightening of the body (久服，轻身), and Yang's text adds "the facial complexion harmonious". This is the *san pin* sense of an upper-grade drug, taken to nourish life; 輕身 "lighten the body" has no pharmacological equivalent and is not weight loss. Yang files the drug in the middle class; where Sun and Yang place a drug in different grades, Sun's grade decides, and the difference is stated here. The Wu Pu and Mingyi readings do not count toward the grade. The text does not mention *jing*, *qi* or *shen* as acted on by this drug, and none is claimed.

**Practitioner's note.** An upper-grade herb whose own paragraph promises a lightened body on long use, in a classic whose grading is a cultivation hierarchy. The classic gives no dose, no duration and no plant description, and the species is uncertain. A cultivator cannot responsibly act on that; the practical lesson is the grade and the missing identification, not a recipe.

### Contraindications

- **Identity.** The classic describes no plant, and the species is the translator's gloss. Do not substitute one *Selaginella* for another, or for a different herb.
- **Blood block, infertility, gynaecological pain.** These are medical conditions; use proper care.
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children.** No statement in the sources retrieved. The indications include gynaecological complaints. [USER: verify]
- **Long use.** The upper-grade promise is the classic's, not a safety finding. No modern safety data were retrieved for this entry. [USER: verify]
- **Text variants.** Sun's e-text is cut off at the end of the 久服 clause; the completion is taken from Yang.
