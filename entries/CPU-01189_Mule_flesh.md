# Mule flesh — Ashvatara (as printed in `_sources/sushruta.csv`); "the mule" in the Ekashapha (unbifurcated-hoof) group, Sushruta Samhita Sutrasthana ch.46 (Bhishagratna)
## *The meat of the mule, the hybrid of horse and ass, as Bhishagratna's English prints it; his text gives the Sanskrit group name Ekashapha for animals with unbifurcated hoofs* | *(Volume III — Animal)*
**Tradition Tags:** SS

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** The locus is the chapter on food and drink, *Sutrasthana* ch.46 ("Annapana-Vidhi-madhyaya"), in Bhishagratna's English (`Sources/SS_Sushruta/text/sutra/SS_Sutra_ch46.txt`). The chapter takes the meat group (line 385, "The meat Group: Now I shall describe the properties of the different species of edible meats") through its classes of animal. The Domestic Animal Group (lines 616-617) is defined thus: "Animals such as horses, mules, cows, bullocks, asses, camels, goats, sheep, and Medapuchhas (fat tailed or Turkish sheep) etc., belong to the group of domestic animals (Gramyas)." The list row that created the entry is `_sources/sushruta.csv` line 231, "Ashvatara (mule)", source "Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana ch.46 (Annapanavidhi); Bhishagratna trans. vol.1".

The general verse for the group follows (lines 621-626): "The flesh of domestic animals is possessed of constructive, tonic and appetising properties, is sweet in taste and digestion. It destroys the deranged Vayu and produces the Kapham and Pittam." Then the individual animals are taken in turn: the goat, "moderately cooling in its potency ... heavy and demulcent" (lines 625-637); sheep, "constructive, tonic and heavy, and generates the Pittam and Kapham" (lines 637-639); the Medapuchcha, "aphrodisiac and has properties similar to those of mutton" (lines 639-641); beef (lines 643-646). The mule is named in the sentence that follows (lines 646-649): "The flesh of an animal with unbifurcated hoofs (Ekashapha such as, the horse and the mule etc.), has a slightly saline taste, and is possessed of properties similar to those of mutton." So the text assigns the mule's meat, along with the horse's, the taste "slightly saline" and the properties of mutton, which are those quoted above.

The mule appears elsewhere in Bhishagratna's Sushruta in lists of domestic-animal products, none of which is the meat as a drug of its own:
- *Sutrasthana* ch.45, the Urine Group (line 1687): "The urine of cows, buffaloes, goats, sheep, mules, horses, and camels are commonly hot and bitter, and leave a saline after-taste", with a use in worms, obesity, poisoning, piles, ascites and other conditions listed there.
- *Sutrasthana* ch.45, a translator's footnote to the milk section (lines 441-444): milk of "a doe, or of a she-mule, or of a cow-rhinoceros" may be included in the list as beneficial for external applications.
- *Uttaratantra* ch.41, on phthisis (line 224): the diet of such a patient "may also consist of Mudga-soup, or of Adhaki-soup, or of the flesh of an ass, camel, elephant, mule or horse variously prepared and well cooked".
- *Uttaratantra* ch.60 (line 264): dung of the mule is named among the dungs of an ass, horse, owl, camel, dog, jackal, vulture, crow and boar, pasted with the urine of a she-goat and cooked with oil, in a passage whose context is the treatment of seizure by a *Graha* (the passage speaks of "the attack even of a violent Graha"). *Chikitsasthana* ch.11 (line 222), a chapter on the urinary diseases (*prameha*): "The powdered dung of a camel, a mule, or an ass should be administered to him in food".

### Active Components

No text read names a constituent. In general terms, equine muscle meat is protein, with fat, B vitamins, iron and other minerals, as for any red meat. Mule meat is not distinguished chemically from horse or donkey meat in anything read.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The Sushruta text records mule meat as an ordinary article of diet in the class of equine meats, with mutton-like properties, and once as part of a phthisis diet. The Codex records that and does not recommend eating, or prescribing, it. Reasons: in many places and cultures the slaughter and sale of equine meat for human food is restricted or forbidden by law or by religious and cultural rule, and some jurisdictions treat equines as companion animals; equine meat can carry parasites (including *Trichinella*) and drug residues from veterinary treatments; and nothing in the sources retrieved gives an indication that cannot be met by ordinary food. Phthisis (tuberculosis) is a bacterial infection that needs modern diagnosis and drug treatment, not a diet. No modern protocol is invented here.

### Preparation & Dosing

The sources give no preparation, other than the Uttaratantra instruction for the phthisis diet that the flesh be "variously prepared and well cooked" (ch.41, line 224). No quantity or dose is given for the mule flesh. No dose is given in the sources retrieved. [USER: verify]

### Scientific Correlation

General, well-established facts only. Red meat is a source of protein, iron and vitamin B12. Equine meat, like other meat, must be cooked through to kill parasites such as *Trichinella*. ### Traditional Energetic Classification

**Ayurveda (SS):** For the equine (Ekashapha) meats including the mule, Sushruta ch.46 prints the taste: "a slightly saline taste", and the properties are "similar to those of mutton" (lines 646-649). Those are, as printed for mutton (lines 637-639): "constructive, tonic and heavy", generating *pitta* and *kapha*. For domestic animals as a group the text prints (lines 621-626) "constructive, tonic and appetising ... sweet in taste and digestion", destroying deranged *vayu* and producing *kapha* and *pitta*. A separate *virya* (potency) and *vipaka* (post-digestive taste) for the mule are not printed in the lines read, except that the group verse gives "sweet ... in digestion"; the "slightly saline taste" for the equines sits against that without being reconciled in the text. ### Cultivation Register

**Ayurveda / Sushruta (SS).** *(b) Framed; no cultivation status.* Mule flesh is attested in the food chapter (*Sutrasthana* ch.46), in the domestic-animal meat group, with the properties of mutton, and once in a phthisis diet (*Uttaratantra* ch.41, line 224). "Constructive" and "tonic" are properties listed for the whole group of domestic-animal meats in a chapter on food; they are not a placement in a *rasayana* or *vajikarana* chapter, and I found no such placement for the mule in the lines read (a search of the Sushruta chapter files on disk for "mule" returned only the passages cited above). No claim about *ojas*, *tejas* or *prana* is made for it. The sattvic, rajasic and tamasic food lists belong to *Samkhya*-era material and not to Sushruta, and are not used here.

**Practitioner's note.** Nothing here is a practice for a cultivator. The mule is an ordinary meat in a food chapter, with the properties of mutton, and the Codex does not recommend eating it. Where a cultivator wants the Sushruta framework for diet, the chapter's grouping of foods by taste, potency and effect on the *doshas* is the thing to read, not this one animal. ### Contraindications

- **Legal and cultural:** the sale and consumption of equine and mule meat is prohibited or restricted in many countries and is forbidden in several religious dietary laws; check the law and the person's own rules before offering it.
- **Parasites and residues:** equine meat can carry parasites such as *Trichinella* and drug residues from veterinary medicines; cook thoroughly and use only inspected meat.
- **Tuberculosis and chronic illness:** a diet of any meat is not a treatment for phthisis or any other infection.
- **Ethics:** the Codex does not recommend slaughtering an animal for these uses.
