# Darbha / Cogongrass — Darbha / Kusha (Sanskrit), Bai Mao Gen (Chinese, "white grass root"), Halfa (Arabic-Unani)

## *Imperata cylindrica* (L.) Raeusch. | Family: Poaceae

**Tradition Tags:** CM, SBJ, SS
**Energetic Classification:** Cold and dry; cooling to the blood (Galenic — cold in the first-to-second degree)
**Training Phase:** Purification

---

### Origin & Tradition

**Shennong / Chinese materia medica (SBJ):** The rhizome, *Bai Mao Gen* ("white thatch-grass root"), is a long-standing item of the Chinese materia medica. It is classed as sweet and cold, entering the lung, stomach and bladder. Its two canonical actions are **cooling the blood to stop bleeding** (epistaxis, blood in the urine, coughing of blood) and **clearing heat to promote urination** — a diuretic that drains damp-heat and eases oedema and hot, scanty urination. The fresh root juice was prized for feverish thirst and stomach heat.

**Sushruta / Ayurveda (SS):** As *darbha* or *kusha* grass, *Imperata* and its close sacred relatives are woven through the Ayurvedic and Vedic corpus. The rhizome and root are used as a cooling diuretic (*mutrala*) and haemostatic (*raktastambhana*), directed at burning urination, urinary gravel and bleeding disorders (*raktapitta*). *Kusha* grass is simultaneously a ritual purity object and a medicine — the two roles are not separable in the tradition.

**Avicenna / Unani (CM):** Grasses of this type (*halfa*) enter the Unani repertory as cooling, drying diuretics used to flush the urinary tract, relieve hot dysuria and calm internal heat and bleeding — a humoral reading closely parallel to the Chinese "cool the blood, promote urination" pairing.

**Doctrinal & Symbolic Layer:** *Darbha/kusha* is one of the most sacred plants of the Vedic world — used to purify ritual ground, strain sacrificial fluids, and seat the practitioner. Its symbolism is filtration and purity: a grass that cleans. That doctrinal role maps directly onto its medicinal signature as a **cooling diuretic and blood-cooler** — a plant that clears heat and drains what is stagnant or hot, literally straining the fluids of the body as its sacred form strained the fluids of the rite.

### Active Components

- **Simple sugars and polysaccharides** (the sweet, demulcent fraction of the fresh rhizome).
- **Phenolics and flavonoids** — antioxidant and anti-inflammatory constituents identified in rhizome/root extracts.
- **Triterpenoids (e.g. cylindrin, arundoin) and phytosterols** — characteristic of the rhizome.
- **Chlorogenic and caffeic acid derivatives; glycosides** — among the 70+ constituents catalogued for the species.
- Root extracts have also yielded a range of cytotoxic metabolites in profiling studies.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Darbha is a **Purification-phase** substance: a cooling drainer. Where the practitioner's state runs hot, congested or feverishly agitated — heat rising, fluids scanty and dark, low-grade bleeding or inflammation — the rhizome cools the blood and opens the water-course, moving damp-heat downward and out. It is gentle and food-adjacent (sweet, non-toxic in traditional use), making it suitable for extended purification work rather than a single sharp intervention. Pair it with Foundation nutritives when purification risks over-draining; use it alone when the task is to clear and cool.

### Scientific Correlation

According to PubMed: Jung & Shin (2021), *Molecules* — a review of *Imperata cylindrica* reporting 72 identified constituents (saponins, flavonoids, phenols, glycosides predominating) and preclinical immunomodulatory, antibacterial, antitumour, anti-inflammatory and hepatoprotective activities in vitro and in vivo, with the species also noted for traditional diuretic use. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules26051454.

Nayim et al. (2021), *BioMed Research International* — a methanolic root extract inhibited proliferation of human cervical cancer cells (HeLa, CaSki), inducing concentration- and time-dependent apoptosis and G0/G1 cell-cycle arrest; UHPLC-HRMS profiling tentatively identified multiple bioactive metabolites (including epicatechin, caffeic acid and myricetin). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/4259777.

**Evidence grade:** The haemostatic and diuretic uses are **traditional (TCM and Ayurvedic), with supporting phytochemical rationale** but limited controlled human data. The antitumour, anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory findings are **preclinical (in vitro / animal)**. No robust clinical trials establish efficacy or human dosing.

### Traditional Energetic Classification

- **TCM:** *Bai Mao Gen* — sweet, cold; enters lung, stomach, bladder; cools blood, stops bleeding, clears heat, promotes urination.
- **Ayurveda (SS):** *darbha/kusha* — *madhura* (sweet) *rasa*, *sheeta virya* (cooling), *mutrala* (diuretic) and *raktastambhana* (haemostatic); pacifies *pitta*.
- **Galenic/Unani (CM):** cold and dry; cooling, drying diuretic for hot dysuria and internal heat.

### Contraindications

- Cold, deficient or damp-cold constitutions may be aggravated by this cooling diuretic; avoid in cold-type diarrhoea and spleen-yang deficiency (TCM framing).
- Diuretic action warrants caution alongside prescription diuretics or in dehydration and electrolyte imbalance.
- *Imperata cylindrica* is an aggressively invasive grass in many regions; harvest only from clean, uncontaminated ground (rhizomes accumulate soil pollutants).
