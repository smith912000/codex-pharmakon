### Origin & Tradition

**Assyrian (AT).** The attestation rests on a list row and nothing more. The project's list `_sources/assyrian.csv` has one row, line 55: "Alkali / potash (niru / qili)", botanical/chemical column "Natron or plant ash (sodium/potassium carbonate)", class mineral, source reference "Assyrian tablets; africame citing cuneiform; used with plant resin in soap-making preparations", flagged id-uncertain. The "africame" reference is a popular website and was not used. The spine note adds that the row was kept as "alkali / potash (niru)" and that the list's chemical gloss is "natron or plant ash".

The Assyrian text on disk (Thompson 1923, `Sources/AT_Assyrian/text/Thompson_1923_Assyrian_Medical_Texts.txt`) is OCR of the cuneiform plates of 660 tablets and cannot be searched for a substance; a search of it for "alkali", "potash", "natron", "soda" and "ash" returned no usable line, and `Sources/AT_Assyrian/extract/README.md` says "nothing extractable". The transliterated names *niru* and *qili* are the list's and could not be confirmed against a text. The note's statement that the material was "used with plant resin in soap-making preparations" was not read in any source; soap-making is also not a medical use. The blocking warning is in force: no tablet number, deity or demon is given, and no identification is asserted. [USER: verify]

### Active Components

None can be stated for the material as the list names it. In general terms, "alkali" in the older chemistry means the carbonates of sodium or potassium, from mineral natron or from the ashes of plants; they dissolve in water to give a strongly alkaline solution and react with fats to give soap. This is a general statement, not a reading of any tablet.

### Effect & Use Case in Kenetica

Historical record only; not a recommended practice. The only thing the sources retrieved say is that a list carries the name. No text was read that says what the Assyrian physician did with it. Concentrated alkali burns skin and eyes. The Codex offers no protocol, dose or preparation.

### Contraindications

- **Caustic injury:** strong alkali burns skin, mouth and eyes and is dangerous if swallowed.
- **Identification:** the list itself flags the identification as uncertain; "natron" and "plant ash" are different materials.
- **Wound use:** nothing here supports applying alkali to broken skin or the eye.
